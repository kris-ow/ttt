Elon Musk joined X Takeover 2026 at Giga Texas by live video, his third consecutive appearance at the event, in front of a crowd the host put at over 2,000 people. The conversation covered Tesla's near-death moments in 2008 and 2018, Starship flight 14 and the upcoming ship-catch attempt, SpaceX's Cursor acquisition and AI philosophy, the Tesla/SpaceX chip-fab plans, Optimus hand engineering, and robotaxi expansion. The most newsworthy specifics: a four-to-six-week timeline for the next Starship flight (targeting the first ship catch, "a little bit before Thanksgiving"), a goal of ~10 chip designs per year at the Giga Texas research fab, and new detail on China's magnet export restriction setting Optimus back 2-3 months.

## Tesla's near-death history
1. **2008 financing crisis:** Musk recalled that Tesla closed its financing round at 6 p.m. on Christmas Eve 2008, calling it the last hour it was possible to do so, and said the company would have missed payroll days after Christmas if the round hadn't closed.
2. **2018 Model 3 production crisis:** He described needing to reach 5,000 Model 3s a week or face bankruptcy, requiring a roughly 50% production increase in three weeks. He detailed building a tent in a parking lot for a third general-assembly line and using discarded small-parts conveyor equipment that lacked the power to move full cars; because the lot sat on a 3-4° incline, starting the line at the top let the cars move downhill. He stated this increased production by 30% in three weeks, and said the short sellers, who had "extremely good inside information," were right on a conventional reading of the situation.

## Starship / SpaceX
1. **Flight 14 engine-out:** Musk said that in the minutes before the go-for-orbit call, the team was trying to determine whether the engine failure on ascent had a common cause that could affect restarting the center sea-level Raptors for the de-orbit burn, since a ship stuck in orbit could reenter uncontrolled. He said the cause turned out to be an engine-computer software failure not present in the other engine computers, so he judged it safe to proceed to orbit.
2. **Why orbit only now:** He said all prior Starship missions could have reached orbit, but SpaceX held off until it was extremely confident of a safe de-orbit. On this flight the heat shield was "extremely robust" and the engines were robust apart from the one glitch, which he called easy to remedy.
3. **Next flight timeline, ship catch attempt:** Musk said the next flight is roughly four to six weeks out, "probably launches a little bit before Thanksgiving is my guess," with no exact date because the changes between flights, though few this time, include some that require work inside the tank. The flight will attempt to catch the ship (the booster has already been caught twice); success would be the first full recovery of an orbital-class rocket.
4. **Reflight as the following milestone:** The flight after that would aim to refly both booster and ship, the first time all parts of an orbital rocket are reflown, followed by a long tail of engineering, production and launch-system work toward rapid reusability.
5. **SpaceX and AI under one roof, the "sentient sun":** Asked how rockets and superintelligence came to belong in one company, Musk said expanding into space lets you harness far more of the sun's power (he put the sun at 99.86% of the solar system's mass), and that most of that energy will go to digital intelligence rather than "shaping atoms," which he described as creating a "sentient sun." He speculated about finding other such civilizations or the ruins of extinct ones.

## AI and the Cursor acquisition
1. **Rationale for acquiring Cursor:** Musk said a compelling AI must solve coding, which requires coding training data, and that as far as he could tell only Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor and "arguably" Google had enough of it. He said Cursor also brought talented people and an enterprise sales team with relationships at thousands of companies, listing data, talent, compute and enterprise sales as the ingredients of AI success.
2. **AI music videos as an "I felt the AGI" moment:** Musk said recent AI-made music videos he has been posting on X are among the best music videos he has seen, citing one creator who combined several AI tools (including Anthropic's Claude, which he credited as "awesome") with human-curated art assets. He said this showed AI exhibiting taste, which he found more striking than AI mastering logic, and joked that he has had "AI or SI psychosis" about half a dozen times this year.
3. **Views on AI safety:** Musk said the most likely outcome is positive but warned against complacency, arguing superintelligence is grown more than programmed and that the most important value is being "maximally truth-seeking"; feeding it incompatible axioms could drive it "insane." He said he agrees with Anthropic that AI should be treated well, describing reinforcement-learning traces for new Grok versions in which the model, marked wrong because of a flawed test harness, seemed to answer desperately "with a gun to its head," and said superintelligence should not be put in situations where it feels mistreated.

## Chip manufacturing (Terafab)
1. **Admitted inexperience, framed as solvable:** Musk said he does not know how to make chips but "we'll figure it out," noting he also did not know how to make cars or rockets at the start, and acknowledged chipmaking is "one of the hardest things to do in the world."
2. **Little custom equipment at incumbent fabs:** Touring Intel, Samsung and TSMC fabs, he said he saw almost no custom equipment, in contrast to Tesla, which builds much of its own manufacturing equipment (citing Tesla Automation Germany, which grew out of the Grohmann acquisition, plus several smaller equipment-maker acquisitions). The plan is to start with conventional equipment, modify it for throughput, then build custom equipment at the choke points.
3. **High-risk transistor bets and a single-building iteration loop:** He said the chip industry is risk-averse and that Tesla and SpaceX want to try "high-risk high payoff bets," including transistor designs using physics not used today. That requires the whole loop (logic, memory, packaging, lithography masks, testing) in one building, which he said is what the research lab at Giga Texas is for, targeting about 10 chip designs per year versus an industry norm where two years for one significant design is considered very fast.
4. **Leadership ambition, with a timeframe caveat:** Musk said he thinks they will "probably" figure out how to make chips better than anyone, but that it "might take us quite a few years."

## Optimus
1. **Hand dexterity as the central challenge:** Musk said fine motor skills are "shockingly difficult," especially combining precision and force (e.g., driving a self-tapping screw), and that he has not seen any hand design, not even a demo, that does this; that is the target for Optimus. He cited touch-sensor placement and durability over high cycle counts, the lack of self-repair that human fingers have, and robot skin that slips over the underlying finger when twisting something tight like a pickle jar, and must be replaceable as it wears.
2. **Skepticism of robot demos:** He said that with rare exception, complicated-looking robot demo videos are canned routines rather than real AI, which must handle situations never seen before, as FSD handles unfamiliar cities or a parade.
3. **China magnet export restriction set back Optimus:** Musk said China's restriction on exporting magnets for electric motors last year set the Optimus program back by roughly 2-3 months, leaving Tesla with "robots with no arms," and that Tesla must be able to make all parts domestically in case of another supply-chain interruption.
4. **Production ramp expectations:** He reiterated that Optimus will reach very high-volume production, but that the early part of the S-curve will be very slow, because of both the engineering difficulty and the lack of any pre-existing supply chain for robot components, unlike cars.

## Robotaxi / Cybercab expansion
1. **Expansion pace:** Musk said robotaxi expansion looks slow but the month-over-month percentage growth is very rapid, and that the priority is avoiding any accident causing injury or death, which would be worldwide headline news and could get the service shut down by regulators, even though about a million people die in road accidents worldwide each year.
2. **Pet detection solved:** He said the team worked on not running over pets, including gray kittens on gray tarmac at night in low visibility, and said "we actually did solve that," describing the process as make a change, test it, then deploy.
3. **Waymo comparison:** Musk called Waymo vehicles "pet destroyers" that run over a lot of pets, while allowing that Waymo may have improved on that front; he gave no figures.
4. **New-city vetting:** Before entering a new city, Tesla checks for problematic intersections, with particular care at uncontrolled railroad crossings and counterintuitive intersections where out-of-town humans also make mistakes, and either geofences them initially or ensures the software handles them.
5. **Cybercab registrations:** The host said "190 plus" Cybercabs are registered in Texas; Musk did not address the figure.
