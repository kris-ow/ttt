Elon Musk sat down with The Economist at Tesla's Giga Texas facility for a wide-ranging interview covering AI timelines, robotics, geopolitics, taxation, and — in a lengthy, combative back half — a sharp disagreement with the host over Musk's characterization of crime, immigration, and politics in Europe. The most newsworthy claims: Musk said he expects AI to exceed the sum of all human intelligence in "roughly 5 years," predicted "money won't matter" by 2036, and reiterated a 10-to-20% estimate of catastrophic AI/robot risk while saying he has concluded there's no way to stop AI's momentum and that the best approach is to "look on the bright side."

## AI Timelines, Risk, and Coordination Among Labs
1. **Superintelligence timeline:** Musk said that within roughly 5 years, he expects AI to exceed the sum of human intelligence, and within 10 years (2036) there won't be anything AI can't do better than humans "apart from being human, perhaps."
2. **Vision of abundance:** He described the most likely 2036 outcome as "an age of amazing abundance" where "anyone can have anything they can think of," contingent on avoiding "a massive global thermonuclear war."
3. **Risk estimate unchanged but framing shifted:** Asked whether he still holds his earlier stated 10–20% estimate of AI/robots posing an existential risk, Musk said "I still think there's a risk associated with AI and robots. It's not zero," but said he's philosophically concluded that stopping the trend is not possible and perhaps shouldn't be attempted, since the most likely outcome is abundance.
4. **Proposal for inter-lab coordination:** Musk said he recommended to Demis Hassabis (after discussing Hassabis's proposal for a safety regulator) that leading AI companies hold a recurring call — "once every few weeks" — to discuss safety issues, and that competitors get roughly a week or two of early access to test new frontier models for harmful effects before release, comparing the structure to the Motion Picture Association's film-rating system. He said he intends to also raise this with Dario Amodei.
5. **Government's role:** Musk said government should retain power to act if a company is warned its model is dangerous and refuses to address it — citing the recent case where Amazon (not government) flagged cybersecurity risks in Anthropic's "Mythos" model to the White House, prompting export-control action.
6. **On rival labs and Anthropic's "Mythos"/Fable models:** Musk said Anthropic's Fable model is "still clearly the smartest model," but that China's Kimi K3 model (released "last week") is "getting quite close." He said Anthropic has had a more advanced model ready internally since around February and "could release it at any time."
7. **China's AI/electricity position:** Musk said China's AI progress is constrained mainly by chip access, not electricity, noting China "already" has more electricity than the US, Europe, and India combined, and predicted China will reach roughly four times US electricity production, "roughly proportionate to population." He said outside China, the binding constraint is electricity/power/cooling, not chips, and cited this as the rationale for AI data centers in space.
8. **Job displacement:** Musk said AI will soon do any digital/computer-based job better than any human, citing software engineering, where he said AI is "already" better than "at least 90%" of professional software engineers, heading toward "Stockfish level" — a reference to the chess engine so strong it would beat world champion Magnus Carlsen even on a phone. He said this generalizes to "everything," and that physical/robotic labor is a matter of time as humanoid robots gain end-effector capability under large AI models.
9. **Economic policy speculation:** Musk suggested "the Treasury should just simply issue people checks" and predicted deflation, not inflation, will be the future economic issue, reasoning that if goods/services output grows faster than money supply, prices fall. He also said "work is going to be optional" and likened future labor to gardening — done for pleasure, not necessity — and cited Ian Banks's Culture novels as his reference point for an AI-abundant future.

## Corporate Control, Taxes, and Succession
1. **Personal tax figures:** Musk said he pays about 40% national income tax on stock options plus roughly a third of California's state tax rate (which he estimated around 15%), totaling "about 45%," and that at death, another roughly 45% will be taxed, leaving him with "a quarter" of his current holdings.
2. **Rationale for SpaceX's IPO:** Musk said one reason he took SpaceX public was to let the public own a piece of it, since private companies are capped at a few thousand shareholders while public companies can have millions.
3. **Why he wants voting control:** Musk defended his concentrated voting control (he said he holds roughly 80% of voting shares) as necessary to pursue 5-to-10-year (or longer) horizon projects — citing SpaceX's Moon/Mars ambitions — without quarterly-earnings pressure from portfolio managers whose compensation depends on short-term performance. He compared this to Larry Page/Sergey Brin's control of Alphabet and Mark Zuckerberg's control of Meta.
4. **Key-man risk:** Asked what happens if he "gets run over by the proverbial bus," Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would perform well for "several years" given existing roadmaps, though comparing the situation to Apple after Steve Jobs — solid execution, but missing "the incredible breakthrough products."

## Space, Mars, and Long-Term Vision
1. **Starship launch cadence goal:** Musk said Starship, which he called "the largest, most powerful flying object ever made," is intended to launch "more than once per hour" (no date given for reaching this cadence).
2. **Mars/consciousness framing:** Musk said his guiding interest is "the set of actions that propagate consciousness into the future to the maximum degree possible," describing a hoped-for spacefaring civilization "among the stars," and cited Star Wars — the first movie he saw in a theater at age 6 — as a formative influence.
3. **AI progressing as predicted:** Musk said AI development is "pretty much happening as I thought it would," crediting Ray Kurzweil's predictions as "remarkably accurate."

## Starlink and Ukraine
1. **Starlink's role in Ukraine:** Musk noted the Ukrainian government has "publicly thanked Starlink many times" for its role in the country's defense.
2. **Whitelisting Starlink terminals:** Musk explained that Russian forces obtained Starlink units smuggled through Ukraine into Russian-occupied eastern Ukraine and used them in some cases for attacks; in response, Starlink worked with the Ukrainian government to create a whitelist of approved terminals, which he acknowledged also cut off some innocent users needing internet for work or education.
3. **Position on ending the war:** Musk said he hopes for a peace agreement soon, arguing the front line has "barely moved" for "two or three years" while people die daily, and that expecting full Russian withdrawal is unrealistic. He said he has "been advocating peace for a long time," including a past post suggesting concessions to Russia, framing this as pragmatism rather than pro-Russia sentiment.

## DOGE, US Politics, and Foreign Aid Cuts
1. **Self-assessment on political involvement:** Musk said, "I think I got a little too involved in politics, got carried away, frankly," regarding his DOGE-era work, but stood by the underlying goal of addressing the US deficit, noting that interest payments on the debt exceed total spending on the Department of War and intelligence combined.
2. **Denial that aid cuts caused deaths:** When the host pressed him on reports that abrupt USAID-related cuts caused deaths (e.g., from halted antiretroviral funding), Musk repeatedly said "Zero" people died as a result, arguing large foundations (he named the Gates Foundation and, referencing Bezos, MacKenzie Scott's philanthropy) had the capacity to fill funding gaps and calling contrary claims "false," "nonsense," and politically motivated fabrications by affected organizations.
3. **Support for the Trump administration:** Musk said the current administration is "on balance... excellent" and "vastly better than the alternative," while noting no administration is perfect and he won't agree with everything it does.

## Europe, Immigration, and Free Speech (contested exchange)
1. **"Civil war in Britain" claim reaffirmed:** Musk reaffirmed his prior statement that "civil war in Britain is inevitable... if current trends continue," attributing this to growth of a population he characterized as holding views "antithetical to Western beliefs," and estimated this civil war is "probably 20 years away."
2. **Rejects "far right" label:** Musk objected strongly to being characterized as supporting the far right, saying he supports "normal people" and citing three principles — secure borders, safe cities, sensible spending — that he says used to be mainstream positions.
3. **Disputed claims about crime and safety:** The host disputed Musk's portrayal of the UK/Europe as unsafe, citing that "violent crime in the UK is not where you think it is... it's coming down," and that London is safer than any US city; Musk maintained his position ("I think I am [right]"), and both agreed to seek fact-checking and proposed Musk take a tour of Britain with the host.
4. **On immigration generally:** Musk clarified he is "in favor of immigration" if the immigrant will be "a productive member of society," honest, and law-abiding, and said he supports controlled rather than open borders; he acknowledged being an immigrant himself.
5. **Defense against racism/anti-Muslim accusations:** Musk cited his children with a half-Indian partner and the racial diversity of senior executives at his companies as evidence against racism accusations, while saying he opposes immigration by people with views he considers "antithetical" to Western values.
6. **On being "loathed":** Musk said he doesn't care that some people loathe him, argued more people like him than dislike him given his following (he cited a figure around a quarter billion X followers), and claimed journalists and media are more widely despised than he is, citing an approximate 15% favorable view of journalists in the US.
7. **Self-assessed predictive track record:** Musk said, "I'm very good at predicting the future... my batting average is very high for a human," acknowledging he isn't always right but claiming a "Cassandra effect" where people disbelieve his accurate predictions.
