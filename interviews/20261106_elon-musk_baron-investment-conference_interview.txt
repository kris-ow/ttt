Elon Musk appeared in a fireside chat with Ron Baron at the 33rd Annual Baron Investment Conference in New York, covering the planned Terafab chip facility, SpaceX/Starlink's data-center and revenue trajectory, Starship's reusability program, space-based AI compute, humanoid robot economics, and broader predictions about AI's effect on the global economy. The most newsworthy material: Musk's stated expectation that SpaceX's annualized revenue run rate will exceed $100 billion this December and could exceed $500 billion by December of next year, with a small but rising probability of reaching $1 trillion, alongside plans to attempt the first tower catch of a Starship ship, as opposed to a booster (Ship 42), "in about a week" from the interview.

## Terafab and US Chip Manufacturing
1. **Scale of the plant:** Musk said the planned production Terafab, located in Grimes County, Texas near Texas A&M, aims for a terawatt a year of chip production — roughly 30 times his estimate of next year's total global AI chip output (which he put at 35–45 gigawatts versus this year's 20–25 gigawatts). He said the project would cost "a trillion dollars" but would be built in phases without needing that capital immediately.
2. **US chip capacity is small relative to Asia:** Musk said TSMC is building fabs in Arizona (some operational) and Samsung has operational fabs in Texas, including one built for Tesla dedicated to its AI5 and AI6 chip designs, but that even the best-case total of announced US fabs is roughly 10% of Asia's chip production capacity, which he called a geopolitical risk.
3. **Research fab in Austin:** A separate research chip fab is being built at Giga Texas in Austin to experiment with new fabrication approaches — including attempting logic, memory, packaging, and lithography under one roof — before transferring successful techniques to the production Terafab. Musk said a process that normally takes a year might be compressed to a month.
4. **Efficiency opportunity:** Musk said wafers in conventional chip fabs spend more than half their time being transported or waiting rather than being processed, and estimated only around 20% of the time is "useful" atomic-level work — an inefficiency he believes Tesla can improve on.

## Data Centers, Compute, and SpaceX/Starlink Revenue
5. **Vertical integration advantage (Baron's figures):** Baron, not Musk, laid out the data-center economics: by his account SpaceX builds a data center in 8–9 months (up from roughly 4 months previously) for about $35 billion per gigawatt, versus roughly 2.5 years and $50–60 billion for the industry, because it builds for its own compute needs (cars, robots, rockets) rather than needing signed leases first; he added that SpaceX would rent capacity out until it needs it for Grok, at roughly $50 billion a year. Musk did not restate these figures; he answered with the revenue growth numbers below.
6. **Revenue growth figures:** Musk said SpaceX's Q2-to-Q3 revenue growth this year was over 80%, and he estimated Q3-to-Q4 growth at roughly 70%, with a similar rate expected Q4-to-Q1. He explicitly distinguished his $500 billion December-next-year ARR figure as "my best guess," not a promise, and said a $1 trillion ARR, while still unlikely (he estimated roughly 2%), is "no longer out of the question."
7. **"Jose meme" on labor bottlenecks:** Musk argued that data center buildout is bottlenecked by the number of people doing actual physical work, not by purchase orders, illustrating with a meme of one worker digging a hole watched by eight others; he said SpaceX and Tesla are among the few companies that do the actual physical work themselves.
8. **Community impact in Memphis:** Musk said SpaceX's Colossus data center site has made the local Memphis economy boom, with full restaurants and hotels and wages at times double the prevailing local rate, and said the company has supported local schools; he suggested opposition to data centers tends to come from areas that don't yet have them.
9. **Space-based data centers:** Musk said a satellite carrying an Nvidia GPU and another with a Google TPU are currently operating in orbit. SpaceX expects to begin launching its first "Starmind" computers — co-designed with Nvidia — in Q3 of next year, scaling to "significant volume" in 2028 and "very large volume" in 2029. He said reaching terawatt-scale or even 200-gigawatt-scale AI compute isn't feasible on the ground given that total US average power consumption is just over 500 gigawatts.
10. **Starlink capacity and agentic traffic:** Musk cited Cloudflare ("if I read that correctly") as reporting that agentic AI internet traffic already exceeds human traffic and a Cloudflare prediction that within five years agentic AI traffic will be 1,000 times human traffic, which he said may be an underestimate. SpaceX currently has 11,000 satellites in orbit (mostly version 2) and has begun launching version 3 satellites, which Musk said have more than 10 times the capability of version 2, with roughly 10 times as many to be launched. He said next-generation laser inter-satellite links will run at 10 terabits/second, versus roughly 100 gigabits/second currently — a 100x improvement.
11. **Hypothetical space-compute revenue:** Musk offered an illustrative calculation that 100 gigawatts a year of AI compute in space, even valued conservatively at $10 billion per gigawatt, would represent a trillion dollars of cumulative annual revenue stacking each year; at $50 billion per gigawatt, he said that would be $5 trillion.

## Starship and Launch Costs
12. **Reusability and cost to orbit:** Musk said Falcon 9 achieves roughly 80% reusability (booster and fairings recovered, but not the upper stage, whose cost he compared to a medium-sized jet). He said Starship, designed for full and rapid reusability via tower-arm ("chopsticks") catches of both booster and ship, could in principle allow the Super Heavy booster to be reflown as often as once per hour. He estimated the cost per kilogram to orbit could fall below $100, and that within "probably two or three years" the cost per ton to orbit could be less than air freight from California to Australia.
13. **Heat shield challenge:** Musk called Starship's reusable orbital heat shield one of the hardest technical problems humans have attempted, noting the Space Shuttle's heat shield required nine months of repair between flights and its main tank was never reused. He said SpaceX has been working on this for roughly a decade and has achieved multiple precise simulated-tower soft landings of the ship at sea.
14. **Upcoming tower catch attempt:** Musk said SpaceX plans to attempt catching Ship 42 with the tower arms "in about a week from now" (relative to the Nov. 6, 2026 interview date).

## AI's Economic Impact and Robotics
15. **Digital AI economic uplift estimate:** Musk estimated digital AI alone (excluding robotics) could increase the global economy by 20–30%, which he put at roughly $25 trillion (Baron cited a $133 trillion global economy), and said it could roughly double white-collar productivity.
16. **Humanoid robot productivity and fleet-size estimate:** Musk said a humanoid robot working 24/7 without breaks could be a minimum of five times as productive as a human worker. Using an estimate of roughly 120 million "net positive output" workers in the US, he estimated the US workforce's output could be matched by roughly 20 to 30 million robots.
17. **Robot cost:** Musk said a humanoid robot would initially cost about the same to build as a car, with costs declining over time; Musk agreed when Baron cited a $30,000 cost per robot falling toward $20,000. Asked whether the robots would be rented or sold, Musk said they would likely be rented, though he characterized this as still undecided ("I think... probably rent them").
18. **Superintelligence saturating demand:** Musk said the demand for digital superintelligence will ultimately come from superintelligence itself, enabling "universal high income" and medical care "better than the best medical care on Earth today," and compared today's monetary system to historical use of gold and silver coins, predicting it will seem similarly archaic to a future superintelligence.

## China Competition
19. **Power and chip gap with China:** Musk estimated China's electricity output is roughly three times that of the US currently, trending toward four times, and said industrial capacity is roughly proportionate to electricity output. When Baron said China is behind on chips, Musk added "for now." He did not confirm or provide a figure for China's annual power capacity additions when Baron cited 300 gigawatts/year, saying "I'm not sure if that's a steady-state power number... I don't think it's quite that high, it's a little lower than that."
20. **Space as competitive edge:** Musk said the US could remain economically competitive with China by moving AI compute to space, where he believes hundreds of gigawatts or even a terawatt per year could be deployed, exceeding what he implied China could match — though he did not state a specific figure for China's space-compute capacity.

## OpenAI and Business Philosophy
21. **OpenAI's founding and departure:** Musk said he founded OpenAI as an open-source nonprofit and that it is now "a closed-source, maximum-profit entity," calling this "literally the opposite" of his original intent. He said he was offered an ownership interest when he left and declined, saying he was confused how a commercial interest could exist in a nonprofit, and noted he provided "almost all the capital in the beginning," named the company, and recruited key people.
22. **China market entry:** Musk said Chinese officials initially insisted Tesla take a local joint-venture partner (Baron cited a normal 49% foreign ownership cap), but after years of negotiation China changed the law to allow Tesla 100% ownership of its factory there.
