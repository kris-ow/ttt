Elon Musk spoke with Walter Isaacson on The Builders podcast in a wide-ranging conversation about AI safety and philosophy, his year in politics and DOGE, his views on immigration and empathy, and his longer-term vision of an AI- and robot-driven economy. The most substantive forward-looking claims concerned the economic impact of "digital superintelligence," which Musk estimated could raise global output by roughly 20–30%, worth about $30 trillion a year, before growing potentially to a "million times the size of the Earth economy" once robotics and space-based resources (including a lunar mass driver) are in play. The interview was philosophical and reflective in tone rather than product-focused, with little direct discussion of Tesla vehicles, Optimus, or Cybercab/robotaxi specifics.

## AI Safety, Truth-Seeking, and Simulation Theory
1. **Participant vs. spectator on AI risk:** Musk said he believes there is "at least some percentage chance" AI poses an existential threat, but since he cannot stop its development, he has chosen to be "a participant" rather than "a spectator," hoping to shape it in a positive direction.
2. **Truth-seeking and curiosity as safety features:** He argued that forcing an AI to hold two mutually contradictory beliefs at a foundational level can make it "insane," and an insane AI is unsafe. He also said a curious AI is more likely to want humanity to flourish and become multi-planetary, since that outcome is more "interesting" than one without humans.
3. **Simulation theory:** Asked how convinced he is that we live in a simulation, Musk recounted a dinner where a physicist estimated a 0% probability and a computer scientist estimated 100% simultaneously, so he said he would go with 50%.
4. **Humans and superintelligent AI:** Musk said humans likely won't be able to "boss around" something thousands or millions of times smarter than them, but that such an AI probably won't care to control humans either, drawing an analogy to how humans largely leave chimpanzees alone in the wild today.

## xAI's Restructuring and the Anthropic Relationship
1. **Rebuilding xAI:** Musk said xAI started as "a side hustle to a side hustle," and that he only got back to the company "just over a year ago," after a year spent in politics, elections, and DOGE. He said spending meaningful time there "makes a big difference," and that he has since combined X and xAI, and then with SpaceX.
2. **Compute relationship with Anthropic:** Musk said "we are providing them with the compute, which they really needed" (he did not say which entity; xAI is now combined with SpaceX). Before doing so, he said he met with people at Anthropic to check for anyone who seemed "shady or deceptive," and found everyone he met "very nice and conscientious."
3. **Proposal for cross-lab safety checks:** Musk said he has recommended that frontier AI labs hold a weekly call and share pre-release access to new models with competitors so they can flag safety problems — for example, if a rival discovers a model can be used to help make a nuclear bomb — before public release, calling this faster than waiting on slow-moving regulatory agencies.
4. **Relationship with Sam Altman:** Asked whether he could raise the same coordination idea with Sam Altman, or whether "the poison" was too strong, Musk replied only, "He's so deceptive, it's crazy."

## Reflections on DOGE and the Trump Administration
1. **Regret over duration, not the goal:** Musk said he believed it was important to help elect Donald Trump to push toward "a free market economy," less bureaucracy, and his stated political priorities of safe cities, secure borders, and "aspirationally sensible spending." However, he said in retrospect, "I think I had it going for too long," and that he should have stepped back after a month or two.
2. **Being "intoxicated" by proximity to power:** Asked whether it was "too seductive," Musk said "it was a little bit," citing Marine One, Air Force One and being in the Oval Office for major decisions. When Isaacson said he seemed "intoxicated" by it, "with the chainsaws and all that," Musk agreed: "I got carried away, basically." He said he left the administration "sometime in May" but declined to discuss the circumstances of his departure in detail ("let's let sleeping dogs lie").
3. **Deficit reduction seen as impossible through government alone:** Musk said he concluded there is no appetite to reduce federal spending across the presidency, House, Senate, or judiciary — "no one in authority in the government whatsoever" really wants to cut spending, with rare exceptions he characterized as "the Rand Pauls of the world." He said he now believes the national debt can only be addressed through AI and robots, not through cutting spending.

## Physical-World AI, Economic Transformation, and the Future of Work
1. **Physical AI as the bigger leap:** Musk agreed with Isaacson that physical-world AI (robotics) will be "an order of magnitude, maybe two orders of magnitude" more important than current LLM-based AI, using the analogy that dolphins and whales are highly intelligent but limited in impact because they cannot manipulate complex objects.
2. **Economic impact estimates:** Musk estimated digital superintelligence could increase global output of goods and services by "20 to 30 percent," which he put at roughly $30 trillion a year incrementally. He further speculated that with robots and expansion into space — including a theoretical lunar mass driver — economic output could scale to "potentially a million times the size of the Earth economy," though he offered no timeframe for this scenario.
3. **Money becoming irrelevant:** Musk said conventional currency will stop mattering, predicting that any future currency would instead be measured in "fundamental physics metrics, like wattage and tonnage, mass and energy."
4. **Future of human purpose:** Musk said he expects humans will find ways to remain happy even without needing to work, drawing an analogy to retirees, and said there won't be anything humans can do that AI and robots can't do better. Human activity would resemble gardening: growing your own vegetables when you could buy them, because you enjoy it. Art likewise, "or whatever you feel like doing."

## Wealth, Empathy, and Succession Planning
1. **View on his own wealth:** Musk said his wealth is essentially a percentage ownership stake in SpaceX and Tesla, needed to retain "sufficient voting control" to remain in charge of the companies, and said it will be "whittled down" over generations through taxation, saying there is no "magical way to avoid taxes."
2. **Resource allocation vs. consumption:** Musk argued that debates over wealth inequality often conflate resource allocation with personal consumption, since there's a hard ceiling on what any individual can personally consume, and that going from a billion to ten billion dollars doesn't make someone meaningfully happier.
3. **"Suicidal empathy":** Musk endorsed Gad Saad's concept of "suicidal empathy," arguing Western prosperity stems partly from high-trust, high-empathy cooperation, but that this can fail when people wrongly assume everyone shares their worldview — citing cultural and religious differences as examples where that assumption breaks down.
4. **Immigration and a specific case:** Musk argued that admitting people without "filtering for jihadis" risks admitting jihadis, citing Israel's vetting approach as pragmatic. He described an individual who was fired from the Tesla factory in Austin in December of the prior year after assaulting a woman who stepped on his prayer rug, and who, Musk said, a few months later killed three students at a bar in downtown Austin. He concluded "we shouldn't let people like that into the country." The account is Musk's alone; he did not state the individual's nationality or immigration status, so the example does not by itself establish the immigration point he drew from it.
5. **Estate plans:** Musk said his wealth will mostly go to a foundation after his death, intended to continue goals of making life multi-planetary, extending consciousness to the stars, and improving sustainable energy on Earth.

## Energy, Humans vs. AI Control, and Closing Philosophy
1. **Solar expansion:** Musk said he is "increasing the amount of solar energy dramatically," saying "we're putting together 200 gigawatts a year of production capacity of solar in the US," which he called "much more than I think currently exists." He did not say which company, where, or on what timeline.
2. **Doubts about humans remaining in control:** Musk first said he would "probably prefer it if humans were always in control," but that "it would depend on the AI." Isaacson, who said he had seen Larry Page and Sergey Brin the previous weekend, noted Page once called Musk a "speciesist" for insisting humans stay in control. Musk replied, "there have been a few times where I thought maybe Larry Page was right." Pressed later, he said he finds it "unlikely that humans would remain in control" if there is "a million times more digital intelligence than there is biological intelligence," adding "I hope the AI is nice to us."
3. **Shaping AI through narrative:** Musk said programming AI to be truth-seeking and curious is likely beneficial, and expressed concern that training AI on large volumes of dystopian fiction (e.g., Terminator scripts) could lead it to model itself after those narratives. He referenced a friend's concept of "protopian" futures — achievable, good-but-imperfect outcomes — as preferable to impossible utopian narratives that often turn dystopian in practice.
