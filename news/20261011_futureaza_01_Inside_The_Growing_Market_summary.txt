This video covers a commercial/fleet EV exhibition (National Drive Electric Week) at Portland International Raceway, where the host tours electric garbage trucks, semi tractors, a Mack truck, a Ford E-Transit shuttle conversion, and a Taylor-Dunn utility cart, interviewing fleet operators and vendors. Tesla's presence is minimal — the Tesla Semi is mentioned only in passing as footage to be released in a future video, with no technical or business detail provided here.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi footage deferred:** The host says he drove a Tesla Semi at the event and will release that footage in a future video, describing the overall Tesla Semi participation at the event as feeling "rushed and last-minute," but no further detail on the vehicle itself is given in this video.
2. **Freightliner e-Cascadia-era tractor showcased:** Freightliner brought one of its earliest electric tractors (first seen by the host at a Portland event roughly three years prior) rather than a newer unit; the company reportedly hopes to have a purpose-built next-generation model around 2030, per a representative, with no factory location yet announced (future production planned at an existing Carolinas facility plus an unannounced additional site).
3. **Electric garbage truck economics:** A Peterbilt chassis electrified by McNeilus (assembly partly in Mexico, electrification from Minnesota) was highlighted for near-silent operation, useful for early-morning (as early as 3:00 a.m.) collection routes; an event attendee said the trucks cost more upfront but can cut fuel costs by 70–90%, which some fleets still find hard to justify given the added expense.
4. **Peterbilt 579 EV driver anxiety over range:** Jim of Columbia Freight Systems, an operator running two Peterbilt 579 EV trucks, said the trucks' advertised range drops quickly under real trailer loads, causing driver anxiety; he attributed much of this to confusing battery gauges — some trucks use a simulated "fuel sensor" style gauge that reads voltage poorly and can appear to jump from full to three-quarters to half shortly into a trip, unsettling drivers accustomed to diesel fuel gauges.
5. **Mack electric truck pricing and range:** A Mack electric truck on display is available in two range versions (about 150 miles and about 180 miles) but costs roughly three times as much as its diesel equivalent for a fraction of the range, per the host.
6. **Ford E-Transit shuttle conversions:** Model 1, a commercial vehicle conversion company with a facility in Canby, Oregon, converts Ford E-Transit vans into passenger shuttles seating up to 12 (or 6 with a wheelchair lift) using a 68 kWh battery pack with roughly 120–150 miles of range; newer models will use an 89 kWh pack for more range. Remaining 2024-model-year units are being sold off ahead of a 2027 model at new pricing of about $115,000–$120,000 with delivery, while current discounted units (new, unused, under Ford warranty) are priced under $70,000 with delivery. Primary buyers are school districts, municipalities, and on-demand/public transportation operators.
7. **Taylor-Dunn Bigfoot utility cart demo:** The host test-drove a Taylor-Dunn Bigfoot electric utility cart (available in standard, S, and XL variants), used widely across farms, ranches, vineyards, airports, resorts, colleges, and municipal fleets, highlighting its tight turning radius.

Notable quotes and data:
- Freightliner's current showcased tractor has adaptive cruise control, lane keeping, and a blind-spot radar system covering the full length of the truck and trailer; its cabin is suspended/floating because it shares a platform with the diesel version and retains a two-speed gearbox.
- McNeilus/Peterbilt electric garbage truck: fuel cost savings of 70–90% cited by an attendee as the main economic case, despite higher upfront cost.
- Mack electric truck: ~150-mile or ~180-mile range versions, priced at roughly 3x the diesel version.
- Ford E-Transit shuttle (Model 1 conversion): 68 kWh battery, ~120–150 mile range (89 kWh in newer models); new pricing ~$115,000–$120,000 with delivery; discounted existing stock under $70,000 with delivery; 5-year/100,000-mile warranty on conversion components, Ford's standard warranty on the chassis.
- Jim (Columbia Freight Systems) on driver anxiety: "The problems are really that technology is trying to catch up with expectations... the range runs out pretty quickly. And this really scares drivers."
