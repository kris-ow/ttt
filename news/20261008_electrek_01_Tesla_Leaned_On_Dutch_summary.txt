Electrek's Fred Lambert covers a Reuters investigation, based on emails obtained via public-records requests, alleging Tesla pressured the Dutch regulator RDW to scale back its review of "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" before RDW approved the system — an approval Tesla is now using to push for EU-wide adoption.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Tesla disputed RDW's review costs and scope:** Per Reuters' review of emails, in November 2024 a Tesla staffer told RDW that starting vehicle tests by month-end was "mission critical" and that leadership would "measure the goodwill" of the regulator based on meeting that date. In April 2025, Tesla called RDW's €365,000 invoice for five months of work an "extremely high cost," withheld payment pending an hour-by-hour staff accounting, and objected to paying for five RDW staffers to visit Giga Berlin. A week later, RDW invited Tesla to suggest how it "might adjust or reduce the required effort moving forward." The article also cites instances where Tesla pushed to substitute its own test reports for RDW's in-person witnessing (RDW offered partial remote monitoring), resisted a meeting on OTA updates (RDW said it didn't want to "dive too deep" and didn't "question the quality and process" of OTA updates), and requested advance notice of RDW's planned tests and safety-driver names, which RDW granted while citing "shared goals and objectives" with Tesla.
2. **RDW defends its process but withholds underlying data:** RDW told Reuters its manufacturer engagement "does not compromise the independence or thoroughness" of its assessment, but it redacted large portions of the emails and has not published its own FSD test data, citing Tesla trade secrets.
3. **Safety researchers dispute Tesla's "evidence dashboard" methodology:** Tesla shared an "evidence dashboard" with EU member states in April and posted a European safety study on X, calling it "one of the key pieces of evidence" behind Dutch approval. Seven traffic-safety researchers interviewed by Reuters said the FSD sample (in the tens or hundreds of thousands of miles) was too small to compare against hundreds of millions of miles of manual Tesla driving in Europe, that Tesla measured "surrogate" behaviors (honking, hard braking, hard cornering) rather than crashes, and that FSD miles came from Tesla's own professional test drivers, not regular owners. Tesla's own disclosures reportedly state the comparisons can't be read as "causal estimates," per the article, even as Tesla and Musk have publicly linked FSD approval delays to preventable road deaths (Musk said delaying French approval "will cost lives"; a former NHTSA statistician, Marco Benedetti, called such "lives saved" framing "nonsensical"). The article notes Tesla previously used a similar methodology in the US (comparing airbag-deployment crashes on FSD to all tow-away crashes) to claim FSD was 10x safer than human drivers, a figure later revised to 7x, and that this same US research was reportedly sent to European regulators.
4. **Owner lobbying campaign:** At last year's shareholder meeting, Musk asked European customers to pressure regulators, and a Tesla X account linked owners to RDW's contact form. Public-records requests show Tesla owners sent dozens of emails to Swedish and Norwegian regulators repeating safety claims. Norway's Stein-Helge Mundal reportedly warned Nordic counterparts that regulators would "need to use a lot of effort to answer misled consumers," telling Reuters: "We do not approve systems because they are popular, and we do not reject them because they are controversial."

## Market & Competition
5. **EU approval status and timeline:** Eight EU countries now allow FSD — Netherlands, Lithuania, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Slovenia, Czechia, and newly Croatia — but Germany, Italy and Spain have not signed on, and Sweden wants the EU to reject FSD unless Tesla removes the ability to set speeds above the posted limit. The EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles did not vote on October 6 despite Tesla pointing to that date; a bloc-wide vote (needing 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of EU population) is now expected no earlier than December. France's transport minister Philippe Tabarot, who said in July France couldn't back FSD, posted last month about a "constructive exchange" with Musk and working with Tesla on changes "that will enable France to support its approval." The article notes Tesla's European sales fell 28% last year and frames FSD as a rare differentiator against BYD and Volkswagen in Europe.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the reporting doesn't prove FSD is unsafe but shows Tesla treats the EU approval process as an obstacle to manage rather than an independent safety check, substituting proxy data and "lives saved" rhetoric for the disengagement-rate data it won't publish, while mobilizing its fan base against regulators who ask questions; he argues RDW's willingness to "reduce the required effort" after a billing dispute is inappropriate for a decision affecting 450 million people, and calls for Tesla to publish European customer data and let RDW release its test results.
