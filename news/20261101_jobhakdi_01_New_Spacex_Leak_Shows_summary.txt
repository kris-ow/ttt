## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Capex leak and Musk's rebuttal:** Two unnamed modelers project SpaceX is on track to spend roughly $470 billion in capex over the next five quarters (about 18 months) — roughly double what Amazon, Google, and Meta each spend — funded by fast build speed, with their model projecting SpaceX quarterly revenue reaching about $122 billion by Q4 2027 (up from about $14 billion in Q3) and roughly $150 billion in debt over the same period. In response, Elon Musk posted that "our capex efficiency is greatly underestimated," which the host reads as a direct rebuttal implying the $470B capex figure is overstated.
2. **Host's capital-efficiency framework:** The host argues that because SpaceX's data centers reportedly pay back their capex in about six months (versus roughly 18 months to build for competitors like Nebius and CoreWeave), the same amount of rolling debt can fund roughly double the gigawatt capacity over a given period compared to slower builders — and that after about 18 months SpaceX can begin a third build in parallel with no new debt, since profits from the first build start funding it. This is the host's own framework/argument, not a figure stated by Musk.
3. **Valuation and target framing:** The host argues SpaceX, at a market cap he states as $1.86 trillion, trades far below what its projected revenue run rate implies — citing a potential forward P/E in the single digits (around 7–7.5x) if revenue reaches roughly $150 billion with compute capacity scaling toward a stated target of 12 gigawatts by the end of next year. He calls SpaceX "the most short-term undervalued stock in the entire market" and reiterates conviction that the stock could roughly double in a short period, while acknowledging he was "punished" by Friday's selloff and that his portfolio took a hit from being heavily positioned in SpaceX.
4. **Tesla–SpaceX merger timing:** The host says a merger announcement (previously discussed as expected around December 3rd per earlier commentary) is "getting close" but stresses it's a process, not a fixed plan, dependent on a committee's determinations. He personally expects Tesla to receive a 30–40% valuation premium in any merger, and argues investors should think in absolute market-cap terms rather than fixating on a share-exchange ratio, since the ratio depends on where each company's valuation stands at the time.
5. **Orbital compute roadmap:** The host reiterates his own prediction that SpaceX will begin deploying compute satellites in significant numbers (describing "significant" as just a few megawatts) in Q4 2027, which he frames as a major future stock catalyst, and notes (agreeing with a viewer comment) that orbital compute is expected to be far better suited to inference than training.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Macrohard/Colossus-style buildout details:** Per Elon Musk's own comments relayed by the host, "Macrohard" and "Macrohard-er" facilities each have 220,000 Blackwell GB300 chips configured for training with 800-gig NICs; "Gun Range" will house the same chip configuration as "Spam," with Spam having 200,000 GB300s configured for inference (simpler networking, less than half the fiber). Musk said he walked the whole Colossus 2 site at midnight on Halloween and reported good progress, with Gun Range tracking to be operational by end of November and Spam in December.
2. **Scale and revenue extrapolation:** The host states the combined buildout (Macrohard, Macrohard-er, Mini-hard, Spam, Gun Range) is targeting roughly 1.2 million Blackwell 300 chips and about 3 gigawatts of compute capacity by the end of the year (roughly eight weeks from the video's publish date) — up from about 450 megawatts earlier in the year. Based on his own extrapolation that SpaceX was at a $26 billion annualized revenue run rate on roughly half a gigawatt, the host calculates that scaling to 3 gigawatts (6x the compute) could put SpaceX at roughly a $150 billion run rate by year-end, versus a market expectation he cites of about $100 billion — this is the host's own derived estimate, not a figure stated by Musk or the market.
3. **Anthropic compute commitment:** The host references an upcoming separate video noting that Anthropic's S-1 reportedly discloses a commitment of $125 billion to SpaceX compute through 2030, which he expects to increase over time.
