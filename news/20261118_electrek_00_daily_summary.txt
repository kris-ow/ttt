Electrek covered two Tesla stories today: the slow buildout of the public Megacharger network for the Semi, and a regulatory-driven tightening of FSD driver monitoring in Europe set against looser handling in the US. Fred Lambert wrote both and frames Tesla as behind on infrastructure and inconsistent on safety.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi Megacharger network at 7 sites:** Electrek reports Tesla opened its first Texas Semi Megacharger in Mesquite, near Dallas, with 8 stalls rated up to 1.2 MW each. Tesla's map now lists 7 public sites and 44 stalls across four states, up from four sites and 26 stalls in early October. Per Electrek, Tesla's Q4 2025 shareholder report showed about 37 sites planned for 2026, and Semi program lead Dan Priestley has said Tesla wants 46 by early 2027. Electrek calculates Tesla would need 30 more sites in 43 days and at the current pace would end the year with around 10; it notes Pilot said nine more of its sites are under construction, so a late burst is possible. Electrek adds that Tesla launched volume Semi production in September and says Chicago's first 50 Semis are en route. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/18/tesla-semi-megacharger-network-mesquite-texas/)

## Autonomous Driving
2. **Stricter FSD monitoring in Europe, looser in the US:** Electrek reports the Dutch regulator RDW approved an over-the-air update for Tesla Assisted Driving (FSD Supervised in Europe) on HW4 cars. It brings earlier attention warnings, a full strike for inattention during a manoeuvre, a hands-on-wheel requirement above 130 km/h and confirmation to set a maximum speed above the displayed limit. RDW says testing found no adverse effects. Ten other countries have adopted the Dutch approval, with Finland the latest; EU-wide approval awaits a TCMV vote. Electrek contrasts this with US moves it describes as loosening monitoring, including Musk's texting remarks, and notes NHTSA cited Musk's texting post in an engineering analysis. Lambert argues the update shows Tesla can build tighter monitoring when regulators demand it and calls the US approach hard to defend. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/18/tesla-fsd-driver-monitoring-europe-stricter-than-us/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's opinion: Tesla builds stricter safeguards only where regulators force it and relaxes them where it can, leaving US drivers with the liability. It also treats the Megacharger rollout as well short of Tesla's own target.
