## Robotaxi

- **Record Cybercab lot count at Giga Texas:** 102 Cybercabs were spotted at the outbound lot at Giga Texas — a new record — with 55 additional units observed in Dallas. Per Sawyer Merritt: "these things are really stacking up now." The combination of factory-lot and regional deployment signals represents the clearest on-the-ground evidence of a production ramp to date.

- **Cybercab EPA filing discloses full specs:** Official EPA documents reveal front-wheel drive, ~48 kWh battery, 219 hp, 163 kW motor, 326V system voltage, 3,113 lb curb weight (lightest in Tesla's lineup), GVWR of 3,730 lbs, and an equivalent all-electric range of 418 miles. The lightweight design likely drives the unusually high range figure; these are government-sourced numbers with high credibility for unit economics modeling.

- **Cybercab clears EPA certificate of conformity:** Tesla received an EPA certificate of conformity for the Cybercab, confirming federal emissions compliance for US market introduction. Per FutureAzA, Tesla reportedly designed the Cybercab to meet FMVSS safety standards without requiring an NHTSA exemption or the 2,500-unit production cap. Texas state AV operator permits are said to already be in place.

- **Fleet contracting, not growing, per Electrek:** Electrek's Fred Lambert reports Texas's DMV database (updated under a law effective May 28) shows Tesla holds 42 driverless operation authorizations in Texas versus Waymo's 577 and Avride's 317. The active fleet visible in the past 7 days on RobotaxiTracker.com stands at just 31 vehicles total. The unsupervised fleet peaked at 25 in late April 2026 and has since declined to 14 — well short of Musk's original Austin target of 500 by end of 2025, later revised to ~60. Musk now cites the FSD v15 software rewrite as the prerequisite for aggressive scaling, pushing any real ramp to late 2026 or 2027.

- **Lambert's editorial framing:** Lambert argues Tesla's clean NHTSA crash record (18 incidents since Austin launch vs. ~697 for Waymo) reflects minimal exposure rather than system superiority — Waymo runs ~3,000 vehicles and 500,000+ paid trips per week. He notes Tesla's 17 unredacted crash narratives are dominated by rear-endings and sideswipes by human drivers, consistent with Waymo's own pattern. The expanding geofence (now covering the full Austin metro) is characterized as a costless metric obscuring a shrinking operational fleet.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD v14.3.4 rolling out:** A new sub-version, v14.3.4, began rollout with the same release notes as its predecessor — confirming continued iteration cadence but no major capability leap.

- **Waymo acquires $220M Arizona proving ground:** Waymo purchased a 5,500-acre facility near Whitman, Arizona for $220 million, comprising a 115-acre city course, 35-acre vehicle dynamics area, four-mile oval, and dedicated freeway course. This expands Waymo's existing network (Castle, CA and Transportation Research Center, OH). FutureAzA's hosts interpret the investment as a signal that Waymo is not close to solving autonomy at scale.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Figure AI producing ~250 robots/month as of May 2026:** Scott Walter of Robo Strategy, citing a recent visit to Munro & Associates, reports Figure was producing approximately one robot every 30 minutes (~250/month) as of last month. Figure has locked the Figure 4 design and is pre-building "Bot Q" manufacturing infrastructure so the next-generation launch does not restart from zero. Walter highlights Figure's use of casting over CNC machining from aluminum billets — a key differentiator from Chinese manufacturers, whose CNC-based approach hits a production ceiling around 1,000 units and cannot scale an order of magnitude without switching methods.

- **Physical AI intelligence framed at GPT-3 equivalent today:** Andrew Kang of Robo Strategy estimates robots currently operate at roughly a GPT-3 level of physical intelligence — sometimes useful but unreliable. He argues the jump to GPT-5 equivalent for physical AI will compress to one to two years (vs. three-plus years for LLMs) because post-training techniques, RLHF analogs, and data infrastructure can transfer from the LLM world. Beyond that threshold, manufacturing capacity becomes the binding constraint.

- **"Language steering" and in-context learning emerging in 2026:** Kang identifies plain-language task instruction execution ("put that cup in the dishwasher") and single-demonstration in-context learning as early capabilities now coming online in frontier physical AI models.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO closes above $2.5T market cap:** SpaceX raised a record $85.7 billion in its IPO (638,888,888 Class A shares), with the stock up 19.6% on its second day of trading. Ron Baron purchased $1 billion of IPO shares; Baron Capital's total SpaceX holdings now stand at $25 billion. SpaceX stock was up approximately 29% since IPO as of June 16, trading around $160.

- **SpaceX acquires Cursor for $60 billion:** Per Jo Bhakdi, SpaceX completed the acquisition of Cursor — described as the leading agentic coding harness — for $60 billion, following a $10 billion call option Musk had previously purchased. Bhakdi argues this gives SpaceX a vertically integrated "holy trinity" in enterprise AI: harness (Cursor), frontier LLM (Grok), and compute (Colossus). SpaceX's own investor presentation cites a $22.7 trillion TAM in enterprise AI applications.

- **Chinese EV makers turning profitable; BYD market cap context:** Several Chinese EV manufacturers — NIO, Xpeng, BYD, Xiaomi, Li Auto — have recently reported profitability, though FutureAzA notes BYD and Xiaomi are conglomerates where EV-specific margins are difficult to isolate (Xiaomi's auto unit remains loss-making). Separately, Robo Strategy's Andrew Kang notes BYD's market cap is roughly 5% of Tesla's despite higher vehicle volumes — illustrating how margin compression from intense domestic competition suppresses valuations of Chinese manufacturers.

- **Robo Strategy (NASDAQ: BOT) launches with $2B equity facility:** Robo Strategy went public in May 2026 as a fund giving retail investors access to private robotics companies, charging 2.5% management fee with no carry. It announced a $2 billion at-the-market equity facility with Roth post-IPO. Portfolio highlights include Path Robotics (arc welding) and Standard Bots (first American-made industrial arms since Unimation in 1961). Figure AI — in which Kang invested $19 million at a $2.6 billion post-money valuation — is now valued at $39 billion.
