This May 4th livestream from the "futureaza" channel covers a broad sweep of Tesla and EV industry news, framed around the thesis that Tesla continues to outperform critics' expectations across multiple fronts. The host highlights Tesla's global EV sales dominance, the 10 billion FSD mile milestone, robotaxi expansion data, the Tesla Semi production line completion, and competitive dynamics with legacy automakers and Chinese EV brands.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model Y and Model 3 Lead Global EV Sales:** According to a "world for March report card" cited in the video, the Model Y ranked number one and Model 3 ranked number two globally among individual EV model sales. The host notes that BYD's strategy of producing many market-specific models dilutes individual model sales figures, and that a chart ranked by total revenue rather than units would be even more skewed toward Tesla. Chinese competitors such as the Xiaomi SU7 barely cracked the top 20, while its Model 3 competitor did not appear on the list at all.

2. **Model Y Leads California Q1 Sales:** The host highlights that the Model Y was the bestselling car in California for Q1 2026, competing directly against the Toyota Camry, Honda Civic, and Honda CR-V — a remarkable achievement given the price differential with those vehicles.

3. **Chinese-Made Model 3 Launched in Canada at Record Low Price:** Tesla began selling Chinese-manufactured Model 3s in Canada at C$39,940 (approximately US$29,000), described as the lowest price ever for the model in that market. The host explains that Canadian trade legislation reserved 50% of an import quota for vehicles with an import price under C$35,000, while allowing the retail price to be higher. Tesla leveraged this to sell Chinese-made units while also qualifying to sell an equal number at higher price points. The host frames this as Tesla taking advantage of a window before Chinese EV brands can establish their own Canadian infrastructure.

4. **380,000-Mile Model 3 Battery Durability:** The host highlights a real-world case of a Tesla Model 3 with 380,000 miles on its original battery, showing 34.2% degradation — down to 67% capacity, meaning range dropped from approximately 240 miles originally to approximately 158 miles. The host notes the vehicle was charged predominantly via DC fast charging/Supercharging given the daily mileage, and emphasizes that very few internal combustion vehicles survive to that mileage without major drivetrain replacements.

5. **Nissan Abandons US EV Plant Plans:** Nissan cancelled plans to spend approximately half a billion dollars building an all-electric vehicle plant in Mississippi, pivoting back to gas and hybrids. The host attributes this to an inability to make EVs financially viable at competitive price points, and notes that the elimination of both EV incentive carrots and regulatory sticks in the US removes manufacturer motivation to solve the problem.

6. **GM Introduces Cadillac Lyriq Hearse Variant:** GM announced a professional vehicle variant of the 2027 Cadillac Lyriq — a coach-built funeral hearse — which the host treats as darkly symbolic commentary on GM's EV strategy.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **10 Billion Cumulative FSD Miles Reached:** Tesla crossed 10 billion cumulative miles driven on Full Self-Driving, described by the host as a major milestone that had been treated as a "magic number" by the community. For context, the host notes that 7 billion miles were crossed on December 27th, 8 billion on February 18th (a 53-day gap), with the pace accelerating significantly to reach 10 billion. The host cautions that the milestone is a guideline rather than a hard threshold for any specific regulatory or capability unlock.

2. **FSD Version 14.3.2 Rollout:** During Q&A, the host references a comment stating that FSD version 14.3.2 is already deployed to over 40% of the eligible fleet and is expected to go wide soon. The host does not independently verify this but does not dispute it.

3. **Hardware 4 Update Activity:** A viewer comment noted that Hardware 4 received its first update since version 2026.2, while Hardware 3 vehicles had received three or four updates in the prior two weeks. The host acknowledged this without further commentary.

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Tracker Data Referenced Live:** The host displays RobotaxiTracker.com on screen during the stream, describing it as showing how many robotaxis are operating, miles driven, and real-time ride metrics. At the time of the stream, the tracker showed 91 active riders, an average fare of $5, and an average wait time of approximately 20 minutes (though the host notes it had been as low as 7 minutes earlier). The host frames this as evidence the robotaxi network is growing, describing it as "the wild west — except there are laws."

2. **California Enacts New Driverless Vehicle Law:** California passed new legislation targeting driverless vehicles including the Tesla Cybercab and robotaxi fleet, specifically prohibiting illegal U-turns. The host notes the law was partly prompted by a Waymo vehicle performing U-turns observed by a police officer. Unlike traditional traffic violations, the law does not impose per-incident fines on individuals but instead allows for accumulated violations to restrict future rollout or suspend a company's driverless vehicle license — a potentially significant financial risk for operators.

3. **Cybercab Revenue Potential vs. One-Time Vehicle Sales:** In a comparison of Tesla vs. Ford strategies, the host argues that a Cybercab generating $10,000–$30,000 per year in recurring revenue is a fundamentally superior business model to a one-time vehicle sale generating $5,000–$7,000 in profit. This is framed as the core reason Tesla's approach is strategically superior.

## Market & Competition

1. **BYD in Talks to Acquire Part of Volkswagen's Dresden Plant:** BYD is reportedly in discussions to take over a portion of Volkswagen's Dresden manufacturing facility, which would make BYD a domestic European manufacturer and exempt it from EV import tariffs. The host notes Germany offers a robust automotive supply chain and deep manufacturing expertise despite higher costs than Spain (which BYD had also considered).

2. **Tesla vs. Ford Strategy Comparison:** The host critiques a financial media comparison of Tesla's and Ford's paths to 2026 profitability. Tesla's approach centers on Cybercab, robotaxi, Optimus, and FSD subscriptions as recurring revenue streams, while Ford's focuses on the F-150, Super Duty, Transit, and "Ford Pro software." The host notes Tesla's R&D and capex at approximately $1.95 billion quarterly versus Ford's $9–10.5 billion annually, arguing Tesla generates disproportionate output for the spend. The host dismisses the comparison's identified "key vulnerabilities" for both companies as poorly researched.

3. **Lucid/Hertz Fleet Management Subsidiary:** Hertz announced a new fleet management subsidiary called "Oro Mobile Mobility," naming Uber as its first major partner in a push into autonomous robotaxi and ride-share operations. Hertz shares jumped 22% to a three-month high on the announcement. The host is skeptical, noting Uber lacks the infrastructure to clean or maintain autonomous fleets and has been indiscriminately partnering with any autonomous vehicle developer out of desperation.

4. **Tesla Capex Increase — Nvidia Beneficiary Near-Term:** Tesla announced a "very significant increase" in capital expenditures, with a focus on AI software, AI training, battery, powertrain, chip design, and general AI infrastructure. The host notes that in the near term, Nvidia is the primary beneficiary of this spending, but that Tesla is laying the groundwork to eventually manufacture its own chips. Ironically, despite Yahoo Finance framing this as bullish for Nvidia, Nvidia's stock declined following the announcement.

## Electric Vehicles (Tesla Semi)

1. **Tesla Semi Production Line Completed at Giga Nevada:** The Tesla Semi production line has been completed, though the host notes "completed" in Tesla's context means it will continue to evolve. The host distinguishes between the new production line and the adjacent pilot line building. A new customer ordered 40 Tesla Semis at the ACT demo event during the week of the video. The host frames this as the beginning of a meaningful production ramp.

2. **Tesla Unveils Base Charger for Semi Fleet Operators:** Tesla unveiled a new charger for the Tesla Semi called the "Base Charger," designed for overnight depot charging for fleets that do not operate 24/7. At 125 kW output, it can add up to 60% of range in 4 hours and fully charge overnight. The unit includes a 6-meter cable, supports 150 amps continuous output, includes an integrated AC/DC converter (no external cabinet needed), and up to three units can be daisy-chained. It uses the MCS (Megawatt Charging Standard) protocol. Deliveries are expected to begin in early 2027.
