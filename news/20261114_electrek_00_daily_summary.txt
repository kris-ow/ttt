Electrek's coverage today centers on Tesla opening Cybercab orders for personal use, with both articles digging into the mandatory subscription model and its implications for the Robotaxi thesis.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab orders open at $29,990 plus mandatory $299/month subscription, geofence-only:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's official Robotaxi account invited "select customers" to order a Cybercab for personal use at $29,990 (before taxes) with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit and estimated December 2026 delivery in Austin; a $299/month "Autonomy Subscription" is pre-checked on the order page and required for the car to operate, per the order page's fine print. The car only works within Tesla's Robotaxi service areas across eight cities in Texas and Florida. The article cites independent tracker Koby Pierce's testing finding actual coverage is only 59%-78% of Tesla's advertised geofences in six cities checked, with Austin at about 59%, and notes NHTSA has ordered Tesla to prove under oath that a brakeless car meets federal safety standards, a response Tesla submitted October 30 and asked to keep confidential, with no ruling yet. Lambert's take: Tesla is selling an unfinished product to fans, and questions why existing Model Y FSD owners in the same geofences can't get unsupervised access already if the tech works. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-cybercab-personal-use-orders-299-subscription-geofence/)

2. **Cybercab subscription agreement terms: Tesla can reprice, suspend, or geofence-shrink at will:** Fred Lambert details the posted "Cybercab Autonomy Subscription Agreement," which states the car "will not operate without an active Autonomy Subscription," lets Tesla change the $299/month price with one month's notice, restricts driving to Tesla-defined geofences Tesla can "expand, reduce, relocate, or otherwise modify... at any time," and allows suspension after three "strikeouts" for improper use (with no prorated refund). Tesla can remotely access, locate, restrict or control the vehicle; collects cabin camera "Autonomy Data" retained 30 days by default; and bars owners from using the car on ride-hail, delivery or rental platforms without authorization — killing the "earn money while you sleep" pitch Musk made for years. The article notes Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call comments from AI chief Ashok Elluswamy that Cybercab and Model Y robotaxis already run the same software, with customer-car network access possibly around Q2 2027, and quotes Mark Valorian describing the deal as "an endless variable-rate lease with a ridiculous down payment." Over five years with 59 paid subscription months, total cost reaches $47,631 before insurance, registration, charging and taxes. Lambert's take: the car is "for suckers," designed to extract a fresh $299/month revenue stream unencumbered by Tesla's unfulfilled decade-old FSD promises to existing owners. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/14/tesla-cybercab-autonomy-subscription-agreement-terms/)

## Editorial Stance
Both articles, written by Fred Lambert, are sharply critical, characterizing the Cybercab subscription model as exploitative and predicting disappointment for buyers, while contrasting Tesla's treatment of new Cybercab customers against long-paying Model Y FSD owners who still lack unsupervised access.
