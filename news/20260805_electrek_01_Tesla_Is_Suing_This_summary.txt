Tesla is suing Manitoba's provincial government in Canada's Court of King's Bench after being excluded from the province's EV rebate program, arguing the exclusion was unreasonable, untransparent, and politically motivated.

## Market & Competition
1. **Manitoba dropped Tesla from its EV rebate program:** Manitoba launched a C$25 million EV rebate program in summer 2024 (up to $4,000 off new EVs under $70,000, $2,500 off used EVs). In its March 2025 budget, the NDP government removed Tesla and Chinese-made vehicles from eligibility; Finance Minister Adrien Sala called it "Trump-proofing" and an "elbows up" response to U.S. tariffs, while Premier Wab Kinew tied it directly to Elon Musk's support for Trump, saying "we cut him off."

2. **Tesla was the program's biggest beneficiary before exclusion:** Court documents obtained via freedom-of-information requests show Tesla vehicles received 337 rebates worth roughly $1.3 million between August 2024 and February 2025 — over 20% of all EV rebates the province distributed. Tesla says the ban has cost its Canadian customers at least $560,000 in lost rebates since.

3. **Tesla's legal argument:** Filed for judicial review in June, Tesla argues it was singled out — no other automaker with a U.S. parent was excluded — despite being registered as a Canadian corporation in Nova Scotia and employing Canadians. Tesla says it received no formal explanation and learned of the decision through media reports. It is seeking reinstatement in the program and retroactive rebates for customers who bought Teslas after the cutoff. Manitoba is asking the court to dismiss the challenge.

4. **Broader pattern of Canadian rebate exclusions:** Tesla was earlier banned from Canada's federal EV rebate program in 2025 after filing what the program described as a suspicious number of last-minute claims as it wound down. British Columbia, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island also excluded Tesla from their rebate programs in 2025 citing similar trade-tension grounds.

5. **Ontario precedent:** In 2018, Tesla sued Ontario's government after being left out of the wind-down of its EV rebate program; a judge ruled the treatment "arbitrary and unlawful" and forced the province to pay eligible Tesla buyers. The article notes a Manitoba win could give Tesla a template to challenge the other provincial exclusions.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is sympathetic to Manitoba's move, framing it as a consequence of the Trump administration's trade actions against Canada and Elon Musk's political spending and influence; he argues companies and billionaires who influence politics bear responsibility for resulting political fallout, drawing an analogy to U.S. sanctions on Chinese companies over CCP ties.
