## Brief

- Texas Cybercab registrations hit a record 285 in one day
- FSD V15 now featured on Tesla.com homepage

- AI5 chips go to Optimus first, cars stay on AI4
- Starship upper stage caught by tower for first time

## Robotaxi
- Per Sawyer Merritt citing Texas DMV data, Tesla registered a record 285 Cybercabs for its Robotaxi fleet in a single day, lifting cumulative registrations to 1,107 and past 1,000 for the first time. Registrations signal vehicles being readied for the fleet, not vehicles in service.
- Tesla expanded its Dallas robotaxi service area, per Cern Basher and Phil Beisel.
- Musk said he hopes to have Starlink in all Tesla models, not just the Cybercab, by the end of 2027, excluding China, where Starlink is banned. Per Herbert, new Cybercabs in Dallas already have Starlink hardware built in.
- Jo Bhakdi argues the Cybercab ramp gives Tesla shareholders leverage in merger talks. He says a rollout plus a market turnaround could push TSLA to $500 by Christmas.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's U.S. website homepage now features FSD V15, per Sawyer Merritt.
- FSD v15 adds a generative "reality view" visualization that reconstructs the scene from the AI's perception output. Beisel says that in heavy rain and fog it appears to subtract the weather, and he argues it shows the vision stack perceives the world as well as or better than humans. He adds that it does not prove the car makes the right decisions, which only safety data can show.
- Musk shared a video of FSD v15 driving through a heavy downpour with a badly obscured windshield while the screen still tracked every surrounding car, writing "Tesla has superhuman vision."
- Per Basher, Musk said v15 does not need AI5 and can run on AI4 or 4.5. Beisel says v15 is a larger-parameter model, rumored to be 10x larger, which he could not confirm. He rules out the full v15 for HW3.
- The EU postponed its FSD vote by only a few days. Beisel says the vote does not need France and expects approval to be inevitable, though it may not land on December 15.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk said AI5 is going to Optimus and cars will remain at AI4, which he called enough to solve superhuman driving. Electrek, citing a Commercial Times report, says Global Unichip is giving its limited 2027 TSMC 3nm capacity to Google and Microsoft data center chips first, leaving Tesla a small allocation. Large-scale AI5 adoption in cars would come only after N3 capacity expands in 2028.
- Electrek says Tesla plans AI5 in Optimus around April 2027 and external Optimus deliveries in late 2027. Electrek argues the timing points to chip rationing rather than choice.
- A Tesla shelf company in Germany was renamed Tesla Machining Technologies Management SE. It is registered at the headquarters of EMAG, which builds production systems for humanoid robot gear parts. Tesla Germany's sales chief became managing director on November 9, and no deal has been announced.
- Beisel says Optimus shares FSD's universal vision encoder. He notes its planning problem is far harder, with roughly 100 control outputs versus about three for a car.

## Electric Vehicles
- Per Electrek, used Cybertruck prices are up 10 to 15% in the month since Tesla announced the end of production. CarGurus puts the average at $89,715, up 9.84% over 30 days, while Visor shows a like-for-like rise of 15% and a $101,784 average ask.
- Tesla now sells only the Cyberbeast, which was raised this week to $114,990. Electrek reads the spike as dealer price-testing and discontinuation hype, and notes the end of parts and service support could weigh on used values later.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- The FT reports that Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to negotiate an all-stock merger, per an X account cited by Jo Bhakdi. Directors are Roelof Botha and Randy Glein for SpaceX, and Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung for Tesla. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs advise SpaceX, and Evercore advises Tesla.
- The FT report cites a combination value of about $3.5 trillion and a possible year-end close. An insider says the sides are "not close yet," and regulatory scrutiny is expected over SpaceX's U.S. defense work and Tesla's China business.
- Approval requires more than 50% of all outstanding Tesla shares, with abstentions counting as no. A special meeting is expected after Tesla's December 3 annual meeting.
- Bhakdi's view is that the premium must fall between about 30% and 50%, which at a TSLA price below $420 means roughly $500 per share. He attributes SpaceX's weakness to the anticipated premium and puts its fair value at $170–180 versus about $130 now.
- On Wednesday (2026-11-25), SpaceX said it will give one newly issued Class A share to each active Trump account, with transfer in December. Musk will match it, with his shares transferring when his lockup expires in June 2027.
- Per Sawyer Merritt's calculations, the total is about 142 million shares, roughly 70 million each from SpaceX and Musk plus over 2 million from Gwynne Shotwell. That is about $18 billion at Wednesday's price, or roughly $250 per child.
- Another Sawyer Merritt post puts the cost at over $9 billion per year at the current share price and describes the gift as annual, which differs from the one-time gift in Herbert's account. Herbert estimates dilution at under 1%, and the issuance remains subject to third-party and government approvals.
- Herbert flags SPCX pressure from merger talks and the large lockup expiration on December 8.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- On November 25, the FCC under Brendan Carr approved up to 1 million SpaceX satellites for orbital computing, the first U.S. license for data centers in space. Milestones are 1 satellite by 2033, 100,000 by 2035, 500,000 by 2038 and 1 million by 2040, under a 15-year license.
- Other terms include orbits of 500 to 1,000 km, with laser links to Starlink as the primary communications path. Petitions from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society were denied.
- Per Beisel, SpaceX lists the AI1 satellite's compute payload at 175 kW average and 250 kW peak, so a million satellites at peak would equal 250 GW. Basher estimates the full constellation at roughly $3–4 trillion of investment.
- SpaceX unveiled a supercomputer plan for Cochise County, Arizona, which Musk called Colossus 3. It involves over $50 billion of investment, 1,000 permanent jobs and $3.6 billion in grid upgrades, and a public meeting is set for December 9. Beisel puts the campus at about 6 GW.
- Per the New York Times on November 23, Musk told SpaceX employees the company is behind in AI and that renting out compute would be more profitable. Musk says Anthropic wants another 10 GW from SpaceX.
- Per SemiAnalysis, Anthropic will drop about 50,000 older leased GPUs, which Herbert says is about 15% of the deal and leaves newer chips unaffected.
- Beisel believes Nvidia is SpaceX's chip of choice for its terrestrial build-out and for phase one of its orbital compute satellites.

## SpaceX — Starship
- The latest flight brought the Starship upper stage back to a tower catch for the first time, while the Super Heavy booster aborted its catch. Quincy Lee says the vehicle called it off because it was a little low on propellant.
- The catch arms slightly peeled ablative coating off the ship. Lee still calls the heat shield the main unresolved challenge for rapid, fully reusable flight, and thinks Musk is targeting a turnaround of 2 to 10 hours.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX is forgoing $738 million in federal BEAD broadband subsidies and instead will provide free equipment to rural households. The deal runs under a new NTIA agreement published November 23. It guarantees at least 100 Mbps down and 20 Mbps up, a low-cost plan and 10 years of service.
- Per Musk, Starlink is approved in 30 of Africa's 54 countries, and Viettel has started selling it in Vietnam. Per Herbert, it is on almost 67,000 ships, the US Starlink Mini roam plan is down to $99, and United has it on 729 aircraft.
- Lee says that with oversubscription each launch of 27 V3 satellites (about 1 Tbps each) could add roughly a million subscribers. That is Brian's estimate, supported by Lee's oversubscription math.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- Lee argues SpaceX has absorbed the industry's new launch capacity for Starlink, so outside customers effectively can't buy a Falcon anymore. He expects Starship to keep most of its capacity for SpaceX's own use.

## Market & Competition
- Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on November 23, 11 days after his resignation. Musk wrote "The error has been fixed" and confirmed he spoke with President Trump about Huang beforehand.
- Per Beisel, Musk visited Nvidia headquarters the day Huang returned. A community note called the photo fake, but Beisel says Huang's outfit matches his return day.
- Per Brian and Lee, Amazon Leo has about 397 satellites and lost 40 in the failed ULA mission. Lee says Leo needs roughly 1,000 satellites to avoid coverage gaps and others lack the launch volume to build a competitor at scale.
