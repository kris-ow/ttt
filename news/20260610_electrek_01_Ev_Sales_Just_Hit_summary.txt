U.S. EV sales hit their strongest month since federal tax credits were eliminated, with over 85,000 EVs sold in May 2026, even as average transaction prices continued to fall for the 11th consecutive month year-over-year.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EV sales rebound post-tax-credit elimination:** Early estimates show more than 85,000 EVs sold in May 2026 — the best month since federal EV tax credits were ended at the close of Q3 2025. Average transaction price for a new EV fell to $54,532, down 4% year-over-year. Automakers spent an average of 14% of ATP on incentives (~$7,600 per vehicle), nearly double the industry average and roughly flat from April.

## Market & Competition
2. **Tesla dominates U.S. EV market and drives prices lower:** Tesla accounts for roughly half of all U.S. EV sales. The average Tesla transaction price fell 1% from April and 3.4% year-over-year. 96% of Tesla's May sales came from the Model 3 (ATP: $49,082) and Model Y (ATP: $51,537). Because of Tesla's outsized market share, its pricing exerts disproportionate downward pressure on industry-wide EV averages.

## Editorial Stance
The author suggests that despite the loss of federal tax credits, demand is holding up due to price cuts, aggressive automaker incentives, and rising gas prices — framing May's results as a sign of resilience in EV adoption.
