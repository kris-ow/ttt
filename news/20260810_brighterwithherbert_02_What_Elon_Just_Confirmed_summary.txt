This video recaps a weekend of Elon Musk statements on SpaceX's revenue trajectory and Starlink's bandwidth/market-share potential, alongside fresh data on institutional Tesla buying and continued Cybercab/robotaxi fleet expansion across multiple US cities. A guest, described as a multi-decade supply chain executive and manufacturing quality consultant, provides risk-adjusted commentary throughout, generally endorsing the bullish trajectory while flagging execution risk in robotaxi safety incident rates.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Trillion-dollar revenue target pulled forward to 2030:** A SpaceX executive stated on a call that internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue (not ARR) have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029; the company also expects to reach over $100 billion ARR by December of this year. Musk confirmed on X that the trillion-dollar figure refers to actual revenue generated in the year 2030 itself, not cumulative revenue through that year. The guest cautioned that guidance this far out warrants a wide "guard band" given typical variance in multi-year corporate projections.
2. **SpaceX valuation context:** The guest noted SpaceX's current valuation is around $1.7 trillion, which he characterized as not unfair given the growth trajectory, while suggesting there is still room to run as execution continues.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Musk's bandwidth and revenue math for Starlink:** Musk said Starlink's V2 system, currently generating roughly $20 billion ARR this year, will be succeeded by V3 satellites (launched via Starship) offering roughly 100x the total system bandwidth versus V2 (driven by ~10x more satellites and ~10x more capacity per satellite). Musk stated that even if revenue per gigabit drops 10x, SpaceX communications revenue could still exceed $200 billion a year; he called this non-speculative. Sawyer Merritt and Aaron Bernett (unaffiliated commentators) provided supporting math on satellite counts and bandwidth multiples that Musk endorsed.
2. **Market share and long-term revenue scenarios:** Musk said that even if the overall communications market merely doubles, he'd expect Starlink to reach at least 25% market share outside China (potentially China too), implying over half a trillion dollars in annual revenue; he added it's "not out of the question" Starlink could carry over 50% of global internet traffic long-term, implying over a trillion dollars in annual revenue — both explicitly framed by Musk as longer-term/speculative scenarios.
3. **Separate analyst estimate on Starlink standalone value:** An All-In podcast host estimated Starlink generated $4.3 billion in quarterly revenue and $2.6 billion in adjusted EBITDA, with 12 million subscribers (doubled year-over-year) and ~$66/month ARPU, growing 20% quarter-over-quarter; he projected this could scale to roughly $40 billion in annual revenue and $30 billion in free cash flow, potentially making Starlink alone worth a trillion dollars in market cap within about 18 months to two years if a 30x multiple is applied — his own estimate, not confirmed by Musk.
4. **T-Mobile CEO downplays Starlink threat; Musk responds:** T-Mobile's CEO said satellite competition from Starlink has been "exaggerated" and satellite tech will remain complementary to existing networks; Musk responded "Laughing 100%." The guest offered a mixed view, saying Starlink has likely built a technically superior system but the timeline and extent of disruption to incumbent carriers remains uncertain.
5. **Flush-mounted Starlink dish spotted on Cybercab:** Spotters identified what appears to be a new flush-mounted, gold-covered Starlink dish integration on a staged Cybercab, an upgrade from previously seen exposed dishes; Musk said all cars will eventually have Starlink connectivity, calling it the only way to deliver high bandwidth to billions of vehicles.

## Financials
1. **Institutional buying wave in Q2 (per 13F filings as of June 30):** BlackRock added roughly 6,000 shares for about $2.5 billion, pushing its total stake to 214 million shares (~$90 billion); Deutsche Bank added about 1.48 million shares (~$622 million inflow), bringing its holding to 9.6 million shares (~$4 billion); Société Générale's US arm bought about $4 billion of stock, raising its Tesla weighting from 1% to 3% of its portfolio and moving its position to roughly 11.2 million shares (~$4.7 billion); State Street bought 2.76 million more shares (~$1.16 billion), becoming the third-largest institutional holder with about 117 million shares. Institutions now reportedly own about 55% of Tesla stock versus roughly 20% for retail. The guest noted this data reflects positioning as of June 30, before the stock's subsequent late-July decline, and it's unclear whether institutions have since added or reduced further.

## Robotaxi
1. **Austin fleet approaching 100 cars, mostly unsupervised:** Active Robotaxi fleet: 839 (RobotaxiTracker.com, as of 2026-08-07). For Austin specifically, a tracker cited in the video put the local fleet near 98 cars with 85 unsupervised (~87% of the local fleet), alongside an estimate of nearly 500,000 unsupervised miles — both figures described by the host as uncertain in accuracy. A frequent on-the-ground observer said weekend demand and wait times were up due to higher ridership, not reduced service, with several newly discovered unsupervised license plates found over the weekend.
2. **New robotaxi staging hubs identified — Fort Worth, Dallas, and more:** Community spotters found roughly 105 Cybercabs staged at a Dallas-area lot and confirmed a new Cybercab hub in Fort Worth (Giga Texas's fourth such hub), alongside staged vehicles reported in Las Vegas, Nashville, and San Diego. One commentator (Dillon Loomis) estimated Tesla may have around 2,000 Cybercabs stationed and testing across dozens of US cities, a figure the guest called reasonable but unverified.
3. **Steering wheels still present on staged test vehicles, described as temporary:** Photos from a San Antonio Tesla sales/service location and elsewhere showed staged Cybercabs still fitted with removable steering wheels, used for data collection to fit FSD models to the vehicle before wheels are pulled and cosmetic panels installed. The guest interpreted growing lot inventories (rather than shrinking or cars being returned to Giga Texas) as evidence of steady progress rather than a hardware or design setback.
4. **Guest raises safety incident-rate concerns ahead of scaling:** The guest argued that current robotaxi incident rates — citing anecdotal issues like vehicles looping to find parking or getting stuck — are not yet low enough to support the planned 100x fleet scale-up, since incidents scale proportionally with fleet size in an unsupervised system. He said Tesla needs to drive these incident rates down before comfortably scaling further, and noted Tesla has said its robotaxi FSD releases are ahead of the consumer FSD releases.
5. **Feature-set development on newer FSD version referenced from earnings call:** The guest referenced Tesla's July 22 earnings call comments that the newer FSD release (referred to generically due to unclear version number) had reached roughly 30–40% of its planned feature set at that time, with one unaffiliated poster (Gemma) estimating around 15% week-on-week mileage improvement — implying roughly a doubling of miles every five weeks. These figures were described as third-party estimates, not confirmed by Tesla.
