Herbert argues that Elon Musk campaigned to get Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO because both SpaceX's and Tesla's AI plans depend on Nvidia until Terafab reaches volume production, which Musk targets for the end of 2028. In the host's framing, Musk's plan is "Nvidia before Terafab": rent Nvidia chips now, resell compute to customers such as Anthropic to help fund that bridge, and switch to in-house chips later. SpaceX has the most exposure. Tesla's exposure is mainly its training compute.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **$44B Vera Rubin financing, the largest GPU financing to date:** On November 20, Apollo and Goldman Sachs priced about $44B of senior and subordinated notes. The notes fund a vehicle that buys next-generation Nvidia Vera Rubin chips and leases them to SpaceX for its ground computing network. SpaceX pays over five years, starting from delivery in the first half of 2027. SpaceX described the deal in October as roughly a $40B lease, and it was upsized on investor demand. The host estimates the cost at about $9B a year plus interest. The largest bond tranche reportedly yields about 8.26%, which the host cites as evidence that money is expensive even for strong AI borrowers.

2. **Nvidia exclusivity and the timing scare:** On August 4, the day of SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company, Musk said SpaceX would use only Nvidia GPUs. This covers the Memphis clusters, the Vera Rubin order and the planned orbital computer. Nvidia reportedly holds just over 120 million SpaceX shares. Huang resigned on November 12 and was reinstated on November 23. The financing priced on November 20, so for eight days Musk's largest chip order sat with a CEO-less supplier. Two days after the resignation, Musk said many of his deals were handshake agreements with Huang rather than written contracts, and that some critical deals could be lost without him. The host reads this as a fear about losing priority in Nvidia's supply queue, since Nvidia's written supply commitments rose from about $279B to about $390B in one quarter.

3. **Anthropic compute resale:** In May, SpaceX leased the entire Colossus 1 building in Memphis to Anthropic, covering over 300 MW and over 220,000 Nvidia GPUs. Anthropic went public on November 19. Its final prospectus shows commitments of up to $120.5B for SpaceX's Nvidia-based compute through 2029, about $3.1B a month (roughly $37B a year) at full capacity, which is expected in December. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei praised the arrangement on CNBC. Musk says Anthropic told him an extension is extremely likely and that it may need about 10 more GW. Anthropic has not confirmed this. Anthropic can also exit on 90 days' notice once its commitment period ends in early 2027. The host warns that a six-month Vera Rubin delay would leave a customer waiting on capacity SpaceX does not have.

4. **New York Times report and the Jefferies initiation:** According to the New York Times, citing three people familiar with the remarks, Musk told employees that SpaceX lags in AI and that renting compute to competitors is more profitable than using it in-house, which disappoints him. Musk replied that the paper "presents the obvious as news." Separately, Jefferies initiated SpaceX at buy and forecasts 22.5 GW of compute and nearly $400B in AI revenue by 2029. SpaceX has not issued such guidance.

5. **Star Mind orbital compute and the lender terms:** Nvidia is co-designing SpaceX's first orbital compute module, the Space-1, based on Vera Rubin. Nvidia claims it delivers up to 25x the AI processing of the H100. Earlier in November, Musk told Ron Baron that launches should begin in Q3 2027. The Financial Times reported that SpaceX's creditors want to stop about $40B of collateral chips from going to orbit, since they could not be repossessed there. Philip Johnson of Starcloud called the headline misleading, saying lenders simply want different terms for space compute. Huang has said space data-center economics are weak today but will improve over time.

6. **Musk warns of a 2027 cash crunch:** In a November 21 post, Musk said that a few months' lag between paying for compute and earning from it adds hundreds of billions of dollars of working capital. He called 2027 a year of survival and predicted that roughly 200 smaller GPU-rental "neoclouds" face mass failure and consolidation. Huang has described the problem more mildly, as a capital shortage. The host argues that the winners will be firms with cash and a place near the front of Nvidia's queue.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **AI5 goes to Optimus first:** Musk said Tesla's AI5 chip will go into Optimus, not cars. The training that drives Optimus and FSD progress runs on an Nvidia-powered Cortex cluster in Austin. Musk also wrote that Tesla's real-world AI will improve by an order of magnitude every year. The host says that pace depends on Nvidia supply until Terafab is running.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Cars stay on AI4, with a memory cut in AI4.5:** Musk said vehicles will remain on AI4, which he says is enough for superhuman driving, plus a small AI4.5 update. Because RAM is very expensive, Tesla is cutting AI4.5 memory from 32GB to 24GB. The host takes this as a sign of an industry-wide memory squeeze, in which Nvidia's purchasing clout matters.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Report on a merger committee:** On Tuesday, November 24, the Financial Times reported that Tesla and SpaceX have appointed five independent directors to discuss a possible merger through a share swap. Reuters could not immediately confirm the report, and no agreement has been reached. The host notes that a merger would put both businesses under a single Nvidia relationship.

2. **SpaceX benefits more than Tesla:** The host argues that SpaceX gains most from Huang's return because of the $44B order, the Anthropic contracts and the orbital computers. Tesla's exposure is limited to the pace of its training compute. Terafab serves as long-term insurance for both.

## Market & Competition
1. **The Nvidia CEO saga:** Huang admitted to an affair on November 8, and the board opened an investigation the same day. Reports also linked him to a woman US intelligence suspects of involvement in data transfers to China, and to a relationship with a subordinate.
   * **Resignation:** Huang resigned on November 12. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. The stock fell as much as 14% that day before closing down 4%.
   * **Musk's campaign:** Musk publicly signed a petition for Huang's reinstatement, which passed 206,000 signatures. At the DealBook Summit, Musk compared the ouster to a team firing Michael Jordan.
   * **Earnings without Huang:** Nvidia reported $115.8B in revenue, up 103%, on November 17. The same day, its new chairman said the board did not intend to bring Huang back.
   * **Reinstatement:** President Trump urged a reinstatement on Sunday. The board voted unanimously on Monday, November 23, citing guidance from the highest levels of the US government. Nvidia closed at $165, up 31%, and the broad market had its best day since April 2025.
   * **Who gets credit:** Trump told CNBC the board would not have acted without him. The host says no one can claim Musk's posts caused the reinstatement.

2. **Unresolved risks:**
   * **Investigations:** Nvidia's investigations continue. California's Department of Justice is running its own probe, and Reuters reports the SEC is reviewing Nvidia's accounting. Huang faces a closed-door House hearing on December 1 and public testimony before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.
   * **Market reaction:** Nvidia gave back about 7% on Tuesday. Goldman and Bank of America both cautioned that reinstatement does not resolve the reviews.
   * **Supplier concentration:** SpaceX has no second supplier if, for example, memory packaging or Taiwan manufacturing is disrupted.
   * **China exposure:** Nvidia has stopped AI chip shipments to China and guides to zero China data-center revenue next quarter. That could free supply for US buyers, but export controls are exactly what Congress plans to question Huang about.
   * **Upper-bound figures:** The host flags several headline numbers as ceilings or claims, not commitments:
     * Anthropic's $120.5B is an "up to" figure.
     * The extra 10 GW is Musk's claim and has not been confirmed by Anthropic.
     * Terafab's 2028 target is conditional on everything going to plan.

## Financials
1. **Terafab timeline defines the Nvidia dependence:** At the TomorrowX Summit, Musk said Terafab will be the largest building on Earth, at about 100 million sq ft. He said the research fab should produce a first test chip around June 2027, with phase one reaching mass production by the end of 2028 "if all goes well." The host concludes that every major SpaceX and Tesla training run depends on Nvidia for at least the next two years.
