This video from the Cern Basher channel features a multi-person discussion analyzing comments made by Elon Musk and Tesla executives (including Ashok and Vibob) during what appears to be a recent earnings call or investor event. The core thesis is that the primary bottleneck for Tesla's robotaxi expansion is not safety — Tesla has had zero incidents to date — but rather operational "convenience" issues such as cars getting stuck in infinite routing loops or becoming overly cautious in ambiguous situations. The hosts argue that these issues are solvable within the FSD 14.3 series of updates, without needing to wait for the full version 15 architectural overhaul.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 as the key unlocking version:** Elon Musk characterized FSD version 14.3 as a "major architectural update" for Full Self-Driving and robotaxi. The hosts interpret his comments to mean that the 14.3 series — not the forthcoming version 15 — is sufficient to enable unsupervised Full Self-Driving deployment. One host notes that version 14.3.1 introduced reasoning capabilities, but initially only applied them to end-destination parking, implying that reasoning will be extended to more edge cases (e.g., railroad crossings, prolonged stops at red lights) in subsequent point releases within the 14.3 series.

2. **Version 15 as a longer-term architectural overhaul:** Elon said version 15 will be a "complete overhaul of the software architecture" and will run on AI 4 hardware — clarifying that AI 5 is not a prerequisite. He described it as arriving "hopefully later this year" but "certainly by early next year" (i.e., by early 2027). Version 15 is framed not as the enabler of unsupervised FSD, but as a further leap in safety magnitude beyond what 14.3 already provides.

3. **Safety level claims:** Elon stated that even with version 14, Tesla FSD is "significantly safer than human" driving. He suggested version 15 will take safety to "another level," though Tesla has not publicly quantified a specific safety multiple (e.g., 2x or 5x safer than humans). The hosts note Tesla does not officially state it is a specific multiple above human safety level — just "above."

4. **Unsupervised FSD global rollout ambition:** Elon stated Tesla has "a whole pipeline of major improvements to Full Self-Driving" that they believe will lead to unsupervised Full Self-Driving being available "anywhere in the world that it is legal to do so." He also indicated unsupervised Full Self-Driving should be available in certain regions by end of 2026.

## Robotaxi

1. **Real bottleneck is operational, not safety:** Elon identified the primary limiter on wider robotaxi deployment as not safety failures but "convenience issues" — situations where the car "gets paranoid and gets stuck." Specific examples cited include infinite routing loops (where the car repeatedly routes back to the same blocked road) and excessive caution in ambiguous environments like road construction. Elon stated explicitly: "Those are by far the issues that we have had to resolve as opposed to direct safety issues." One host confirmed experiencing an infinite loop personally in his own Cybertruck.

2. **Zero incidents to date:** Both Elon and Ashok emphasized that Tesla's robotaxi program has had zero accidental injuries across its entire deployment history. The hosts characterize Tesla as being "more cautious than Waymo," noting that Waymo has historically been more willing to accept risk in its expansion approach.

3. **Expansion to Dallas and Houston:** Tesla has expanded its robotaxi service to Dallas and Houston using the same software stack as the Bay Area deployment. The hosts note that the Dallas and Houston vehicles are operating unsupervised (without safety drivers), which they view as a positive signal that the core safety bar has been met. Current fleet figures referenced are consistent with RobotaxiTracker.com data: approximately 5 vehicles in Dallas and 6 in Houston operating unsupervised.

4. **QA fleet vs. customer fleet dynamic:** Ashok explained that Tesla is increasing its QA (quality assurance) fleet to conduct rigorous validation across many U.S. cities — including rural areas — while also wanting to grow the customer-facing fleet to generate "useful metrics" for safe scaling. The hosts observe that Cybercab engineering prototype vehicles have been spotted in many cities and rural areas around the country, suggesting broad geographic validation activity.

5. **Cybercab production has begun — fleet deployment implications:** The hosts assert that Cybercab production has started at Giga Texas (referencing Joe Tegtmeyer's drone video coverage), and that the newly produced Cybercabs are being moved off the lot rather than accumulating in parking. Approximately 14 new production Cybercabs were reportedly spotted recently. The hosts argue it would make no logical sense to begin production without intent to deploy those vehicles into the robotaxi fleet, and they expect these vehicles to begin appearing in Austin, Dallas, and Houston in the near term. They note that Cybercabs without steering wheels would logically go directly into robotaxi expansion rather than QA fleet service.

6. **Scale and revenue ambiguity:** Elon said unsupervised FSD and robotaxi revenue would "not be super material this year" but would be "material, probably in a significant way, next year" (i.e., 2027). The hosts express frustration that neither "super material" nor "material in a significant way" are quantified. Similarly, Vibob stated Tesla "grew the robotaxi fleet quarter over quarter" and expects to keep ramping — without specifying absolute numbers. One host speculatively floated 10,000 robotaxis by end of 2026, but immediately acknowledged this is unconfirmed and may not align with Tesla's actual plans.

7. **Expansion pace uncertainty:** The hosts note that the language Tesla used — "ramping the fleet," "wider deployment," "expanding geographies" — is entirely unquantified, making it impossible to set reliable expectations for fleet size milestones. They caution viewers that any specific numerical expectation risks being wrong because Tesla is not providing target numbers.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybertruck FSD behavior anecdote:** One host mentioned experiencing an infinite routing loop personally in his own Cybertruck (not in a robotaxi context), corroborating Elon's description of the operational issues limiting robotaxi deployment. This illustrates that the edge-case loop problem is not unique to the robotaxi program but exists in the broader FSD consumer experience.
