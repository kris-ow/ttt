It covers half a mile
in the time it takes other
cars to cover a
quarter mile.
This is a statement from Tesla.  A
quarter-mile race allows you
to test this by giving
two metrics: time
and
finishing speed.
They indicate
different things.  Give the
same car
perfect traction and the
time
will be significantly reduced.  The final
speed will hardly
change.  This is because the
final speed
depends on the
power.
Speed ​​requires
energy, and the cost
increases
proportionally to the square of
the speed.  But the
faster you go, the
less time the engines
are running on each
meter of track.  So the
final speed
increases only
proportionally to
the cube root of the
power.  Want to
double your finishing
speed?  This
requires eight
times more
power.
Tesla's target figures are
about a megawatt of
power and 1,700
kilograms of weight.
Even without
air resistance and with
perfect traction
, the engines would not be able to
accelerate the car to the
finish line much above 200
miles per hour.  So,
when she finally
hits the drag strip,
time will tell on the grip.    The
finishing speed
will show the power.
