## Robotaxi

1. **New Dallas–Fort Worth Robotaxi Depot Discovered:** A letter of intent submitted for a permit revealed a robotaxi depot in Irving, Texas (Dallas–Fort Worth area). The facility includes 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building spaces, 17 EV charging stations, and around-the-clock services including cleaning and tire replacement. Panelists noted this closely mirrors a previously reported Las Vegas facility of similar size and layout. The discussion emphasized that the number of fleet parking spaces at the depot does not represent a cap on fleet size — the depot is a service location potentially covering several cities, suggesting the actual fleet it supports could be many multiples larger.

2. **Automated Cybercab Servicing Design:** Panelists highlighted that the Cybercab was designed from day one with automated servicing in mind, including x- and y-axis access throughout the vehicle to minimize friction during robotic cleaning and maintenance. The 64 building spaces are speculated to be individual bays, each equipped for automated servicing without requiring repetitive human labor. This design philosophy — removing humans from the operational loop — is seen as a key differentiator from competitors who struggle to integrate automation into product design.

3. **Capex Signal and Scale of Ambition:** The panel interpreted these depot investments as a visible component of Tesla's significantly increasing capex spend. Elon Musk's stated goal is for robotaxi to be ubiquitous — not a service where users must hunt for a car — drawing a comparison to how Uber transformed personal transportation. The buildout is described as foundational infrastructure for what the panel expects will eventually be a massive fleet rollout.

4. **NHTSA Safety Data: Tesla Robotaxi Safer Than Humans:** Updated NHTSA data, in which Tesla unredacted its incident narratives for the first time, revealed 17 total incidents since the Austin robotaxi launch in June 2025. AI analysis of those narratives classified 53% as the fault of other parties and 41% as Tesla's fault — meaning approximately 7 incidents were attributable to Tesla. An analyst referenced as "Reigns" conducted a full analysis concluding that Tesla's robotaxi is 1.81 times safer than human drivers as of April 2026, based on incidents per miles driven. Panelists noted this figure likely understates actual safety because minor incidents like curb scrapes — which human drivers routinely do not report — are mandatory reportable events for robotaxi operations. The trend since January 2026 was described as moving in the right direction.

## Autonomous Driving

The panel also noted that China has strong incentives to adopt autonomous driving technology given road safety challenges, and that FSD approval could spur domestic competition as well.
