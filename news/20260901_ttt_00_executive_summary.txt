## Robotaxi
- Tesla registered its first Cybercabs in Texas DMV filings on August 31, scaling from 1 to 45 by day's end, alongside 79 Model Ys added August 27 — bringing the Texas-registered robotaxi fleet to 314 vehicles (45 Cybercabs, 269 Model Ys). Multiple sources say this confirms Thursday's launch attendees will ride Cybercabs on public roads.
- VIN sequencing (observed numbers around 2,400) leads two independent commentators (Bhakdi, jobhakdi) to estimate over 2,000 Cybercabs may already exist — described explicitly as inference from VIN numbering, not confirmed fact.
- Austin's robotaxi geofence expanded ~9% to about 264 square miles, its first growth in roughly 10 months, extending toward Flugerville; Dallas's geofence grew 115% to 81 square miles two days prior. Other cited footprints: Miami 31 sq mi, Orlando 26, Houston 25, Tampa 24.
- Driverless Cybercab footage surfaced in Austin, including pickup/drop-off runs and a Whole Foods parking-lot maneuver; Tesla's Robotaxi account captioned one clip "Getting my shopping done."
- Tesla published a Cybercab structural safety guide detailing ultra-high-strength steel on A/B pillars, door rings, and overhead rail, with reinforced plastic/glass-fiber exterior panels.
- Night-shift robotaxi fleet-supervisor hiring posted in roughly 11 new metros (Atlanta, Nashville, Bellevue, San Diego, and others) beyond existing Texas/Arizona/Florida/Bay Area operations.
- Bhakdi puts >90% cumulative probability on 10,000 live Cybercabs within nine months, versus Morgan Stanley's estimate of 30,000 by 2030 — the central bull/bear divergence investors are watching. He expects a possible "sell the news" dip after Thursday, with likely under 25 cars in public service by Friday.
- Electrek's Jameson Dow argues the Cybercab concept traces to Musk's 2016 "Tesla Network" pitch and that Tesla still hasn't delivered owner-operated robotaxi capability; the piece frames Thursday's event as recycled hype rather than a new product.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek reports Tesla's own NHTSA filing marks automation "Engagement Status" as "Verified Engaged" in a March 25, 2026 crash in Batavia, Illinois that killed Maggie Espinosa, 37, a mother of five — a detail absent from police and prior media reports. Tesla redacted the crash narrative, software version, and operating-area confirmation as confidential business information.
- Electrek argues the unprotected left-turn maneuver involved is a City-Streets function basic Autopilot cannot perform, making FSD's involvement likely, though not confirmed by Tesla; the outlet stresses it is not alleging fault, only withheld data. One timestamp discrepancy (12:54 UTC filing vs. ~4:19 p.m. local police report) is flagged as unresolved. The Kane County State's Attorney's Office is reviewing; no charges filed.
- Tesla's global FSD (Supervised) fleet has driven 14 billion cumulative miles, up from 13 billion on August 3. Musk said pothole avoidance is "coming soon," without a version or timeline.
- Commentators on a separate podcast (unverified opinion, not independently confirmed) argued FSD leads global competitors by a wide margin, citing Xiaomi as "very good" but years behind, Huawei's system as still heuristic, and Rivian/Lucid as trailing.

## Energy
- Tesla discontinued the Solar Roof in August, redirecting its solar-roof webpage and removing it from site navigation; only about 3,000 systems were installed in the US over ~7 years, peaking at 21–32/week versus Musk's earlier promise of 1,000/week.
- Certified installers (Elevated Roofing, PGT Home Energy, Westfall Roofing, Saguaro Solar) report six-figure losses from training and tooling investment now stranded; Tesla says it will honor existing warranties, but installers doubt long-term parts availability and warn of high future service costs.
- SpaceX is moving to in-house gas turbine blade/vane casting to unblock data-center power buildout, with Musk saying turbines are sold out through 2030 and only three global casting firms exist, all backlogged; he said in-house casting could pull delivery timelines forward by up to 18 months. Musk also said SpaceX and Tesla are each building 100 gigawatts/year of solar production capacity.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Morgan Stanley (Adam Jonas) modeled Starship revenue reaching $3.5 trillion by 2040, based on 5,800 annual launches tied to the new $100 billion Starbase Louisiana project (13 of 15 planned pads operational by end of 2027); Musk responded his own best guess for hitting that revenue figure is 2033, implying a much higher internal trajectory.
- Musk clarified the ~$1 billion APR Energy turbine acquisition was made through SpaceX, not personally, despite media characterizing it otherwise.
- SpaceX's September 9 lockup/unlock date is flagged as a potential source of stock resistance, per one trader tracking SPCX options positioning; shares are said to have more room to run before then.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX filed for FCC approval to launch Starlink V3 satellites for the first time aboard Starship test flight 14; V3 is designed for over 10x the capacity of current satellites (SpaceX's stated goal, unverified in operation).
- Starship V3 is designed to carry 100 metric tons to orbit fully reusable; a future V4 could carry 200 tons. Commentary stressed "tons to orbit per year" via repeated reuse — not single-flight payload — as the key economic metric.

- The Pentagon's "Grok for Government," hosted on SpaceX's Starshield, has surpassed 1.7 million unique users out of ~3 million Department of War personnel.
