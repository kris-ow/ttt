Two Tesla-focused commentators preview Tesla's imminent Q2 earnings release, trading predictions on cash flow, cash on hand, margins across automotive, energy, and Cybercab, and giving competing EPS estimates before the numbers are announced.

## Financials
1. **Negative cash flow expected:** One speaker expects cash flow to be negative for the quarter and cash on hand to decline by a couple of billion dollars from a prior level cited around $47 billion, driven by heavy factory, data center, Cybercab, and Optimus-related spending; both speakers cite a market projection of roughly $10 billion in negative cash flow for the year, attributing the drag partly to AI compute chips (Cortex-related infrastructure) that can't yet be depreciated because they're not fully in operation.
2. **EPS estimates diverge widely ahead of earnings:** One speaker's own estimate is 57 cents; he cites a "street" figure around 55 cents, another number floating around 47 (possibly revised to 54), and says some forecasters are above 60 cents, including a named forecaster who was confident it would exceed 60. A separate commentator (James Stephenson) is cited as having raised his estimate to somewhere in the high 60s to low 70s range, though one speaker suggests this forecaster relies heavily on Loki data.
3. **Post-earnings stock reaction framed as a coin flip:** Asked what happens to the stock after earnings, one speaker jokes it will go down; citing a query to Grok, they note that when a beat is expected and prior estimates have run low, the odds of the stock rising afterward were put at roughly 52-53%.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab cash flow and cost trajectory:** Cybercabs already produced (estimated by one speaker at more than the reported count, possibly two or three thousand) are described as negative cash flow, sitting in inventory. Current per-unit cost is estimated in a range with a high end around $25,000, with one speaker theorizing costs could eventually fall to around $12,000 if battery production (using 4680 cells) becomes fully vertically integrated, citing a discussion with Grok that estimated roughly $4,000 in savings per vehicle from this approach plus domestic manufacturing tax credits at the cell and pack level. A comparison is drawn to Ford's stated goal of a $30,000 midsize electric pickup with margin, with one speaker arguing Cybercab could come in under $16,000 if Ford's target is achievable, while the other is skeptical Ford can hit $30,000 profitably.
2. **Production volume still small versus legacy EV makers:** One speaker notes Tesla has produced roughly as many Cybercabs to date as Lucid produced in its entire prior year (with the other suggesting it might only be a quarter of that), underscoring that neither Cybercab nor Lucid yet benefit from volume-driven cost reductions.

## Energy
1. **Megapack margins expected to hold near prior highs:** Margins on Megapack are projected to remain around 30%, though both speakers flag this as a difficult figure to predict; one attributes resilience to long-term customer relationships and pricing strategies (e.g., quoting favorable terms to build loyalty even amid cost changes) rather than purely being explained by an supposedly unlimited total addressable market.
2. **Stationary storage cell costs seen as flat to down:** One speaker states (based on knowledge of Tesla's battery suppliers, without disclosing specifics) that raw material costs for the cells used in stationary storage have not risen and are probably down slightly. Both note that because these cells are a commodity with well-known market pricing, competitors can benchmark Tesla's costs — except for landed cost data on 4680 cells, which is not public and would be a competitive differentiator if Tesla shifts to 4680s for storage.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Automotive margins projected higher on FSD attach rate:** Automotive gross margins are projected around 20%+ but potentially higher, tied largely to Full Self-Driving uptake; a figure attributed to a named analyst (Brian Wong) suggests each 1 percentage point increase in FSD uptake adds about six cents to earnings. One speaker notes vehicle sales for the quarter were strong, calling it the best second quarter on record.
