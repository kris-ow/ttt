This video unpacks a November 2nd, 2026 Washington Post report that SpaceX is in talks to either buy Boost Mobile or use its network, via its parent company EchoStar. The core thesis: satellites alone can't make SpaceX a mobile carrier — it needs ground coverage, spectrum, and an existing customer-billing relationship — and Boost supplies exactly those missing pieces, with the Starlink-T-Mobile partnership and spectrum deals explaining how this story has unfolded.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Boost Mobile deal is about owning the customer relationship, not just satellite reach:** SpaceX is reportedly in talks with EchoStar to buy Boost Mobile or secure network access through it; the video frames the strategic logic as SpaceX wanting to be the company that bills the customer directly, rather than just supplying a satellite feature inside someone else's plan (e.g., T-Mobile's).
2. **EchoStar/Boost business snapshot (June 2026 quarter):** EchoStar's wireless business (primarily Boost Mobile and Gen Mobile) had 7,375,000 subscribers, average monthly revenue per wireless subscriber of $38.60, and lost about 118,000 retail wireless subscribers that quarter. Boost's own 5G radio network carried zero customer traffic as of November 15th, 2025, with all traffic moved onto AT&T's network, while Boost retained the customer billing, activation, and account systems; it also retains access to T-Mobile under separate arrangements.
3. **AT&T ground network agreement runs through 2031:** EchoStar's 2025 annual report states AT&T agreed to provide network service to Boost through December 31st, 2031, with extension options — a multi-year ground infrastructure arrangement SpaceX would effectively be negotiating for if it pursues Boost.
4. **T-Mobile's existing Starlink partnership doesn't resolve the carrier question:** The Starlink–T-Mobile direct-to-cell partnership (announced August 2022) and the live "T-Satellite" product (as of November 2nd, 2026: $10/month add-on, included in Experience Beyond and Go 5G Next plans, supporting texting, WhatsApp voice chat, photos via iMessage/Google Messages, and location sharing, with ordinary voice calling still on T-Mobile's roadmap) show SpaceX acting as a satellite supplier to a carrier. T-Mobile CEO Srini Gopalan said on the October 28th, 2026 earnings call that he saw no room for a network rental arrangement with SpaceX, since renting network access to a company that would then compete for T-Mobile's own customers creates a direct conflict of interest.
5. **Spectrum deals give SpaceX frequencies but phone support varies by band:** EchoStar agreed in September 2025 (expanded November 2025) to sell spectrum licenses to SpaceX; the FCC approved the transaction on May 12th, 2026, explicitly separating the spectrum from EchoStar's wireless business and customers. EchoStar moved the licenses into a trust on May 22nd, 2026, with final transfer to SpaceX targeted for November 30th, 2027. PC Mag reported in March 2026 that existing smartphones didn't support the 2 GHz satellite frequencies SpaceX was acquiring in that deal. A separate low-band (800 MHz) spectrum agreement announced October 8th, 2026 is better suited to existing devices and to penetrating obstacles like walls, with SpaceX targeting closing in the first half of 2027.
6. **SpaceX's own ground and satellite buildout timeline:** On the August 4th, 2026 earnings call, Gwynne Shotwell said SpaceX plans to build small cellular base stations across the country, including alongside Starlink broadband dishes, to provide ground coverage. On the October 20th, 2026 call, SpaceX said it plans to deploy its second-generation mobile constellation through 2027 and turn on service in early 2028. Separately, Bloomberg reported on October 22nd, 2026 that SpaceX is in talks with infrastructure companies including Crown Castle about cell site access across the U.S.
7. **An existing Boost–SpaceX commercial tie already exists:** In September 2025, Boost and SpaceX announced a commercial agreement giving Boost subscribers access to next-generation Starlink phone service (text, voice, and broadband), plus a referral-fee arrangement where EchoStar earns a fee for customers it sends to Starlink — distinct from, and smaller in scope than, an outright Boost acquisition or full network-access deal.
