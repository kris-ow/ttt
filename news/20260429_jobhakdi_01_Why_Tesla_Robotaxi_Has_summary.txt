This video from Jo Bhakdi, published April 29, 2026, argues that Tesla's robotaxi rollout has already passed its inflection point and that concerns following the recent earnings call are overblown. The central thesis is that all fundamental technical and infrastructure components of Tesla's autonomous transport system have been "effectively solved," and the program is now in an optimization and scaling phase rather than a research phase. Bhakdi uses the Cybercab's unusual wheel engineering as a case study to illustrate Tesla's systems-level thinking, and defends his earlier fleet size predictions against criticism that the rollout is behind schedule.

## Robotaxi
1. **Current Cybercab fleet size and growth trajectory:** Bhakdi reports that the active unsupervised robotaxi fleet currently stands at 643 vehicles as of the time of recording, compared to his earlier prediction of approximately 1,800 by April/May 2026. He acknowledges missing that target but frames the miss as only two to three months behind schedule, noting that the monthly growth rate of roughly 50–100% is "pretty impressive." He estimates the fleet could reach approximately 800–900 vehicles by the end of May 2026.

2. **Inflection point argument:** Bhakdi contends that Tesla has already crossed the "Rubicon" — that the robotaxi program has passed its inflection point. He argues that no component of the system represents a "frontier type research problem" any longer. FSD safety is described as "effectively solved," and remaining work involves fine-tuning convenience, eliminating connectivity issues, and calibrating all subsystems to work together reliably at scale.

3. **Known operational issues being actively resolved:** Citing content shared by Dillon Loomis, Bhakdi notes that current Cybercab and robotaxi service issues are not FSD-related but infrastructure-related — including loss of connectivity, screens going black, and vehicles stopping due to broken AWS/server connections (not AT&T cellular connections). He frames these as expected growing pains in the optimization phase, not fundamental obstacles.

4. **Broader vision for autonomous transport:** Bhakdi positions the Cybercab not as a standalone car product but as the first pillar in a comprehensive autonomous transport system intended to eventually replace ride-hailing, taxis, public transit, buses, trains, personal car ownership, truck driving, and delivery vans. He argues this civilizational framing explains Tesla's engineering choices.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD declared effectively solved for safety:** Bhakdi states that based on the earnings call, Tesla has communicated that the safety problem for Full Self-Driving is "effectively solved," and that from this point forward development is focused on leveling up convenience without compromising safety — a qualitative shift from research to refinement.

2. **Cybercab wheel engineering as systems-level autonomy optimization:** Drawing on analysis by Christopher Dungeon, Bhakdi details the Cybercab's asymmetric wheel configuration — 18-inch steel wheels in the front and 21-inch aluminum wheels in the rear. The engineering rationale includes: reduced frontal area and rotational inertia at the front for aerodynamic efficiency; fewer tire rotations per mile at the rear to reduce bearing wear for vehicles expected to drive 100,000+ miles per year; steel front wheels being cheaper and better suited for hubcaps while aluminum rear wheels cut unsprung weight on drive wheels; and a forward-shifted center of gravity promoting stable understeer, which is described as "safer for autonomy." Bhakdi argues this demonstrates Tesla is engineering the Cybercab as a component in a transport system, not merely as a consumer vehicle.

## Market & Competition
1. **Institutional vs. retail investor sentiment post-earnings:** Bhakdi notes that following the earnings call, institutional investors reaffirmed their price targets and expressed confidence that the robotaxi program is on track, while retail investors appear to have panicked. He characterizes this as an unusual case where institutions are "a little bit ahead" of retail sentiment, and uses it to reinforce his own bullish stance on the stock.
