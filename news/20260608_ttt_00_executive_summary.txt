## Robotaxi
- **Nevada AV permit targets 5,000 vehicles in year one.** Tesla officially applied for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Nevada covering Clark County, including Harry Reid International Airport and Henderson Executive Airport, seeking approval for up to 5,000 robotaxis within the first 12 months post-approval. Per Jeff Lutz (former supply chain C-level at Motorola, Google, and Lenovo), companies do not submit inflated regulatory numbers — doing so invites scrutiny — making this figure a genuine near-term deployment intent. For context, Waymo's current nationwide fleet is approximately 3,700 vehicles.
- **Texas DMV data shows 51 registered robotaxi vehicles.** Tesla Robotaxi LLC added four more Model Y vehicles to its Texas-registered fleet, bringing the total to 51. This is the official public mechanism for tracking Texas fleet size.
- **RobotaxiTracker.com update pipeline paused through August.** The tracker's maintainer, Ethan, was hired by Tesla as a summer intern and publicly stated he cannot update the site for several months. Investors should weight community-reported fleet data accordingly until he returns.
- **Cybercab sightings in Seattle and staged lots across Dallas, Houston, and Austin** were cited by Bhakdi, corroborated by drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer on X. Cybercab entered production approximately two months ago (around early April 2026). Bhakdi identifies the World Cup matches beginning in Dallas this week as an ideal PR window for a Cybercab public launch.
- **Robotaxi demo expected in August 2026**, alongside a next-generation Roadster demo that has reportedly also been pushed to August.

## Market & Competition
- **JP Morgan raises Tesla price target from $145 to $475**, a 227% increase, upgrading to neutral. Sources attribute the reversal partly to JP Morgan's desire to participate in the SpaceX IPO and repair its relationship with Elon Musk; the prior Tesla analyst has since been replaced. The JP Morgan–Tesla lawsuit (filed 2021 over stock options) was resolved in November 2024.
- **Dwan Yongpin, the "Chinese Warren Buffett," discloses a ~$1.27B Tesla position** — approximately 3.4 million shares at a reported average of ~$371/share, representing ~6.3% of his $20B portfolio and his fifth-largest holding. Notably, Yongpin had been a vocal Musk skeptic; he reversed after experiencing Tesla's technology firsthand, describing it as "truly remarkable" while explicitly separating his views on Musk from the investment case.
- **Morgan Stanley purchased ~$1.26B of Tesla stock in Q1 2026.** Combined with the JP Morgan upgrade and Yongpin's position, institutions now own over 51% of Tesla stock — up from below 50% — while retail ownership has dropped to approximately 31%.
- **SpaceX IPO priced at $135/share (fixed, no range), ticker SPCX, listing June 12.** Targeting ~$75B in proceeds at a ~$1.75T implied market cap; up to $85.7B if underwriter overallotment is exercised. Underwriting fee is below 0.75% — far below the typical 4–7%. Polymarket is projecting a day-one closing valuation of $2.3T.
- **Google signed a $920M/month compute deal with SpaceX/xAI** (October 2026–June 2029, 90-day termination clause). Combined with Anthropic's existing deal, SpaceX is collecting $2.17B/month from these two customers — an annualized run rate of ~$26B.
- **Terafab chip manufacturing facility: Phase 1 budget $55B, total projected ~$119B**, per Grimes County, Texas filings from early May 2026. Grimes County commissioners voted 4-to-1 to approve full property tax abatement on machinery and equipment. Intel is referenced as a partner. Phase 1 alone exceeds the entire ~$53B U.S. CHIPS Act. ASML's CEO described Musk as "very serious" in ongoing talks.
- **Tesla AI 5 chip taped out April 15, 2026.** A single AI 5 chip is claimed to perform roughly on par with an Nvidia H100 for Tesla's specific inference workloads; two chips together approach Nvidia B200 Blackwell performance. Tesla claims ~10x performance per dollar and ~3x performance per watt versus AI 4. Elon Musk stated AI 5's first deployment target is Optimus and Tesla's AI supercomputer — not vehicles — with AI 4 described as already sufficient for superhuman autonomous driving. Next chip, AI 6, is expected in 2027 on Samsung's newer process; AI 6.5 to follow on TSMC Arizona.
- **Friday June 6 sell-off** — largest single-day decline since Liberation Day 2025 — was driven by a strong jobs report that markets interpreted as rate-hike signaling, compounded by Iran launching four waves of ~10 rockets at Israel (all intercepted by Iron Dome, no damage). Tesla hit an intraday high of $423 before closing around $391. By Monday, Tesla recovered ~$17 (implying ~$408–410). Bhakdi and the investingagainstthegrain host both characterize Friday as a tactical pullback within an intact AI bull market.
- **Physical AI framed as the next institutional rotation.** Jeff Lutz argues Tesla is the only publicly traded company with large-scale physical AI assets (autonomous vehicles, humanoid robots) and predicts a sector rotation from AI infrastructure plays into physical AI will drive meaningful multiple re-rating for Tesla.

## Humanoid Bots
- **AI 5 chip destined for Optimus first**, per Elon Musk — not vehicles. The host of farzad-fm frames this as a signal that Tesla has shifted its identity beyond being a car company.
- **Digital Optimus — Tesla's vision-based AI agent — identified as potentially the fastest-scaling Tesla business line** because it is a purely digital service with no hardware constraint. Elon Musk reportedly suggested some form of Digital Optimus could be operational by end of 2026. Per cernbasher, SpaceX's S-1 prospectus frames enterprise AI applications (Digital Optimus + xAI's Macrohard) as ~80% of SpaceX's $20.5T TAM — larger than Starlink and orbital launch combined — and both segments depend on Tesla's vision technology. Very few Tesla investors have modeled this opportunity.
- **Optimus reveal listed as a near-term catalyst**, expected within the next four to eight weeks from the June 8 publish date (i.e., through late July/early August 2026). Jeff Lutz described Optimus as "a Tesla product with a SpaceX brain in it."

## Energy
- **Houston Megapack factory exterior construction is complete**, per aerial footage cited by brighterwithherbert. The facility is a 50 GWh factory; interior fit-out and capital equipment installation remain. Pace of construction suggests earlier-than-expected operability.
- **Battery energy storage bans spreading in U.S. municipalities**, including an outright ban in Black Diamond, Washington. FutureAzA argues the fire concerns driving these bans are largely outdated given the industry's shift to LFP chemistry, which is significantly less prone to thermal runaway.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Q2 2026 auto deliveries tracking ~28% higher year-over-year through May**, per Jeff Lutz, with most countries showing meaningfully higher volumes. Q2 results expected in the third week of July 2026.
- **Used Cybertrucks listed at $72,900 (Foundation Series) sold roughly half of new inventory within one day**, per FutureAzA — a demand signal at lower price points.
- **FSD Supervised user adoption broadening to older demographics**, per Sawyer Merritt's X digest: an elderly couple drove New Hampshire to Connecticut with zero interventions; an 89-year-old reportedly uses FSD for the majority of her driving. A third-party written review (highest-engagement Tesla post of the day) described FSD handling lane changes, passing, signaling, and parking "with almost eerie confidence" across varied environments.
