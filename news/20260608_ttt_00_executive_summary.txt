## Robotaxi
- **Nevada permit application targets 5,000 vehicles in year one.** Tesla officially filed for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Nevada covering Clark County, including Harry Reid International Airport and Henderson Executive Airport, requesting up to 5,000 robotaxis in the first 12 months post-approval. For context, Waymo's entire current U.S. fleet is cited at approximately 3,700 vehicles, making Tesla's single-state target potentially larger than the nationwide incumbent.
- **Texas DMV registered fleet reaches 51 vehicles.** Tesla Robotaxi LLC added four Model Y vehicles to its Texas DMV registration, bringing the official state total to 51. This is the public tracking mechanism for the Texas fleet; RobotaxiTracker.com's community data may lag going forward after its maintainer, Ethan, was hired by Tesla as a summer intern and stated he cannot update the tracker through at least August 2026.
- **Robotaxi demo expected in August 2026.** Reports indicate a dedicated robotaxi demonstration event is being planned for August, potentially coinciding with Tesla's next-generation Roadster demo. Cybercabs have been photographed staged in Seattle and in large parking lots across Dallas, Houston, and Austin, with drone footage cited on X corroborating preparation activity. Cybercab entered production approximately two months ago, per Bhakdi.
- **World Cup cited as near-term PR catalyst.** Dallas hosts World Cup matches beginning this week. The convergence of Cybercab staging in Texas, the SpaceX IPO, and the World Cup audience is being flagged by multiple commentators as a high-visibility window for a public Cybercab launch moment.

## Autonomous Driving
- **Six months of Texas robotaxi safety data building the Nevada regulatory case.** Since the June 22, 2025 Austin launch, Tesla has accumulated roughly six months of operational robotaxi data submitted to NHTSA showing very few — possibly zero — major accidents. This safety record is expected to anchor the Nevada permit application.
- **FSD viral review signals broadening adoption demographics.** A third-party written FSD Supervised review (highest-engagement Tesla post of the day per Sawyer Merritt's digest) describes the system handling lane changes, passing, signaling, and parking "with almost eerie confidence" across varied environments with no sudden surprises or jerky corrections. Separately, anecdotal reports cite an elderly couple completing a New Hampshire-to-Connecticut trip with zero interventions and an 89-year-old as a regular FSD user. No miles-per-intervention or disengagement data were cited.
- **FSD vision stack identified as the foundation for Digital Optimus.** Per analysis of SpaceX's IPO prospectus, the same vision technology underlying FSD is described as powering "Digital Optimus" — Tesla's AI agent product — meaning FSD development is directly upstream of what SpaceX identifies as ~80% of its total addressable market.

## Humanoid Bots
- **AI 5 chip deploys to Optimus first, not vehicles.** When asked where AI 5 would land first, Musk's answer was Optimus and Tesla's AI supercomputer — not self-driving cars. The older AI 4 chip is described as already sufficient for autonomous driving at a superhuman level, signaling a deliberate sequencing decision.
- **AI 5 chip performance claims.** Taped out April 15, 2026, Tesla claims a single AI 5 performs roughly on par with an Nvidia H100 for Tesla's inference workloads, two chips approach Nvidia B200 Blackwell performance, and the chip delivers ~10x performance per dollar and ~3x performance per watt versus AI 4. A single AI 5 chip is claimed to have ~5x the useful compute of two AI 4 chips combined.
- **Digital Optimus framed as Tesla's fastest-scaling potential business.** Because Digital Optimus is a purely software-delivered AI agent service with no hardware constraint, it could theoretically scale faster than robotaxi or physical Optimus. Musk reportedly suggested some form of Digital Optimus could be operational by end of 2026, though analysts note xAI's current development pace warrants caution on that timeline.
- **Optimus reveal listed as a near-term catalyst.** An Optimus reveal is expected within the next four to eight weeks from the June 8 publish date, described as part of a cluster of catalysts alongside the robotaxi demo and Roadster reveal, all pointing toward August.

## Energy
- **Houston Megapack factory exterior complete.** Aerial footage shows the exterior of Tesla's 50 GWh Houston Megapack production facility appears fully constructed. Interior fit-out and capital equipment installation remain pending, but the construction pace is being flagged as faster than expected.
- **Tesla vehicle-to-load rolls out in Australia.** Tesla has enabled vehicle-to-load functionality in Australia, at least for the Model Y Performance variant. Full vehicle-to-grid capability remains constrained by the absence of required onboard circuitry in most Tesla vehicles.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Q2 deliveries tracking ~28% year-over-year through May.** Auto deliveries are running approximately 28% higher year-over-year on a net basis with most markets up, setting up for a potentially strong Q2 report. Official Q2 results are expected in the third week of July 2026.
- **Used Cybertruck sells quickly at lower price points.** Tesla listed a batch of used Cybertrucks with Foundation Series pricing starting at $72,900; roughly half the newly listed inventory sold within one day, suggesting meaningful latent demand at reduced price points.

## Financials
- **JP Morgan raises Tesla price target 227%, from $145 to $475.** JP Morgan — described as holding a "permanent bear" stance on Tesla for years — upgraded Tesla to neutral with a $475 target. The hosts attribute the move primarily to JP Morgan's desire to participate in the SpaceX IPO and repair its relationship with Elon Musk following the resolution of their mutual lawsuit in November 2024.
- **Morgan Stanley purchases ~$1.26B of Tesla stock in Q1 2026.** The buy, combined with the JP Morgan upgrade and Dwan Yongpin's position, reflects a broader institutional rotation: institutions now own over 51% of Tesla stock, up from below 50%, while retail ownership has declined to approximately 31%.

## Market & Competition
- **Dwan Yongpin discloses ~$1.27B Tesla position.** The Chinese value investor known as the "Chinese Warren Buffett" reported 3.4 million Tesla shares worth approximately $1.27 billion as of Q1 2026, representing ~6.3% of his $20B portfolio and his largest percentage increase in Q1. His reported average purchase price was ~$371/share. He had previously been a vocal Musk skeptic and reversed after experiencing Tesla's technology firsthand.
- **SpaceX IPO: $135/share fixed price, $1.75T implied market cap, listing June 12.** The offering targets approximately $75 billion in proceeds (up to $85.7B with overallotment) across 555 million Class A shares, with retail investors accessing the same price as institutions under ticker SPCX. Underwriting fees are reportedly below 0.75%, versus the typical 4–7% range.
- **Google signs $920M/month compute deal with SpaceX.** Google is paying SpaceX $920 million per month for compute access from October 2026 through June 2029, with either party able to exit on 90 days' notice. Combined with Anthropic's existing arrangement, SpaceX is collecting $2.17 billion per month from these two customers alone — an annualized run rate of approximately $26 billion.
- **Terafab chip fab: $55B Phase 1, $119B total.** Grimes County, Texas commissioners voted 4-to-1 to approve tax abatements for the SpaceX Terafab facility. Phase 1 costs are $55 billion — exceeding the entire ~$53B U.S. CHIPS Act — with a total projected cost of approximately $119 billion. Elon Musk is reportedly in direct talks with ASML; the ASML CEO described Musk as "very serious." Intel is named as a partner.
- **Tesla stock pulled back to ~$391 Friday before Monday recovery.** Tesla hit an intraday high of $423 on Friday, June 6, before selling off to close around $391 — part of the NASDAQ's largest single-day decline since Liberation Day 2025, triggered by a strong jobs report and Iran-Israel escalation. By Monday, Tesla recovered approximately $17. The Fear and Greed Index dropped from 61 to 42 over the week.
