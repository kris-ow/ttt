The biggest
energy drain in AI isn't
computation.  This is
data movement.
Adding two numbers
on a chip is cheap, but
getting a single
number from DRAM, the memory that
powers it, requires
6,400 times more
energy.  And when the Nvidia A100
trains an
image model, 60 to 84% of the
processing power
is spent
moving data.
What is left is used for calculation
.  Why?
Data is mostly
transmitted as
electricity over copper,
and copper works like a
garden hose.    The
faster you push, the
more signal
is converted into
heat.  Therefore, between
machines, data
is transferred into light
through connectors.
Nvidia's Jensen Huang
counted six for
each GPU.
Because if we had a
million GPUs,
multiplied by six,
right?  That would be one million
six million
30 watt transceivers,
180 megawatts per
transceiver.  They didn't
do any
calculations, they
just moved
the signals.
Every watt spent
shuffling
numbers never goes
into computing.  Therefore,
the latest switches
place the light
directly on the chip housing
, and according to Nvidia,
the consumption of each
connection drops from 30
watts to about
nine.  Because in AI, the
most expensive part has
never been
the calculations.
