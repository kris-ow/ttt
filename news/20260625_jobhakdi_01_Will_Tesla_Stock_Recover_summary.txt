Jo Bhakdi hosts a live stream addressing Tesla's steep five-day stock decline, arguing the selloff is driven by inorganic market distortions — likely linked to SpaceX liquidity flows and broader sector rotation — rather than any fundamental deterioration at Tesla. His core thesis is that the accelerating Cybercab manufacturing ramp makes an inflection point in commercial robotaxi deployment inevitable and imminent, and that the stock's weakness is temporary and increasingly irrational given the company's improving fundamentals. He draws on a technical analysis shared by "Lord Pretty Flaco" and fields extensive viewer questions about trading strategy, FSD readiness, a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger, and the broader market.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab production already exceeding 1,000 units:** Jo Bhakdi estimates Tesla has already produced over 1,000 Cybercabs, based on visual sightings across multiple locations. He reports seeing over 100 units in the Giga Texas parking lot in Austin, over 50 in Houston, over 50 in Dallas, an unspecified number in Los Angeles and Phoenix, and a large contingent in the Bay Area. He projects that Cybercab production will exceed 1,000 units in July alone, putting the cumulative total at roughly 2,000 by end of July.

2. **Manufacturing scale as the core bull argument:** Bhakdi's central thesis is that Tesla's operational, finance, and manufacturing departments — which he describes as best-in-class — would not greenlight mass-scale Cybercab production without a clear line of sight to commercial deployment. He argues it is economically irrational to accumulate thousands of Cybercabs (which cannot be sold and serve only one purpose — paid robotaxi operation) unless deployment is imminent. He dismisses the idea that Tesla needs thousands of units purely for testing purposes as "ridiculous."

3. **Geofenced rollout as bridge to full autonomy:** Bhakdi argues Tesla does not need perfect, ungated FSD to begin monetizing Cybercabs. He believes a transition period has already begun in which geofenced areas, remote operators, remote monitoring, and default safety systems can enable meaningful fleet deployment — potentially absorbing multiple thousands of Cybercabs — even before FSD achieves complete autonomy. He estimates full, un-geofenced autonomy may still be 12+ months away (possibly end of 2026 or into Q1 2027), but this does not preclude an early commercial rollout.

4. **Tesla vs. Waymo competitive framing:** Bhakdi contends Tesla only needs to be "more effective" than Waymo in geofenced areas to begin winning the robotaxi market, not perfect in absolute terms. He believes this competitive threshold has already been crossed or is very close, supporting the case for near-term deployment.

5. **Path to $1,000+ per share tied to Cybercab scale:** Bhakdi states that once Cybercab deployment reaches 1,000–2,000 units on a steep month-over-month scaling curve, he believes Tesla's stock can run to $1,000 per share extremely quickly. He says reaching $1,000 in 2026 is possible (not certain) if a commercial rollout occurs. He views $1,500 as a stretch in the near term.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD still not ready for full unsupervised deployment — but close:** Bhakdi is more measured on FSD than some commentators he references. He acknowledges ongoing user complaints about edge cases and says he takes them seriously. He believes FSD will not achieve complete autonomy until probably end of 2026 or Q1 2027, possibly even another year out. His bullishness on Tesla is not predicated on FSD being perfect today, but on Tesla's organizational competence and the inference drawn from the Cybercab manufacturing ramp.

2. **FSD version 14.3.4 referenced:** A viewer comment references FSD version 14.3.4 as the current build, and Bhakdi acknowledges some users may not yet be on it. He does not offer independent confirmation of the version number.

3. **FSD version 15 as potential geofence unlock:** Bhakdi references a view — which he attributes to some community members — that version 15 (expected around October–December 2026) may be the version enabling truly un-geofenced commercial operation. He does not endorse a specific timeline but uses this framing to argue that geofenced deployments can begin well before that milestone.

## Market & Competition

1. **Inorganic stock movements linked to SpaceX liquidity:** Bhakdi's primary explanation for the erratic Tesla price action — a strong rally on Monday with no news, followed by a severe drop on Tuesday with no meaningful news — is that SpaceX-related liquidity flows are distorting Tesla trading. He explicitly distinguishes this from market manipulation, calling it a distortion caused by cash being pushed in and out non-organically. SpaceX shares reportedly fell ~16% on one day of the week discussed.

2. **Broader market sector rotation observed:** Bhakdi notes unusual cross-market behavior: Micron Technologies surged ~15% on strong earnings (validating the AI memory cycle), yet Nvidia, Amazon, and Apple all declined on the same day for no obvious reason. He also observes biotech and diagnostic stocks (Tempus, Personalis, Garden Health cited by name) rising ~10% with no catalyst, suggesting a rotation out of AI and into other sectors that he finds fundamentally unexplained.

3. **Uber and Lyft viewed as uninvestable:** Bhakdi states he cannot understand why anyone would buy Uber or Lyft stock, characterizing them as effectively dead once Tesla's robotaxi deployment materializes as widely expected. He uses the phrase "Russian roulette with seven bullets."

4. **SpaceX stock expected to fall significantly:** Bhakdi expresses a strongly bearish view on SpaceX's publicly traded shares. He does not see how SpaceX avoids falling below $135 and likely below $100, citing increasing selling pressure expected through November 2026. He views $75 as a reasonable entry point for accumulation but says he will wait for momentum and chart confirmation rather than a specific price level. He cites a viewer's $75 price target and calls it "reasonable." He notes SpaceX trades at a multiple far above comparable companies including Palantir, Tesla, Anthropic, and OpenAI.

5. **Tesla–SpaceX merger discussed but seen as unlikely in 2026:** Bhakdi addresses persistent community speculation about a Tesla–SpaceX merger. He believes a merger will ultimately happen and would add a significant premium to Tesla shares (he says he would vote yes at $750 per share and would be skeptical at $500). However, he argues the deal is far more legally and operationally complex than most commentators acknowledge. Key concern: Tesla conducting a merger of this scale just months after SpaceX's IPO would invite SEC scrutiny over whether merger plans were known at IPO and not disclosed. He thinks 2027 is a more realistic and de-risked timeline. He also dismisses the "merger of equals" structure as impractical given the extreme volatility of both stocks (a standard 20% deal-break clause would be triggered almost daily).
