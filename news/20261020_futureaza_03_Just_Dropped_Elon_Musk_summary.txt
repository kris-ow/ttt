This video covers SpaceX's Q3 2026 earnings call, with Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Brett (Bret Johnston, presumably referred to as "Brett") presenting operational and financial results across launch, Starlink, and super intelligence (SI/compute) segments, followed by an analyst Q&A that includes direct Musk commentary on a possible SpaceX–Tesla merger.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed satellites; Flight 15 to attempt full reusability:** Musk said Starship Flight 14 reached orbit for the first time and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites. Flight 15, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and the ship — which, if successful, would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle. Musk called this a "profound technology milestone" and said success is "more likely than not" but not guaranteed.
2. **Louisiana spaceport announced:** SpaceX announced development of a new spaceport in Louisiana, which Musk said could eventually have on the order of 20+ launch towers and enable transport of millions of tons per year to orbit. Shotwell said the company's internal goal is a first launch from Louisiana in 2028, though Musk said 2029 is "highly likely," with launching "at scale" from the site expected by 2029.
3. **Launch cadence targets ramping into 2027:** Musk expects Starship to fly weekly in the first half of next year, then twice weekly or more by the end of next year, as rapid/complete reusability is achieved, reducing refurbishment time between flights.
4. **Q3 launch cadence and manifest:** SpaceX flew 35 launches in Q3, including 21 Starlink Falcon 9 flights, two Starship flights, a Falcon Heavy (carrying NASA's Roman Space Telescope), 12 other customer missions, and six national security launches. New business signed in the quarter included three additional Dragon human spaceflight missions (through 2028), a Starlink contract on NASA's Artemis 3 mission, 18 new National Security Space Launch missions, a lunar-surface payload delivery contract via Starship, and three new Starfall missions.
5. **On-orbit refueling and FAA engagement:** Shotwell said SpaceX plans to attempt on-orbit refueling early next year, aims to refly the Starship second stage by early next year, and has had "significant constructive engagement" with the FAA, expecting the Flight 15 license to include return-to-launch-site and tower catch for both stages.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Flight 14 Starlink deployment equaled 10 Falcon 9 launches:** Musk said Flight 14 deployed 26 terabits per second of Starlink bandwidth — equivalent to 10 Falcon 9 launches — despite the ship being only about half loaded.
2. **100x bandwidth growth targeted within ~3 years:** Musk projected Starlink could have roughly 100x its current bandwidth within about three years via a new generation of satellites (10x+ bandwidth each) and a constellation scaling toward roughly 100,000 satellites, driven partly by surging bandwidth demand from AI/SI agents.
3. **Subscriber and market growth:** Starlink ended Q3 active in 174 markets (new launches in Vietnam and Uganda), adding 1.6 million consumer subscribers to reach just under 14 million global broadband consumer subscribers (CFO cited 13.6 million), plus additional enterprise and aviation subscribers not included in that figure.
4. **Aviation and mobile expansion:** Starlink is now contracted for roughly 12,000 aircraft (~25% of the addressable fleet), with agreements with three of four major US carriers and several international airlines (Lufthansa, Swiss, Copa, Korean Air, Austrian, Asiana flew first Starlink-equipped flights in the quarter). Aviation production has quadrupled since the start of the year with plans to double again by year-end. Starlink Mobile surpassed 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter; the FCC approved launch of 15,000 Mobile V2 satellites, and SpaceX announced a low-band spectrum acquisition to support becoming a major US mobile carrier.
5. **Financials — Connectivity segment:** Connectivity revenue was $5.2 billion, up 69% year-over-year and 22% sequentially; consumer ARPU rose 5% sequentially to $69/month; enterprise and government revenue grew 91% year-over-year; gross margin was 55%, including a one-time ~3% benefit from tariff refunds.
6. **Disaster response:** Starlink supported 25 crisis responses in Q3, including the Venezuela earthquake, a western Colombia earthquake, and Hurricane Leel in Hawaii; in Venezuela alone it donated over 1,400 kits reaching over 250,000 people.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Massive compute buildout underway:** SpaceX is targeting roughly 200,000 GB300 GPUs brought online per month for the rest of this year (a Q&A figure specified 220,000/month), continuing into next year with Rubin-generation compute. Musk said he's "highly confident" of reaching an incremental 10 GW of compute online in 2027 (roughly 13 GW total, atop ~2.5–3 GW by this year's end), with a "non-zero but small" possibility of exceeding 20 GW.
2. **Power/cooling as the bottleneck, not chips:** Musk said the constraint on SI deployment is power, cooling, and infrastructure rather than chips, and described SpaceX's advanced rocket-engine casting capability being repurposed to manufacture turbine blades and vanes — a bottleneck he says is 12-18 months behind the rest of turbine production — giving SpaceX a claimed 12-18 month power deployment advantage over competitors.
3. **Grok and Grokbot growth:** Musk said SpaceX is planning the most powerful AI training cluster online by end of Q2, with 440,000 Rubin-class computers deployed for training. Grokbot (launched in August) reportedly reached 800,000 paid weekly users earlier this month, more than doubling in a month; Musk called it the fastest-growing product he's seen. Enterprise customers with over $1 million in run-rate revenue (including Cursor) grew 50% quarter-over-quarter.
4. **Financials — SI segment:** SI segment revenue rose to $7.6 billion (up $5 billion sequentially, $6.7 billion year-over-year), with $7 billion from "SI solutions and infrastructure" ($68 million from the Cursor acquisition). SI adjusted EBITDA was $5 billion; SI operating income was $431 million, or $1.6 billion excluding $1.2 billion of stock-based compensation tied to the Cursor deal. The company ended Q3 with 1.7 GW of nameplate compute capacity and expects to reach nearly 2.2 GW by end of October, with 2.5–3 GW line of sight by end of Q4.
5. **Terafab chip fabrication timeline:** Musk said internal (unconfirmed, "big error bars") targets are a functional test chip around May/June next year at the Austin research lab, with aspirational volume production around Q3 2028 at the main Terafab site, which he said is aimed at eventually producing a terawatt of SI chips per year.
6. **Compute pricing and allocation:** Musk said SpaceX aims for a roughly 50/50 split of compute between internal use and third-party rental (dynamic, could shift), rents at spot-market rates rather than long-term deals, and sees short-term contracts renewing at higher dollars-per-watt. He said demand is overwhelming supply across all GPU/TPU types, and a six-month compute payback period next year is "not impossible."

## Financials
1. **Q3 total company results:** Revenue grew 161% year-over-year to $14.6 billion; adjusted EBITDA grew 306% year-over-year to $8.8 billion. Space segment revenue was $1.8 billion (up 84% sequentially, 6% year-over-year). Total capex was $9.6 billion. Backlog stood at $63 billion as of September 30th, with $17.5 billion recognized as deferred revenue and about $42 billion expected to be recognized within the next year.
2. **Forward guidance:** SpaceX reiterated a goal of $100 billion in annualized run-rate revenue (ARR) by December of this year. Q4 capex guidance was raised to $35–40 billion; Q1 2027 total company revenue guidance was set at $33–37 billion. Musk separately projected SpaceX's SI business alone could reach $500 billion to $1 trillion in annual recurring revenue by the end of 2027.
3. **Tesla–SpaceX merger commentary:** Asked directly about a potential merger, Musk said both companies have formed special committees of independent directors to discuss the idea (he is excluded from those discussions as a conflicted party), that the committees have not reached agreement on terms, and that a merger should not be assumed to be a certainty. He said a Tesla shareholder vote would require 50% of all shares outstanding (not just those voted) — a "high hurdle." Musk said he personally believes combining the companies "would be a good idea" given synergies in SI software, chip design, Terafab, robotics, and satellite/connectivity, but stressed the decision rests with the special committees and shareholders.
