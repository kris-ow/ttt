Electrek covered two Tesla product stories today: the Cybercab's lack of home charging as Tesla starts selling it to individuals, and Tesla's Powercore solid-state transformer arriving to compete with a startup founded by a former Tesla executive.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab can't charge at home:** Electrek reports Tesla is taking orders for the $29,990 Cybercab, with first deliveries expected in Austin next month. The car has no onboard charger, so it accepts only NACS DC fast charging (no Wall Connector or Level 2). Electrek cites the order agreement limiting charging to DC fast charging and requiring the owner to be present for the entire trip, so the car can't charge itself overnight. The $299 monthly autonomy subscription is also required. Tesla has shown a wireless pad since 2023 and won an FCC waiver in February, but has never priced it or given a launch date. Fred Lambert calls this a fleet vehicle sold to consumers to hit a deadline, and the value proposition "pretty awful." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/17/tesla-cybercab-home-charging-wireless-problem/)

## Energy
2. **Tesla Powercore transformer:** Electrek reports Tesla announced Powercore on X: a solid-state medium-voltage transformer combining inverter and breaker functions, rated 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV, with grid-forming support up to 10 MVA peak. It is made in America and due to start operating in 2027. Tesla gave no efficiency, pricing, factory location or product-pairing details. Rival Heron Power, founded by ex-Tesla SVP Drew Baglino, plans volume production of its 5 MW Heron Link in 2027 and says it has 50 GW of orders in the pipeline. Lambert calls the launch "a little awkward" for Tesla and says he wouldn't be surprised if Heron is ahead, though he thinks the transformer shortage leaves room for both. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/17/tesla-powercore-solid-state-transformer-heron-link/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's view is skeptical of Tesla on both fronts: the Cybercab was engineered for fleet use, with the cost of cutting corners landing on the first consumer buyers, and Tesla's Powercore announcement is thin next to Heron's published numbers, named customers and factory plans.

Notable quotes and data:
- Cybercab pricing: $29,990 plus a required $299 monthly subscription, with first deliveries expected in Austin next month (Electrek, Cybercab article).
- Order agreement language: the owner is "responsible for registration, insurance, parking, charging, general cleaning and all other costs" (Electrek, quoting Tesla's autonomy agreement).
- Wireless charging: Tesla's video showed 25 kW charging with efficiency "well above 90%"; Tesla has not sold, priced or dated the pad (Electrek, Cybercab article).
- Powercore: 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV, grid-forming up to 10 MVA peak, operating in 2027 (Electrek, Powercore article).
- Heron Link: 5 MW per unit, with Heron Factory One in Morgan Hill planned for 10,000 units a year (over 40 GW annual capacity) and commercial production in late 2027 (Electrek, Powercore article).
- Heron funding: $38M Series A, $140M Series B, and a $60M credit facility (Electrek, Powercore article).
