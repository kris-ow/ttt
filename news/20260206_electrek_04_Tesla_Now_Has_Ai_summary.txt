Tesla has launched an AI training center in China to develop its Full Self-Driving (FSD) system locally, addressing a longstanding handicap caused by China's data localization laws.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **China AI training center now operational:** Tesla VP Grace Tao confirmed to Cailian that Tesla has put an AI training center into operation in China with "sufficient computing power to support development of assisted-driving features," though she did not disclose location, investment size, or computational capacity.
2. **Why this matters:** China's data localization laws had barred Tesla from sending Chinese driving data to its US training infrastructure. At FSD's China launch in February 2025, Elon Musk said Tesla had instead used publicly available internet video of Chinese roads and signs to train in simulation — a workaround the article calls far from ideal given China's distinct traffic patterns and signage. A local training center now lets Tesla train on actual local driving data while complying with localization rules.
3. **No clear approval timeline:** Despite the infrastructure progress, Tao could not provide a specific deployment timeline, saying only that Tesla would "actively engage in assisted driving initiatives in China." Musk had told the World Economic Forum in Davos last month that he expected FSD approval in China by February, a claim the article says was quickly disputed by Chinese government sources.
4. **Current product status:** Tesla currently offers "Intelligent Assisted Driving" in China (a renamed FSD), available for purchase at RMB 64,000 (about $9,220). Starting February 14, Tesla will discontinue the one-time purchase option globally in favor of a monthly subscription model.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chinese rivals advancing on L3 autonomy:** Chinese EV makers including Huawei, Xpeng, and Li Auto have been developing autonomous driving systems trained entirely on local data, and the article reports thousands of Level 3-capable vehicles are expected on Chinese roads in 2026 following recent regulatory approvals. Shanghai-based angel investor Yin Ran is quoted saying intensified competition between Tesla and local rivals will benefit Chinese consumers as automakers race to deliver affordable self-driving systems.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the China training center is a bigger deal than it appears, since Tesla's real-world data advantage — a key competitive moat — had been nullified in China by localization laws; it has taken nearly a year since FSD's China launch to establish this infrastructure. He questions whether Tesla has lost too much ground to local competitors like Huawei and Xpeng, which have had a head start training on Chinese data and reportedly perform well on local roads. A top reader comment (16 likes) is more skeptical, noting Tesla's US autonomous tech, despite a decade of data access, still trails other brands and isn't fully autonomous, questioning why China would be different.
