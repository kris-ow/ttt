This video from Munro Live features an interior review of the Cadillac Escalade IQ electric SUV, presented by a self-described Cadillac enthusiast who attempts to apply a critical eye despite her personal affinity for the brand. The core thesis is that while the Escalade IQ is a genuinely original electric vehicle (not a rebadged existing model) and largely delivers on luxury expectations for its $150,000 price point, there are several notable quality and design shortfalls — particularly in stitching consistency, second-row seat materials and functionality, and some finish details — that fall short of what a buyer should expect at this price.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Escalade IQ as a Unique Electric Platform:** The reviewer emphasizes that unlike the original Escalade, which was a rebadged Yukon, the Escalade IQ is a purpose-built electric vehicle unique to itself. She views this as a meaningful distinction and credits Cadillac for developing an original platform rather than converting an existing one.

2. **Price Point and Value Assessment:** The Cadillac Escalade IQ is priced at $150,000. The reviewer acknowledges that a significant portion of that cost is in the drivetrain and technology, and estimates that a comparable-sized EV without luxury features might cost $80,000–$90,000. She concludes that the overall package largely justifies the cost, but with specific reservations.

3. **Interior Materials — Strengths:** The upper door roll features real cut-and-sew stitching on a thin foam-backed material, which the reviewer considers appropriate for the price. The lower instrument panel is fully wrapped in what she believes is a silicone-based artificial leather (not PVC or PUR), a material Cadillac has reportedly been investing in. She notes that using artificial leather allows for a single wide continuous sheet — something difficult to achieve with natural leather hides at this vehicle's scale. Pillar trim and overhead lining use artificial suede.

4. **Interior Materials — Weaknesses and Quality Issues:** The reviewer identifies several quality shortfalls inconsistent with the $150,000 price tag. Stitching on the top roll and overhead lining is uneven and wavy in places, with frayed threads visible and stitching pulling away at corners. A door panel has an awkward formation where it hugs the instrument panel, breaking the visual line. The faux wood grain trim throughout uses a high-gloss finish, which the reviewer dislikes — she prefers open-pore texture that mimics real wood. The cargo area shows raw-cut hardboard edges beneath carpet, which she considers a cheap finish detail.

5. **Second Row Seat Criticisms:** The reviewer is critical of the second-row seats on multiple fronts. The plastic side shields on the second-row seats have sharp parting lines, a glossy low-quality texture, and are described as indistinguishable from what one might find in a $30,000 vehicle — she suspects the CAD geometry was borrowed from another vehicle. Despite the front seats offering heated, cooled, and massage functionality, the second-row seats are manually adjustable, which she argues is unacceptable at this price point, noting that power second-row seats have been implemented in vehicles costing less than half as much.

6. **Integrated Seatback Screens — Skepticism:** The Escalade IQ includes integrated video screens in the front seatbacks for second-row passengers. The reviewer dislikes this feature for several reasons: the screens are bespoke to the vehicle, making them expensive to replace (she estimates five times the cost of a consumer product like an iPad); they cannot be updated as display technology improves over the vehicle's lifespan; and their exposed position makes them vulnerable to damage, especially from children. She contrasts this with recessed seatback screen designs she has worked on previously, which trade screen size for better protection.

7. **Third Row and Seat Controls:** The third-row seats are fully powered and can be folded and raised from rear cargo area controls. However, the second-row seats — despite having a power release — cannot be raised from the rear controls, only folded forward. The reviewer flags this as an inconsistency in the seat control logic.

8. **Design Details — Positives:** The reviewer highlights several design choices she appreciates. Chrome accents are used sparingly and with a textured finish that gives a jewelry-like quality at no additional manufacturing cost (the texture is cut directly into the injection mold tool). Door pocket edges use a generous radius rather than sharp parting lines typical of low-cost vehicles. The glass panoramic roof, while fixed (non-opening, non-shaded), is tinted. The historical Cadillac crest with marletes (heraldic ducks) is preserved on the instrument panel, even though simplified crest versions without marletes are used on all other badges throughout the vehicle.

## Market & Competition

1. **Cadillac's Historical Identity vs. Present Execution:** The reviewer contextualizes her criticism within Cadillac's heritage as a brand that was once "the standard of the world," historically first with its own engines and technology. She argues that the Escalade IQ represents a meaningful return to originality for the Escalade nameplate as an EV, but that some execution details undermine the premium positioning the brand commands.
