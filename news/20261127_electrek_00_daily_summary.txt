Electrek's two Tesla pieces today cover chip supply and Cybertruck pricing: a supply-chain report on why AI5 is going to Optimus before cars, and used Cybertruck prices rising after Tesla announced the end of production.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **AI5 going to Optimus may be a supply issue:** Fred Lambert reports that Musk said cars will remain at AI4, which he called enough to solve superhuman driving, and that AI5 is going to Optimus. Citing a Commercial Times report, Electrek says design partner Global Unichip is giving its limited 2027 TSMC 3nm capacity to Google and Microsoft data center chips first, leaving Tesla a small allocation. Per the report, that volume goes first to Optimus, with large-scale adoption in cars only after N3 capacity expands in 2028. TSMC's second Arizona fab (which Musk has said will produce AI5) is scheduled for the second half of 2027, and Samsung expects volume production of Tesla's AI chips at its Taylor, Texas fab in the second half of 2027. Electrek notes Tesla plans AI5 in Optimus around April 2027 and external Optimus deliveries in late 2027. Lambert argues the timing points to rationing rather than choice, citing Tesla's memory cuts and AI4's doubled cost, and compares "AI4 is enough" to the earlier HW3 story. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/27/tesla-ai5-optimus-real-reason-tsmc-3nm/)

## Electric Vehicles
2. **Used Cybertruck prices climb after production end:** Electrek reports used Cybertruck prices are up 10 to 15% in the month since Tesla announced the end of production. CarGurus puts the average at $89,715 (up 9.84% over 30 days, while the broader used market fell 1.37%). Visor shows a like-for-like rise of 15% and an average asking price of $101,784 across 171 trucks. After Musk's November 6 announcement, Tesla raised prices, stopped selling every Cybertruck except the Cyberbeast, and this week raised it to $114,990. One New Jersey dealer asks $150,790 for a used Cyberbeast, which CarGurus flags as overpriced. Lambert argues these are asking prices, that Tesla's removal of cheaper trims is pushing the middle of the market up, and that the end of parts and service support could weigh on used values later. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/27/used-cybertruck-prices-jump-after-tesla-kills-it/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's view is skeptical of Tesla's framing: Lambert reads the AI5 decision as chip rationing dressed up as a choice, and treats the used Cybertruck price spike as dealer price-testing and discontinuation hype rather than real demand, advising buyers not to overpay.
