ArcBest (ARCB) logistics company is purchasing two Tesla Semi electric trucks for its ABF Freight fleet following a 2025 pilot program that achieved 1.55 kWh/mile efficiency over 4,494 miles — a roughly 9% improvement over earlier figures from Tesla, DHL, and Saia.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Pilot-to-purchase conversion:** ABF Freight's 2025 Tesla Semi pilot ran over three weeks, averaging 321 miles/day on routes between Reno, NV and Sacramento, CA, including Bay Area and rail shuttle operations. The two newly purchased Semis will primarily support linehaul operations within California, with planned expansion to Reno and potentially additional locations. ABF Freight president Matt Godfrey stated the trucks will be evaluated across more lanes and operating conditions using the same TCO, efficiency, safety, and employee experience benchmarks applied to its diesel fleet.

2. **Efficiency comparison:** ArcBest's 1.55 kWh/mile result compares favorably to earlier figures: Tesla's own 2022 claim of 1.7 kWh/mile, DHL's 1.72 kWh/mile, and Saia's 1.73 kWh/mile. Driver feedback from the pilot highlighted visibility, comfort, and performance on challenging grades including Donner Pass (7,200-foot climb).

3. **Production and order momentum:** The first Tesla Semi from the new dedicated high-volume production line adjacent to Gigafactory Nevada rolled off in late April 2026. Notable recent orders include WattEV's 370-truck order (~$100M, described as the largest single electric truck deployment in California) and a combined 60-truck order from two port drayage operators through Forum Mobility. DHL, CEVA Logistics, and others have also been expanding Tesla Semi testing.

## Market & Competition
4. **Competitive range landscape:** The article notes the Freightliner eCascadia tops out at ~230 miles (550 kWh battery) and the Volvo VNR Electric at ~275 miles (564 kWh), contrasting with Tesla Semi's ~500+ mile range from its 822 kWh battery pack at ArcBest's demonstrated efficiency levels.

## Editorial Stance
The author views ArcBest's move from pilot to purchase as a maturity signal for electric trucking, noting that applying standard TCO benchmarks — rather than treating EVs as "science projects" — is meaningful validation. The author flags Tesla's production ramp as the key remaining uncertainty, stating little has been heard about ramp progress since production began.
