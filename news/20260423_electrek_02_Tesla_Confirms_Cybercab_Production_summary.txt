Tesla has confirmed that Cybercab production has officially begun at Giga Texas, with Elon Musk announcing the start during the Q1 2026 earnings call. However, the vehicle is being produced before Tesla has solved unsupervised autonomous driving.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab production confirmed underway:** Musk confirmed on the Q1 2026 earnings call that "We have just started production of Cybercab," warning that initial output will be slow before ramping "kind of exponential towards the end of the year" due to a new supply chain. The first unit rolled off the line in February, but continuous production only began in April 2026. Tesla is building both a steering-wheel-less version and a steering-wheel-equipped variant.

2. **NHTSA cap does not apply to Cybercab:** VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy confirmed the Cybercab is not subject to NHTSA's 2,500-unit annual production cap for autonomous vehicles. Tesla designed the Cybercab to comply with all existing FMVSS standards through the standard self-certification process — the same used by conventional automakers — requiring no regulatory exemption. Drone footage has confirmed federal compliance stickers on production units. This makes the ongoing Congressional debate over the SELF DRIVE Act (which would raise the cap to 90,000 units) irrelevant to Tesla's Cybercab scaling plans.

3. **Leadership exodus from Cybercab program:** Three senior leaders have departed since February: vehicle program manager Victor Nechita, OTA and ride-hailing infrastructure director Thomas Dmytryk (after 11 years), and assembly leader Mark Lupkey. Tesla reportedly has no original program managers remaining on any of its production vehicles.

## Autonomous Driving
4. **Unsupervised FSD timeline and current safety gap:** Musk said unsupervised FSD would reach customer vehicles "probably Q4" of this year (2026), though the article notes Tesla's FSD timeline predictions have historically been off by years. Tesla's current supervised robotaxi fleet crashes at roughly four times the rate of human drivers — one crash per 57,000 miles versus a human benchmark of one crash per 229,000 miles. Musk acknowledged software issues including cars getting "scared to move" or stuck in infinite loops.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that production and certification milestones are secondary concerns — the critical unsolved problem is autonomy. He contends that every Cybercab produced without functional unsupervised driving is an "expensive paperweight," that Waymo is years ahead on autonomy, and that until the software problem is solved, Cybercabs will only be used in small-scale geofenced pilot projects.
