The article reports that Dutch Tesla-only leasing company Mistergreen is reportedly facing bankruptcy after betting its business on Elon Musk's claim that Teslas would become "appreciating assets" via future robotaxi revenue, a bet the company lost as Tesla vehicles depreciated faster than the industry average.

## Market & Competition
1. **Mistergreen's collapse:** According to reports from Europe, Dutch Tesla-only car rental firm Mistergreen has wiped out its bondholders and is selling off its operations; it reportedly has been unable to repay bonds issued to buy its Tesla fleet since last year, with investor losses described as in the tens of millions of dollars (amount unconfirmed). A group of Dutch, Belgian, and German leasing companies will reportedly purchase the remaining fleet.
2. **Business model rationale:** Mistergreen's 2022 annual report stated its fleet strategy was based on the belief that Tesla's hardware and software were "prepared for future self-driving cars" and that this would support better residual values; at its peak the company's fleet exceeded 4,000 Tesla vehicles.
3. **Musk's "appreciating asset" claim:** The article recalls Elon Musk's 2019 statement that buying a Tesla meant buying "an appreciating asset – not a depreciating asset," and that he suggested a Model 3 could be worth $100,000–$200,000 as a robotaxi. The article says this prediction has not materialized, with Tesla vehicles depreciating faster than the industry average, which it attributes partly to Tesla's own price cuts and the lack of delivered full autonomy.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's editorial take (Fred Lambert) argues Mistergreen's failure illustrates the danger of relying on Musk's unfulfilled robotaxi/FSD promises, noting Musk has promised "1 million robotaxis by the end of the year" since 2020 while, as of late 2025, Tesla only has a small supervised pilot program in a geofenced area of Texas. The piece frames betting capital on near-term consumer-vehicle-to-robotaxi conversion as financially reckless, and the top reader comment (by "Tom D," 42 likes) echoes that business owners who believed the claim showed poor judgment given that cars mechanically depreciate.
