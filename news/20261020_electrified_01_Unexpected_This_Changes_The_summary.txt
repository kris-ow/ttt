This Electrified update covers the Terafab construction site's rapid buildout, new technical detail on the Tesla Roadster's drivetrain and COPV refill costs, the Tesla annual meeting proxy filing (no merger vote included), a fresh ARK Invest 2031 Tesla price-target report, a California FSD class-action ruling in Tesla's favor, Elon Musk's plan to gift SpaceX shares to children via Trump accounts, and updated Robotaxi registration numbers in Texas.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab site expansion visible from drone footage:** A video from Joe Tegtmeyer showed major progress at Terafab (the Tesla/SpaceX compute site), with land clearing, groundwork, and concrete foundations now visible; the host notes the site has expanded significantly since clearing began in early August, with tree removal and building demolition ongoing to widen the footprint.
2. **Merger timeline: no vote at December 3rd AGM:** Tesla's newly filed annual meeting proxy contains only three items — reelecting Elon Musk, Robin Denholm, and James Stephenson, voting on 2025 executive compensation, and ratifying PwC as auditor — with nothing on the Tesla-SpaceX merger. The host now believes the AGM will not include a merger vote, and any special shareholder vote is most likely to occur in 2027, though an 8-K announcement could come at any time; Barclays' Dan Levy is cited (without special insider knowledge, per the host) suggesting an announcement could come within 6–12 months if not tied to the AGM.
3. **Deal-closing precedent suggests a long process:** Comparing the three largest market-cap mergers of the past decade — AT&T/Time Warner (116 days to vote, 600 days total), Paramount/Warner Bros Discovery (55 days to vote, 221 days total), and BMS/Celgene (99 days to vote, 321 days total) — the host argues a combined Tesla-SpaceX entity (estimated around $4 trillion) would dwarf these deals by roughly an order of magnitude in inflation-adjusted terms, implying an unusually long and complex process; he estimates 1–2 years from announcement to close.
4. **Executive cross-following and lockup expiry add intrigue:** The host notes SpaceX and Tesla executives are increasingly following each other on social media (e.g., Gwynne Shotwell following Tesla's main account, Michael Truell following Lars Moravy), and flags that the last tranche of non-Musk SpaceX locked-up shares is set to release around December 8th, which could make SpaceX reluctant to announce the merger before Q1 2027 to avoid added volatility.
5. **Musk plans to gift SpaceX shares to American children:** Elon Musk said he plans to give one SpaceX share to every kid in America next year via the Invest America/Trump accounts program, calling it "the first of many." Brad Gerstner called it potentially the largest philanthropic gift in history, citing roughly 70 million eligible accounts (yielding estimates of $10–15 billion based on share count times SpaceX's share price); the host flags skepticism about Gerstner's "largest in history" characterization.
6. **SpaceX TAM commentary tied to compute demand:** Elon Musk said SpaceX's previously stated $28.5 trillion total addressable market (from the IPO prospectus) was an underestimate and is "vastly higher," which the host attributes largely to compute-leasing agreements and rising rates for frontier compute access.

## Financials
1. **ARK Invest's new 2031 Tesla price targets:** ARK's base case projects a $4,400 share price by 2031 (implying roughly a 62% CAGR and a $17.5 trillion market cap), with a bear case of $13.4 trillion and a bull case of $20.4 trillion market cap. The host contrasts this with ARK's 2021 targets (split-adjusted: ~$1,000 base, ~$500 bear, ~$1,333 bull for 2025), noting Tesla's actual 2025 average share price was about $360 and year-end price about $485 — meaning Tesla missed even ARK's old bear case on average pricing.
2. **ARK model assumptions on robotaxi and Optimus:** ARK's base case assumes Tesla cumulatively deploys 8 million robotaxis by 2031, with Tesla's operating cost per mile seen as about 40% lower than Waymo's next-generation robotaxi. ARK also now models external Optimus sales scaling to 1 million units generating about $200 billion in robot-as-a-service revenue by 2031 — which the host notes is far more conservative than Musk's own guidance of 100 million to 1 billion Optimus units by 2031.
3. **ARK's AI infrastructure and merger assumptions:** ARK assumes Tesla scales AI infrastructure spending from about $7 billion cumulative as of December 2025 to about $225 billion by end of 2031. ARK explicitly excluded Tesla's China robotaxi operations, Tesla Semi, FSD licensing, and the Tesla-SpaceX merger from its model, but stated a merger could occur within the next five years, potentially funding SpaceX's orbital data centers via robotaxi cash flow while SpaceX's AI models could support Optimus planning.
4. **Market close:** TSLA closed the day at $368.60 (not the garbled figure stated in the transcript), up 3.76%; SpaceX closed at $147.96, up 1.32%, versus the Nasdaq-100 up 1.17%.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas robotaxi registrations climb:** Tesla added 34 new Model Y vehicles to its registered robotaxi fleet in Texas, bringing the total to 803 registered vehicles, with 254 added in October alone; robotaxi operating hours have reportedly expanded to 6:00 a.m. to midnight.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Appeals court tosses FSD class action certification:** A US appeals court threw out an order that would have allowed California Tesla owners to pursue a class action alleging Tesla misled them about FSD capabilities, ruling there were too many individualized questions about what each buyer saw before purchase. This is a procedural win for Tesla against class-action treatment, but does not rule on the underlying merits, and individual owners can still sue separately; the case remains pending.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster drivetrain and performance claims:** Tesla's new Roadster drive unit and high-voltage battery deliver 1 megawatt of power — about one-third more power than the Model S Plaid — at half the mass, using new 4680 cells with a new chemistry and improved thermal management; the host argues this represents a genuine step-change in power-to-weight ratio (not just the addition of fans/thrusters) and expects some of this technology to trickle down to other Tesla models over time.
2. **COPV nitrogen refill cost pushback:** Responding to claims refills would cost thousands of dollars, the host calculates the rocket version's 600L compressed nitrogen tank (holding roughly 250 kg of nitrogen at about $11/kg) would cost only about $28 to refill, and likely under $100 even after tripling for margins; the production version may move to an onboard air-compression system rather than external nitrogen refills, which could be more complex and costly to engineer.
3. **Roadster speed and stunt claims:** Elon Musk said the Roadster could reach a peak speed over Mach 0.5 (roughly 400 mph), floating potential future demos like flying over the Snake River Canyon or Grand Canyon and landing on its wheels; the host notes the Grand Canyon's narrow points (~600 ft) and wide ones make such a jump impractical even with the car's thruster technology.
4. **Paint shop removal signals volume strategy:** Tesla's removal of paint shops for the Semi and Cybercab lines signals plans for high-volume production of those vehicles, whereas keeping a more limited approach for the low-volume Roadster matters less.
