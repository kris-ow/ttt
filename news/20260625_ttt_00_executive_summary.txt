## Autonomous Driving
- **NHTSA removes manual brake pedal requirement for AV-only vehicles.** The agency framed the update as necessary for America to lead "the greatest technological revolution in vehicle tech since the Model T." Braking performance standards are preserved; only the physical control method is deregulated. A 30-day public comment period opens before DOT finalizes the rule. For Tesla, this reduces the risk that NHTSA later challenges Cybercab's existing FMVSS self-certification.

- **UN adopts first global autonomous vehicle framework.** Backed by the US, EU, China, Japan, Canada, and UK, the UNECE framework mandates safety management systems, credible testing, and continuous in-service monitoring — but is hardware-agnostic, with no lidar or radar requirement. One notable clause states the framework is written with the expectation that ADS software does not include online in-vehicle learning that self-modifies system behavior; Tesla's current model (offboard training, static OTA pushes) is compliant today. The framework is not binding until individual countries incorporate it into national law.

- **FSD 14 rolls out to Australia, New Zealand, and the Model Y L.** The Model Y L activation marks the first time that variant has accessed Full Self-Driving anywhere in the world.

- **FSD 14.3.4 sideswipe avoidance clip circulates.** An owner reported the system briefly crossed a double yellow line to avoid a lane-drifting vehicle, with no oncoming traffic present, and described the maneuver as "superhuman driving skill."

- **FSD China test fleet at 200 vehicles, targeting 600.** Each vehicle averages 280 km/day (~173 miles) and the fleet uploads approximately 1.7 TB of data daily, housed on Tesla's Phase 2 Shanghai infrastructure to satisfy local data rules. OTA approval timelines have stretched from 2 weeks to 60 days, constraining update cadence. Tesla has partnered with BYD for mapping. FSD 14 will be limited to AI4 hardware; broader approval is targeted for Q3 2026.

- **Texas fatal crash attributed to driver, not FSD.** Tesla's telemetry — cited publicly by Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy the same day — showed the accelerator pedal was fully depressed throughout the incident, including after impact. Multiple sources note FSD's documented failure mode is hesitation or stopping, not uncontrolled acceleration.

- **Two FSD 14.3.4 edge-case failures flagged by community.** In one clip, the system drove over a fallen gas can without reacting; in another, it prepared to proceed through a lowered railroad crossing arm. Tesla's Yun Tasai acknowledged the gas-can clip and forwarded it to the engineering team.

## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab cumulative production estimated above 1,000 units per Bhakdi.** He reports visual sightings of 100+ units at Giga Texas (Austin), 50+ in Houston, 50+ in Dallas, and additional vehicles in Los Angeles, Phoenix, and the Bay Area. He projects production will exceed 1,000 units in July alone, putting cumulative output at roughly 2,000 by end of July.

- **Cybercabs can be converted to pedal/wheel-free configuration on-site, in hours.** Per Bradford Ferguson, the conversion can be performed in a small tent at any staging lot, suggesting Tesla could rapidly reconfigure staged inventory for deployment without returning units to a factory.

- **Tesla posts Senior Analyst, Robotaxi Logistics role in Austin.** Responsibilities include owning final-mile logistics for North American robotaxi deployments, optimizing routes, selecting carriers, and managing rail partners and distribution hub workflows — a concrete signal of scaling deployment preparation.

- **Zoox reveals updated mass-production robotaxi design, claims up to 100 units/week capacity.** The design includes seating and interior refinements. For context, 100 units/week is a modest rate relative to Tesla's stated manufacturing ambitions.

## Energy
- **Tesla–Nat Power Megapack partnership: 25 GWh Phase 1 across Italy and the UK, ~$5B in revenue.** The full buildout could exceed 100 GWh and generate up to $15B over 20 years. Hosts note the previous largest single-source battery storage installation was reportedly 12.3 GWh, making Phase 1 alone more than twice that scale. Tesla's Lathrop factory has ~40 GWh annual capacity; a planned Houston factory is targeted at 50 GWh.

- **Tesla signs LOI for 2.8 GWh Green Turtle Megablock project in Belgium.** Tesla and Giga Storage will build one of Europe's largest battery storage projects in Limburg, with Tesla handling full EPC, battery supply, commissioning, and long-term maintenance using its latest-generation Megablock technology. Final contracts are expected in summer 2026. Sawyer Merritt estimates project value at approximately $700M. This is the first Megablock deal announced at gigawatt-hour scale.

- **Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home virtual power plant aggregates 16+ GWh of distributed capacity.** The system draws on hundreds of thousands of Powerwalls, EVs, and 8 million+ smart thermostats, operating primarily through demand-response (deferred charging and thermostat adjustment) rather than vehicle-to-grid discharge. The Virginia data center corridor alone reportedly has 300 MW available for immediate use. The partnership projects up to $170B in US electricity bill savings over the next decade.

- **Q2 energy deployment could be a significant catch-up quarter.** Per Brian (FutureAzA), Q1 came in below expectations due to pipeline timing rather than demand softness. He sees potential for 25–30 GWh deployed in a single quarter, though he does not commit to Q2 specifically.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Giga Berlin targets 7,500 units/week by October 2026 — approaching full capacity.** A second wave of 1,000 hires follows an April announcement of 1,000 workers targeting ~6,000 units/week by end of June. At 7,500 units/week × 50 weeks, annual output reaches ~375,000 units, matching Tesla's stated Model Y capacity for the plant — the first time Tesla has planned to utilize near-full Berlin capacity, roughly five years after opening. Total new jobs announced in recent weeks: 3,500, representing a 30%+ headcount increase from a base of ~11,000. The host estimates the incremental 125,000 annual units add roughly $6.2B in revenue and $1.2B+ in gross profit at ~20% margins.

- **Giga Berlin battery cell expansion: 18 GWh of 4680 capacity targeted for 2027, adding 1,500 jobs.** This is a separate hiring tranche from the production headcount expansion announced this week.

- **Cybertruck earns IIHS Top Safety Pick+ for 2026 — the only pickup truck to do so.** It avoided every pedestrian collision scenario tested: daytime crossing child, nighttime crossing adult, and nighttime parallel adult. The Ford F-150 and Ram 1500 rated "very poor" in the moderate overlap front test.

- **Tesla Vision airbag system enables deployment decisions up to 70 milliseconds earlier.** Exterior cameras identify the severity and timing of an imminent collision before impact sensors trigger alone, and the improvement rolls out over-the-air to existing vehicles.

- **Model Y L US launch reportedly imminent, with deliveries potentially as early as August 2026.** Per the host and a cited Verge report, the vehicle will be built at Giga Texas. The variant features a longer body, larger battery, and more interior space.

- **Polestar banned from selling new vehicles in the US.** The Commerce Department's BIS denied clearance under the connected vehicle rule barring Chinese or Russian software and hardware in connectivity systems. Existing Polestar 3 and 4 inventory may sell through; 2027 model year vehicles will not be authorized. Volvo (also Geely-owned) received a waiver in May 2026.

- **Tesla ranked #1 and #2 most American-made vehicles for the sixth consecutive year.** The Model Y and Model 3 hold the top two spots in the cars.com 2026 rankings across all vehicle types.

- **European demand rebound accelerating at Giga Berlin.** After a ~27% sales decline last year, Tesla EU registrations rose 67% year-over-year in April and are reportedly more than doubling in June. Battery-electric vehicles reached 19.7% of new EU registrations through April, up from 15.3% a year prior. France, Sweden, Denmark, and Ireland all posted triple-digit percentage increases in April.

- **Slate Auto announces sub-$25K EV truck for Q4 2026.** The pickup starts at $24,950 with 205-mile range, a 63 kWh battery, and 0–60 mph in 8.0 seconds. Tesla has no directly competing product in that price range.
