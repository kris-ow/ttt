The host (back after a personal hiatus) recaps the fast-moving Nvidia/Jensen Huang scandal and resignation, then pivots to its potential relevance for SpaceX/Tesla given Elon Musk's and Nvidia's close partnership, before covering a cluster of Tesla Robotaxi, Starlink, Starship, SpaceX compute, Cybertruck, FSD regulatory, and Tesla–SpaceX merger updates.

## Market & Competition
1. **Jensen Huang resigns amid scandal:** Nvidia announced Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO, and board member effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress (referred to by the host with a garbled name) serving as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. The host traces a rapid timeline: reports of a Nvidia executive (referred to by the host as "Alice Wong") allegedly being a Chinese intelligence asset who grew close to Huang; Huang publicly admitting to an extramarital affair with her; a second alleged affair with another Nvidia VP who reported directly to him; a securities class action filed against Nvidia and Huang; an independent board investigation led by a law firm; a House Select Committee on the CCP chair threatening subpoenas; and, per the host's citation of reporting, a possible third affair. The host stresses these remain largely allegations without confirmed facts, and argues the speed and decisiveness of the board's action suggests there is more to the story than has been publicly disclosed — though this is the host's own inference, not a confirmed fact.
2. **Potential Huang–Musk connection floated:** After Zero Hedge suggested Musk "hire Jensen," Musk replied it "would be an honor." The host notes SpaceX and Nvidia engineering teams are already co-developing a new server rack (referenced as "Starmind") designed for use in space and on Earth, and speculates — explicitly calling it speculation, not a confirmed plan — that Huang could eventually be involved with a SpaceX/Tesla-adjacent project such Terafab, pending clarity on the national-security allegations against him.

## Robotaxi
3. **24/7 Robotaxi operation launched:** Tesla's Robotaxi account announced the service is now running 24/7 in every city where it's available, which the host ties to prior comments from Ashok Elluswamy that a new piece of the V15 tech stack would enable round-the-clock operation.
4. **Fleet expansion to eight cities:** Tesla Robotaxi added Fort Worth and San Antonio, bringing the total to eight active cities. The host cites a Texas fleet count around 1,300+ vehicles with roughly 573 being Cybercabs (figures as stated in the video; some digits may be imprecise due to transcript rendering). Population coverage is now about 4.53 million versus Waymo's roughly 9.68 million. Unsupervised miles have surpassed 5 million, described as up more than 13x since late July. The host notes it's been under ten months since unsupervised Robotaxi launched in Austin.

## SpaceX — Starlink / AI & Compute
5. **Starlink Mini 2 announced:** SpaceX opened invitation-only pre-orders for a Starlink Mini 2 featuring a built-in swappable battery (up to 4.5 hours), a thinner design, improved Wi-Fi, and a switch to USB-C; shipping is expected next month.
6. **Starlink infrastructure eyed for AI training:** Musk said Starlink's software for managing data movement in space is well-suited to training "super intelligence," and the host notes Grok's new training/reinforcement-learning stack reportedly incorporates breakthroughs from Starlink engineers — though Starlink is not currently being used to train AI models commercially; the host frames this as a long-term development.
7. **Next-gen Starlink V4 bandwidth plans:** Musk described a potential path to around 100 lasers per satellite via smaller laser size and reduced satellite spacing, potentially adding another order-of-magnitude increase in inter-satellite bandwidth.
8. **Large-scale solar ambitions:** Following news that SpaceX ordered equipment from Singulus (Germany) for 100 gigawatts/year of HJT solar manufacturing capacity, Musk said SpaceX is aiming for 1 terawatt/year of solar from Earth, scaling to over 1 petawatt/year from the Moon.
9. **New SpaceX supercomputer facilities confirmed:** SpaceX officially confirmed two new supercomputer facilities — one in Cochise County, Arizona (~$50 billion) and one near Starbase/Brownsville, Texas (~$24 billion) — together representing more than $74 billion in planned investment. The Brownsville site will generate its own electricity rather than draw from the local grid.
10. **Anthropic compute deal uncertainty:** Unconfirmed rumors suggest Anthropic might end its compute leasing arrangement with SpaceX by mid-next year. Musk responded he'd be "happy to take that compute back" but expects Anthropic to want more, not less, compute. A commentator (referred to generically) suggested SpaceX could end the Anthropic deal on 90 days' notice and re-lease at 2-3x pricing if demand tightens; Musk replied compute pricing works both ways and SpaceX revenue would drop if compute becomes abundant and cheap. The host flags a widely circulating claim that U.S. AI/data centers are "fully compromised by China" tied to the Huang affair as unsubstantiated speculation with no credible sourcing.

## SpaceX — Starship
11. **Flight 15 scheduled:** SpaceX is targeting a Starship test flight for November 13, with a launch window opening at 4:45pm Central. The plan includes catching both booster and ship (ship catch expected Saturday afternoon if on schedule), roughly 14 orbits over about 22 hours, and deployment of 26 Starlink V3 satellites.

## Electric Vehicles
12. **Cybertruck production extended, then to end:** Musk said Cybertruck production will be extended through the end of summer 2027 to work through backlog, while separately stating the line will shut down in about 8 months due to the opportunity cost of Cybertruck's production complexity versus using that capacity/engineering for Cybercab. The host pushes back strongly on Musk's framing that Tesla didn't anticipate Cybertruck's complexity, calling it "gaslighting," and notes Unplugged Performance offered to fund and run Cybertruck (plus a proposed three-row Cyber SUV and an autonomous cargo hauler) via an investor consortium at no cost to Tesla.

## Autonomous Driving
13. **FSD approved in Finland:** Tesla's assisted/autonomous driving software was approved in Finland, bringing the EU country count to 11 of the needed 15, with population coverage now at about 15.5% (needing over 65%).

## Financials
14. **Tesla–SpaceX merger talks reportedly progressing:** Citing Bloomberg reporting relayed by a commentator, the host says merger negotiations are progressing with a possible announcement as early as early December, though the same reporting notes the companies have also discussed a scenario where announcement slips into early 2027. Key sticking points cited include Tesla's China operations, SpaceX's U.S. defense contract sensitivities, and the number of shares SpaceX would offer Tesla shareholders. The host also walks back an earlier comparison to a prior merger scenario where both companies' stocks dropped roughly 35%, clarifying that short-selling arbitrage alone would not guarantee a drop of that magnitude.
