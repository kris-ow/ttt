Tesla released a video showing its Semi electric truck charging at a peak of 1.2 MW (1,206 kW), offering the first clear look at the "Megacharger" performance needed to support long-haul electric trucking.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi hits 1.2 MW peak charging:** Tesla's official Semi account posted a video of engineers monitoring a charging session where power output peaked at 1.2 MW. A top comment on the article notes that around the 0:08 mark in the video, the truck's state of charge appears to be at 11%. The video did not show additional details such as how long the peak rate was sustained.
2. **Consistent with V4 Charging Cabinet specs:** The 1.2 MW figure matches capabilities Tesla announced earlier in 2025 for its V4 Supercharger cabinet architecture, which supports 400V-1000V vehicle architectures and can deliver up to 500 kW for cars (e.g., Cybertruck) and up to 1.2 MW for the Semi.
3. **Author's efficiency estimate:** The article calculates that at 1.2 MW, the truck would add roughly 20 kWh per minute, and — assuming an estimated ~850 kWh pack based on Tesla's touted 1.7 kWh/mile efficiency — theoretically could reach a 10-80% charge in under 45 minutes, provided the charging curve doesn't taper significantly. This is presented as the author's own estimate, not an official Tesla figure.
4. **Semi production ramp timeline:** Tesla is preparing volume production of the Semi at a new factory expansion near Gigafactory Nevada, targeting start of production in the first half of 2026 and ramp to volume production in the second half of 2026.
5. **Original performance target:** Tesla had previously claimed the Semi could charge 70% of its range in 30 minutes, for a truck with a 500-mile range and an estimated 800-900 kWh battery pack — a very high power requirement compared to the 250-350 kW typical of passenger EV charging in North America.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that charging downtime, not torque or towing capability, has been the trucking industry's central skepticism about electric semis since Tesla unveiled the Semi in 2017; he says hitting 1.2 MW during a mandatory ~30-minute US driver break would meaningfully change the economics versus diesel. He notes 1.2 MW is extreme but not unprecedented (citing that some Chinese passenger EVs now charge near 1 MW), and raises the open question of how quickly Tesla can deploy Megachargers broadly, suggesting Tesla will likely lean on its own batteries at charging sites to manage grid demands from such high peak rates, since obtaining 1.2 MW utility connections at truck stops nationwide poses a major grid challenge.
