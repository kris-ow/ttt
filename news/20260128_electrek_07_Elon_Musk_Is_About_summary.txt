The article reports that the SEC has filed for summary judgment against Elon Musk, asking a federal judge to find him liable for violating securities disclosure laws during his 2022 Twitter acquisition, arguing the case is clear-cut enough to skip trial.

## Market & Competition
1. **SEC's strict liability argument:** The SEC contends Musk's state of mind is irrelevant — he crossed the 5% Twitter ownership threshold on March 14, 2022, triggering a mandatory 10-day disclosure window under Section 13(d), but didn't file until April 4. The SEC's filing states Musk's defense "incorrectly argues that Section 13(d) requires the SEC to prove state of mind," calling this unsupported by statute or precedent. Between the deadline and actual disclosure, Musk purchased $500 million worth of Twitter shares funded by Tesla stock sales.

2. **Financial exposure:** The SEC is seeking disgorgement of at least $150 million, which it says Musk saved by buying Twitter shares cheaply before disclosure caused the stock to surge over 27%. Beyond this, the agency is seeking civil money penalties and a permanent injunction barring Musk from future Section 13(d) violations, citing his 2018 "funding secured" settlement as evidence he is a repeat offender.

3. **Next steps:** If the judge grants summary judgment, the case moves directly to a remedies phase without trial; if the judge finds Musk's state-of-mind or constitutional defenses warrant further review, the case could proceed to a full trial. The SEC's filing also rebuts Musk's selective-enforcement claim, noting he hasn't identified anyone else who violated Section 13(d) without facing enforcement.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the SEC appears to have a strong legal case because filing-deadline violations are more objectively provable than fraud intent, and frames a potential large fine as a positive sign given what he characterizes as weak SEC enforcement generally during the Trump administration — while noting this particular case predates that administration.
