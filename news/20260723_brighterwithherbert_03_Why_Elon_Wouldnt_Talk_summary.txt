This video features host Eric and futurist/blogger Brian Wang (Next Big Future) dissecting specific, checkable milestones Tesla disclosed on its recent earnings call — covering the Terafab chip/memory facility, Optimus production ramp, Cybercab/robotaxi scaling, Tesla Semi autonomy, and AI5/AI6 chip timing. Wang frames these as concrete near-term commitments rather than vague long-term promises, while both speakers flag real uncertainty about pace, particularly for Optimus and Cybercab fleet scale-up.

## Financials
1. **Terafab treated as its own event:** Elon Musk reportedly said Terafab (the chip/memory manufacturing initiative) deserves "its own day in the spotlight" rather than being folded into the earnings call, and said it will get a location announcement soon. Wang speculates (his own guess, not confirmed) that Terafab's dedicated announcement may be timed around a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger vote process, which he guesses could see a SpaceX earnings call announcement around early August, a Terafab presentation shortly after, and a shareholder vote around November — all explicitly presented as Wang's speculation, not stated Tesla/SpaceX plans.
2. **Micron memory supply deal:** On the call, Musk reportedly thanked Micron for a multi-year supply commitment at what he called a reasonable price, despite Micron being able to charge premium prices elsewhere given industry demand. Wang argues memory (from Micron, and reportedly also SK Hynix) is currently the binding bottleneck for deploying Nvidia Reuben/Blackwell-class chips and Tesla's own hardware, since chips are unusable at scale without sufficient high-bandwidth memory.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab as core Tesla–SpaceX merger synergy:** Wang argues Terafab is the "golden example" synergy case for why Tesla and SpaceX might merge, since scaling chip/memory production 10x–100x benefits both companies' AI and compute needs — though the specific merger timeline he offers (early August SpaceX call, September Terafab event, November vote) is explicitly his own guess, not a confirmed date.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production lines starting at Fremont:** Tesla shared photos of its first-generation Optimus production line, now occupying floor space previously used for Model S/X manufacturing at Fremont; Musk called the line "stunning to see." Wang notes this likely represents dozens of sub-assembly lines (component/actuator lines) feeding a final assembly line, with a full public reveal ("Tesla day"-style tour) possibly coming later.
2. **Training-first strategy, not customer sales yet:** Both speakers argue Tesla's near-term Optimus units (Gen 3, in the thousands) are primarily being used for training/data collection — sent to a university and factories, with select employees possibly testing units at home — rather than sold commercially, aiming for one-shot/generalized learning rather than task-specific programming. Wang cautions the public may be overestimating how soon Optimus becomes broadly capable, calling the pace "a lot slower than you think," with a mass-market (10-million-unit-scale) version likely "several years" away.
3. **FSD vision stack reused for Optimus/computer control:** A Tesla executive (per the host, referencing Ashok Elluswamy) reportedly noted that Tesla's FSD-derived vision system is being repurposed both for Optimus's physical navigation and for "digital Optimus"/computer-use control, processing large volumes of visual frames to interpret screens and make decisions.
4. **AI5 chip volume production and generalized learning:** Tesla confirmed on the call that AI5 chips are on track for volume production around the middle of next year, with Musk expressing strong confidence in the chip team and describing AI6 as also on track. Wang argues AI5-level local compute and memory will make Optimus/robotaxi hardware desirable on its own merits (as an edge AI device), independent of near-term functional capability, and that Tesla's vertical control of its own chip and memory supply is a structural advantage over humanoid competitors like Figure that lack an in-house supply chain.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab production timed to robotaxi rollout pace:** Tesla said Cybercab production will be matched to robotaxi service needs; FSD version 15 has reportedly driven with zero incidents over roughly 38,000 miles, which Wang argues supports scaling toward statewide (not just city-level) robotaxi deployment, potentially in Texas and Florida. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active Robotaxi fleet stands at 788 vehicles as of 2026-07-23 (Bay Area 643, Austin 106, Dallas 20, Houston 19).
2. **Diverging year-end fleet estimates:** Wang estimates only a ~20% chance of a "big number" (20,000–40,000 Cybercabs) by year-end, expecting a small number without a phase change, while the host maintains an independent estimate of 10,000–15,000 Cybercabs in robotaxi service by year-end; both agree the ramp could slip several months if it depends on large year-end production/delivery batches.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi autonomy and volume timing:** Tesla said Semi volume production is on track but not until next year, with self-driving capability targeted for around the end of this year or early next year; Musk said Model 3, Model Y, and Cybercab are being prioritized for FSD before Semi. Wang argues the economics of autonomous "robo-trucking" (which he estimates at roughly $500,000/year value versus roughly $50,000/year in fuel savings alone) are strong enough that Tesla could profitably deploy trucks itself even without third-party buyers, similar to how SpaceX Wang says launched the large majority of its own satellites.
