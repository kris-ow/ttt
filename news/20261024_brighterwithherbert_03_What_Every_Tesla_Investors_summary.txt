This video is a shareholder-advocacy discussion between two Tesla/SpaceX retail investors (Amy and Alexandra Merz) hosted by Herbert, urging Tesla shareholders to sign a petition/letter to Tesla's board supporting a Tesla–SpaceX merger and to prepare to vote in favor when the time comes. The core thesis: a merger would combine complementary cash flows and a "full stack" AI/vertical-integration platform, and retail shareholder turnout could be decisive given historically high non-voting and invalid-vote rates.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Merger process update:** Per the speakers, Elon Musk stated during a SpaceX earnings call that two special committees (one at Tesla, one at SpaceX) have been established to evaluate a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger; no agreement has been confirmed yet, and Musk reportedly said it's unclear whether the committees will reach a full agreement.
2. **Shareholder letter campaign:** The two guests are running a shareholder petition (hosted on SurveyMonkey, linked via Alexandra Merz's pinned X post) asking signers to state their Tesla share count and whether they are also SpaceX investors, which will be printed and mailed to Tesla's board; organizers reported roughly 3,800 signers representing about 11 million shares within 24 hours of launch, with a plan to deliver it to the board within days.
3. **Merger rationale argued by speakers:** Amy and Alexandra argue the merger makes sense because Tesla and SpaceX have each built "half of the AI stack," pointing to joint ventures like Terafab (which Amy says could become several times larger than Nvidia, targeting roughly 1,000 gigawatts of annual compute capacity versus Nvidia's current 20–30 gigawatts) and Macrohard (aiming to replace enterprise software). They frame full merger as the next step to eliminate friction between these temporary JV structures and to combine the two companies' cash flows for cheaper financing.
4. **Exchange ratio and price speculation (no confirmed terms):** There is no set price or exchange ratio yet. Alexandra Merz said she personally hopes for an exchange ratio around 4.2 SpaceX shares per Tesla share and floated a minimum Tesla share price target of $690 as an ambitious personal wish, not a confirmed figure. Amy said she has heard an estimate around a 3:1 ratio, and separately cited investor Brad Gerstner as having suggested a price range of roughly $450–550 per share — both described as outside estimates, not finalized terms.
5. **SpaceX revenue context cited in the discussion:** Amy noted SpaceX's reported guidance of roughly $35–37 billion in revenue for the first quarter of 2027, along with new businesses such roughly 800,000 signups for a Grok-related bot product and growing supercomputing/compute-rental revenue, as factors SpaceX's side may use to argue for a higher valuation in merger negotiations.
6. **Process timeline outlined by Alexandra Merz:** Expected sequence: special committees reach a conclusion → joint Tesla/SpaceX press release announcing the merger → both companies file Form 8-K within about four days (including a link to the merger agreement) → a detailed proxy statement (potentially hundreds of pages, including fairness opinions) follows weeks later → roadshows to build institutional and retail support → a shareholder vote requiring more than 50% of outstanding shares, which Alexandra estimates could occur in the first quarter of 2027 "or maybe later."
7. **Retail voter mobilization concern:** Alexandra said that historically 15–20% of Tesla shareholders don't vote and another 10–15% cast invalid votes, with all such non-votes effectively counting against the board's position — meaning retail turnout is framed as critical to reaching the 50% threshold needed for merger approval.
8. **Speculative combined valuation:** Amy cited Tesla's standalone incentive-plan-implied valuation of $8.5 trillion and a $7.5 trillion figure for SpaceX, and speculated that a combined entity could be valued as high as $100 trillion, explicitly describing this as an unknown upper bound rather than a projection with a stated basis.
9. **Consequences if no merger occurs:** Amy argued that if the merger talks fail, SpaceX would likely reduce reliance on Tesla and build more capabilities in-house, diminishing Tesla's role in SpaceX's business and signaling reduced mutual support between the companies — her own framing/analogy rather than a stated company position.
