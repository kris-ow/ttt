This video is a conversation between Brian (FutureAzA) and Will from Tesla Jigsaw discussing the current state of FSD approval in the UK, the regulatory hurdles involved, and what UK Tesla owners can expect and when. The core argument is that the UK remains significantly behind other markets in approving supervised FSD due to a combination of post-Brexit regulatory complexity, strict type approval requirements, and Tesla's apparent prioritization of EU momentum first. The hosts are cautiously optimistic that, whenever FSD does arrive in the UK, it will be a far more refined product than early adopters in other markets had to endure.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **UK FSD subscription currently delivers only Enhanced Autopilot features:** Will tested the FSD subscription in the UK and found it provides only summon, navigate on autopilot, and lane changes — functionally equivalent to Enhanced Autopilot. Critically, these features are hobbled by UK-specific regulation, including "system-initiated maneuvers" that require the driver to touch the steering wheel within 3 seconds during a lane change or the system aborts the maneuver. Will found this dangerous and frustrating, and canceled his subscription. He emphasized this is not Tesla's fault but the result of regulatory constraints.

2. **Tesla has not yet formally submitted a type approval application to the VCA:** Will explained that there is no public confirmation that Tesla has formally submitted an FSD type approval application to the UK's Vehicle Certification Agency (VCA). He believes Tesla is likely prioritizing EU momentum first, using the Dutch RDW approval as the foundation, before tackling the separate post-Brexit GB type approval process. UK-specific requirements include testing on British roads, roundabouts, and narrow lanes.

3. **Tesla has been collecting UK road data for a long time:** Will noted that Tesla has been gathering shadow mode data from UK drivers using Autopilot, and Tesla employees have been testing the system on UK roads for over a year. He referenced a video of Tesla's system navigating Swindon's Magic Roundabout well over a year ago, and a Tesla-released video of a vehicle driving autonomously around London. He argued Tesla has substantial data; the remaining challenge is formal regulatory compliance demonstration.

4. **Supervised FSD timeline for the UK is late 2026 or early 2027 at best:** Will assessed the most optimistic realistic timeline for supervised FSD reaching UK customer vehicles as late 2026 or early 2027. He described the UK as likely being "the last domino to fall" among markets receiving FSD, which he found deeply frustrating given that FSD is already rolling out across multiple EU countries (Belgium, Denmark, Lithuania, Estonia) and beyond.

5. **UK automated vehicles safety principles consultation began June 17, 2026:** Will shared that the UK Department for Transport launched a consultation on draft safety principles for automated vehicles on June 17, 2026, with a closing date of September 9, 2026. He described this as a small but genuine positive signal, as these principles will feed into permanent UK rules covering supervised FSD, unsupervised FSD, and eventually robotaxi operations. Brian framed the meetings and consultations as necessary hurdles on the path to approval.

6. **Australia and New Zealand received FSD version 14 on June 19, 2026:** Brian noted that Australia and New Zealand — both right-hand drive markets — received FSD version 14 on June 19, 2026. He speculated that data gathered from right-hand drive operation in those markets may be transferable and beneficial for the UK approval process. Will agreed that by the time FSD reaches the UK, it could be version 14 or potentially much later versions, meaning UK users will receive a dramatically more mature product.

7. **FSD versions 13 and above described as transformatively better:** Both hosts agreed that FSD version 13 and above represents a qualitative leap that most UK users don't appreciate. Will described it as "silky smooth" and "utterly boring because it does the job so well." Brian shared his own experience struggling to make FSD videos because the system handles challenges without incident, and noted that on version 14 he no longer feels urgency to test new updates because the system simply works. Will's personal FSD experience was on a Cybertruck running version 12.5.5 (outside the UK).

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo is testing in London under the Autonomous Vehicles Act 2024:** Will explained that Waymo is able to test on London roads because the UK's Autonomous Vehicles Act of 2024 permits commercial robotaxi and autonomous vehicle testing. He noted that Waymo's stated goal (as of Will's last knowledge) was to offer actual public robotaxi rides in London by September 2026. He clarified this has no direct bearing on FSD approval, since Waymo operates at Level 4+ autonomy whereas FSD is a Level 2 supervised system subject to different regulatory rules.

2. **Tesla could theoretically deploy Cybercabs in London under existing AV law:** Will observed that because the Autonomous Vehicles Act of 2024 covers Level 4 and above autonomy, Tesla could in theory place Cybercabs in London for testing under the same framework Waymo is using. The regulatory bottleneck specific to UK customers is the Level 2 supervised FSD product in customer-owned vehicles, which requires separate type approval.

3. **UK-based AV company Wave also testing vision-only system:** Will mentioned a UK company called Wave that operates a vision-only, AI-based autonomous system using a bolt-on unit fitted to existing cars. Wave's longer-term goal is to have their technology integrated directly into manufacturers' vehicles. Will described them as an interesting company to watch alongside Waymo in the UK AV landscape.

4. **Competing automakers' autonomy claims met with skepticism:** Brian expressed strong skepticism toward other automakers claiming they will catch up to Tesla in autonomy "next year," arguing that those manufacturers underestimate the challenge, have far less real-world training data, and cannot substitute simulation for real-world miles. He said the idea that best-in-class AI engineers alone will close the gap misunderstands the scope of the problem.
