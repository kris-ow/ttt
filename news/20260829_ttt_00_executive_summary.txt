## Brief

- Cybercab launch event set for September 3rd in Austin
- Starbase Louisiana announced, $100B planned investment
- Robotaxi service now runs 6am–10pm, 7 days a week
- Cybertruck prices rise up to 7%, dual-motor now $74,990
- Jo Bhakdi reaffirms 10,000 Cybercabs by year-end call
- 4680 battery hit ~25% of Tesla's cost-reduction target per analyst

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Robotaxi account confirmed expanded 6am–10pm daily hours on August 26, plus an upgraded dispatch/routing system and a larger unsupervised fleet; a community tracker cited ~908 vehicles, above RobotaxiTracker.com's stale 839 figure from August 7.
- Cybercab launch event confirmed for September 3rd in Austin per Sawyer Merritt, expected to formally add the steering-wheel-free vehicle to the service; Tesla VP Lars Moravy says no 2,500-unit/year cap applies since Tesla self-certifies the vehicle.
- Cybercab validation/mapping testing now spans 88 cities across 24 states (32 added in the prior 20 days), with a vehicle filmed in Midtown Manhattan; Tesla is hiring AI safety operators in 36 U.S. cities and began hiring vehicle operators in Frankfurt this week.
- Jo Bhakdi reaffirmed his call of 10,000 Cybercabs live by year-end, projecting growth to 60,000 by end of 2027.
- Texas robotaxi registrations sit around 270 vehicles (all Model Y, no Cybercabs yet), with a ~40% one-day jump reported recently; analyst Cern Basher modeled paid autonomous miles could reach roughly 630,000–3.5 million currently depending on assumed weekly growth rate off Tesla's disclosed 380,000-mile Q2 base.
- Nevada's robotaxi permit covers up to 5,000 vehicles across Clark County; Vegas Loop approved for 123 stations (still FSD-with-safety-driver, not yet fully driverless).

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD 14.3.8 released this week; FSD V14 Lite extended to HW3 owners in Australia and New Zealand. Tesla's Summer Update adds a "Preferred routes" feature prioritizing previously-driven navigation routes.

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck prices rose up to 7% by trim, with the dual-motor variant up $5,000 to $74,990 — following two years of price cuts, read as a possible demand signal though tariffs/input costs are an alternative explanation.
- Model Y L customer test drives began at select U.S. showrooms ahead of deliveries; Tesla discontinued the white interior option on European Model Y Premium trims in favor of a new "Zen Grey" interior.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus factory at Giga Texas progressing rapidly from dirt pad to steel structure and roofing, per drone footage.
- Investor Jason Calacanis said, after viewing the newest Optimus hardware, he believes it is roughly 1.5 generations ahead of top Chinese robots on hardware and about five generations ahead on intelligence — an enthusiastic, non-independent claim.

## Energy
- Tesla Semi rollout event scheduled for September 24th at a factory next to Giga Nevada, designed for a target rate of ~50,000 trucks/year at full ramp.
- Analyst Jordan Giesige argues the 4680 battery's real priority order was strategic supply-chain risk first, cost second, performance third; strategic risk is "mostly" addressed, cost reduction is roughly 25% of the way to Tesla's ultimate target, and performance is on par with — not ahead of — competing high-nickel cells, with bigger gains expected from a future third-generation cell.
- Forum Mobility began installing Tesla Megacharger MCS units at its flagship Port of Long Beach truck depot.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX announced Starbase Louisiana in Vermilion Parish: a $100 billion planned investment, 10 launch pads, over a dozen towers, a ~7.5M sq ft Starfactory, and a ~195-square-mile site. Construction starts next year, first launch targeted 2029, with a long-term goal of 30+ flights/day and a stated full-build target of 10,000 launches/year.
- Louisiana officials expect 3,000 direct jobs; Musk reportedly said the real figure will be much higher, unconfirmed.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX acquired EchoStar spectrum for roughly $19.5 billion (65 MHz nationwide) to enable direct-to-cell phone connectivity.
- Research firm Moffett Nathanson flagged multiple technical and operational hurdles for SpaceX's direct-to-cell "small cell" ambitions, arguing the business case hinges heavily on securing an MVNO deal; the core challenge is bridging the power/size gap between tiny phone antennas and distant, fast-moving satellites.
- Starlink launched "Personal Maritime," a new $185/month plan for small boat owners with unlimited data in territorial waters and no 30-day travel limit.
- Starlink remains unavailable in Nepal and China and not yet live in India; pricing varies sharply by country (e.g., ~$25/month in Nigeria due to a local cash-export cap).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX and Nvidia are designing a space-based version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin compute system; the first "Starmind" AI satellite targets Q4 2027 (larger deployment in 2028), described as ~70 meters tip-to-tip with ~150 kW solar generation sustaining ~120 kW of continuous compute.
- Musk reportedly told SpaceX employees AI revenue could exceed everything else the company does combined — an internal claim, not independently verified.
- Analyst Cern Basher argued SpaceX's data center business alone could generate $300–500 billion in annual revenue if it scales to 5–10 GW of compute, comparable to Nvidia's current ~$400 billion annual revenue.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- JPMorgan reiterated an overweight rating on SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) this week with a $240 price target.
