Tesla has quietly removed Basic Autopilot from its Dutch configurator, making the Netherlands the first European market where buyers must pay for FSD (Supervised) to access any active driver-assistance features beyond GSR2-mandated emergency lane-keeping.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Netherlands becomes FSD-subscription-only market:** Tesla's Dutch configurator now offers only FSD (Supervised) — at €99/month, a €7,500 one-time purchase, or "Add later" — with no Basic Autopilot option at any price. The change mirrors Tesla's January 2026 move in North America, where Autosteer was paywalled behind the FSD subscription, leaving new vehicles with only Traffic-Aware Cruise Control as standard. The Netherlands was the first European country to grant FSD Supervised type approval, via Dutch RDW under UN R-171, in approximately mid-April 2026, enabling this rollout.

2. **Broader European rollout likely imminent:** Other EU markets (Germany, France) still include Basic Autopilot as standard ("Inklusive" on Tesla's German configurator, with Enhanced Autopilot available for €3,800). However, Tesla has set a May 21, 2026 deadline for one-time FSD purchases across other European markets, signaling an imminent shift to subscription-only. Flanders is described as already moving toward FSD recognition.

## Market & Competition
3. **Competitive disadvantage on standard features:** EU GSR2 regulations (in force since July 2024) require emergency lane-keeping on all new cars, but competitors including Toyota, Hyundai, and Volkswagen include active lane-centering as standard on vehicles priced below a Model 3. A base Model 3 in the Netherlands starts at €36,990 and now ships with only the GSR2 safety nudge — no Autosteer — unless buyers pay €99/month. Comparable vehicles like the VW ID.4 and Hyundai Ioniq 5 include those features standard at similar price points.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek views this as Tesla deliberately padding FSD's perceived value by moving standard features behind a paywall rather than delivering genuine self-driving capability. The author is skeptical the strategy will succeed in Europe, where alternatives with standard driver assistance are widely available and the backlash is expected to be stronger than in North America.
