This video features FutureAzA host Brian interviewing Quincy from Electric Era Charging, a Washington State company founded by former SpaceX engineers, about their newly announced "CoPower Platform" — a power capacity-as-a-service product targeting edge AI data centers. The core thesis is that battery-based energy storage, managed by sophisticated software, can dramatically reduce electricity costs for edge data centers while simultaneously alleviating strain on the power grid through load shifting. The video explores the technical, economic, and national security dimensions of battery storage as a foundational technology for the AI-driven energy future.

## Energy

1. **CoPower Platform Announcement:** Electric Era is publicly announcing its CoPower Platform, described as a "power capacity as a service" offering for edge AI data centers. The product has been in stealth development for approximately six to seven months. It integrates multiple third-party hardware vendors — battery systems, power conversion systems, medium voltage switchgear, and transformers — all managed by Electric Era's proprietary software platform originally developed for their EV fast charging business.

2. **Physical System Configuration:** A representative deployment shown during the video features approximately six energy storage systems, each with roughly 5 megawatt hours of capacity and approximately 2 megawatts of discharge power. This yields a total of approximately 30 megawatt hours and roughly 12 megawatts of deliverable power to a data center at any given time.

3. **Load Shifting as the Core Value Proposition:** The platform's primary economic mechanism is load shifting — charging batteries during low-demand, low-cost periods (typically mornings) and discharging during peak demand periods (typically 4–9 p.m.). This reduces both the energy cost (cost per electron) and the infrastructure/demand charge component of the data center's electricity bill, which Quincy describes as saving "a shocking amount of money."

4. **Autonomous Software-Driven Grid Optimization:** The software platform autonomously monitors a variety of grid price signals throughout the day and programmatically decides how to charge and discharge the storage systems to optimize costs. This is described as an extension of the same software Electric Era already deploys across the country for EV fast charging, simply applied to a larger load with bigger hardware.

5. **Edge Data Centers as the Target Market:** Electric Era is explicitly not targeting large hyperscale AI superfactories, which run at full load continuously and require on-site generation (solar, natural gas, or nuclear) rather than load shifting. Instead, the focus is on smaller edge data centers running real-time, low-latency workloads — such as AI call centers, ordering kiosks, or autonomous vehicle inference systems — which have a diurnal demand shape aligned with human activity patterns, making time-of-use arbitrage viable.

6. **Voltage Flicker Mitigation as a Secondary Use Case:** Beyond load shifting, Quincy explains that battery systems also act as power quality filters. AI data centers training large models can cause "voltage flicker" on the local grid — rapid voltage swings caused by large numbers of GPUs switching on and off synchronously. Battery storage can absorb and smooth these fluctuations before they propagate to the broader electrical service territory, benefiting all ratepayers.

7. **Grid Scale Context and Long-Term Industry Outlook:** The U.S. currently has approximately 1 terawatt of grid capacity but averages only about 500 gigawatts of consumption. Quincy argues that battery storage allows more of that existing capacity to be utilized by raising the average load closer to the peak. He projects that the industry will need to deploy terawatt-hours of energy storage over the next dozen or so years, calling it a "major major shift" in how the grid operates.

8. **Battery Price Trends:** Battery pack prices have declined dramatically over recent years. When Quincy first began building battery-backed power infrastructure, pack prices were approximately $200 or more per kilowatt hour. Today, cell-level prices are approximately $70 per kilowatt hour, with pack-level prices running roughly 20–30% higher. A lithium price spike occurred around November 2022, followed by a sharp decline, and prices are now described as stabilizing and beginning to rise again in a more sustainable fashion.

9. **LG Energy as Selected Vendor; Domestic Manufacturing Emphasis:** Electric Era has selected LG Energy as its battery vendor for the CoPower Platform, noting that LG Energy produces batteries in America. Quincy frames domestic manufacturing as important both for job creation and supply chain resilience, citing lessons from the COVID-19 pandemic and the Ukraine war as examples of how apparently secure global supply chains can rapidly fail under pressure. Brian echoes this, framing energy independence as a matter of national security.

10. **Shared Technology Stack Across EV Charging and Data Centers:** Quincy emphasizes that the software "secret sauce" powering the CoPower Platform is the same platform developed for Electric Era's EV fast charging network, already deployed across the country. The company views these two product lines as increasingly intertwined and hints at future announcements as the two applications converge further technically.
