## Robotaxi
- **Austin geofence more than doubles.** Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi operating area in Austin expanded to more than double its prior coverage, per Sawyer Merritt — a direct driver of higher utilization and accelerated vehicle transitions to unsupervised status.
- **Unsupervised vehicles now operating on highways.** Per Cern Basher and Phil Beisel, unsupervised robotaxis are running highway segments within the Austin geofence — notable given Tesla's historical caution at speed.
- **Ashok framed today as a turning point.** Cern Basher cited a public post from Tesla's Ashok describing unsupervised Austin service as "robotaxi the way it was intended starts in earnest today."
- **Operational frontier is now the passenger experience.** Phil Beisel argues the safety box is "largely checked"; remaining challenges are pickup/dropoff precision, dynamic routing, and in-vehicle communication — gaps he frames as Grok voice integration's job to close.
- **Grok voice on AI4 hardware: Phil Beisel gives 20% odds.** He argues all Grok inference currently runs in the cloud, cannot borrow FSD compute cycles without degrading safety performance, and will likely require the AI5 chip for full integration.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD V14.3.3 rolling out.** The update improves driver monitoring system sensitivity — specifically eye gaze tracking, eyewear handling, and accuracy in variable lighting. Incremental release, but directly relevant to the supervised-to-unsupervised regulatory pathway.
- **Lemonade offers up to 50% insurance discount for FSD use in Indiana.** The insurer explicitly cites FSD as "twice as safe," framing the discount as a direct actuarial pass-through — the first major commercial validation of Tesla's FSD safety data narrative. Program is Indiana-only for now but signals potential multi-state expansion.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Germany: +322% YoY in May 2026.** Tesla registered 5,111 vehicles, per Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority — a standout result in a difficult European market.
- **Japan: #3 luxury brand in May.** Tesla registered 1,996 vehicles (+182% YoY), passing Audi. Year-to-date registrations of 8,194 (+157% YoY) put Japan on pace for Tesla's best year ever there.
- **Australia: Model Y was the #1 selling vehicle of any kind in May.** Tesla hit a record 20% EV market share — the first time an EV has led overall monthly sales in Australia.
- **China wholesale +39% YoY in May** — highest figure of 2026 so far, per analyst Roland Percher's data cited by Herbert Ong and FutureAzA. Hosts note the rebound predates the recent rise in gas prices.
- **Used Cybertruck program launched June 1.** Tesla began selling used Foundation Series units directly at $66,200 with $2,500 nationwide shipping; inventory sold out almost immediately, reinforcing that price has been the primary adoption barrier.
- **Reuters rumor: new sub-Model-Y SUV.** Four anonymous sources describe a ~14-foot vehicle, cheaper than Model 3's $37,000 starting price, built in China. Herbert Ong and FutureAzA are skeptical — Giga Shanghai is reportedly at capacity, a clean-sheet platform is unlikely, and Elon's own recent quote ("something way cooler than a minivan, but smaller than a Model Y") doesn't map cleanly to the Reuters description.

## Energy
- **SpaceX purchased $269M in Tesla Megapacks in April 2026.** Combined with $430M purchased by SpaceX/xAI in 2025, cumulative known Megapack purchases from this single related-party buyer total approximately $699M, per Sawyer Merritt citing SpaceX's new filing.

## Market & Competition
- **SpaceX IPO officially priced at $135/share, $1.75T valuation.** The offering targets $74.4B in proceeds (~555.55M shares), rising to ~$85.7B with overallotment, per Sawyer Merritt. IPO date targeted around June 12, 2026.
- **SpaceX S-1A acquisition language fuels merger debate.** New language authorizing equity issuance "in connection with future transactions" — combined with 36 billion authorized shares vs. ~12.86 billion outstanding — is being read by Jo Bhakdi, Alexandra Merz, and the brighterwithherbert analysis as a deliberate signal that a Tesla acquisition is being prepared, not boilerplate.
- **Merz merger timeline: announcement by August 15, 2026.** Her reasoning ties to Musk's 2018 option exercise deadline (adding ~300M Tesla shares at a $23 strike, ~$7B cost), favorable regulatory environment under the current administration, and a shareholder vote timed to the November 2026 annual meeting. She estimates a single combined ticker by Q2 2027.
- **Bhakdi and Merz converge on a ratchet structure.** Both favor a one-to-one market cap offer with a guaranteed floor per Tesla share, which would lock in Tesla's price at announcement and de-risk the shareholder vote; Bhakdi notes Musk has "no incentive of driving that merger ratio in any way" given marginal impact on his voting control.
- **Bhakdi's 12-month directional scenario: Tesla ~$3T, SpaceX ~$1.5T.** He argues Tesla will appreciate significantly on robotaxi and Optimus catalysts while SpaceX faces a lag in justifying its multiple post-IPO — making Tesla the larger entity by market cap and complicating a simple one-to-one offer.
- **Index rule changes engineered to support SpaceX.** Nasdaq allows top-40 market cap companies to join the Nasdaq 100 after 15 trading days (effective May 1, 2026); S&P 500 consulting on cutting its seasoning window from 12 to 6 months; FTSE Russell proposing eligibility after 5 trading days. Merz frames these as deliberately timed to stack forced passive inflows ahead of a merger announcement.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Jensen Huang's capability chart places "Tesla vision tech" in the top-right quadrant** — highest capability, highest compute — alongside world foundation models. Cern Basher and Phil Beisel interpret this as external validation that Tesla's data-driven approach is the leading edge of real-world robotics training.
- **Phil Beisel frames Optimus as the world's most important continuous visual data collector.** His argument: current LLMs are trained on compressed text; Optimus at scale will generate the continuous real-world observation data needed to build genuinely rich world foundation models.
