Tesla has pushed the planned public demo of its next-generation Roadster to August or later, according to The Information, citing four sources with knowledge of the program. The delay marks at least the eighth timeline slip since the prototype was first unveiled in November 2017.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Demo delayed to August or later:** Tesla's planned Texas demo event for the Roadster has been pushed to August or later, per The Information. Previous targets included April 1, 2026 (set by Musk at the November 2025 shareholder meeting), then late May or early June, then "maybe in a month or so" during the Q1 2026 earnings call. The prototype was originally unveiled in November 2017 with a promised 2020 production start; production has since been pushed to 2027–2028.

2. **SpaceX cold gas thruster system ("A71") is the focus:** The demo is intended to showcase a cold gas thruster system developed in collaboration with SpaceX, internally codenamed "A71." Tesla and SpaceX employees demonstrated the A71 system to Musk in late April 2026 — an internal milestone that helps explain the continued public delay. The system is meant to dramatically improve acceleration, cornering, and braking, and Musk has claimed a 0–60 mph time of 1.1 seconds and the ability to briefly lift off the ground.

3. **Two Roadster variants planned:** Tesla plans to produce a limited-edition SpaceX version featuring the thruster system — which replaces the rear seats with roughly 10 cold-air rocket thrusters — alongside a "scaled down" version of the vehicle.

4. **Reservation holders still waiting:** Early reservation holders paid $50,000 (or $250,000 for the Founders Series) beginning in 2017 and have yet to receive a production vehicle. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's publicly reported attempt to cancel his reservation in October 2025 was thwarted when the reservation email address was found to be inactive. Competitors including Rimac, Lotus, and Xiaomi have shipped high-performance EVs in the interim.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert expresses exhaustion at the repeated delays and skepticism that a production vehicle is imminent, noting that real engineering work and a shipping product are "very different things." He calls the possibility that the Roadster's near-decade delay was driven by a desire for a hovering SpaceX edition "the dumbest thing I've ever heard," arguing it advances neither EVs nor Tesla's core mission.
