Host Herbert and guest analyst Cern Basher (CFA, Brilliant Advice) review Tesla's Q3 production/delivery beat, break down the energy storage business and Robotaxi fleet math from Texas registration data, and close with a SpaceX compute buildout projection and its implied GDP and revenue impact, based on comments from Elon Musk and Jensen Huang at a Washington DC event.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Q3 deliveries beat fears:** Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3 against analyst estimates as low as roughly 446,000; production was 464,000 (lower than expected). Basher notes a ship with several thousand vehicles was still approaching Europe when the quarter closed, suggesting deliveries could have been even higher (possibly near 490,000) with better timing.
2. **Historical context:** Q3 production was the fourth-highest quarterly production total ever (the record, 494,000, was set in Q4 2023); Q3 deliveries were the third-highest ever, just under the record of 497,999 set a year earlier during the final quarter of EV tax incentive eligibility. Tesla crossed 10 million cumulative vehicles produced and delivered sometime during the quarter, a milestone the company did not publicize.
3. **Year-over-year growth:** Tesla has delivered about 106,000 more vehicles through three quarters this year versus the same period last year (up almost 9%), despite losing the tax credit; Q2 alone was up 96,000 YoY. Basher notes Tesla needs just 311,000 Q4 deliveries to beat last year, and 483,000 to make this the best delivery year in company history.
4. **FSD cited as a demand driver:** Both Basher and the host point to a surge of social-media testimonials (e.g., accounts like "no safe words" on X) as evidence that Full Self-Driving is becoming a major, non-linear demand driver for Tesla, independent of EV-vs-gas preference; Basher expects this effect to accelerate as public perception builds.
5. **Inventory at historic lows:** Days-of-supply fell to about 11 days; accounting for roughly three weeks of average global transport time, Basher estimates true underlying inventory is effectively negative (~minus 7 days), indicating Tesla may be losing time-sensitive customers due to insufficient on-lot stock. He flags that production constraints — including battery pack issues raised on the prior earnings call — need resolution for Q4 to hit strong numbers.

## Energy
1. **Energy storage deployment:** Tesla deployed 13.7 GWh of energy storage in Q3 (third-highest quarter, below Wall Street expectations of roughly 15 GWh); trailing-12-month deployment hit a record 50.2 GWh, with cumulative deployment surpassing 145 GWh. Basher attributes quarter-to-quarter lumpiness partly to grid interconnection delays — Megapacks can sit deployed for months before being "turned on" and counted.
2. **Capacity buildout:** Tesla's stated annual storage manufacturing capacity is about 66 GWh (roughly 6 GW Powerwall, ~40 GW Megapack domestically, ~20 GW in China); a new Megapack facility near Houston is expected to add about 50 GWh/year of capacity, which Basher says should lift deployment once ramped.
3. **"EV-equivalent" framing of energy business:** Basher converts Powerwall/Megapack deployments into 75kWh vehicle-equivalents, showing a record trailing-12-month figure of 669,333 "vehicle equivalents," more than doubling over the past two years (up from 314,000 two years ago). Combined with actual vehicle deliveries, Tesla's trailing-12-month "total vehicle equivalent" output reached a record 2.412 million units.
4. **Supercharger network growth:** The Supercharger network delivered 2.4 TWh of energy in the quarter, a notable step-up from the prior quarter, which Basher attributes to both a larger Tesla fleet and growing use by non-Tesla EVs on the network.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas fleet registrations:** 578 Robotaxi vehicles are now registered in Texas (420 Model Ys and 158 Cybercabs) — this is a different, more current figure than RobotaxiTracker.com's stale Austin-area numbers, and is attributed directly to Basher's own Texas registration tracking.
2. **Paid autonomous miles trajectory:** Per Tesla's prior earnings call, Ashok Elluswamy stated the company had completed 380,000 cumulative paid autonomous miles and was growing at double-digit week-over-week rates (commonly assumed to be 10%); at the Cybercab launch event, he said cumulative paid autonomous miles had reached about one million, which Basher calculates implies roughly 17% week-over-week growth since the earnings call.
3. **Fleet-size modeling:** Using the 17% weekly growth assumption and an estimate of ~100 miles/day per vehicle, Basher models that Tesla currently needs a fleet of roughly 386–450 vehicles to sustain that growth rate — consistent with the 578 registered — but would need roughly 1,400–1,500 vehicles by late November and about 3,000 by end of December to keep pace. He frames this as a speculative extrapolation, not official guidance, and notes the next real data point should come on the Q3 earnings call (expected October 21).
4. **Comparison to Waymo:** The host notes Tesla's Robotaxi fleet, even scaled to the low thousands, would remain smaller than Waymo's active fleet (cited at 4,000), but argues reaching that range would still mark a meaningful inflection point for market perception.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Musk/Huang GDP framework:** In an interview with Gavin Baker in Washington DC, Elon Musk said each 1% increase in US power usage (~5 GW) could yield roughly a 1% increase in national GDP, driven by rising "useful intelligence per watt"; he said 10 GW of added power could drive about a 2% GDP increase. Jensen Huang reportedly corroborated this, estimating each gigawatt of AI power generates approximately $50–60 billion in economic output per year.
2. **SpaceX compute growth projection:** Citing a projection from a third-party analyst (Brett Winton/ARC-style estimate referenced by Basher), SpaceX is projected to grow from about 1.1 GW of active compute at the end of 2025 to roughly 2.1 GW this year, 7.5 GW in 2027, 13.1 GW in 2028, 17.5 GW (described as "closer to 10 than 5" in context) in 2029, 33 GW in 2030, and 67 GW by 2031 — a roughly 40x increase over the period. Musk reportedly said he would be disappointed if actual figures were this low, implying he expects higher.
3. **Implied GDP contribution:** Applying the Musk/Huang GDP-per-gigawatt framework to this compute growth path, Basher estimates SpaceX's data-center buildout alone could add about 40 basis points to US GDP in 2026, roughly 1.4% in 2027, 2.4% in 2028, and about 12% by 2031 (measured against this year's GDP base, not incremental year-over-year). This is Basher's own extrapolation, not a figure stated directly by Musk or Huang.
4. **Revenue implications:** Basher and the host discuss that SpaceX's compute capacity reportedly stood at about 1 GW in 2025 and is on track to reach about 2 GW by the end of this year; the host extrapolates this to roughly $100–120 billion in associated revenue and speculates about multi-trillion-dollar valuation implications if the growth path continues — framed explicitly as speculative, back-of-envelope reasoning by the host rather than a company-confirmed figure.
