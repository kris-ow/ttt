This episode recaps SpaceX's Starship Flight 15, in which the ship was caught by Mechazilla for the first time while the Super Heavy booster diverted to a controlled offshore water landing after an automated abort, and covers a cluster of Tesla Robotaxi/Cybercab, FSD regulatory, and Giga Berlin news from the same weekend.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Booster loss explained, ship catch succeeds:** SpaceX said 32 of 33 Raptor engines lit for the boost-back burn, but it ended early due to low fuel mass (root cause under investigation); the booster's autonomous guidance system then triggered an automated abort and diverted it to a successful offshore landing burn before its flight termination system was triggered post-landing. Ship SN42 was caught by Mechazilla after roughly 22.5 hours in orbit — the first successful ship catch.
2. **Heat shield and refurbishment:** Per SpaceX, only one of about 18,000 heat shield tiles was lost during flight (not officially confirmed, but the working assumption); Elon Musk inspected SN42 and said it "looks great," with the ship potentially reflyable within a few weeks, though full airplane-like zero-refurbishment reusability still requires fixes. Chopstick contact during the catch scraped some material off the ship's side, which the host characterizes as a relatively easy fix compared to the catch itself.
3. **Green flame cause and fixes:** Musk attributed the brief green flame during the booster's landing burn to burning copper from the regenerative cooling circuit as fuel depleted, causing the main combustion chamber to run oxygen-rich — a sign of slight engine underperformance. Fixes include improved Raptor engine performance/consistency (version 3.2) and flight software changes to cut off most engines about one second earlier (MECO) to preserve propellant margin.
4. **Starship V4 details:** Musk said the next-generation Starship V4 will be about 70 meters long (~40% longer), with over 60% more propellant load, 9 engines (three additional higher-thrust Raptor vacuum engines), propellant cost per flight dropping below $500,000, and payload exceeding 200 tons — versus a stated current ~$1 million fuel cost for roughly 100 tons to orbit once rapid reusability is achieved.
5. **Mass-to-orbit comparison and Musk's exchange with a critic:** A SpaceX figure (per the host, "Deleon") stated SpaceX has launched more mass to orbit this year than the entire continent of Europe has launched in human history. Musk responded to a critic (Matt Stoller) who argued SpaceX's progress is just capital/engineering applied at scale, countering that SpaceX delivers 85% of all Earth payload to orbit via Falcon while Blue Origin and Boeing/ULA, despite comparable or greater funding, have not matched that reliability — noting ULA's Vulcan rocket suffered an anomaly ~10 minutes into a flight over the same weekend, failing to deliver 40 Amazon LEO satellites to orbit.
6. **Next flight timing:** Some observers speculate Flight 16 could launch from Florida, unconfirmed by SpaceX; the host thinks a flight before year-end is possible but would not be surprised if it slips to January.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Samsung Austin fab expansion for Starlink chips:** Samsung plans to expand its Austin, Texas chip plant to produce the STM32V8 microcontroller used in Starlink satellites' laser communication system, targeting operations by 2030, with a $1.9 billion investment; Samsung said it cannot officially confirm individual customer contracts but that chances have grown the chips will be made at the Austin fab at the customer's request.

## Robotaxi
1. **Valuation already pricing in success:** Responding to a claim that "moderate Cybercab success is already priced into Tesla" at a roughly $1.6 trillion valuation, Musk replied "of course" — which the host interprets as Tesla still needing to earn that valuation, reiterating that even 10,000 robotaxis generating ~$30,000/year profit each would be only $300 million in profit, underscoring that much of the long-term upside is already reflected in the stock price.
2. **Miami geofence expansion:** The Robotaxi account announced expanded Miami coverage; population coverage is now about 5.1 million for Tesla versus about 9.7 million for Waymo.
3. **NHTSA safety report — three Tesla-at-fault incidents:** Of seven incidents in a recent NHTSA report (one in August, six in September), three were attributed to Tesla: a Model Y turning into a low-hanging chain, a Model Y driving over a pothole near a work zone at 29 mph (rider alleged injury, Tesla disputes), and a Cybercab driving over a raised parking lot edge, across a sidewalk, and down a curb — two of the three Tesla-at-fault incidents occurred in Dallas, one in Austin.
4. **Dallas Cybercab fleet and November launch:** Over 50 badged Cybercabs were spotted in Dallas, some with apparent Starlink integration; Tesla is reportedly set to launch Cybercab service in Dallas sometime in November.
5. **Personal Model Y robotaxi trials pulled forward:** Responding to a diamond-level Uber driver in Austin using FSD who said he'd rather let his Model Y work the robotaxi network, Musk said trials could start as soon as next month ("if things go well"). This is earlier than Tesla's prior guidance on an earnings call that customer-owned Model Y robotaxi network participation would begin around Q2 2027. The host cautions this remains conditional and likely to start as a limited pilot rather than scaled availability.
6. **Cybercab delivery/pricing and the MKBHD bet:** MKBHD said he's buying a Cybercab, with delivery selectable across any active Texas robotaxi geofence city; Musk said nationwide availability will follow regulatory approval but called the current monthly fee too high, saying it should instead match Tesla's FSD/connectivity pricing for other models (around $115/month). Musk also repeatedly engaged on social media pushing for MKBHD to honor a bet (shaving his head) tied to Tesla delivering a Cybercab to a customer next month; the host speculates Musk may be pushing the initiative partly to win this bet, since delivering to even one customer would satisfy it.
7. **Giga Berlin $3.3B Cybercab expansion:** Tesla will invest about $3.3 billion at Giga Berlin, including a new production facility in Grünheide that could make it Germany's largest auto plant by capacity, targeting production of up to 1 million Cybercabs per year. Tesla told Reuters it will submit a building permit application by year-end, with construction starting in the first half of 2027 and production starting in 2029; several hundred new jobs are sought for the initial phase, and Tesla's careers page already lists 59 Germany job postings for Cybercab. Production timing in 2029 would follow roughly two years after hoped-for EU supervised FSD approval in the coming months.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Dutch regulator approves updated FSD assisted-driving release for HW4+:** The RDW (Netherlands) approved a new Tesla assisted-driving update for Hardware 4+ vehicles that tightens driver-monitoring requirements — hands required on the wheel above 130 km/h (~80 mph), earlier warnings for inattentive drivers, and a full strike for inattentiveness during a maneuver — which the host frames as Tesla addressing regulator concerns ahead of broader FSD approval.

## Market & Competition
1. **Market reaction to Flight 15 and stock closes:** Both TSLA and SPCX traded down on the day following Flight 15 (TSLA closed around $379.74, down 2.69%; SPCX closed around $137.36, down 1.26%, against a Nasdaq-100 decline of about 2.67%), which the host attributes to substantial anticipated success already being priced into both stocks beforehand.
2. **Nvidia/Jensen Huang speculation dismissed:** The host declines to analyze swirling, often contradictory reports about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang (succession planning, a possible return as CEO, stock transfers, past workplace conduct allegations), calling the coverage largely unverified "theater" and saying more transparency from Nvidia's board is needed.
