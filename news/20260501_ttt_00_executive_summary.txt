## Robotaxi

- **Unsupervised fleet recently jumped from 17 to 26.** The rapid move is being watched closely; per Bhakdi, the fleet is growing at roughly 5 vehicles per day, making 100 unsupervised units a non-trivial near-term probability. The panel on brighterwithherbert treated the acceleration as meaningful even at low absolute numbers.
- **Cybercab on the network: weeks vs. months.** Jeff Lutz and Cern Basher expect the first Cybercab available to hail in Austin within weeks; Herbert Ong is more cautious at three-plus months, citing the vehicle's limited FSD testing history and the reputational stakes of any early incident with the iconic form factor. The panel expects Tesla to distribute early units internally or to a small trusted group before any broad launch.
- **Next cities imminent: Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas.** Florida cities may launch roughly simultaneously; Las Vegas is tied to a later F1 event window. Miami's F1 race weekend was flagged as a missed high-visibility opportunity.
- **Android app now live — "basic minimum."** The Tesla robotaxi app launched on Android approximately nine to ten months after iOS. Alexandra Merz described it as a necessary threshold crossed, not a growth catalyst, given thin vehicle supply and long wait times in Austin.
- **1,800 robotaxis is Bhakdi's forcing function.** He argues that milestone — not the Optimus reveal — is what compels analyst upgrades and drives the stock into a $600–$1,000 range, because it represents real cash flow rather than a demo event.
- **Waymo's unit economics structurally broken at scale; Cybercab the disruptor.** Per Cern Basher and Roberto, New Street Research analysis shows Waymo exhausts its premium-fare niche as fleet density rises, forcing lower revenue per vehicle while costs stay high. Tesla's projected $1.00–$1.50/mile fare (vs. Waymo's ~$5/mile) not only undercuts Waymo but potentially substitutes for personal car ownership, unlocking non-linear demand expansion. Ark Invest projects ~$0.70/mile.

## Autonomous Driving

- **10 billion cumulative FSD miles expected ~May 3, 2026.** Elon Musk has previously cited ~10 billion miles as the threshold for sufficient safety validation at scale. Extrapolating current pace, Cern Basher projects ~16.5 billion total FSD miles and ~6.5 billion city miles by year-end 2026.
- **FSD 14.3 shipped in April; remaining issues are operational, not safety-critical.** Per the earnings call cited by the brighterwithherbert panel, Musk characterized the outstanding items as convenience and edge-case behavior fixes — correct pickup/drop-off locations and awkward maneuvers — not fundamental safety gaps.
- **Cortex 2 training cluster coming online.** Part of Tesla's next-generation AI training supercomputer began spinning up in April, reportedly approaching ~50% capacity. Full ramp is ongoing.
- **FSD supervised approval in China rumored around a Trump presidential visit ~two weeks out.** Alexandra Merz explicitly flagged this as unconfirmed speculation, with Israel also cited as another market on deck for supervised FSD approval.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus Gen 3 reveal on a 90-day timeline from earnings call.** Bhakdi assigns high probability the reveal will not slip materially; he frames it as a soft catalyst (no immediate cash flow, no forced analyst upgrades) but a key long-term valuation driver — the differentiator between his $4,000 price target by 2030 and ARK's $2,900.
- **Optimus production line build-out begins after Model S/X line sunsets in May.** The SNX line shutdown frees factory space at Giga Texas; Jeff Lutz expects start-of-production more likely late Q3 or Q4 2026 rather than the July–August timeframe cited on the earnings call. Elon described the transition as a "herculean effort."
- **Competitive pressure intensifying.** Jeff Lutz noted 2026 is a convergence year for humanoid robots, with both Chinese and U.S. competitors targeting several thousand units this year. Alexandra Merz argued Tesla's intentional public silence is strategic given copycat risk from China.

## Energy

- **Tesla Semi Basecharger launched at $40,000; Megacharger priced at $188,000 for two posts.** The Basecharger (125 kW, 60% range in four hours) targets depot/overnight use; the Megacharger (1.2 MW, 60% range in 30 minutes) handles en-route fast charging. Both use the open MCS 3.2 standard — not Tesla-locked — meaning Daimler, Volvo, and Scania trucks could theoretically use the hardware. Basecharger deliveries begin early 2027; 66 Megacharger locations planned with the first open in Ontario, California.
- **Network fee set at $0.08/kWh** for revenue-generating sites — 20% below the $0.10/kWh charged on the Supercharger for Business program.
- **xAI Megapack revenue: $430M in 2025 plus $78M through February 2026.** Per Tesla's 10-K/A filing, Megapack sales to xAI's data centers represent a material and ongoing revenue stream. Tesla's Audit Committee reviewed all transactions, which were conducted at rates available to unaffiliated third parties.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Semi start of production confirmed or near-confirmed.** The brighterwithherbert panel cited a Tesla-affiliated account (Dan Priestley) using the phrase "start of production," drawing a parallel to the Cybercab's February first-unit milestone that was followed by official SOP roughly two months later.
- **SpaceX purchased 1,279 Cybertrucks in Q4 2025** — 18% of all U.S. Cybertruck registrations that quarter — at a time of weak consumer demand, per Tesla's 10-K/A. These purchases account for the bulk of Tesla's $143.3M in SpaceX vehicle revenue for 2025.
- **Tesla Model X fastest-selling used vehicle in the U.S. in March 2026.** Per an iSeeCars study cited by Sawyer Merritt, the Model X found a buyer in 26 days on average vs. roughly two months for a typical used car — supporting residual value assumptions and brand health.
- **Gas prices hit $4.30/gallon nationally; Los Angeles averaging $6.10/gallon.** Prices rose 7% in a single week, strengthening the total-cost-of-ownership case for EVs and improving robotaxi per-mile energy economics.
- **Model Y L deliveries begin in Australia.**

## Financials

- **Tesla 10-K/A discloses $573.4M in 2025 related-party revenue from Musk entities** — $430.1M from xAI (primarily Megapacks) and $143.3M from SpaceX (primarily Cybertrucks) — plus $24.8M in expenses paid to Musk-affiliated entities including xAI, SpaceX, X, The Boring Company, and a Musk security company. All transactions reviewed by Tesla's Audit Committee.
- **$2 billion xAI investment converted to SpaceX equity.** Tesla agreed in January 2026 to invest $2 billion in xAI Holdings' Series E; after SpaceX acquired xAI Holdings, Tesla's preferred stock converted to SpaceX Class A common stock. The investment completed March 12, 2026; Tesla's resulting stake is reportedly less than 1% of SpaceX. Electrek flags the conversion as a governance concern given the Audit Committee-approved investment ultimately landed in yet another Musk-controlled entity.
- **Elon's 2018 Tesla compensation (303M shares) converted from options to restricted stock.** All milestones were completed 2018–2022. Shares vest January 2028; Tesla intends a structured repurchase to cover his tax obligation without open-market selling. Elon must hold 55% of remaining shares until 2033. Alexandra Merz characterized media reports of imminent selling as misleading.
- **Q1 2026 energy segment missed; management described weakness as "lumpy."** A Q2 recovery is expected. Automotive and energy gross margins were otherwise characterized as solid, with a warranty true-down described as constructive.

## Market & Competition

- **Legacy auto Q1 results broadly poor; Tesla gaining EV market share.** Jeff Lutz characterized competitor EV results as "atrocious," with some Western European OEMs posting single-digit gross margins. He dismissed legacy automakers' hybrid pivot as an inefficient "sugar high."
- **Vanguard 13G filing was a restructuring, not a share sale.** Alexandra Merz clarified: Vanguard split into two entities; one filed showing 210M shares while the second (~40M shares) had not yet filed separately. Total Vanguard Tesla holdings are unchanged. The filing used a total share count of 3.75 billion, inclusive of all restricted share packages.
- **Tesla stock ~25% below its December 16, 2025 all-time high of $489, trading around $379** per Bhakdi. He characterizes Tesla as trading at roughly a 50% discount to intrinsic value in his model and maintains a $4,000 price target by 2030 (inclusive of Optimus, exclusive of SpaceX merger optionality).
