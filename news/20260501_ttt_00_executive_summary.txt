## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab production running ~12 units/day** per multiple sources, with hosts arguing this already exceeds Waymo's daily fleet addition rate. The production target trajectory is 100/day and eventually 1,000/day; many units are reportedly rolling off the line without steering wheels or pedals.
- **Cybercab on the hailing network is "imminent" but timing disputed.** Jeff Lutz and Cern Basher expect the first hailable Cybercab in Austin within weeks; Herbert Ong urges caution, citing the Cybercab's limited FSD testing history as a new form factor and the permanent brand-damage risk of any early public incident. Tesla is expected to distribute early units internally or to a small trusted group before any broader launch.
- **Next cities on deck: Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas.** The Florida cities may launch roughly simultaneously; Las Vegas is tied to a later F1 event as a target window. The panel debated whether deploying two or three cars constitutes a true "launch," suggesting Tesla may need new vocabulary for first-unit milestones vs. meaningful public service.
- **Bhakdi flags 1,800 active robotaxis as the key stock forcing function**, projecting a re-rating to $600–$1,000 once that threshold is crossed. He distinguishes this from softer catalysts like the Optimus reveal, which generates no immediate cash flow and therefore does not compel analyst upgrades. Fleet is growing at approximately five robotaxis per day per his observation.
- **Waymo's unit economics are structurally broken at scale** per Cern Basher and Roberto, citing New Street Research: higher fares per mile exhaust quickly as fleet size grows, leaving expensive vehicles idle — which is why Waymo keeps expanding to new cities rather than scaling deeply in any one market. Waymo Austin fleet is estimated at roughly 300 vehicles with no scalable pipeline to grow beyond that; the Jaguar I-Pace platform is out of production with no finalized successor.
- **Cybercab's cost structure reframes the entire market.** At a projected $1.00–$1.50/mile (vs. Waymo's ~$5/mile and Uber's ~$4/mile), Bhakdi and Cern argue ride-hailing becomes a substitute for personal vehicle ownership — unlocking non-linear demand expansion that eliminates the idle-fleet penalty constraining Waymo. Ark Invest projects ~$0.70/mile.
- **Tesla as "the new Uber":** Cern Basher and Roberto note Tesla is replicating Uber's aggregation model but with personal vehicle owners — offering owners payment for fleet access during peak demand — functioning as a platform operator rather than purely an asset owner. Robotaxi is also framed as a future mobile compute node platform, described as "increasingly appearing to be a significant advantage for Tesla."
- **FSD supervised approval in China rumored around a Trump visit** approximately two weeks from recording, with Elon potentially part of the delegation, per Alexandra Merz — explicitly flagged as unconfirmed. Israel cited as another market where FSD supervised approval is on deck.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD V14 Light coming to Hardware 3 vehicles in the US**, with international expansion to follow pending regulatory approvals. Tesla stated it cannot provide definitive dates. As of recording, the HW3 rollout had not yet occurred.
- **10 billion cumulative FSD miles expected to clock around May 3, 2026.** Extrapolating the current pace, Cern Basher projects ~16.5 billion total miles and ~6.5 billion city miles by year-end 2026. Elon has previously cited ~10 billion miles as the threshold for sufficient safety validation at scale, though the panel emphasizes safety improvements are continuous.
- **Cortex 2 training supercomputer is online and approaching ~50% capacity**, per the brighterwithherbert panel. Full ramp remains ongoing.
- **GM's 2028 full self-driving claim met with heavy skepticism** from the futureaza hosts, who noted every major automaker made equivalent claims targeting 2020 back in 2012–2016 and none delivered.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Building permits filed in Travis County for Optimus production facilities** at Tesla's northwest campus, reported by Joe Tegtmeyer. Hosts expressed surprise at the speed of site clearing and preparation.
- **Optimus Gen 3 reveal on a 90-day timeline per Tesla's earnings call**, flagged by Bhakdi as likely the first commercial version of Optimus. Bhakdi categorizes the reveal as a soft catalyst — bullish for retail sentiment but not a forcing function for analyst upgrades since it generates no immediate cash flow.
- **Optimus production line build-out begins after Model S/X line sunsets in May.** Per the brighterwithherbert panel, start of production is estimated July–August per the earnings call, with Jeff Lutz suggesting "end of summer" is more realistic and first units possibly not rolling off until late Q3 or Q4 2026. Elon described the factory transition as a "herculean effort."
- **Chinese and U.S. competitors both targeting several thousand humanoid units in 2026**, per Jeff Lutz. Alexandra Merz argued Tesla's strategy of showing less publicly is intentional given copycat risk from China, noting visual imitation can be manufactured even without functional parity.

## Financials
- **Tesla's amended 10-K/A (filed April 30) discloses $573.4M in 2025 revenue from Musk-affiliated entities:** $430.1M from xAI (primarily Megapack sales to power xAI data centers) and $143.3M from SpaceX (primarily Cybertruck purchases). An additional $78.1M in xAI Megapack revenue was recognized through February 2026 alone. Tesla paid $24.8M to Musk-affiliated entities across SpaceX, xAI, X, The Boring Company, and a Musk security firm.
- **SpaceX purchased 1,279 Cybertrucks in Q4 2025** — 18% of all U.S. Cybertruck registrations that quarter — at a time of weak consumer Cybertruck demand. These purchases account for the bulk of the $143.3M in SpaceX revenue.
- **Tesla's $2B xAI investment converted to SpaceX equity.** Agreed January 16, 2026; completed March 12, 2026; Tesla's resulting SpaceX stake is reportedly less than 1%. The filing reviewed by Electrek raises governance concerns given Musk controls counterparties on both sides of hundreds of millions in transactions.
- **Energy segment was a Q1 miss**, characterized by management as "lumpy" rather than structural, with a strong Q2 recovery expected. Q1 automotive gross margins were described as good, with some constructive one-time items including a warranty true-down.
- **Elon's 2018 Tesla compensation package** (303M shares) has been converted from options to restricted stock following Tesla's departure from Delaware jurisdiction, vesting January 2028 with a structured repurchase planned to cover tax obligations. Elon must hold 55% of remaining shares until 2033.

## Energy
- **Tesla launches Semi Charging for Business program** with two products: the Megacharger (1.2 MW, 60% range in 30 min, starting at $188,000 for two posts) for en-route fast charging, and the new Basecharger (125 kW, 60% range in 4 hours, starting at $40,000) for depot/overnight use. Both use the open MCS 3.2 standard, compatible with Daimler, Volvo, and Scania trucks.
- **Basecharger deliveries begin early 2027**; 66 Megacharger locations planned with the first public station open in Ontario, California. Revenue-generating sites pay $0.08/kWh — 20% less than Tesla's Supercharger for Business rate.
- **xAI Megapack revenue ($430M in 2025 + $78M through Feb 2026)** represents a significant ongoing related-party energy revenue stream. The filing states transactions were conducted at rates available to unaffiliated third parties.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Semi production has officially begun**, confirmed by footage from Zangler showing an active manufacturing floor. At a ~$300,000 landed cost, Tesla Semi undercuts legacy competitors Freightliner (eCascadia priced at $400K–$500K); Freightliner's own production reportedly dropped from ~1,000 units last year to a target of ~100 this year.
- **Model X is the fastest-selling used vehicle in the US**, finding a buyer in 26 days on average in March 2026 per iSeeCars — roughly half the time of a typical used car. Multiple Tesla models are selling quickly.
- **Model Y L is expanding internationally** — deliveries commenced in Australia and the model is now available in India. Strong signals suggest a US launch is being deliberately held back to avoid cannibalization of current Model Y sales.
- **U.S. gas prices hit $4.30/gallon nationally** (Los Angeles averaging $6.10/gallon), up 7% in one week — a structural tailwind for EV total cost of ownership and robotaxi per-mile energy economics.
- **Giga Berlin reached 1 million drive units produced**, a manufacturing milestone for Tesla's European facility.
- **Model S/X production lines expected to sunset in May 2026** to free factory space for the Optimus production line build-out.

## Market & Competition
- **Samsung equipment move-in ceremony at Taylor, Texas chip plant**, formally installing machinery to produce Tesla's AI4 self-driving chips. The ~$37B facility broke ground late 2022, ran approximately two years late, and is expected to begin production later in 2026. Elon reportedly toured the facility with Samsung executives.
- **SpaceX solar cell factory under construction in Bastrop, Texas**, with active recruiting for a Director of Solar Production. Hosts flagged this as significant given solar's natural fit with Tesla Energy and as fuel for ongoing Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation.
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation remains elevated** following the Terafab announcement and SpaceX solar factory news. Bhakdi maintains his $4,000/2030 price target for Tesla plus additional "SpaceX optionality." Alexandra Merz speculated that in a merger scenario, operational milestones in both Musk compensation packages could be restructured, though she noted this is speculation.
- **Legacy auto Q1 results described as "atrocious"** by Jeff Lutz, with some Western European OEMs posting single-digit gross margins. Tesla is characterized as gaining EV market share as competitors pivot to hybrids, which Lutz frames as an inefficient "sugar high" given dual-powertrain cost structures.
- **The Boring Company will take over Las Vegas Monorail infrastructure** at end of life, removing rails and integrating the corridor into the Vegas Loop — dramatically increasing Tesla vehicle visibility for international visitors.
