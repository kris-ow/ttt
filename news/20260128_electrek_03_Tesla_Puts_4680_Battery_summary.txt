Electrek reports that Tesla has resumed building Model Y battery packs with its in-house 4680 cells, more than two years after shifting 4680 production to Cybertruck, with Tesla framing the move as a supply-chain hedge against tariffs rather than a cost-driven battery upgrade.

## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Tesla's stated reason for reintroducing 4680 cells:** In its Q4 2025 shareholder update, Tesla said it "has begun to produce battery packs for certain Model Ys with our 4680 cells, unlocking an additional vector of supply to help navigate increasingly complex supply chain challenges caused by trade barriers and tariff risks." Tesla also stated it now produces dry-electrode material for 4680 cells with both anode and cathode made in Austin, and expects domestic cathode material production in Texas and LFP lines in Nevada to begin in 2026. The article notes Tesla's new battery-manufacturing table shows "installed capacity," not actual production.

2. **Timeline of the 4680 program's troubled history:** Per the article: Tesla unveiled the 4680 cell at Battery Day in September 2020, promising it would cut battery costs in half and enable a $25,000 Tesla. It produced 1 million 4680 cells at its Kato Road pilot facility by February 2022; the first Gigafactory Texas Model Ys with 4680 cells and structural battery packs shipped in April 2022; by December 2022 weekly production reached 868,000 cells, enough for about 1,000 Model Ys; Tesla opened public orders for the 4680 Model Y AWD at $49,990 in April 2023 but discontinued it by September 2023, reportedly to redirect cells to the incoming Cybertruck. Cybertruck deliveries began November 2023 and became the sole 4680-equipped vehicle, but the truck's weak sales ("commercial flop," per the article) meant little cell demand through 2024-2025.

3. **L&F cathode deal write-down:** In December 2025, Tesla's 4680 supply-chain partner L&F Co. wrote down its cathode supply deal with Tesla — reported at $2.9 billion — by over 99%, to just $7,386, citing a "change in supply quantity." The article presents this as evidence the 4680 program is not ramping at scale.

4. **Scrapped $25,000 Tesla plan:** The article states that the $25,000 Tesla the 4680 cell was originally meant to enable has been scrapped entirely by Musk.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this is "a quiet admission that the 4680 program hasn't gone according to plan," asserting the cell has been repositioned from a promised cost-cutting breakthrough into a tariff hedge, with genuine progress only on domestic vertical integration (dry-electrode cells made in Austin) but not on the original Battery Day vision. A top-voted reader comment (20 likes) notes the 4680-pack Model Y reportedly had a poor charging curve with 10-80% charge times over 40 minutes, calling the reintroduction a downgrade for road-trip usability.
