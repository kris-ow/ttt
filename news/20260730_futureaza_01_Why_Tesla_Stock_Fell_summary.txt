This futureaza episode, hosted by Brian with guest Herbert (from "Brighter"), argues that mainstream headlines calling Tesla's Q2 earnings "disastrous" overstate the negatives, and that the ~13%+ stock drop was driven primarily by softer Robotaxi growth expectations and AI-sector-wide jitters rather than the underlying financials, which the hosts characterize as mixed but broadly solid.

## Financials
1. **Stock drop attributed mostly to Robotaxi guidance, not financials:** The hosts say the sell-off was driven by softer-than-hoped Robotaxi expansion signals rather than the quarterly numbers themselves. Citing Jo Bhakdi, the host relays a claim that Tesla's stock fell roughly twice as much as the broader "AI basket" sell-off would predict, with Bhakdi attributing only about 2 percentage points of the decline to the actual financial results (attributed claim, not independently verified by the hosts).
2. **Record revenue beat consensus:** Tesla posted revenue described as a company record around $28 billion, beating a consensus near $26 billion, with automotive revenue up 23% year-over-year; trailing 12-month revenue topped $100 billion for the first time.
3. **Free cash flow came in less negative than feared:** The hosts say expectations were for free cash flow around negative $3 billion, but the actual figure landed near negative $1 billion (exact figure not given precisely), which they attribute to heavy simultaneous investment across roughly nine or ten major initiatives (semi factory, Megapack factory in Texas, new AI data center, Cybercab ramp, Optimus buildout). Capex was cited around $25 billion, with Herbert suggesting it should be even higher given the ongoing buildouts. Cash on hand was reported at $43.5 billion.
4. **Margin compression driven by ZEV credit decline:** Margins missed expectations, which the hosts partly attribute to ZEV/regulatory credit revenue dropping sharply after a change in federal CAFE standards eliminated the need for automakers to buy credits in the US. Herbert expects margin to recover toward a previously guided 22%+ range in Q3/Q4 as FSD subscription revenue scales.
5. **FSD becoming a recurring revenue stream:** Active paid FSD customers globally are estimated around 1.5 million, split roughly 55% one-time purchases and 45% subscriptions (implying roughly 600,000 subscribers), which the hosts estimate generates on the order of $60 million/month in near-100%-margin revenue.
6. **Services and Energy deployment metrics:** Services and other revenue reportedly hit $4.6 billion with record profitability, up 50%. Energy deployment was 13.5 gigawatt-hours for the quarter, which the hosts call surprisingly low in isolation but suggest reflects a large backlog (framed as roughly three years of future revenue already contracted, per Herbert's characterization).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries with FSD as a demand driver:** Tesla posted its best-ever Q2 and third-best delivery quarter overall, without major pull-forward incentives like the prior IRA-driven quarter. The hosts frame this as validation that FSD adoption (cited as up 55%) is now functioning as a genuine demand driver, alongside recovery in Europe and China and a softening earlier in the US. They expect further sales upside once the Model Y L becomes available in the US in coming quarters.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi expansion pace reset expectations:** The stock reaction is linked to Tesla signaling a slower, more measured city-by-city Robotaxi rollout rather than rapid multi-city expansion many investors had expected. Herbert argues Tesla's actual strategy is to perfect efficiency, infrastructure, and a "ridiculously safe" FSD version (referred to as version 15) before shifting to state-by-state scaling, rather than continuing city-by-city growth. He cites Ashok Elluswamy's statements about sustained double-digit weekly growth and Elon Musk's comment about growth being around or above 10% per week, cautioning that markets wrongly extrapolated this rate flatly across the rest of the year rather than as a precursor to a later scaling jump.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus expected to follow the same "slow then sudden" playbook as Robotaxi:** Herbert argues Tesla is deliberately avoiding narrow pick-and-place use cases (which he says other robotics companies already do) in favor of building a generalized, any-task learning system. He expects the next Optimus hardware version to be used primarily for training purposes (with early units possibly going to Tesla employees' homes, evidenced by app permissions requesting camera data access for training), followed by a subsequent version — which he places in the second half of next year — intended for mass commercial production at a targeted rate of 10 million units annually once the relevant factory ramps.
