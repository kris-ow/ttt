Jo Bhakdi delivers a post-earnings review of Tesla's Q1 2026 results, arguing that despite a sharp stock sell-off triggered by a surprise capex increase, the underlying earnings report was strong across multiple dimensions. His core thesis is that Tesla is in a "goldilocks moment" for investors, with several underappreciated catalysts — Cybercab production launch, Optimus Gen 3 commercialization, and accelerating robotaxi deployment — that could drive the stock back to all-time highs within 90 days. He frames the market's negative reaction as a sentiment-driven overreaction typical of a "schizophrenic" macro environment.

## Financials
1. **Auto Gross Margin Beat:** Tesla reported auto gross margin of 19.2% against analyst expectations of 17.5% — a significant beat that Jo Bhakdi describes as "mind-boggling" for an auto manufacturer operating under intense industry pressure. He interprets this not just as a financial win but as confirmation that Tesla remains the world leader in manufacturing efficiency, which he views as critical infrastructure for scaling Cybercab, Optimus, and energy products.

2. **Capex Increase to $25 Billion:** Elon Musk opened the earnings call by announcing a capital expenditure increase from $20 billion to $25 billion for 2026 — $5 billion more than previously disclosed. Q1 spending came in below expectations, meaning the remaining three quarters will require approximately $9 billion per quarter versus the previously expected $5 billion. Bhakdi views the capex increase as unambiguously bullish given Tesla's manufacturing track record, but acknowledges the current bearish market sentiment punished the stock for it, with shares dropping from a peak of $405 before the call to around $380 after hours.

3. **Stock Reaction and Trading Position:** The stock opened the next day down approximately 2.84%. Bhakdi disclosed his own trading position — short puts at strikes of $390, $385, and $380 — with a blended break-even around $375. He expressed confidence the stock would recover and stated his belief that Tesla is positioned to reach all-time highs within 90 days, contingent on no macro collapse.

## Robotaxi
1. **Paid Robotaxi Miles Doubled with Zero Incidents:** Tesla reported that paid robotaxi miles nearly doubled quarter-over-quarter in Q1, reaching close to 1 million miles, with no incidents reported. Bhakdi emphasizes the safety record as critical to avoiding any event that could derail the scaling operation, noting Waymo is at approximately 50 million miles for comparison context.

2. **Unsupervised Fleet Expansion to New Cities:** The unsupervised robotaxi fleet, which Bhakdi notes began only on January 25th of this year, has grown and recently added two new cities — Dallas and Houston. The current fleet consists of approximately 17 unsupervised vehicles, described as Model Y units at this stage, though Bhakdi highlights the rapid pace of city additions as more significant than the current small fleet size.

3. **12-City Target by End of 2026:** Elon Musk reiterated a target of approximately a dozen cities by end of year. Bhakdi had predicted this commentary in advance and does not consider it disappointing, though he notes some investors reacted negatively. He flagged that Tesla acknowledged getting "caught in loops at intersections," indicating edge cases that require additional training data before the next inflection point in scaling speed.

4. **10,000+ Unsupervised Robotaxis as Bull Case:** Bhakdi maintains his forecast of 10,000+ unsupervised robotaxis deployed by year-end as his base-to-bull case, with 20,000 as a stretch. He bases this on Cybercab production ramp analysis (see Electric Vehicles section) and argues the market underestimates exponential scaling dynamics. He announced a forthcoming video with an updated robotaxi scaling model to substantiate this forecast.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybercab Production Officially Started:** During the earnings call itself, Tesla posted on X confirming that Cybercab production has officially begun — transitioning from pilot/handmade testing phases to real production line output. Bhakdi notes the fleet has already been producing 20–40–60 units per week over the past four weeks. He distinguishes official production from pilot phases as a meaningful threshold, meaning the manufacturing infrastructure is now committed and must generate throughput.

2. **Cybercab Ramp Projection via Cybertruck Analogy:** Bhakdi applies the Cybertruck production ramp curve as a proxy for Cybercab scaling, noting Cybertruck was described by Elon as "painfully slow." If you overlay that same ramp curve starting from yesterday's production launch, it would imply roughly 25,000 units by year-end. Because Cybercab is described as significantly simpler and smaller than Cybertruck, Bhakdi argues that even cutting that ramp curve in half yields over 10,000 Cybercabs produced by end of 2026 — underpinning his unsupervised robotaxi fleet forecast.

3. **Delivery Growth and Broader Recovery:** Tesla showed delivery growth across key markets including Europe, with Bhakdi describing a broad recovery from a "challenging 2025." Gross margins improved despite the expiration of tax credits, which he views as further confirmation of operational strength.

4. **Roadster and New Vehicles:** Elon Musk indicated the Roadster is coming within approximately a month. Bhakdi briefly references the "flying car theory" around the Roadster but treats it as a minor catalyst, not a primary driver.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Miles and Subscriptions Surging:** FSD miles driven by Tesla owners increased 90% quarter-over-quarter, while FSD subscriptions grew 16% sequentially. Bhakdi interprets the divergence — subscription growth of 16% vs. miles growth of 90% — as evidence that existing FSD users are driving substantially more miles on the system, which he takes as a strong signal that FSD capability has improved meaningfully enough to change user behavior.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Production Starting in Q2 2026:** Elon Musk announced on the earnings call that Optimus production will begin in Q2 2026. Bhakdi characterizes this as an underappreciated catalyst, noting Tesla has been deliberately quiet about Optimus due to competitive sensitivity — unlike FSD, humanoid robot internals are less publicly understood, making strategic silence more valuable. He expects the Q2 production start will coincide with a public reveal of Optimus Gen 3, which he believes could drive the stock up approximately $100 as a "strong soft catalyst" — meaning it won't force analyst upgrades based on cash flow, but will compel action from neutral-to-positive investors.

2. **Optimus as Long-Term TAM, Not Near-Term Cash Flow:** Bhakdi explicitly states he does not expect Optimus to contribute meaningfully to free cash flow in the next 24 months, contrasting it with robotaxi which he views as the near-term "forcing function" for Tesla's repricing. However, he describes Optimus as representing an even larger total addressable market than robotaxi over a longer horizon.

## Market & Competition
1. **Capex Sentiment vs. Fundamentals:** Bhakdi draws a comparison to Meta's prior earnings call, where a large capex announcement also triggered a sell-off despite being fundamentally positive. He argues that in the current "schizophrenic" market — one that intellectually accepts AGI-era revenue potential but emotionally reverts to skepticism — large capex announcements are being penalized regardless of their strategic merit. He notes Tesla's $25 billion is modest compared to Amazon, Microsoft, and others in the Magnificent 7 spending $160–200 billion annually, while Tesla is the only one actually manufacturing large physical products.

2. **Waymo Comparison:** Bhakdi references Waymo's approximately 50 million paid miles as a benchmark against Tesla's nearly 1 million, acknowledging the gap but framing it as closeable given Tesla's doubling pace each quarter.
