The article argues Tesla's Cybertruck program is underperforming badly, estimating Q4 2025 Cybertruck sales at roughly 5,500 units (combined with negligible Semi volume) and full-year 2025 sales at about 21,500 units globally, while noting the Ford F-150 Lightning — despite being canceled for weak demand — sold roughly 27,300 units in the US in 2025, more than Tesla's Cybertruck.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck sales estimated via indirect math:** Because Tesla does not report Cybertruck sales separately (bundling it with Model S, Model X, and Semi under "Other Models"), the article estimates Cybertruck volume by subtracting an assumed 6,000 units for combined S/X sales from the "Other Models" total, leaving roughly 5,600 units for Cybertruck and Semi combined in Q4 2025, and ~5,500 for Cybertruck alone after excluding minimal Semi pilot-production volume. This yields an estimated full-year 2025 Cybertruck total of about 21,500 units globally — the article's own derived estimate, not a Tesla-reported figure.
2. **Year-over-year decline:** The article states Tesla previously confirmed Cybertruck sales of about 5,000 units in Q2 2025, and that sales fell by nearly 50% year-over-year in the most recent quarter despite price cuts and a short-lived cheaper trim that was later discontinued.
3. **F-150 Lightning comparison:** The Ford F-150 Lightning delivered approximately 27,300 units in the US in 2025 even as Ford wound the program down ahead of cancellation (announced in December, as Ford pivots to an EREV strategy); Ford's sales fell about 18% year-over-year, a smaller decline than Tesla's.
4. **SpaceX bulk purchase:** The article says SpaceX, Musk's private company, bought over 1,000 Cybertrucks last quarter — about 20% of Tesla's quarterly Cybertruck sales — yet sales still fell more than 50% year-over-year that quarter.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the Cybertruck program is failing (running at roughly 10% of planned production capacity, by the article's estimate) and that SpaceX's bulk purchases are not a sustainable fix. He contends Tesla could boost sales by distancing the vehicle from Musk's "toxic brand" and abandoning the 4680 battery cells, which he says made the truck more expensive and shorter-range than originally promised. He also criticizes Musk for not pivoting to a more traditional design despite having said he would if Cybertruck failed, attributing this to ego. A top reader comment (33 likes) adds that Tesla has similarly abandoned or stalled the Model 2 and Roadster programs and criticizes Musk's handling of PR and brand image.
