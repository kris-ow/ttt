## Robotaxi
- Cybercab rides are live to the public in Dallas, Tesla's second Cybercab city after Austin. Tesla's Eric called the vehicle version 1.5, with factory-fitted Starlink and a new rear trunk camera. Spencer's count puts roughly 200 badged Cybercabs in Dallas, though not all are necessarily giving rides, and a robotaxi tracker listed 192. One early rider reported a 20-minute trip, a 2-minute pickup and an $11 fare.
- Starlink hardware on the Dallas Cybercabs is reportedly V5, with power draw about half of V4, per the Electrified host. Ashok Elluswamy said Starlink is not required for safe operation and serves navigation, customer service and fleet management.
- Tesla is inviting more Texas customers to order the Cybercab for personal use at $29,990 before taxes, with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit. It requires a $299 monthly subscription that includes maintenance, and deliveries start in December. Specs: 279-mile range, 20.1 cu ft of storage, 176 inches long, 123 miles added in 50 minutes at a Supercharger, 4-year/50,000-mile warranty. Per Futureaza, vehicles are geofenced to robotaxi areas, can't be used commercially, and Tesla can buy the car back at market rate. Musk said select customers will be allowed to add their own Model Y to the fleet.
- Musk said Cybercab's two seats cover about 90% of passenger miles. For families, Tesla plans an autonomous Model Y with no steering wheel or pedals for six or seven people. He also said California Cybercab approval could come perhaps early next year.
- Tesla added about 200 Cybercabs to its Texas fleet in one day this week, per Cern Basher. He calculates that holding 17% weekly growth needs about 400 a week by year-end, which would hit stated Cybercab capacity by March or April.
- A new analysis from Martin Viecha's physical-AI data company has Tesla likely above 450,000 weekly miles in Austin versus Waymo around 400,000, with Waymo flat since April. Nationwide, Waymo still leads roughly 10x.
- Tesla's safety report covers about 3.1 million driverless miles from January 22 to October 18, 2026, with zero injuries in collisions where its robotaxi initiated contact. Farzad notes this is not a zero-collision claim. In October, NHTSA began reviewing a petition alleging robotaxi school-zone violations, listing four possible incidents with no crashes or injuries.
- Images circulate of a Cybercab colliding with a box truck. Fault is unknown and is likely to surface in NHTSA Standing General Order data, per the Electrified host.
- Las Vegas permits give 120 days to launch. With the full permit issued August 20, the deadline is around December 18, and Tesla could request an extension.

## Autonomous Driving
- Per Electrek, Italy's transport ministry granted provisional approval to Tesla Assisted Driving, the European name for FSD (Supervised), making Italy the 12th European country to allow it. Minister Matteo Salvini announced it on X after a technical feasibility check. Tesla Europe said rollout begins soon, with no firm date, and the system remains Level 2.
- Electrek estimates Italy's roughly 59M people take coverage close to 30% of Europeans. Its estimate is that Germany (which said in October it would back approval) plus the 12 approving countries reach roughly 47% of EU population, against the 65% (and at least 15 of 27 states) needed for the bloc-wide vote, expected in December at the earliest. France refused national approval in July, and Spain hasn't recognized the Dutch approval.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- At the DealBook Summit, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX "probably" are better together but that this is not a merger at any cost. Votes "may or may not succeed, at least in the case of Tesla." He said he must represent other SpaceX shareholders and avoid a non-economic deal because he would be sued. Alexandra Merz reads him as increasingly affirmative.
- Per Merz's reading of the SpaceX S-1, a derivative suit needs 3% of shares, cases are confined to the Texas business court without a jury, and the business-judgment presumption means plaintiffs must prove fraud, intentional misconduct or a knowing legal violation. A Futureaza host argued that either a Tesla premium or no premium invites lawsuits from one side.
- Per Futureaza, construction of the Grimes County Terafab project, once under Tesla's purview, has transferred to the SpaceX side.
- Market close November 19 (Electrified): Tesla $374.41, down 2.43%. SpaceX $138.43, down 3.53%. Futureaza said both stocks were down about 5% midday on its recording day.

## Financials
- Merz's vote math for a Tesla merger: Musk votes about 20% in favor, roughly 500 million shares follow ISS and Glass Lewis, and about 30% of shares don't vote and count as no. To reach 50% of outstanding shares, seven of ten remaining voters must say yes, and nine of ten to reach 60%.
- Musk said he should not be compensated for merely merging, and that Tesla options contingent on Tesla execution should not vest because of a merger. Merz wants operational milestones carried over and market-cap thresholds reset for the combined entity, which she thinks would be at least $3.5 trillion.
- At the December 3 annual meeting, ISS and Glass Lewis recommended against re-electing Robyn Denholm. Tesla answered with a five-page letter noting the firms previously opposed pay awards that shareholders approved with 75% (2025) and 77% (2018) of votes cast. Merz expects Denholm to be re-elected and says Tesla is really fighting to break the roughly 500 million-share block before a merger vote.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk wants the Optimus demo to be authentic, not staged, and was non-committal on timing ("maybe a month or two"). He expects Optimus to do dishes in about 12 to 18 months. The Electrified host notes this conflicts with Musk's January prediction that Optimus would beat top human surgeons in three years, and concludes Tesla does not really know the pace.
- Musk said 24/7 heavy-industry jobs make the most economic sense first, with home use later. Sawyer Merritt shared a new look at Tesla's Optimus production line.

## Energy
- Tesla filed a Texas tax-break application for a $3.1B ultra-high-purity polysilicon refinery, with construction expected in 2027 and operations in 2030. It is the upstream piece of Tesla's domestic solar platform and the 100 GW-per-year Project Crystal Sun. Tesla told Texas it is weighing other sites and wouldn't build in Texas without the tax break. No capacity figure was given.
- At RE+, Tesla introduced Powercore, a solid-state medium-voltage transformer, inverter and breaker in one unit. It delivers 7.4 MW continuous at 34.5 kV AC with 98.86% peak efficiency and a warranty of up to 25 years. It is made in the US, with first units operating in 2027.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla announced a $3 billion Cybercab production hall at Giga Berlin with capacity up to 1 million per year, per Futureaza. One host believes production is targeted for 2029, and another said Tesla expects roughly two years to build and begin production.
- Musk visited Giga Shanghai for the first time since 2023 and said Tesla will keep increasing production in China. Futureaza says the factory has built 810,000 vehicles this year against capacity of up to 950,000.
- Tesla is still selling only the $62k Launch Series Model Y L in the US, more than 4.5 months after introduction, per Sawyer Merritt. That is more than twice as long as the smaller Model Y Launch Series was available in early 2025.
- EV Semi Fleet Corp received the first of what the host believes are seven ordered Semis in San Bernardino. Per Futureaza, over 50 Semis were sold to an Illinois customer, and Tesla opened a Megacharger in Illinois. No one knows 2026 Semi output. On Kalshi (a channel sponsor), "over 1,000" yes trades near 13 cents, and an observer reports the fairing wrinkles are eliminated.

## Market & Competition
- Sawyer Merritt reports US lawmakers are closing in on a deal to ban Chinese cars, which would keep BYD and Geely out of the US market.
- Waymo paused San Francisco service after vehicles got stuck on flooded streets, per the Electrified host.
- Musk called Jensen Huang the "Michael Jordan" of the industry and said removing him did not seem good for shareholders. The panel debated Nvidia's board action as a key-man and succession-risk lesson.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Per Futureaza, Mechazilla caught the Starship upper stage after 14 orbits, about 22.5 hours after launch. Host Brian says it will cut launch costs and enables a full load of 60 Starlink V3 satellites per launch.
- Musk confirmed Ship 42 will be kept for display and not reflown. Starship deployed the first 26 operational Starlink V3 satellites at the end of September 2026.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said Starlink Mobile V2 starts flying next year with up to 40x the bandwidth of current direct-to-cell V1, and a V4 is planned to connect ordinary phones. He expects micro ground stations bundled with terminals to enter production next year.
- At DealBook, Musk said commercial Starlink is civilian infrastructure and not a weapon of war, while Starshield is government-controlled. He said Ukraine's president is asking to use commercial Starlink for offensive operations, and SpaceX has declined.
- Per Starlink's updates page, SpaceX operates more than 11,000 satellites serving about 12 million customers in 100+ countries. SpaceX's Q3 2026 release put consumer subscribers at 13.6M. Each V3 is designed for 1 TB per second, roughly 10x V2.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk said SpaceX is bringing roughly 0.5 GW online per month and aims for over 1 GW per month by end of 2027. He thinks Anthropic is unlikely to cancel its lease deals and will most likely want an incremental 10 GW. Per Futureaza, CFO Bret Johnsen said compute investment pays back in under a year. Satya Nadella said he would lease SpaceX capacity if available.
- SpaceX is rewriting its training stack mainly in C and some C++, per Musk, who said orbital AI data-center satellites are simpler than Starlink V3.
- Semiconductor equipment maker GST is in talks to supply scrubbers and chillers to Terafab, which asked about short lead times.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
