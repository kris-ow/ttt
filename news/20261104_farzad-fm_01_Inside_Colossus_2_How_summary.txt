This video breaks down SpaceX's Colossus 2 AI compute campus (spanning Memphis, TN and Southaven, MS) following SpaceX's acquisition of xAI on February 2, 2026, arguing that the real competitive advantage lies not in chip purchases but in how fast SpaceX can get power, cooling, and networking operational to turn purchased GPUs into usable capacity. It uses construction timelines, power infrastructure details, and the Anthropic cloud deal and recent segment financials to make the case that speed-to-operation is the core driver of profitability.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **1.2 million chip goal and building breakdown:** On October 31, 2026, Elon Musk set a goal of roughly 1.2 million Nvidia Blackwell chips in Colossus 2 by end of 2026. Per his report: the MacroHard building held 110,000 GB200 and 220,000 GB300 chips; MacroHarder and MiniHard each held 220,000 GB300 chips, all configured for training. A facility called "Gun Range" (a former police shooting range) was scheduled to be operational by end of November 2026 with a similar training configuration, and a facility called "Spam" (a former Hormel storage warehouse) was planned for December 2026 with about 200,000 GB300 chips configured for inference.
2. **Reused buildings and temporary power bridge the gap:** Colossus 1 reused the shell of a former Electrolux factory in Memphis (2024); Colossus 2 construction began with purchase of a warehouse in March 2025. Colossus 1's first phase ran on 150 megawatts of temporary gas turbine power plus 150 megawatts of Tesla Megapacks by May 1, 2025, before permanent grid connection was complete — allowing compute to start before construction finished.
3. **Colossus 2 power infrastructure and litigation:** Per SpaceX's July 30, 2026 update, the Southaven facility was running on 69 temporary mobile turbines, with a permanent power plant under construction featuring 41 turbines and 1.2 gigawatts of capacity; all temporary turbines were scheduled for dismantling by July 2027. On October 30, 2026, a federal judge allowed a lawsuit over the Southaven turbines to proceed under the Clean Air Act.
4. **Networking determines usable compute, not just chip count:** Macro Hard and Mini Hard were built with 800 gigabits-per-second network cards for training. Musk said on September 25, 2026 that the 110,000-chip increments are driven by the number of fiber optic cables a central switch can accommodate. Inference-focused "Spam," per Musk's October 31, 2026 update, uses a much simpler network with less than half the fiber of training buildings, since independent inference queries don't require cross-server training synchronization.
5. **Construction speed as competitive advantage:** Colossus 1's first 100,000-chip H100 cluster went from construction start to online in 122 days (announced September 2, 2024); a separate Nvidia figure cited 19 days from first rack arrival to training start. SpaceX's June 2026 prospectus estimated the first Colossus 2 cluster would take about 91 days (~3 months), versus the ~15-16 months for Stargate's Abilene first phase and roughly 2 years for Microsoft's Fairwater, Wisconsin facility. The prospectus attributes this speed to in-house execution and shared culture, and cites Colossus 2's per-megawatt construction cost as significantly below industry benchmarks.
6. **Orbital computing plans:** On October 15, 2026, Musk announced mass production of the "Starlink" computer (developed with Nvidia) is scheduled for 2027; in August 2026 he discussed heat dissipation in orbit as an active design challenge for this in-space computing effort.

## Financials
1. **Anthropic cloud compute deal:** In May 2026, SpaceX signed cloud agreements with Anthropic covering about 325,000 Nvidia GPUs across Colossus 1 and 2, with Anthropic paying $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 after reduced initial rates, terminable with 90 days' notice after the first three months — implying roughly $15 billion over 12 months at the stated rate.
2. **Superintelligence segment revenue and margin:** For the quarter ended September 30, 2026, SpaceX's superintelligence segment (cloud services, AI products, advertising) generated $7.56 billion in revenue, its largest segment that quarter — $7.022 billion from solutions/infrastructure and $538 million from advertising — but only $431 million in operating profit (roughly 6% operating margin). By contrast, the communications segment (including Starlink) generated $2.367 billion in operating profit in the same quarter.
3. **New Vera Rubin chip commitment:** On October 24, 2026, SpaceX signed a financial lease commitment for Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin GPUs; the transcript cites a dollar figure for base payments that is ambiguous in the available transcript and is omitted here, with the deal expected to close by end of 2026 and deliveries in the first half of 2027.
