Tesla has introduced a new "Standard Long Range" Model Y trim in Europe and a non-Long Range "Standard" Model Y in Canada, both supplied from Gigafactory Berlin rather than Tesla's US factories.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Canada gets a cheap, German-built Model Y Standard:** The new Model Y Standard starts at $49,990 CAD (roughly $35,000 USD per the article) in Canada, a steep drop from the Long Range AWD "Premium" trim. Specs: 463 km estimated range, 0-100 km/h in 7.2 seconds, 201 km/h top speed, rear-wheel drive. The author confirmed these units are imported from Gigafactory Berlin rather than Tesla's North American plants, which the article attributes to Tesla avoiding tariff complications tied to recent US-Canada EV trade disputes by sourcing from Germany instead.
2. **Europe adds a Standard Long Range trim, expanding to five Model Y variants:** Following the "Standard" trim launched in Europe in early October, Tesla has added a "Standard Long Range" version — the same decontented car with a larger battery. European (WLTP) specs: 657 km range, 0-100 km/h in 7.2 seconds, priced at €44,990. Europe's Model Y lineup now has five trims versus three in Canada. The "Standard" trims across markets drop features including the rear screen, some speakers, larger wheels, faster acceleration, ambient lighting, ventilated/heated textile seats, upgraded suspension, sound-dampened glass, and an uncovered glass roof.

## Market & Competition
3. **Competitive pressure cited as driver of Europe's new trim:** The article notes Tesla faces pressure from new entrants like the BMW iX3 and Mercedes-Benz GLC EV at the top end, and from Chinese automakers at the lower end of the European market.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the Canadian pricing excellent, noting the Model Y Standard is cheaper than the average gas car in Canada and arguably better priced than in the US, with Canadian buyers also getting FM radio and Autopilot as standard due to the European sourcing. He frames the European Standard Long Range trim as a direct response to competitive squeeze from German automakers and Chinese EV makers, and references his own recent reporting that Tesla's Gigafactory Berlin is "currently under threat" (detail not elaborated in this article). A top user comment, highlighted by the author, argues the Germany-to-Canada sourcing represents a "major failure of the tariffs" since it ships German-built cars to Canada rather than creating US manufacturing jobs.
