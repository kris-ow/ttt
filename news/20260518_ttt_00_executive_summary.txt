## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.3 rolls out nationally with reduced driver nagging.** Musk publicly called the release "a banger," highlighting reduced nag frequency as the headline improvement. Tester Sawyer reported going over a minute without a prompt; Jeff Lutz (Munro-adjacent supply chain CEO) said his Model X would not nag him at all — his own road-watching instincts were more frequent than any system prompt — describing it as feeling "unofficially like unsupervised." Notably, the Cybertruck still nags on the same build, which Lutz attributes to its ~1,450 lb weight penalty and longer stopping distances.

- **Smart Summon top speed raised 33% to 8 mph.** The increase from 6 mph is perceptible in real-world use per Sawyer Merritt, who noted vehicles now "flow better with traffic." The aggressive off-the-line acceleration present in 14.3.2 has been smoothed out. "Hey Grok" voice activation is now live; setting it on one Tesla automatically applies to a user's other vehicles.

- **Intervention counter and redesigned disengagement categories added.** The main screen now shows a live miles-since-last-intervention streak, resetting on disengagement. Intervention popup categories have been updated: "Preference" and "Discomfort" replaced by "Parking" and "Other," signaling Tesla is refining how it classifies and learns from driver takeovers.

- **Reviewer Omar — forwarded by Musk — called 14.3.3 "the first truly refined build"** and "the first version that should have gone wide," after driving 41 miles without intervention over three days. The national rollout is not geofenced to Austin or Dallas, distinguishing it from supervised robotaxi deployment.

- **Musk declared FSD 14.3 "the last piece of the puzzle for unsupervised FSD,"** though he qualified it with "I think." A Tesla executive confirmed the robotaxi deployments in Austin, Dallas, and Houston are "essentially 14.3 variants" and "obviously safe," and that Tesla will continue expanding on the 14.x series until Version 15 — described as "a major upgrade" — is validated.

- **Unsupervised FSD for customer vehicles targeted for Q4 2026, geography-gated.** Musk's framing: "I think we would release unsupervised gradually to the customer fleet as we feel like a particular geography is confirmed to be safe." Analysts at cernbasher interpreted this to mean owners within an already-approved robotaxi geography could be enabled without waiting for a broader V15 rollout.

- **Hardware 3 confirmed incapable of unsupervised FSD**, reversing hints from prior quarterly calls. Tesla is offering HW3 owners a discounted trade-in toward an HW4 vehicle or an in-place upgrade replacing both the computer and cameras. A "distilled" V14 build for HW3 will retain all supervised FSD features. Hosts at cernbasher estimated possibly ~500,000 HW3 vehicles have purchased FSD that cannot transfer, with retrofit costs of ~$5,000–$7,000 per vehicle; they framed Tesla's upgrade offer as a fleet-recruitment mechanism rather than a pure liability.

- **Tesla first vehicle — not just first EV — to pass NHTSA's new ADS safety test**, per the brighterwithherbert/Lutz discussion. The host argued countries approving FSD at scale could see immediate road safety improvements given the technology's documented trajectory.

- **Electrek challenged Musk's safety claims as contradictory.** At the Smart Mobility Summit, Musk said FSD is on a "path" to being "an order of magnitude safer than humans." Six weeks earlier on X, he claimed FSD is already "10X safer" with "unequivocal" statistics. Electrek's author argues these two positions cannot simultaneously be true and detailed methodological flaws in Tesla's Vehicle Safety Report: FSD usage is concentrated on highways (the safest road type), Tesla counts only airbag-deployment crashes while NHTSA baselines include all police-reported crashes, and Tesla's demographic skew further flatters the comparison.

- **Tesla posting China FSD road-testing jobs**, including data labelers, intelligent driving testers requiring domestic and international travel, and city-specific smart driving test technicians — a signal of significant FSD expansion activity in China per FutureAzA.

- **Waymo issued a software recall** after vehicles in Texas were swept away in floodwater. FutureAzA explained the root cause as a hand-coded rules conflict: vehicles were programmed both to avoid water and to proceed through it under certain conditions, producing inconsistent behavior. A separate Waymo vehicle was filmed running a red light on a left turn. Host argued both failures reflect structural limitations of rule-based systems that a neural-net approach avoids by learning from observed vehicle behavior.

## Robotaxi

- **Tesla robotaxi 1.8x safer than human drivers as of April 2026 — first month to surpass human safety baseline.** Analyst "Reigns" used NHTSA crash report data and RobotaxiTracker.com fleet mileage estimates (claimed <5% error) to calculate a three-month rolling crash rate. The human baseline used was 249,000 miles per accident (including reported and non-reported crashes). April 2026 was the first month Tesla's robotaxi exceeded that rate.

- **Full NHTSA incident narrative breakdown released: 53% other-party fault.** Tesla provided unredacted narratives for all 17 robotaxi incidents since the Austin launch in June 2025. Of 17 incidents: 9 (53%) were other parties' fault; 7 (41%) were Tesla's fault. Of the 7 Tesla-at-fault incidents: 2 backing collisions, 2 side swaps, 2 road hazards, 1 stationary object collision — two described as curb rashes, two occurring under teleoperator control. January's five incidents drew negative media coverage; the host noted all five occurred at under 4 mph. February had zero incidents; March had two.

- **Dallas-Fort Worth facility permit filed**, seeking to lease space for cleaning, service, and repair of the autonomous vehicle fleet. The planned facility includes 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building spaces, 17 EV charging stations, interior/exterior cleaning, tire replacement, software upgrades, and 24/7 maintenance — pending approval. This follows previously reported permits for a 36,000 sq ft Cybercab car wash in Las Vegas and a similar facility in Austin. Twelve production Cybercabs were also spotted by drone in Houston. Jeff Lutz described these buildouts as "the template for how they're going to have to set up in major cities."

- **Robotaxi app decompiled, revealing seven categories of operational features under development:** (1) Remote operator VoIP voice calls triggered by roadblocks, construction, or emergency; (2) Proactive remote assistance — FSD computer flags potential problems to Tesla ops center before incidents occur; (3) Manual override and remote start for driverless Cybercabs; (4) Ride-hailing and dispatch logistics; (5) Rider cabin sync — passenger phone becomes primary interface for display, routing, payment, and device correlation; (6) Digital vehicle immobilization kill switch; (7) Dynamic safety monitoring including vandalism detection, unresponsive passenger detection, unaccompanied minor mode, and pet transit mode.

- **Robotaxi expansion targeted across 12 states.** Cernbasher hosts projected this translates to 15–20+ cities (Texas alone has three, Florida would likely have at least three metro areas) and estimated approximately 10,000–15,000 unsupervised robotaxi vehicles by end of 2026 at roughly 300–500+ vehicles per city. Both hosts expressed frustration that Musk gave no order-of-magnitude signal for what "large-scale" deployment means.

- **Cybercab spotted driving in Las Vegas on May 17**, per Sawyer Merritt. No operational context (supervised vs. unsupervised, testing vs. service) was provided. Las Vegas is not among cities currently tracked by RobotaxiTracker.com.

- **Musk confirmed driverless operations in three Texas cities** at the Smart Mobility Summit and said widespread US deployment is expected by end of 2026. Electrek contextualized this against a decade of missed autonomous timelines and noted the current unsupervised fleet represents approximately 30 vehicles across three Texas cities per RobotaxiTracker.com, arguing that scaling to "widespread" US coverage in seven months has no demonstrated precedent.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Musk predicted intelligent robots will "far outnumber" humans on the planet** — not by a small margin. An Optimus unit was present on the exhibition floor at the Smart Mobility Summit, which Musk jokingly called "Optimus Subprime."

- **Economic framework: robot workforce could produce an economy 10–100x larger than today's.** Musk's framing: output equals productivity per capita times population; a massive, highly productive robot workforce scales both. He described the end state as "universal high income" — not a safety net, but genuine abundance — a framing he explicitly distinguished from universal basic income.

- **Musk did not dismiss the Terminator scenario**, calling it "unlikely but not impossible" and saying people should remain "a little paranoid" about robot safety.

- **Optimus factory in California expected by December 2026**, per Larry Goldberg (serial entrepreneur, VC investor) on brighterwithherbert. Goldberg compared Optimus's opportunity size directly to SpaceX's AI data center opportunity, saying he cannot determine which is larger — both are "gigantic."

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y price increases across all trims:** RWD up $1,000, AWD up $1,000, Performance up $500, per FutureAzA. Even post-increase, the price remains lower in real terms than after the April 2024 price increase when adjusted for ~6% cumulative inflation.

- **Two new paint colors — Marine Blue and Frost Blue — added for Model 3 and Model Y in the US.** Canada receives only one of the two, attributed to differences in factory supply for the Canadian market.

- **Tesla cited as primary beneficiary of California's $1 billion EV truck rebate program**, on the basis that no other manufacturer has the volume or competitive pricing to capture comparable share.

- **Tesla down 5% on Friday May 16**, per investingagainstthegrain. The host attributed the drop primarily to broader market selling pressure — Iran-related oil spike, rising Treasury yields, NASDAQ selloff — rather than any Tesla-specific negative catalyst. Tesla is down approximately 3.61% year-to-date, the MAG7 laggard.

- **WTI crude opened above $107/barrel, Brent above $110/barrel**, driven by escalating US-Israel-UAE-Iran tensions. Reports of a White House Situation Room meeting on military options, a large US Air Force weapons airlift to the Middle East, and Trump's public warning that "the clock is ticking" are flagged by investingagainstthegrain as potential precursors to imminent kinetic action.

- **CME is pricing a rate hike in January 2027** with no cuts expected before then, per investingagainstthegrain. The 10-year Treasury yield is cited at 4.5–4.6% and rising; the host argues sustained 4.5–5% yields are incompatible with current equity valuations.

- **Nvidia earnings due ~May 21**; investingagainstthegrain forecasts a record quarter and post-earnings price of $250–$270, citing strong AMD results, cloud provider capex, and Neocloud demand as indirect corroboration. A strong Nvidia print combined with an Iran resolution is framed as the catalyst for SPX toward 8,000.

- **SpaceX IPO set for June 12 on NASDAQ**, per Larry Goldberg on brighterwithherbert. Float is approximately $75 billion against a total valuation of $1.4–$2 trillion. Index fund inclusion expected in waves beginning 5 days post-IPO. Goldberg expects the IPO to be 12x oversubscribed. A Tesla-SpaceX merger is described as likely but not before IPO lockup periods (3–6 months) expire — Goldberg does not expect a merger announcement until at least late 2026.

- **Goldberg forecasts Tesla at ~$500 by December 2026**, which would put Tesla's market cap at just over $2 trillion — roughly comparable to his expected stabilized SpaceX valuation of $1.8–$2.2 trillion. He argues merger-fairness debates based on today's prices are premature; both stocks need stable post-lockup prices to establish a valid exchange ratio.

- **Starlink margins cited at 60–70%** versus Tesla automotive margins of ~14–18%. Goldberg's central argument: Starlink is 1–2 years ahead of Tesla's robotaxi business on the development curve, is already profitable, and its earnings quality is superior to Tesla's despite lower absolute revenue ($18–18.5B full-year vs. Tesla's ~$100B annualized).
