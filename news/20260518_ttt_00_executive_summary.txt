## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.3 rolling out nationally with dramatically reduced nagging.** Elon Musk publicly called the release "a banger," citing reduced driver monitoring as the headline improvement. Tester Sawyer Merritt reported it on his Model Y (software 2026.14.6.6) and documented the changes firsthand; Jeff Lutz (supply chain executive, Munro Live guest) said he could not get his Model X to nag him at all, describing it as feeling "unofficially like it was operating as unsupervised." Nagging remains more present in the Cybertruck, which Lutz attributes to its approximately 1,450 lbs of additional weight and longer stopping distances.

- **Smart Summon speed raised 33% to 8 mph.** The prior cap was 6 mph; Merritt confirmed the speed increase is perceptible in real-world use. The "Hey Grok" wake word is now enabled for hands-free voice activation; setting it on one vehicle automatically applied across Lutz's other Teslas.

- **Reviewer Omar called 14.3.3 "the first truly refined build"** and "the first version that should have gone wide," reporting 41 miles without intervention over three days. Musk forwarded the review publicly. The release is rolling out nationally — not restricted to robotaxi geofences.

- **New intervention counter and reclassified takeover categories.** A live miles-since-last-intervention streak counter now appears on the main screen, with a longest-streak tracker in the FSD app. Tesla replaced "Preference" and "Discomfort" takeover categories with "Parking" and "Other," alongside retained "Navigation" and "Critical" options — suggesting refined learning from driver takeovers. Some community members flagged streak-preservation as a potential safety concern; Lutz and the host were skeptical it would materially affect behavior.

- **FSD 14.3 confirmed as "the last piece of the puzzle" for unsupervised expansion.** Elon Musk stated this on the Q1 2026 earnings call, qualified with "I think." A Tesla executive confirmed the robotaxi variants running in Austin, Dallas, and Houston are "essentially 14.3 variants" and "obviously safe," and that Version 15 — described as "a major upgrade" — is not a prerequisite for continued geographic expansion. Hosts at cernbasher interpret this to mean meaningful city-by-city scaling can proceed under the 14.x series now.

- **Unsupervised FSD for consumer vehicles targeted for Q4 2026, geography-gated.** Musk said on the earnings call he expects unsupervised FSD to reach customer cars "probably in the fourth quarter," released geography-by-geography "as we feel like a particular geography is confirmed to be safe." Customers living within an already-approved robotaxi geography could be enabled without a broader Version 15 rollout.

- **Hardware 3 confirmed incapable of unsupervised FSD.** Tesla reversed prior ambiguity on the earnings call, stating HW3 "simply does not have the capability." Tesla is offering HW3 owners a discounted trade-in toward an HW4 vehicle or an in-vehicle hardware-and-camera upgrade. A "distilled" Version 14 build for HW3 is planned — all features except unsupervised capability. Upgrade timing was cited as "this summer" by Tesla; one host disputed this, projecting next year. Estimated retrofit cost: $5,000–$7,000 per vehicle; estimated HW3 vehicles with untransferred purchased FSD: possibly ~500,000 (hosts flagged uncertainty on that figure).

- **Tesla became the first vehicle — not just the first EV — to pass NHTSA's new ADS safety test**, per brighterwithherbert. The host framed this as a meaningful regulatory milestone supporting international FSD approvals.

- **Musk at the Samson Smart Mobility Summit (Israel, 2:30 AM Austin time):** Stated the path to autonomous vehicles being "at least an order of magnitude safer than human drivers" is "very clear" — not a question mark. Predicted that within 10 years, roughly 90% of all distance driven on Earth will be handled by AI, making human-driven cars a niche recreational activity. Electrek noted a contradiction: six weeks prior on X, Musk claimed FSD is *already* "10X safer" — these two positions cannot simultaneously be true.

- **Tesla posting China FSD road-testing jobs**, per FutureAzA — roles include data labelers, intelligent driving testers requiring flexible domestic and international travel, and city-specific smart driving test technicians. Framed as a significant signal of FSD momentum in China.

- **Waymo red-light and flood incidents highlighted as structural contrast.** A Waymo vehicle was captured running a red light on a left turn; separately, Waymo issued a software recall after vehicles in Texas were swept away by floodwaters, traced to a conflicting rules-based programming issue. FutureAzA argued these failures illustrate a structural advantage for Tesla's neural-net approach, which generalizes from observing real-world behavior rather than relying on hand-coded rules.

## Robotaxi

- **Tesla robotaxi reached 1.8x safer than human drivers in April 2026** — the first month to surpass the human baseline, per analyst "Reigns" using NHTSA crash data and fleet mileage estimates from RobotaxiTracker.com. Human crash rate baseline used: 249,000 miles per accident (including reported and non-reported). Mileage estimation error claimed at under 5%.

- **NHTSA incident breakdown since June 2025 launch: 53% other-party fault.** Tesla provided unredacted narratives for all 17 reported robotaxi incidents. Of these, 53% were attributed to other parties; only 7 incidents (41%) were Tesla's fault. Of those seven: two backing collisions, two side swaps, two road hazards, one stationary object. Two at-fault incidents occurred under teleoperator control. FutureAzA noted that five January incidents — which drove widespread "four times less safe" media coverage — all occurred at under 4 mph. February had zero incidents; March had two.

- **Dallas-Fort Worth infrastructure permit filed.** Tesla is seeking to lease a facility for cleaning, service, and repair of its autonomous vehicle fleet. Plans include 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building spaces, 17 EV charging stations, exterior and interior washing, tire replacement, software upgrades, and 24/7 maintenance — pending city approval. This follows a 36,000 sq ft Cybercab car wash facility permit in Las Vegas (prior week) and a similar Austin facility. Twelve production Cybercabs were also spotted by drone in Houston. Jeff Lutz called these buildouts "the template for how they're going to have to set up in major cities."

- **Robotaxi app decompiled, revealing seven categories of operational features under development:** (1) Remote operator VoIP voice calls — direct teleoperator audio link triggered by roadblocks, construction, or emergency button press; (2) Proactive remote assistance — FSD computer flags potential problem states before issues arise; (3) Manual override and remote start for driverless Cybercabs — authorized app-based manual control and remote dispatch of empty vehicles; (4) Ride-hailing and dispatch logistics; (5) Rider cabin sync — passenger phone becomes primary interface for display control, routing, payment, and device correlation; (6) Digital vehicle immobilization kill switch; (7) Dynamic safety monitoring including vandalism detection, unresponsive passenger detection, unaccompanied minor mode, and pet transit mode.

- **Expansion to 12 states projected to yield 15–20+ cities**, per cernbasher hosts. Texas alone has three cities; Florida would likely contribute at least three major metros. At 300–500+ vehicles per city, hosts project 10,000–15,000 unsupervised robotaxis by end of 2026 — consistent with their pre-call estimate.

- **Cybercab spotted driving in Las Vegas on May 17**, per Sawyer Merritt. No operational context provided (supervised vs. unsupervised, testing vs. public service). Las Vegas is not currently tracked by RobotaxiTracker.com as an active deployment city.

- **Musk confirmed unsupervised operations in three Texas cities** at the Samson Summit, expecting the service to be "widespread in the US by the end of this year." Electrek flagged this as consistent with a decade-long pattern of missed timelines and noted the gap between ~39 unsupervised vehicles today and "widespread" coverage in seven months.

- **Tax incentive may accelerate infrastructure capex.** Lutz noted that under current legislation, robotaxi-related capital expenditure can be expensed in the first year, providing a meaningful financial incentive to front-load infrastructure buildout and offset gains elsewhere.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Musk at Samson Summit: intelligent robots will "far outnumber" humans on the planet.** A Tesla Optimus unit was on display at the summit's exhibition floor, which Musk jokingly called "Optimus Subprime." He tied the humanoid robot thesis to an economic output framework: productivity per capita times population, with a large, highly productive robot workforce potentially growing the global economy 10 to 100 times larger than today — enabling what he called "universal high income," distinct from a safety net and framed as genuine abundance.

- **Optimus factory in California expected by December 2026**, per Larry Goldberg (VC investor, brighterwithherbert). He described Optimus as one of Tesla's four major future product lines and said he cannot determine whether Optimus's economic opportunity is larger or smaller than SpaceX's AI data center opportunity — characterizing both as "gigantic."

- **Musk did not dismiss the Terminator scenario**, calling it "unlikely but not impossible" and saying people should always be "a little paranoid" about robot safety — a more nuanced acknowledgment than he typically receives credit for, per brighterwithherbert host.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla dropped 5% on Friday, May 16**, per investingagainstthegrain host, attributed primarily to broader market selling pressure (Iran conflict, rising yields) rather than Tesla-specific news. Tesla is down approximately 3.61% year-to-date, making it the laggard among the Magnificent 7. Host describes weekly and monthly charts as "extremely healthy" and expects a bottom by approximately May 23.

- **Macro headwinds flagged: WTI crude over $107, Brent over $110, 10-year yield at 4.5–4.6%.** CME is pricing in a rate *hike* in January 2027 with no cuts expected before then. Host cites midterm election year seasonality (historically weak May–October), Tom Lee's "painful summer" warning, and speculative excess in triple-leveraged semiconductor ETFs as compounding risk factors.

- **SpaceX IPO set for June 12 on NASDAQ**, per Larry Goldberg. Float is approximately $75 billion against a total valuation of $1.4–$2 trillion. Index fund inclusion expected in waves: one group 5 days post-IPO, a second 5 days later, smaller funds 15 days later, S&P 500 inclusion slower. Goldberg expects the IPO to be oversubscribed approximately 12 times.

- **Tesla-SpaceX merger of equals framed as likely but post-lockup.** Goldberg argues a merger announcement is unlikely before IPO lockups expire (typically 3–6 months post-June 12), as no stable SpaceX market price would yet exist against which to set exchange ratios. He forecasts Tesla stock at approximately $500 by December 2026, which would put Tesla's market cap near $2 trillion — roughly comparable to his expected stabilized SpaceX valuation of $1.8–$2.2 trillion, making a "merger of equals" framing plausible at that point.

- **Model Y prices raised:** RWD +$1,000, AWD +$1,000, Performance +$500, per FutureAzA. Host notes the post-increase price remains lower in real terms than after the April 1, 2024 price increase when adjusted for roughly 6% cumulative inflation.

- **Two new paint colors launched for Model 3 and Model Y in the US:** Marine Blue and Frost Blue. Canada receives only one of the two due to factory supply differences.

- **California announced $1 billion EV truck rebate program.** FutureAzA argues Tesla is positioned to capture a disproportionate share given volume and pricing advantages relative to other manufacturers.
