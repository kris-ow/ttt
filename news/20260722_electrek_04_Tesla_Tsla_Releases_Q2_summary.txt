Tesla reported Q2 2026 results showing record revenue but a significant miss on profit, with operating income and free cash flow both deteriorating sharply despite record vehicle deliveries.

## Financials
1. **Record revenue, big EPS miss:** Tesla posted revenue of $28.24 billion, up 26% year-over-year and above the roughly $26.4 billion Wall Street expected — the company's first real revenue growth in over a year and the first time trailing-twelve-month revenue topped $100 billion. Non-GAAP EPS came in at $0.33, down 18% year-over-year and well below the $0.53 analysts expected.

2. **Margins hold at gross level, collapse below it:** Total gross margin was 16.8%, down only 41 basis points year-over-year despite record volume. But operating income fell 57% to $398 million and operating margin sank to 1.4% from 4.1% a year ago, as operating expenses jumped 47% to $4.35 billion, driven by spending on AI, Optimus, robotaxi, and stock-based compensation tied to the 2025 CEO pay package.

3. **Cash burn and capex surge:** Capital spending more than doubled to $5.8 billion, pushing free cash flow to negative $1.1 billion — Tesla's first cash-burning quarter since early 2024. GAAP net income was $1.11 billion, down just 5%, aided by a $590 million gain in other income including mark-to-market gains on Tesla's bitcoin holdings and currency effects.

4. **Regulatory credits collapse:** Tesla booked just $146 million in regulatory credit revenue, down 67% year-over-year and less than half Q1's $380 million — the lowest in years, following a steady decline from a 2024 peak of $2.76 billion annually. The article attributes this to the $7,500 federal EV tax credit expiring September 30, 2025, and a federal law change zeroing out fuel-economy penalties that had driven rivals to buy Tesla's credits, meaning this revenue source is not expected to return.

## Electric Vehicles
5. **Record deliveries confirmed:** Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2, up 25% year-over-year (previously disclosed), with production of 451,758 vehicles and services revenue up 50% year-over-year.

## Energy
6. **Energy storage growth:** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of energy storage in Q2, up more than 40% year-over-year (previously disclosed figure, not part of the new earnings release itself).
