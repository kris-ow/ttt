This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Jeff Lutz — a multi-decade supply chain executive and CEO of a high-tech manufacturing consulting firm — discussing Tesla's accelerating robotaxi expansion, the significance of Tesla's 10 billion supervised FSD miles milestone, Tesla's continued dominance in global EV sales, and the competitive landscape including Ford CEO Jim Farley's repeated calls to block Chinese EV imports. The core thesis is that Tesla's robotaxi fleet growth rate is more meaningful than its absolute size, and that Tesla's cost structure and software-first approach position it far ahead of competitors in both autonomous vehicles and EVs.

## Robotaxi

1. **Biggest single-day unsupervised fleet increase ever:** Tesla added seven unsupervised robotaxi vehicles in a single day, bringing the total to 33 unsupervised units per RobotaxiTracker.com — broken down as 22 in Austin, 5 in Dallas, and 6 in Houston. Herbert and Jeff Lutz emphasize that the rate of growth — approximately 20% in a single day, and roughly 50% over approximately 10 days — is more significant than the absolute number at this stage. Jeff notes that Tesla appears to be operating a mathematical model tracking the probability of incidents based on fleet size, and that growing confidence in the system's performance is driving these additions.

2. **Austin robotaxi expands to nighttime unsupervised operation:** For the first time, Tesla's Austin unsupervised robotaxi fleet began operating in the evenings, having previously been limited to midafternoon hours. This is flagged as a meaningful operational milestone, suggesting improved system confidence across more challenging driving conditions.

3. **Staged vehicles signal continued near-term expansion:** Footage of a Houston lot shows additional robotaxi vehicles staged and ready for deployment, with license plates already assigned. Jeff interprets this as evidence that Tesla will continue pulling vehicles into service as system performance metrics continue to improve, and that internal debate is ongoing about the pace of ramp — which he describes as normal for any product launch.

4. **San Antonio hiring signals imminent expansion:** New job postings for a robotaxi operations manager in San Antonio, Texas were spotted, alongside fleet support hiring listings referencing Orlando, Dallas, San Francisco, and Miami. Herbert and Jeff view the San Antonio posting as a strong signal that it will be the next city to receive robotaxi service, joining Austin, Dallas, and Houston in Texas. Cybercab prototypes were also spotted in the San Francisco area.

5. **January incidents shaped the conservative ramp pace:** Herbert explains that five low-speed fender-bender incidents in January generated outsized negative global media coverage, and that Tesla deliberately slowed unsupervised fleet growth in the following months to create separation and avoid compounding that narrative. Jeff adds that doubling the fleet without first resolving those issues would have statistically doubled the incident count, justifying the pause.

6. **Cybercab repairability as a structural cost advantage:** Jeff argues that unlike competitors whose sensor-laden vehicles (some costing several hundred thousand dollars each) are expensive and difficult to repair, the Cybercab's modular "Unboxed" assembly design will translate into modular field disassembly and repair, making it potentially the most repairable vehicle in the robotaxi space. He contrasts this with Waymo, which Herbert notes is reportedly losing $5–6 billion per year — far more than many expected — attributing Waymo's losses to high part counts, complex sensors, and high repair costs.

7. **Tesla's robotaxi rollout compares favorably to all others despite self-imposed missed milestones:** Jeff argues that when measured against any other robotaxi rollout, Tesla's is fast if not the fastest — and crucially, without the sensor "crutches" that competitor systems rely on. He believes competitor systems are self-limiting because their unit economics don't work at scale: the more vehicles they deploy, the greater their losses. Tesla's cost structure, by contrast, allows for much wider and more profitable scaling.

8. **California autonomous vehicle traffic law effective July 1:** A new California law taking effect July 1, 2026 will allow tickets to be issued to autonomous vehicles that violate traffic laws, including running red lights or failing to stop for pedestrians. Jeff views this positively, noting it introduces a third-party performance evaluator beyond customers, which he believes is good for the overall system.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla surpasses 10 billion supervised FSD miles:** Elon Musk publicly highlighted that Tesla has now driven over 10 billion miles on supervised FSD. When pressed on whether this number has special significance, Jeff says it was likely an approximation of magnitude rather than a precise threshold — meaning it signals Tesla is operating at a scale where the system should be capable of outperforming human drivers, but there was no single "light switch" moment at exactly 10 billion. He notes the 10 billion milestone correlates roughly with the development of the current FSD version (referred to in discussion as "14.3"), and believes Tesla is now months away from a fully unsupervised software version capable of wider deployment.

2. **FSD currently approximately 90% safer than human driving:** Jeff references that Tesla's computer-assisted driving is performing at approximately 90% safer than human driving, and argues this should be the central metric in any public or regulatory debate about deployment — not isolated incident counts stripped of context.

3. **Robotaxi fleet receives new software first:** Jeff notes that the controlled robotaxi fleet appears to be the first to receive new FSD software versions, potentially running a more advanced build than what is available on personally owned vehicles. This gives Tesla a live testing environment while continuing to improve the system.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model Y and Model 3 rank #1 and #2 globally in March:** A new report covering the top 20 bestselling EVs globally in March 2026 shows the Model Y and Model 3 holding the top two spots. Jeff emphasizes that BYD requires eight or nine models on the same list and still doesn't match the combined volume of Tesla's two models — a reflection of Tesla's superior manufacturing leverage and supply chain concentration.

2. **Model Y is the #1 bestselling car overall in California in Q1:** In California, the Model Y outsold every vehicle — including gasoline-powered cars — in Q1 2026, topping the Camry, Civic, CR-V, and other traditional bestsellers.

3. **Tesla leads US customer loyalty at 61%:** Per S&P Global Mobility data (as of February 2026), Tesla ranks #1 in US customer loyalty with a 61% rate, ahead of Subaru, Toyota, and others. Jeff notes this is actually a strong loyalty number in the industry context, and Chamath Palihapitiya is quoted as saying it's hard to understand what the other 39% are thinking once you've driven a Tesla.

4. **China-made Model 3 launched in Canada at $29,000 USD:** Following Canada's decision to allow a certain level of Chinese EV imports, Tesla began offering Giga Shanghai-built Model 3s in Canada at approximately $39,000 Canadian (roughly $29,000 USD) — a 17% cut to the Model 3 Performance price and approximately 50% cheaper than the long-range variant previously available. The average new car price in Canada is approximately $63,000 Canadian, making the Model 3 over $24,000 Canadian cheaper than the average new vehicle. A Canadian buyer quoted in the video noted electricity is 85% cheaper than gasoline per mile in their area.

## Market & Competition

1. **Ford CEO Jim Farley continues lobbying against Chinese EV imports:** Farley has appeared in multiple media interviews over the past month repeating calls for the US government to block Chinese EV imports, while simultaneously admitting he visits China six to seven times per year and describing it as "the most humbling thing I've ever seen," citing China's far superior in-vehicle technology and the fact that 70% of global EVs are made there. Jeff finds the messaging incoherent and is concerned about Ford's strategic direction, noting Ford's April numbers showed declines across gas, hybrid, and EV segments.

2. **Jeff's structural critique of Chinese EV economics:** Jeff argues that BYD and other Chinese EV manufacturers have not demonstrated a sustainable business model — pointing to heavy government subsidies, delayed supplier payments (reportedly 6+ months beyond normal terms), channel inventory being sold to dealers and shell companies rather than end consumers, and poor results when expanding outside China (Europe, South America). He distinguishes design and manufacturing capability, which he acknowledges is real, from a proven, profitable, scalable business model.

3. **Ford approximately 5–7 years behind Tesla's major moves:** Jeff estimates Ford is about 5–7 years behind Tesla on every major strategic move — including equivalent EVs, energy business, and autonomy — and that their lower-cost EV truck is unlikely to reach scale until approximately 2028.

4. **Canada's decision to allow Chinese EV imports criticized:** Jeff argues that Canada's deal (allowing Chinese auto imports in exchange for agricultural trade) will ultimately damage Canada's domestic auto manufacturing industry, as Chinese manufacturers will not re-industrialize Canada — they will simply capture market share.
