## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla began rolling out FSD v15 to a first wave of customers, per Ashok Elluswamy. Release notes cite a shared vision encoder (FSD, robotaxi, Optimus) trained on internet video that Tesla says improves critical intervention rate 25x, though no underlying figure was published and Electrek flags it as hard to verify. Musk said wide release comes in December with the holiday update, calling it the first version worthy of the name "Super."
- Italy granted provisional approval to Tesla Assisted Driving, the 12th European country, announced by Minister Salvini after a technical feasibility check. Electrek estimates Italy plus Germany's stated support put the approving bloc near 47% of EU population, short of the 65% and 15-state threshold for the bloc-wide vote expected in December at the earliest. France refused national approval in July and Spain hasn't recognized the Dutch approval.
- RDW approved an updated release for HW4 cars with tighter monitoring: hands required on the wheel above 130 km/h, earlier warnings, and a strike for inattention during a maneuver. Electrek argues Tesla tightens monitoring only where regulators force it.
- v15 release notes add better detection of small animals, debris, potholes and standing water, speed-camera slowing, and improved school-zone handling. Electrek cites a Johanna.be test in which FSD exceeded the limit in 55% of 30 km/h zones, though the test predates the v15 school-zone fix.

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab rides went live to the public in Dallas, the second Cybercab city after Austin. Roughly 200 badged Cybercabs are in the city by one count (a tracker listed 192), not all necessarily carrying riders. Elluswamy said the factory-fitted Starlink hardware is not required for safe operation and serves navigation, customer service and fleet management. The service area was then expanded 1% to 82 sq mi.
- Texas registrations: Tesla added a record 200 Cybercabs in one day, bringing the total to 773, and about 205 robotaxis in the week versus 24 for Waymo. Tesla stands at roughly 1,500 registered versus about 1,300 for Waymo, per Herbert. A registration is legal authorization, not proof of passenger service.
- Personal-use Cybercab invitations widened to more Texas customers at $29,990 with a $1,000 deposit and $299/month subscription, December deliveries. Musk said the fee should be near $100-$115 and that select customers will be allowed to add their own Model Y to the fleet, with testing possible "next month" versus the Q2 2027 timing given on the October call.
- Musk said the two-seat Cybercab covers about 90% of passenger miles and that Tesla plans an autonomous Model Y with no wheel or pedals for six or seven people. No timeline, price or build location was given. Electrek calls it "yet another vehicle nobody asked for."
- Robotaxi now runs 24/7 in all eight cities. A Martin Viecha-linked analysis has Tesla likely above 450,000 weekly miles in Austin versus Waymo near 400,000, though Waymo still leads nationwide roughly 10x.

## Optimus
- Musk said a real, unscripted Optimus demo comes "maybe in, I don't know, a month or two," and said the humanoid demos people see are staged and programmed. He expects Optimus to do chores like dishes in 12-18 months, with heavy industry first.
- Joe Tegtmeyer's aerial footage shows the Giga Texas Optimus factory at a little over 7 million sq ft by his estimate, steel completion targeted for March or April, with four large foundations poured. He says the site still lacks industrial power and that structural completion does not mean production readiness.
- Humanoid Summit (Dec 1-2, San Mateo) says it is partnering with Tesla for Optimus viewing and FSD demo rides; a live demo is possible but unconfirmed.

## Energy
- Tesla unveiled Powercore, a solid-state medium-voltage transformer, inverter and breaker in one unit: 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV, 98.86% peak efficiency, up to 25-year warranty, made in the US, first units operating in 2027. No price or factory location was published. Rival Heron Power, founded by ex-Tesla SVP Drew Baglino, targets 2027 volume production.
- Tesla filed a Texas tax-break application for a $3.1B polysilicon refinery near Corpus Christi, construction in 2027 and operations in 2030, seeking 100% abatement 2028-2030 and 50% for 2031-2040. Tesla says the project isn't viable without the breaks, and it needs the governor's signature, likely after Jan. 19. It feeds the roughly $10B Crystal Sun solar complex near Houston.
- Tesla opened its first Texas Semi Megacharger in Mesquite (8 stalls, up to 1.2 MW each), the seventh site and 44 stalls network-wide versus about 37 sites planned for 2026 per the Q4 2025 shareholder report. Electrek projects roughly 10 sites by year-end at the current pace.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla announced a roughly $3.3B (about €3B) Cybercab hall at Giga Berlin for up to 1M vehicles/year, permit filing by year-end, construction H1 2027, production 2029. Electrek calls it extremely risky given the EU's 1,500 per-model driverless-registration cap and no European unsupervised approval.
- Argentina opened orders for five Shanghai-built Model 3/Y versions, $40,990 to $53,990, with first deliveries March or April 2027. Electrek's Fred Lambert read Musk's two-minute launch video appearance as a sign he no longer cares about selling cars.
- Musk called the Model Y L "certainly our most popular car" and said Tesla should probably move all Model Y production to it. Tesla quietly dropped the entry AWD Model Y trim, raising the AWD entry price about $8,000.

## Financials
- ISS and Glass Lewis recommended against re-electing Chair Robyn Denholm at the Dec 3 annual meeting; Tesla answered with a five-page letter noting both firms previously opposed pay awards shareholders approved with 75% (2025) and 77% (2018) of votes cast. Alexandra Merz expects Denholm to be re-elected and reads the fight as aimed at the roughly 500M-share block that follows proxy advisers ahead of any merger vote.
- Per Tesla's October proxy, none of the 12 tranches of the 2025 CEO award has been earned. Musk said he shouldn't receive any award merely for doing a SpaceX merger.
- Duan Yongping's H&H added 699,000 Tesla shares (+20%) in Q3 to about 4M shares worth $1.45B, and Altimeter opened a roughly $680M Tesla position while cutting its SpaceX stake 41%.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Anthropic's IPO filing discloses it expects to spend up to $120.5B on SpaceX compute through 2029, about $3.1B/month at full capacity versus $1.25B/month previously disclosed, per Cern Basher via Jo Bhakdi. Musk said Anthropic is "extremely likely" to keep renting and wants an incremental 10 GW, describing notice periods of 3-6 months versus the 90 days Bhakdi cites from the filing.
- Apollo and Goldman Sachs priced $44B in senior and subordinated notes to fund Nvidia Vera Rubin purchases, billed as the largest GPU-backed financing in history, reportedly oversubscribed and upsized. CFO Bret Johnsen said compute pays back in under a year.
- SpaceX's 6 GW Cochise County, Arizona campus (nine buildings, $50B regional investment, up to 24 GWh of batteries, construction starting 2027) was detailed alongside a $24B Brownsville, Texas facility. SpaceX has not named a battery supplier.
- Musk said SpaceX is adding roughly 0.5 GW per month and targets over 1 GW per month by end of 2027, and is on track for $100B annualized revenue as soon as December.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- At the DealBook Summit, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX are "probably" better together but not a merge-at-any-cost case, and that the outcome depends on shareholder votes. Merz's vote math: Musk's ~20% plus non-voters counting as no means seven of ten remaining voters must say yes to reach 50% of outstanding shares.
- Q3 13F filings show Nvidia holding about 130M SpaceX shares (nearly $20B) and Alphabet 555M (about $83B). Kalshi odds cited at roughly 61% for a Tesla-SpaceX merger by March 1.
- SPCX closed Nov 19 at $138.43 (-3.53%) and TSLA at $374.41 (-2.43%).

## SpaceX — Starship
- Analysts reacted to last week's Flight 15 ship catch: Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas called it "a historic inflection moment" that "materially derisked" SpaceX's addressable markets ($300 target), and Citigroup lifted its target to $280 from $240. Musk confirmed Ship 42 will be kept for display, not reflown.
- Musk said Starship V4 will be about 70 m long with over 60% more propellant, payload above 200 tons and propellant cost per flight below $500,000. SpaceX says Florida's Pad 39A tower should be ready by the end of November.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said direct-to-cell V2 satellites begin launching next year with about 40x V1's bandwidth, and that micro ground stations on the 800 MHz band enter production next year. He reiterated that commercial Starlink is not for offensive military use.
- A United Airlines executive called in-flight Starlink Wi-Fi "the biggest near-term competitive advantage that we've had, ever had in United's 100-year history." Samsung is investing $1.9B to expand its Austin fab for Starlink laser-communication chips.

---

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- ULA's Vulcan suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into flight and failed to deliver 40 Amazon LEO satellites to orbit, reportedly its third anomaly in five attempts. Musk said Falcon delivers 85% of all Earth payload to orbit.

## Market & Competition
- California's DMV barred Waymo from freeway operation on Nov 11 over work-zone incursions, and Waymo paused freeway service in all markets. It still reports over 500,000 weekly trips, and later paused San Francisco service after vehicles stalled on flooded streets.
- Nvidia's board said it does not intend to reinstate Jensen Huang and the SEC opened an investigation; Musk said removing Huang did not seem good for shareholders.
- US lawmakers are reportedly closing in on a deal to ban Chinese cars from the US market.

## Bear Case of the Week
- Electrek reported three fatal crashes from NHTSA filings with Autopilot/FSD "Verified Engaged" (74, 71 and 39 mph), with Tesla redacting narratives and software versions as confidential. Tesla's September driver-assist crash filings hit a record 264, up from 236 in July and 135 in March.
- Electrek counted 8 NHTSA-filed Cybercab crashes about two months into Austin rides, including one at 53 mph with a test driver in Philadelphia, and a separate NHTSA report listed three incidents attributed to Tesla, including a Dallas Model Y that hit a pothole near a work zone and injured a rider.
- A panelist claims, citing an unnamed third party, that no new Cybercabs were added to Texas registrations after Oct 28, though the claim is unconfirmed and the surge to 773 came days later, so the signal is mixed.
- Both TSLA (-2.69% Nov 16) and SPCX (-1.26% Nov 16, then -3.53% Nov 19) fell after the Starship catch and Cybercab news, with the Electrified host attributing weakness to success already being priced in; Musk answered "of course" when asked whether moderate Cybercab success is priced into a roughly $1.6T valuation.
- Musk's FSD v15 "25x" critical-intervention claim has no published baseline, Tesla's v14.2.1 handling drew Electrek criticism, and the host expects early regressions from the new architecture.
