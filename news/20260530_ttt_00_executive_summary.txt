## Robotaxi

- **Texas Level 4 self-certification live as of today.** Texas's new autonomous vehicle law took effect on May 30, allowing manufacturer self-certification rather than third-party validation. Tesla certified its Cybercab software as Level 4 on day one, making it legally authorized to operate driverless commercially within a defined operating area — the regulatory unlock bears had called years away.

- **Ashok Elluswamy signals autonomous transit from factory to fleet.** Tesla's head of AI quote-tweeted Elon Musk's factory self-drive video saying Cybercab would soon be "driving itself into Austin city, reporting for duty." The ~20-mile public-road drive from Giga Texas to the Austin operating area — highway, suburb, and city — would mean the fleet can grow itself without human transport drivers. Engineering leadership posting public timelines is described as highly unusual.

- **Elon Musk's factory self-drive video: 15 million views.** Musk posted a 30-second clip captioned "Cybercab driving itself out of the Giga Texas factory." Unlike a Model Y self-parking, the Cybercab has no steering wheel and no pedals — no manual fallback exists, making this a harder engineering bar.

- **Multi-city pre-staging confirmed simultaneously.** Spotters Chris Dearduff (Dallas), SE Robinson Jr. (Houston), and SLA Doc documented Cybercabs in tandem rows at staging hubs in all three Texas cities on the same day the authorization landed. SLA Doc noted the Houston hub's Tesla inventory side had been fully cleared and replaced with red and black Model Y robotaxis and Cybercabs, indicating repurposing from sales to fleet operations. Per SLA Doc: "My guess is that removing the steering wheel will be the final step before they officially join the robotaxi fleet."

- **Cybercabs in Austin confirmed with Starlink Mini antennas.** First spotted May 26 and confirmed by Nick Cruz Patane, many Austin Cybercabs carry flat Starlink Mini units on the rear cabin. Strategic implications: backup connectivity, operating-area expansion beyond cellular coverage maps, and a cross-company vertical integration play that doesn't appear on either Tesla's or SpaceX's balance sheet.

- **Bhakdi flags FIFA World Cup and H1 city-expansion deadline as June catalysts.** World Cup games in Dallas give Cybercabs a high-visibility stage. More critically, Bhakdi argues Tesla's Q4 earnings guidance committed to unlocking additional robotaxi cities in H1 2026, leaving roughly 30 days to deliver before the self-imposed deadline expires — a catalyst he believes the market has not fully priced.

- **Gaps between staged and live remain real.** Staged Cybercabs still have steering wheels attached. Texas's self-certification standard carries no third-party validation, and one serious incident could reopen regulatory scrutiny. A dozen units at a Dallas hub is not a fleet — thousands per city are needed to move the revenue needle.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD Supervised approved in three European countries.** Per Sawyer Merritt, regulatory approval is now live in three European markets, with language "and counting" suggesting further approvals in progress. No country names were specified.

- **Zero-intervention Canada drive completed: 3,760 miles.** A full transcontinental drive across Canada — 3,760 miles (6,051 km) — was completed with zero interventions, the first on record. A user demonstration rather than a regulatory event, but a high-visibility proof point for long-distance, varied-terrain FSD capability relevant to international expansion.

- **China FSD fraud trial: first hearing held today.** A Beijing court heard the opening of a consumer fraud lawsuit filed by 10 Tesla owners seeking 3.95 million yuan (~$583,000). Plaintiffs paid 56,000 yuan each (~$7,800) for FSD between 2019 and 2021 and were excluded when Tesla's Chinese software rollout supported only HW4 — covering all vehicles produced 2019–2023. Tesla disputed the claims, saying some functions are "fully operational" while others are "partially functional" or still in development.

- **Tesla's China rebranding weakens its defense.** Nine days before the hearing, Tesla confirmed FSD (Supervised) availability in China. One week before, it renamed the system "Tesla Assisted Driving" in the Chinese market — a move Electrek characterizes as a tacit admission the original branding was misleading. Plaintiffs purchased under the "Full Self-Driving" name.

- **Grok Build actively used to accelerate FSD and Cybercab development.** Per Cern Basher (Brilliant Advice), Tesla's AI VP confirmed Grok Build is in active use for both programs. Tesla AI engineers have effectively unlimited token access within the Musk ecosystem's vertically integrated compute infrastructure — a productivity advantage over teams paying per-token to external providers.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Macrohard (Digital Optimus) quoted in SpaceX S-1; product targeted end of 2026.** SpaceX's prospectus states: "We believe Macrohard has the potential to fundamentally transform how companies across all industries are structured and operate, thereby allowing dramatic increases in human productivity and prosperity." Described as a joint Tesla-SpaceX agentic AI that replicates computer-based human work via vision rather than APIs — capable of operating legacy software interfaces without custom integrations. Elon Musk has indicated a product launch by end of 2026. Basher draws a direct valuation comparison to Anthropic's trajectory from near-zero to close to $1 trillion in four to five years.

- **Optimus building at Giga Texas: vertical structure just starting, production unlikely before 2027–2028.** Per Brian (FutureAzA), vertical structural elements have only just begun going up on the Optimus manufacturing facility. The building is large and unlikely to be substantially complete in 2026; equipment installation after construction adds further lead time. Brian cautioned against expecting robot production by end of 2026, estimating end of 2027 or 2028 as more realistic.

- **SpaceX framed as Optimus's largest long-term customer.** Basher argues Moon bases and Mars colonies are environments where Optimus is not merely useful but essential — and no competing robotics company has equivalent privileged access to that demand. Near-term, SpaceX's Starbase, Starlink factories, and rocket manufacturing sites are cited as natural early deployment environments.

## Energy

- **SpaceX purchased ~$500M in Megapacks from Tesla in 2025**, up from just under $200M in 2024, per Basher. Demand is driven by AI data center power stabilization needs. Meta is also cited as a recent Megapack customer, suggesting broader data center adoption.

- **Corpus Christi lithium refinery: one of two lines operational, second line materials arriving.** Per Joe Tegtmeyer (FutureAzA), the kiln is running 24/7 on Line 1. Equipment consistent with Line 1's build-out has begun arriving for Line 2, though Tegtmeyer characterizes construction start as potentially toward year-end with no high urgency visible. Lithium hydroxide monohydrate from the refinery is already being delivered to the cathode plant at Giga Texas.

- **Full vertical integration expanding simultaneously.** Tegtmeyer confirmed the cathode plant at Giga Texas is receiving new equipment and adding covered storage, while the 4680 manufacturing area has active permit filings, interior construction, and concrete slabs being cut for new equipment. All three stages — lithium refining, cathode production, 4680 cells — are expanding at once.

- **Unique low-chemical process creates saleable byproduct.** Unlike conventional refineries using sulfuric acid and brine ponds, Tesla's facility produces an inert silicate-like byproduct it sells to local concrete manufacturers. The plant recycles most water and chemicals internally. An environmental runoff study raised concerns, but Tegtmeyer noted it lacked upstream baseline samples and that the process fundamentally differs from conventional lithium refining.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab certified at 165 Wh/mile** — ~35% more efficient than the most efficient Model 3, approximately one-third the energy use of Cybertruck. At 100,000 miles/year per vehicle, the efficiency gap versus a 250 Wh/mile baseline saves ~8,500 kWh/vehicle/year; at $0.12/kWh, that is ~$1,000/vehicle/year. Scaled to one million Cybercabs, the hardware efficiency advantage strips ~$1 billion/year from fleet operating costs.

- **Tesla Model Y price cut in India: ~12% reduction.** Tesla introduced a new "Premium RWD" variant while removing the Long Range RWD from the Indian lineup, lowering the entry price by approximately 12% vs. the initial launch configuration — a demand-stimulation shift toward volume over premium positioning in an early-stage market.

- **SpaceX purchased ~$131M in Cybertrucks in 2025.** A large fleet is deployed at Starbase. Basher characterizes this as the right vehicle for SpaceX's operational needs rather than a bailout — and notes the relationship could grow as SpaceX expands to new launch sites. Tesla Semi identified as a potential larger long-term SpaceX logistics vehicle.

## Financials

- **China FSD lawsuit: triple-damage exposure on 1M+ HW3 vehicles.** Under China's Consumer Rights Protection Law, plaintiffs seek full refunds plus triple damages. With an estimated 1 million+ HW3 vehicles in China and hundreds of additional owners consulting lawyers, Electrek's Fred Lambert argues scaled exposure could reach billions. Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in FSD/Autopilot-related lawsuits globally. No ruling timeline was disclosed.

- **SpaceX IPO expected ~June 12, targeting $75B+ raise.** Per Basher, proceeds are earmarked in part for the initial phase of Terafab — the joint Tesla-SpaceX chip fabrication initiative with Intel already announced as a partner. Nvidia is cited as a potential capital partner.

## Market & Competition

- **Terafab positions Tesla-SpaceX as chip-independent at scale.** Basher frames the joint fab initiative as vertical integration of the semiconductor supply chain required to scale robotaxi and Optimus without external bottlenecks. SpaceX is already the largest printed circuit board manufacturer in the U.S. via its Starlink terminal factory — cited as proof of the ecosystem's track record in manufacturing vertical integration.

- **Tesla lithium refinery's three-year head start described as durable moat.** Competitors who waited while lithium prices were elevated remain without equivalent domestic refining infrastructure. The combination of the unique low-chemical process, saleable byproducts, and simultaneous cathode and 4680 expansion is presented as difficult to replicate quickly.

- **Tesla brand reputation recovered sharply from May 2025 nadir.** A poll of the 100 most visible companies showed Tesla fell to 95th place (5th from last) by May 2025 — coinciding with vandalism and violence against sales and service centers. A more recent version of the same poll shows significant recovery. Per Brian and Herbert (FutureAzA), negative coverage is driven by click economics rather than coordinated campaigns, and improving sentiment is expected to shift editorial tone as publishers follow engagement.
