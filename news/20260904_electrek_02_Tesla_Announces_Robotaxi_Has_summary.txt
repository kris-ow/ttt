Tesla announced its Robotaxi fleet has driven a total of 1 million unsupervised miles (no human anywhere in the control loop), a figure disclosed by VP of Autopilot/AI Ashok Elluswamy at Tesla's Cybercab event. The author notes this is a large jump from the 380,000 unsupervised miles reported six weeks earlier at Tesla's Q2 earnings call, but cautions this doesn't necessarily signal accelerating growth since Tesla only began logging any unsupervised miles at the start of the year and has since shifted nearly all non-Bay-Area miles to unsupervised mode.

## Robotaxi
1. **Unsupervised mileage jumped sharply:** Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi miles rose from 380,000 (reported on the July 22 Q2 earnings call, with "zero notable incidents" claimed by Tesla despite at least 17 incidents noted in government documents as of May) to 1 million miles as of the Cybercab event — an increase of about 620,000 miles in roughly six weeks, per Elluswamy.
2. **Paid ridership had stalled, may now resume growth:** As of the Q2 call, total paid Robotaxi miles were around 2.5 million, with paid ridership flat at roughly 900,000 added miles in each of the prior two quarters. The new unsupervised-mile jump suggests total paid miles will likely grow again this quarter, according to the article's analysis.
3. **Cybercab deployment limited so far:** Reports cited by the article indicate only 45 Cybercabs have been registered for deployment in Austin — about one-tenth the number of Model Y Robotaxis registered in the city, only a small fraction of which operate at any given time.
4. **Tesla courting business clients:** At the same event, Tesla began pitching commercial/business clients on potential moneymaking opportunities using the technology, which the author notes depends on autonomy being reliable outside small geofenced areas — a goal it says remains unmet.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo far ahead on mileage:** Waymo announced last week it has completed 200 million miles, far exceeding Tesla's 1 million-mile unsupervised total. Waymo passed the 1-million-mile mark in 2023 and currently operates in 14 cities, with more planned.

## Editorial Stance
The author is skeptical of the "growth" framing, arguing the mileage jump partly reflects Tesla only recently starting to count any unsupervised miles rather than a genuine acceleration in fleet activity, and criticizes Tesla for pursuing commercial deals while ordinary FSD customers still cannot use full unsupervised capability.
