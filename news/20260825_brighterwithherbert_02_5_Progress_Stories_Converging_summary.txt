This episode of brighterwithherbert, featuring guest Jo Bhakdi, covers five converging progress stories across Tesla and SpaceX: expanded Cybercab testing across more US states and cities, German government interest in autonomous shuttles, visible construction progress at the Giga Texas Optimus factory, a new SpaceX-Nvidia orbital AI compute partnership, and JP Morgan's bullish SpaceX note citing Grok/Cursor momentum. The overall thesis is that incremental, converging progress across both companies is easy to underestimate because investors either over-extrapolate early hype or over-extrapolate a subsequent plateau.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab testing footprint expands sharply:** A community tracker (Py) reports Tesla's Cybercab testing has expanded to 24 states (the host misspoke "50" before correcting) and 88 cities as of the video's date, up from prior figures 20 days earlier — two new states and 32 new cities added, including new spotting in Buffalo, Queens, and Midtown Manhattan, New York. Bhakdi estimates roughly 1,000–2,000 Cybercabs are currently deployed nationally (not all in live service), a figure he says is higher than most people assume.
2. **Fleet math and scaling expectations:** Bhakdi and the host discuss that of the estimated ~2,000 deployed Cybercabs, roughly 1,000+ carry steering wheels for ongoing validation/testing in new cities and states and may never convert to driverless service; the remainder are more likely to scale into commercial use. Bhakdi believes Tesla could plausibly reach a fleet run-rate around 2,000 by September and over 10,000 by December if scaling proceeds normally, though he cautions this depends on execution.
3. **First Cybercab public rides expected in September:** The host has been invited to a Tesla event on September 3rd expected to be among the first opportunities to hail a Cybercab; Bhakdi expects the initial rollout to exceed a token number (more than 10 vehicles) rather than a purely ceremonial launch, while cautioning against high expectations.
4. **Germany explores autonomous shuttles with Tesla:** Brandenburg's transport minister (referred to by the host as Robert Kumbach/Crumbach) confirmed his ministry is in talks with Tesla about deploying driverless shuttles to serve rural areas currently underserved by bus service (some running only twice daily). Bhakdi frames this as politically significant but preliminary — the minister does not control FSD regulatory approval, but signals coordination among German states. Bhakdi estimates the total addressable market for rural on-demand robotaxi service at roughly 2 million vehicles across the US and Europe under conservative trip-frequency assumptions, acknowledging this estimate could be off by a wide margin.
5. **EU vote on supervised FSD approval looms:** The host cites an early-October date (potentially around October 8th) for an EU vote on approving supervised FSD; Bhakdi suggests Tesla has incentive to avoid a large-scale, risky Cybercab rollout beforehand to prevent any incident from jeopardizing the vote, favoring a smaller, flawless demonstration instead.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Giga Texas Optimus factory construction progressing fast:** Footage shared by a source (Joe Tegtmeyer) shows the Optimus factory site at Giga Texas going from bare dirt in March to a steel-and-roof structure within five months; the source estimates another six to eight months of construction remain before the structure is complete. The facility is intended to eventually produce Optimus robots at large scale (a stated target of 10 million units per year), though the video does not confirm a timeline for that capacity.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX and Nvidia design space-optimized AI rack for Starmind satellites:** Nvidia and SpaceX announced a space-optimized version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack system, intended to launch to orbit in Q4 2026 as the basis for the first-generation Starmind AI satellite, with significant scale-up planned for 2028. Elon Musk described the design as simpler, lower-cost, denser, and lighter than a traditional terrestrial rack. Bhakdi characterizes this as one rack per satellite, optimized for inference workloads (comparable speed to terrestrial data centers for inference, though inferior for large training clusters), and reiterates his own prediction of roughly a gigawatt of space-based compute by 2029, scaling 5–10x annually thereafter — a projection he acknowledges as his own forecast, not a company guidance figure.
2. **Bhakdi disputes a $1.5 trillion valuation skepticism take:** Bhakdi references a comment from another investor/commentator (Chris Camillo, an early SpaceX investor from around a $30 billion valuation) expressing skepticism that a $1.5 trillion valuation for SpaceX is realistic. Bhakdi disagrees, arguing that if earnings scale into the hundreds of billions, such a valuation would not be unreasonable, though this remains his own hypothetical framing rather than a company projection.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Terafab's first infrastructure component installed:** A source (Joe Tegtmeyer) reports Terafab has already installed its first piece of infrastructure — a Starlink ground station/gateway (similar to those at Starbase and the Bastrop Starlink factory) — which Bhakdi calls faster-than-expected progress. Bhakdi frames Terafab overall as more of a long-term strategic/competitive forcing function against Nvidia's chip dominance than a near-term chip manufacturing facility, expecting its ultimate ownership/operation structure to become clearer only years out.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **JP Morgan reiterates SpaceX overweight rating, $240 price target:** JP Morgan cited growing confidence in Grok's AI prospects and momentum from SpaceX's Cursor acquisition, noting Cursor's product (including a "Grok Bot" agent feature) is driving significant business adoption. The video states Cursor generates $4 billion in annual recurring revenue, with 75% from businesses.

## Market & Competition
1. **Bhakdi bullish on Grok/Cursor vs. Anthropic and OpenAI:** Bhakdi says he is "extremely bearish" on Anthropic and OpenAI relative to Grok and Chinese AI competitors, arguing Cursor's team excels at productization and user experience (citing new "Grok Bot" agent features, including shareable AI agents) while OpenAI/Anthropic tools remain clunky for real-world workflows. This is presented as his personal view based on hands-on testing, not confirmed company data.
