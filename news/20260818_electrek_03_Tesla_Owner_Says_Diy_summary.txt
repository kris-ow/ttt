The article covers a Tesla owner's DIY hardware upgrade from HW3 to HW4, using it to argue Tesla could be doing these swaps more proactively than it claims, and situates this against Tesla's long history of unfulfilled full self-driving hardware promises and mounting legal exposure.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **History of hardware promises:** Tesla's 2016 blog post claimed all cars then being produced had "full self-driving hardware," but the original hardware (HW1) proved insufficient, leading to successive versions HW2, HW3, and now HW4 (also branded AI4/AI4+), with future AI5 and AI6 still promised. Tesla offered free HW2→HW3 swaps for FSD purchasers (though the article notes Tesla still charges some owners $1,000 for this hardware), while HW3→HW4 is described by Tesla as more complex since it requires upgrading cameras.
2. **DIY upgrade case:** Michal Gapinski, a Polish Tesla owner with a 2022 Model 3 (HW3) who runs a company called Tesla Android, posted on Twitter/X that he retrofitted his car to HW4, calling the process "not as bad as it seemed" and requiring only "light ECU hacking" and materials like epoxy and zip ties. The project is still incomplete — he says he needs "a bit of stuff left to get all the features" — and no driving video has been shared, only photos of service mode and the in-progress work. Gapinski said Tesla "could have done it ages ago."
3. **Musk's microfactory proposal:** In April, Elon Musk proposed building "microfactories" in major cities to handle HW3-to-HW4 swaps at scale, but the article says there has been no further update on this plan since.
4. **Legal and business context:** The article states that court cases in multiple territories have found Tesla liable to repay owners over false advertising related to undelivered self-driving capability, and that individual small-claims cases and country-wide class actions (which the article says could cost Tesla tens of billions of dollars) are pending; Tesla's stated response to these class actions has been to tell owners to "be patient." Tesla has also stopped selling FSD as an upfront purchase, moved to a subscription-only model, and no longer allows transferring a purchased FSD license to a new vehicle.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla owes free (not paid) HW4 upgrades and license transfers/refunds to owners who purchased FSD, given legal findings of false advertising, and contends Tesla's "microfactory" idea is more feasible than previously assumed given Gapinski's solo DIY success. The author characterizes Tesla's current approach as effectively "no plan, other than running out the clock," betting that HW3 cars will gradually leave the road and reduce Tesla's obligation, while legal and public pressure builds against that strategy.
