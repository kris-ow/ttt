This Munro Live episode is a design-focused walkthrough of the Rivian R2 with Rivian's Chief Design Officer, Jeff Hammoud, covering cost-reduction strategies, body structure, interior trim choices, and styling carried over from the R1. Tesla and SpaceX are not mentioned; the only relevance to TTT's mission is that Rivian is a competitor in the broader EV market that Tesla investors track.

## Market & Competition
1. **Rivian R2 positioned as a lower-cost, smaller sibling to the R1:** Rivian's Chief Design Officer describes the R2 as a distillation of the R1's design essence into a smaller, cheaper package, with the standard trim priced at $45,000; the vehicle keeps R1's fast windshield angle and two-box silhouette while shortening the rear overhang (no third row) and pushing wheels further to the corners.
2. **Off-road specs and cost-engineering detail:** The R2 offers 9.6 inches of ground clearance and 32-inch outer-diameter tires across all variants (compared by the designer to a Jeep Wrangler), while extensive engineering effort went into full glass drop without a division bar or belt molding, simplified wheel liners with fewer fasteners despite high wading-depth requirements, and reduced-cost interior materials (molded plastics below a "waistline," simplified seat wrapping) intended to preserve a premium feel while cutting parts and cost versus the R1.

Notable quotes and data:
- R2 standard trim price: $45,000.
- Ground clearance: 9.6 inches (245 mm); tires: 32-inch outer diameter across all variants, compared to a Jeep Wrangler.
- Designer's quote on hidden engineering work: "your best job at doing this type of work is when the customer doesn't pay attention to it."
- Rivian CEO's reported directive that the R1 underbody "should look like something that a kid would want a poster on their wall."
- R2 interior comes in a standard trim (one interior option) plus premium/performance trims offering two interior choices (Coastal Cloud and Black Crater).
