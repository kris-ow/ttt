## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Morgan Stanley Flags Sharp FSD Mileage Acceleration:** In a new $TSLA note, Morgan Stanley reported that Tesla owners are now driving approximately **26.7 million miles per day** with FSD active, up from **18.5 million miles/day in April 2026** — a roughly 44% increase in roughly one month. Morgan Stanley explicitly tracks this metric as a proxy for both FSD subscriber growth and increasing engagement among existing subscribers. No specific subscriber count or revenue figure was included in the post, but the acceleration in daily FSD mileage is a strong leading indicator of FSD monetization momentum and data accumulation for the autonomous driving thesis. This post drew the highest engagement of the day (3.2K likes, 373 reposts), suggesting significant investor attention.

2. **$145M Tesla Megapack Facility Coming Online in Australia:** A new battery energy storage facility called Limondale BESS in Australia — valued at **$145 million** — is about to be activated. It comprises **144 Tesla Megapacks**, uniquely registered to charge at **100 megawatts** and discharge at **50 megawatts**. This deployment reinforces the continued real-world scaling of Tesla's Energy division and represents a notable single-project revenue event for Megapack.

3. **Waymo Expands with New "Ojai" Robotaxi Vehicle:** Waymo announced the launch of public rides in its new robotaxi platform called the **Ojai**. Initially, select riders in **San Francisco, Phoenix, and Los Angeles** will have access to free rides, with plans to expand to more riders and new cities over coming months. The free-ride introductory model mirrors prior Waymo expansion playbooks. While this is a competitor development rather than a Tesla story, it is relevant context for the robotaxi market: Waymo continues to expand its footprint and introduce new hardware just as Tesla is scaling its own Cybercab/robotaxi fleet.

4. **High-End EV Competition: Mercedes AMG GT EV Pricing Revealed:** Mercedes officially announced pricing for its all-electric AMG GT 4-Door Coupe EV. The **GT 55 starts at $180,000** and the **GT 63 starts at $228,000**, with fully loaded GT 63 configurations exceeding **$250,000**. Specs include up to **315 miles EPA range**, up to **1,153 hp**, a **2.0-second 0-60 mph** time, and a peak charging speed of **600 kW**. This competes at the ultra-premium end of the EV market, above Tesla's current lineup, and is unlikely to materially impact Tesla's volume or margin story.
