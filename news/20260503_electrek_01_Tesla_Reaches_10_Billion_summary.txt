Tesla's FSD (Supervised) fleet has crossed 10 billion miles — the exact threshold Elon Musk set in January 2026 as necessary for "safe unsupervised" driving. The article examines whether this milestone actually signals readiness for Level 4 autonomy, and concludes it does not.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **10 billion mile milestone reached, but goalposts have moved:** Tesla's safety page confirms the FSD (Supervised) fleet has surpassed 10 billion miles. Musk designated this figure as the data threshold needed for safe unsupervised driving in January 2026 — after Tesla failed to deliver unsupervised FSD by end of 2025 as previously promised. Notably, Musk had previously indicated 6 billion miles would be sufficient, making 10 billion itself a moved goalpost.

2. **Data collection rate has accelerated sharply:** The fleet was logging approximately 29 million miles per day by late April 2026, up from 14 million miles per day at the start of the year.

3. **Tesla's safety figures carry methodological caveats:** Tesla's safety page reports one major collision per 5.3 million miles under FSD (Supervised), versus one per 660,000 miles for the average U.S. driver. However, experts have criticized Tesla's methodology, noting it uses different crash-counting methods than the NHTSA data it compares against. Tesla's own Austin robotaxi fleet, which logged roughly 800,000 miles through February 2026, reported 14 crashes to NHTSA — approximately four times the average human-driven crash rate in similar urban conditions.

4. **Consumer unsupervised FSD pushed to Q4 2026 at earliest:** Musk has deferred unsupervised FSD for consumer vehicles to Q4 2026 at the earliest, with heavy caveats noted.

## Market & Competition

5. **Waymo contrast — liability and scale:** Waymo operates at Level 4 across 10 cities with driverless operations, targeting 1 million weekly rides with its 6th-gen system. Waymo reports 90% fewer serious injury-causing crashes and 82% fewer airbag deployments than human drivers across 127 million miles of autonomous driving. The article emphasizes the key distinction: Waymo accepts legal responsibility for driving; Tesla does not under its "Full Self-Driving" branding.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that 10 billion miles is a marketing milestone, not a functional unlock for autonomy — and that Musk has been wrong about FSD timelines more than any other topic. Lambert contends the real barrier is not data volume but Tesla's unwillingness to accept legal liability for unsupervised driving, and that no specific mileage number can resolve the near-infinite long tail of edge cases.
