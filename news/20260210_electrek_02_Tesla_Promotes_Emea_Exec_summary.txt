Tesla has promoted EMEA Vice President Joe Ward to oversee global sales, service, and delivery operations, a move reported by Bloomberg and occurring amid a severe European sales downturn and continued executive turnover.

## Financials / Market & Competition
1. **Ward's promotion follows another executive exit:** Joe Ward, a Tesla veteran since 2010 (started as a UK logistics intern, later led Sales Administration in the Netherlands from 2012) and EMEA VP for over five years, now leads global sales, service, and delivery. This follows Raj Jegannathan's departure after 13 years at Tesla; Jegannathan, an engineering executive (VP of IT, AI Infrastructure, Apps, Infosec, and Vehicle Service Operations), had been leading sales operations since July 2025 after filling in for Troy Jones, who left as VP of North America Sales after 15 years. The article frames this as part of a broader exodus of veteran executives, including vehicle program managers for Cybertruck, Model 3, and Model Y, and Omead Afshar, a former Musk aide who briefly led sales before leaving.

## Electric Vehicles
2. **European sales collapse continues into 2026:** Tesla's European sales fell 48% in Germany in 2025, with declines continuing in 2026: UK sales down 57% in January, Netherlands down 67%, and France down 42%. The article attributes this to a lack of new models or significant vehicle refreshes and to Elon Musk's political activities alienating European buyers.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls Ward's promotion an "interesting choice," noting he is a seasoned veteran but is being elevated from Tesla's worst-performing region. The author argues sales leadership turnover is a symptom, not the cause, of Tesla's demand problem, and that with automotive revenue representing about 70% of Tesla's $24.9 billion last-quarter total, fixing sales is critical — but characterizes Ward's role as merely slowing the decline of Tesla's auto business while Musk pursues AI and robotics ambitions. A top-voted reader comment (21 likes) argues sales declines aren't Ward's fault and criticizes Musk's pay package instead.
