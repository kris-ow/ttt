This video features futurist Brian Wang (Next Big Future) arguing that Tesla and SpaceX's combined ability to rapidly deploy batteries, natural gas turbines, and virtual power plant software to unlock "stranded" US grid capacity could create an AI energy business potentially larger than Tesla's robotaxi opportunity, generating tens to hundreds of billions in annual revenue per gigawatt deployed.

## Energy
1. **Grid peak-shaving unlocks massive hidden capacity:** Wang cites a Duke Energy paper ("Rethinking Load Growth") arguing that curtailing power draw during evening peak hours (roughly 16 of 168 hours per week, ~10% of the time) could unlock up to 100 gigawatts of new load on the existing US grid without new power plants — because utilities today can't commit power due to peak constraints, not average capacity limits.
2. **Tesla's Megablock/Megapod strategy as the enabling "Lego" pieces:** Tesla's factory-built Megablock batteries (Wang cites a claim of 1 gigawatt-hour deployable in 20 business days, shown at a Tesla Energy event roughly nine months prior), combined with mobile natural gas turbines (from Caterpillar, GE, Mitsubishi, APR Energy, and South Korea's Doosan) and virtual power plant software, let Tesla/SpaceX supply the "missing" peak power in months rather than the ~5 years typical for new turbine orders, according to Wang.
3. **Turbine fleet scaling claims:** Wang states SpaceX/xAI's turbine fleet grew from 27 to 46 units (16–38 megawatts each) between roughly December and May, adding ~700 megawatts toward the ~1 gigawatt powering Colossus 2; he also references Doosan orders (five 380-megawatt units, ~1.9 gigawatts) with some deliveries expected before end of 2026 and others in 2027, and Starship-based AI compute payloads potentially adding 7–14 megawatts per launch starting as early as Q3 2026/Q2 2027 (Wang's own estimate, expressed with uncertainty).
4. **Regulatory/permitting workaround claim:** Wang asserts many of these turbine installations are proceeding without full air permits, with lawsuits pending on some units, and predicts the Trump administration would frame this as a national security matter to let deployment continue — a speculative characterization from Wang, not confirmed regulatory fact.
5. **Master Plan Part 3 framing:** Wang frames the current grid-shaping capability as only a small fraction (he estimates ~2%) of Tesla's broader "Master Plan Part 3" vision of electrifying the world, suggesting the combined Tesla-SpaceX entity could become the world's largest energy company powering the world's largest AI business.

## Financials
1. **Revenue-per-gigawatt monetization claims:** Wang extrapolates from Google's reported ~$11 billion/year deal for ~0.2 gigawatts of B200 compute (110,000 GPUs) to argue each gigawatt of AI compute can generate roughly $50–100 billion/year in revenue at 50%+ margins, and that Tesla/SpaceX could scale toward 10–20 gigawatts/year within two to three years — figures he acknowledges sound extreme but says are "not made up," based on realized deals.
2. **Cost advantage from Tesla's own AI5 chip:** Wang claims that if Tesla's AI5 chip matches Nvidia Blackwell performance on inference (per Elon Musk's stated claim) at roughly one-tenth the cost (~$3 billion vs. ~$30 billion per gigawatt of chips), Tesla/SpaceX could deploy far more compute per dollar of capex than competitors reliant on Nvidia hardware — contingent on TSMC/Samsung production volume.
3. **Individual turbine/substation deal economics:** Wang walks through several bottoms-up figures: a single 30-megawatt gas turbine could generate ~$2 billion/year in compute-hosting revenue; a $50 million payment to speed a Tennessee Valley Authority substation could unlock 150 megawatts worth an estimated $7.5 billion/year, and scaling that across 10 locations could yield ~$75 billion/year — presented as illustrative, back-of-envelope extrapolations rather than confirmed guidance.

## Market & Competition
1. **xAI/Grok positioned to challenge Anthropic/OpenAI on compute monetization:** Wang argues that once Tesla/SpaceX's own frontier AI models (Grok, and potentially Optimus-related AI) are selling inference directly to customers rather than just renting out compute, the revenue per gigawatt could exceed simple rental economics — pushing back on the assumption that Anthropic and OpenAI will dominate the AI compute market.
2. **Speed advantage vs. competitors in standing up chips:** Wang cites an analysis (from a firm he refers to as Epic AI) showing Tesla/SpaceX stood up compute capacity in about one year versus two to eight years for other companies in comparable buildouts, framing speed itself as a major profitability driver since faster deployment reduces financing costs.
