Google just opened its AI paper reviewer
to the whole world. It already gave
private feedback on more than 16,000
research papers. It's called Deep Review
from Google Research and it's not a
chatbot. Google calls it an agentic
system that checks the technical details
in complex documents. AI made writing
way faster. But the number of people who
can review it stayed flat. Google says
three of the biggest AI conferences got
more than 74,000 submissions this year.
Three years ago, it was under 25,000.
Writing got cheap. Checking didn't. So,
how does an AI check a whole paper
without making stuff up? Four steps per
Google. It cuts the document into
logical pieces. It gives the dense
parts, like proofs, more compute.
Specialized agents review every piece at
the same time, each one seeing the full
document. Then one last agent pulls it
together, cross-ch checkcks the sections
and checks claims against outside
sources so it doesn't hallucinate. Does
it work? In one conference pilot, Google
says more than half the authors ran
brand new experiments because of the
feedback. Now anyone can use it. Two
free reviews a week on PDFs or plain
text and more with a Gemini API key. It
runs on Gemini 4 Argon and Google says
uploads aren't used to train its models.
Google says it handles design docs,
legal and financial documents, too.
[music] Here's the part I care about. An
agent is AI as a worker. You give it a
goal, it breaks that into tasks, does
them, checks the work, and goes again.
And the way I see it, consistency is the
key. Models drift. So, you have to build
safeguards around them. Deep review is
that safeguard for a whole document. My
projection, every AI that writes is
going to need an AI that checks it
because writing got cheap and checking
didn't. The checker becomes the product
and Google just opened its checker to
the whole world.
