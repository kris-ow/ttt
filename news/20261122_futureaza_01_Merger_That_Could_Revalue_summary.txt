The host and guest Herbert discuss which events in the next 12-24 months could truly revalue Tesla and SpaceX, as opposed to merely moving the stocks. The host's thesis is that a Tesla–SpaceX merger is the dominant catalyst because it could force index providers to reclassify the company as technology. Herbert argues most other items (FSD, robotaxi, Optimus, Terafab, Starlink) are incremental or slower to show up in earnings.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger as the main revaluation event:** The host says the merger would turn Tesla into a combined SpaceX–Tesla entity, and the real revaluation comes when Nasdaq and the S&P stop classifying it as a consumer/automotive company and treat it as technology. Citing Alexandra Merz, he says the consumer ETF sector holds about $23 billion in assets versus $110-120 billion for technology, roughly four to five times more. He argues the resulting passive inflows would overshadow any merger premium paid to Tesla shareholders. He expects this around September or October 2027, if everything works out. Herbert says index classification is outside his expertise and he cannot say whether it will happen.

## Financials
1. **FSD adoption and margin:** Herbert says institutional analysts forecast slow FSD adoption, which he thinks is wrong because FSD either works or it doesn't. He cites Ashok Elluswamy and CFO Vaibhav Taneja as saying the adoption rate on new cars reached 77% last quarter, up from 50% two quarters earlier, with buyers subscribing despite a free month. He frames FSD revenue as near-pure profit and says a million cars a year would add about $100 million a month, or $1.2 billion annually.
2. **Terafab:** Herbert says chips and solar will not move numbers soon. Chip value only shows up through avoided purchases and supply delays, and markets rarely credit unbuilt capacity. Even at a 10% probability of success, he says a discounted-cash-flow view would add substantial value, but markets want proof first. He credits Cern Basher with a good attempt at showing what Terafab could be worth.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Regulation is the gating factor:** Herbert says FSD growth now depends on approvals. He mentions an EU vote around December 3 or 6, possible expansion in China, and other countries.
2. **Robotic trucking is secondary:** Herbert says Ashok Elluswamy has said trucking comes only after the robotaxi problem is solved, with consideration starting around the end of 2027. Herbert expects deployment in about three years and little impact within 24 months.

## Robotaxi
1. **What makes it a stock catalyst:** Herbert says robotaxi becomes a real catalyst at around 5,000 vehicles, once quarterly earnings growth is visible. He compares this to Nvidia, whose stock moved only after chip purchases grew quarter after quarter. He says a meaningful part of Cybercab and robotaxi is already priced in, and that Elon Musk replied "Yes, of course" to a post from Gali making that point.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **No significant Optimus revenue in 24 months:** Herbert says Musk's 2027 plan is to build 5,000-20,000 robots for internal use through the Optimus Academy, with no third-party sales until 2028 (Tesla's Q3 call guided to external Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027). Fremont will not reach a million-per-year rate until at least the end of 2027. Joe Tegtmeyer estimates the Giga Texas building shell will be done in about six months. Herbert's best case is 40,000-50,000 robots by the end of 2027, and he expects a margin impact only near month 24. He cites a seven-to-one robot-to-car production ratio, so 50,000 cars would translate to 350,000 robots, but he says that scale is two to three years out.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Steady but outsized by AI:** Herbert says only about two Starships a month would be needed to outpace current Starlink capacity additions, producing a continuous flow of money. He compares Starlink Mobile to robotaxi for Tesla, but says it pales next to AI data centers. He says SpaceX's market cap is already $1.8 trillion and that Starlink won't be what moves the stock.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Reusability milestone met a muted reaction:** Herbert calls the recent Starship ship catch monumental because reusability sharply cuts launch costs and enables orbital data centers. The shares fell a little anyway. He attributes this to investors waiting for a product that sells, calling these story stocks until real performance shows.
