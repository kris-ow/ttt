The host (futureaza) and guest Randy Kirk debate whether Tesla will build a "Cyber SUV," pointing to a small background model shown near the Cybercab area as the only real hint so far. They tie the discussion to weak Cybertruck delivery numbers and speculate about how a lower-cost SUV variant, possibly dual-purpose for human driving and future robotaxi use, could salvage underused production capacity.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck demand looks weak, though ramp effects muddy the picture:** The host argues Cybertruck sales are "low and a bit disappointing," comparing the softness to the Model S/X volume decline that preceded the cancellation of a shift. Randy Kirk pushes back, noting reservations/orders reportedly stretch out to January, meaning low output could reflect intentional pacing rather than pure lack of demand.
2. **Q2 combined delivery figure raises concern:** Randy Kirk cites a combined delivery number of 12,364 units for Cybertruck, Model S, Model X, and Semi for the recent quarter, which he says is inadequate for a production line designed to run at a much higher volume; he notes this figure likely also includes a small number of Cybercab units that may have been internally transferred to count as deliveries (a point attributed to a commentator named James Stephenson).
3. **Cyber SUV concept discussed as a possible way to reuse the Cybertruck line:** The host makes the case that a Cyber SUV, potentially built on the Cybertruck platform using composite (non-stainless) body panels instead of steel, could be cheaper, lighter, and allow rounded styling while skipping the expensive paint shop — addressing both cost concerns and unused capacity on an underperforming line.
4. **Speculation on a premium SUV replacing Model X:** The host suggests a high-trim version of a Cyber SUV could serve as a replacement for the Model X, targeting buyers who want a "fancy" full-size SUV rather than a Model Y or Cybertruck; this is explicitly framed as speculation, not confirmed product plans.
5. **Six-door SUV concept floated as speculative:** The host recalls that Elon Musk had discussed a six-door SUV concept in recent weeks; both hosts treat this as likely brainstorming and highly uncertain, discussing at length the practical challenges (wheelbase length, feasibility) without confirming any real product plan. Randy Kirk calls the idea "bonkers" but concedes a six-door version could theoretically follow a standard four-door version.
6. **Dual-use design (steering wheel vs. no steering wheel) discussed as a hedge:** The host proposes that a human-drivable Cyber SUV could share an identical interior/cabin structure with a future unsupervised (steering-wheel-free) version, with only the dashboard and controls swapped later — allowing Tesla to sell volume now while keeping optionality for autonomous versions later.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comparison to Model S/X production shift cancellation:** The host recalls predicting Model S/X would not be cancelled unless production fell under one shift — which happened in a prior quarter — and argues similar volume weakness is now visible in Cybertruck numbers, prompting reconsideration of the line's future use.
