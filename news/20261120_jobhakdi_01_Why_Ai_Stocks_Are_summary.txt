The video argues that AI-linked stocks are badly undervalued despite surging earnings, citing the S&P 500's PEG ratio at its lowest level since 1995. The host's thesis is that the market is pricing in an AI collapse around 2028–2029 that he regards as delusional, and that a sentiment shift could multiply valuations across the whole AI trade, including SpaceX and Tesla.

## Market & Competition
1. **Stocks lag despite strong news:** The host says the Nasdaq composite is down 5.44% over six months, SpaceX is down 14%, Nvidia is down 28% (he references Jensen Huang's resignation and possible return), and Micron is down about 5%. He notes AMD is up 18% and Microsoft is about 8% up year to date. Nebius is down 40% over six months but still up 39% year to date. He sees this as proof that good news is being sold.
2. **Record-low PEG ratio:** He says the S&P 500 PEG ratio is 0.72x, the lowest since 1995. By comparison, he cites 2.4 after the 2020 COVID rally, 2.0 at the 2000 internet bubble peak, and 1.6 before the 2007 crash. He attributes the low reading mainly to exploding earnings rather than falling prices.
3. **"Nubble" thesis:** The host coins "nubble" (no one believes it) for the current market, which he describes as irrational anti-exuberance, the opposite of a bubble. In his view the market is assuming AI is an anomaly that implodes around 2028–2029, shown by Micron trading at a P/E of about 10 and a 2027 forward P/E of roughly 4–5. He says the smartest analysts, investors and operators he knows disagree with that assumption.
4. **Upside scenario:** If sentiment normalizes to a PEG of around 2, he argues the market and every AI stock, naming SpaceX, Tesla, Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix, AMD, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft, could triple without any earnings growth. He adds that earnings growth of 100% over two years for most of these companies, and 1,000% for SpaceX, would compound on top of that. These are his projections, not sourced forecasts.
