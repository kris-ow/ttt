This compressed episode from Dillon Loomis covers several fast-moving Tesla developments including the first production Cybercab engineering test in Austin, Optimus manufacturing line progress following a Lars Moravy interview, Q2 delivery forecasts, a Tesla Semi accident in Nevada, and a hire signaling Tesla's Terafab ambitions. The host's core thesis is that, despite investor frustration with years of delayed promises, Tesla is quietly building the infrastructure for transformative new industries, and a near-term robotaxi scaling announcement may be imminent around the July 4th holiday weekend.

## Robotaxi

1. **First Production Cybercab Engineering Test in Austin:** Tesla released video of what it described as the first production Cybercab undergoing an engineering test in Austin. The host notes Tesla has generated some confusion because earlier vehicles were thought to be production versions. Joe Tegtmeyer reported that Tesla is conducting extensive pickup and drop-off testing around the main factory, simulating passenger activities likely in preparation for the robotaxi service. Separately, a Cybercab was spotted in Austin with decals, no steering wheel, and a supervisor seated on the passenger side.

2. **USA 250th Anniversary Wrap and Anticipated Announcement:** Joe Tegtmeyer shared images of a Cybercab wrapped in special livery celebrating the US semiquincentennial — the 250th anniversary of the signing of the Declaration of Independence. The host, who has been predicting some level of robotaxi scaling "fireworks" for the summer, suggests the timing around July 4th could coincide with a major announcement, referencing a Tesla event teased for the following Tuesday.

3. **Cybercab Manufacturing Line ~90% Automated:** A key takeaway from the Lars Moravy interview (see Humanoid Bots section) was that the Cybercab manufacturing line is already approximately 90% automated. Tesla has also implemented what it is calling "full self-hearing" — a process where Cybercabs use their onboard microphones to autonomously listen for creaks or rattles during NVH testing, and with AI training can identify the source of a noise and flag what needs to be fixed.

4. **Potential Miami Expansion:** Coral Gables, Florida fire personnel participated in specialized training with Tesla engineers focused on Tesla's robotaxi platform. Tesla indicated its robotaxi service may potentially expand to the Miami area in the future.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Lars Moravy Interview — Optimus Manufacturing Lines Arriving:** Herbert and Jeff conducted an interview with Lars Moravy (Tesla VP Vehicle Engineering) in which he confirmed that the Model S/X lines at Fremont have been largely cleared out and the first Optimus manufacturing line has arrived in the United States. "Arrived" means physically delivered, not yet fully installed, though Moravy said installation has already begun. Validation of the manufacturing machines is happening at Tesla's German automation facility (formerly Kuka/Grohmann Engineering), after which lines are shipped to the US, where they can be powered on and reconnected in 2–3 days and fully proved out in about a week.

2. **~40 More Lines to Come; Production Timeline Pushed to Q4:** Moravy indicated there are approximately 40 more manufacturing lines to go, covering actuators, each limb and limb subcomponents, the torso, and the battery pack — a highly modular system. Despite the relatively fast individual line setup time, the sheer number of lines required means production is not expected to start until sometime in Q4 2026 at the earliest.

3. **Elon Musk Tempers Expectations on Optimus Scale:** Speculation circulated on X (from a user called Dr. Jack) that Tesla engineers realized they were ahead of schedule and that a major demo of Optimus V3 showing robots already in meaningful production was imminent. Elon Musk directly pushed back, stating that Optimus production will be "extremely slow at first as everything is new" and that "this is not like making a car." The host, who says he has been more bearish on Optimus timelines than most, reinforces that even if early test builds begin in 2026, no meaningful scale will materialize this year — calling predictions of 20,000, 50,000, or 100,000 units in 2026 unrealistic.

4. **Elon Musk Walks the Optimus Production Line:** Elon Musk shared a photo on social media with the caption indicating he was walking the Optimus production line at Fremont, signaling active executive involvement in the ramp-up.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Q2 2026 Delivery Forecasts:** Tesla is expected to release its Q2 2026 delivery numbers on Thursday (2026-07-03 implied). At the time of recording, forecasts varied widely: James Stevenson at 482,000; Troy Teslike at 466,000; Khi's forecast at 468,000; and the analyst consensus as low as 406,000. For context, the host notes Tesla's highest-ever Q2 delivery figure was Q2 2023 at 466,140 units.

2. **Tesla Semi Accident in Western Nevada:** A Tesla Semi struck two passenger vehicles that were stopped at a highway intersection approximately 40 miles east of Reno, Nevada. The married couple in one of the struck vehicles did not survive; a third vehicle's occupant suffered life-threatening injuries and was airlifted. Investigators indicated the semi driver may have fallen asleep. The host raises the question of why automatic emergency braking or other safety systems did not engage, and expects NHTSA to investigate the driver monitoring system. The host acknowledges this is a developing story.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla Terafab and Intel Hire:** Tesla has hired Gary Jiang, a veteran of Intel who most recently managed the construction of Intel's high-volume manufacturing facility in Arizona, overseeing installation of fab equipment, factory startup, certification, and the transfer of Intel's 18A (1.8 nanometer) process technology from a development fab in Oregon to high-volume production. The Terafab is set to license Intel's next-generation 14A (1.4 nanometer) process technology, and Tesla is reportedly Intel's first customer for 14A. The host notes 14A is not ready today, but Elon Musk has hinted it should be available by the time the Terafab reaches some level of manufacturing readiness. The current plan allocates approximately 20% of Terafab's compute for Earth-based applications and roughly 80% for orbital use (via SpaceX).

2. **Intel 18A Yield Progress:** As of June 2026, Intel has reportedly improved yields on its 18A process to approximately 80%, up significantly over the prior 6–9 months from earlier levels of roughly 30–40%. The host cautions this figure is largely industry speculation assembled from public breadcrumbs, as Intel has not disclosed exact numbers. While 80% is described as respectable progress, it is still not best-in-class (target is 90–95%). Progress on 18A is expected to inform learnings for the 14A node, which in turn de-risks Tesla's Terafab investment.
