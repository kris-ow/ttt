This video is a first-person ride-along in one of the first Tesla Cybercabs deployed to the public in Austin, with the host and a companion documenting the vehicle's interior, software, and driving behavior, plus a Q&A with the in-car Grok assistant about specs and software version.

## Robotaxi
1. **First public Cybercab ride:** The host and a companion took what they describe as one of the very first Cybercabs available for public pickup in Austin, riding without any safety driver, steering wheel, or pedals in the vehicle — distinct from Model Y robotaxis in the fleet, which retain a steering wheel.
2. **In-cabin features:** The Cybercab has a large, high-resolution touchscreen (described by the host as noticeably clearer/higher pixel density than their home setup) supporting Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, Apple Podcasts, Hulu, and Netflix; seats recline significantly via touchscreen or app controls; the cabin has adjustable accent lighting and dome lights; doors are butterfly-style; climate and media preferences save to a rider profile and carry over between rides; and the app can edit the destination or drop-off point mid-ride.
3. **Rider pickup detection:** Per the in-car Grok assistant, the vehicle detects an approaching rider's phone and the closest door swings open automatically once it picks up the signal.
4. **Ride quality observations:** Both riders described the ride as very smooth and comfortable, noting the vehicle handled traffic, turns, and stops in a way that felt smoother than a human driver; the host said he "completely forgot" he was in a self-driving vehicle.
5. **No side mirrors:** The host noted the Cybercab has no side-view mirrors.
6. **Grok's stated specs (unverified/leaked):** In response to questions, the in-car Grok assistant said it had no confirmed official specs, but relayed what it called "unofficial chatter": a 48 kWh battery, 165 Wh/mile efficiency, an active hood for pedestrian safety, at least 10 airbags, and a manual emergency charge cable release hidden behind the rear wheel liner. Grok explicitly caveated that these figures come from leaks, not official Tesla information, and no exact software build number was confirmed.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Software version in the robotaxi fleet:** Tesla engineers reportedly told the host at an event that the vehicle they rode was running FSD version 15, though it was unclear whether this was the finalized version; Grok separately said unofficial reports point to early FSD v15 builds running on the robotaxi fleet (including Cybercabs), separate from the consumer FSD 14.3 branch, with no exact build number confirmed.
2. **Starlink hardware:** Grok said the rider rules mention Starlink hardware and that "some" Cybercabs have a flush Starlink dish on the roof, saying this particular vehicle likely has it too, but had no information on how riders could connect to it.
