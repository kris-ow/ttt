Jo Bhakdi analyzes SpaceX's newly amended S1 IPO filing — scheduled to go public on June 12, 2026 — and argues that the authorization of 36 billion shares (against only ~12 billion currently issued) is a strong signal that SpaceX is structurally preparing to acquire Tesla, not merely raise capital. The core thesis is that the share authorization is far too large to be explained by normal capital raises or conventional acquisitions, and can only make sense in the context of a Tesla merger — potentially structured as a 1-to-2 or even 1-to-3 share exchange favoring Tesla shareholders.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX S1 share structure signals a Tesla acquisition:** The amended SpaceX S1 reveals two share classes: Class A common stock (~6.91 billion shares issued and outstanding, 1 vote per share) and Class B shares (~5.95 billion, 10 votes per share, held predominantly by Elon Musk). Total shares currently defining SpaceX ownership are ~12.86 billion. However, 36 billion shares have been authorized — roughly three times the total current share count. Bhakdi argues this cannot be explained by capital raises (which would never dilute SpaceX by 300%) or normal acquisitions (which typically involve 1–10% of shares), and can only be intended for a major acquisition of a company valued two to three times SpaceX — i.e., Tesla.

2. **Legal constraints delay a merger announcement post-IPO:** Bhakdi notes that SpaceX cannot announce a Tesla acquisition immediately after going public on June 12, 2026, as failing to disclose a known acquisition intent in the S1 would constitute a violation of disclosure duties. A meaningful gap between the IPO and any merger announcement is therefore legally necessary, placing the earliest realistic merger initiation in 2027.

3. **Predicted valuation trajectory sets up a 1-to-2 merger:** Bhakdi's central scenario for early 2027 is Tesla reaching a $3 trillion market cap (~$800/share) while SpaceX deflates approximately 25% from its IPO valuation of ~$2 trillion, landing at ~$1.5 trillion. This would make a 1-to-1 merger impractical and instead suggest a 1-to-2 structure, in which SpaceX issues ~24 billion new shares to acquire Tesla — well within the 36 billion authorized. He argues SpaceX could technically even support a 1-to-3 merger if Tesla reached $4 trillion against a $1 trillion SpaceX.

4. **Elon's control and economics remain intact under a 1-to-2 merger:** In a 1-to-1 merger (both entities at equal valuation), Elon's economic ownership of the combined entity would be ~34% and his voting control ~81%. In a 1-to-2 merger (Tesla twice the size of SpaceX), his economic ownership declines modestly to ~29–30% — a difference Bhakdi characterizes as negligible — while his voting control remains ~78%, well above both the 51% and 66% control thresholds. Even in a 1-to-3 scenario, Elon would retain a total voting majority due to his dominance of the 10x Class B shares.

5. **Tesla shareholders would have strong incentive to vote yes:** In a 1-to-2 merger, Tesla shareholders would receive two-thirds of the combined entity while effectively acquiring SpaceX at half its standalone value. Bhakdi argues this eliminates any risk of a shareholder vote rejection — particularly if Tesla shares are at $800 by the time a vote is called, with robotaxi scaling already underway. This structure, he contends, solves all alignment problems simultaneously: Elon retains control, Tesla shareholders are rewarded, and SpaceX gains a path to funding its orbital AI ambitions projected for 2028.

6. **Bhakdi's investment stance: hold Tesla, skip SpaceX IPO:** Bhakdi states he is not participating in the SpaceX IPO, citing a high probability of paper losses over the subsequent 9 months post-IPO, and believes Tesla is the superior vehicle for capturing the combined upside. He remains long-term bullish on SpaceX but views the IPO entry point as unfavorable relative to Tesla.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab scaling as a forcing function for Tesla's valuation:** Bhakdi reiterates his previously stated projection of 15,000 Cybercabs operating unsupervised by end of 2026, which he acknowledges is criticized but stands behind based on observed facts on the ground. He argues this Cybercab scaling is the key catalyst that would drive Tesla to a $3 trillion valuation (~$800/share) and eliminate the era of delays, making the merger math work in Tesla shareholders' favor.
