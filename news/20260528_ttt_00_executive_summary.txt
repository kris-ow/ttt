## Robotaxi

- **June 30th city-launch deadline is 33 days out.** Tesla's Q4 2024 earnings document — not a Musk tweet — committed to robotaxi coverage in Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix by June 30th, 2026. Bhakdi characterizes the IR document as significantly more credible than off-the-cuff statements and views the deadline as a hard forcing function. Nationwide AI safety operator hiring across 15+ states simultaneously (per "Tesla Yoda" post dated May 27th) is interpreted as an operational signal that deployment is imminent.

- **Fleet growth stalled since ~May 9th in all three Texas cities.** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, roughly 13 Cybercabs are staged in Dallas but appear inactive. Houston depot activity — Model Ys with hazard lights on, carpeting removed from trunks, non-robotaxi vehicles cleared from the lot — is interpreted as "all hands on deck" preparation for imminent Cybercab deployment. Approximately 80% of robotaxi rides over the past 7 days have been unsupervised.

- **Bhakdi estimates 200+ serially produced Cybercabs are already deployed "in the wild,"** citing Joe Tegtmeyer drone footage from May 27th showing consistent production cadence at Giga Texas. Exact count unverified.

- **Cybercabs spotted in Austin running Starlink Mini units bolted to the rear hatch.** Brian White (FutureAzA) explains Starlink would serve as a redundant backup to 5G — critical for dead zones or carrier outages — and could invisibly offload fleet-scale overnight software updates at scale.

- **Reuters found Tesla conducted weeks of intensive nightly mapping** at both the Warner Bros. studio lot (before the October 2024 Cybercab reveal) and Austin's robotaxi zone before its June 2025 launch — annotating curbs, traffic lights, signs, and road markings. Utah labeling staff doubled to ~300 in the six months pre-launch. This directly contradicts Musk's repeated claim that Tesla's approach requires no "laborious local mapping." One former employee described the Austin zone: "You can't get creative outside of that."

- **Pony AI raised its 2026 fleet target to 3,500 vehicles** after Q1 2026 revenue of $34M beat the $21M consensus estimate. However, the company posted a $58M net loss against that revenue — losing nearly twice what it earns. Hosts note fares would need to be ~300% higher to break even, and Waymo is losing ~$5B/year at roughly $100 per ride. Robotaxi unit economics remain structurally challenged across the industry.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Reuters investigation: Tesla's "10x safer" FSD claim inflates safety by ~3x.** University of Michigan researcher Marco Benedetti corrected Tesla's methodology — which compared airbag-deployment crashes in Tesla's fleet against federal data including all tow-truck crashes — and found the true ratio drops to roughly 3x farther between crashes, not 10x. That figure itself is further distorted by the age gap between Tesla's fleet (4.1 years average) and the U.S. fleet (12.8 years). Ten of 11 traffic-safety researchers who reviewed the methodology told Reuters it amounted to misleading marketing.

- **Tesla's own AI trainers overwhelmingly distrust FSD.** Seven of nine former data labelers told Reuters they wouldn't trust FSD to drive them. Labelers described routinely seeing FSD fail to yield to emergency vehicles, give motorcyclists adequate space, brake on freeway off-ramps, and avoid construction zones. A specialized "trauma team" in Palo Alto reviewed near-misses with pedestrians including children in crosswalks. FSD was also reported regularly exceeding speed limits by 20–30 mph in "Mad Max" mode, with one instance of 60 mph in a 25-mph zone.

- **NHTSA has 4 active FSD/Autopilot investigations open,** including a probe into dozens of cases where FSD ran red lights or turned into oncoming traffic, and a review of whether the 2023 Autopilot recall was sufficient. Tesla was also hit with a $243M verdict after an Autopilot crash in Florida killed a 22-year-old woman.

- **FSD 14.3's most significant feature is a full compiler and runtime rewrite from the ground up** (per Dillon Loomis), positioning it as a foundational build rather than a polished release. Performance is mixed: impressive supervised behavior captured in the Netherlands, but a parking failure observed that would have driven into a shopping cart stall without intervention, plus some phantom braking.

- **Waymo paused Atlanta service after vehicles repeatedly drove into flooded roads,** triggering a software recall. Brian White contrasts this with Tesla's neural-net approach to contextual road assessment. Separately, Xpeng CEO He Xiaopeng declared the company is "absolutely adamant" it no longer needs lidar — lidar on existing Xpeng vehicles is now purely ornamental — which hosts frame as validation of Tesla's vision-only thesis.

## Humanoid Bots

- **First steel structure erected at the Optimus factory site at Giga Texas,** per Joe Tegtmeyer. The site's stated capacity is 10 million units per year; second-phase land reclamation is also underway. No production timeline disclosed.

- **Munro Live demoed the Inspire Robotics RH56DFQ dexterous hand** (~$10,000, 6 degrees of freedom, micro linear actuators) as the first installment in a planned humanoid robot component teardown series. The hand uses pressure sensors at the actuator base rather than fingertips — a common misconception flagged by the presenter. A full humanoid robot teardown is planned in a future episode. No direct Optimus content, but frames the complexity and cost spectrum of dexterous hand hardware.

- **Hosts put ~80% probability on China eventually permitting Tesla to sell Optimus and build a factory there,** citing the FSD data-sequestration precedent (local training data, no data in or out) as the likely regulatory blueprint.

## Energy

- **Tesla Oasis in Lost Hills, CA — 164 stalls, world's largest charging station — peaked at 112 simultaneous vehicles on Memorial Day weekend.** The station runs entirely on solar and Megapacks with only an emergency grid backup, a design that avoided prohibitively expensive transmission line upgrades. Brian White discusses the economics of replicating this model: ground-mount solar is cheapest; canopy solar is most expensive but justified in high-electricity-cost states.

- **Megapack 3 Belgium deployment: 180 units, 700 MWh capacity, total project value >$116M.** Construction began on the site of a former power plant; grid connection scheduled for November 2026 with commissioning by end of 2027. This is one of the first large-scale Megapack 3 deployments in Europe.

- **Tesla Supercharger opened its first stations in Colombia,** and Volvo announced that starting Q4 2026, Volvo EV drivers will access 20,000+ Supercharger stations across 29 European countries via the Volvo app. Tesla China also expanded non-Tesla Supercharger access via WeChat prepaid cards — eliminating the need to download the Tesla app — with a projected ~$45M in annual incremental revenue across 1,000 open stations at 10 stalls each if two additional non-Tesla sessions per stall per day are captured.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab efficiency confirmed at 165 Wh/mile** by Lars Morvy (Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering) on the Ride the Lightning podcast — the most efficient vehicle Tesla has ever produced. Engineers told Brian White "5.5 miles per kWh is what we're allowed to say," implying real-world performance is better. White argues this translates directly into fleet utilization advantage: less time charging versus competitors like Waymo.

- **Tesla Europe registrations: +46.5% YoY in April 2026, totaling 10,654 units** across EU, UK, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland; EU-only growth exceeded 67%. This is the third consecutive month of YoY growth (following +84% in March, +12% in February). Year-to-date EU/EFTA/UK sales reached 89,429 units, up 45.8%. France and Spain are reportedly at record highs.

- **Next-gen Roadster confirmed for Giga Texas; alpha prototypes are in testing.** Lars Morvy described it as the first Tesla motor designed with a "pure performance mentality." A new test track under construction at Giga Texas is speculated to be Roadster-related; Brian White floated the October 10th X Takeover event as a possible reveal occasion.

- **China issued 154,000 new NEV license plate quotas exclusively for BEVs** (119,000 family, 35,000 individual), with the family eligibility score hitting a historic low. One analyst cited in the video estimates Tesla could capture ~23,000 incremental sales at ~15% of the allocation, given Model Y and Model 3 buyers match the target demographic.

- **Tesla China rebranded supervised FSD as "Tesla Assisted Driving"** in the owner's manual, with three visible tiers. Hosts interpret this as regulatory positioning ahead of a broader FSD rollout in China rather than a capability change.

## Market & Competition

- **GM/Samsung fully paused construction of their $3.5B "Synergy Cells" battery plant,** with county officials citing the end of EV incentives and economic factors. A "substantial completion" target of end-2027 means only the building shell — no equipment installed. Brian White frames this as a complete retreat from serious EV ambitions and a forfeiture of international markets.

- **GM is directing billions toward ICE:** $300M for transmission capacity near Detroit (plus $300M the prior year), $40M for Ohio transmissions, and $500M+ for Ontario V8 propulsion plants building sixth-generation engines for the Corvette and full-size pickups — totaling $6B+ in investments since 2025 largely centered on the redesigned Silverado. Hosts use the Blockbuster/Nokia analogy: profitable today, strategically blind.

- **BYD sold 101,000+ vehicles in EU/EFTA/UK in the first four months of 2026,** up ~144% YoY, exceeding Tesla's volume. Chery Auto grew 338% YoY in the same markets. Volkswagen and Stellantis are each reportedly cutting ~1 million units of production capacity. EU full BEV share reached 19.7% YTD vs. 15.3% a year ago; hybrids still lead at 38.2%.

- **Elon Musk's real-world voting power is ~11% today** (413M fully owned shares ÷ 3.75B outstanding), not the ~20.3% beneficial ownership figure in SEC filings (which includes 303M unexercised 2018 options carrying no current voting rights). Post-exercise (window: May 21–August 15, 2026), voting power rises to ~17.6%. If all 12 tranches of the 2025 award vest (requiring ~$8.5T market cap), maximum voting power reaches ~28.1%.
