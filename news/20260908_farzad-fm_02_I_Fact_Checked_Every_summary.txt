This video is a broad public-policy explainer on the AI data center debate — power costs, water use, noise, jobs, and tax revenue — arguing that both pro- and anti-data-center narratives are distorted, and proposing a "cost protection + abundance dividend" framework as a fix. It contains no substantive discussion of Tesla, SpaceX, or their products; Tesla and SpaceX are not mentioned.

## Market & Competition
1. **Data center power draw and price effects:** Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory estimates data centers could consume about 12% of all US power by 2030; an MIT study found data center growth from 2010–2024 raised average US retail power prices by about 2.7% (2.1% for homes), while another study found data center demand can lower rates in regions with spare grid capacity by spreading fixed costs across more usage. A Virginia state review found current rates generally make data centers pay their cost of service, but warned fast growth could add $14–$37/month to a typical home bill by 2040 if cost-assignment rules aren't changed.
2. **Water use claims debunked:** US data centers used about 17.4 billion gallons of water directly in 2023 (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory), with water intensity varying by more than 10,000x depending on cooling design and climate; a widely-cited water-use comparison for a proposed Google site in Uruguay (cited from Karen Hao's book "Empire of AI") was off by a factor of 1,000 and was later corrected by Google but continues to circulate. Google's Dallas, Oregon data center used roughly 355 million gallons in 2021 (~29% of the city's water use), while US golf courses used about 30x more water than data centers in 2024 and California almond orchards used roughly 90–100x more.
3. **Jobs and tax revenue tradeoffs:** A typical large Virginia data center project employs roughly 1,500 construction workers at peak for 12–18 months, but the finished facility supports only about 50 full-time jobs (roughly half contractors); a 2026 NBER study found data center openings correlate with more construction/data-processing jobs, higher wages and home values nearby, but also higher power prices over time. Tax outcomes vary widely — Loudoun County, Virginia and St. Louis cite major public revenue benefits, while a Georgia state evaluation found its data center tax break produced a small negative return, and Virginia's exemption was estimated to have saved the industry about $928 million in one fiscal year.
4. **China's data center/power buildout:** The host states China added more solar power in a year than the US has in total and turned on more than 550 gigawatts of power in 2025 (characterized as roughly half of the US national grid), framing this as evidence that a US data center moratorium (e.g., proposed by Senator Bernie Sanders) would simply cede AI infrastructure leadership to China rather than reduce AI's disruptive effects.
