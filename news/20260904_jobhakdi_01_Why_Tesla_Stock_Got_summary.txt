The host argues Tesla's stock drop was not a "sell the news" reaction to the Cybercab launch event but a response to news that NHTSA has opened an audit into Tesla's self-certification of Cybercab, which the host believes Tesla is very unlikely to survive because Cybercab lacks human-usable brake pedals required under FMVSS 135. He frames this as a serious but ultimately resolvable overhang, expecting the stock to recover once institutional investors understand Tesla has fallback options.

## Robotaxi
1. **NHTSA audit of Cybercab self-certification:** The host reports NHTSA has initiated an audit into Tesla's self-certification of Cybercab, triggered he believes within about 24 hours of the commercial Cybercab launch. He argues the audit is very likely to find Tesla's self-certification invalid because FMVSS 135 requires human-usable brake pedals, which Cybercab does not have, and that Tesla's legal argument (that the "purpose" of the rule is satisfied without literal pedals) will not hold up under audit scrutiny.
2. **Precedent from Waymo (2022 timeline):** The host cites a Waymo NHTSA process starting around June 30, 2022 (self-certification announcement), with a special order audit initiated roughly 68 days later, a response about 60 days after that, an on-site inspection about 19 months later, and Waymo ultimately failing the audit and instead securing an exemption capped at 2,500 vehicles — a process he says took about 2.4 years total. He is concerned Tesla's audit is moving far faster (initiated within roughly a day versus 68 days for Waymo), which he speculates may reflect lobbying pressure from Uber, Lyft, and Waymo.
3. **The 2,500-vehicle exemption cap:** The host explains that if Tesla fails the audit, it could seek an NHTSA exemption, but that exemption is statutorily capped at 2,500 vehicles and cannot be raised without new Congressional legislation — which he sees as a very difficult path given the "totally not predictable" nature of relying on Congress, especially amid upcoming midterms.
4. **Pending NHTSA rule change as the key mitigating path:** NHTSA published a proposed rule change (referenced as related to FMVSS 135) on June 25/26 to modernize brake-pedal requirements for autonomous vehicles; the public comment period closed August 26. The host suspects Tesla deliberately scheduled the Cybercab launch event on September 3 — after the comment window closed — to avoid tipping off competitors who could have flooded the rulemaking with opposing comments. He estimates a low-to-moderate chance the rule is finalized by Q1–Q2, with November as the earliest realistic effective date, and six-to-nine months as more likely.
5. **Two competing timelines and Tesla's fallback plan:** The host frames the situation as a race between the audit concluding (which would force a shutdown or cap at 2,500 units) and the new rule becoming effective (which would largely remove the brake-pedal problem). He believes Tesla can mitigate downside by deploying Cybercab variants equipped with physical brake pedals and/or steering wheels if needed, arguing this would not meaningfully delay the robotaxi scaling trajectory even if the audit fails, though it could raise costs and introduce some safety trade-offs (e.g., passengers accidentally engaging pedals).
6. **Overall stock impact assessment:** The host's bottom line is that the audit news is genuinely negative and a real overhang causing uncertainty for institutional investors, but he does not believe it will change Tesla's underlying scaling trajectory, since Tesla can adapt via pedal/steering-wheel-equipped variants; he expects the stock to recover over the following one to two weeks as this understanding spreads, though he flagged his own bias may be skewed too positively.
