This video is an on-location interview from the "futureaza" channel with the CEO/founder of Yatri Motors, a Nepal-based electric motorcycle manufacturer, covering the company's origin, engineering approach, battery chemistry choice, supply chain, regulatory battles, and upcoming P2 scooter launch. It has no Tesla or SpaceX content and only tangential relevance to EV industry trends generally; it is included in TTT's scope only nominally and does not move either investment thesis.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nepalese EV startup building in-house, not importing kits:** Yatri Motors, founded in 2017, designs and manufactures electric motorcycles/scooters in Nepal in-house — including chassis, wiring harnesses, PCB design/firmware, and battery pack assembly — rather than importing kits from China or India, according to the company's CEO.
2. **New P2 scooter uses NCM (2170 LG) cells over LFP:** The CEO said the company chose NCM chemistry (2170-format LG cells) instead of LFP for higher energy density and discharge capacity, citing Nepal's mountainous terrain, limited packaging space, and range requirements; the scooter has an onboard AC charger (under ~2.5 hours full charge) and access to the company's own DC fast-charger network (claimed ~80% in 40 minutes).
3. **Manufacturing facility and launch timeline:** The CEO said a manufacturing facility is going live in September, with pilot production at month's end and a public rollout of the P2 scooter starting in October; pricing is set to be announced September 17. Year-one factory throughput capacity is stated at about 10,000 units, scalable to 30,000–40,000 units/year with minor upgrades.
4. **Early bookings and prior regulatory battle:** The CEO said three-digit numbers of bookings were received within 3 days of an auto show without price disclosure. He also described a roughly 1.5-year legal dispute with the Nepali government over vehicle registration policy for indigenous manufacturers, which he characterized as a "grand success" once resolved via a new government directive, contrasted with the company's earlier P1 motorcycle platform, which struggled commercially due to this policy gap. He noted Yatri pays 16% more importing battery pack raw materials than finished-goods importers pay, and that battery packs represent roughly 35–45% of vehicle cost.
