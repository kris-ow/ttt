This short clip from Jo Bhakdi argues that Starlink, while profitable, is a comparatively weak use of SpaceX's limited launch capacity next to a hypothetical orbital compute business he calls "Starmine" (satellites doing AI compute in space). He frames this as an opportunity-cost problem: every Starship launch slot used for Starlink is launch capacity not used for the more lucrative compute satellites.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink profitability questioned relative to alternative uses of launch capacity:** Bhakdi states Starlink is "just not that profitable" compared to orbital compute satellites, arguing that with roughly 10,000 Starlink satellites already deployed, SpaceX is not generating anywhere near the profit level he calculates compute satellites could produce — making the point that launch capacity itself, not Starlink's business, is the binding constraint.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Starmine compute economics outlined:** Bhakdi estimates that 4,000 of his proposed "Starmine" compute satellites would deliver 1 gigawatt of compute capacity, which he values at roughly $50 billion in revenue and roughly $35 billion in profit — a figure he uses to argue compute satellites are a far higher-margin use of launch mass than Starlink satellites. He acknowledges SpaceX is not currently generating anywhere near this level of profit, since this is a forward-looking/hypothetical comparison rather than a reported figure.
2. **Launch capacity compared to Tesla battery allocation:** Bhakdi draws an analogy to Tesla allocating scarce battery cells to the Cybercab over the Semi truck because Cybercab is far more profitable per battery — he states the same cell going into Cybercab generates roughly six times the profit of one going into Semi, and argues SpaceX will similarly prioritize loading Starship with compute satellites over Starlink satellites because he estimates doing so yields roughly 10–15 times more profit per launch.
