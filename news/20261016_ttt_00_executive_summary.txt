## Autonomous Driving
- A 22-year-old driver told investigators he was asleep with FSD engaged when his Tesla struck a parked CHP patrol car on I-80 near Fairfield, CA just before midnight on Oct. 13, seriously injuring two officers; CHP has not confirmed the model, software version, or verified FSD was active. Electrek's Fred Lambert notes this follows a 2021 NHTSA probe and 2023 recall of 2M+ vehicles over emergency-vehicle crashes, and argues Tesla shares responsibility for the "Full Self-Driving" name and Musk's past comments that drivers can text "depending on context."
- Rivian launched an invite-only "Autonomy Early Access Program" for Gen 2 R1/R2 owners, with a Point-to-Point feature starting in December for California — incremental competitive pressure on FSD.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Cybercab robotaxis recorded zero crashes or accidents in their first two weeks of public rides, per NHTSA data cited by Sawyer Merritt.
- Tesla added 30 more Cybercab registrations with the Texas DMV, bringing the cumulative total to 349.
- Investor commentary (davelee channel) frames a Cybercab ramp across Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Miami, Tampa and Las Vegas showing up in revenue and margins as the key re-rating catalyst, though one speaker cautioned robotaxi rollouts have consistently run slower than market expectations.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's next-gen Roadster was re-unveiled at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas facility after a ~9-year delay; Elon Musk confirmed the rocket-thruster trim ("Rocket") will be a real, ownable car, though he said he's unsure it will be legal in all jurisdictions. Tesla now quotes "over 300 miles" of range (down from the original 2017 claim of 600 miles) and a 230+mph top speed; three trims (Performance, Thrust, Rocket) were announced, with only Rocket carrying cold-gas thrusters and listed as invite-only, "not approved for public road use."
- Electrek's technical review questioned several of the event's headline claims: a stated 4,700kg downforce figure looks implausible for the car's likely weight; 0-60 times (1.0s recorded, 0.81s live, sub-0.59s tweeted) rely on rear thrusters producing 80kN of thrust rather than tire traction; and thrusters appeared to fire before the car moved in slow-motion footage, raising timing questions.
- At the live demo, attendees were kept roughly a quarter-mile away and the driverless demo car flew only halfway up the ramp — both read by independent attendees as Tesla signaling the tech isn't yet safe for full deployment.
- Q3 production (fourth-best quarter ever) and deliveries (third-best ever) both closed in on prior-year records set under expiring EV incentives; cumulative production and shipments both passed 10 million units during the quarter, unannounced by Tesla, per cernbasher's data review.
- Tesla needs 311,000 Q4 deliveries to beat last year's full-year total and 483,000 to set an all-time annual record; the review flagged battery-pack production constraints mentioned on the last earnings call as the key swing factor for Q4.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Elon Musk said SpaceX's market cap could exceed $100 trillion within a decade barring a "force majeure" event — a forward-looking, hedged projection, not confirmed guidance.
- Investor commentary (davelee channel) speculated the Roadster event, held at a SpaceX facility and using SpaceX-derived thruster tech, functioned as an implicit "merger roadshow," with both TSLA and SPCX up roughly 3% on the day by the speakers' own account; no shareholder meeting date has been set.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- United Airlines said over 5.4 million passengers — about a third of its customers — flew on Starlink-equipped flights last month, with satisfaction scores over 40 points higher than other Wi-Fi providers; Starlink is now installed on 43% of United's fleet, and the airline launched a real-time installation tracker.

## SpaceX — Starship
- A technical explainer detailed why SpaceX abandoned carbon fiber for stainless steel around 2018–2019: steel strengthens at cryogenic temperatures and melts near 1400°C versus carbon fiber resin degrading at just a few hundred degrees, and costs roughly $3/kg versus $135/kg for carbon fiber (Musk's estimate).
- A separate explainer on lunar missions noted launch-count estimates for one Artemis landing vary widely: Musk has cited 4–8 tanker flights, SpaceX roughly 10, and NASA's full-campaign estimate is close to 20. Ship-to-ship propellant transfer, following March 2024's intra-ship demo, is planned for 2026.
