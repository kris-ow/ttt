This futureaza video, hosted by Brian with guest Randy, covers September global EV sales rankings dominated by the Tesla Model Y and Model 3, regional Tesla sales trends (strong outside China, weaker inside it), and the expansion of Tesla's robotaxi geofence maps, including a new San Antonio zone and upcoming Las Vegas/Nevada deployment, alongside a tangent on Boring Company tunnel projects.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y and Model 3 lead global September EV sales:** Global plug-in vehicle registrations rose 3% year-on-year to 2 million units for September, close to the record set the prior December. The Tesla Model Y ranked first, about 100,000 units ahead of second place, the Tesla Model 3. The rest of the top 10 included Geely, BYD, an unidentified brand, BYD Dolphin, MG, and Leap Motor — notably with no legacy automakers (Toyota, Volkswagen, Kia, Ford, Hyundai) represented; of the top 20, 17 models were Chinese and one Japanese, with the top two partly manufactured in China.
2. **Model 3 not being phased out despite lower margins, per hosts:** Despite online speculation that the Model 3 might be discontinued, the hosts argue this is unlikely since it runs at full capacity across two factories; they note many Model 3 buyers are in markets without Full Self-Driving approval, where robotaxi monetization is still far off, but could eventually generate residual income for owners.
3. **Hosts expect continued Model Y/3 growth via production increases, not new models:** Randy attributes further sales growth to production ramps (already increased in Berlin, planned in Austin and China) and manufacturing simplification (fewer parts, lower costs) rather than new variants; the hosts speculate combined Model 3/Y volume could approach 2 million units excluding Cybertruck in the coming year.
4. **Regional sales data from analyst Roland Pircher (Polyly):** Chinese year-to-date sales lag prior years amid broader Chinese automotive market weakness, though Tesla's China market share has held up and October saw a record number of exports. Spain reportedly saw its strongest quarterly block yet with roughly 1000% growth versus the prior quarter; Korea was cited as on pace to be the fastest imported brand to reach 100,000 units this year (unconfirmed, attributed to a third-party data source); Japan showed Tesla market share around 1.5%, called strong for a new entrant as Toyota/Subaru's new BEV model draws more competition.
5. **Randy attributes 2026 sales resilience to multiple factors:** Randy says investor concern over Tesla's global sales has faded relative to the prior two years, crediting factors such as high fuel prices, improving FSD, Cybertruck launch hype, and social media visibility (Instagram/TikTok) for driving growth outside China; he frames China's softness as a broader market/economic issue rather than Tesla-specific.

## Robotaxi
1. **San Antonio robotaxi geofence launched:** Tesla launched a new robotaxi geofence zone in San Antonio on the Monday before this video, described as unusually large and appearing to encompass the airport (though airport access may be governed separately by port authority rules).
2. **Coverage maps show Tesla robotaxi zones now rival Waymo's footprint:** A side-by-side map comparison shows Waymo's deployment zones (blue) against Tesla's robotaxi/Cybercab zones (red, not yet including the new San Antonio zone), which the hosts describe as having grown very large.
3. **Randy flags unknown demand side of robotaxi expansion:** Randy reiterates a long-standing point that Tesla has not disclosed rider demand data (only observable wait times), so it's unclear whether demand will keep pace as more vehicles are added in cities like Austin or launched fresh in San Antonio; he suggests Tesla likely tracks this internally and that fleet deployment pace depends on an unpublished demand curve. He proposes Tesla could run local PR/outreach or first-ride-free promotions in new cities to lower the barrier to entry, similar to Musk's past demand-generation tactics (e.g., PayPal signup bonuses).
4. **Driverless Tesla robotaxis seen as lower-cost to leave idle than Waymo's:** Randy argues Tesla's unsupervised robotaxis cost little to leave idle (no driver wages, can park and wait) compared to Waymo, where downtime is described as two to three times more costly; he also notes idle Teslas avoid running climate control for occupant comfort in extreme weather.
5. **Las Vegas/Nevada robotaxi deployment pending a December deadline:** Nevada has approved up to 5,000 robotaxi units, with testing underway in Las Vegas and Reno; per the hosts, Tesla's permit will expire if vehicles aren't launched by December 18, and vehicles are reportedly already being staged at bases ahead of road deployment.
