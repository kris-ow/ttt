This episode covers Tesla's efficiency improvements to the Cybercab robotaxi and plans for a smaller battery, the announced wind-down of Cybertruck pickup production at Giga Texas, Terafab's newly reported chip-production targets, and SpaceX's preparations to attempt catching both Starship stages on its next test flight.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab efficiency and future battery shrink:** The Cybercab (the two-seat, steering-wheel-free robotaxi being assembled at Giga Texas and running paid trips in Austin since September) uses a 4680-cell battery in a steel case with individually sealed, replaceable modules, rated for at least 500,000 miles. At 3,113 lbs and using an 11:1 gear ratio, it achieves 6.18 miles per kWh, which the video calls the most efficient production vehicle made. Musk posted on X that future efficiency gains could let Tesla cut the battery from the current 47.6 kWh to about 40 kWh (roughly 16% less), which the host estimates could let Tesla build about 19% more vehicles from the same battery supply volume.
2. **New color and service expansion:** Musk said an upcoming Cybercab update will add a "liquid silver" color option alongside the current gold units in Austin; the host notes a silver Cybercab appeared in a SpaceX Terafab concept render released in August, though this is circumstantial. Tesla expanded Austin robotaxi service to about 440 square miles (its largest coverage yet) and said Dallas robotaxi rides would begin this month, with Cybercab deliveries to customers planned for late 2026.
3. **Production capacity vs. demand:** Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy said during the Q3 report that actual Cybercab line capacity significantly exceeds stated capacity, and Tesla will produce only as much as the robotaxi service needs — framed by the host as the opposite trajectory from the declining Cybertruck pickup.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck pickup production wind-down:** Musk announced on November 6 that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck pickup production at Giga Texas around mid-next year to free factory space for other models, later clarifying the line would be dismantled around June or July. Musk said the backlog of orders exceeds what can be produced before closure, so Tesla will add an extra shift but will still have to refund deposits to many customers; he said the decision was about prioritizing other models, not lack of demand or cost.
2. **Pricing and final order window:** Tesla raised Cybertruck prices several times since August, with the latest increase shortly before the wind-down announcement. The company has stopped taking orders for four-wheel-drive versions, leaving one model available at roughly $110,000 (about $10,000 more than at launch), with new orders quoted for March–June 2027 delivery — just before the line closes.
3. **Sales history and standing vs. competitors:** Cybertruck deliveries began in November 2023 at up to $120,000 for Foundation Series trucks. Per Cox Automotive estimates cited in the video, Tesla sold about 39,000 Cybertrucks in the US in 2024, roughly 20,000 last year, and fewer than 12,000 this year — well short of the Giga Texas line's 125,000/year design capacity, now reportedly running at about a quarter of that. Despite the decline, Cybertruck remains the top-selling electric pickup, with about 11,600 units sold through September versus roughly 5,300 for the Chevy Silverado EV and roughly 4,600–4,700 each for the GMC Sierra EV and Rivian R1T.
4. **Registered units in Texas:** As of the end of October, 573 Cybertrucks had been registered in Texas, per the video.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Terafab's reported wafer production target:** According to the video, Terafab has told Japanese equipment manufacturers it plans to produce 1 million silicon wafers per month starting in 2032 — about 700,000 for memory chips and over 300,000 for logic chips; the host says the memory figure would be roughly comparable to Samsung's entire DRAM production. Some equipment suppliers reportedly described Terafab's delivery timelines as extremely demanding but agreed to proceed.
2. **Site and infrastructure plans:** The Terafab plant is being built in Grimes County, Texas, near College Station, planned at about 100 million square feet (~3.6 square miles). SpaceX says it will build its own natural gas power plants and battery arrays so the factory isn't dependent on the local grid. Construction is underway despite over 100 residents voicing water, energy, and rural-impact concerns at a June hearing; county commissioners approved tax breaks for the project.
3. **Austin pilot lab and cost estimates:** Musk said Tesla will begin installing equipment at a smaller Austin research lab in the first half of next year, targeting a first test chip around May or June and mass production in Q3 2028. Intel is involved, with Musk saying Tesla plans to use Intel's 14A process technology. Musk said Terafab equipment costs will reach tens of billions of dollars by early 2028 and likely over $100 billion long-term; he said he expects Terafab to become the most valuable building in the world, with economic value potentially rivaling the GDP of a top-20 country (Musk's framing, per the video).

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15 catch attempt scheduled:** SpaceX plans to launch Starship Flight 15 on Friday, November 13, pending regulatory approval, with a 75-minute window opening at 4:45 p.m. Central Time and backup windows through November 19, per an announcement by SpaceX VP of Launch Kiko Donchev and confirmed on SpaceX's website. The flight uses Booster 22 and Ship 42, both of which completed pre-flight engine tests in late October. The goal is to catch both the booster and the Starship upper stage with the tower's mechanical arms — SpaceX has caught the Super Heavy booster multiple times since October 2024 but has never caught the upper stage.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Flight 14 recap:** SpaceX released a 21-minute film, "Go for Orbit," documenting Flight 14 (September 28), the first Starship flight to reach orbit, during which 26 next-generation Starlink satellites were deployed before the ship splashed down in the Pacific.
