This video analyzes the amended SpaceX S-1A IPO filing and argues that six deliberate strategic moves embedded in the document reveal Elon Musk as an exceptionally sophisticated long-term operator. The presenter walks through each change in the filing, arguing that together they form a coherent, multi-layered strategy to protect mission continuity, reward loyalists, attract forced institutional buying, and keep maximum optionality — including a potential acquisition of Tesla. While the video is primarily about SpaceX, it carries direct implications for Tesla investors due to the acquisition language in the filing.

## Market & Competition

1. **Potential SpaceX Acquisition of Tesla via Equity Issuance:** The amended S-1A filing includes new language in the acquisitions and strategic transactions section stating SpaceX "may issue a significant amount of equity in connection with future transactions." The presenter interprets this as a deliberate signal — not boilerplate — that a large stock-funded acquisition could be forthcoming. The two most-cited targets are Tesla and Cursor (a developer coding tool). The presenter notes that Elon already folded xAI into SpaceX earlier in 2026, and that this filing language represents "the strongest hint yet" that Tesla could follow. However, the presenter explicitly cautions that the filing does not say Tesla is being acquired — only that the door is now open in writing.

2. **Anthropic Paying SpaceX ~$1.25 Billion Per Month for Compute:** The filing discloses that Anthropic — a direct competitor to xAI's Grok — is paying SpaceX approximately $1.25 billion per month for access to roughly 325,000 Nvidia GPUs, hyperscale-class CPUs, exabyte-scale storage, and high-speed networking. The total deal value is approximately $45 billion running through 2029. The presenter notes an earlier draft of the filing disclosed a lower GPU count of around 220,000, so the amendment revealed a larger footprint. Critically, either party can exit with 90 days' notice, which the presenter frames as intentional: Elon is monetizing idle compute while reserving the right to reclaim it for Grok the moment xAI needs it. The presenter characterizes this as "making the competition pay for his buildout."

3. **Index Rule Changes Creating Forced Institutional Buying:** As of May 1, 2026, Nasdaq updated its rules to allow newly listed companies ranking in the top 40 by market cap to join the Nasdaq 100 after just 15 trading days, eliminating the old minimum float requirement. For low-float stocks like SpaceX, a waiting multiplier of up to 3x effectively inflates the company's index weight beyond what its small tradable share base would normally earn. Separately, the S&P 500 opened a consultation in May 2026 proposing to cut the seasoning window from 12 months to 6 months for mega-cap IPOs and to waive the four-quarter profitability test for sufficiently large companies. FTSE Russell went further, proposing index eligibility after just five trading days for companies above the top 500 size threshold. The presenter frames this as a wall of automatic buying from index-tracking ETFs (QQQ, SPY, etc.) arriving on a predictable schedule — timed to absorb selling pressure from early holders and the no-lockup 5% directed share program.

4. **SpaceX Valuation and IPO Structure:** SpaceX is described as being valued at approximately $1.5 to $1.75 trillion, which the presenter characterizes as likely the largest IPO in history. Up to 5% of IPO shares have been reserved for selected employees and friends and family of executive officers, with no lockup period — meaning these holders can sell on day one. The presenter frames this as a deliberate loyalty mechanism, with participants selected at the discretion of executive officers (i.e., Elon and his leadership). Meanwhile, Elon himself agreed to a 366-day lockup on all of his shares with no early release provision — framed as a confidence signal to the market.

5. **Voting Control and Acquisition Power:** Elon owns approximately 42% of SpaceX's equity but controls approximately 85% of votes via a dual-class share structure — Class A shares (1 vote each, sold to the public) and Class B shares (10 votes each, held by Elon), with Elon holding roughly 93.6% of Class B stock. The filing also confirms that Elon can vote approximately 1 billion performance-based restricted shares granted in January 2026 — tied to market cap milestones across 15 tranches and to building a permanent human colony on Mars with at least 1 million people — before those shares have vested. The presenter argues that combined with the equity issuance language, this means Elon could unilaterally execute a Tesla acquisition using SpaceX stock without needing any other shareholder's approval.

6. **Float Restrictions and Worker Wealth Creation:** Over 60% of the company — approximately 7.8 billion shares — is restricted from immediate sale, with some holders locked up through the quarter ending June 2027. The presenter notes this limits selling pressure and stacks with forced index buying to support the post-IPO price. On the human side, the presenter highlights that SpaceX has historically paid lower cash salaries in exchange for richer equity — distributed not just to executives but to technicians, assembly crews, and floor workers at Starbase and Hawthorne. The IPO converts years of illiquid paper equity into real, sellable money. The presenter notes that executives like Gwen Shotwell and the CFO are crossing $1 billion each, but emphasizes that blue-collar workers who held equity for 5, 10, or 15 years are becoming millionaires. American Airlines was also added as a Starlink customer in this amended filing.

## Financials

1. **Grok/xAI Subscription Revenue Now Appearing in SpaceX Filings:** As of the end of 2025, SpaceX reported 4.1 million paid X Premium subscribers and approximately 900,000 paid Super Grok subscribers. The presenter notes this is the first time the AI subscription business has appeared in SpaceX's numbers, representing the beginning of real recurring revenue from the xAI side of the combined entity — alongside the rocket and satellite businesses.

2. **Underwriter Commission Waiver on Overallotment:** The investment banks running the IPO agreed to take no discount or commission on shares sold under the overallotment option, meaning that money stays with the company and its holders rather than going to Wall Street. The presenter flags this as an investor-friendly structural detail embedded in the filing.
