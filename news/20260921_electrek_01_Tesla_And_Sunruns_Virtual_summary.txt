Sunrun and Tesla dispatched a record 580 MW from a pooled fleet of over 140,000 home batteries to California's grid during a September 9 heat wave, the largest residential distributed power plant event recorded to date.

## Energy
1. **Record dispatch breakdown:** Of the 140,000-plus batteries in the fleet, about 110,000 were Tesla Powerwalls (Sunrun says it owns or operates 55% of them), which delivered 517 MW of the total; the remaining 63 MW came from over 30,000 batteries from other manufacturers in Sunrun's fleet. The three-hour evening dispatch on September 9 was enough to power every household in Sacramento County during peak hours, according to the article.

2. **Grid programs used:** The batteries fed power through two California programs — the California Energy Commission's Demand Side Grid Support (DSGS) program, which triggers automatically once the day-ahead price crosses $200/MWh, and the CPUC's Emergency Load Reduction Program (ELRP), which utilities called directly during the heat wave. The next evening, September 10, Sunrun dispatched over 140 MW more at Southern California Edison's request.

3. **Scale versus peaker plants:** Sunrun CEO Mary Powell said the distributed home battery fleet is operating at a scale larger than many peaker power plants combined; the article notes typical natural gas peaker plants run in the 50–250 MW range, meaning this dispatch matched or exceeded several combined.

4. **Broader VPP buildout:** This event builds on Tesla's 2020 deal making Sunrun its biggest Powerwall installer and Tesla's earlier virtual power plant experiments. In June, Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced a 16.8 GW virtual power plant aimed at data centers, using home batteries and smart thermostats to help meet AI-driven power demand; this residential dispatch is described as a live version of that same model.

5. **Projected savings:** A Brattle Group report cited by Sunrun projects up to $206 million in net savings for Californians by 2028 from this kind of distributed battery dispatch, with participating homeowners paid for power sent back to the grid.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this dispatch demonstrates a strong case for home solar and storage, calling a grid that relies on distributed home batteries cheaper and faster to deploy than new peaker plants or utility-scale storage. He raises an open question of how far this model can scale before affecting the business case for utility-scale storage and gas peakers, noting California currently has the batteries, programs, and price signals aligned in a way most other states do not.
