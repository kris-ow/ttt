Tesla has filed a building permit with Clark County, Nevada for a dedicated Tesla Semi service center in Las Vegas, marking one of the first concrete signs of the service network being built to support the recently launched Class 8 electric truck.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Permit details:** A Clark County permit (BD26-39451) describes a "Tesla Semi Service Center. Tenant Improvement in new shell building" at 3780 N Las Vegas Blvd, part of the LV Logistics One industrial development. Tesla is listed as tenant (property owner is a Dallas-based entity), the use is classified as "Repair Garage," and the status is "Awaiting Plans," meaning construction drawings have not yet been submitted — so the facility is not opening imminently. A neighboring building in the same development is a 152,256-square-foot logistics building with 32-foot clear height, 20 dock-high doors, and a 122-foot truck court, giving an indication of the scale of space Tesla may be moving into.

2. **Strategic location rationale:** Las Vegas sits on the I-15 freight corridor connecting Southern California's Inland Empire to Las Vegas and Salt Lake City, with that route (roughly 230–270 miles) falling within the Standard Range Semi's 325-mile rating. The article notes Nevada received only two of the 64 planned Megacharger sites announced in February, with the only currently operating Nevada site located at Giga Nevada in Sparks, about 440 miles from Las Vegas — a gap this service center could help address.

3. **Semi production ramp context:** Tesla began volume production of the Semi at its new Sparks factory last month, with claimed capacity for 50,000 trucks annually. Customers at the launch event included PepsiCo, DHL, US Foods, and ABF; Einride placed an order for 500 trucks in August. Tesla has shown plans for about 20 dedicated Semi service locations across the US, and the article states this Las Vegas filing is the first such location found in a public permit record.
