A 22-year-old Tesla driver told investigators he was asleep with FSD engaged when his car crashed through a lane closure and struck a CHP patrol car on Interstate 80 in Fairfield, California, seriously injuring both officers inside. The driver sustained only minor injuries; CHP has not confirmed the Tesla model, software version, or independently verified that FSD was active — the driver's statement is currently the only evidence.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **The crash and scene details:** Just before midnight on October 13, CHP officers were parked in a Dodge Charger managing a lane closure (four lanes shut with flares and cones) for an earlier fatal crash on eastbound I-80 when the Tesla drove through the closure at freeway speed and struck the patrol car; dashcam footage captured the impact, released by CHP on Thursday. CHP stated that driver-assistance technology does not relieve drivers of responsibility to safely operate their vehicles.
2. **Pattern of Tesla crashes into emergency scenes:** The article notes NHTSA opened an investigation in August 2021 into Teslas on Autopilot crashing into stopped emergency vehicles, which led to Tesla's December 2023 recall of over 2 million vehicles with an over-the-air update adding driver-attention alerts. The author notes this hasn't solved the issue, citing a June 2024 incident in which a Tesla on FSD hit a parked Fullerton police car at another fatal-crash scene, where that driver admitted to being on his phone.
3. **Driver monitoring concerns:** The author states FSD (Supervised) is designed to use the cabin camera to monitor driver attention and disengage if the driver isn't watching the road, and questions why a driver who was allegedly asleep could travel far enough to cause this crash. The article references a prior Electrek report that Tesla's driver monitoring can reportedly be bypassed easily, even with an object like a dollar bill placed in front of the camera. It also notes Elon Musk claimed in December that FSD drivers can text while driving "depending on context of surrounding traffic" — a practice illegal in California.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the driver bears responsibility if he fell asleep at the wheel, but contends Tesla also shares responsibility for marketing a product named "Full Self-Driving" and for Musk's public claims that drivers can text while using it; he cites Tesla's recent agreement to drop the "Full Self-Driving" name in Europe after German regulators called it misleading. He argues emergency-scene crashes are exactly the failure mode NHTSA flagged in 2021, and that Tesla should not claim it's safe to look away until it is willing to accept liability for crashes under unsupervised FSD.
