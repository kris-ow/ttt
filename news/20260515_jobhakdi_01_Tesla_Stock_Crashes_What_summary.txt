In this live session, Jo Bhakdi reacts to a sharp Tesla stock decline on what he describes as a low-volume, post-"sell-the-news" day following the Trump-Xi Beijing summit, arguing the drop is a normal market retracement rather than a thesis-breaking event. His core argument is that Tesla's bull run toward a $490 price target remains intact, supported by Cybercab ramp-up, global EV sales strength, Terafab developments, and a potential SpaceX merger catalyst. The session blends tactical options commentary with broader macro context and audience Q&A.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla underperforms NASDAQ on low volume:** Tesla fell approximately 4.3% on the day versus NASDAQ's decline of roughly 1.23–1.26%, which Bhakdi notes far exceeds what Tesla's beta would imply (approximately 2.5% down). He attributes much of this to a "sell the news" dynamic following the Trump-Xi Beijing summit, where markets had priced in enthusiasm and then retreated. He emphasizes the drop occurred on notably low volume — approximately 40 million shares versus an average of approximately 65 million — which he interprets as a weak bearish signal, suggesting the move lacks conviction.

2. **Weekly and monthly context remains bullish:** Despite the daily decline, Bhakdi notes Tesla is still positive for the week after a significant run-up. Over one month, he notes Tesla is up nearly 8%. He frames the day's drop as a normal breather after an approximately 20% run-up since April 9th, pointing to NASDAQ's roughly 9.44% gain over the prior month as evidence the broader bull run is intact.

3. **Trump-Xi summit framed as a macro positive, not a Tesla catalyst:** Bhakdi dismisses expectations that the Beijing summit would produce specific Tesla announcements (such as FSD approval in China), noting that Xi Jinping's agenda encompassed far larger geopolitical issues including Taiwan, Iran, and nuclear weapons. He views the most meaningful outcome as Trump inviting Xi to the White House in September and the generally cooperative tone, which he sees as positive for supply chain preparation for Optimus and potentially lower tariffs over time.

4. **Oil shock viewed as transient and positive for EVs:** Bhakdi argues that rising oil prices represent a supply shock rather than sustained monetary inflation, and that prices will revert rather than continue compounding year over year. He views elevated oil prices as a net positive for Tesla EV and Semi sales while they persist, and expects the Strait of Hormuz situation to resolve because both the US and Iran face too much geopolitical pressure from their respective allies to sustain the disruption.

5. **AGI crossing a threshold in real time:** Bhakdi expresses a strong view that economic AGI — defined as AI replacing workers at scale — is being crossed "as we speak," with capability parity for most jobs potentially achieved within three months, leaving deployment as the primary remaining constraint.
