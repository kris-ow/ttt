Dillon Loomis walks through Elon Musk's recent technical explanation of FSD V15 in plain terms. He covers the universal vision encoder (UVE), the 3-billion-parameter driving policy, and the 1-billion-parameter diffusion model behind the new "reality view" visualization. His thesis is that Tesla's single end-to-end network gives it a structural advantage, and that much of it transfers to Optimus. Perception, the world simulator and training methods carry over, but the robot's action policy will be far harder than driving.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Pixels in, controls out, explained:** A pixel is three numbers (red, green and blue values), so a 5-megapixel camera produces about 15 million numbers per frame. Citing past comments from Ashok Elluswamy, the host says a 30-second context window comes to roughly 2 billion input tokens once camera video, map and navigation data, kinematics and external-microphone audio are combined. The network compresses all of that into two outputs: steering and acceleration.
2. **The universal vision encoder:** The UVE cuts each frame into patches and multiplies the pixel values by trained weights to produce "latent features" (tokens). These tokens are compared across cameras and over time to infer depth and motion. The UVE makes no driving decisions; it only decides what information the downstream models receive.
   - It was upgraded with training on millions of internet videos, for better generalization and data efficiency.
   - The host credits this with a 25x improvement in critical intervention rate.
3. **V15 architecture per Musk:** The UVE features are forked to two models: the roughly 3-billion-parameter driving policy and a separate 1-billion-parameter diffusion model that generates the reality view.
   - Driving decisions never wait on the visualization.
   - The diffusion model is single-step. It starts from the previous reality-view frame, adds noise and denoises it, which keeps frames continuous.
   - It outputs eight images at 36 frames per second at UI-screen resolution. UVE frames run at 18 Hz, spread over four cycles.
   - It runs on half of one "trip" engine, about 8% of AI4's neural accelerator capacity. This follows from half a trip out of three per chip across two redundant chips.
   - Tesla moved a "tiny decoder" from off-chip DRAM into on-chip SRAM to reduce latency on a bandwidth-constrained chip.
   - Newer UVE features are "interleaved" into later stages of the network to cut latency further.
4. **Parameter count still open:** Many expected V15 to be about 10x V14's reported ~1B parameters. Musk's figures sum to 4B (3B policy plus 1B diffusion) and leave the UVE's size undisclosed. The host doubts the encoder alone accounts for another 6B, so V15's total size remains an open question in his view.
5. **Reality view is not a world model:** A world model changes its scene in response to actions. The reality view only represents the current scene and is better seen as a lightweight descendant of Tesla's world model. Musk says Tesla has had a "pixel perfect" world model since 2025, which is key for synthetic data generation.
6. **Caution on manual driving:** With V15, the visualization (including a 360° bird's-eye view) is shown even in manual driving, but it requires paying for FSD. In one clip the host reviews, a car coming from the right appears in the view only after it is plainly visible on camera. The host warns against relying on the visualization for manual decisions, especially in parking lots.
7. **A decade of groundwork:** The host traces the reality view through several earlier steps:
   - Andrej Karpathy's "vector space" at AI Day 2021.
   - Phil Duan's occupancy network at AI Day 2022, which was then "not shown in our customer-facing visualization".
   - High-fidelity Park Assist in 2023.
   - A 2025 patent on displaying 3D occupancy built from 2D camera data.
   - Elluswamy's 2025 talk on generative Gaussian splatting, which builds a 3D scene in about 220 milliseconds.

   This week, Phil Duan said Tesla had always wanted the UI to show the world through the FSD model's eyes. The host argues that segmentation, occupancy, Gaussians and the visualization are all outputs of one end-to-end network, not bolted-on modules. Competitors are still stitching separate models together.
8. **AI5 hint:** Musk said AI5 is "roughly 12 times more compute," which would make this whole process much easier. The host notes Musk has said AI5 is for Optimus only, with cars staying on AI4 and AI4.5. He nonetheless would not be surprised to see AI5 in vehicles around late 2028 or 2029.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **What transfers to Optimus:** The cameras, vision encoder and world simulator will be shared across FSD and Optimus. The policy and outputs will differ sharply:
   - Optimus must control legs, hips, torso, arms, wrists and fingers.
   - It must maintain active balance at all times.
   - It must manage grip force and contact, whereas cars spend their days avoiding contact.
   - It lacks FSD's customer data-collection flywheel.

   Elluswamy said in 2026 that Optimus will use the same end-to-end, pixels-in, controls-out approach. In 2025 he said that once the FSD and Optimus models unify, "it's going to be fire."
2. **World simulator and closed-loop training:** In open-loop testing, a recorded clip is replayed and the policy is checked against what the human driver did. The recording never reacts, so the consequences of the policy's own choices are invisible. Closed-loop training fixes this: the simulator generates the result of each action, so Tesla can rerun near-crashes and rare edge cases many times and grade each new build before it ships. Elluswamy has said the same video-generation models will work for Optimus navigating the Gigafactory, letting the robot train without risking hardware or people. The host's conclusion is that crossover is strongest for perception ("seeing") and weakest for action ("doing").
