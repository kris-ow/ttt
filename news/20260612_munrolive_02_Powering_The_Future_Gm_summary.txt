This video from Munro Live features a floor interview at GM's "Empower" event, where host interviews Andy Ary, a battery engineer at GM Energy, about GM's new partnership with Peak Energy to develop grid-scale sodium-ion energy storage systems. The core thesis is that sodium-ion chemistry — passively cooled, longer-lasting, and built from abundant domestic materials — is uniquely well-suited for stationary grid storage, offering lower operating costs and greater supply chain resilience than incumbent lithium-ion chemistries.

## Energy

1. **GM and Peak Energy Partnership on Sodium-Ion Grid Storage:** GM Energy has announced a collaboration with Peak Energy to advance grid-scale stationary energy storage using sodium-ion battery chemistry. GM contributes next-generation cell development, R&D labs, and manufacturing process expertise from its Warren, Michigan campus, while Peak Energy brings a novel passively cooled energy storage system architecture already developed through prior engineering work. The goal is to combine Peak's system-level design with GM's cell chemistry improvements to create a superior, lower-cost grid storage product.

2. **Why Sodium-Ion for Grid Storage:** Andy Ary explains that sodium-ion chemistry is specifically well-matched to stationary grid storage requirements because it operates across a wider range of temperatures and achieves far greater cycle life than automotive lithium-ion cells. These properties allow the elimination of active liquid cooling systems, removing the parasitic electricity draw needed to "care and feed" conventional battery chemistries. The result is a passively cooled system with lower operating costs and reduced complexity — what Ary describes as delivering the "lowest cost electron and the lowest worry electron" to utility customers.

3. **Passive Cooling Architecture Using Thermodynamics:** The Peak Energy system stacks nine large battery modules on top of each other at a deliberate tilt, with gaps between them. As heat rises, it naturally draws airflow between the packs — a chimney effect — keeping temperatures even without any active cooling hardware. Ary draws an analogy to consumer electronics (iPhones, power tools, flashlights) to illustrate the design goal: silent, stationary objects that simply do their job without maintenance concern.

4. **Cycle Life and 20-Year Design Target:** The sodium-ion grid storage system is designed to last 20 years or more, requiring 10,000 to 20,000 full cycles. Ary notes this is roughly 3 to 4 times the cycle count compared to other chemistries in GM's portfolio that are not tailored for storage applications. By contrast, an EV battery reaching 1,000 full cycles on a 300-mile-range vehicle would have covered 300,000 miles — far fewer cycles than a daily-cycled grid storage system needs.

5. **Material Cost Advantages of Sodium-Ion:** Sodium-ion cells can use aluminum foil on both the anode and cathode, whereas lithium-ion cells require more expensive and heavier copper on the anode due to the underlying electrochemistry. Ary notes that using aluminum on a lithium-ion anode would cause immediate alloy-formation degradation, a problem that does not exist with sodium-ion. Additionally, sodium as a raw material is abundant domestically — Ary jokes that the state of Michigan sits on top of a giant salt mine — offering a pathway to greater supply chain resilience, though he acknowledges further processing to battery-grade material is still required.

6. **Next-Generation Cell Development and Manufacturing Bridge:** GM is developing a next-generation sodium-ion cell at its Warren, Michigan R&D and prototype labs, targeting higher energy density and lower cost with the potential to outperform more mature chemistries over time. GM's Battery Cell Development Center can produce several thousand cells per day at production speeds, bridging the gap between prototyping and gigascale manufacturing. The interviewer also notes GM's broader partnership ecosystem includes PG&E and Charge Point in addition to Peak Energy.

7. **Second-Life Battery Packs at GM Plants:** A brief announcement noted at the event's opening states that GM plans to deploy second-life battery packs at its own operating plants starting next year (2027), beginning at a site in Michigan.

8. **GM Energy Bidirectional Chargers Now Ready:** Also announced at the event, GM Energy's bidirectional chargers are officially ready to support Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) functionality.
