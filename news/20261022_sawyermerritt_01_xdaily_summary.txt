## Executive Summary
Both Tesla and SpaceX reported Q3 2026 earnings on the same day, giving an unusually dense data dump: Tesla showed strong FSD attach-rate and subscription growth plus Robotaxi/Optimus expansion news, while SpaceX posted explosive revenue/EBITDA growth and massive capex guidance tied to its AI compute buildout. Wall Street commentary (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Brad Gerstner) continued to focus heavily on a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger and SpaceX's compute/neocloud strategy, with JPMorgan sharply raising its SpaceX price target.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla Q3 2026 earnings — FSD and subscriptions driving the beat narrative:** Tesla reported Q3 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.48 (vs. $0.45 est.), revenue of $28.05B (vs. $28.3B est.), adjusted EBITDA of $3.90B, GAAP net income of $787M, and free cash flow of $1.53B. FSD attach rate in North America hit a record 75% of new deliveries (up from 55% in Q2 2026), and active FSD subscriptions rose 67% YoY to 1.74 million (up 260,000 from Q2), the largest quarterly subscriber add in Tesla's history. Tesla also said it ended Q3 with its largest order backlog for passenger vehicles in four years, though battery availability remained the limiting factor on auto volumes. Elon Musk said on the call that demand is "growing rapidly" and exceeding production, and reiterated a target of 5 million vehicles/year by 2030.

2. **Robotaxi expansion — Dallas launch, new cities, safety reporting site:** Tesla officially announced public Unsupervised Cybercab rides launching in Dallas next month, and said robotaxi service is coming "soon" to Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio. Tesla launched a new Robotaxi safety report website featuring a live mile counter and reporting zero people injured, zero airbag deployments, and zero pedestrians/cyclists hit to date. The Unsupervised Robotaxi fleet is now averaging about 80,600 miles/day, a figure Tesla says is rising as the fleet and operating hours grow. Tesla also published new video compilations showing autonomous vehicles handling emergency-vehicle stops, pedestrians, heavy rain, and tricky real-world scenarios, alongside a new "first responder" microsite describing protocol training with emergency services.

3. **Cybercab production and customer delivery timeline:** Lars Moravy said Tesla is "trying to get Cybercabs to customers by the end of this year, 2026," with units currently produced on the Texas line going entirely to Tesla's internal Robotaxi fleet; external deliveries would follow as production scales. Elon Musk separately stated that, absent government restrictions, Tesla will "end up doing a majority of all passenger miles in any country where it is allowed to operate."

4. **FSD Unsupervised customer rollout and mapping architecture:** Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy said FSD Unsupervised could begin rolling out to customer-owned cars in select U.S. areas in Q2 2027, after which those customers could add their vehicles to the robotaxi network. He also said Tesla is building its own in-house mapping system using "an entirely new architecture with AI" that learns both individual driver and fleet-wide preferences, rather than relying on third-party maps.

5. **Optimus: production start, AI5 chip, and upcoming demo:** Tesla said it has officially started Optimus production on high-volume lines in Fremont, describing the units coming off the line as the first "production quality" bots, while noting hardware and manufacturing processes will continue to be iterated. Elon Musk said Optimus growth will follow an S-curve with an initially "shallow slope," that the "vast majority" of a 1-million-unit/year line is already installed at Fremont, and that a video demo of Optimus performing non-pre-programmed tasks could be released "in about a month or two." Musk also said integrating the next-gen AI5 chip into Optimus is a near-term milestone, and separately said AI5 chips could begin shipping to customers in late 2027.

6. **Compute buildout — Terafab, training compute, Austin Fab:** Tesla said it plans to reach 400 MW of AI training compute (referred to as "SI training compute") by year-end, supporting both vehicle and humanoid robot autonomy software, and that construction continues on the Austin Fab, with equipment commissioning targeted for 2027. Elon Musk separately described a facility called "Terafab" as having economic value that would place it "in the top 20 of countries, in a building" (qualitative claim, no specific valuation given).

7. **SpaceX Q3 2026 earnings — triple-digit growth, heavy capex guidance:** SpaceX reported Q3 2026 revenue of $14.58B, up 161% YoY (from $5.58B) and up 87% QoQ (from $7.81B), with adjusted EBITDA of $8.81B, up 306% YoY and 149% QoQ. SpaceX guided Q4 2026 capex to $35–40 billion, which the poster noted is roughly equal to Tesla's total capex over the prior four years combined; the post also claimed compute payback periods under 12 months and that SpaceX would nearly double its compute capacity (detail cut off in source).

8. **SpaceX valuation and merger commentary from analysts:** JPMorgan raised its SpaceX price target to $500 (from $240), implying a $6.6 trillion market cap, and forecast 2027 SpaceX revenue of $254 billion with computing capacity potentially reaching 10 GW by year-end; the analyst reiterated seeing "strong strategic logic" in a potential Tesla-SpaceX combination. Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas framed SpaceX's neocloud/compute buildout as a "funding strategy" letting SpaceX have others pay for infrastructure it will ultimately use to train its own robots. Brad Gerstner commented on a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger, noting Tesla's special committee has a unique fiduciary duty given Musk's involvement in both companies, and suggested (his own estimate) a share price range of roughly $420–$550/share in such a scenario.

9. **Miscellaneous product/hardware notes:** New photos surfaced of the Tesla Semi production line. Tesla launched a new iPad app and showed a new iPhone "Duo" app screenshot featuring a paint-color-matched glow effect around the car. Separately, Hutto, Texas City Council directed staff to evaluate costs for a three-vehicle Cybertruck test for its police department after a council member's personal cost experience with fuel/maintenance; a Las Vegas police department reference was also mentioned but cut off in the source.
