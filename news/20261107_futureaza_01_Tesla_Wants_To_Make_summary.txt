This episode of Futuraza discusses Tesla's Cybercab robotaxi rollout strategy, its stated goal of driving ride costs toward 30 cents per mile (potentially under a dollar per ride), the geographic pattern of geofence expansions, the regulatory outlook after the midterm elections, and a comparison with Waymo's expansion strategy and cost structure.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab approaching Waymo's Texas fleet size:** As of recording, the hosts cite registered robotaxi counts of roughly 1,044 for Tesla versus roughly 1,059 for Waymo in Texas — a gap of about 15 cars — with the host expecting Tesla to overtake Waymo within the week or by the following week. One host downplays the milestone's significance, arguing the more meaningful threshold would be reaching around 2,000 vehicles rather than crossing Waymo's current count.
2. **Geofence size comparison across cities:** The hosts cite current geofence sizes (units unspecified in transcript, likely square miles) as: Austin 272, Houston 254 (reportedly increased roughly tenfold the prior week), Fort Worth 143, Dallas 81, Miami 31, Orlando 26, and Tampa 24. One host theorizes Tesla is prioritizing Texas (Austin, Fort Worth, Dallas, eventually Houston) to eventually link these geofences into one large multi-city network, rather than expanding piecemeal into Florida or Nevada.
3. **Nevada permit and expected launch timing:** Nevada has reportedly issued Tesla a permit covering up to 5,000 units. The hosts note a rule requiring launch within 90 days of permit approval, implying a Las Vegas launch before the end of the year (per the hosts' reading of the rule), though the scale of that launch is unknown.
4. **Highway and expanded operating hours enabled:** One host notes that roughly a week before this recording, Tesla expanded Robotaxi operating hours, and that Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla would begin operating robotaxis on highways, at higher speeds, and in a wider range of weather conditions — changes the hosts say have already been implemented.
5. **Sub-dollar ride pricing goal and cost target:** The hosts say Tesla executives (CFO Vaibhav Taneja, VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy, and Elon Musk) stated on the earnings call that Cybercab production and FSD/Autopilot safety work are in final stages ahead of regulatory approval, and that Tesla is "hyper-focused" on reaching a cost of around 30 cents per mile, with an ultimate goal of rides costing less than a dollar — framed as changing transportation broadly, not just robotaxis.
6. **Ashok's "millions" comment on fleet deployment:** One host notes Ashok Elluswamy said twice on the earnings call that Tesla looks forward to deploying "millions" of robotaxis in the coming months — a figure the host initially assumed was a slip but now takes as intentional, interpreted as Tesla preparing for scaled deployment contingent on regulatory approval.
7. **Cybercab's trunk reportedly reserved for same-day cargo delivery:** One host states that Cybercab's trunk cannot be opened by riders because it is used to carry cargo for same-day delivery, which the host suggests helps subsidize/lower the cost of rides.

## Market & Competition
1. **Regulatory outlook shifts to state-by-state after midterms:** The hosts argue that the midterm elections have significantly reduced the likelihood of a federal autonomous-vehicle regulatory framework, leading them to expect state-by-state approval processes rather than a national mandate — a dynamic they believe will slow but not stop robotaxi expansion, with growth concentrated in states that are receptive.
2. **Waymo's "other bets" losses and conversion-model inefficiency:** Citing Google's reported "other bets" segment figures from the prior week, the hosts say Waymo (part of that segment) is losing approximately $2 billion per quarter, a figure they say is growing. The hosts criticize Waymo's strategy of partnering with and converting multiple third-party vehicles (citing Zeekr, Hyundai, Jaguar, and a newly announced Toyota partnership) as costly and inefficient, asserting that per-vehicle conversion costs approach what Tesla spends building an all-new Cybercab, and noting Tesla's claim that Cybercab is certified for roughly three times the range of a Waymo vehicle (cited by the host as 500 miles).
3. **Speculation on Waymo's long-term strategy:** One host raises a theory attributed to Jo Bhakdi that Waymo's current approach is about securing regulatory approvals and market presence before eventually introducing its own purpose-built vehicle while moving away from lidar toward neural-network-based perception. A second host instead speculates Waymo could eventually be sold to Uber or a struggling traditional automaker (mentioning Nissan, Subaru, and Mazda as examples of automakers facing difficulties), arguing that having software and hardware split across separate companies is an unsustainable structure.
