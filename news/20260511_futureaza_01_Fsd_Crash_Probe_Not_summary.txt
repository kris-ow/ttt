This is a Monday morning news roundup from FutureAzA host Brian, covering a broad range of Tesla and EV industry stories from May 11, 2026. The video touches on the end of Model S/X production, Tesla's FSD regulatory engagement in Ireland, a competitor AV crash investigation, battery technology comparisons, and a range of market and competitive developments. The tone is conversational and editorial, blending news summaries with commentary and audience Q&A.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **End of the Model S and X Era:** Tesla has produced its last Model S and Model X at the factory, marking 14 years of history for the Model S and 11 for the Model X. The final vehicles were signed by factory workers in a graffiti-style tribute. However, with just 72 hours' notice, Tesla delayed the planned final delivery event, which Brian suggests may be because production was not actually fully complete — citing leaked footage reportedly showing a worker installing the final brake booster.

2. **Giga Shanghai Best April Ever:** Giga Shanghai recorded its best-ever April, producing 79,000 cars, putting it on pace for approximately one million vehicles per year from the 10-million-square-foot factory. This marked the sixth consecutive month of growth as the factory continues to rebound.

3. **Model 3 Global Sales Strength:** Despite narratives about the Model 3's decline, Brian notes it was the second best-selling EV in the world in March, and Canada has now been added to its strong markets, with tens of thousands expected to be sold there.

4. **Tesla Semi Battery Capacity Published:** California regulators published the Tesla Semi's official battery capacity — 822 kWh for the long-range variant and 548 kWh for the standard range. Brian downplays the significance of battery size alone, emphasizing range as the key metric. He notes stacking incentives in California, Oregon, Washington, New Jersey, New York, and other states on top of an already competitively priced vehicle could make the Semi significantly more attractive.

5. **Tesla Semi Appearing on European Websites:** The Tesla Semi is now appearing on Tesla's websites in Europe, including the UK, though only the standard (short) range version is listed so far.

6. **Model Y Tops New NCAP ADAS Test:** A Model Y manufactured on or after November 12th, 2025 became the first vehicle to pass a new NCAP ADAS test covering pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention. As of two days before the video, no other automaker had passed more than three of the four criteria, leaving Tesla ahead by at least 33% on those safety metrics.

7. **Tesla Owner Loyalty Remains Strong:** Despite brand controversies, Tesla maintains high owner loyalty — 63.6% among Asian households and 61.9% among Hispanic households, down from the 80%+ of early adopter days but still notably strong.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Engages Irish Authorities on FSD Approval:** Ireland's Department of Transportation confirmed in a statement to RTÉ News that Tesla is currently engaging with Irish authorities, including the Irish approval body, regarding their FSD system. The department noted that negotiations will continue on both national and EU levels but that no timeline for EU approval can be provided. Brian notes that EU-level approval would unlock Ireland as well.

2. **Unusual Volume of NHTSA Tesla Complaints:** Brian relays a secondhand account from someone with a contact at NHTSA, who noted Tesla was receiving more complaints than there were units of the relevant car sold — suggesting organized bad-faith complaint filing intended to obstruct Tesla's regulatory progress, rather than legitimate consumer grievances.

## Robotaxi

1. **Competitor AV Company Under NHTSA Investigation:** A Tesla robotaxi rival (not named in the transcript but described as operating in Dallas and Austin, developing since 2017, and available on Uber) is under NHTSA investigation following 16 accidents involving its automated driving system, resulting in one minor injury and property damage. Alleged ADS failures included changing lanes into the path of other vehicles, failing to respond to vehicles entering the lane, and striking partially obstructed stationary objects. Brian notes the headline required putting Tesla's name in it to generate clicks, despite Tesla not being the subject.

2. **Customer-Owned Vehicles Entering the Taxi Fleet:** In Q&A, Brian was asked when customer-owned vehicles would enter the robotaxi fleet, calling it "the trillion dollar question" with no current answer. He added that a step change in Cybercab deployment is not expected in April or May 2026, but that it is "definitely" coming in 2026 with no roadblocks having appeared — quite the opposite.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Piper Sandler Says Optimus Is Free at Current Valuations:** Piper Sandler published an update arguing that at $400 per share, investors are effectively getting Optimus for free — meaning their valuation model has not yet assigned any value to Optimus. Brian is skeptical, questioning how they arrived at a trillion-dollar valuation without counting Optimus, and noting that Piper Sandler says they will begin incorporating Optimus into valuation models once revenue arrives.

## Energy

1. **New CATL Sun WODA LFP Battery:** A new LFP battery from CATL called the Sun WODA features a 62.5 kWh capacity (fully usable), ultra-fast 3C charging enabling 10–80% charge in 15 minutes (versus 25 minutes previously), and a 0–62 mph time of 5.2 seconds (versus 6.1 seconds). Brian frames this as evidence that LFP batteries have already addressed most of what solid-state batteries were hoped to solve — lower cost, faster charging, and fire resistance — making solid-state less urgently necessary.

2. **4680 Battery Falls Short on Energy Density:** Citing analyst Yash Bajaj, Brian notes that Tesla's 4680 battery achieves 244 Wh/kg versus 269 Wh/kg for the 2170 cells — approximately 8% lower energy density. In real-world charging, 15 minutes of charging delivers 27 kWh. Brian acknowledges the 4680 likely offers cost advantages but concedes it underperforms on energy density metrics relative to 2170s.

3. **Wind and Solar Already Cheaper Than Fossil Fuels:** New gas capacity costs $70–85 per MWh, new coal costs approximately $100 per MWh, while wind and solar cost $54–82 per MWh — already cheaper unsubsidized.

## Market & Competition

1. **China Investigates EV "Battery Locking" Claims — Then Debunks Them:** Chinese state media reported that EVs rated for 500 km of range were being reduced to under 300 km via over-the-air battery management updates. Multiple automakers including BYD, Tesla, NIO, Xpeng, Zeekr, GAC, and Li Auto denied the claims. BYD threatened legal action. Brian characterizes the story as having snowballed from a vague regulatory inquiry into unfounded accusations, drawing a parallel to the NHTSA complaint manipulation story.

2. **European Automakers Selling Factories to Chinese Partners:** Stellantis announced it is considering selling an underutilized factory in Spain to its Chinese joint venture partner Leap Motors. Brian argues this doesn't merely "risk" strengthening Chinese automakers — it guarantees it. He notes European factories are operating at roughly half capacity and face structural competitive pressure from Chinese EV makers' technical advancement and low production costs. The observation is that steep import tariffs are being bypassed by Chinese firms simply building locally.

3. **Tesla–Intel Semiconductor Partnership:** Elon Musk toured Intel's semiconductor fab in Hillsboro ahead of a reported Tesla and SpaceX partnership with Intel. Wedbush published commentary calling it both a "compelling opportunity" and issuing warnings about the gap between announced plans and actual execution, noting the path contains significant risks.

4. **XAI / SpaceX Merger and Anthropic Deal:** Anthropic announced it is renting server space from XAI data centers. The merged company of SpaceX and XAI is expected to be named "SpaceX AAI." Brian notes this as an interesting revenue model for the combined entity.

5. **Tesla Recall of 218,000 Vehicles:** Tesla issued a recall of over 218,000 vehicles in the US, which Brian characterizes as a non-event — the issue was a backup camera glitch resolved over the air.
