This episode of The Tesla Space covers several major Tesla and adjacent tech developments, with the central focus on Tesla Vision's new predictive safety feature and its growing validation from federal regulators. The video also touches on the end of Model S and Model X production, Optimus manufacturing plans, SpaceX/xAI orbital compute developments, and The Boring Company's Nashville expansion.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Predictive Pre-Crash Safety via Tesla Vision:** Tesla has revealed a new safety feature that uses Tesla Vision — its camera-based AI system — to predict a crash before physical impact occurs. Rather than waiting for traditional impact sensors to detect a collision as it begins, the AI analyzes camera data to recognize an imminent crash and pre-deploys airbags and seat belt tensioners up to 70 milliseconds earlier than conventional systems. Tesla notes that body position at the moment of airbag deployment significantly affects injury outcomes, and those additional milliseconds can reduce injuries in certain crash types. The feature will come standard on all new Tesla vehicles and will be delivered via a free over-the-air software update to existing vehicles running the camera-based vision system.

2. **Photon Count Reconstruction for Extreme Glare:** Elon Musk posted on X a demonstration of Tesla's AI capability to reconstruct a driving scene obscured by direct sunlight glare. He shared two images of the same scene: the first showed a washed-out road indistinguishable to the human eye, while the second — processed using what Musk described as "photon count reconstruction" — clearly showed the road, nearby cars, lane markings, and trees. The AI analyzes raw light data underneath the standard visual capture, rebuilding a clearer environmental picture even when the camera sensor is overwhelmed by brightness, directly supporting the case for a cameras-only perception approach.

3. **NHTSA Validation: 2026 Model Y Passes Advanced Driver Assistance Benchmarks:** The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration announced that the 2026 Tesla Model Y became the first vehicle in the United States to pass NHTSA's new advanced driver assistance system benchmark tests. The Model Y passed evaluations for pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention, as well as earlier benchmarks including forward collision warning and crash imminent braking. NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison called the results "a significant step forward" and said the Model Y "demonstrated the life-saving potential of driver assistance technologies and sets a high bar for the industry."

## Electric Vehicles

1. **End of Model S and Model X Production:** Tesla has officially ended production of both the Model S and Model X at the Fremont factory. The company shared photos of the final vehicles rolling off the line and confirmed a farewell delivery event for the final Signature Edition versions of both models is scheduled for May 20th. Videos showed the Tesla team personally signing the last Model S ever produced. The Model S launched in 2012 and the Model X in 2015, and together the two models delivered approximately 750,000 vehicles combined worldwide over their production lifetimes.

2. **Giga Berlin Milestone Mirrors Combined S/X Legacy:** Giga Berlin, which began production in 2022, announced it has now built 750,000 vehicles of its own — all of a single mass-market model. The video highlights this as a striking contrast: what took two flagship models more than a decade to achieve, a single modern factory producing one car matched in just a few years.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Fremont Production Line Repurposed for Optimus:** With Model S and Model X production ending, Tesla plans to repurpose the freed production line at the Fremont factory for Optimus, its humanoid robot. Long-term plans call for eventually producing up to 1 million Optimus units per year at the facility. The video frames this as a thematic passing of the torch — the same factory floor that once hosted Tesla's biggest early gamble is now being handed to what the host describes as an even larger and riskier one.

## Market & Competition

1. **xAI Leases Colossus 1 to Anthropic, Eyes Orbital Compute:** xAI announced a compute partnership with Anthropic, giving Anthropic access to Colossus 1, xAI's supercomputer built using more than 220,000 high-end Nvidia GPUs. According to Elon Musk, xAI has already moved its own AI training to Colossus 2, making Colossus 1 available for lease. Notably, the announcement included Anthropic expressing interest in partnering to develop multiple gigawatts of orbital AI compute capacity — meaning AI supercomputers in space. The video connects this to Terafab, described as a joint project between Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI aimed at producing 1 terawatt-hour of chips annually, with the majority intended for orbital data centers powered by unlimited solar energy.

2. **SpaceX Starship Flight 12 Rehearsal Completed:** SpaceX completed a full launch rehearsal for Starship Flight 12, loading more than 5,000 metric tons of supercooled rocket fuel into the fully assembled Version 3 Starship and its Superheavy booster for the first time. Starship V3 is described as the largest and most powerful rocket SpaceX has ever built. Elon Musk indicated Flight 12 could happen around mid-May if final testing continues to go well. The video frames Starship's progress as critical infrastructure for the orbital data center thesis, as well as for eventual transport of humans and Optimus robots to the moon and Mars.

3. **The Boring Company Enters Residential Infrastructure in Nashville:** The Boring Company announced its first residential station agreement for the Music City Loop in Nashville, covering three downtown residential towers: Prime, Alcove, and Paramount. Residents will be able to take an elevator directly to an underground loop station inside their building, board a Tesla, and travel to Nashville International Airport in approximately 9 minutes or to lower Broadway in roughly 2 minutes. The video characterizes this as a significant shift from connecting large destination infrastructure to integrating the loop system into everyday residential life.
