This is Tesla's Q3 2025 earnings call, featuring opening remarks from Elon Musk and CFO Vaibhav Taneja followed by analyst and investor Q&A. The core narrative: Tesla claims a inflection point in scaling unsupervised full self-driving and robotaxi, record quarterly financial metrics, an accelerating custom AI chip roadmap (AI5), and continued heavy investment in Optimus, alongside a call to shareholders to back Musk's compensation package and board slate ahead of the November 6th vote.

## Financials
1. **Record quarter across multiple metrics:** Taneja said Q3 set records for deliveries, deployments, total revenue, energy gross profit, energy margins, and free cash flow. Free cash flow was approximately $4 billion; total cash and investments ended the quarter above $41 billion.
2. **Delivery growth by region (sequential):** Greater China up 33%, APAC up 29%, North America up 28%, and AMIA up 25% sequentially, driven by new Model Y variants including long wheelbase, performance, and Standard Y.
3. **Automotive margins and revenue:** Automotive revenue rose 29% sequentially in line with delivery growth; automotive margin excluding credits improved from 15% to 15.4%, attributed to lower material costs and better fixed-cost absorption from higher volumes. Regulatory credit revenue declined sequentially.
4. **Tariff impact:** Total tariff impact across both the automotive and energy businesses in Q3 exceeded $400 million, split roughly evenly between them; the energy storage business is more exposed as a share of COGS since supply currently comes from China, though Megafactory Shanghai ramp is helping supply non-US demand and avoid tariffs.
5. **Opex increases:** Sequential opex growth was driven by restructuring costs tied to converging AI chip design efforts, legal expenses from ongoing proceedings, costs related to preparing for the shareholder meeting, and rising R&D employee costs from new performance-based equity awards for AI-focused staff, which Taneja said will continue increasing.
6. **Other income and capex:** Other income fell sequentially mainly due to a smaller mark-to-market gain on Bitcoin holdings (about $80 million in Q3 versus $284 million in Q2), plus FX effects. 2025 capex guidance remains around $9 billion, with Taneja projecting a substantial increase in 2026 to fund growth including AI initiatives and Optimus.

## Robotaxi
1. **Safety driver removal timeline:** Musk said Tesla expects to remove safety drivers from at least large parts of Austin within a few months (by year-end), and expects to be operating robotaxi service in roughly 8–10 metro areas by year-end, including Nevada, Florida, and Arizona, pending regulatory approvals.
2. **Fleet mileage milestones:** Ashok Elluswamy said the driverless Austin fleet has covered more than a quarter million miles, while the Bay Area fleet (still with a safety driver present due to regulation) has crossed more than a million miles; supervised FSD overall has been used for 6 billion cumulative miles as of the day before the call.
3. **New-market rollout approach:** Musk said safety drivers/occupants will be used in new markets for roughly three months as a cautious check for city-specific edge cases before removal, regardless of regulatory requirements.
4. **Cybercab production timing:** Musk said the Cybercab — a vehicle with no steering wheel or pedals, optimized purely for cost-per-mile and comfort rather than performance — begins production in Q2 next year and represents the single biggest planned expansion in production volume.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v14 rollout and roadmap:** Version 14.1 is available to any US customer who opts into "advanced software." Musk said most users should wait for v14.2, when comfort issues will be addressed, since major releases prioritize safety first. Reasoning capability is planned to be added later this year (targeted for v14.3/14.4).
2. **FSD adoption and hardware 3:** Paid FSD customers remain a small share (around 12%) of the current fleet. Tesla has not abandoned Hardware 3 customers and has offered transfer/preferential-rate promotions; a "V14 light" version for Hardware 3 is planned, expected around Q2 next year.
3. **AI5 chip roadmap:** Musk gave a detailed account of the AI5 chip, calling it by some metrics 40x better than AI4 (conservatively framed as at least a 10x improvement), designed with radical simplification (legacy GPU and image signal processor deleted) to fit in a half reticle. Both TSMC (Arizona) and Samsung (Texas) will now focus initially on AI5 production rather than AI5/AI6 split as previously suggested; Tesla's explicit goal is oversupply, with excess AI5 chips usable in data centers alongside existing AI4 and Nvidia hardware.
4. **Grok vs. Tesla AI distinction:** Musk said Tesla's onboard models are roughly 5–10% the size of Grok, which is designed for general intelligence and requires massive compute (Grok 5 reportedly will only run effectively on Nvidia's GB300). Some crossover exists — Grok powers voice interaction in the car and Optimus voice recognition/generation.
5. **Semi truck autonomy:** Tesla's Semi factory is on schedule, with validation trucks on the road, larger builds by year-end, first production builds in early next year, and real volume in the back half of next year. Autonomy work is currently focused on passenger vehicles, but the same technology is expected to extend to Semi once sufficient truck data is collected.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus V3 unveiling timeline:** Musk said a production-intent Optimus V3 prototype will be shown, likely in Q1 (probably February or March), and described it as looking nearly indistinguishable from a person in a robot suit.
2. **Manufacturing challenge and scale ambitions:** Musk emphasized that no existing supply chain exists for humanoid robots, requiring deep vertical integration; Tesla aims for a production line targeting roughly a million Optimus units annually, with production start hoped for toward the end of next year, followed by Optimus 4 (~10 million units) and Optimus 5 (~50–100 million units) as long-term aspirations.
3. **Hand/forearm as hardest engineering problem:** Musk said the Optimus hand and forearm are, from an electromechanical standpoint, more difficult to engineer than the rest of the robot combined, given the dexterity required.
4. **Design iteration continues past production start:** Musk said the hardware design will not be frozen even at start of production; rolling changes will continue as manufacturing difficulties are discovered.

## Energy
1. **Megapack roadmap:** Tesla unveiled Megablock and Megapack 3, with Megapack 4 planned to incorporate substation-like functionality, outputting at roughly 35 kilovolts directly to simplify deployment without needing a separate substation build.
2. **Demand strength and new products:** Demand for Megapack and Powerwall remains strong into next year; Megablock is set to begin shipping next year from Houston. Residential solar demand has surged in the US due to policy changes, expected to continue into the first half of next year with a new solar lease product; Tesla began producing its residential solar panel at its Buffalo factory, with shipments starting Q1.

## Market & Competition
1. **Shareholder vote on compensation and board:** Taneja urged shareholders to support Musk's leadership via two compensation proposals and the reelection of three board members (Ira, Kathleen, and Joe) at the November 6th annual meeting. Musk reiterated that his goal with the compensation structure is to secure enough voting influence (he suggested roughly the mid-20s percent range) to avoid being ousted after building what he calls a "robot army," while remaining removable if he "goes insane." He criticized proxy advisors ISS and Glass Lewis, calling their influence over passive/index fund voting a corporate governance problem.
