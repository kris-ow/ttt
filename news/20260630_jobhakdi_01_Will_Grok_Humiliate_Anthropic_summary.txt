Jo Bhakdi argues that Grok is on the verge of overtaking Anthropic as the leading AI/AGI platform, driven by SpaceX AI's acquisition of Cursor, the Colossus training cluster compute advantage, and rapid iteration speed. The video's core thesis is that Grok 4.5 — now in private beta for SpaceX and Tesla — combined with Cursor's enterprise data footprint, positions the Elon Musk AI ecosystem to surpass Anthropic in both capability and commercial reach within 2026. Bhakdi also shares his personal experience of Claude's deterioration in writing quality and general usefulness, contrasting it with his growing preference for Grok.

## Market & Competition

1. **Grok 4.5 enters private beta for SpaceX and Tesla:** Bhakdi reports that Elon Musk tweeted that Grok 4.5 — described as based on a 1.5 trillion parameter version 9 foundation model with Cursor data, edited and supplemental training — is now in private beta, being rolled out internally for use and testing at SpaceX and Tesla. Reinforcement learning is said to be continuing to significantly improve the model. Fully retrained-from-scratch new models are reportedly planned to be released to SpaceX every month this year, according to Musk's tweet.

2. **SpaceX acquires Cursor for $60 billion:** SpaceX AI acquired Cursor — described as the leading enterprise AI coding harness — for $60 billion, a deal Bhakdi says he predicted. Cursor is characterized as having the highest enterprise adoption across the United States, broader than Anthropic's footprint, with named customers including British Airways, Hilton, Stripe, Adobe, Datadog, Nvidia, and OpenAI. Bhakdi argues the primary strategic value is the training data: Cursor gives Grok visibility into what enterprise coders and forward-deployed engineers actually do across thousands of companies, which he frames as essential for building "true enterprise AGI" — a market he says SpaceX's internal documents have characterized as a $22 trillion total addressable market.

3. **Colossus compute advantage cited as structural edge:** Bhakdi highlights Colossus, described as SpaceX AI's proprietary training cluster, as giving the Grok team the ability to train models faster, deeper, and more comprehensively than competitors. Combined with the Cursor acquisition and the Grok Build coding harness (positioned as a direct competitor to Claude's coding tools and OpenAI's Codex), Bhakdi believes Grok has the ingredients to take the leading position in AI capability.

4. **Anthropic and Claude criticized for deteriorating quality:** Bhakdi describes Claude as having deteriorated sharply in general-purpose writing and reasoning, attributing a notable decline in quality to around the time of Anthropic's "Mythos" or "Fable" release. He characterizes Claude as "sycophantic," producing "lazy replies" and poor English writing. He says ChatGPT remains "heavily woke" and frustrating for similar reasons. By contrast, he describes Grok as now the best performer for writing and general use, and says he finds himself using it "much more these days."

5. **Grok Build harness seen as key competitive lever:** Bhakdi emphasizes that the "Grok Build harness" — Grok's integrated coding and agentic environment — is improving rapidly and daily, and frames harness quality (rather than raw model capability) as the decisive battleground. He argues that frontier models are now capable enough ("AGI core components") and that specialized harnesses built around them are what will determine who wins commercially. He predicts that by end of 2026, AI systems will be able to replace any job, and by mid-to-late 2027 this will be broadly evident across the market.
