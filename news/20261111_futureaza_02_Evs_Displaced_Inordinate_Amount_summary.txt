This episode of a two-host discussion show covers global EV and e-bike adoption trends, their effect on oil consumption, regional EV market-share shifts, and comparative fuel-cost economics between gasoline and electric vehicles — with only incidental references to specific Tesla models used as cost-comparison examples.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EVs and hybrids displacing millions of barrels of oil daily:** One host states that electric and electrified vehicles will displace 2.3 million barrels of oil per day in 2025, with that figure expected to double by the end of the decade as sales continue growing in China, Europe, and India despite a US slowdown. Electric two- and three-wheeled vehicles are cited as currently displacing more oil than cars in some Asian markets, at 1.1 million barrels per day.
2. **Regional EV market share diverging sharply:** Citing a comparison attributed to "Felix Hammer" (an analyst referenced by the hosts), the hosts note China's EV market share grew from roughly 50% to near 60% year-over-year, Oceania from about 12% to 30%, and Central/South America and non-China Asia crossed the 10% threshold, while North America is described as moving in the wrong direction (declining share). The UK is noted as reportedly lowering its 2030 EV target to 60% despite already being near 50%, which one host calls "capitulation."
3. **Norway's fleet-turnover dynamics:** One host notes that although around 98% of new car sales in Norway are electric, electric vehicles make up about 35.5% of the total vehicle fleet, because cars only leave the road upon scrapping and the average scrap age in Norway is cited as nearly 19 years. Using a fleet-turnover model ("Le Raffle"), he projects roughly 50% EV fleet penetration in Norway by 2029, and estimates it would take until 2037 or later — even with near-100% EV sales — to approach full fleet turnover.
4. **US truck market as an adoption bottleneck:** One host argues that the lack of a serious electric competitor in the large-truck segment is a major reason US EV adoption lags, given the popularity of large trucks in the US market.
5. **Gasoline price elasticity and EV/bike sales correlation:** One host states that US gasoline prices, adjusted for inflation, are roughly in the middle of the range seen over the last 25 years, though still comparatively low in the Midwest and South. He draws on his bicycle industry background to note that spikes in gas prices historically correlated with spikes in both bicycle and electric-vehicle (including Tesla) sales, and warns that a drop in oil prices below $60/barrel (which he expects "soon") would hurt EV sales.
6. **Comparative fuel-cost-per-$100 analysis:** The hosts review a study/graphic estimating how far various vehicles can travel on $100 of fuel based on home charging rates (not public charging), noting all-electric vehicles consistently outperform plug-in hybrids, with a Toyota Corolla outperforming a Jeep Grand Cherokee hybrid, and the Chevy Silverado ranking lowest at 366 miles on $100 of gasoline. One host flags skepticism about the dataset's claim that a Model Y travels farther on $100 than a Model 3, saying he doesn't believe that's accurate.
7. **Anecdotal cost comparison at a public event:** One host recounts a conversation at a summer outreach booth where a truck owner said a $60 gas purchase ("just a topping up," not a full tank) would, at the host's in-state home charging rates, be equivalent to roughly 3,000 EV miles — illustrating a stark regional cost gap, particularly in the Northwest where electricity is cheap and gasoline is expensive.
