This video is a deep-dive discussion between host Herbert and two guest analysts, Brian Wang (futurist/blogger, Next Big Future) and Cern Basher (chartered financial analyst), examining how SpaceX could scale from a confirmed 10 gigawatts to a hoped-for 20 gigawatts of AI compute capacity, and how "stranded" GPU compute industry-wide could become a revenue opportunity for SpaceX's data center business. The core thesis: hyperscalers (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta) are chip- and memory-rich but power/building-constrained, while SpaceX can build power and data center shells faster than anyone else, positioning it to rent out finished capacity to others' stranded GPUs for massive short-term revenue on top of its own compute buildout.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **10GW confirmed, 20GW aspirational per Elon Musk:** Brian Wang says third-party analysis (SemiAnalysis) has confirmed SpaceX can build 10 gigawatts of data center capacity, with Musk reportedly saying after Q2 that he could do 10 gigawatts and after Q3 that the power/electrical infrastructure for 20 gigawatts is feasible — but compute/chip supply, not power, is the constraint on reaching 20GW, and Musk reportedly put it at no longer 0% but still "not likely."
2. **Stranded compute thesis:** Wang argues the real bottleneck across the industry is partially-built data centers sitting with chips but no completed power/cooling infrastructure. He cites Google's Q3 earnings call statement that it is renting third-party compute due to supply constraints, and says Google and Anthropic are leasing capacity from SpaceX — Google at roughly $1 billion/month ramping in Q4, and Anthropic (per its S-1, Wang says) going from roughly $1.2 billion to $3.1 billion per month. Wang speculates (unconfirmed) that hyperscalers could effectively "ship" idle racks into SpaceX-built, completed halls to monetize them while their own sites remain under construction.
3. **SpaceX's power sources for the buildout:** Wang details multiple power sources: Doosan reactors (five 380-megawatt units arriving from 2026–2027, with a further seven ordered but not expected until 2029), APR Energy-sourced aircraft-derived turbines (30–40 megawatts each), roughly 1.2–1.7 gigawatts already at Colossus 2, Megapacks, and a reported leak describing a 10-gigawatt Memphis buildout split across grid power, behind-the-meter natural gas, and solar. Wang also credits SpaceX's Tesla-linked virtual power plant/Autobidder expertise with helping it secure faster grid allocation from utilities.
4. **Monetization math (Wang's model, speculative):** Wang's working estimate is roughly $30 billion per gigawatt per year in revenue if SpaceX leases completed buildings/power to others bringing their own chips, versus roughly $50–60 billion+ per gigawatt if SpaceX supplies the chips itself; he frames a scenario of 18 gigawatts total (10 SpaceX-owned, ~5 more from leased/deals) generating a company run rate near $600 billion, with roughly $500 billion from training/owned compute and $150 billion from leased capacity — explicitly caveated as his own model, not a confirmed figure.
5. **Construction cadence and site tour:** Wang projects SpaceX needs to complete roughly 24–30 Vera Rubin-generation data center "halls" (each ~600–700 megawatts) by 2027 to hit 20GW, implying building two to three per month — a pace neither guest could yet confirm is on track based on known/visible construction. Cern Basher walks through satellite imagery of Colossus 2 in Memphis/Mississippi (Macrohard, Macrohard-er, and a "Mini Hard" building roughly 312,000 sq ft housing ~220,000 GPUs) and a new Giga Texas-area site near Starbase/Brownsville (444 acres disannexed into Starbase, with two larger "Mini Harder" buildings potentially ~500,000 sq ft each under construction, plus an additional 893 acres north of the port not yet developed). Reuters reportedly (per Basher, unconfirmed) also flagged Austin/Bastrop as possible additional SpaceX data center sites.
6. **Build time per facility:** Both guests estimate a single "Mini Hard" data center building takes roughly five to seven months to construct, citing SemiAnalysis's five-month estimate and Musk's reported timeline (dirt work in March/April, completion targeted for November).
7. **Alternative "grid edge" modular compute concept:** The hosts discuss a New Street Research theory (cited as published by an analyst there) of thousands of smaller modular "mega pod"-style inference sites (potentially Nvidia DSX pods) installable in roughly 14 days at locations like Supercharger sites, involving partners the discussion links to Nvidia, Dell, Foxconn, Wistron, and others — both guests view this as a plausible supplementary approach but note it isn't expected to ship at volume until roughly Q1–Q2 2027 and still depends on scarce cooling-module supply chains.
8. **Nvidia's incentive alignment:** Cern Basher notes a tension for Nvidia — it wants chips installed and generating usage-based revenue share, but also doesn't want SpaceX's capacity to substitute for direct hyperscaler purchases of next-generation (Vera Rubin) chips; both guests frame Nvidia's reported move to help arrange financing for data center buildouts as evidence it wants the broader ecosystem funded, not just SpaceX.
