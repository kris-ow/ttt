This video features Brett Johnson, SpaceX's Chief Financial Officer (and the only CFO the company has ever had), delivering a formal investor-facing presentation on SpaceX's business across three core segments: space launch, connectivity (Starlink), and AI infrastructure. The central thesis is that SpaceX's unrivaled vertical integration — from rocket manufacturing to satellite deployment to AI compute — positions it to capture a total addressable market exceeding $28 trillion across space, connectivity, and enterprise AI. The content is entirely focused on SpaceX and has no direct relevance to Tesla's autonomous driving, robotaxi, Optimus, or energy thesis.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX's launch milestones and Starship roadmap:** Johnson outlines a series of industry firsts, including the first private liquid-fuel rocket to reach orbit (Falcon 1, 2008), first private docking with the International Space Station (2012), first propulsive landing of a rocket booster (2015), first reuse of an orbital-class booster (2017), and first private crewed launch to the ISS (2020). SpaceX completed 165 Falcon 9 launches in 2025. Starship version 3 is expected to carry approximately 100 metric tons to orbit, with version 4 already in design and targeting approximately 200 metric tons — a doubling of version 3's capacity.

2. **Starlink constellation scale and user growth:** SpaceX operates roughly 9,600 satellites, representing approximately 75% of all active maneuverable satellites in orbit. Starlink ended 2024 with approximately 4.4 million users, grew to approximately 8.9 million by end of 2025 (roughly 100% year-over-year growth), and reached approximately 10.3 million users by the end of Q1 2026. The network covers over 164 countries and territories, representing coverage for over 3 billion people.

3. **V3 satellite and Starship launch capacity step-change:** The current V2 Starlink satellites deliver 96 Gbits per second per satellite, with roughly 27 satellites per Falcon 9 launch. The upcoming V3 satellites represent a 20x improvement in capacity per launch relative to V2 on Falcon. On Starship, 60 V3 satellites per launch — each delivering roughly 1 terabit per second — equates to 61 terabits per second of downlink capacity per launch. Johnson states that after 20 Starship launches, SpaceX will be deploying 1.2 petabytes of capacity per year, positioning Starlink as a meaningful percentage of global internet traffic.

4. **Direct-to-device and Gen 2 expansion:** SpaceX's Gen 1 direct-to-device service was described as a proof of concept — 650 satellites deployed, service activated with 30 mobile network operators globally (largest being T-Mobile in the US), covering 1.9 billion people. Gen 2 is described as full 5G quality to existing handsets and is positioned as the transformational solution to connect the remaining ~3 billion people excluded from the digital economy.

5. **AI infrastructure — Colossus 2 and orbital compute:** SpaceX has built the Colossus 2 data center, described as the world's largest coherent supercomputer, using Nvidia GB300 chips. It is described as the first gigawatt-scale training cluster and includes the first gigawatt-scale mega battery installation. SpaceX also signed a hosting deal with Anthropic and a coding capability deal with Cursor. Johnson highlights orbital AI compute as a near-term future direction, leveraging existing Starlink hardware (ion thrusters, inter-satellite laser links, flight computers) with solar power and radiative cooling to create low-cost, clean-energy AI infrastructure in space.

6. **Total addressable market framing:** Johnson frames SpaceX's opportunity as nearly $6 trillion across its current three segments (space, connectivity, AI infrastructure), and over $28 trillion when including the broader enterprise AI market. Future growth vectors cited include point-to-point terrestrial transportation, space manufacturing, asteroid mining, and a lunar economy, with boots on the moon targeted within the next couple of years.
