## Executive Summary
Today's posts surface three meaningful Tesla investor signals: Tesla's European FSD monetization strategy shifted entirely to subscription, removing one-time purchase options; Meta committed ~$200M to Tesla Megapack batteries for AI datacenter infrastructure in Wyoming; and Tesla is expanding its vehicle sales footprint into Bulgaria. The overall tone is quietly bullish, with recurring evidence of Tesla Energy becoming critical infrastructure and FSD transitioning toward a recurring revenue model in Europe.

---

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **FSD Europe Moves to Subscription-Only, Eliminating One-Time Purchases:** Tesla has ended one-time FSD purchases across Europe, making subscription the only available option. Pricing is set at £99/month in the UK and €99/month in most other European countries. This is a notable monetization shift — it converts FSD from a one-time capital purchase into a recurring revenue stream, which could improve revenue predictability and increase lifetime value per vehicle if retention is strong. It also lowers the upfront barrier to FSD adoption, potentially broadening the addressable customer base in Europe.

2. **Meta to Deploy ~$200M in Tesla Megapack at Wyoming AI Datacenters:** Meta is purchasing approximately $200 million worth of Tesla Megapack batteries to help power AI datacenters in Wyoming. Sawyer frames this as further evidence of Tesla Energy becoming critical infrastructure for the AI buildout — a theme that has been growing as hyperscalers seek large-scale grid storage solutions. This is a significant named customer win for Tesla Energy and reinforces the commercial demand signal for Megapack at scale.

3. **Tesla Expanding into Bulgaria:** Tesla filed to launch vehicle sales and service in Bulgaria for the first time last month, and has now posted multiple new job listings in the country ahead of the launch. While Bulgaria is a smaller market, it reflects continued geographic expansion of Tesla's direct-sales model in Europe.

4. **Mercedes Plans Urban Autonomous Driving Launch in Germany by End of 2026:** Mercedes announced plans to roll out an urban point-to-point automated driving system in selected German cities by end of 2026, positioning itself as one of the first carmakers to offer such technology in Germany. This is a competitive development worth monitoring, though Tesla's FSD approach and scale differ materially from Mercedes' city-limited, likely ODD-constrained system.

## Signal Strength: **Bullish** — The combination of Tesla Energy securing a major ~$200M AI infrastructure contract with Meta, FSD Europe transitioning to a recurring subscription model, and continued geographic expansion into new markets collectively reinforces multiple legs of the Tesla growth thesis.
