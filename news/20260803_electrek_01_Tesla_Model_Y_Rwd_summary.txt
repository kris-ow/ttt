Electrek's Bastien Theron reviewed the 2026 Tesla Model Y RWD (Standard) — the Canadian LFP version — over roughly 550 km, giving it an overall score of 80.2/100. He found the technology and efficiency strong but the driving dynamics disappointing, calling it "the least Tesla-like Tesla" he's driven.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Range, efficiency, and charging:** The Canadian LFP-equipped Model Y RWD is rated for 463 km (288 miles) at 136.4 Wh/km, and the reviewer matched this almost exactly over his test drive; on back roads only he achieved 8.0 km/kWh. A 10-80% Supercharger session took 24 minutes (peak charging capped at 175kW/2.77C), and the LFP pack supports V2X discharge above 12kW (he reached 14kW) without battery-degradation concerns, versus needing to limit his own 2018 Model 3 to under 4kW.
2. **US vs. Canada battery/motor differences:** US-built units use a smaller 69.5kWh NCA pack (96S40P) versus the Premium's ~80kWh (96S46P); non-US units (Shanghai/Berlin) get a ~63kWh LFP pack. Both share a new, more efficient rear-motor design. Acceleration is the weak point at 7.2s 0-100km/h in Canada (6.8s 0-60mph in the US), the slowest Tesla yet; the reviewer suspects this is software-limited to differentiate from the Premium trim.
3. **Pricing and trim cuts:** Priced at $39,990 in the US ($6,000 below Premium) and $49,990 CAD in Canada ($15,000 below Premium), the Standard trim removes a long list of features versus Premium: smaller wheels, non-adaptive headlights, passive suspension, a 7-speaker sound system (vs. 15-speaker), reduced seat heating/no ventilation, no rear touchscreen or armrest, an opaque-covered (rather than see-through) panoramic roof, a steel instead of aluminum hood, no frunk seal, and no towing package. Charging power drops to 175kW peak from 250kW on Premium.
4. **Cabin, storage, and UI:** Storage is a strength (deep frunk, subfloor trunk bin, open center console); cabin noise is low at 66dB but wind noise creeps in due to lack of double-pane glass, and the closed roof feels cramped. The reviewer called the UI "one of the best" tested despite no driver display, but criticized the sound system as muffled and bass-heavy, and disliked the blended regen/hydraulic brake-by-wire pedal feel.
5. **Autonomous Driving — FSD (Supervised):** The reviewer called FSD (Supervised) "the best point-to-point Level 2 system available in North America," working well roughly 98% of the time but with two flagged issues: attempting to use disabled/forbidden parking spots, and inconsistent speed-limit sign recognition. It requires a $99/month subscription with no purchase option, and Autosteer/lane-centering is no longer offered outside of that subscription — a change the reviewer dislikes, since it removes what was previously "Autopilot" for non-subscribers.
6. **Safety:** NHTSA gave the 2026 Model Y a five-star overall rating with five stars in every category, and the reviewer states it is the first vehicle to pass NHTSA's newly updated ADAS testing (pedestrian AEB, lane-keeping, blind-spot warning/intervention). IIHS results aren't yet published; Euro NCAP had rated the refreshed 2025 Model Y Best in Class.

## Editorial Stance
The reviewer initially was skeptical of the Standard trim as a departure from Tesla's promised low-cost EV, but after testing concludes it's a compelling entry point for tech-focused buyers who value software/efficiency over driving dynamics; he criticizes the opaque roof cover, steel hood, unsealed frunk, brake-pedal feel, and mandatory FSD subscription model, and argues the vehicle didn't hit the sub-$35,000 US / $42,000 CAD price point needed to be a "game changer" for mass EV adoption.
