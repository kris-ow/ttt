Tesla briefed JPMorgan analysts at its Fremont factory on FSD, Cybercab, and Optimus timelines; the article, written skeptically, compares these claims against Tesla's history of missed prior promises.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v15 pitched as a "step-change":** Tesla told JPMorgan that FSD v15 is built on seven "core technologies," about 40% of which are already running in the Austin robotaxi fleet, with early feedback described as "encouraging." The article notes FSD v14 shipped in October 2025 and Musk called v14.3 the "last piece of the puzzle" in April, framing v15 as at least the third consecutive version pitched as the breakthrough.
2. **HW4/AI4 claimed sufficient for unsupervised driving:** Tesla said its current HW4 (AI4) computer can run v15 and unsupervised FSD, while simultaneously rolling out AI4.5, a chip with roughly 10% more compute and about twice the memory. The article notes Tesla made the same "sufficient hardware" claim about HW3 for years before reversing it — HW3 cars now get a reduced FSD v14 "Lite," amid reported rising HW3 computer failures. Tesla's next-gen AI5 chip has been delayed to mid-2027, and Cybercab is now said to launch on AI4/AI4.5.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab scaling guidance:** Tesla told JPMorgan it is confident it can scale Cybercab in the near term, is deliberately limiting further Model Y robotaxi conversions, is using an "unboxed" manufacturing process, and is targeting a long-run cost around $0.30 per mile, with fleet expansion accelerating from late 2026 into early 2027, contingent on safety levels. The article contrasts this with Tesla's actual Austin driverless service still running on a small number of cars over a year after launch, and Tesla's disclosed total of 380,000 unsupervised miles since launch, versus Waymo's over 200 million miles.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Gen 3 sales timeline:** Tesla said Optimus Gen 3's design is finalized, its supply chain is "essentially locked in," and a production line is being installed in Fremont's former Model S/X facility. External commercial sales could start "as early as" H2 2027, with long-term capacity ambitions of about 1 million units at Fremont and 10 million in Texas. The article notes Tesla has repeatedly delayed the Gen 3 reveal, and that Musk previously acknowledged no Optimus robots are doing useful work at Tesla after earlier claiming otherwise; Musk had also previously claimed Tesla would have 5,000 Optimus units last year, versus what the article describes as only a few robots currently in testing.

## Financials
1. **Analyst context:** JPMorgan replaced longtime Tesla bear Ryan Brinkman with Rajat Gupta, who upgraded Tesla to Neutral with a price target around $445; Gupta previously made a bullish call on Carvana. The article characterizes the note as largely relaying Tesla's Fremont guidance without significant pushback.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues the note's value is in revealing what Tesla tells investors privately, not in JPMorgan's rating, and contends all four claims (FSD v15, HW4 capability, Cybercab scaling, Optimus 2027 sales) echo prior Tesla promises that were later missed or walked back. He states he will treat unsupervised FSD on HW4 as real only when a customer car performs it with Tesla accepting liability.
