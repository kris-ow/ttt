This is a Monday news roundup from futureaza covering a new minor Tesla accessory, Nvidia's open-sourcing of an Alpamayo reasoning model for autonomous vehicles, Waymo's tariff-laden Zeekr robotaxi import, a comparison of Rivian's hands-free driving to Tesla FSD, GM's mixed signals on batteries and China strategy, several battery/energy storage developments, and a rumored Tesla flying Roadster demo. The host's throughline is skepticism toward competitors' autonomy claims and confidence that Tesla's cost and data advantages will persist.

## Robotaxi
1. **Waymo's Zeekr import called an expensive misstep:** Waymo has imported over 3,200 Zeekr vehicles for its U.S. robotaxi fleet despite a steep tariff; the host estimates that even optimistically priced units (~$30,000) would reach roughly $60,000 after a 100% tariff and closer to $100,000 once shipping/logistics and upfit costs are included — versus Tesla's Cybercab, which he expects to launch at roughly half that price and fall further with scale. He argues 3,200 units isn't enough volume to secure meaningful purchasing power.
2. **Tesla flying Roadster demo rumored:** Citing a single-source report the host does not fully endorse, a demo of a flying Roadster configuration is rumored, with observers required to stand hundreds of yards away for safety, remote operation (no driver inside), and no street-legal design — consistent with a similar rumor the host says he heard roughly a year ago.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Rivian Autonomy Plus vs. Tesla FSD comparison critiqued:** A CNBC review comparing Tesla FSD to Rivian's Autonomy Plus hands-free system credits Rivian with surpassing legacy competitors like GM's Super Cruise and having a "giant leap forward," with point-to-point driving expected later this year (2026) and a $50/month price. The host dismisses the coverage as lacking critical analysis, calling Rivian's current system merely competent highway ADAS (a "zero to one" step) that hasn't tackled the harder problems of city streets, intersections, and pedestrians — the core of the self-driving challenge.
2. **Host reports Rivian declined to give him an autonomy demo:** The host says he sought a demo/test drive of Rivian's autonomy tech after being told Rivian's autonomy day had no space for him, and later was told Rivian had "stopped doing demos" — which he says he knew to be untrue from community contacts. He infers Rivian is not ready to show its autonomy system to independent analysts and expresses no confidence in Rivian's autonomous driving approach, also noting Rivian says LiDAR will be necessary but isn't equipping it yet, and that a Rivian salesperson could not answer battery chemistry or sourcing questions.
3. **Alpamayo reasoning model released for autonomous vehicles:** Nvidia opened Alpamayo 2 Super, a reasoning model for robotaxis/self-driving cars, for commercial use, with the broader model family surpassing 500,000 downloads via a Linux Foundation-hosted open-source release that lets automakers fine-tune and sell derivative products. The host argues the model would take three to five years to actually deploy in cars if it were production-ready today (which he says it is not), and that open-sourcing the model doesn't confer much advantage since companies won't share the proprietary training data needed to make it useful.

## Market & Competition
1. **GM sends conflicting signals on batteries and China strategy:** Samsung SDI is buying out GM's roughly 50% stake in their Indiana battery joint venture, which GM attributes to weaker-than-expected EV demand; simultaneously, GM/LG plan to restart battery cell production at an Ohio plant, and GM extended a roughly 30-year joint venture in China (SAIC) by another 20 years despite geopolitical tensions. Meanwhile Chevy is exiting China after over two decades of sales, with Chevy's China retail sales reportedly down 99%, though GM says it will keep Chinese plants running to export vehicles (not to the U.S.). The host calls GM "floundering" given the inconsistency.
2. **China EV sales decline attributed to fading incentives, not price wars:** Chinese EV deliveries fell 3.9% in July from a year earlier, the seventh straight month of decline, which reporting attributed to fading incentives and a "persistent price war." The host disputes the price-war framing, arguing lower prices should support volume, not cut it, and that most Chinese EV makers remain gross-margin positive even as they trim margins to expand sales.
3. **Chery buys Nissan plant in Africa; global manufacturing shifting to lower-cost regions:** Chinese automaker Chery has purchased a Nissan plant in Africa as EV production shifts to new, cheaper-labor markets to avoid tariffs and shipping costs; the host cites his own research on Ethiopia (where he'll be traveling in December) noting plans there for dozens of factories to assemble EVs domestically.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla's minor "new product" is an under-seat storage tray:** The video's teased "newest product" is a roughly $50 under-seat hidden storage tray, which the host acknowledges has drawn some criticism (comparisons to cheaper aftermarket alternatives) but he defends Tesla's choice not to include it standard, since it adds parts to warranty.
2. **BYD/Denza fast-charging and low-cost EVs highlighted as competitive threats:** BYD's Denza Z9 GT, using a new "flash" charging blade battery, reportedly charged to 97% in about 12 minutes and performs well in cold conditions (down to about -22°F/-30°C context implied); separately, an entry-level BYD SUV with the same flash battery launched in China at roughly $21,000, which the host estimates could cost $35,000–$40,000 if exported with tariffs and shipping — framing this as the real competition for U.S. automakers in non-U.S., non-China markets.

## Energy
1. **New utility-scale battery storage projects continue rolling out:** A new large Tesla Megapack-scale energy storage facility is being built in Australia, and Europe's largest battery — a 500 MW / 1 GWh storage system — has come online in Scotland at a former coal mine site.
2. **Sodium-ion batteries improving but with tradeoffs:** Sodium-ion battery prototypes are approaching 200 Wh/kg, and a sodium-ion electric mining truck with a 676 kWh battery was delivered in China. The host cautions that despite better cold-weather performance, sodium's larger ion size limits volumetric energy density versus lithium-ion, meaning less range/space efficiency even if gravimetric density is matched, and that lithium-ion's cold-weather performance may be underrated by comparison.
3. **Tesla's Project Crystal Sun solar facility progressing slowly:** Tesla's ~$10 billion solar cell and module manufacturing facility (Project Crystal Sun) in Fort Bend County, Texas has a confirmed location and initial work underway, but the host expects it will likely take up to five years to materialize given construction and tooling timelines; he notes Tesla could sell output to itself (superchargers, Giga facilities) if third-party demand is insufficient, similar to SpaceX's internal launch use.
4. **California EV rebate expands to three brands:** California's instant EV rebate is now available for Tesla, Hyundai, and Lucid (with more brands expected), limited to first-time EV buyers only.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX AI compute revenue could surpass rest of business by September:** The host cites a claim that SpaceX's AI-related revenue may eclipse the rest of SpaceX's business by September (2026), attributing this to strong monthly compute-lease income and describing roughly a 12-month payback period on compute infrastructure investment.
