Tesla filed two new USPTO trademark applications on February 3 for the Roadster, including one showing what appears to be an updated vehicle silhouette.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New trademark filings:** Tesla filed a stylized "ROADSTER" wordmark trademark and a separate filing depicting a vehicle silhouette described in the filing as "a triangle design consisting of three flowing, curved lines." Both were filed on an "intent to use" basis, meaning Tesla has not yet commercially used the logos but is securing nationwide priority to block others from registering similar marks before a public announcement, per trademark attorney Josh Gerben.

2. **Long history of delays:** The Roadster was unveiled in November 2017 with production promised for 2020; the date has since slipped multiple times (2022, 2023, 2024, and most recently 2025). In November 2025, Tesla pushed the Roadster demo to April 1, 2026, with production now expected in 2027 or 2028. Musk joked the April Fools' Day demo date gives him "some deniability" if things don't go as planned.

3. **Flying/SpaceX package claims:** Musk has repeatedly claimed the Roadster will be able to "fly" via a "SpaceX package" with cold-air thrusters and has suggested it could hover and "won't really be a car." In a January interview, Musk said, "If safety is your number one goal, don't buy the Roadster," calling it "the best of the last of the human-driven cars."

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is skeptical, noting trademark filings are weak indicators of an imminent product launch and that, given the Roadster's history of broken promises, "we'll believe it when we see it." He also notes the design shown is nearly a decade old and Tesla's final design is not guaranteed to match it.
