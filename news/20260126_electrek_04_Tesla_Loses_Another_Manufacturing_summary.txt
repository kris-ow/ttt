Electrek reports that Benjamin Bate, Tesla's Director of Vehicle Operations and Engineering at Fremont, has left after over 8 years to become Plant Manager at Chemelex, a Redwood City-based electric thermal and sensing solutions company — the latest in a string of senior manufacturing departures.

## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Bate's departure and career at Tesla:** Bate joined Tesla in January 2018 as Maintenance and Controls Manager for paint operations, rising through roles including Senior Manufacturing Manager – Paint Operations, Senior Manager – General Assembly Operations, Director of Manufacturing for Model 3 and Model Y (Dec 2020–Aug 2023), and finally Director of Vehicle Operations and Engineering (Aug 2023–Jan 2026). Before Tesla he worked nearly two years at Ford as a Manufacturing Engineering Manager in Paint operations. He oversaw production of Tesla's two best-selling vehicles at its most important factory for nearly three years before moving to an industrial equipment company outside EV manufacturing.

2. **Pattern of senior departures:** The article lists other recent high-profile exits: Drew Baglino (18-year veteran, powertrain/energy engineering lead, left April 2024), Model Y program manager Emmanuel Lamacchia and Cybertruck program head Siddhant Awasthi (both announced departures the same day in November 2025), and Omead Afshar (oversaw North America/Europe sales and manufacturing, left mid-2025). The article also states Tesla has lost its long-time head of software David Lau, two top car designers, its top crash safety architect, its head of North American sales, and the director behind Tesla Energy products, plus unspecified numbers of directors, senior managers, and engineers.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Musk has shifted Tesla's focus to robotaxis and Optimus at the expense of vehicle development, making manufacturing leadership roles less attractive even though factory cash flow funds those AI efforts; he notes Tesla has reportedly favored hiring cheaper, inexperienced college graduates over replacing departing veterans, citing prior Electrek reporting on employee morale problems. The article also relays an unnamed source's view that top engineers are leaving partly out of boredom, since Tesla's focus on autonomy/robots means the cars themselves get little innovation attention. A reader comment (not the author's view) argues Tesla has given up on automobiles, calls the Cybertruck a failure citing multiple recalls, and contends Tesla lags competitors on solid-state battery development.
