The video argues that FSD version 15 — a full architectural overhaul with roughly 10x the parameters of the current v14 model — is the key remaining catalyst before Tesla scales its robotaxi fleet, drawing on Elon Musk's and Ashok Elluswamy's public statements, Ashok's recent social media posts, and community-tracked fleet data to build a bull case around fleet expansion timing.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **What V15 actually is:** V15 is described as Tesla's "large model" (roughly 10 billion parameters) versus the current v14 "small model" (roughly 1 billion parameters), running only on AI4 hardware (Hardware 3 cars remain on a lighter version of v14). Elon Musk first announced the 10x-parameter model on August 6 of the prior year, targeting an end-of-next-month release that did not ship; Musk later clarified (April 9 this year) that v14's rapid small-model progress delayed the large model, which became V15. Ashok Elluswamy has said V15 is built from roughly seven major improvement tracks developed in parallel, though Tesla has not disclosed what those tracks are.
2. **Earnings call disclosures on V15 timing:** On the Q1 call (April 22), Elon Musk said V15 would arrive "hopefully by the end of this year but certainly by early next year" and would take safety "to another level," while indicating unsupervised FSD scaling would wait until v14.3 was finished, validated, and released before going to large scale. On the Q2 call (July 22), Ashok Elluswamy said the robotaxi fleet was already running early V15 builds — a flip from the old pattern where customer cars got new software first — with about 40% of the seven planned improvement tracks merged, and used the phrase "ridiculously safe" to describe the finished version's safety target. He also disclosed over 380,000 miles of unsupervised robotaxi driving across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents as of that call.
3. **Ashok's September posts point to 24/7 operation:** Responding to a fan on September 4, Ashok Elluswamy said the "next tech to emerge on the V15 plan" — expected "next month or so" (i.e., around October) — would enable round-the-clock robotaxi service, up from the current roughly 6 a.m.–10 p.m. operating window. The host frames this as a roughly 50% increase in available operating hours at no added vehicle cost, though notes it hinges on Ashok's stated timeline holding. Separately, on September 11, Ashok replied to a driver's account of FSD avoiding a collision by saying the next V15 upgrade brings earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction time, and better collision avoidance.
4. **Fleet mile growth math:** Ashok cited 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles on the July 22 Q2 call and, at the Cybercab event on September 3, said the fleet had passed 1 million unsupervised miles — a jump the host calculates as about 17% compounded weekly growth over roughly six weeks (Tesla's own stated phrasing is "more than 10% a week"). The host's own projection that this could rise to 20–25%+ weekly growth once V15 fully ships is explicitly his own speculative estimate, not a Tesla-stated figure.
5. **Destination/parking memory as an underrated V15 feature:** Elon Musk stated (replying to a user on June 17) that upcoming FSD releases will let the car remember individual parking and drop-off preferences at home, office, school, etc., and said destination parking is "by far the biggest reason" people intervene with FSD today, while critical safety interventions are "extremely rare." In a separate reply (July 18) to a complaint about FSD exiting carpool lanes, Musk said the car will learn and match individual driving/route preferences. The host notes Musk did not explicitly attach this feature to V15 by version number, and that v14 already lets users manually select and save preferred drop-off/parking options per destination — Musk is describing the car learning these automatically. The host argues this matters more for robotaxi fleets, where one car learning an optimal pickup spot (e.g., a hotel side entrance) could propagate to the whole fleet, versus Waymo's manual, hand-mapped approach.

## Robotaxi
1. **City-by-city fleet snapshot (community tracker, pulled midweek):** Per the community Robotaxi tracker cited by the host: Austin ~198 cars (no safety driver, ~168 seen in last 30 days), Dallas 50, Houston 32, Tampa 14, Miami 4, Orlando 3, and roughly 700 cars in the Bay Area (all with safety drivers due to California law). The host notes these tracker figures differ from RobotaxiTracker.com's own most recently published canonical breakdown and are described as the host's own pull from the tracker site rather than the canonical snapshot. Waymo is cited (attributed to the host) as having about 4,000 cars, versus Tesla's driverless fleet of roughly 300 across all cities.
2. **Cybercab-specific and Texas DMV registration figures:** The tracker showed 49 Cybercabs built in Texas, with 42 seen in service, all in Austin. Separately, Texas DMV autonomous-driving registrations for Tesla Robotaxi LLC stood at 439 vehicles (390 Model Ys plus 49 Cybercabs), up 258 in the prior 30 days but flat for the most recent 7 days — a pattern the host interprets (his own inference, not Tesla-confirmed) as the initial launch batch being in place while the next batch awaits a further V15 software milestone.
3. **Investor watch list for coming months:** The host lays out five things to monitor: (1) whether 24/7 robotaxi hours actually arrive in October as Ashok indicated; (2) whether the flat Texas DMV registration count (439) starts climbing again; (3) whether Florida cities (Miami, Orlando, Tampa) scale from single digits to dozens of cars; (4) whether Ashok reports meaningfully more than 40% of V15's improvement tracks merged on the Q3 earnings call (late October); and (5) the actual customer-facing V15 rollout, expected by Elon's stated window of end of this year to early next year.
