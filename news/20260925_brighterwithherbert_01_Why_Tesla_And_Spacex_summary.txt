A panel of Tesla/SpaceX bulls discusses a busy week across both companies: robotaxi fleet registration numbers, a new Grokbot agent integration in Tesla vehicles, a possible leak of Optimus Gen 3, China EV manufacturing geopolitics, the upcoming Tesla Semi event, and SpaceX's expanding role in space-based AI compute and satellite iteration. The throughline is that both companies appear to be accelerating simultaneously across autonomy, AI agents, humanoid robotics, and orbital infrastructure.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas registrations hit 420 Model Y / 69 Cybercab:** Tesla itself tweeted that Texas now has 420 Model Y and 69 Cybercab registrations (panelists note these are Elon-associated meme numbers), with one panelist citing 28-29 vehicles added in a single day recently. One panelist estimated total registered Robotaxi fleet vehicles (including Florida, Dallas, Houston) may be around 500.
2. **Growth-rate math implies fleet must scale fast:** A panelist calculated that sustaining the reported ~17% week-over-week paid autonomous miles growth requires roughly 200–250 vehicles now, ~4,000 by around early February, ~8,000 by March, and 15,000–18,000 by May — implying either a slowdown or a major near-term fleet expansion, possibly gated by an FSD software version.
3. **Model Y-to-Cybercab ratio expected to flip:** Panelists expect the current split (more Model Ys than Cybercabs in service) to reverse as Tesla works through initial reliability/wear testing on Cybercab units before ramping production and mix.
4. **Disputed low-end fleet estimates:** The panel is skeptical of claims that only a small number of Cybercabs are in active service, arguing vehicle registrations are the only objective data point and that community tracking claims (either too low or too high) lack verifiable methodology.
5. **Leaked Cybercab consumer software (Australia):** A YouTuber in Australia reportedly showed what may be consumer-facing infotainment software for a privately-owned Cybercab, including in-car map navigation not present in the robotaxi fleet software shown at Tesla's Cybercab event. Panelists were skeptical this represents an official 2026 consumer launch and suggested it may have been an unintended software leak during a demo unit's international shipment.
6. **Possible regional fleet-operator model floated:** One panelist speculated (as personal opinion, not confirmed) that Tesla may eventually license regional exclusivity or a bidding system to third-party operators for Cybercab fleets in smaller markets, while retaining direct control in hub cities like Austin, Dallas, and Houston.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD approval math for Europe:** A panelist stated that EU-wide FSD approval requires 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of the population, and cannot pass without at least one of Germany, France, or Italy on board — making Elon Musk's recent outreach to French and German officials strategically significant. Germany has scheduled a vote for October 6th and is reportedly reviewing Denmark's regulatory approach.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Possible Optimus Gen 3 image leak via Android app:** Images labeled "Gen 3" were found referenced in Tesla's Android app code, showing a more human-like body (smoother shoulders, hips, elbows) than the current Gen 2.5 design. Panelists were skeptical of why such images would appear in-app this early and could not confirm authenticity.
2. **Chinese suppliers reportedly certified for Optimus production:** The host said increasing reports suggest Chinese suppliers have been certified to produce up to 5,000 Optimus units, though this is described as still emerging/unconfirmed information.
3. **Giga Berlin training program reportedly underway:** An unverified report describes an Optimus prototype (appearing to be Gen 2.5) at Giga Berlin behind frosted glass with a "work in progress" sign; factory management reportedly told the source that starting in mid-August, selected Giga Berlin employees would wear camera-equipped backpacks/helmets to record movements for Optimus training data.
4. **Panel expects an Optimus update at Q3 earnings:** With Tesla having previously said Optimus production would begin around July/August, panelists expect Tesla to provide a status update at its earnings call in roughly a month (late October), and hope analysts ask directly about production/training progress.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Skepticism over Chinese EV manufacturing in the US:** Following a Trump–Xi meeting, reports suggested Trump may be considering allowing Chinese EV makers to build factories in the US. Panelists argued the economics don't work given the gap between Chinese factory labor costs (roughly $4/hour) and US union labor ($55–70/hour) across the full tier 1–5 supply chain, plus longer Chinese supplier payment terms (120–180 days); they expect this not to materialize under the current administration.
2. **BYD's overseas expansion struggling:** A panelist's research found BYD's planned factories in Hungary (delayed by a change in government), Turkey ($1B/150,000-unit plant abandoned before groundbreaking), Mexico (blocked over anticipated US tariffs), and Malaysia (cancelled) have all stalled or failed; smaller plants continue in Pakistan (~25,000 units/year) and existing operations run in Thailand, Brazil (~100,000 units/year, targeting 300,000+), Uzbekistan, Cambodia, and a growing India presence. The panelist argues BYD's overseas profitability is made abroad, not in China, and manufacturing economics only work where local content rules force in-country production (e.g., Brazil).
3. **Speculation linking China trade talk to SpaceX–Tesla merger and Giga Shanghai:** A panelist floated speculation (explicitly unconfirmed) that China could use Giga Shanghai as a bargaining chip tied to a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger, and referenced an earlier Wall Street Journal report (which Musk called "fake news") about a possible sale of Tesla Shanghai — the panelist believes elements beyond the sale claim may still be under discussion in Europe and China, and personally predicts a merger matters more to Musk than trade concessions.
4. **Tesla Semi event framed as a sales/commercial milestone:** Panelists expected the (same-day) Tesla Semi event to function as a customer and sales celebration — following a similar pattern to the Cybercab launch — with a stronger focus on cost/economics messaging aimed at commercial fleet buyers (e.g., UPS/FedEx-type customers) than prior Tesla product reveals, given the ~4 years since the original Semi event.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Google TPUs to launch to orbit October 1, per Sundar Pichai post:** A panelist cited Google CEO Sundar Pichai posting on X that Google is sending TPUs to space via SpaceX, scheduled for October 1st — cited as evidence that space-based data center demand is expanding beyond SpaceX's own capacity plans to include third parties (also referencing Nvidia testing its data-center/chip design with SpaceX).
2. **Panel calls this the week's most material development for the combined thesis:** Multiple panelists pointed to this Google/SpaceX space-data-center news as the most consequential update of the week for the long-term SpaceX (and potential merged SpaceX-Tesla) thesis.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Elon Musk floated merging Starlink and Star Mind satellite designs:** Musk reportedly suggested Gen 3 Starlink satellites might be combined with "Star Mind" into an all-purpose satellite. One panelist expressed skepticism this is literally feasible given Starlink's low-Earth-orbit placement versus Star Mind's sun-synchronous orbit and differing power needs, but views it as evidence of rapid iterative satellite design improvements.
