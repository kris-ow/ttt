## Autonomous Driving

- **Belgium FSD supervised approval imminent:** Flemish mobility minister Annick De Ridder stated FSD (Supervised) testing on public roads in Flanders could begin "within days" as regulators review Tesla's documentation. This is the most concrete European regulatory FSD milestone to date.
- **FSD v15 flagged as gating event for scale launch:** Per the brighterwithherbert Cybercab rollout analysis, Tesla has stated it is waiting for FSD version 15 before launching at "super scale." A version bump to 15 is the key software signal to monitor.
- **Cortex 2 now online:** Per Loomis, Cortex 2 has begun running training workloads as disclosed in Tesla's Q1 deck — over 130,000 H100 equivalents, up from Cortex 1's 100,000+, though still in early ramp.
- **10B FSD miles mostly on Model Y/3 — Cybercab restarts form-factor curve:** Tesla's 10 billion supervised FSD miles were accumulated almost entirely on Model Y and Model 3, meaning Cybercab requires its own platform-specific data accumulation before unsupervised deployment — not from zero, but not from 10 billion miles.
- **AI4 confirmed sufficient for unsupervised Cybercab operation:** Bhakdi states Elon was "crystal clear" on the Q1 earnings call that AI5 is not required for fully unsupervised operation; AI5 will be an upgrade, not a prerequisite.

## Robotaxi

- **Revised Cybercab deployment timeline: July 2026:** Loomis formally updates his prior September 15th prediction to July 2026, reasoning Tesla needs ~three months from production start to validate the unboxed manufacturing method, safety tolerances, and Etherloop reliability. He notes Tesla has no urgency to rush given Model Y is functioning at current small scale.
- **Cybercab FSD porting simpler than Cybertruck:** Loomis corrects a prior implicit assumption — the Cybertruck's porting challenges (four-wheel steering, variable steering ratio, non-standard camera placement) do not apply to the Cybercab, which has no rear-wheel steering, no traditional steering wheel, front-wheel drive, steer-by-wire, and camera placement close to the Model 3.
- **Cybercab production: 20–40/day per Tegtmeyer drone footage; 70+ units in outbound lot:** Production rate estimates diverge slightly by source — brighterwithherbert cites 20–40 units/day; Loomis estimates 5–15/day — but both note a single-day observation of 70+ Cybercabs in the Giga Texas outbound lot, described as the largest single gathering observed to date. No Cybercabs have entered live passenger service; all 39 unsupervised vehicles in service are Model Ys.
- **Proposed data accumulation target before Cybercab passenger service:** ~50–100 million miles over two to three months on ~1,000 Cybercab units, with Tesla employees driving. Brand-protection rationale explicitly frames the Cruise 2023 pedestrian-dragging incident as the institutional cautionary tale.
- **Austin service struggles: 50% of rides have 15+ minute waits:** Per a Reuters-cited Austin officials slideshow and a three-week April 2026 tracking study, wait times exceeded 15 minutes roughly half the time, exceeded 25 minutes more than 25% of the time, and no cars were available 27% of the time. Tesla is operating ~50 vehicles in Austin vs. 250+ Waymo vehicles. Dallas service also drew criticism for navigation errors, long waits, and destination inaccuracies.
- **33 US cities where Tesla is actively hiring for robotaxi roles:** Job posting analysis on X identifies 33 cities with open Tesla autonomy operations roles — treated by brighterwithherbert as a more reliable forward signal than public statements. At 100 vehicles per city, that implies 3,300 vehicles from expansion markets alone.
- **End-of-year fleet scenarios: 3,300 (floor) to 15,000 (bull case):** Production math at ~30 Cybercabs/day over 150 days yields ~4,500 units; three Texas cities at ~1,100 each plus 5–7 expansion cities at 500–1,500 yields a combined range of ~6,000–12,000 optimistically, with a conservative floor of ~3,300.
- **Pony AI break-even benchmark: 23 rides/vehicle/day:** Pony AI announced unit economics break-even in two Tier 1 Chinese cities at that ride rate. Loomis flags this as a useful benchmark for when Tesla begins reporting ride-level data.
- **Waymo expanding to Miami, targeting 1,400+ sq miles across 11 cities:** Waymo announced Miami expansion with Austin, Atlanta, Houston, and Bay Area next in pipeline.

- **Optimus 2026 supply chain: Chinese suppliers committed to 70,000-unit readiness:** Bhakdi references a December 2025 leak from "Tesla Ming" — a well-sourced Chinese industry source — indicating Chinese Optimus suppliers prepared supply chain capacity for 70,000 units in 2026. Bhakdi interprets this as confirmation Optimus is entering mass production this year; the capital expenditure commitment by suppliers is cited as the credibility signal.

## Energy

- **BYD surpassed Tesla as world's top BESS integrator in 2025:** BYD shipped over 60 GWh of energy storage globally in 2025 (13% market share) vs. Tesla's 46.7 GWh (10%), per Benchmark Mineral Intelligence. Tesla's deployment grew 49% year-over-year but was not enough to hold the top position it had held in 2023 and 2024.
- **Global BESS market nearly doubled:** Global installations hit ~315 GWh in 2025 (+51% YoY); cell shipments for stationary storage exceeded 600 GWh. China alone installed 65 GWh in December 2025 — more than the entire US deployed in the full year. Eight of the top 10 integrators are Chinese.
- **BYD HaoHan capacity advantage is structural:** BYD's HaoHan system offers a 14.5 MWh standard configuration — nearly 3x Tesla's Megapack 3 at 5 MWh per unit. BYD has a 12.5 GWh HaoHan project underway in Saudi Arabia. Electrek's Fred Lambert flags Tesla's reliance on CATL and BYD for Megapack cells as a structural vulnerability the Houston Megafactory (targeting 50 GWh by late 2026) and the $4.3B LG Energy Solution deal (LFP cells starting August 2027) must address.
- **Giga Berlin 4680 expansion: $250M for 18 GWh annual capacity:** Tesla is investing ~$250 million to more than double 4680 production capacity at Giga Berlin to 18 GWh annually, creating ~1,500 jobs. At that output, Loomis estimates the factory could supply cells for ~240,000 vehicles per year — potentially covering nearly all of Giga Berlin's output in-house.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Giga Shanghai production record through April 2026:** Through-April 2026 cumulative production at Giga Shanghai is at its highest level ever, ahead of both 2024 and early 2025 (which was impacted by the Model Y/3 changeover). This precedes broader FSD approval in China or the EU.
- **Tesla Semi battery capacities officially confirmed:** Standard range: 548 kWh; long range: 822 kWh. European Semi launch is 2027 at earliest, standard range only, with Giga Berlin the speculated manufacturing site.
- **California HVIP incentives make Semi cost-competitive with diesel:** HVIP funding of $120,000–$330,000 per truck brings the net Semi cost to approximately $170,000 at the minimum incentive — described as roughly price parity with a comparable diesel. Oregon and Washington also have incentive programs active.
- **Tesla Semi payload on par with diesel despite heavier battery:** EV weight exemption allows 82,000 lb GVW vs. 80,000 lb for conventional trucks; transmission-free design and reduced chassis reinforcement limit weight penalty to ~2,000 lbs, preserving ~45,000 lb cargo capacity.
- **Covenant Logistics running 3-week Tesla Semi 500-mile variant demo:** Per Matt Mullen on LinkedIn, Covenant Logistics is demoing the 500-mile Semi on a 200-mile round trip, with more details forthcoming.
