## Electric Vehicles

- Tesla unveiled the next-gen Roadster at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas site, with three trims: Performance (sub-1.6s 0-60, $50k deposit), Thrust (sub-0.9s 0-60, $250k deposit), and Rocket (invite-only, sub-0.59s 0-60, 230+ mph, not street-legal). A live 0-60 run read 0.81s, later revised by Tesla to 0.67s.
- The Rocket and Thrust trims use SpaceX-derived cold-gas thrusters built on COPV tank technology holding up to 10,000 PSI; the Performance trim instead relies on suction fans and active aero generating roughly 2g of downforce — technology Musk said is F1-banned. Rear wing and track-mode actuators share components with Optimus.
- All trims use a new aluminum-can 4680 cell targeting 300+ mile range, 800V architecture, and 10-85% charging in ~10 minutes (500+ kW at V4 Superchargers per Lars Moravy). Production targeted for 2028, with only 4-5 prototypes built so far; the Roadster will be built at a new "RFTX" building at Giga Texas.
- Cox Automotive estimates Tesla sold ~99,000 Model Ys in the US in Q3 (37% of the EV market); in China, Model Y was September's best-selling car of any type, lifting Tesla to ~6% China market share. Canada's Model 3 premium import quota is exhausted until March.
- Slovakia became the 9th European country to approve FSD Supervised ("Tesla Assisted Driving"). Jefferies raised its Tesla price target from $350 to $400; RBC maintained $480.
- Giga Berlin's planned expansion building spans ~536,000 sq m, more than double the existing plant, with construction starting in 2027.

## Robotaxi

- Texas Cybercab registrations rose from 45 (Sept 1) to 349, per Sawyer Merritt, plus 420 Model Y robotaxis — 769 total Tesla robotaxis registered in the state. A Texas-wide tracker cited by one host put Waymo at ~1,185 vehicles versus Tesla's 739, though Tesla added ~300 vehicles in the trailing 30 days versus Waymo's ~200.
- NHTSA's incident report through Sept 15 recorded zero accidents or injuries in the first two weeks of paid Cybercab trips; a separate review of nine total robotaxi incidents found all were property-damage-only at 5 mph or less. NHTSA opened an Oct 9 defect petition over alleged school-zone speeding by ~1,000 Model Y/Cybercab robotaxis; Tesla must respond by Oct 30.
- Eric Earley, described as head of the Cybercab project, said progress feels slow but exponential growth is expected in coming months; one host observed Cybercabs added in a single day jumped from under 40 to over 150 around the X Takeover event. Rider reports on drop-off accuracy were mixed.
- Austin service hours expanded to 6am-11pm with shorter waits, automatic trunk opening, and an in-car Grok bot. Tesla is hiring robotaxi market-leader and vehicle-operator roles across 34 U.S. cities in 19 states plus D.C., with no launch dates confirmed.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- Barclays analyst Dan Levy suggested a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement is possible by December, tied to Tesla's shareholder meeting due by Dec 6, with a vote expected 6-12 months after announcement; Barclays' base case is an all-stock deal with Musk retaining voting control. Kalshi reportedly priced merger-within-a-year odds at 60%; Dan Ives put it above 80%; Oppenheimer argued the companies are better off separate. Musk has not commented this week.
- SpaceX will report Q3 earnings Tuesday Oct 20 at 3:30pm Central, a day before Tesla's Oct 21 call — scheduling proximity one host speculated could be more than coincidence.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

- SpaceX confirmed a 444-acre data-center site near Brownsville, Texas (purchased late August, since removed from city limits), with construction underway resembling the "Mini Hard" design used at Colossus 2. Roughly 400 MW of gas turbine power is already installed, believed to now exceed 500 MW, fed by a new dedicated gas pipeline.
- Revenue projections for the site are explicitly speculative: each building could house ~220,000 Nvidia GPUs generating an estimated $22-30 billion/year; a six-building cluster could reach $130-180 billion/year, per analyst Cern Basher's modeling — not confirmed SpaceX guidance.
- Jensen Huang said Nvidia and SpaceX engineers are collaborating on new space server/data-center architecture, with a jointly developed Starlink computer entering mass production next year. Musk said SpaceX's compute target could exceed 10 gigawatts by end of 2027.
- TSMC's CEO reportedly said any Terafab factory should be run by TSMC, which one host says contradicts Musk's Oct 7 statement that Tesla would build and operate the factory itself. Separately, construction continues at two Terafab sites in Texas (near Giga Texas and Grimes County) as a joint Tesla-SpaceX chip venture.

## SpaceX — Starship

- The FAA opened an environmental review to raise Starbase launch cadence from 25/year currently to 100 in a first phase, eventually up to 456/year; public comment runs through November 30.
- Ship 40 returned to Starbase after 75 days at sea for heat-shield inspection. Musk said Flight 15 would occur "a little before Thanksgiving," with SpaceX attempting its first tower catch of the ship on that flight.

## SpaceX — Starlink

- A Ukrainian military unit reported Starshield terminals shutting off above ~160 km/h, disrupting drone operations; Musk responded that Starshield is owned and controlled by the US government, with restrictions set by the US military, not him. Normal operation reportedly resumed by Thursday.
- Jefferies projects Starlink could be on nearly a quarter of the world's aircraft by 2028, up from ~9% today. United reported ~5.4 million Starlink passenger uses in September; Southwest is installing it fleet-wide; 10 million people in Japan have connected via phone through Docomo.

## Energy

- Taiwan's Apollo Power is purchasing 260 Megapacks (~1 GWh). Tesla's chief energy officer Mike Snyder is reportedly stepping down as the company expands its battery business and shifts focus toward AI and robotics, per the Wall Street Journal.
- Q3 Megapack deployment numbers disappointed some analysts versus production capacity (~10 GWh Lathrop, ~10 GWh Shanghai, 10+ GWh Giga Texas); one host attributes the gap to lumpy deployment timing rather than a supply constraint, expecting catch-up in Q4/Q1.
