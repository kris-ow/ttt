The host recounts the 11-day saga of Jensen Huang's forced resignation from Nvidia on November 12 and his return, then argues the resulting selloff and rebound do not change the AI-buildout thesis. His case is that SpaceX and the hyperscalers can fund the buildout from their own cash flows, so the financing stress falls mainly on neoclouds.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia reversal drove a sharp rebound:** The host says Huang was pushed out by the board over alleged affairs, and that Trump publicly urged Nvidia to rehire him. He claims, without citing sources, that Marco Rubio and Scott Bessent likely initiated the move. At the open on the day of his return, Nvidia was up about 28%, adding nearly a trillion dollars in market cap per the host. Nebius and CoreWeave rose about 30%, Micron about 13%, SK Hynix about 17%, and the VIX fell about 25%. The host says this followed a roughly 12% Nasdaq drawdown from the peak. Tesla rose sharply and SpaceX about 10% at the open, and the host says he bought SpaceX calls on Friday.
2. **Three tactical selloff drivers, two now fading:** The host lists Huang's resignation (a real risk, now reversed), the Democrats' midterm wins and anti-AI rhetoric, and the credit/financing crunch. He expects Democratic governors not to undermine their own state economies and Congress to have little power to act against data centers. He welcomes community-protective data center rules and says they would not stop SpaceX from building out 10 gigawatts next year.
3. **Market-structure and tail risks:** He sees cyberattacks and hacking as the main remaining tail risk. He also warns that agentic trading and high correlation could produce violent short-term swings, and he urges caution on margin and short options. He stays bullish on the AI trade, expects possible further near-term dips, and says memory and semiconductor makers are supply-constrained, so a slower capex pace would not hurt their revenue.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Working-capital squeeze hits neoclouds, not SpaceX:** The host cites Musk's November 21 post saying compute is hard to bring online reliably and that next year's cash shortage will be "nuts". Delays of a few months between paying for compute and earning revenue would mean hundreds of billions of dollars in incremental working capital. The host's illustration: $50B of loans for a 1 GW data center at 10% interest costs $5B a year, so a six-month delay costs $2.5B. He says a 50% higher cost would make it $3.75B per six months.
2. **SpaceX as self-funded GPU buyer:** He argues SpaceX can fund many gigawatts from cash flow and investment-grade credit, putting its investable capacity at $200–300B (his estimate). He calls SpaceX the biggest GPU purchaser from Nvidia, citing roughly $100B–150B of cash on hand (SpaceX reported $89.86B in cash and marketable securities for Q3). He says Musk benefits when neoclouds fail because less supply means higher compute spot prices. He expects the buildout to be increasingly funded by SpaceX and hyperscaler cash flows rather than debt, and estimates $200–400B of credit needed over the next couple of years.
