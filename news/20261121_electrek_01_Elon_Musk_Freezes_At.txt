Tesla officially launched in Argentina on Friday with President Javier Milei on stage in Buenos Aires, and Elon Musk joined by video from his plane to welcome the brand’s newest market. It didn’t go well.

Musk got through one line about Tesla before stalling out: “Sorry, I don’t really know what I want to say.” The call ended about two minutes in, without any real pitch for the Model 3 or Model Y that Tesla had just put on sale.

## Two minutes of “I don’t know what to say”

The launch took place at Arenas Studio in Buenos Aires’ La Boca neighborhood. Milei spoke for less than eight minutes, then the host asked the room to welcome “our next guest,” and Musk popped up on the giant screen from what looked like an airplane cabin. Milei burst out laughing and bounced on his feet.

Musk started fine: “Hello, Argentina. Well, here we are. It’s a joy to share Tesla with all of you and to establish ourselves there.”

 
 
 

Then he started glancing off to each side. “Actually, I don’t really know what to say, other than thank you for this welcome to Tesla in Argentina,” he said. He briefly mentioned a charging network with state-owned oil company YPF, thanked the audience again, and then: “Sorry, I don’t really know what I want to say.”

And that was it. The feed cut before he even said goodbye.

Milei tried to save it. Back on stage, he told the crowd that “those who do a lot, talk little,” and called Musk “the Leonardo da Vinci of the 21st century.”

The clip is now spreading fast in Argentina, with one post of the video racking up over 1.1 million views overnight.

## What Tesla is selling in Argentina

Tesla opened online orders on Friday for five versions of the Model 3 and Model Y, all imported from Gigafactory Shanghai. Here are the list prices, VAT included:

- Model 3 RWD: $40,990

- Model Y Premium RWD: $43,990

- Model 3 Premium Long Range: $46,490

- Model Y Premium Long Range: $49,990

- Model 3 Performance: $53,990

Tesla adds about $600 in reservation, destination, and documentation fees on top of that, and first deliveries aren’t expected until March or April 2027. There are no dealers. Sales happen online, with showroom space at the Unicenter and Alcorta Shopping malls in Buenos Aires.

Argentina is Tesla’s fourth direct-sales market in South America after Chile, Colombia, and Uruguay, which launched in July. Tesla is late to the party, though. BYD already leads Argentina’s EV market after Milei’s government cut import taxes on electric vehicles.

## Electrek’s Take

That was awkward. Like, really awkward. 

I am sure Musk has a lot on his mind right now, but selling Tesla vehicles clearly isn’t one of them, because he couldn’t come up with anything to say about it. 

Launching a new market has one job: get people excited to buy the cars. Musk had a sitting president cheering for him on stage, a room full of fans, and every camera in Buenos Aires pointed at him. He couldn’t come up with a single reason to buy a Model Y. 

This isn’t stage fright. Musk has been telling investors for two years that Tesla’s future is robotaxis and Optimus robots, not selling cars. Meanwhile, Tesla’s deliveries fell 8.6% to 1.636 million in 2025, its second annual decline in a row. The guy who is behind the brand doesn’t care about selling these cars anymore, and this clip shows it better than anything I’ve seen.
