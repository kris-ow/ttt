## Autonomous Driving

- **Musk's 10-year AV forecast:** At the Samson International Smart Mobility Summit in Israel, Musk stated: "10 years from now, probably 90% of all distance driven will be driven by the AI in a self-driving car." He added AI already drives over 90% of his personal miles.

- **FSD 14.3 / 14.3.3 early rollout — mixed feedback:** Fewer than ~50 vehicles were on 14.3 as of yesterday per trackers. Musk described it as "a banger" that "nags less," and V14.3.3 delivers over a minute before a nag prompt in some cases. But early reports include phantom braking complaints, a critical disengagement within the first 30 seconds, a parking regression (vehicle backed into a curb), and continued left-lane camping in standard mode.

- **FSD 14.3 UI changes:** The intervention feedback screen now categorizes disengagements as navigation, parking, critical, or other. A new streak feature tracks consecutive time without disengagement.

- **Smart Summon speed raised to 8 mph**, up from 6 mph previously.

- **FSD regulatory expansion — Israel:** Musk said he hopes FSD will receive regulatory approval in Israel "sometime this year," speaking at the Samson summit.

- **Einride counterpoint:** Electrek flagged that Einride is already operating Level 4 autonomous electric semi trucks commercially, including a signed Amazon middle-mile deal — framing Musk's "widespread unsupervised FSD by year-end" pledge as a recurring undelivered promise.

- **Tesla's physical AI as an underappreciated moat:** A Tesla AI executive (identified as "Yantasai") was quoted: "Making seamless real-world agents is a lot harder than digital world agents… It may not be considered a frontier lab, but it is truly frontier." Jeff Lutz, a supply chain executive on Herbert Ong's show, stated he knows of no company doing physical real-world AI at Tesla's scale.

- **Waymo safety failures cited as competitive contrast:** FutureAzA documented a Waymo vehicle running a red light in Dallas (LiDAR is colorblind to signal color), a separate flood-related incident where a software update designed to allow Waymo vehicles to drive through standing water resulted in a vehicle being swept away, and a recurring incident in which 30–50 empty Waymo vehicles jammed a residential neighborhood and became stuck when a resident placed a barricade.

## Robotaxi

- **17 Austin crash reports reviewed:** Tesla released unredacted crash data for its Austin robotaxi fleet. Of 17 documented incidents, the large majority involve other vehicles or objects striking a stationary or lawfully operating Tesla — rear-ends at stop signs and red lights, a bus bike rack scraping a stopped vehicle, a scooter hitting a parked Tesla. Only two incidents were flagged as genuinely worth addressing: a vehicle driving into a chain across a parking lot entrance, and a construction barricade scrape — the latter of which occurred after a remote teleoperator took over control from the ADS.

- **Teleoperators implicated in at least two incidents:** In two of the 17 events, a safety monitor requested remote operator assistance; in at least one case the teleoperator then drove the vehicle up a curb into a metal fence. One minor injury was reported total across all 17 incidents — sustained when another driver rear-ended the Tesla robotaxi.

- **Cybercab spotted with license plate in Houston**, per user Sloak. Texas Transportation Code requires license plates on all vehicles operated on public roads; Dillon Loomis framed it as a required but not conclusive step toward deployment.

- **Musk reaffirmed widespread US robotaxi deployment by end of 2026** at the Samson summit. Loomis noted Musk has been "a literal yo-yo" on rollout timelines and counseled skepticism on any specific date.

- **India factory plans reportedly abandoned**, per a Chinese financial outlet, citing supply chain gaps, tariff negotiation deadlock, infrastructure shortfalls, and market mismatch. One analyst suggested this could be a negotiating tactic, and that Cybercab's eventual global readiness could reopen the conversation.

## Energy

- **Houston solar factory confirmed by Electrek:** Tesla is building a fully vertically integrated solar panel manufacturing facility at its Brookshire, Texas site (Empire West Business Park, ~35 miles west of Houston), co-located with the Megapack Megafactory. The leased footprint is 1.65 million square feet across two buildings, with a planned additional 600,000 sq ft building. Capex across multiple simultaneous builds exceeds $250 million; manufacturing environments are described as cleanroom-grade.

- **Full vertical integration:** Unlike simple panel assembly, Tesla is building the complete chain: ingot growth, wafer slicing, PV cell production, and finished panel assembly — consistent with the previously reported $2.9 billion deal to purchase Chinese solar manufacturing equipment, including from Suzhou Maxwell Technologies.

- **Scale context:** Tesla's existing Buffalo facility produces ~300 MW/year. The Houston operation targets 100 GW annually — a 300x increase — versus First Solar's projected 17.7 GW by 2027. The stated 100 GW target would exceed double the entire 43 GW the US installed in 2025. Electrek's Fred Lambert characterized the timeline as "wildly ambitious to completely delusional," citing Chinese export approvals and construction pace as key risks.

- **Capex signal:** On the Q1 2026 earnings call, CFO Vaibhav Taneja raised 2026 capex guidance to over $25 billion from a prior $20 billion figure, citing solar manufacturing equipment as a driver of the incremental $5 billion.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Musk's 5-year humanoid forecast:** At the Forbes Innovator Summit, Musk projected "at least 100 million humanoid robots, but maybe a billion" by approximately 2031, and stated that "digital intelligence will exceed the sum of all human intelligence" within five years.

- **Physical AI TAM framing:** Herbert Ong and supply chain executive Jeff Lutz argued Tesla's physical AI opportunity extends beyond cars and humanoid robots to any physical device with a chip and a camera — factory equipment, agricultural machinery, smart home devices — drawing a parallel to Jeff Bezos's startup Prometheus, which is pursuing AI-enablement of existing factories.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Semi demand inflection:** The New York Times published a positive profile of the Tesla Semi. California trucking firms have submitted subsidy applications for over 1,200 Tesla Semis — more than all applications for other electric truck brands combined since the incentive program launched in 2019. The early fleet achieved over 95% uptime in 2025, with 75% of service visits completed under 24 hours. Diesel prices have risen ~50% since a recent conflict, improving the Semi's economic case.

- **Competitor responses:** Volvo Trucks North America's president said he does not view the Semi as disruptive, citing a planned longer-range model next year. Daimler Truck North America cited insufficient high-powered charging infrastructure as a barrier to long-haul EV truck adoption.

- **Cybertruck Wade Mode incident:** A Cybertruck owner drove into Grapevine Lake in Texas to test Wade Mode, became stranded, and was arrested on multiple charges. Wade Mode supports a maximum ~32-inch wade depth; Musk's 2022 claim was that the Cybertruck would be "waterproof enough" to serve as a boat. Tesla's warranty does not cover water damage.

- **EV road-use fee proposed:** Bipartisan House legislation would impose a $130 annual fee on EV owners and $35 on plug-in hybrid owners for road maintenance, rising $5/year from 2029 to a maximum of $150/$50 — in addition to existing state registration fees.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO imminent:** The S1 filing was expected on May 20 per multiple sources. Rumored IPO date is June 12 on NASDAQ under ticker SPCX, following a 5-for-1 stock split (from $527 to $105). Rumored underwriters are JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Bank of America as equal co-leads, with an unusually high ~30% retail allocation. IPO valuation estimates range from $1.75 trillion (Cern Basher) to $2.2–$2.4 trillion (pre-market trading implied). BlackRock has announced a $5–$10 billion investment; Ron Baron has placed a $1 billion IPO order; Musk confirmed he will not sell shares.

- **Tesla-SpaceX merger debate:** Chamath Palihapitiya has publicly stated the merger is "99% sure" to happen, per Jo Bhakdi, who draws a parallel to Chamath's earlier SpaceX-Anthropic suggestion that materialized within days. Ron Baron, when asked, said "I don't know," citing good arguments on both sides. Jo Bhakdi's modeling suggests a merger is likely short-term negative for Tesla shareholders over 12–18 months (due to capital rotation into SpaceX) but strongly positive beyond that inflection. Bhakdi also raises a structural risk: if Tesla's market cap rises materially above SpaceX's before a merger is struck, the deal math becomes difficult.

- **Andrej Karpathy joined Anthropic**, stating the next few years at the frontier of LLMs will be "especially formative." SpaceX signed an agreement to give Anthropic access to Colossus 1 earlier in May 2026. Dillon Loomis framed these moves as deepening the overlap between Tesla, SpaceX, and frontier AI organizations.

- **Ford announced a five-year, up to 20 GWh battery energy storage supply agreement with renewable developer EDF** — a signal of growing non-Tesla competition in grid-scale storage, where Megapack currently leads.
