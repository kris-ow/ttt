This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg (serial entrepreneur and venture capital investor) analyzing multiple interviews of SpaceX President and COO Gwyn Shotwell, focusing primarily on her response to questions about a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger, as well as her comments on SpaceX's AI satellite plans, chip manufacturing ambitions, and Starship progress. The core thesis is that Shotwell's non-denial of a Tesla-SpaceX merger — combined with other signals — indicates a merger/acquisition is likely, and that Shotwell would be an ideal COO for a combined entity.

## Market & Competition

1. **Gwyn Shotwell's Non-Denial of a SpaceX-Tesla Merger:** In a CNBC interview conducted by Morgan Brennan, Shotwell was asked to respond to analyst Dan Ives of Wedbush's assertion that SpaceX and Tesla will eventually merge, with Ives putting the probability at over 80% and targeting 2027, and claiming the groundwork is already in place. Shotwell responded that a merger "might make Elon's life a little easier," acknowledged "there's no question that there are synergies between Tesla and SpaceX," and said there is "definitely a convergence of what we're all trying to accomplish in the future," before adding she was not currently focused on "that part of the future, at least." Herbert and Larry Goldberg interpret this as a tacit confirmation rather than a denial, with Goldberg noting he predicted this outcome in a white paper published in February 2026.

2. **Timing of a Potential Merger/Acquisition:** Larry Goldberg argues that an acquisition offer will not materialize immediately but rather sometime in early next year (i.e., early 2027), citing several reasons: Tesla's robotaxi rollout needs more time to establish clarity and drive Tesla's share price higher; SpaceX shareholders face lockup periods on their newly issued public stock and would not want a massive merger before those expire; and SpaceX has a large operational workload to manage in the near term. He contrasts his view with another commentator ("Alexandra") who reportedly believes an acquisition could happen sooner.

3. **SpaceX S-1 Amendment and Acquisition Capacity:** Herbert references language in SpaceX's S-1 amendment stating that SpaceX may be increasing its available shares in order to potentially conduct large acquisitions, which he cites as a structural signal supporting the merger thesis.

4. **Key SpaceX Executives Cited as Merger Assets:** Larry Goldberg highlights SpaceX CFO Brett Johnson as a key figure who has managed SpaceX's public market process and steered the company through multiple financing rounds. Goldberg suggests that a combined Tesla-SpaceX quarterly earnings call featuring both Shotwell and Johnson would represent a dramatic upgrade in executive communication quality. Shotwell herself is described as an engineer with over 20 years at SpaceX, employee number 11 at founding in 2002, and estimated by Herbert to have accumulated approximately $2 billion in SpaceX stock.

5. **SpaceX's AI Satellite and Chip Plans:** In her CNBC interview, Shotwell stated that AI satellites are "simpler" to develop than Starlink satellites, though she cautioned it is "not a slam dunk." She cited supply chain challenges, specifically the need to get solar cells right and the need to build a lot of chips — not because SpaceX wants to be a chip manufacturer, but because she believes chip manufacturers are not "thinking about scaling in the same ways" SpaceX is, or do not believe SpaceX's scale ambitions. Herbert frames this in the context of Elon Musk's previously stated goal of getting 1 gigawatt of compute into space by 2027, a timeline Musk reportedly pulled forward by one year. Goldberg revised his own forecast, saying he had not modeled orbital data centers until 2028 but now believes SpaceX could reach perhaps half a gigawatt of operational orbital compute by the end of next year (end of 2027).

6. **Starship Flight Progress and Orbital Timeline:** During a factory tour with Morgan Brennan of CNBC, Shotwell indicated that Starship flight 13 was approximately one month away from the time of filming (placing it around early-to-mid July 2026 based on her "beginning of June" reference), that flight 14 would attempt at least an orbital injection, and that she expects Starship to be in orbit "by the end of this year" (2026). She also stated SpaceX aims to fly "every month." Goldberg expressed skepticism about reaching a cadence of one launch every two days by the end of next year (2027), suggesting one every five days with successful booster recovery would represent a strong outcome.

7. **SpaceX IPO Day and XAI Acquisition:** At the SpaceX IPO, Shotwell gave a speech highlighting the company's history of achieving things critics said were impossible — including getting the first liquid-fueled private rocket to orbit in 2008, achieving 165 Falcon 9 launches in the prior year, and developing Starship. She also announced that through the merger/acquisition of xAI, SpaceX as a group now owns "the largest coherent gigawatt-class compute on the planet." She noted that more than half of SpaceX's approximately 22,000 employees purchased additional stock at the IPO opening, totaling almost $1 billion.

8. **SpaceX Natural Gas Infrastructure Plans:** Goldberg and Herbert note that Shotwell mentioned in her CNBC interview that SpaceX plans to build its own natural gas pipelines and is possibly looking at extracting natural gas itself — though Herbert corrected himself to note she said "possibly extracting," not definitively. This is framed as consistent with SpaceX's pattern of vertical integration.

9. **Potential AI Company Acquisitions by SpaceX:** Shotwell mentioned in her interview that AI companies are among the types of companies SpaceX could potentially acquire using its newly public stock. Goldberg speculates that "Cursor" (a coding AI product) could be one such target, citing its potential fit with Grok and its position at the forefront of technology, while noting cultural fit would be a requirement.
