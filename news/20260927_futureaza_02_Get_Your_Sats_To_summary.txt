This is a co-streamed live commentary (futureaza with "Dr. Know It All") covering SpaceX's Starship Flight 14, notable as the first Starship flight to attempt full orbital insertion and deploy operational Starlink V3 satellites into the constellation. Much of the runtime is casual banter, but the hosts track the official SpaceX broadcast closely, relaying mission milestones, technical explanations, and commentary on the flight's outcome.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **First-ever Starship orbital insertion achieved:** After Super Heavy booster liftoff with 33 Raptor engines, hot-staging, and a boost-back burn, the booster suffered ice clogging in landing-burn engines (as on the prior flight) and made a hard splashdown in the Gulf after only 8 of 13 engines relit for landing (per the official broadcast recap, sourced to SpaceX commentary, later corrected to 11 of 13 relit with a "partial success"). Starship's ascent burn lost one Raptor Vacuum (RVac) engine, which triggered an extended internal debate within SpaceX before mission control gave a "go" call to proceed with the orbital insertion burn — the first time Starship has ever entered orbit.
2. **Reduced-scope mission after engine anomaly:** Due to the RVac failure on ascent, SpaceX opted for a shortened mission — one full orbit and an early de-orbit rather than the originally planned six orbits over ~10 hours — landing instead in the northern Pacific after roughly 3 hours 8 minutes total flight time. Officials, per the broadcast, framed the decision as conservative risk management given uncertainty about propellant/hardware health for the required de-orbit burn.
3. **Heat shield tile upgrades tested:** Flight 14 incorporated tile modifications based on data from Flight 13's heat-shield recovery from the Indian Ocean, including added retention pins on ascent-risk tiles, closed gaps to prevent plasma intrusion behind tiles, and reflown recovered tiles mounted on the ship's skirt area. Additional experimental tiles were placed on the leeward flap side to test attachment methods without risking primary heat-shield integrity.
4. **Booster landing outcome:** Super Heavy made what commentary described as a hard/partial-success splashdown off the coast of Starbase; the official broadcast recap noted it was not as soft as the prior flight's booster landing, with two of the planned relight engines failing to ignite.
5. **Starship reached orbit ~18 years after Falcon 1's first orbital flight:** SpaceX's on-air host (Dan) noted the milestone landed on the same calendar day as Falcon 1's first successful orbital flight, described as "18 years to the day."
6. **Louisiana launch site development:** SpaceX aired a segment on a new Gulf Coast launch site under construction in Vermillion Parish, Louisiana (near Pecan Island), intended to support "thousands of launches per year" at scale for Starship. SpaceX officials cited plans to bring roughly 10,000 jobs to the region and noted 70% of the Starbase, Texas workforce is local to the Rio Grande Valley, which they aim to replicate at the new site.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **26 Starlink V3 satellites successfully deployed:** All 26 V3 satellites were deployed and confirmed to have made contact with the ground/constellation via laser and RF links shortly after deployment, marking the first attempt (versus prior suborbital test deployments) to place V3s into the operational constellation. SpaceX said the satellites should begin serving customers within a few weeks.
2. **V3 satellite capabilities:** Per the official broadcast, Starlink V3 offers roughly 10x the downlink capacity, 22x the uplink capacity, and about 10–11x more beams than the V2 Mini satellite; each V3 adds about 1 terabit per second of capacity, so the 26-satellite deployment added roughly 26 terabits — described as about 10x the capacity of a single Falcon 9 V2 Mini launch. SpaceX also stated a future 60-satellite full Starship Starlink load would deliver about 20x the capacity of a single Falcon 9 launch.
3. **Heat shield imaging via Starlink satellites:** Several of the deployed satellites carry cameras and flashlights used to image Starship's heat shield during flight and transmit that data to engineers in near-real time, a capability first demonstrated on the prior flight and repeated here.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok 4.7 released:** SpaceX/xAI-related segment noted Grok 4.7 was released "last week" as a newer, more capable coding/knowledge-work model trained with longer reinforcement learning on harder tasks, and includes native support for "Grokbot," an AI agent tool.
2. **Memphis/South Haven supercomputing buildout:** The official broadcast described SpaceX-linked supercomputing facilities in Memphis, Tennessee and South Haven, Mississippi spanning over 2.5 million square feet with millions of GPUs and over 2 gigawatts of compute, with more than $90 billion in regional investment cited since 2024.
