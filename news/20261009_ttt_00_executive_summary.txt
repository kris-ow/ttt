## Autonomous Driving
- **Tesla drops "Full Self-Driving" branding in Germany, switching to "Tesla Assisted Driving" (TAD)** following pressure from Germany's Federal Ministry of Transport, which called the FSD name misleading on October 6. Tesla offered the new name during September talks with Transport Minister Steffen Bilger.
- **Speed-limit concession tied to the rebrand:** Germany and Tesla agreed the system can go up to 10% over posted limits (down from the Netherlands-approved 50% over), with Germany backing this position in the broader EU approval process; Tesla will need to modify FSD behavior to comply. Musk thanked Germany on X ("Danke schoen").
- **Rebrand is largely cosmetic so far:** the new German page is a translated version of the US FSD page with the product name swapped, retaining the "drive almost anywhere" and "7x safer" claims; in the US, tesla.com/fsd still sells "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" at $99/month. Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla only made the change because a regulator demanded it, and asks why the US hasn't followed.
- **EU-wide approval vote reportedly slipped to December at the earliest,** requiring 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of the EU population; France and Sweden have raised speeding-related concerns. Eight EU countries are currently listed as approved (Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia, Czechia, Croatia); Germany is not yet on the list.
- **Slovakia expected to become the ninth EU country to approve FSD "within days,"** per Transport Minister Jozef Raz, who cited data showing reduced accident rates.
- Tesla's European sales reportedly rose sharply in September, which panelists attributed to fuel prices, local Berlin manufacturing, and potential EU restrictions on Chinese EV imports — not FSD approval, which hasn't happened yet.

## Robotaxi
- **Texas robotaxi fleet continues to scale**, with tracker data showing Cybercab count rising from 45 (Aug 31) to 169 and Model Y robotaxis growing from 269 to 420, for a combined 589 vehicles; a separate tracker figure this week showed a record 868 Cybercabs in Austin (670 reportedly driverless) plus roughly 300 more in Dallas.
- Musk said robotaxis will operate until 11 p.m., with the main technical focus now on avoiding nighttime collisions with hard-to-see animals.
- First-hand Cybercab rides in Austin were described by multiple riders as polished and mature — responsive app, confident low-latency navigation, no steering wheel or pedals — contrasted favorably against nearby Waymo vehicles described as more hesitant.
- A separate post highlighted Tesla's vision-only robotaxis reportedly performing well at night in heavy rain, pushing back on prior skepticism about camera-only systems in adverse conditions.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- **SpaceX agreed October 8 to buy up to 14 MHz of paired nationwide 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management** (price undisclosed), pending FCC approval (~180-day target review). This follows the FCC's October 6 approval of a 15,000-satellite direct-to-phone constellation offering more than 100x the bandwidth of SpaceX's current generation.
- Musk called the spectrum deal "the last critical piece of the Spectrum puzzle needed for SpaceX to provide complete phone coverage in America," adding "Very big deal."
- AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile shares fell roughly 5–8% in after-hours trading on the news. SpaceX pushed back on AT&T CEO John Stankey's claim that its service can't penetrate buildings, saying the new low-band spectrum improves indoor coverage; the carriers formed a joint venture October 1 to pool their own spectrum for satellite-to-phone service in response.
- Deployment timeline implies roughly end-of-2027 for service launch, per Gwynne Shotwell's August comments (satellites launching "next year," service the year after); the host notes Musk-affiliated timelines are typically directionally correct but late.
- SpaceX added an $100 cash referral program for new Starlink subscribers.
- **American Airlines expanded its Starlink rollout to its entire mainland fleet of 1,030+ aircraft** (up from a previously announced 500), with installations starting early 2027; Delta and JetBlue have not yet announced adoption.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- **SpaceX's internal AI tool (Rockbot/Grok bot) will route queries to whichever model performs best for a given task** — including Claude Opus 5.5 and soon Midjourney — rather than exclusively using its own models, per statements attributed to Musk. Panelists read this as xAI prioritizing distribution over owning the top model while leveraging its compute position.
- SpaceX is reportedly looking to raise $40 billion in debt for Nvidia chip purchases, atop prior IPO proceeds and existing debt, as part of a target of roughly 10 gigawatts of compute capacity next year requiring next-gen ("Vera Rubin") Nvidia chips expected in Q1/Q2.
- Musk reportedly claimed SpaceX's valuation could eventually exceed world GDP (~$126 trillion) "by orders of magnitude" — characterized by panelists as a speculative, decade-plus-out claim, not a near-term target.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- **Terafab will be independently owned and operated by Tesla/SpaceX, not TSMC,** per Musk, clarifying an earlier report of TSMC involvement; TSMC may lease capacity but nothing more. The facility is described as roughly 50x existing chip manufacturing capacity, with no formal partnership agreements confirmed signed yet.
- Elon Musk received the National Medal of Science at a White House Science & Tech Innovation Summit; Trump called him an "industrial titan" and credited SpaceX with reducing NASA's reliance on foreign rockets and Tesla with producing the first commercially viable EVs with consumer self-driving technology.

## Humanoid Bots
- A newly published Tesla patent application describes a 3D tactile touch sensor for Optimus's hands, intended to detect touch, pressure, and force to improve grip and object manipulation.
- Panelists discussed whether Optimus should wear context-branded clothing (e.g., hotel uniforms) to aid social acceptance, while cautioning that making the robot look too human risks an uncanny-valley effect.

## Energy
- Tesla's California Virtual Power Plant program has returned a cumulative 5 GWh to the grid across 10 major events in 2026, described as enough to power roughly 1.2 million US homes (unit basis — likely peak-demand window — not specified).
- Tesla opened its first East Coast Semi Megacharger station, in Georgia, with 8 stalls each capable of up to 1.2 MW.
