Tesla exhausted its entire allocation of California's new MyFirstEV rebate program in five days, spending an estimated $18 million in combined state and manufacturer-matched rebates on Tesla vehicles alone.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **MyFirstEV program mechanics:** California's MyFirstEV program launched with $135.5 million in state funds, matched dollar-for-dollar by participating automakers for a combined pool of about $271 million, per Governor Gavin Newsom's office. It offers first-time EV buyers $3,500 off a new EV priced under $50,000 (or $1,750 off a used EV under $25,000), applied at point of sale with no income cap. Funds are split across roughly 13 automakers (~$9 million in state funds each before matching). Tesla, Hyundai, and Lucid were the first brands live starting August 3; Ford, Rivian, Chevy, and Kia join later in August, with Toyota, Honda, and Subaru starting in September.
2. **Program structure favors Rivian/Lucid over Tesla:** The article notes (referencing its own prior July coverage) that the $50,000 price cap is waived for EVs built by California-headquartered, EV-only automakers — a description matching Rivian and Lucid but not Tesla, which relocated its headquarters to Texas in 2021. Only Tesla's sub-$50,000 Model 3 and Model Y configurations qualify for the rebate.
3. **Tesla's California sales volume:** Tesla registered 45,953 vehicles in California in Q2, up 11.8% year-over-year, according to the California New Car Dealers Association — roughly 500 cars a day. Tesla accounted for 56.7% of all ZEV registrations in the state through June, with the Model Y alone booking 54,327 registrations in the first half of the year and holding 57.5% share of the luxury compact SUV segment.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the five-day exhaustion isn't surprising given Tesla's outsized California EV volume, and cautions against reading it as a demand signal: Tesla's California registrations fell 24% in Q1 after the federal $7,500 tax credit expired and remain down 6.5% year-to-date, even though Q2 rebounded 11.8% and Tesla still controls over half the state's EV market — meaning a $9 million rebate pool would empty in days "no matter what your year-over-year chart looks like."
