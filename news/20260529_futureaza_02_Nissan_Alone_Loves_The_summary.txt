This episode of the Tesla Beat podcast (episode 147) covers a wide range of Tesla and EV-adjacent news, including the retirement of the Model S and X, FSD expansion globally, Cybercab infrastructure buildout in Texas, Ferrari's controversial new electric vehicle, and Tesla's dominance in markets like Norway and Washington State. The hosts — John, Joe, Brian, and Matthew (MDR) — offer enthusiastic firsthand perspectives on Tesla's accelerating autonomous driving and robotaxi momentum, while also previewing two upcoming community events.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model S and X Farewell Event:** Tesla held a sendoff event for the final Model S and X units at what appears to be a Giga Texas facility. Attendees included Tesla executives Lars Moravy and Franz von Holzhausen, along with Elon Musk, who joined after attending a White House meeting with other major tech CEOs. The final Model S units were a limited special edition — approximately 250 cars — featuring gold Plaid badging, gold stitching, Alcantara interior, a special opening screen, and employee signatures embedded in the door seals and badging. The hosts reflected emotionally on the car's legacy, noting it won Motor Trend Car of the Year and that it effectively launched the modern EV era. MDR expects a Model X replacement later in 2026, with the Roadster slated to become the new flagship.

2. **Ferrari Electric "Luche" — Universal Criticism:** Ferrari unveiled a new electric four-door vehicle priced at approximately $600,000–$650,000, which the hosts unanimously criticized for its styling (described as a cross between a Charger and a Prius), its price-to-value ratio, and its strategic incoherence. The car reportedly has more horsepower than a Tesla Model S Plaid but inferior range despite a larger battery than the Cybertruck. The hosts argued Ferrari should have followed Lamborghini's Urus playbook and made a luxury electric SUV instead. The hosts predicted it would sell very few units — possibly only 10–20 — and that Ferrari's real goal may be to claim they "tried" EVs to comply with European regulations while continuing to sell ICE vehicles. Nissan was noted as one of the few to publicly appreciate the design, with the implication that Nissan could build something similar at a fraction of the price.

3. **Cybertruck Standard Range & Growing Visibility:** The hosts noted a new standard Cybertruck variant is coming and that Cybertruck sightings are becoming increasingly common even in smaller Midwest towns, with three to five sightings per day in some areas. Jason Cammisa (referenced from a YouTube video) called the Cybertruck the best vehicle ever created. Doug DeMuro, in the same video, recommended that 99% of people switch to electric for 99% of trips.

4. **Model Y Dominates Norway and Washington State:** The Model Y became the first EV to achieve 100,000 registrations in Norway in just four years, meaning approximately 1 in every 29 passenger cars on Norwegian roads is now a Tesla Model Y. In Washington State, the Model Y is described as the bestselling vehicle in the state for several years and the most over-represented vehicle compared to national averages. Tesla is reportedly struggling to find adequate storage/delivery space on Seattle's east side due to high sales volume, and has suspended vehicle deliveries at its Belleview location.

5. **Model Y Recall — Incorrect Weight Certification Sticker:** Approximately 14,500 Model Y units received a recall due to mislabeled weight certification stickers. The issue caused a temporary shipment halt and was known internally before becoming public.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised Now Available Across North America and Growing Internationally:** Full Self-Driving supervised is now available across all of North America (including Mexico and Puerto Rico), China, Australia, New Zealand, South Korea, Netherlands, and Lithuania, with three additional countries added recently. The hosts expressed particular excitement about Netherlands — specifically Amsterdam — as one of the most complex driving environments in Europe, and noted that successful FSD deployment there positions Tesla well for broader European rollout.

2. **FSD Navigating New Infrastructure Before Maps Updated:** Matthew (MDR) shared a firsthand account of FSD (described as version 14.3) successfully navigating a brand-new freeway on-ramp in Kirkland, Washington on its first day open — with no signage and incorrect map data — by visually identifying and taking the correct route before the driver was even aware the road existed. FSD also independently handled a flagged road closure, pulling into the oncoming lane as directed by a flagger. The hosts used this as evidence of the gap between Tesla's FSD and all other vehicles.

3. **Texas Authorizes Driverless Commercial Operation; Level 4 Certification:** Under a new Texas law that took effect May 28, 2026, Tesla has been authorized to operate driverless vehicles commercially. Tesla has officially certified the software running on its robotaxis as Level 4 autonomous. The hosts also noted Elon Musk posted a video of a Cybercab leaving the production line without a steering wheel.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Maintenance Facility Planned in Irving, Texas:** Tesla is planning a new maintenance facility for Cybercabs and robotaxis in Irving, Texas (near Dallas, home of AT&T Stadium). The hosts noted this appears to be the second such facility announced after one in Las Vegas. The facility buildout was attributed to a tip from a community account ("Marco") and seen as evidence of serious operational preparation for large-scale Cybercab deployment.

2. **Cybercab Sightings in Austin, Dallas, and Houston:** Community members are posting video footage of Cybercabs operating in Houston and Dallas in addition to Austin. The hosts referenced robotaxi tracker data and noted that Cybercabs without steering wheels are being spotted leaving Giga Texas and appearing in Arizona as well. The overall tone was that a large-scale launch appears imminent and that regulators have effectively been pre-empted by Texas state law.

## Market & Competition

1. **Starlink Airline Adoption — Delta as Outlier:** United Airlines expects Starlink on 80% of its fleet, and Southwest Airlines expects 300+ aircraft equipped with Starlink by end of 2026. Delta has chosen a competitor provider, which the hosts criticized as a strategic misstep, particularly given a Blue Origin launch mishap that the hosts said would delay the competitor's satellite constellation by 8–15 months. The connection to Tesla/SpaceX is the Starlink-Cybercab integration being developed.

2. **SpaceX IPO Ticker Announced:** The ticker symbol for SpaceX's anticipated public offering — expected around June 12, 2026 — will reportedly be SPCX. The hosts noted this briefly as adjacent Tesla ecosystem news.
