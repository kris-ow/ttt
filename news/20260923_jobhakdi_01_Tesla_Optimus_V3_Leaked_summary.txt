## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus V3 leak surfaces in Tesla's Android app:** A leaked image was reportedly found hidden in the code of Tesla's Android app (cited version 4.60.5), showing what is claimed to be Optimus V3. The host stresses this is not a confirmed official reveal — the image itself was likely produced by Tesla but extracted by an outside party — though he says it has been validated by multiple people as not fabricated.
2. **Design shifts toward a more human, less robotic look:** Compared to Optimus 2.5, the leaked V3 design covers hard mechanical structure with softer, human-like contours, which the host argues reduces the "uncanny valley" scare factor relative to more mechanical-looking Chinese humanoid robots — he sees this as a deliberate Tesla product/psychology strategy rather than pure engineering.
3. **Third-party technical breakdown (attributed to "Tesla Ming," a China-based commentator):** Per this source, V3 reportedly has fully enclosed/sealed joints (no exposed actuators or wiring) for factory safety and improved dust/grime (IP) ratings, a single monolithic composite torso shell housing a stated 2.3 kWh battery pack and central compute, hand actuators relocated from the hand into a slimmer forearm using tendon/string-driven finger mechanisms (similar to the approach used by 1X Technologies), and slimmed-down lower legs/feet intended to reduce motor torque demands and improve gait.
4. **Unconfirmed Chinese supply-chain reports point to a fast production ramp:** Citing Chinese press (referencing outlets around September 16–21) and other unconfirmed leaks, the host relays reports that Tesla teams are auditing Chinese suppliers for Gen 3 mass production, that suppliers reportedly already hold orders of varying size pending an audit, that an earlier September audit wave had reportedly prepped for multi-10,000-unit production, and that a 5,000-unit component batch is reportedly already being delivered. He also cites unconfirmed internal figures of 10,000–20,000 units in 2026, rumors of a ramp toward roughly 1,000 Optimus units per week by year-end, and a separate rumor of 15,000 parts kits shipped to Tesla. All of this is explicitly flagged as unconfirmed by Tesla.
5. **Host's bull case for early, imperfect commercial deployment:** The host argues Optimus doesn't need to be commercially superior to humans to sell in the tens/hundreds of thousands, because businesses (retail, factories, agriculture, McDonald's-type operators) will want to start piloting robots roughly two years ahead of the point they become truly cost-effective, implying Tesla could be supply-constrained if initial performance is decent when it goes commercial (which he expects next year).
6. **Expected reveal timing:** The host's personal guess is that Tesla will not officially reveal Optimus V3 immediately off the leak, estimating a Q2 next-year reveal as most likely, arguing Tesla can't wait too long given expected competing humanoid products next year but also isn't ready to show it yet.
7. **Skepticism about a critic's view on Tesla's robotics talent:** The host references a named critic (Chris Camillo) as being doubtful Tesla's robotics team is world-class compared to specialized humanoid robot startups, and pushes back, arguing Elon Musk's ability to engineer the surrounding ecosystem (manufacturing, supply chain, deal-making) is a major asset regardless of raw robotics talent.
