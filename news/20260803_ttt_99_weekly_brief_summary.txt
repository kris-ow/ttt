## Brief

- WSJ: Tesla weighs China sale to clear SpaceX merger path

## Robotaxi
- Lars Moravy confirmed directly to Sawyer Merritt that Cybercab and Robotaxi face no vehicle-count cap, quashing ~2,500-unit speculation drawn from Zoox's separate NHTSA-capped exemption.
- Bhakdi pushed his 15,000-Cybercab year-end call back to roughly Q1 2027, citing a slower ramp; scenario modeling from brighterwithherbert/Cern Basher pegs a base case of ~900-1,000 vehicles and ~$120M run-rate by end-2026, scaling to ~$40B by end-2027 in the base case ($80B bull case).
- Driverless Cybercab sightings around Giga Texas rose roughly 2.5x from early to late July per Joe Tegtmeyer footage, with fully driverless units now spotted on Austin public roads beyond employee testing.
- Tesla disabled audible turn-signal/hazard sounds fleet-wide in unsupervised mode; scattered service hiccups reported (a Houston rider told to exit and cancel, a Dallas car stuck in a turnaround loop).

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD v15 is already running in the robotaxi fleet per Ashok Elluswamy, with roughly three of seven planned new features integrated; consumer unsupervised release not yet active.
- Tesla's first EU FSD safety dataset: supervised FSD 5.2x safer than manual driving across 65 million km in five countries, with zero collisions across 26 million highway miles.
- A federal whistleblower suit filed by ex-Houston FSD manager Javier Madrono alleges an unsafe 38:1 operator-to-manager ratio and a fatigue-linked crash; multiple hosts flag the claims as likely to miss Texas's 90-day filing window and inconsistent with internal messaging.
- NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation into ~1.2 million 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y vehicles over 156 complaints of front lower lateral link (suspension) separation; agency says it knows of no crashes or injuries.
- A coast-to-coast "Cannonball" run hit zero disengagements over ~2,800 miles on FSD 14.3.6, a new record, pushing the vehicle past 20,000 total FSD miles.

## Financials
- Q2 recap sharpened this week: revenue $28.2B (+26% YoY), EPS $0.33 vs. ~$0.49-0.53 estimates, capex $5.79B (+142%); free cash flow came in near -$1B, better than a feared -$3B, against $43.5B cash on hand.
- TSLA fell as much as ~38% over five days and ~25% over the trailing month post-earnings before stabilizing near $311; multiple analysts (Bhakdi) attribute only ~2-3 points of the drop to Tesla-specific news, with the rest tied to a broader momentum-stock liquidation (a leveraged hedge fund, "Situational Awareness," absorbed by Citadel).
- FSD subscriptions reached ~1.5 million, up 56% YoY; institutional ownership hit an all-time high (over 2B of ~3.95B shares ex-Musk) while retail ownership fell to ~20% from over 50%.
- Tesla trades near 156-200x forward earnings depending on source, a level one host concedes is a legitimate bear-case argument even amid the pullback.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla built its 10 millionth vehicle — a Diamond Black Model Y at Fremont — timed to Giga Nevada's 10-year anniversary; Electrek notes deliveries fell from a 2023 peak of 1.81M to 1.64M in 2025 (-9% YoY) against installed capacity over 2.375M units.
- Tesla sued Cybertruck tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group, seeking emergency access to a Troy, Texas plant after the supplier allegedly demanded $250,000/week; Tesla says on-hand parts inventory could run out within days and rebuilding tooling would take five to six months.
- Cybertruck PCS warranty extended to 8yr/150k miles for 2024-25 models (7yr/70k for 2026 models) with reimbursement for prior out-of-pocket repairs; new Cybertrucks now ship with carbon-fiber underbody panels.
- Musk confirmed Tesla will fully open-source the discontinued Model S/X design and software; Canadian Model Y delivery estimates now stretch into early 2027, attributed to Giga Berlin capacity rather than demand.

## Energy
- Tesla signed over 640 MW of new solar-plus-storage PPAs in one week: a 509 MW Arizona project with ContourGlobal/KKR (90% output, ~1.4+ GWh storage, online 2028), a 140 MW Texas farm from Zelestra, and three Concord New Energy PPAs totaling 469 MW AC feeding the Supercharger network from H1 2027.
- Tesla shipped its first Megapacks using domestically made LFP cells from Sparks, NV, qualifying for a ~$35/kWh domestic-manufacturing credit worth an estimated $245M annually.
- A $225M Megapack order from Neoen (Australia, 12th project, pushing combined capacity past 2 GW) closed out a heavy energy-contracting week.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- WSJ reported Tesla advisers discussed selling or spinning off Tesla's China business to remove a national-security obstacle to a Tesla-SpaceX merger; Musk called it "absurdly fake news," saying it "never even came up in the discussion ever." Analysts split on credibility — one pegs actual divestiture odds at 10-15%.
- SpaceX shares fell to roughly 44-49% below their post-IPO high (~$108-114, from a $135 IPO price), attributed mainly to the looming share-lockup unlock (float expanding from ~5% to 25-35%) beginning August 6, not fundamentals.
- SpaceX will hold its first public-company earnings call August 4, using a custom Grok-built Q&A platform; Wall Street's average 12-month target sits at $230 (high estimate ~$800).
- Merger speculation intensified after Musk reportedly brought an M&A lawyer onto Tesla's earnings call and neither he nor Shotwell denied a combination when asked; one analyst (Merz) speculates an announcement window of August 10-15 with a shareholder vote 3-4 months later.

## SpaceX — Starship & Launch
- Starship's most recent flight: the booster completed a full 33-engine boost-back but only 8 of 13 landing-burn engines relit, causing a hard splashdown, while the ship made a soft, intact Indian Ocean landing and deployed 20 fully functional V3 Starlink satellites. Flight 14 will attempt the program's first tower catch of the ship along with an orbital insertion attempt.
- SpaceX won a $1.6 billion Space Force task order for 18 Falcon 9 launches from Vandenberg through end of 2027, the largest publicly disclosed task order yet under NSSL Phase 3 Lane One.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX compute-lease deals now total roughly $28B/year under contract (Anthropic ~$15B, Google ~$11B, Reflection ~$2B); combined future sites could reach ~1.2 million GPUs.
- Musk confirmed SpaceX's engineering archive (excluding ITAR material) will feed a supplemental training run for Grok's next model.
- SpaceX is acquiring Cursor (developing its own LLM, profitable at over $4B ARR), with closing expected late August/September.

## Bear Case of the Week
- TSLA fell as much as ~38% over five days following earnings, with the stock at one point down ~25% over the trailing month — the sharpest post-earnings drawdown in the thesis's recent history, though most hosts attribute the bulk to a broader forced-liquidation selloff rather than Tesla fundamentals.
- NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation into 1.2 million 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y vehicles over 156 suspension-detachment complaints causing loss of steering control.
- A federal whistleblower lawsuit alleges Tesla ran an unsafe 38:1 operator-to-manager ratio at its Houston FSD testing operation and links a crash to exhaustion-driven guidance, though the claim's procedural validity is disputed.
- Tesla's emergency lawsuit against tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group warns that Cybertruck parts inventory could run out "within days," with no alternate supplier able to produce replacement tooling for five to six months.
- SpaceX shares remain down 44-49% from their post-IPO high heading into the company's first public earnings call, with a further share-lockup unlock beginning August 6 expected to add selling pressure.
