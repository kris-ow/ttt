This video is a live stream covering Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call, hosted by "investingagainstthegrain" with a panel of analysts and commentators including James (Invest Answers), Larry Goldberg, Nick Gibbs, Jeff Lutz, Alexandra Merz, and Cern Basher. The panel analyzes the earnings deck as it drops, live-streams the official Tesla earnings call featuring Elon Musk and CFO Vaibhav Taneja, and then conducts a post-call discussion. The core thesis across the panel is that Tesla is at a major inflection point driven by the convergence of Cybercab production launch, robotaxi expansion, Optimus development, and FSD adoption growth — though the panel is critical of Tesla's communication style on the call itself.

## Financials

1. **Free Cash Flow Dramatically Beats Expectations:** Tesla reported Q1 2026 free cash flow of just over $1.4 billion positive, against Wall Street consensus estimates of approximately negative $1.9 billion — a swing of roughly $3.5 billion. The panel attributed this primarily to stronger-than-expected gross margins driven by lower vehicle material costs and growth in high-margin services revenue (FSD). Cash on hand increased to $44.7 billion in the quarter.

2. **Gross Margin Returns to Multi-Year Highs:** GAAP gross margin came in at 21.1%, with auto gross margin excluding credits at 19.2% (up sequentially from 17.9%). The panel noted this is the highest gross margin level since approximately Q4 2022. Auto gross profit per vehicle delivered, excluding credits and leases, exceeded $8,000. One-time benefits included approximately $230 million from warranty true-ups and some tariff relief, though no benefit from the recent Supreme Court IPA tariff ruling was recognized due to ongoing uncertainty.

3. **Capex Guidance Raised Sharply to $25 Billion for 2026:** CFO Vaibhav Taneja disclosed that Tesla now expects over $25 billion in capital expenditures for full-year 2026, up $5 billion from prior guidance of $20 billion. In Q1, Tesla spent approximately $2.5 billion in capex (or roughly $4.5 billion including the ~$2 billion equity investment in SpaceX/XAI made in January). Management explicitly stated that free cash flow will be negative for the remainder of 2026, describing this as the right strategy for long-term positioning. The panel noted six factories are currently going into or already in operation.

4. **Services Revenue at All-Time Record for Q1:** Services and other revenue reached $3.745 billion, up approximately 10% sequentially from Q3's $3.475 billion and down slightly from Q4, representing a 51% increase year-over-year. The panel noted this is primarily driven by FSD subscriptions and supercharging, and that this was an all-time Q1 record for the segment. Energy generation and storage revenue fell from approximately $3.8 billion to $2.4 billion quarter-over-quarter, with management confirming the business is inherently lumpy tied to customer deployment timelines.

5. **Net Income Impacted by Bitcoin and FX:** Net income was impacted by mark-to-market charges on Bitcoin holdings, which depreciated 22% compared to the prior quarter, as well as unfavorable FX impacts from intercompany transactions. Net profit margin was approximately 6.5%.

6. **CEO Compensation Expense Hits P&L:** Operating expenses increased sequentially due to a full quarter of stock-based compensation for the 2025 CEO compensation plan, for which one milestone is still deemed probable. The weighted average diluted share count remained essentially flat at approximately 3.538–3.539 billion shares.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Demand Rebounds in Key Markets:** CFO Taneja reported demand resurgence across multiple regions. Giga Berlin reached a record output of over 61,000 units in Q1 2026. France and Germany showed over 150% quarter-over-quarter growth in deliveries. South Korea and Japan also showed growth. The US saw slight quarter-over-quarter delivery growth. Tesla ended Q1 with its highest Q1 order backlog in over two years. The recent uptick in gas prices provided a tailwind, though management noted improvement began before gas prices rose.

2. **Battery Cell Availability Remains a Production Constraint:** Management explicitly flagged battery pack capacity as the biggest limiter on vehicle production growth. Remediation efforts underway include ramping the Model Y in-house 4680 battery pack in Berlin, adding capacity at the Reno facility (retooling lines after nearly 10 years), and ramping in-house LFP module production in China. Tesla also began ramping LFP production for Megapacks.

3. **Model 3 Affordability Highlighted:** CFO Taneja noted that when Model 3 launched with a promised $35,000 starting price, that equates to approximately $48,000 in today's dollars, while the current starting price of Model 3 is below that figure with a substantially more capable product.

4. **Vehicle Sales Strategy Shift — FSD as the Product:** Management stated Tesla has evolved its sales strategy to position FSD as the primary product and the vehicle as merely the delivery mechanism.

5. **Hardware 3 Cannot Achieve Unsupervised FSD:** Elon Musk confirmed that Hardware 3 vehicles lack the capability to run unsupervised FSD, citing that Hardware 3 has only 1/8 the memory bandwidth of Hardware 4, which is a critical bottleneck for AI inference. Tesla is offering discounted trade-ins toward Hardware 4 vehicles and plans to offer in-car hardware upgrades (requiring replacement of both the computer and cameras). To do this efficiently, Tesla plans to establish micro-factories or small upgrade centers in major metropolitan areas. A distilled version of V14 software for Hardware 3 is expected to be released by end of June, enabling park-to-drive and equivalent features to V14 on Hardware 4.

6. **Tesla Roadster Debut Possible in About a Month:** Elon Musk mentioned that a Tesla Roadster debut could happen in approximately a month, contingent on testing and validation. He described it as potentially "one of the most spectacular demos ever" but noted it won't be a significant revenue driver. He also confirmed that long-term, the only manually-driven Tesla will be the new Roadster — all other models are intended to be autonomous.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3 Is a Major Architectural Update — Last Piece of the Puzzle:** Elon Musk confirmed that FSD V14.3 represents a major architectural update and is essentially the last piece needed to enable unsupervised FSD. However, he clarified that further software improvements (including V15, a complete software architecture overhaul) are known and in the pipeline, and Tesla will not go to very large-scale unsupervised FSD deployment until those improvements are validated and released, because they could materially improve safety.

2. **FSD Version 15 — Complete Software Overhaul Planned:** Elon Musk described V15 as a "complete overhaul of the software architecture" that will run on AI4 hardware. He stated it is expected by the end of 2026, or certainly by early 2027. V15 is intended to further elevate safety beyond already-above-human levels. The robotaxi fleet in Austin, Dallas, and Houston is running on V14.3 variants.

3. **FSD Subscriptions Hit Nearly 1.3 Million Paid Customers Globally:** CFO Taneja reported nearly 1.3 million paid FSD customers globally as of Q1. FSD subscriptions grew 180,000 in the quarter (the fastest sequential quarterly growth ever for FSD). Approximately 14% of all Tesla vehicles ever delivered now have an active FSD subscription. The incremental take rate for Q1 was over 50% (though the panel noted this is skewed by the removal of outright purchase option driving people to subscribe). FSD miles driven reached approximately 9.7 billion, on track to cross 10 billion by approximately mid-May.

4. **FSD Approved in Netherlands; EU-Wide Review Expected in Q2:** Tesla received FSD supervised approval in the Netherlands. The broader EU review is expected in May (submitted to Brussels), potentially enabling FSD across Europe by Q2. China approval is also in progress, with hopes for broader approval by Q3 2026. Management noted Europe data was incorporated into the same underlying FSD architecture used in the US.

5. **AI4 Chip Sufficient for Unsupervised FSD; AI5 Redirected:** Elon Musk confirmed that AI4 hardware is expected to achieve unsupervised FSD at safety levels far exceeding human drivers, meaning AI5 is not immediately needed in vehicles. AI5 will go into Optimus and data centers (Cortex). An AI4 upgrade (dubbed "AI4.1" or "AI4 plus") is planned, upgrading RAM from 16GB to 32GB per SoC (64GB total), with approximately 10% improvements in compute and memory bandwidth. This upgrade depends on Samsung completing modifications and is expected to go into production around mid-2027. AI6 design is underway and Dojo 3 concepts are being discussed.

6. **AI5 Chip Tape-Out Completed Early:** The AI5 chip tape-out was completed ahead of schedule, achieved through the team working every weekend for six months straight including all holidays. Elon Musk personally participated. No major design mistakes were encountered.

7. **Unsupervised FSD to Customer Cars — Likely Q4 2026:** Elon Musk estimated, with caveats, that unsupervised FSD will be made available to customer-owned vehicles approximately in Q4 2026, rolled out gradually by geography as each area is confirmed safe.

8. **Hardware 3 Upgrade Path Detailed:** For Hardware 3 owners with purchased FSD, Tesla is offering a discounted trade-in toward a Hardware 4 vehicle or an in-car upgrade (computer + camera replacement). Micro-factory upgrade centers are being planned for major metro areas to execute this efficiently at scale.

9. **Cortex 1 Operational; Cortex 2 Online and Running Training Workloads:** Cortex 1 is in production with over 100,000 H100-equivalent GPUs. Cortex 2 has started running training workloads (an early ramp phase). A picture of Cortex 2 was referenced as being posted publicly.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Production Has Started:** During the live stream, Jeff Lutz reported breaking news that Tesla's X account confirmed Cybercab production has officially started, posted approximately 10 minutes before the announcement. The earnings deck described Cybercab as being in "pilot production" at time of publication. Elon Musk confirmed on the call that production has "just started." He cautioned that initial production will be very slow, following a typical S-curve for any new product with an entirely new supply chain, expected to ramp exponentially toward year-end and into 2027.

2. **Cybercab, Tesla Semi, and Megapack 3 All On Track for Volume Production in 2026:** The earnings deck explicitly states all three are on schedule for volume production in 2026. The panel noted this is one of the most significant headlines from the deck.

3. **Robotaxi Expanding to Dallas and Houston — Unsupervised from Day One:** Tesla expanded robotaxi operations to Dallas and Houston, launching unsupervised (no safety driver) from the outset, which the panel noted is unprecedented — other companies (notably Waymo) spent two or more years in supervised mode in new cities. The earnings deck listed seven cities in various stages from "ramping unsupervised" to "preparations underway."

4. **Target: Approximately a Dozen States by End of 2026:** Elon Musk stated Tesla hopes to have unsupervised FSD or robotaxi operating in approximately a dozen or more states by end of 2026. He emphasized a very cautious approach — zero incidents and zero injuries to date with the unsupervised expansion. He confirmed robotaxi revenue will not be "super material" in 2026 but expects it to be material and possibly significant in 2027.

5. **Primary Bottleneck Is Operational, Not Safety:** Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswami detailed that the primary issues slowing robotaxi scale-up are operational edge cases, not direct safety incidents: cars getting "scared" and refusing to cross railroads or proceed at stuck lights; cars caught in recursive infinite loops (e.g., attempting a turn into a construction zone repeatedly); and a notable incident where a group of Tesla robotaxis were stuck in a left-turn lane in Austin because a Waymo had crashed into a bus blocking the road.

6. **Cybercab to Replace Model Y Fleet Over Time:** The earnings deck explicitly states that once in production, Cybercab will begin replacing the existing Model Y fleet and is expected to become the largest volume vehicle in Tesla's fleet over time. Elon Musk noted that 90% of miles driven are with one or two people, making the two-person Cybercab the logical primary vehicle.

7. **Fleet Ramp Projections (Analyst Panel Estimates):** Jeff Lutz estimated hundreds of Cybercabs per month in April, ramping to approximately 1,000 per month by end of June, with the expectation that by end of Q3 Tesla could produce the equivalent of the entire Waymo fleet in one month, and within six months could produce the Waymo fleet's equivalent in one week. Nick Gibbs suggested Tesla could have more robotaxis deployed than Waymo's entire fleet by end of May or June 2026 due to the forcing function of Cybercab production.

8. **Robotaxi Revenue in Services Line:** CFO Taneja confirmed robotaxi revenue is included in the Services and Other segment. Tesla is making deliberate infrastructure investments to support robotaxi scaling. The fleet grew quarter-over-quarter in Q1.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus V3 Reveal Expected Around Mid-2026:** Elon Musk stated the V3 Optimus design is "almost ready to demonstrate" with some aesthetic finishing touches remaining. He expects to be able to show it off approximately mid-year 2026. However, Tesla is deliberately delaying the reveal to avoid competitors doing frame-by-frame analysis and copying the design — and intends to show it closer to the point of production.

2. **Optimus Production Start at Fremont — Late July/August 2026:** Elon Musk stated Optimus starter production at Fremont is assumed to be somewhere around late July to August 2026. The final Model S/X production will end in early May, after which the production line will be fully dismantled. Elon explained the full process: dismantling the old line starting from sub-components up to final assembly (over several months), then installing new cabling, machines, and testing for the Optimus line. He stated completing this transition in approximately four months would be "an insanely fast speed" unprecedented by any company on Earth.

3. **Optimus First Large-Scale Factory Preparations Beginning in Q2 2026:** The earnings deck states preparations for Tesla's first large-scale Optimus factory will begin shortly in Q2 2026 — earlier than the previously expected Q3 start. A second Optimus factory is being constructed at Giga Texas, expected to start production around summer 2027.

4. **Optimus Production Rate for 2026 Cannot Be Predicted:** Elon Musk explicitly stated it is "impossible to predict" the Optimus production rate for 2026 given the entirely new product, entirely new production line, and approximately 10,000 unique components that all must go right. He stated it "will move as fast as the least lucky, slowest, dumbest part in the entire 10,000." Initial skills will start simple (factory tasks) and build up from there.

5. **Optimus Useful Outside Tesla Expected Starting 2027:** Elon Musk stated Tesla expects to be able to make Optimus useful outside of Tesla (i.e., for external customers) sometime in 2027. He reiterated his conviction that Optimus will be "probably the biggest product ever," not just Tesla's biggest product.

6. **Optimus Intelligence Architecture — Local + Grok Orchestration:** Elon Musk described the planned intelligence architecture for Optimus: sufficient local on-board AI to continue functioning even without cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity (analogous to how FSD works offline in cars). Grok is intended as the orchestration AI (similar to a manager) and for voice interaction. He estimated Optimus could work for several hours without requiring management oversight from a large AI model.

## Energy

1. **Q1 Energy Storage Deployments Decline Significantly:** Tesla deployed 6.8 gigawatt hours of energy storage in Q1 2026, a 38% sequential decline. Management attributed this to the inherently lumpy nature of the business tied to customer deployment timelines. Despite the decline, Tesla still expects full-year 2026 energy storage deployments to exceed 2025 levels. Energy gross margins hit a record 39.5%, partly due to one-time benefits of over $250 million from recognition of tariffs paid in prior quarters. On a normalized basis, management expects energy margin compression going forward due to increasing competition and tariff impacts (most battery cells are sourced from China).

2. **Megapack 3 Factory in Houston On Track:** Tesla's new Megapack factory outside Houston (referred to as "Megafactory") is on track to begin production later in 2026. Starter production for the new Megapack factory is on track per the earnings deck.

3. **Residential Solar in Correction; New Products Introduced:** Management noted the US residential solar market is going through a correction following the loss of the homeowner tax credit. Tesla introduced a solar lease product to capture the tax credit itself and offer competitive pricing. Tesla also debuted its own solar panel with superior performance and aesthetics, plus its own mounting system for a fully integrated home energy ecosystem. Strong demand is expected to resume in the second half of 2026.

## Market & Competition

1. **Goldman Sachs Safety Report Validates Tesla Robotaxi Advantage:** The panel referenced a Goldman Sachs safety report comparing Tesla and Waymo incident data using NHTSA filings, which the panel noted is the first time Goldman Sachs has dug into incident data between the two companies. The report concluded that Tesla's robotaxi has a "massive advantage in safety" and that the advantage becomes more obvious at scale. Goldman called Tesla a "true leader in autonomous driving AI." The panel noted no Tesla robotaxi incidents since January 15, 2026.

2. **Waymo Comparison on Fleet and Timeline:** The panel noted Waymo spent over two years in supervised mode in both Scottsdale/Phoenix and the Bay Area before going unsupervised. Jeff Lutz predicted Tesla could produce the equivalent of Waymo's entire fleet in one month by end of Q3 2026, and in one week within six months after that.

3. **Terafab Structure and Intel Partnership Disclosed:** Tesla disclosed that its research semiconductor fab on the Giga Texas campus will be approximately a $3 billion initiative capable of producing a few thousand wafers per month, intended for R&D and testing new chip manufacturing physics. Intel will partner on the core manufacturing technology using Intel's 14A process node. SpaceX will handle the initial phase of the larger-scale Terafab. The research fab will uniquely integrate lithography mask creation, logic, memory, and packaging under one roof for rapid R&D iteration. The panel noted the complexity of the Tesla/SpaceX joint governance structure as a friction point.

4. **Tesla's $2 Billion SpaceX/XAI Equity Investment:** Tesla recorded a $2 billion equity investment in SpaceX (specifically XAI) during Q1, which was announced on approximately January 28, 2026. The panel debated whether this was included in the original $20 billion capex forecast.

5. **Tariffs Creating Cost Pressure Across Businesses:** CFO Taneja noted that both tariffs and sustained high interest rates continue to add to automotive costs. Interest rate subvention costs are recognized upfront. In the energy business, tariffs can have outsized impacts since most battery cells are sourced from China. Tesla is actively regionalizing and vertically integrating critical supply chains in response to trade and geopolitical uncertainty.
