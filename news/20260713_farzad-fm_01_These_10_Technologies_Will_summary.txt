## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
1. **Waymo scale vs. Tesla's vision-only bet:** The host states Waymo now runs about 500,000 paid robotaxi rides per week across roughly 10 US cities (up from ~50,000/week roughly two years earlier), at an annualized revenue run rate the host puts near $350 million, with Google guidance for profitability around 2027. Tesla is framed as taking a cheaper, camera-and-neural-network-only approach instead of LiDAR, having filed a Nevada permit for up to 5,000 vehicles; the host says Tesla's rollout has been "far slower than many expected" but considers the underlying vision-only technology now proven, citing Tesla coast-to-coast drives without driver intervention.
2. **Path to driverless normalization:** The host predicts that by the mid-2030s a car with no driver will feel as unremarkable as an elevator without an operator, framing both Waymo and Tesla as evidence the underlying self-driving technology is now workable at scale even though current systems remain geofenced and weather-sensitive.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Unit economics framed as the biggest technology shift of the decade:** The host estimates humanoid robots could cost $20,000–$30,000 per unit, yielding a fully loaded operating cost of roughly $1.50–$2 per hour versus about $40/hour for fully loaded human labor — a claimed 90%+ cost reduction — against a global physical labor market the host sizes at over $40 trillion/year.
2. **Tesla Optimus targets and competitive field:** The host cites Tesla's targets of roughly $20,000 per unit cost and up to 1 million Optimus units per year, with a factory eventually aimed at 10 million units annually, while noting competitors including Figure, Boston Dynamics, Agility, Apptronik, and Chinese manufacturers pursuing the same goal, enabled by recent AI breakthroughs in robotic perception and manipulation rather than hardware limits.
