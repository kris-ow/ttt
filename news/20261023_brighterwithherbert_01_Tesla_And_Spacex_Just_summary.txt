This panel discussion (Alexandra Merz, Jeff Lutz, and a third analyst referred to as "CERN") covers the formation of special merger committees at Tesla and SpaceX, SpaceX's dramatically raised revenue guidance, and new details from Tesla's earnings call on Robotaxi/Cybercab scaling, FSD growth rates, and Optimus. The throughline is that both companies appear to be moving faster on execution than their public guidance suggests, while the merger process remains legally constrained and slow.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Special committees confirmed, no agreement yet:** Both Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to explore "deepening Tesla's relationship with SpaceX," per Tesla's 10-Q filing, which explicitly includes "without limitation" a possible business combination. Elon Musk stated there is no agreement yet and no guarantee a merger happens, and said any deal would need a 50% vote of all outstanding Tesla shares (not just those voting). SpaceX's own 10-Q reportedly indicates no shareholder vote is needed there, since Musk holds a controlling >80% voting stake.
2. **Vote math is harder than it looks:** Alexandra Merz noted that although Musk holds roughly 20–28% of Tesla's vote, 15–20% of shares typically don't vote and about 11% are broker non-votes, meaning roughly 25–32% of the vote base is effectively unavailable before counting starts — making the needed 30% of the remaining shares harder to secure than a simple 80/20 split suggests. Merz also voiced growing concern that institutional shareholders may resist approving a merger because it would permanently cede their voting influence, contrasting with her more confident view "a couple of months ago."
3. **Possible December 2027 timing window:** Merz laid out a theory (her own speculation, not confirmed) that the "optimal" merger consummation date would coincide with Musk's ~$60 billion tax bill due January 19, 2028, a planned charitable donation, and SpaceX's S&P 500 inclusion — all of which only align on specific S&P inclusion dates, with December 2027 flagged as the most likely window given a merger process she was told takes 9–12 months. She cautioned this is speculative ("nobody really knows. This is guessing") and that China or EU regulatory approval could delay the timeline.
4. **Litigation risk framing:** Merz said she has observed a surge of Wall Street litigators and lawyers following her online in recent days, which she interprets as people positioning to build shareholder lawsuits around the merger process; she said Musk must now be extremely cautious publicly ("no likes, no comments, no nothing") until an agreement is announced.
5. **Proposed exchange ratio is speculative:** Merz reiterated her own hoped-for (not confirmed) Tesla-to-SpaceX conversion ratio of 4.2, which she calculated would represent roughly a 61% premium to Tesla's price at time of recording — higher than the 30–40% premiums some others (including a figure she attributed to "Brett Kersner" at 40%) have floated. She stressed this is her preference/guess, not a reported figure, and that she does not expect the market to actually land there.
6. **SpaceX revenue guidance jump:** Elon Musk said SpaceX could end 2027 with an annual recurring revenue run rate of $500 billion to $1 trillion, up from a range of $300–500 billion he reportedly stated at an internal all-hands meeting on August 11th. SpaceX is also guiding to $33–37 billion in revenue in Q1 of next year. Panelists noted SpaceX was at roughly $18 billion in revenue six months prior.
7. **Data center buildout and spot pricing:** Panelists discussed SpaceX's "miniyard" data center sites in Texas (near Starbase/former Brownsville land), describing them as a productized, repeatable design that takes about five months to build. One site has three miniyards under construction with room for up to ten more, with each potentially generating $30–50 billion in annual revenue if spot power pricing holds. SpaceX reportedly secured turbine/blade-and-vane manufacturing capacity itself and bought out production capacity of at least one external supplier, which panelists say gives it leverage to secure "matched sets" of cooling, Megapacks, and turbines ahead of competitors.
8. **Megapack-SpaceX link (speculative):** One analyst estimated, based on rough unverified math, that roughly 10 gigawatts of power might require 2,500–5,000 Megapacks, cautioning "don't hold me to it." Panelists speculated SpaceX is a major buyer of Tesla's Megapack output, including a new Houston Megapack factory with an estimated 50-gigawatt capacity, positioned geographically between Starbase, Texas and a potential future Starbase Louisiana site.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
1. **Customer-owned Cybercabs targeted for Q2 next year:** Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy said Cybercab and Model Y run the same software stack, soon to be released to customers as part of a V15 software release; he said Tesla expects to turn on the first customer-owned vehicles joining the robotaxi fleet around Q2 of next year, with most of the fleet still company-owned at that point.
2. **Cybercabs are transport-as-a-service, not personal-use vehicles:** Elon Musk and Lars Moravy both indicated Cybercab will be primarily offered as a transport-as-a-service product rather than a personally driven car, with Tesla aiming to deliver the first Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, though nearly all current production is going to Tesla's own robotaxi fleet for now.
3. **Expansion cities named:** Panelists said Elon Musk indicated Cybercab/robotaxi service will next expand to Dallas, with additional cities including Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio also named for expansion.
4. **Autonomous mile growth accelerating:** One analyst calculated week-over-week growth in Tesla's reported autonomous/paid-autonomous miles has been accelerating across three data points: ~17.1% week-over-week from July 22 to September 3 (reaching 1 million miles), ~17.7% from September 3 to September 30 (reaching ~1.875 million miles), and ~20.7% from September 30 to October 21 (reaching 3.3 million miles) — an average of about 18.1% but trending toward 21%.
5. **Possible Cybercab production ceiling by April:** A third-party analysis (attributed by the panel to "GMA JP") suggested that if Cybercab production capacity does not exceed a stated 125,000-unit level and current mileage growth rates continue, Tesla could hit a production ceiling as early as April of next year. Lars Moravy was quoted as saying Tesla has been "bursting" the Cybercab line to test its throughput limits.
6. **Safety bar set at zero incidents:** Panelists said Tesla's stated goal is zero people injured and zero robotaxi airbag deployments or pedestrian/cyclist strikes since the program launched, which one panelist characterized as an extremely high bar that will be difficult to sustain at scale.
7. **European FSD expansion:** Panelists noted FSD is live in nine European countries, and Elon Musk said he would be surprised if EU-wide approval took longer than Q1. China progress on FSD/Optimus was described as comparatively stalled, with Merz speculating (her own theory, flagged as speculation) that delaying Chinese FSD approval may be a deliberate negotiating card tied to merger-related regulatory approval from China.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **China manufacturing status unclear:** Merz noted there has been no confirmation of an Optimus production line in Berlin or Shanghai, contrasting with an active bot line in Fremont and plans referenced for a much larger-scale line in Austin; she raised this as an open question for how a Tesla-SpaceX merger agreement might treat Chinese regulatory approval.

## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Tesla Q3 results characterized as strong amid mixed reaction:** Jeff Lutz said Tesla reported its highest order backlog in over four years, auto gross margins met or beat expectations (beating when excluding tariff rebates), expanding factory capacity, low inventory, and energy storage sold out through nearly 2027, alongside a cash balance nearly $5 billion above expectations. He said the muted stock reaction partly reflects Tesla not providing numerical financial guidance (unlike SpaceX's explicit $500B–$1T figure), despite what he called improving execution and credibility.
