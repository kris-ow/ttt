This episode of "brighterwithherbert" features host Bert and guest Jeff Lutz — a multi-decade supply chain executive and CEO of a high-tech manufacturing consulting firm — covering a busy weekend of Tesla-related developments. The central themes are SpaceX's IPO amendment hinting at a potential Tesla acquisition, the rapid expansion of FSD approvals across Europe, Texas regulatory authorization of Tesla's driverless commercial operations, and mounting infrastructure signals pointing toward imminent robotaxi scaling.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX S-1 Amendment Hints at Strategic Acquisitions:** SpaceX filed an amendment to its Form S-1 ahead of a planned IPO on June 12th, adding new language stating it "may issue a significant amount of equity in connection with future transactions" — notably using the plural "transactions." Bert and Jeff Lutz highlight this as a meaningful hint at a potential Tesla acquisition or merger, since the Cursor deal was already known when the original S-1 dropped, suggesting the new language covers additional undisclosed deals. Jeff emphasizes the current administration's first half as a favorable window for strategic tie-ups across tech. Elon Musk has agreed to a 366-day lockup on all his SpaceX shares following the IPO, while 5% of IPO shares have been reserved for employees, friends, and family of executive officers with no lockup period.

2. **Tesla's Competitive Moat vs. AI Hardware Cycle:** Jeff Lutz argues that Tesla and SpaceX occupy uniquely durable competitive positions compared to AI hardware plays like memory chip manufacturers, which face cyclical risk and multiple competitors. He contends no company has a consumer-facing autonomy solution comparable to FSD, no one has a robotaxi network with Tesla's cost structure and volume, and no one has anything matching Starship or Starlink — making Tesla's high valuation multiple defensible. He draws a parallel to how Nvidia's AI infrastructure thesis confounded bears like Michael Burry, who predicted an imminent crash in November/December but was proven wrong as Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron all reached all-time highs. Jeff suggests physical real-world AI at scale may be one of the biggest market events in history, and that markets may not yet be ready for millions of autonomous cars and reasoning robots.

3. **Near-Term Milestone Slippage vs. Long-Term Thesis Intact:** Jeff candidly acknowledges Tesla has missed several near-term milestones — including robotaxi deployment targets, the Optimus reveal (originally suggested for March), and the Roadster — noting a pattern of delays. However, he argues the key question is whether short-term slippage is delaying the long-term vision (1 million Optimus per year, 10 million per year eventually, large-scale robotaxi deployment), and that current hiring and infrastructure investment signals suggest the long-term trajectory remains intact. He cautions against expecting Tesla's physical AI products to be "printing revenue" in Terafab-scale numbers before 2027–2028.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised Approved in Estonia — Third European Country:** The Estonian Transport Authority approved FSD supervised driving effective May 29th, following the Netherlands and Lithuania. Belgium's Minister of Transport, Anek Ditter, publicly stated that "if all goes well, approval will follow in the coming weeks," noting Belgium has already completed the required 5,000 kilometers of testing. Tesla executive for AI, Yantasai, responded "Welcome to the autonomy future." Tesla is also hiring vehicle operators in Slovenia and Austria, which Yoda (a commentator cited in the episode) interprets as a signal those countries may be next. Jeff Lutz predicts clusters of European approvals throughout the summer, describing the regulatory process as largely a "paperwork loop" now that the Netherlands approval provides a template other EU member states can leverage.

2. **World's First Fully Autonomous Coast-to-Coast Drive Across Canada:** David Moss, Devin Olsen, and Scott completed what is described as the world's first fully autonomous coast-to-coast drive across Canada using Tesla FSD, starting at Horseshoe Bay in West Vancouver and ending in Nova Scotia. The trip covered 3,700 miles over 4 days and 21 hours, accomplished using FSD version 14.3 with zero disengagements — including in parking situations. The hosts note this is now the fourth coast-to-coast autonomous drive in the past 5 months (Los Angeles to Myrtle Beach, New York to Los Angeles, New York to Los Angeles again, and Vancouver to Halifax), totaling 12,427 miles across all four trips, all performed in regular production vehicles with no special modifications. Bert and Jeff compare the significance of this achievement to AlphaGo or GPT, with David Moss himself quoted as saying it is "a bigger step change than AlphaGo or GPT as there's no special machine behind it."

3. **FSD Daily Miles Surging Per Morgan Stanley:** In a new research note, Morgan Stanley estimates Tesla owners are now driving 26.7 million miles per day with FSD, up from 18.5 million miles per day in April — a significant jump in just a couple of months. Morgan Stanley states this metric has bearing on FSD adoption and, given FSD's approximately 90% gross margins, can meaningfully uplift Tesla's profitability. The note also cites normalized NHTSA data implying improvement in robotaxi miles per accident, and Morgan Stanley's AlphaWise data shows increased hiring in northern metro areas for AI safety operators — which they interpret as a sign Tesla is gearing up to expand robotaxi operations.

4. **FSD Reasoning Capabilities Highlighted:** Jeff Lutz shares a personal FSD reasoning anecdote: while attempting an unprotected left turn, his Tesla detected a cyclist on the sidewalk approximately 50 feet away, shifted itself into reverse to clear the crosswalk, waited for the cyclist to pass, then completed the turn — all without input. Ashok Elluswami separately shared a video of a Tesla navigating extremely tight corners in China, including backing up to reposition, demonstrating FSD operating without pre-mapped routes in a challenging real-world environment.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Drives Itself Out of Giga Texas:** Elon Musk posted a video of the first Cybercab driving itself out of Giga Texas unassisted — exiting the factory with no steering wheel and parking itself in the lot. Ashok Elluswami added that "soon it will drive itself into Austin and report for duty," which Bert interprets as a direct statement of intent for Cybercab robotaxi deployment in Austin. Joe Tegtmeyer documented Cybercabs making autonomous two-mile trips to the west end of the Giga Texas lot, and multiple videos show dozens of Cybercabs lined up at the factory with hatch covers open and wheel covers staged for shipping.

2. **Texas Authorizes Tesla for Driverless Commercial Operations at Level 4:** Effective May 28th, under Texas's new driverless vehicle law, Tesla received authorization from the State of Texas to operate driverless vehicles commercially, self-certifying as Level 4. James Stevenson is credited with finding this regulatory filing. Jeff Lutz dismisses prior bear arguments that Tesla would fail to qualify under the new law, calling such concerns predictable and noting Tesla had clearly been working toward this qualification.

3. **Robotaxi Fleet and Infrastructure Build Signals:** The hosts reference the active robotaxi fleet count — citing approximately 700 total active robotaxis (per RobotaxiTracker.com) versus Waymo's approximately 3,700 — while noting Waymo started its first driverless rides about six years ago. Jeff argues Tesla's rollout is the fastest in history in context, and that the relevant signal is not the current fleet count but the silent infrastructure build: service centers, charging stalls, wash facilities, and now fleet supervisor hiring across multiple cities. New job postings show Tesla hiring robotaxi fleet supervisors (in addition to AI safety operators) across multiple cities. Jeff envisions a future where Cybercabs drive autonomously from the factory to city depots, self-charge wirelessly, and are distributed at a scale that fundamentally shifts the personal car ownership vs. ride-share ratio.

4. **Driverless (Unsupervised) Fleet Context:** The hosts note that not all ~700 active robotaxis are fully driverless/unsupervised, and suggest the current relative plateau in driverless vehicle numbers is tied to an anticipated upcoming software release that will address remaining edge cases — after which they expect the unsupervised fleet count to move up significantly.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **European Tesla Sales Skyrocket in May:** Tesla registrations surged dramatically across Europe in May, with France up 655%, Denmark up 136%, Spain, Sweden, and Norway all showing increases. Jeff attributes the growth to multiple converging factors: the new Model Y now being in inventory (vs. supply constraints in the comparable period of 2025), falling brand-damage headwinds, high European gasoline prices (compounded by energy import dependency), Tesla's strong net promoter score driving word-of-mouth, and FSD approval adding incremental stickiness. He also notes that electrified vehicle registrations broadly (including BEV, plug-in hybrid, and hybrid) rose approximately 21% in Europe in April and made up more than two-thirds of total regional registrations.

2. **Camouflaged Model Y L Spotted Testing at Fremont:** A camouflaged vehicle believed to be the longer-wheelbase Model Y (Model Y L) was observed testing at the Fremont factory. Jeff Lutz, drawing on his supply chain background, explains that even though the car is already built in other countries, US production requires requalification of locally sourced suppliers and localization work on component databases. He describes US launch as "imminent" and expected in the second half of 2026. Bert notes this would give consumers more choice and extend the already dominant Model Y lineup.
