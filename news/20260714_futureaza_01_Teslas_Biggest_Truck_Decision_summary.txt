The video, hosted by Brian (Futureaza) with guest Randy Kirk, debates whether Tesla should build a smaller/cheaper pickup truck given persistent rumors and weak Cybertruck sales, while surveying upcoming low-cost electric trucks from Ford, Telo, and Slate as market signals for what compact EV trucks can realistically achieve.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck sales weakness and pricing dilemma:** The host argues Cybertruck isn't "doomed" but needs a lower price; he suggests Tesla faces a decision in the coming months on whether to hold or raise Cybertruck's price as new competing trucks roll out. Randy Kirk notes Cybertruck's "look at me" styling drew backlash during a period when Musk faced intense public criticism, which he believes likely dampened sales, and questions whether a larger, more attention-grabbing truck can help sales recover.
2. **Lars Moravy comments on a smaller Cybertruck:** The host cites Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy as having said in an interview roughly a year ago that Tesla has discussed making a smaller pickup as Cybertruck sales struggled, calling it something Tesla has "always talked about."
3. **Discontinued small Tesla pickup comparison:** The host references a smaller, discontinued Tesla-adjacent pickup that sold just over 10,000 units despite a low price and good range/utility, arguing this poor performance undercuts the case for Tesla building a compact truck, noting the Ford Maverick outsold it by a wide margin.
4. **Ford's upcoming electric truck (2028, ~$30,000):** Ford is developing a midsize electric pickup (informally referenced, name not finalized) targeted for around 2028 at roughly $30,000 on a universal EV platform. Randy Kirk is skeptical Ford can profit at that price, citing battery cost (likely at least 100 kWh pack) as a major expense despite Ford's efforts to onshore battery capacity; Ford has not disclosed cost details and tends to limit early access to influencers rather than analysts.
5. **Telo's MT1 compact truck:** Telo is on track to deliver its MT1 truck by the end of this year, featuring a usable bed, compact footprint, range up to 350 miles, and a starting price around $41,500. Telo is outsourcing body-in-white manufacturing to a Midwest company to avoid needing its own full assembly line.
6. **Slate's $25,000 truck and financial risk:** Slate is targeting a base price of about $25,000 (roughly $25,950 with delivery; destination fee unconfirmed, host estimates around $1,100), with a Crayola partnership for wrap colors, and is described by Munro Live as an early investor/engineering partner that spun Slate out. Randy Kirk expresses concern Slate will lose money at that price point given limited production scale and lack of full vertical integration, and both speakers doubt aftermarket accessory sales (a key profit source) will materialize if sold to fleets or lower-income markets.
7. **Rivian secondary offering and stock reaction:** Rivian shares fell (the host says about 13% intraday) after announcing a public offering plan of roughly $1.5 billion despite an upbeat revenue forecast; the stock has since rebounded, and the market reportedly absorbed the raise easily.
8. **Debate over demand for small/cheap trucks:** Both speakers question whether compact trucks have real US demand, arguing most American truck buyers use them for image/lifestyle rather than utility, and that "small" no longer means "cheap" due to fixed costs (computers, airbags, crashworthiness) regardless of vehicle size. They note stronger potential use cases in Latin America and Mexico (denser roads, weaker charging infrastructure) and suggest a government/fleet motorpool use case (e.g., Parks and Rec-style fleets) could suit a smaller Tesla truck, though fleet buyers wouldn't purchase margin-rich accessories.
9. **Speculation on Tesla's next vehicle direction:** Randy Kirk says he sees low likelihood of Tesla building a smaller pickup, viewing other vehicles as higher priority; he and the host discuss a "cyber SUV" as more likely than a smaller truck or a work van, citing shrinking van market demand (referencing the discontinued Ford Aerostar) and declining minivan popularity as families have fewer children.
