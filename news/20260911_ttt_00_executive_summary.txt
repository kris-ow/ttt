## Brief

- Cybercab now publicly hailable in Austin, wait times ~1 hour
- NHTSA opens Cybercab audit query same day as launch
- Musk amplifies FSD misuse video, adding lawsuit exposure
- SpaceX: new $1.11B/month compute deal, ~$100B ARR target by year-end
- Tesla, SpaceX reveal European Semi specs ahead of Hannover launch
- Boring Company raises $3B Series D at $23B valuation

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab became publicly hailable in Austin the Friday after last week's launch event; riders describe it as extremely smooth and quiet. Texas DMV registrations rose from 45 on launch day to 49 within days, with public demand reportedly pushing wait times to about an hour.
- Tesla posted a fleet-operator signup form (no financial commitment) covering vehicle purchasing, mobility hubs, and deployment region, which panelists characterize as demand-gauging rather than a launch of consumer or fleet sales.
- One commentator argues Tesla's real moat is FSD software, app, and remote-ops infrastructure — not vehicle ownership — making a lease/sell model to third-party fleet operators (echoing Waymo's Avis arrangement) plausible, though not expected at scale until FSD V15 is broadly deployed and NHTSA's audit query resolves.
- Starlink hardware confirmed installed in production Cybercabs coming out of Giga Texas, per in-car Grok assistant confirmation; Wi-Fi login wasn't yet functional.
- A rider ("Dirty Tesla") was banned from the robotaxi service for seven days after stepping in front of a Cybercab to test its response.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA opened an audit query into Cybercab's federal safety compliance on September 3rd, the same day as the public launch — described by one commentator as a routine compliance step, contrasting with Zoox's longer, more adversarial NHTSA history.
- Musk quote-tweeted a viral video (9M+ views) of a 98-year-old Model Y owner saying he no longer watches the road while using FSD ("I trust it 100%"); Electrek argues this undercuts Tesla's driver-supervision defense in ongoing crash litigation, citing a $243M Florida jury verdict and up to $14.5B in total lawsuit exposure across roughly two dozen cases.
- Ashok Elluswamy previewed FSD V15 as bringing earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction time, and "significantly better safety and collision avoidance," per one host.
- Germany's KBA is reportedly weighing an exemption route to recognize FSD ahead of a scheduled vote at the TCMBB meeting roughly a month out; Tesla Japan says its near-term priority is getting FSD itself legalized in Japan this year, with Cybercab approval a separate later step.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla published European Semi specs ahead of its Hannover IAA launch: up to 550 km range at 40 tonnes, 800 kW Megacharger charging (~60% recovery in 30 min), 9,100 kg curb weight — no price or on-sale date given. Electrek flags the curb weight matches the US Standard Range spec, suggesting the 550 km figure may not represent a long-range pack; manufacturing location (Nevada vs. Berlin) remains unresolved.
- Terafab construction is underway in Grimes County, with foundation work, footing excavation, and grading visible via drone footage.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus V3's public demo has reportedly slipped again, now pushed toward closer to production start, which speakers attribute to preventing competitors from copying the design.
- Jason Calacanis described a private Optimus demo as far ahead of a recent humanoid robot competition, claiming hardware "one and a half generations" ahead of Unitree/X-Humanoid and software roughly "five generations" ahead — an unverified claim the hosts treat skeptically as likely rehearsed.
- A dedicated Optimus Gigafactory, under construction since around March, is estimated at six to eight more months to complete the structure.

## Energy
- Tesla launched Powerwall 3–Cybertruck PowerShare integration, extending home backup by over three days (Cybertruck's ~123 kWh pack vs. Powerwall 3's ~13.5 kWh); Powerwall 2 Plus compatibility is coming later this year.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX signed a new compute hosting deal worth $1.11B/month starting December 1, 2026 (~$13B incremental ARR), reinforcing its guided ~$100B ARR target by year-end 2026 versus under $19B in 2025 companywide revenue.
- Compute leasing prices at $30–$50/watt, with SpaceX near the high end; CFO says multi-year-old compute chips are holding lease value better than expected.
- Orbital compute satellites targeted to fly next year, aiming for tens of gigawatts by 2028; terrestrial compute ended this year at just over 2 GW, guided to 5–10 GW next year.
- Grok 4.7 delayed a few days per Musk, citing reinforcement-learning tuning issues around response length and self-checking rigor.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 13 achieved a precise ocean splashdown with the vehicle towed back for direct heat-shield inspection; Flight 14, planned later this month, will be the first revenue-generating flight, carrying production V3 Starlink satellites.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink broadband is live in over 170 countries; three of four largest global airlines are now customers. Full 5G-quality direct-to-device service is targeted for first-half 2028 in the US, built on next-gen satellites reusing the V3 bus.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- The Boring Company raised a $3B Series D led by the UAE at a $23B valuation.
- Analysis (Alexandra Merz) argues a Tesla–SpaceX merger announcement window is closing within roughly five weeks to preserve eligibility for S&P 500 inclusion at the September 2027 rebalancing, tied to Texas shareholder-meeting timing rules.
