The article reports that Milan Kovac, Tesla's former senior vice president and head of the Optimus humanoid robot program, has been hired by Hyundai's Boston Dynamics as a group adviser and outside director, per a KED Global report.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Kovac's Tesla background and departure:** Kovac, a Belgian national, joined Tesla in 2016, led development of Tesla's second-generation Autopilot system from 2019 to 2022, then led Optimus factory pilot operations before being promoted to senior vice president and head of Optimus in 2024. He left Tesla in June 2025, a departure the article says sent the Optimus program into disarray and forced a production delay due to an unexpected redesign.

2. **New Hyundai/Boston Dynamics role:** Hyundai appointed Kovac as group adviser and outside director for Boston Dynamics on Friday, according to the KED Global report. The article frames this as both a blow to Tesla and a coup for Hyundai's humanoid robot ambitions.

3. **Other recent Hyundai hires:** Earlier the same week (Tuesday), Hyundai appointed Park Minwoo, described as a former Tesla and Nvidia engineer, to lead its Advanced Vehicle Platform division and its mobility software unit 42dot.

4. **Boston Dynamics' Atlas progress:** The article notes Boston Dynamics unveiled its next-generation electric Atlas humanoid robot at CES 2026, demonstrated operating autonomously inside Hyundai factories (contrasted with Tesla's Optimus demos, which the article says have primarily relied on human teleoperation). The article states Boston Dynamics, backed by Hyundai and integrated with Google DeepMind, has started Atlas production, with all capacity going to Hyundai in 2026 and shipments to other customers starting in 2027. Hyundai reportedly plans to deploy tens of thousands of Atlas robots into its manufacturing facilities and is building a robotics factory with capacity for 30,000 robots per year, part of a stated $26 billion US investment.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla talent losses cited by the author:** The author's commentary notes Tesla has lost other Optimus-related talent, specifically citing AI team lead Ashish Kumar's departure to Meta, as part of a broader pattern of key personnel leaving the program.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Kovac's move to a direct competitor is a damning signal about internal dysfunction in Tesla's Optimus program, asserting there is no evidence Tesla holds any lead in humanoid robotics, that Musk's Optimus production goals have consistently missed targets, and that losing the person meant to lead Optimus to Hyundai undercuts Musk's claims that Optimus will justify Tesla's valuation.
