## Brief

- Starship survives ocean splashdown intact, still transmitting via Starlink
- In-space Raptor relight demonstrated on latest Starship flight
- Tesla robotaxi runs FSD v15 fleet-wide, seven upgrades over v14
- Q2 revenue beats at $28.2B, EPS misses at $0.33 vs $0.50
- Tesla operating cash flow hits record $18.7B TTM, up 13.1% YoY
- Tesla secures new $30 billion debt facility

## Financials
- Q2 results were mixed: revenue beat at $28.2 billion while EPS missed at $0.33 versus a $0.50 estimate; the stock sold off sharply post-earnings, which one analyst attributes to higher capex and the lack of robotaxi updates rather than the headline numbers (per jobhakdi).
- Trailing-12-month operating cash flow hit an all-time high of $18.7 billion, up 13.1% year-over-year, per a social-media post cited by jobhakdi — cash he argues is being funneled into Optimus, robotaxi/Cybercab scaling, and Terafab.
- Tesla secured a new $30 billion borrowing facility; Q2 capex was $6 billion, with cash falling only $1.2 billion to roughly $40 billion on hand as the operating business funded much of the spend (Larry Goldberg, brighterwithherbert). Goldberg predicts full-year capex will exceed Tesla's ~$25 billion guidance and long-term debt could reach ~$50 billion within a year, absent a Tesla-SpaceX merger.
- Musk reportedly thanked Micron and Nvidia on the earnings call for securing Tesla a large, reasonably priced memory allocation despite what he called insane current memory pricing — framed by the host as a supply-chain edge over other "Mag 7" companies.

## Robotaxi
- Four Tesla executives gave four angles on why the robotaxi fleet remains small: Lars Moravy cited city-by-city regulatory clearance, Vaibhav Taneja cited operational kinks (charging, cleaning, support) needing to be solved at smaller scale, Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is deliberately proving generality across many cities rather than doubling down on one, and Musk said scaling proceeds while trying to ensure zero harm to humans or even pets.
- Ashok said Tesla has driven roughly 380,000 fully unsupervised miles with zero notable incidents (only other vehicles bumping into stationary Teslas recorded), and that Tesla expects to eventually roll out to entire states at once rather than city by city — a claim the host says no other autonomy company makes.
- By comparison, Waymo has logged over 220 million rider miles through March versus Tesla's roughly 2.5 million total paid robotaxi miles (compounding ~10%/week); Waymo has filed ~1,400 NHTSA incident reports since 2021 with 117 injuries and two fatal crashes (Waymo was the struck vehicle in both), plus a recall covering 1,200+ vehicles.
- The host notes Tesla has missed its own robotaxi guidance for three consecutive earnings calls, including an earlier target of covering the US population.
- One analyst (jobhakdi) said comments from Musk suggest robotaxi/Cybercab expansion is delayed and he's "getting less bullish" on a near-term inflection point.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD version 15 is now deployed on the robotaxi fleet, cited as carrying seven major improvements over version 14; Musk reiterated Tesla's camera-only approach is validated as safe for functional autonomy, though no executive specified the validation milestone required to trigger broader scale-up.
- Cortex 2 compute capacity is now over 115 megawatts (roughly doubling over the recent quarter), with its first half currently dedicated to FSD training rather than Optimus, supporting a new FSD version with a roughly 10x parameter increase; the second half of the building, still under construction, is expected to roughly double total capacity to about a quarter-gigawatt by year-end (Larry Goldberg).

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus V3 production was said to be starting around this quarter at Fremont, feeding an "Optimus Academy" reinforcement-learning program where bots train other bots; Ashok described the method as "pixels in, controls out," the same recipe as FSD.
- Ashok claimed Optimus's hand dexterity equals or exceeds a human hand — flagged by the host as unverified pending video evidence.
- Fremont's former Model S/X lines are being converted for Optimus production later this year; Musk cautioned the ramp will be slow given roughly 10,000 unique parts that could each gate the line.
- One host theorizes V3 is a "training generation" for the Academy while a redesigned V4, built in Austin, will be the manufacturing-scale generation timed with AI5 chip volume production, which Musk said is hoped for next year.

## SpaceX — Starship
- A Starship test flight saw the vehicle survive ocean splashdown intact, remaining afloat and still transmitting live video via Starlink — described as an unexpectedly positive durability outcome (per Sawyer Merritt's digest).
- SpaceX released a new camera angle showing Super Heavy booster separation, and a follower-captured video from South Africa reportedly shows an in-space Raptor engine relight, a key milestone for future orbital maneuvering and Mars-transit capability.
- A brief, unconfirmed teaser suggests the next Starship flight could attempt a tower catch of the booster and/or ship.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX is preparing further Colossus data-center buildout phases that one analyst says could meaningfully raise EBIT and materially improve valuation if realized this year (jobhakdi).
- Separately, Goldberg argues xAI/SpaceX's faster data-center builds let it deploy cutting-edge chips roughly a year ahead of rivals using conventional subcontracted buildouts, with those chips lasting longer than expected (~6 years vs. a predicted 2–3).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's valuation remains rich at roughly 500x earnings despite a recent stock price drop and heavy share-unlock-driven selling pressure; one analyst says he remains unwilling to buy but believes Colossus earnings could lower the effective multiple toward ~50x if realized this year (jobhakdi).
