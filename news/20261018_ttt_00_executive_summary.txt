## Humanoid Bots
- Elon said fine motor skills in the Optimus hand remain "shockingly difficult," demonstrating screw-driving and jar-opening tests live at a Giga Texas X Takeover; he said no hand design, including demos, can yet do these tasks and called most competitor robot demos staged, not real AI.
- China's magnet export ban set Optimus back two to three months by leaving Tesla temporarily without functioning hands, per Elon, who said Tesla needs domestic production capability regardless of Chinese supply.
- Tesla halved AI5 chip RAM (now 72GB LPDDR5) and cut AI6 memory to a third (144GB LPDDR6) specifically to free up chip volume for Optimus production, per Elon.
- New Austin job postings for "Optimus General Assembly" process engineers and an "Optimus Park Coordinator" point toward an approaching assembly and fleet-management ramp.
- Chinese supplier Sinjian Transmission's ChiNext filing claims Optimus V3 hardware design is essentially frozen and targeted for mass production by end of 2026; other supplier claims (GMN's 5,000-unit order, The Paper's three-supplier report) remain unconfirmed or denied.
- Giga Texas's Optimus plant (just under 7M sq ft) is about 40% complete, with main steel structures expected finished by end of Q1 2027; estimates point to limited Texas production beginning late next year, alongside a Fremont line already under construction.
- Production estimates diverge sharply: The Information cites a few dozen/week in Q2 rising to several hundred/week by August with a 1,000/week year-end internal goal; JP Morgan expects commercial sales in H2 2027; Oppenheimer's Colin Rush expects limited revenue until 2029 and likely ramp delays.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Investor Larry Goldberg's Monte Carlo model (20,000 simulations) pegs SpaceX base-case value at $322/share, high case $1,337, low case near $8, with median $228 versus a $156 close; orbital compute capex-per-watt is by far the largest value swing factor (~$560/share).
- Model shows SpaceX needing outside funding (median gap ~$77–80B) by end of 2028 in all simulated paths, with cash hitting zero around Q3–Q4 2027; a rumored $40B credit facility was excluded as unconfirmed.
- Musk reportedly retweeted Goldberg's analysis but said he expects SpaceX revenue to reach the trillions within under ten years, implying greater bullishness than the model.
- SpaceX's S-1 reportedly splits the company into three business lines, with launch the smallest by revenue; Starlink generates nearly 3x launch revenue and is described as the only consistently profitable segment.
- A roughly 1.3B-share unlock (~40% of float) plus a second ~328M-share tranche — combined ~1.6B shares, about half the float — is expected within days of Q3 earnings, per Bhakdi, who predicts an earnings-day rally followed by unlock-driven selling.
- Merger speculation persists after SpaceX's earnings date moved up to land one day before Tesla's; commentators (Bhakdi, davelee's guest Emmett) continue to float a Tesla-SpaceX merger as upside catalyst, though this remains speculative and unconfirmed.

## Robotaxi
- Firsthand rider accounts (futureaza, davelee) describe markedly improved Cybercab experience in Austin — praised in-cabin "Hey Grok" integration, power doors, and screen versus the Model Y robotaxi, though highway-avoidance routing, imprecise climate control, bench-seat design, and lack of a charging port remain cited friction points.
- Airport pickup/dropoff remains blocked by permitting, not technology, affecting both Tesla and Waymo similarly, per riders.
- Bhakdi frames Cybercab fleet scaling as Tesla's key near-term stock catalyst independent of the earnings print: roughly 500 Cybercabs by December could support $400–420, scaling to ~2,000 could support ~$500.

## Financials

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk said SpaceX and Nvidia's jointly engineered "Starmind" computer is "far better than almost anyone expects" and will reach volume production next year; Nvidia's Jensen Huang confirmed joint engineering of a server architecture for space and Earth use.
- SpaceX targets first AI satellites launching late 2027 with significant scale in 2028, building a "gigasat" factory in Bastrop, Texas aiming for a gigawatt of orbital compute per year, scaling 10x annually thereafter (Musk's stated aspiration).
- Google researchers estimate meaningful orbital compute scale requires ~1,800 Starship launches over 10 years; Starship has never flown more than five times in a year, and Flight 14 (Sept 28) marked its first orbital reach before an early return from engine loss.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Mars launch windows recur only every ~26 months due to orbital mechanics, versus three-day Moon transit times, explaining SpaceX's apparent prioritization of Moon missions this year, per a farzad-fm explainer (its own interpretation, not a stated SpaceX position).
- Musk has discussed sending Starship to Mars in large "flotillas" of potentially a thousand-plus ships in future windows, framed as long-term vision rather than near-term plan.
