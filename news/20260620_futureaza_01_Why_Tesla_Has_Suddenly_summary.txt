This video features FutureAzA host Brian and guest Dr. Knowit All discussing why Tesla has gone quiet about Optimus, speculating on the key technical and strategic obstacles facing the humanoid robot program. The central thesis is that Tesla's silence is not necessarily a bad sign — the conversion of the Fremont Model S/X line to Optimus production and the under-construction Austin mega-factory are interpreted as the most meaningful positive signals — but that significant unsolved problems remain, particularly around hand dexterity and the AI hardware required to run a humanoid robot brain.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Tesla's silence on Optimus and what it signals:** The hosts note that it has been roughly a year and a half since a meaningful Optimus public update — with the last substantive demo dating to approximately October 2024. The guest expected Tesla to respond when Figure completed what was advertised as an eight-hour shift but reportedly stretched to around 200 hours of continuous operation sorting objects, yet Tesla made no public response. The hosts speculate that if Tesla had something compelling to show, they would show it, and therefore the silence suggests the program is still in active development rather than near-complete.

2. **The hand as the central unsolved problem:** Dr. Knowit All identifies the robot hand as the primary bottleneck — both for the humanoid robot industry broadly and for Tesla specifically. No consensus exists on whether the correct approach uses tendons, miniature actuators in each joint, a combination, or something entirely novel. He notes that Agility Robotics has partially sidestepped the problem with simpler end effectors, and Chinese competitors tend to showcase mobility (martial arts, jumping) while largely avoiding highly dexterous hands. He references Elon Musk's response approximately one month prior (implying approximately May 2026) to newly published Tesla hand patents, in which Musk reportedly acknowledged that the patented approach "didn't work" — suggesting Tesla is already on a fourth or fifth iteration of the hand design.

3. **Fremont line conversion and Austin mega-factory as positive signals:** The hosts view Tesla's decision to stop producing the Model S and Model X at Fremont — freeing that line for Optimus pilot production — as a significant and deliberate commitment, noting it carries emotional weight given the Model S's historic importance to Tesla. They also reference a large-scale Optimus factory under construction in Austin. Their interpretation is that Tesla would not halt a revenue-generating vehicle line or build a facility of that scale without having something real to build.

4. **Earlier public events reframed as recruitment, not product demos:** The hosts discuss that Tesla's large Optimus showcase events — including one the guest attended, described as an autonomy day-style event that featured the Cybercab and also included a robot hand demonstration — were framed by Tesla as recruitment tools rather than product announcements. The hosts interpret Tesla's current silence on public demos as consistent with having already recruited the talent it needed and wanting to reduce competitive intelligence leakage, referencing Elon Musk's stated concern that public demos give competitors a blueprint to copy.

5. **AI5 hardware as the brain for Optimus:** Dr. Knowit All explains that Tesla is diverging from the rest of the humanoid robot industry, which has largely standardized on Nvidia's Jetson and Thor compute platforms. Tesla is committed to its own AI hardware. He describes the current in-car chip (AI4, formerly called Hardware 4) as sufficient for autonomous driving — which he characterizes as a comparatively simpler sensing and decision-making task confined essentially to one plane with the goal of avoiding contact. By contrast, Optimus must sense and interact with a far more complex three-dimensional world, handle balance, dexterity, and interpret an enormous variety of objects and contexts. He states that AI5 (and eventually AI6) hardware is specifically designed for the humanoid form factor, and suggests AI5 may not even be prioritized for vehicles because AI4 is already adequate for driving.

6. **Early-deployment safety considerations:** The hosts discuss whether early Optimus units will require lockout zones analogous to those used around industrial robots. Dr. Knowit All believes the industry goal is to avoid hard physical cages but expects early versions will employ virtual safety envelopes — effectively stopping or pausing operation if a human comes within a defined proximity (he uses the phrase "invisible hula hoop"). He points to recent incidents from Chinese robot companies where units unexpectedly struck people, and notes that U.S. product liability law makes such incidents commercially unacceptable.

7. **Raw materials as an industry-wide constraint:** Beyond Tesla specifically, Dr. Knowit All flags raw material supply as a foundational challenge for the entire humanoid robot category — getting sufficient quantities of materials needed for actuators, batteries, and motors.

8. **Near-term financial headwinds from Optimus and infrastructure buildout:** The guest warns that in the short term, Tesla's financials may look stressed because capital is being consumed by Optimus manufacturing buildout, robotaxi and Cybercab fleet construction (before those vehicles generate revenue), and the ongoing buildout of Cortex (Colossus) compute infrastructure (referencing what he calls Cortex one and two). He characterizes this as a smoke-before-fire situation — negative-looking near-term financials as a leading indicator of large future bets, not evidence of failure.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **AI4 adequate for FSD/robotaxi; AI5 targeted at Optimus:** Dr. Knowit All states that Elon Musk has indicated AI4 is essentially good enough for on-road autonomous driving already, and that while robotaxis may eventually be upgraded to AI5 for incremental safety gains, it is not an immediate priority. The hardware investment for AI5 and AI6 is framed primarily as an Optimus requirement.

## Financials

1. **Short-term cash crunch anticipated:** The guest explicitly cautions viewers not to expect positive short-term financial results, citing simultaneous capital deployment across robotaxi/Cybercab fleet build-out (pre-revenue), Optimus factory construction, and Cortex compute infrastructure expansion. He frames this as expected and intentional, not as a sign of distress.
