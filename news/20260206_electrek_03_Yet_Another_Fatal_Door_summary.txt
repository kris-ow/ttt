A new wrongful-death lawsuit filed against Tesla in Massachusetts alleges its electronic door handle design trapped a 20-year-old driver, Samuel Tremblett, inside a burning 2021 Model Y after an October 25, 2025 crash, leading to his death; meanwhile China has finalized a regulation requiring mechanical fallbacks for electronic door handles.

## Autonomous Driving / Electric Vehicles (Safety & Litigation)
1. **New lawsuit details:** The suit, Tremblett v Tesla, was filed Wednesday and describes Tremblett's Model Y leaving the road and hitting a tree; he survived the initial crash but could not escape due to the car's electronic interior door handle design, and his remains were found in the rear seat. The lawsuit includes a transcribed 911 call in which Tremblett is quoted pleading for help as the car caught fire. It seeks damages for his "conscious pain and suffering" and alleges 17 total accidents where people were trapped in Teslas (not all fatal).

2. **Door handle design issue:** Tesla's exterior handles sit flush with the body, while interior release handles are electronic, partly to let windows drop slightly before doors open. When the car loses power, occupants must use a manual release in a different location — on the Model Y, the rear manual release is hidden inside a door pocket, a multi-step process the article says is hard for a panicked or unfamiliar occupant to find. Early Model S vehicles had a mechanical fallback at the same location as the electronic release; later Tesla designs removed this co-located fallback.

3. **Pattern of prior incidents and regulatory scrutiny:** The article states Bloomberg counted 15 deaths tied to Tesla's door handle design and notes two other similar lawsuits previously covered by Electrek — one involving three teenagers who died in a Cybertruck, another involving five occupants who died in a Model S — both involving tree collisions, fires, and inoperable interior (and in the Cybertruck case, exterior) handles; none of these cases have been decided. NHTSA has opened an investigation into Tesla's door handle design, after which Tesla said it is "working on" a fix reverting to the original Model S-style co-located mechanical release.

## Market & Competition (Regulatory Response)
4. **China's new door handle regulation:** Many automakers, especially in China's EV industry, have adopted similar electronic door handle designs and seen similar incidents. China proposed a ban on Tesla-style electronic handles in September and finalized it this week; the rule requires mechanical fallbacks for electronically actuated handles and addresses handle placement, exterior operability, and visible markings. It takes effect January 1, 2027, with vehicles already late in design/approval given up to a two-year grace period. The article speculates this could influence or become a model for global door handle design standards given China's size as an auto market.

## Editorial Stance
The author is critical of Tesla's door handle design, characterizing the rear manual release placement as "more egregious" and implying negligence, and suggests further similar lawsuits are likely. The top-voted reader comment (not the author) argues Tesla's frameless door design is unnecessary and that a conventional framed door would allow a cheaper, more reliable mechanical handle with better sealing.
