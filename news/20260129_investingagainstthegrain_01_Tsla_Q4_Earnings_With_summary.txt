This is a live panel show (Herbert/investingagainstthegrain with Alexandra Merz, James from InvestAnswers, Nick Gibbs, Jeff Duan, Cern Basher, and Larry Goldberg) covering Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings deck and call in real time, followed by extensive Q&A analysis. Q4 beat expectations sharply (EPS and revenue above consensus, strong margins and free cash flow), while Elon Musk announced the wind-down of Model S/X production, a Tesla investment in xAI, and plans for a domestic "Terafab" chip/memory facility, alongside continued Robotaxi and Optimus ramp commentary.

## Financials
1. **Q4 beat on top and bottom line:** Tesla reported EPS of 50 cents (versus roughly 40 cents expected per the panel, and above James's own 44-45 cent estimate) and revenue of $24.9B versus $24.7B expected; revenue was still down about 3% year-over-year on lower vehicle deliveries and regulatory credit revenue, offset by growth in energy, services, and FSD-driven automotive sales.
2. **Margins strongest in years:** Non-GAAP gross margin came in at 20.1–20.2%, described as the highest in roughly two-plus years; net margin around 7% (highest since roughly 2022) and operating margin 5.6%. Automotive gross margin was 21% including regulatory credits and 18.6% excluding them — also the highest in about two years. Auto ASP was just over $41,000 against a vehicle cost of about $34,000, yielding roughly $7,000 gross profit per vehicle.
3. **Free cash flow beat expectations:** Free cash flow was $1.4 billion for the quarter, which the panel called well above consensus expectations of zero or negative; full-year 2025 capex came in at $8.5B, below the prior $9-10B guidance, which the panel attributed partly to Cortex 2 buildout being delayed into Q1.
4. **Cash pile and 2026 capex guidance:** Tesla ended the quarter with about $44 billion in cash, even after a $2 billion investment in xAI. CFO Vaibhav Taneja guided to 2026 capex in excess of $20 billion, roughly double 2025's spend, covering six new factories (LFP/lithium refinery, Cybercab, Semi, a new Megapack factory, and two Optimus lines) plus AI compute — excluding any spend on the planned solar cell or chip fabs. Taneja said funding would draw on the $44B cash balance plus potential debt, including loans backed by robotaxi fleet revenue streams.
5. **Digital asset and stock-option accounting flagged:** Net income was hit by mark-to-market losses on Tesla's Bitcoin holding (down 23% quarter over quarter) and FX impacts; panelists noted a roughly $2 billion GAP/non-GAAP gap largely tied to stock-based compensation (implying $10-20B of options value under Black-Scholes) and said the 10-K, expected same-day per last year's pattern, would clarify deferred FSD revenue recognition and stock-option allocation.
6. **xAI investment disclosed:** Tesla disclosed a $2 billion investment to acquire Series E preferred xAI stock, at a stated xAI valuation of $230 billion, framed by executives as part of "Master Plan 4" and shareholder-requested collaboration (e.g., using Grok for autonomous fleet orchestration).

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet size and paid-ride milestone:** Ashok Elluswamy and Lars Moravy said the company is "well over 500" robotaxi vehicles carrying paid customers across the Bay Area and Austin; Musk separately cited the same figure. This is materially above RobotaxiTracker.com's stale active-fleet figure (839, as of 2026-08-07) is not comparable since it's from months earlier — a panelist noted RobotaxiTracker showed 241, well below Musk's 500 claim, and the panel chose to trust Musk's figure over the tracker's.
2. **Unsupervised rides launched in Austin:** Musk said Tesla began fully unsupervised (no safety monitor, no chase car) paid robotaxi rides in Austin in the last few weeks, expanding from safety-monitored fleet operations in the Bay Area and Austin used to gather scaling data.
3. **Planned city/coverage guidance in the deck:** The earnings deck listed planned robotaxi coverage cities for first half 2026, including Tampa and Orlando as new additions alongside previously known markets; the panel debated whether this constitutes binding guidance, concluding it raises the bar for Tesla to explain any delays but isn't strictly binding.
4. **Doubling cadence and population coverage target:** Musk said the fleet's rate of expansion "will probably double every month" and that Tesla expects fully autonomous vehicles covering "somewhere between a quarter and half" of the US by year-end 2026, pending regulatory approval, or "dozens of major cities" if approvals proceed city-by-city. The panel noted Musk's past guidance (50% of US population by end of 2025) was not met, and viewed the new target with some skepticism ("grain of salt").
5. **Owner fleet participation ("Airbnb model") reiterated:** Musk reiterated (without a firm timeline) that Tesla plans to let owners add/subtract their cars from the autonomous fleet, potentially earning more than their lease cost — a concept he has mentioned before but has now repeated multiple times in one call.
6. **Safety streak data point:** Cern Basher cited a "current safety streak" of 389,160 miles without incident for the robotaxi fleet, compared to an average human driver's roughly 300,000 miles between insurance-reported crashes.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscriptions cross 1.1 million:** Active FSD subscriptions reached 1.1 million (up from about 1 million in Q3), described by the deck as up 38% year over year; monthly (subscription-only) FSD signups more than doubled in 2025, while the one-time $8,000 purchase option is being phased out February 14, 2026 in favor of a subscription model — 70% of the 1.1 million were upfront purchases, only about 330,000 pay the ~$99/month subscription.
2. **FSD version improvement cited by Jeff Duan:** Citing third-party FSD tracker data, Duan said the rate of both critical and non-critical disengagements improved roughly 8-10x between FSD versions 13 and 14, calling it one of the more credible relative data points available from that source.
3. **AP4.5 mislabeling correction:** Tesla began informing customers that a "hardware 4.5" label was a mislabeling error — all vehicles currently delivered use AI4/hardware 4, and Tesla will correct labeling going forward; no AP4.5 exists.
4. **Musk on remaining unsupervised rollout pace:** Musk said FSD is already 100% unsupervised in the Austin robotaxi service (no safety monitor, no chase car) but that broader customer rollout will proceed cautiously city by city, reducing driver monitoring as each software release proves safety in handling unusual intersections.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Model S/X production winding down for Optimus factory conversion:** Musk announced Tesla will end Model S and X production next quarter, converting the Fremont production space into an Optimus factory targeting eventual capacity of 1 million Optimus units per year; panelists (particularly Cern Basher) argued this could be far more valuable per square foot than S/X production given Optimus's potentially much higher revenue and margin per unit, though Tesla did not state this comparison itself.
2. **Optimus Gen 3 unveil and production timeline:** Musk said Tesla plans to unveil "Optimus 3" in a few months, calling it highly capable and closely human-like, with mass production start targeted for the end of 2026; he cautioned that because Optimus uses an entirely new supply chain, its manufacturing ramp will follow a longer S-curve than products with existing supply chains.
3. **Current Optimus deployment still R&D-stage:** Musk said Optimus is still in R&D and is "not in usage in our factories in a material way" — older versions are deprecated as new ones are iterated — with no plans for significant production volume until roughly year-end 2026.
4. **China cited as primary humanoid competitor:** Asked about competition, Musk said China is the only serious competitive threat in humanoid robotics, citing its manufacturing scale and AI capability, but argued Tesla leads in hand dexterity, real-world AI, and production scaling — the three hardest problems in humanoid robotics, in his view.

## Financials / Market & Competition
1. **Chip and memory constraints prompting a domestic "Terafab":** Musk said Tesla's AI5 chip design is his top personal priority (he spends most Saturdays and part of Tuesdays on it), with AI6 expected to follow within about a year. He said Tesla currently has a chip/memory supply solution for roughly the next three years via partners (TSMC in Arizona, Samsung in Texas) but beyond that will be supplier-constrained, especially on memory, since there are no advanced memory fabs at scale in the US. He said Tesla will likely build its own large-scale "Terafab" integrating logic, memory, and packaging domestically to mitigate this and geopolitical risk.
2. **Tariff and lithium cost pressures on energy margins:** Jeff Duan noted LFP cell tariffs rose to roughly 82% effective January 1, 2026, and Tesla's Nevada LFP factory (about 7 gigawatt-hours) covers only a fraction of needed capacity, raising near-term cost pressure; lithium hydroxide prices were noted to be up about 120% over six months, which Duan attributed to suspected Chinese supply-side controls rather than pure demand dynamics.
3. **EV competitive landscape framed as thinning, not growing:** Panelists (particularly Jeff Duan and Larry Goldberg) argued Western and Chinese competitors are retrenching from EV production, and BYD's rising unit volume has come with falling income per vehicle and shrinking margins, indicating underlying channel and supplier-payment stress — a claim attributed to the panelists' analysis, not sourced to company disclosures.
4. **Robotaxi pricing comparison:** A panelist noted a trend where Uber prices have been chasing Waymo's, Waymo has slowed price growth and begun cutting slightly, and Tesla's robotaxi pricing remains "less than half" of the others — an unsourced, panelist-asserted comparison.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Panel consensus: SpaceX IPO, not a Tesla merger:** Panelists (Larry Goldberg, Alexandra Merz, James, Nick Gibbs) unanimously rated the odds of a SpaceX-Tesla reverse merger as at or near zero, arguing the major underwriting banks want a "clean" IPO and that a merger would be too complex, dilutive, and politically risky; some speculated Tesla might make a modest, standalone investment (in the range of $5–10 billion, per Nick Gibbs, unconfirmed) rather than a merger.
2. **Rationale for the SpaceX IPO's scale:** Larry Goldberg argued SpaceX needs to raise a minimum of $25 billion, and possibly up to double that, to fund four new launch pads (roughly $1-1.5B each), two large rocket factories, and especially a new orbital data-center business, which he said dwarfs prior SpaceX capital needs; he called Starlink the only currently strongly cash-flow-positive SpaceX business.
3. **Timing speculation:** Panelists speculated (with explicit uncertainty) about IPO timing, floating dates tied to Musk's birthday in June or around July 4th, and one panelist suggested Musk may want to complete the IPO before the midterms to capture a favorable political climate — all framed as speculation, not confirmed fact.
