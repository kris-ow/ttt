This episode covers SpaceX's dramatic recovery of an intact Starship after it landed off-course in the ocean, a new report on the long-delayed Tesla Roadster's possible unveiling this month, an update on the massive Terafab chip facility's land acquisition and construction challenges, and the conclusion of Tesla's nearly three-year labor standoff with Swedish unions.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship ship 40 recovered intact after ocean drama:** On flight 13, Starship tipped over into the Indian Ocean and remained afloat instead of exploding as in prior flights — the first time this has happened. Rough seas in a remote area near Starship's normal splashdown zone off northwest Australia hampered the recovery, and Elon Musk posted that the recovery was "not looking good" at one point. After roughly 24 days at sea, the recovery team towed the ship in the opposite direction to calmer water near Christmas Island, Australia, where SpaceX engineers are now inspecting it before final transport back to Starbase, Texas.

## Electric Vehicles
2. **Roadster unveiling reportedly possible this month:** Per a report from The Information, Tesla could unveil the redesigned next-generation Roadster as early as this month (August 2026) — unconfirmed and not a confirmed Tesla event. The report describes two distinct variants: a limited, non-street-legal SpaceX-branded edition with a cold-gas thruster package (developed with SpaceX) reportedly costing hundreds of thousands of dollars or more, and a separate consumer version with similar design but without the thruster system. The demo is reportedly planned at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas engine test site due to the thrusters' noise and force. Tesla reportedly scrapped an earlier Model S Plaid-based Roadster design in favor of a carbon fiber hypercar architecture. Elon Musk has said the thruster-equipped version could achieve a 0-60 time below 1 second. The host notes Roadster has a long history of missed delivery dates and treats the timing as unconfirmed.

## Market & Competition
3. **Tesla's Swedish union standoff ends after ~3 years with no collective agreement signed:** The dispute began in October 2023 when Swedish union IF Metall demanded Tesla sign a collective labor agreement; Tesla refused, prompting a sympathy-strike blockade involving dock workers, postal workers, cleaners, painters, and electricians. Electricians refused to connect newly built Tesla Supercharger stalls to the grid, leaving more than 250 stalls across 20 locations dark for years (some since 2023) — dubbed "ghost stations" by EV drivers. Strike participation fell from about 130 to roughly 70, then 21, then a handful, as over 90% of Tesla's Swedish workforce continued working and the Model Y remained the country's best-selling car. After 1,021 days, IF Metall called off the action and Tesla bought out the remaining strikers; Tesla never signed a collective agreement, which the union's own leadership reportedly called a failure. With the blockade lifted, Tesla is now switching on the more than 250 previously dark Superchargers, expanding its Swedish network by more than 20%.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
4. **Terafab site clearing underway amid ongoing land acquisition:** Drone footage from Tesla watcher Joe Tegtmeyer shows land-clearing activity at the Terafab site in Grimes County, Texas, about 100 miles from Giga Texas. Per Tegtmeyer's tracking, Tesla and SpaceX currently own around 6,640 acres with another 2,300 acres in final stages of being recorded, and an estimated 2,000–3,000 additional acres still needed to complete the full facility, which is planned to span more than 4 miles end-to-end. The heavily forested, uneven terrain includes ponds, streams, cattle ranches, and a man-made lake sitting in the footprint of the core building that must be drained, plus power lines, gas pipelines, and buried utilities requiring relocation. The project is being built in 10 phases; the first phase alone is estimated to cost about $17 billion, with total estimates across all phases running as high as $120 billion.
