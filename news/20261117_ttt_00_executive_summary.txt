## Robotaxi
- Musk replied "Soon" to an Austin Uber driver who wants to send his Model Y out as a robotaxi, adding "If all goes well, we can start testing next month." That is earlier than the second-quarter-2027 timing Ashok Elluswamy gave for customer cars joining the fleet on the October 21 call, and Musk did not say who would be in a first group. The host expects a limited pilot at first (per brighterwithherbert and Electrified).
- Tesla announced Robotaxi now operates 24/7 in Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando and Tampa. Panelists on cernbasher note Elluswamy had said a couple of months ago that this would come "in a month or so."
- Geofence sizes cited by Sawyer Merritt: Austin 443 sq mi, San Antonio 275, Houston 254, Fort Worth 143, Dallas 81, Miami 59, Orlando 26, Tampa 24. Miami expanded 92% (31 to 59 sq mi). Florida zones remain small, and panelists attribute that to operational readiness rather than technical validation, which is their theory rather than a Tesla statement.
- Cybercab orders opened November 13 by invitation at $29,990 plus a required $299/month, with first deliveries expected in Austin in December. Musk said the fee is too high and should be about $115, and that nationwide availability depends on regulatory approval. The terms bar ride-hailing, delivery and rental use "unless Tesla explicitly allows it in writing or enables it in the product" (per brighterwithherbert).
- Electrek reports the Cybercab has no onboard charger and accepts only NACS DC fast charging, so it cannot charge at home. Fred Lambert calls it a fleet vehicle sold to consumers to hit a deadline. Tesla has shown a wireless pad since 2023 but never priced or dated it.
- Over 50 badged Cybercabs, some with apparent Starlink hardware, were spotted in Dallas. Tesla is reportedly set to launch Cybercab service there in November.
- A NHTSA report covering seven incidents (one in August, six in September) attributed three to Tesla: a Model Y hitting a low-hanging chain, a Model Y driving over a pothole near a work zone at 29 mph, and a Cybercab driving over a raised parking-lot edge and down a curb. Two were in Dallas and one in Austin (per Electrified).
- A panelist claims, citing an unnamed third party, that no new Cybercabs have been added to Texas registrations since October 28. The panel is skeptical this signals a regulatory holdup, and the claim is unconfirmed.
- Tesla crossed 5 million autonomous robotaxi miles last week, and Lars Moravy said Tesla bears liability when its software is the driver (per brighterwithherbert).

## Financials
- Tesla will invest about $3.3B at Giga Berlin in Grünheide for a new facility targeting up to 1 million Cybercabs a year. Tesla told Reuters it will file a building permit application by year-end, with construction starting in the first half of 2027 and production in 2029 (per Electrified and Sawyer Merritt).
- Asked about a claim that moderate Cybercab success is already priced into a roughly $1.6T Tesla valuation, Musk replied "of course" (per Electrified).
- Owner-operated robotaxis would let Tesla expand coverage without buying every vehicle. Tesla has not published a revenue split or who pays for charging and cleaning, so owner earnings figures remain speculative (per brighterwithherbert).

## Autonomous Driving
- The Dutch regulator RDW approved an updated assisted-driving release for HW4+ vehicles with tighter driver monitoring. Hands are required on the wheel above 130 km/h, warnings come earlier, and inattentiveness during a maneuver draws a full strike. The host frames it as groundwork for broader FSD approval (per Electrified).
- Panelists on cernbasher say European approvals are anticipated in the first week of December.
- Mercedes unveiled a point-to-point driving assistance system planned for U.S. launch in 2027, covering city streets, intersection turns and traffic lights (per Sawyer Merritt).

## Energy
- Tesla announced Powercore, a solid-state medium-voltage transformer rated 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV, with grid-forming support up to 10 MVA peak. It is due to operate in 2027, with no pricing, efficiency or factory details given. Rival Heron Power, founded by ex-Tesla SVP Drew Baglino, plans volume production of its 5 MW Heron Link in 2027 and says it has 50 GW of orders in the pipeline. Electrek's Lambert calls the launch "a little awkward" but sees room for both given the transformer shortage.
- SemiAnalysis counts 1,377 Tesla Megapacks at Colossus 2, close to 6 GWh. The host frames SpaceX data center growth as a possible Megapack tailwind.
- Tesla has reportedly broken ground on "Project Crystal Sun," a $10B, 3,000-acre vertically integrated solar manufacturing complex in Fort Bend County with 9,700 projected permanent jobs (per futureaza).

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck production will continue through the end of summer with an added shift before ending. Tesla's stated reason is the need for factory space for Cybercab. The futureaza hosts doubt that rationale and suggest 4680 battery supply, weak sales and manufacturing complexity are the real drivers. The hosts cite roughly 20,000 units sold versus a hoped-for 200,000, which is their estimate and not a Tesla figure.
- A proposal from Unplugged Performance to take over the Cybertruck line drew host skepticism, since it lacks casting and battery pack capability and Tesla would remain a parts vendor.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX caught Starship ship SN42 with Mechazilla after about 22.5 hours in orbit, the first ship catch. Musk inspected it and said it "looks great," with a reflight possible within a few weeks. Chopstick contact scraped some material off the ship's side (per Electrified).
- Of 33 Raptors, 32 lit for the booster's boost-back burn, which ended early on low fuel mass (cause under investigation). The booster's automated abort diverted it to an offshore landing. Musk attributed the green flame to copper burning as fuel ran low, with fixes including Raptor 3.2 and an earlier main-engine cutoff.
- Only one of about 18,000 heat shield tiles was reportedly lost, which is unofficial.
- Musk said Starship V4 will be about 70 m long (~40% longer) with over 60% more propellant, nine engines, payload above 200 tons and propellant cost per flight below $500,000.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas called the catch "a historic inflection moment" and said it "materially derisked" SpaceX's long-term addressable markets. He holds an overweight rating with a $300 target. Citigroup raised its target to $280 from $240, after an earlier move from $200. Bernstein kept an outperform rating.
- Some observers speculate Flight 16 could launch from Florida, which SpaceX has not confirmed. The Electrified host thinks a flight before year-end is possible but would not be surprised by a slip to January.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 15 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites over about 42 minutes, adding about 26 terabits of capacity. Musk said future Starship flights will carry 50 terabits per second.
- A United Airlines executive said in-flight Starlink Wi-Fi is "the biggest near-term competitive advantage that we've had, ever had in United's 100-year history." About 50% of flights are equipped, and he called Delta's choice of Amazon's service a mistake.
- Samsung is investing $1.9B to expand its Austin fab for the STM32V8 chip used in Starlink's laser communication system, with production targeted for 2030.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- ULA's Vulcan suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into flight and failed to deliver 40 Amazon LEO satellites to orbit. Per the Brighter With Herbert host this was the third anomaly in five Vulcan attempts, while futureaza called it the first complete failure.
- Musk countered a critic by saying Falcon delivers 85% of all Earth payload to orbit while better-funded rivals have not matched its reliability.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Per SemiAnalysis estimates, SpaceX's AI division completed its "MiniHard" data center in about five months versus an industry norm of 8-12 months. Colossus 1 delivered 150 MW in 122 days and Colossus 2 took 91 days. The Memphis/Mississippi campus is projected to grow from about 0.4 GW in January to 2.4 GW by end of 2026.
- The Information reports XAI is prioritizing AI coding tools built on Grok, competing with Anthropic's Claude Code, and laid off roughly 60 people from its Grok Imagine team.
- Per the futureaza hosts, data-center ballot measures were reportedly rejected in 8 of roughly 10 places, which could be an overhang for SPCX as an AI company.
- SpaceX reportedly ordered about 100 GW of solar equipment for its "Starmind" program (per futureaza hosts).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX closed about $137.36 (-1.26%) after the catch, per Electrified. The futureaza hosts cite market jitters, including Nvidia earnings and the Jensen Huang story, and say the stock rose nearly 2% intraday Monday after opening lower. One analyst reaffirmed an $800 target, per the hosts.
- The hosts say Alphabet holds about 555 million shares and that an early-round investor sold billions after the IPO.
