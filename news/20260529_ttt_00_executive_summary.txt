## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD daily miles hit 26.7M, up 44% in one month.** Morgan Stanley's new $TSLA note tracked FSD-active miles accelerating from 18.5M/day in April 2026 to ~26.7M/day now — flagged explicitly as a proxy for subscriber growth and engagement. This drew the highest investor engagement of the day (3.2K likes, 373 reposts).

- **Texas driverless commercial operation authorized as of May 28, 2026.** Under a new Texas law that took effect yesterday, Tesla is now legally permitted to operate driverless vehicles commercially in the state. Tesla has officially certified its robotaxi software as Level 4 autonomous.

- **FSD navigating brand-new infrastructure before maps update.** Per host MDR (Future AzA podcast), FSD 14.3 successfully identified and took a newly opened freeway on-ramp in Kirkland, WA on its first day open — with no signage and incorrect map data — and independently handled a flagged road closure by pulling into the oncoming lane as directed. Cited as evidence of genuine visual-first autonomy.

- **FSD 14.3.3 reduces nag meaningfully.** Co-host Jeff Lutz (Piper Sandler interview) reports being able to read emails during drives on the latest release. Piper Sandler's Alex Potter, an FSD daily user, called $99/month "quasi-free" relative to productivity delivered and expects the price to rise substantially as capability becomes undeniable.

- **European FSD supervised approvals accelerating.** Per Bhakdi, the Netherlands was first, followed by Estonia and Lithuania. Czech Republic, Slovenia, and Austria are preparing for unsupervised approvals; Swedish citizens are lobbying their government. Bhakdi characterizes this as a domino effect among smaller European nations, though not a near-term June catalyst.

- **FSD supervised now live across all of North America, China, Australia, New Zealand, South Korea, Netherlands, and Lithuania**, with three additional countries added recently, per the Future AzA podcast.

## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab production at ~30–36 units/day visible at Giga Texas.** Bhakdi highlights Joe Tegtmeyer's photographic coverage showing that volume, implying ~1,000 units/month at current pace. Because Cybercabs ship without steering wheels, they can only function as unsupervised robotaxis — creating structural deployment pressure.

- **Cybercab maintenance facility planned in Irving, Texas** (near Dallas/AT&T Stadium), per a community tip cited on the Future AzA podcast. A prior facility was announced in Las Vegas. Two dedicated Cybercab service hubs now confirmed or in planning across Texas and Nevada.

- **World Cup Dallas deployment theory.** Bhakdi argues Tesla will use the FIFA World Cup opening weekend in mid-June — hosted in Dallas — as a marketing moment, noting 10–20 Cybercabs have already been photographed being prepared there. He calls it a 5–15% near-term stock catalyst even for a small symbolic launch.

- **Repricing threshold: north of 1,000 unsupervised Cybercabs.** Bhakdi says a launch of 100–200 units won't force analyst model changes; the inflection begins above 1,000 deployed, with a more significant repricing requiring 1,800–2,000. He is "100% certain" 1,000+ Cybercabs will be in operation by end of June. He and analysts Cern Basher and Herbert align on a 15,000 unsupervised Cybercab target by year-end 2026.

- **Unsupervised Austin conversion could be imminent.** The Cern Basher panel notes that with roughly half of Austin's ~52-vehicle fleet already running unsupervised, the logical case for keeping the other half supervised is thin. One panelist set an over/under of 10 days for full Austin unsupervised conversion, with June as the outer bound, suggesting Tesla may be running a supervised-vs.-unsupervised A/B safety comparison before removing monitors entirely.

- **Tesla deliberately avoiding remote human backup operators.** The panel argues this is why the rollout is slower than Waymo's — Tesla is resolving every edge case in software rather than patching gaps with offshore remote operators. The tradeoff is a more scalable long-term model at the cost of near-term fleet size.

- **Waymo launches new "Ojai" robotaxi with free public rides** in San Francisco, Phoenix, and Los Angeles, with expansion planned. New hardware introduction underscores that the autonomous ride-hail race is intensifying just as Tesla scales Cybercab.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus V3 expected late July–early August 2026.** Bhakdi notes Elon Musk's prior "90 days" comment now places the debut approximately 45 days out. He flags a "not unreasonable chance" it slips one to two months.

- **Piper Sandler excludes Optimus from $400/share DCF — calls it unmodelable, not unattractive.** Alex Potter says he genuinely believes Musk's characterization of Optimus as an "infinite money glitch" that will make GDP metrics unrecognizable, but that the disruption scope exceeds what can be responsibly put in a spreadsheet. Since the 17 modeled businesses already exceeded the stock price, he did not need to force a number.

- **Optimus deployment in China flagged as national security open question.** Potter and co-hosts agree China will want Tesla to manufacture Optimus domestically, but note US regulatory restrictions on deploying humanoid AI systems there could create a constraint distinct from the EV and software situation.

## Financials

- **Piper Sandler 17-business DCF yields ~$400/share, excluding Optimus and distributed inference.** At Tesla's ~$450s trading price, the market is effectively pricing Optimus at zero or assigning it a positive free-option value. The 77-page second-edition report models revenue streams to 2045.

- **Robotaxi becomes ~45% of Tesla revenue and ~half of gross profit by 2045** in the Piper Sandler model, with new car sales a "rounding error" by that point. Cost per mile modeled from first principles at $0.25, consistent with Tesla's own stated targets. Peak Cybercab production assumed at ~2M units/year in the mid-2030s; global active fleet ~14–15M by 2045.

- **FSD licensing modeled at ~$400 billion in revenue by 2045**, with the OEM licensing inflection pushed to the mid-2030s. Potter expects Western OEMs to eventually capitulate — drawing a Supercharger NACS analogy — but says the first-mover OEM to announce FSD licensing could see a significant stock re-rating.

- **Potter's conservatism is regulatory, not technical.** He states explicitly: "You can't see second place with a telescope." Timeline uncertainty in his model reflects societal and regulatory risk outside the US, not doubt about Tesla's capability lead.

## Energy

- **144-Megapack Limondale BESS facility in Australia coming online; project value $145M.** The system is configured to charge at 100 MW and discharge at 50 MW — a notable single-project revenue event for Tesla Energy.

- **Megapack at Giga Berlin remains unlikely.** Per the Future AzA podcast, while some box-like structures near the Berlin switchyard are unconfirmed, Giga Berlin produces NMC chemistry batteries, not LFP — ruling out Megapack (which requires LFP) production there without a separate dedicated facility.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model S and X production formally ended** with a sendoff event at Giga Texas attended by Lars Moravy, Franz von Holzhausen, and Elon Musk. The final ~250 Model S units were a limited special edition with gold Plaid badging, Alcantara interior, and employee signatures. MDR expects a Model X replacement later in 2026; the Roadster is slated as the new flagship.

- **Model Y: first EV to reach 100,000 registrations in Norway**, achieved in approximately four years — roughly 1 in 29 Norwegian passenger cars is now a Model Y.

- **Ford developing a $30,000 EV truck** on a new "UEV" platform from its Long Beach EVDC. Hosts on multiple channels noted Ford's engineers repeatedly invoking "the best part is no part" and adopting modular assembly, in-house battery development, and a 48V zonal wiring system (cutting 4,000 feet of harness and 22 lbs of copper) — all strategies Tesla pioneered years earlier.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO ticker confirmed as SPCX; anticipated around June 12, 2026.** Bhakdi says he is 95% confident he can buy SpaceX below IPO price within 9 months and has no interest in buying at launch. Both Potter (Piper Sandler) and Merz (brighter with Herbert) see a Tesla-SpaceX merger as likely eventually — Merz projecting an announcement ~August–September 2026 and completion in Q2–Q3 2027 — though Potter flags SpaceX's voting structure and a potential post-merger SpaceX IPO causing near-term Tesla capital rotation as key risks.

- **Three large IPOs (SpaceX ~$2T, OpenAI ~$1T, Anthropic ~$1T) could pressure AI/semiconductor holdings.** Per "Investing Against the Grain," combined new supply could reach ~$200B; the counterargument is $8T+ sitting in US money markets. Consensus investor advice: wait for a 5–10% post-IPO flush or lock-up expiration before entering.
