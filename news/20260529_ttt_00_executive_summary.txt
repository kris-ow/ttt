## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD daily miles surged to ~26.7M/day** from ~18.5M in April 2026 — a ~44% month-over-month increase per a new Morgan Stanley note tracking the metric as a proxy for subscriber growth and engagement. No subscriber count was attached, but the acceleration is a direct leading indicator of FSD monetization momentum and training data accumulation.
- **Piper Sandler's Alex Potter prices FSD at $99/month and calls it "quasi-free."** Potter uses FSD daily and argues the subscription dramatically underprices the productivity value being delivered, drawing a parallel to Steve Ballmer dismissing the iPhone. He expects the price to rise substantially as capability becomes undeniable.
- **FSD licensing to third-party OEMs modeled at ~$400B in revenue by 2045**, with the inflection point pushed to the mid-2030s. Potter argues Western OEMs are pursuing a "Franken-monster" consortium approach that will ultimately fail, and predicts a domino effect similar to Supercharger NACS adoption — the first OEM to announce FSD licensing could see a significant stock re-rating.
- **One in three vehicles on Earth modeled on Tesla's platform by 2045** in Potter's DCF. He acknowledges the figure could be 20% or 40%, with geopolitical uncertainty the key variable.
- **Remaining robotaxi blockers are operational, not safety-related.** Multiple analysts corroborate Musk's earnings-call statement: panelists on Cern Basher's show say they cannot recall the last safety intervention, with remaining issues being navigational preferences — e.g., which parking garage entrance to use, or failing to pull forward under cover in rain.
- **FSD 14.3.3 meaningfully reduces driver nag**, per co-host Jeff Lutz on the Piper Sandler interview, who reports being able to read emails during drives on the current release.

## Robotaxi

- **Piper Sandler models robotaxi cost per mile at $0.25**, derived from first principles: charging infrastructure cost and output, cleaning, depreciation (6-year Cybercab life, straight-line), and wholesale electricity. Potter notes this aligns with Tesla's own stated targets.
- **Robotaxi projected at ~45% of Tesla revenue and ~half of gross profit by 2045**, with new car sales becoming "basically a rounding error" in Potter's model. Peak Cybercab production of ~2M units/year is reached in the mid-2030s, with a global active fleet of 14–15M by 2045.
- **Potter assumes a global robotaxi duopoly with Tesla at 50% share**, with a large Chinese platform as the implied counterpart. The model is built bottom-up from ~900 cities with populations over 500,000, estimating per-capita trips and Tesla's capture rate city by city.
- **Full Austin unsupervised conversion could come within 10 days**, per one panelist on Cern Basher's show, with June as the outer bound. The thesis: with roughly half of Austin's ~52-vehicle fleet already running unsupervised, there is little logical reason to keep the remainder supervised — the panel floats an A/B safety-data collection rationale for the delay.
- **Tesla is deliberately avoiding remote human backup operators**, unlike Waymo, which reportedly uses operators in the Philippines and the US to handle edge cases. The panel frames this as why Tesla's ramp is slower near-term but more defensible and scalable long-term.
- **California's 500-plus supervised drivers were a learning exercise**, not a soft launch — the panel argues Tesla had zero prior experience running a ride-hail service and used that phase to build dispatch, app, and pickup/dropoff operational knowledge before going driverless.
- **Waymo launched public rides in its new "Ojai" robotaxi** in San Francisco, Phoenix, and Los Angeles, initially free to select riders with expansion planned. New hardware entering the market underscores the competitive intensity as Tesla scales Cybercab.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus is excluded from Piper Sandler's $400/share DCF** — not out of skepticism but because Potter says he cannot construct a credible bottom-up model for it. He explicitly agrees with Musk's "infinite money glitch" characterization and says the scope of disruption makes it unspreadsheetable. At ~$450/share, the market is effectively pricing Optimus for free relative to the modeled businesses.
- **US national security risk flagged for Optimus manufacturing in China.** The panel notes China will likely demand Tesla build Optimus domestically, but US regulatory restrictions on exporting strategic humanoid AI systems represent a distinct and open risk — different in kind from EVs or software.
- **Humanoid robot survivors will fit on "one or two hands"**, per Jeff Lutz, with Potter agreeing: the number of companies that can credibly combine a capable AI brain, manufacturable hardware, and cost-effective production at scale is extremely small.

## Energy

- **Limondale BESS in Australia is about to activate**: 144 Tesla Megapacks, valued at $145M, with an unusual asymmetric power profile — rated to charge at 100 MW and discharge at 50 MW. A notable single-project revenue event for Tesla's Energy division.
- **Megapack production at Giga Berlin is unlikely**, per FutureAzA: the facility produces batteries but not LFP chemistry, which Megapacks require. Any Megapack manufacturing in Europe would need a separate dedicated facility.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Ford is targeting a $30,000 electric midsize truck** built on a new "UEV" universal platform, developed at a new EV Development Center in Long Beach, California. FutureAzA and Tesla Life note deep skepticism that $30,000 is achievable given inflation, given the F-150 Lightning was priced at $70,000–$80,000 before discontinuation.
- **Ford's celebrated engineering "innovations" are Tesla's 2015–2020 playbook.** Ford's UEV platform, modular unicasting assembly, "the best part is no part" parts-reduction philosophy, in-house battery development, and a shift to 48V zonal wiring (cutting 4,000 feet of harness and 22 lbs of copper) are all strategies Tesla pioneered years earlier — which the hosts frame as inadvertent validation of Musk's original first-principles approach.
- **Cybercab is in an active validation phase** with units spotted widely across the country, per the Cern Basher panel. One panelist cautions that even an initial symbolic deployment is still likely 3-plus months away and does not constitute a fleet ramp.

## Market & Competition

- **Potter: "You can't see second place with a telescope"** on Tesla's competitive moat in autonomous driving. He argues Tesla's integrated approach — hardware, software, data, cost structure — is fundamentally superior to any consortium-based competitor.
- **SpaceX-Tesla merger: Piper Sandler expects it to eventually happen.** Potter says his baseline has always assumed Musk's companies would ultimately unify given shared mission and engineering cross-pollination. Alexandra Merz specifies a scenario: announcement ~August–September 2026, completion Q2–Q3 2027. Merger governance risks flagged — SpaceX's structure described as a "bunker" with Musk holding 50%+ voting control, potentially complicating FSD licensing deals and China operations.
- **SpaceX/OpenAI/Anthropic IPO liquidity risk is real but overstated**, per Investing Against the Grain. The bear case: ~$200B in new supply forces selling of appreciated AI/semiconductor holdings. The counter: $8T+ in U.S. money market accounts represents dry powder that mitigates the squeeze. Recommended strategy is to wait for post-IPO flush or lock-up expiry rather than buying at launch.
- **Rivian identified as the closest Western Tesla analog** by Potter, crediting their vertical integration and direct consumer model — but flagging their continued lidar reliance as a crutch and their lack of a massive purpose-built data-harvesting fleet as a structural disadvantage.
