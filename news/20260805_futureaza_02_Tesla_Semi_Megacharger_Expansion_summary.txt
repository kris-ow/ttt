This Futurazza episode, hosted by Brian with guest Mark from Tesla Life, covers the Tesla Semi's production ramp, expansion of Megacharger infrastructure across the US, a fatal crash involving a Semi driver falling asleep at the wheel, and continued FSD-for-Semi testing — interspersed with a running "wrong answers only" community game segment.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi production still ramping, not yet at scale:** The hosts push back on a headline framing Tesla's most recent quarterly revenue as "fueled by" Semi production, noting the Semi was likely a drag on results rather than a contributor; Tesla has begun what it calls serial production but the hosts stop short of calling it mass production. Visual evidence from factory-watcher footage (Zangler) reportedly shows only a small number of trucks staged or charging outside the plant.
2. **Fatal crash involving a Tesla Semi:** The hosts reference a fatal crash where a driver fell asleep at the wheel, expressing sympathy for the families involved and noting this underscores the case for future autonomous driving in the Semi.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Semi FSD testing continues with ground-truth equipment:** Tesla is reportedly testing Semis with LIDAR-based ground-truth equipment to validate camera/optical sensor readings, a process previously used during development of FSD for passenger vehicles. Mark cites a claim that Semi FSD could be working by "early next year," which the host treats skeptically given Elon Musk's history of missed FSD timeline predictions, suggesting a more conservative estimate of Semi FSD readiness by 2027.

## Energy
1. **Megawatt/Megacharger network expansion accelerating:** Tesla reportedly has 64 Megacharger locations listed as "coming soon," with dozens expected to open this year — a pace the host says is faster than expected. Currently confirmed locations include sites near Stockton and Reno, California; Fremont; a newly opened public site in Southern California; and Giga Texas, which was upgraded from one charger to a four-stall setup near its delivery center. Detroit was also announced as a future site at an e-mobility summit, though it wasn't shown on a current map the hosts reviewed. The hosts note most near-term Semi sales will concentrate on the West Coast (California, Oregon, Washington) where state subsidies are strongest, with regional/short-haul routes (cited as roughly 84–87% of trucking) prioritized over long cross-country corridors.
2. **California EV truck subsidies still largely unredeemed:** Fleets are reportedly sitting on more than half a billion dollars in unredeemed vouchers from the state's latest funding round, must be redeemed within 18 months, with 77% earmarked for Class 8 electric trucks. California plans to deploy an additional billion dollars in incentives funded via low-carbon fuel standards to sustain momentum in heavy-duty electrification.
