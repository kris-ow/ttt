This episode covers Tesla's newly ramped Semi Gigafactory in Nevada and its adjacent charging infrastructure rollout, a SpaceX update on the Starship upper-stage catch timeline and upcoming orbital flight, and a segment on Neuralink's manufacturing expansion in Texas (included here for context though only tangentially tied to Tesla/SpaceX investment substance).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi factory now running at volume:** Tesla's dedicated Semi factory in Nevada, built directly adjacent to Gigafactory Nevada's battery plant, is now producing trucks at high volume rather than as prototypes; drone footage this week showed staged production units. The factory's proximity to battery production is framed as solving the cell-supply bottleneck that previously caused Tesla to prioritize the Model Y over the Semi.
2. **Capacity and pricing:** The factory is designed for up to 50,000 trucks a year. The Semi is expected to be priced around $260,000 for a standard-range version (325 miles) and roughly $290,000 for a long-range version (500 miles).
3. **Major fleet orders:** Einride, a Swedish freight company, committed to 500 Tesla Semis — described as the largest single Semi order to date. Watt EV ordered 370 units in May for its California freight network.
4. **Europe launch confirmed:** Tesla's official Semi account teased a European version ahead of the IAA Transportation show in Hanover (September 15–20), showing camera-monitor systems replacing side mirrors — a design already legal in the EU since 2016 and used by Mercedes-Benz since 2018. Full European specs and launch details are expected to be revealed at the show. Federal rules in the US still require physical mirrors, with petitions from Tesla, GM, Volkswagen, Toyota, and Daimler pending with regulators since 2014, meaning this mirrorless version cannot be fully deployed domestically.

## Energy
1. **Mega charger network expansion:** Tesla opened its second public Megacharger station in Vernon, California, with six stalls each capable of up to 1.2 megawatts — described as roughly enough power for about a thousand homes simultaneously. It follows the first full-scale site in Bloomington, California, which went live in July; both sit within the freight corridor linking the ports of LA/Long Beach to Inland Empire warehouses.
2. **Broader rollout plan:** Tesla's Semi engineering VP Dan Priestley outlined a roadmap for roughly 46 public Megacharger stations by 2027, with Texas leading at 19 planned sites and California close behind at 17, following Interstate 5 and Interstate 10.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Ship catch delayed, orbital flight confirmed:** Musk said during SpaceX's first investor call as a public company (August 4) that SpaceX planned to attempt the first Starship upper-stage tower catch on Flight 14, tentatively late August. On August 20 he posted that the catch would not happen on that flight and would likely come "in a few months" instead. Musk indicated the delay reflects the far greater precision, speed, and heat challenges of catching the ship versus the already-proven booster catch (achieved three times). Flight 14 will instead attempt Starship's first full orbital flight, after 13 prior suborbital flights.
2. **Reflight timeline:** Musk said the first reflight of a previously used Starship is expected either late this year or early next year, calling it (per Musk) "a fork in the road of history for consciousness reaching the stars."
