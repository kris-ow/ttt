## SpaceX — AI & Compute

SpaceX's CFO is cited as saying the company is on pace for $100 billion in contracted annual revenue by the end of the year.
2. **GPU capacity ramp:** The host cites a figure of 200,000 Nvidia GB300 GPUs going into service in November, with possibly more in December, plus a separately mentioned $6.7 billion cloud revenue contract over six months with capacity ramp-up starting in October. Guest Jeff Vets notes gigawatt capacity (2.5–2.6 GW this year) is tracking above what he believes was projected earlier in the year.
3. **Vertical integration as pricing power:** Ark Invest's Brett Winton is quoted (per the video) arguing SpaceX's strong balance sheet and Nvidia relationship let it sign short-term deals and redirect chip supply to internal needs if demand falls short, giving it leverage to charge a premium — cited as up to a 93% higher price premium for "intelligence as a service" versus competitors reliant solely on the rental market.
4. **Elon Musk's "never seen anything like it" reaction:** Musk is quoted responding to the computing revenue growth figures by calling it "just crazy" and saying he's "never seen anything like it" — this is the quote referenced in the video's title.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab chip-manufacturing plans unveiled:** In a livestreamed interview during an X event, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX will build their own chip-making machines via the Terafab project, arguing the chip industry is "risk averse" because everyone relies on the same suppliers (e.g., ASML). He said Terafab will start with standard industry equipment, then move to specialized machines to boost wafer output, and acknowledged "I still have a lot to learn" about chip manufacturing, comparing it to not previously knowing how to build cars or rockets. He described Terafab's goal as full-cycle, in-house chip iteration (logic, memory, packaging, lithographic masks) at Giga Texas's research factory, aiming for roughly 10 iterations a year versus the industry's typical ~2-3 year cycle. He also said the value of Terafab's output could rank among the top twenty countries by GDP.
2. **Guest skepticism on Musk's framing:** Jeff Vets pushed back on Musk's dual claim that chip manufacturing is both unfamiliar to him and merely standardized/uninnovative, calling both framings "extreme" — noting process yield and cycle-time differences between Intel, Samsung, and TSMC reflect real engineering differentiation not visible on a factory tour. He called Musk's stated awareness of industry risk-aversion "the most important statement" in the interview, arguing vertical integration lets SpaceX/Tesla fail faster and iterate faster than risk-averse incumbents.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink mobile spectrum and future phone chips:** The video reports SpaceX agreeing to acquire spectrum intended to let Starlink mobile service penetrate buildings (addressing an AT&T CEO's stated doubt that Starlink mobile could do so), which Musk reportedly called the "final important element" for full phone coverage in the U.S. Musk confirmed phones released in 2027 and 2028 will include built-in chips supporting this different, higher-bandwidth Starlink spectrum.
2. **AI companies and phones — Qualcomm CEO comments:** The video cites Qualcomm's CEO (name given in transcript as "Cristiano") saying AI companies have told Qualcomm they want phones capable of continuously running 100-billion-parameter AI models on-device by 2028, and that AI companies are beginning to build their own phones (without naming them). The host speculates SpaceX could be one of these companies but presents this as unconfirmed speculation. Jeff Vets noted building an independent modem/communications device is difficult (citing Qualcomm's end-to-end modem, compute, and power-management stack) and expects most AI companies to need to partner with Qualcomm rather than go fully independent.
