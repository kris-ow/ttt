This rocket is made
of roughly the same
material as your
kitchen sink—
stainless steel,
although that wasn't originally
planned.  Starship was to
be made of
carbon fiber—a lightweight
, strong,
high-tech
aerospace
material.  SpaceX even
built a
carbon fiber tank with
a diameter of 12 meters.
But in 2018 and 2019,
they switched to
stainless steel.  So
why did SpaceX choose this
cheap metal?
Let's start with the cold.
The fuel for Starship
is stored at
extremely
cryogenic
temperatures.  Many
metals become
brittle when cooled this way
, but
stainless steel,
on the contrary, becomes
stronger.  Now let
's look at it from the other
side.  During
re-entry through
the atmosphere, the ship
heats up greatly.
Carbon fiber is
bonded with resin, which
begins to break down
at temperatures of
several hundred
degrees.  Stainless
steel melts only
at 1400°C. This is
a huge margin of
safety, which allows you
to reduce heat protection
in those areas that are
not exposed to
maximum
heating.  Next is
money.  Elon estimates
the cost of carbon
fiber at about
$135 per
kilogram, and steel at
about three.  And
steel is easy
to work with.  It can be
welded in a tent in
South Texas,
fixed, and
built quickly.
Carbon fiber
requires giant
furnaces, careful
laying, and a lot of
time.  What's the catch?
Yes, steel is heavier, but
Elon says that when
you factor in
temperature and
unnecessary
thermal protection, that
difference almost disappears.
And you get a rocket
that can be
stamped at
incredible speeds
.  In my opinion, this is the
main point.
I built it, launched it,
saw what broke
, built the next one.
Whoever does it
the fastest
learns the fastest.
So the best rocket
is not the one made of
the most expensive
material, but the one that
can be
built quickly, and that's why
we have what we
have.
