## Brief

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 15's ship reached orbit, deployed 26 new Starlink V3 satellites, spent ~21 hours aloft (~14 orbits), then became the first Starship upper stage ever caught by the tower arms on return. Google's Sundar Pichai publicly congratulated SpaceX.
- The Super Heavy booster aborted its catch attempt and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico instead; Musk said it returned low on fuel with unfinished engine work but that "the loss of this booster does not change our schedule for achieving frequent flights of a fully reusable Starship." He said risking the tower could have cost two to three months.
- Musk inspected the returned ship and said it could likely fly again within weeks of refurbishment, eventually shrinking to days, though "many small fixes" remain before airline-style reuse.
- Responding to a critic questioning SpaceX's edge, Musk said SpaceX delivers 85% of Earth payload to orbit via Falcon, versus Blue Origin's unreliable launch cadence and ULA's under-5% share.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla opened invite-only Cybercab orders for personal (non-commercial) use at $29,990 with a subscription fee reported as either $299 or $300/month depending on source; the car can self-park, reposition via touchscreen at up to 2 mph, and drive itself to service. Commercial use and customization are barred.
- Tech reviewer Marcus "MKBHD" Brownlee ordered a Cybercab tied to an earlier bet to shave his head if Tesla shipped a sub-$30,000 Cybercab this year; he disputes having lost the bet.
- Tesla confirmed a new Giga Berlin production hall dedicated to Cybercab — a €3 billion investment rated for up to 1 million vehicles/year, with a building permit planned by year-end, groundbreaking in H1 2027, and production starting 2029. Electrek calls the bet "extremely risky," noting the EU caps driverless registrations at 1,500 per model/year, Tesla has no European unsupervised-driving approval, and Texas had just 463 registered Cybercabs as of late October.
- Dallas and Las Vegas saw heavy Cybercab test-fleet activity (50+ badged vehicles spotted in Dallas, 53 in a Las Vegas lot), with Dallas commercial rides targeted for November by Tesla but not yet launched as of November 14. Miami's robotaxi geofence roughly doubled, and new night-shift service-tech job postings appeared across several additional Florida cities.
- Electrek's crash-data analysis found 8 NHTSA-filed Cybercab crashes roughly two months into Austin public rides: 4 in driverless mode (all under 5 mph), and 4 with a test driver under Autopilot/FSD, including one hitting a car at 53 mph in Philadelphia where Tesla holds no AV permit. Across 43 Robotaxi ADS reports, median speed was 1 mph versus a 53 mph median among 52 fatal Level 2 crashes in Tesla's broader dataset.
- A separate NHTSA safety report (Jan 22–Oct 18) flagged 7 new robotaxi incidents, mostly other drivers hitting stationary robotaxis; one Dallas incident involved a Model Y robotaxi driving over a pothole near a work zone, injuring a rider who needed medical care and a tow.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek reported three separate fatal crashes from NHTSA filings — El Dorado Hills, CA (3 killed, Autopilot/FSD "Verified Engaged" at 74 mph), Naples I-75, FL (3 killed including a bystander, "Verified Engaged" at 71 mph), and a Naples pole rollover (1 killed, "Verified Engaged" at 39 mph) — in each case Tesla redacted the narrative and software version as confidential, and local reporting never mentioned driver-assist involvement. Electrek frames this as a pattern of concealment.
- Tesla's September 2026 driver-assist crash filings hit 264, a new monthly record, up from 236 in July and 135 in March, per Electrek's NHTSA analysis.
- Tesla FSD (supervised) was approved in Finland, its 11th European country, after Tesla reset speed-limit-exceeding defaults at every car start — addressing concerns previously raised by Germany's transport ministry ahead of a scheduled EU-wide committee vote in December.
- Tesla reportedly targets FSD approval in Japan by year-end (per Nikkei) and plans to launch FSD in Argentina November 20. Full FSD approval in China remains pending; Musk is reportedly joining a Trump trip to Shenzhen November 18–19, though a prior Beijing trip did not yield approval.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

Polymarket odds of a December 31 merger announcement had fallen from 20% to 8.5% before this report.
- Tesla's annual shareholder meeting is December 3 at Giga Texas; no merger item is on the ballot, but Musk is expected to face live questions on it.
- The 180-day post-IPO lockup on SpaceX pre-IPO shares expires December 8.
