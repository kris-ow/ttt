This Monday, May 25, 2026 livestream from FutureAzA covers over 20 news stories spanning SpaceX/Starlink, AI compute deals, solid-state batteries, legacy automaker struggles, Waymo's operational setbacks, and Lucid's precarious financial position. The host's core argument is that legacy automakers (GM, Ford, Stellantis, Vinfast) are strategically adrift — doubling down on ICE, canceling EV programs, and deploying vague software roadmaps — while genuinely disruptive players like SpaceX and Tesla continue to widen their competitive moats. There is no dedicated Tesla robotaxi or FSD segment, though FSD licensing and Waymo's autonomous difficulties are touched on briefly.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Waymo suspends freeway operations amid construction zone failures:** Waymo has halted freeway rides in San Francisco, Los Angeles, Phoenix, and Miami while it works to improve performance in construction zones. The host frames this as a fundamental limitation of rule-based ("if-then") systems versus neural networks — a car following rigid logic stays stuck when stuck, whereas a neural net can adapt. Waymo also previously paused operations in Atlanta and San Antonio to address flooding-related problems. The host uses David Moss's coast-to-coast Canada road trip in a Tesla as a contrasting anecdote, where the car briefly hesitated at a construction zone sign before proceeding on its own — illustrating neural net adaptability.

2. **FSD licensing prospects dimming:** During the Q&A, the host noted that anticipation for a legacy automaker to license FSD has faded significantly. The longer it goes without happening, the more convinced he is that no legacy OEM will ever license it.

## Robotaxi
1. **Lucid/Uber robotaxi moonshot framed as smoke and mirrors:** Uber has committed to raising its total investment in Lucid to $500 million and expanding its vehicle purchase commitment to at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles for potential robotaxi use. However, the host is deeply skeptical: Lucid's autonomous technology is nowhere near prime time, the vehicles (such as the Lucid Gravity) are expensive and thus economically uncompetitive for ride-hailing, and the company has refused to show the host its actual autonomous technology roadmap in depth — which he interprets as confirmation there is nothing substantive to show.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Lucid's financial distress deepens:** Lucid reported Q1 2026 revenue of $282 million, up 20% year-over-year, while production rose 149% — yet approximately 40% of produced vehicles remained unsold. Reuters reported Lucid suspended its full-year forecast after missing quarterly revenue targets. Short interest stands at 48.97% of float. The host notes Lucid's only plausible path to survival is its upcoming midsize "Cosmos" model, roughly Model Y-sized, if it can be manufactured affordably and sold profitably at high volume.

2. **GM doubles down on ICE, pauses EV battery joint venture:** GM is investing $300 million to increase transmission production and doubling a separate $40 million Ohio investment for additional transmission capacity — moves the host characterizes as a short-term profit play that forecloses international competitiveness. Meanwhile, GM and Samsung have indefinitely paused their joint EV battery plant, completing only the exterior shell before walking away. The host speculates Samsung may eventually acquire the facility cheaply and supply batteries to a more committed EV maker.

3. **Ford exits Blue Oval SK battery JV, pivots to grid storage:** Ford plans to exit its Blue Oval SK battery joint venture and take full ownership of both Kentucky plants. Separately, Ford Energy has landed its first customer in battery storage — French power utility EDF Power Solutions — pivoting toward grid-scale energy storage products rather than EV batteries.

4. **Stellantis announces three-platform future with no existing technology:** Stellantis unveiled three pillars for its future product lineup — "Stella Brain" (central compute/software), "Stella Smart Cockpit" (in-vehicle UX), and "Stella Autodrive" (autonomous driving) — all targeted for 2027. The host dismisses the announcement as aspirational given Stellantis's well-documented software weaknesses and lack of autonomous capability. Stellantis also plans to reduce European capacity by more than 800,000 units.

5. **Vinfast sells factories to shed $6.9 billion in debt:** Vinfast is selling two factories for approximately $500 million to a closely related party — an entity headed by someone embedded in the Vinfast corporate family — in order to offload $6.9 billion in debt. The host calls it financial shenanigans: technically legal, but a red flag that effectively transfers debt to investors while making Vinfast appear debt-free despite never having turned a profit.

6. **Solid-state battery progress: Gangfang and Prologium:** A Changan-backed Gangfang solid-state battery has reportedly achieved 1,100 cycles with 400 Wh/kg energy density, with a claimed 500 Wh/kg in production, though not yet at scale. The host also highlights Prologium — founded 20 years ago, holding over 1,000 patents — as a company that has an actual production line for small solid-state batteries and is genuinely commercializing them, in contrast to many startups making unsubstantiated claims. BYD's new Blade battery is noted as brilliant but effectively non-serviceable by design, requiring full replacement on failure.

7. **Solar-integrated EVs can't cover meaningful range:** A study claimed EVs with integrated photovoltaic cells could cover up to 80% of electricity needs, and that truck trailers could generate up to 55 kWh per day from roof-mounted panels. The host dismisses this: at roughly 2 kWh per mile consumption, 55 kWh provides only about 27 miles, and the added drag and weight likely negate any benefit.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX targets 10,000 annual launches within 5 years:** SpaceX is aiming for 10,000 annual rocket launches within five years — approximately 30 per day — up from 170 launches conducted in 2025. Government officials have cited a need for improved reliability, which the host rejects given Falcon 9 Block 5's 99.8% success rate (one failure in 584 launches). He argues the real constraint is physical infrastructure: existing launch sites at Vandenberg and the Space Coast cannot accommodate that cadence, and new sites — potentially offshore or in Nevada desert — will be needed regardless of noise concerns.

2. **Starlink hits 10.3 million paid subscriptions:** Starlink reached 10.3 million paid subscriptions, a 105% increase from approximately 5 million a year prior (i.e., approximately mid-2025). The figure excludes enterprise/government contract customers and covers only directly assigned personal, business, and global priority plans. The host notes Africa and Asia represent large untapped markets as more satellites are deployed.

3. **Anthropic pays SpaceX $15 billion/year for Colossus compute:** Anthropic has contracted to pay SpaceX $1.25 billion per month — $15 billion annually — for access to the Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 supercomputing clusters, representing idle capacity SpaceX is monetizing while its own AI needs are not yet at full utilization. The host views this as an astute business move.

4. **Rivian spin-off Mind Robotics raises $400 million:** Mind Robotics, a Rivian spin-off robotics company, raised an additional $400 million just two months after raising $500 million — totaling $900 million raised in roughly two months.
