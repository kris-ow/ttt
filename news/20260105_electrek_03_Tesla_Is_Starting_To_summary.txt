The article argues that full-year 2025 data from Giga Shanghai confirms Tesla's first year-over-year decline in domestic China retail sales, driven by intensifying competition from domestic EV makers.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **December wholesale hit near-max capacity:** Tesla's Giga Shanghai produced 97,171 wholesale units in December 2025, a figure the article calls close to the factory's theoretical maximum capacity.
2. **Domestic sales decline calculated:** Tesla had 531,855 domestic China sales from January–November 2025, versus 657,105 for full-year 2024. Even in an "impossible" scenario where 100% of December's 97,171 wholesale units were sold domestically, the 2025 total (629,026) would still fall roughly 30,000 units short of 2024. Using a more realistic ~88% domestic split (based on December 2024's pattern), the article estimates final 2025 domestic sales near 618,000 — roughly a 6% year-over-year decline.
3. **Second year of wholesale decline, and accelerating:** The article states 2025 marks Tesla's second consecutive year of declining wholesale deliveries from China, with the year-over-year drop accelerating from 3.2% (2023 to 2024) to 7% (2024 to 2025).
4. **December demand partly pulled forward:** The article notes strong December wholesale numbers were partly driven by fear that Chinese government EV incentives might be reduced in 2026 — a cut the government ultimately did not make.

## Market & Competition
1. **Aging lineup versus fast-moving rivals:** The article attributes the domestic sales decline to intensifying competition from BYD, Nio, Xpeng, and Xiaomi, which it says are iterating faster, undercutting prices, and matching Tesla on tech features, while Tesla relies on the Model 3 and Model Y, which the article describes as getting old.
2. **Fading quality perception advantage:** The article says Tesla still benefits from a perception among some Chinese consumers — mainly older ones — that domestic automakers lag foreign brands on quality and safety, but argues this perception is now outdated and no longer reflects reality.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert frames the findings as a "reality check" rather than "doom and gloom," arguing the narrative of China as Tesla's unlimited growth engine has ended because Giga Shanghai is at capacity and the domestic market is saturated with strong alternatives; he expects competition to intensify further in 2026.
