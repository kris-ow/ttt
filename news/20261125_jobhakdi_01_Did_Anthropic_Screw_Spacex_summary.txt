In a Thanksgiving-eve livestream, the host explains why SpaceX (SPCX) fell nearly 10% at the open on 2026-11-25 and then finished green. The trigger was a report that Anthropic will not renew part of its GPU rental from SpaceX and that SpaceX cut Blackwell spot rental prices. He argues the takeaway that AI compute demand is cracking is "fake news": the dropped GPUs are a small slice of older chips, and live compute prices remain well up over three months. He also covers the December 8 lockup expiry, his SpaceX call positions, expected Tesla–SpaceX merger terms, and Cybercab registration growth.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **The Anthropic/compute-price report:** The report reached the market via an X summary of a SemiAnalysis note. Its first claim is that Anthropic won't renew about 50,000 older Hopper-generation GPUs it rents from SpaceX. Its second is that SpaceX reportedly cut short-term Blackwell rental rates from about $15/hour to $10–12/hour, which could signal the GPU shortage is easing. The host frames compute spot prices as the single gauge of whether AI demand outruns supply. Falling spot prices would mean weakening demand or oversupply, which he says would leave SpaceX "absolutely toast," so the headline hit hard.
2. **Why the host calls it overblown:** The 50,000 GPUs are about 15% of the roughly 325,000 GPUs in the Anthropic–SpaceX deal. They are also the weakest chips, and no Blackwell or Vera Rubin capacity is affected. He says swapping these for newer chips that likely deliver more tokens per dollar is natural, though he concedes he can't confirm that was the motive. The $10–12 Blackwell price only brings small customers down to what Anthropic and Google already pay, and big contracts were not cut.
3. **Anthropic wants 10 more gigawatts:** Per the same summary, Musk says Anthropic told him it wants 10 more gigawatts from SpaceX, not less. The host estimates the existing deal at roughly a quarter-gigawatt of GPUs, which makes 10 GW about 40 times larger. He puts Anthropic's current spend at about $3B a month.
4. **The real risk, per the host:** He is "100% certain" intelligence demand is outpacing compute buildout. The one genuine threat he sees is a continuous, massive (many-thousandfold) improvement in algorithmic efficiency per unit of compute, which could derail return on invested capital (ROIC) for the buildout. He says he sees "zero signs" of that happening now.
5. **Live compute prices still up over three months:** On a third-party compute-price tracker, B200 rental is down about 7% over one month but up 51% over three months. B300 is down 7% over a month but up 12% over three months. H100 pricing is flat and H200 is drifting slightly lower. The host concludes there is no compute price crash and calls these thin, choppy markets that bear watching.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Price action and catalysts:** SPCX dropped nearly 10% to about $114 early in the session, much harder than in earlier sell-offs, then closed up on the day at $127.44. That beat Tesla, Nvidia (down 4%), Micron (down 4–5%) and neoclouds (down 2.6%). The host credits two things for the rebound: investors checking compute prices and concluding the story was overblown, and what he and viewers describe as a government approval that day for 1 million (data-center) satellites.
2. **Final lockup expiry December 8:** The last big unlock, about 800 million shares or 18% of float, comes in 13 days on 2026-12-08. The host expects it to be a headwind but far smaller than the October 22/24 unlock, which he says released 53% of float.
3. **Host's positioning:** He "loaded up" on SpaceX calls, which he says he rarely does:
   - short-dated $160 and $170 calls expiring after January earnings;
   - LEAPs at a $200 strike for December 2027;
   - more broadly, a liking for $200–240 calls.

   He prefers long calls over short puts on SpaceX because its option premiums are thin. He expects a SpaceX share split only around $1,000 a share.

## Financials
1. **Expected merger terms:** The host sees the community converging on a roughly 30% premium for Tesla, around $500 per Tesla share. He thinks that would win a 50% yes vote from Tesla holders. The SpaceX board must also avoid a premium so rich that SpaceX minority shareholders sue. He would vote yes at $500/30% and gets "iffy" below a 20% premium.
2. **Merger as a SpaceX headwind, Tesla tailwind:** With about three SpaceX shares equal to one Tesla share today, he recommends overweighting Tesla roughly 2:1 to 3:1 by dollar value while adding some SpaceX. He frames SpaceX as the side that pays the premium and expects some SpaceX weakness if a deal is announced before year-end. To Tesla holders worried about missing robotaxi upside, he argues the journey continues in SpaceX shares. His comparison: 1 million robotaxis produce roughly the cash of 1 GW of compute, so SpaceX adding 10 GW in 2027 equals the earnings of about 10 million robotaxis.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab registrations nearly doubling:** The host reports 587 Cybercabs registered with the Texas DMV, 283 of them added in one week (about 93% weekly growth), plus 49 more overnight. He forecasts clearly more than 1,000 registered and deployed by year-end, and possibly 2,000 of each. He concedes his earlier call of 10,000 by year-end probably won't happen.
   - He later describes the jump as "100% deployment" growth. The figures he cites are DMV registrations, not deployments, so they do not establish that deployment doubled.
   - He estimates each Cybercab earns roughly $30,000–40,000 a year in margin.

## Market & Competition

That is about a 21% drawdown; he described it as roughly 25%, versus 40–50% declines at Nvidia and Micron. With the fear and greed index at 15 (up from 9–10 intraday), he sees a near-bottom setup. He still warns that the market remains fragile and sharp up-days could reverse.
2. **Anthropic shares:** Recently IPO'd Anthropic fell as much as 13% before recovering from about $74 to close around $82. The host thinks it may have bottomed but calls it venture-like and too risky for him to own.
