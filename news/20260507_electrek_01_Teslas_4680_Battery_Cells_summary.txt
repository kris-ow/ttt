Five years after Tesla's Battery Day promises, real-world data shows the 4680 cell consistently underperforms the supplier cells it is replacing — delivering lower energy density, less range, and slower charging. Tesla is now quietly substituting 4680 "8L" packs for LG cells in European Model Y vehicles, triggering consumer backlash over an undisclosed range downgrade.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Energy density gap:** Tesla's 4680 cells produced at Giga Austin measure 244 Wh/kg, versus 269 Wh/kg for the Panasonic 2170 cells they are meant to replace — a 13% deficit. Tesla has claimed higher density in a latest version, but no independent test results are available.

2. **European Model Y range downgrade:** The new 4680-based "8L" pack carries approximately 79 kWh gross (74 kWh usable), replacing an LG 5M pack rated at 82–84 kWh in the same Model Y Premium Long Range RWD trim. WLTP range drops from 661 km to 609 km — a 52 km (8%) reduction. Pack mass is 447 kg per European certification data.

3. **Charging performance:** First-generation 4680 Model Y (2023) charging power dropped below 100 kW at just 35% state of charge, with a 10–80% charge time exceeding 40 minutes, versus roughly 27–30 minutes for the 2170 Model Y Long Range. An Out of Spec 15-minute test from 10% SOC found the 4680 added only 27 kWh, while a cheaper 62 kWh LFP pack added 29 kWh in the same window. For the European 8L pack, Out of Spec Roaming found charging power already declining at 31% SOC from 155 kW, ranking it the worst battery option in the current European Model Y lineup. Tesla claims up to 250 kW on V4 Superchargers for the 2026 Model Y but no independent confirmation exists for the 8L pack specifically.

4. **Consumer backlash in Europe:** Tesla replaced the LG 5M pack with the 8L without clearly notifying customers who had already placed orders. EV communities in France and Norway are reporting order cancellations. Tesla does not disclose battery type on its configurator or in vehicle specifications, leaving buyers unable to determine which cell they will receive before delivery.

## Market & Competition
5. **Supply chain contraction signals 4680 struggles:** South Korean supplier L&F disclosed its $2.9 billion cathode materials contract with Tesla for 4680 production has been written down to just $7,386 — a 99.9% reduction — citing a "change in supply quantity." The Cybertruck, the primary 4680 vehicle, is selling at an estimated 20,000–25,000 units annually against a factory capacity of 250,000. Tesla's original production targets of 100 GWh by 2023 and 3,000 GWh by 2030 have not been met; as of 2026, the cells are going into one European Model Y trim, select US configurations, and the Cybertruck.

6. **Elon Musk acknowledged core technology failure:** At the 2025 shareholder meeting, Musk stated the dry battery electrode process — the manufacturing centerpiece acquired from Maxwell Technologies and central to Battery Day promises — turned out to be "way harder" than expected and was a mistake.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla's 4680 program has failed to deliver competitive battery cells after five years and billions of dollars, falling behind Panasonic on energy density, LG on charging, and even CATL's LFP chemistry. He contends the quiet cell swap in European Model Ys amounts to a "bait-and-switch" for customers and that the 4680 is at least partly responsible for the Cybertruck's commercial failure.
