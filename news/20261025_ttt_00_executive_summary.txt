## Brief

- SpaceX Q3: $14.6B revenue, first GAAP profit since IPO
- SpaceX compute (SI) segment hits $7.56B, now bigger than Starlink
- SpaceX guides to $33-37B revenue in Q1 2027
- Tesla FSD subscription attach rate hits 75% of buyers
- Customer-car robotaxi (geofenced Austin) targeted Q2 next year
- Starship Flight 15 to attempt full booster-and-ship capture

## Financials
- SpaceX reported $14.6 billion Q3 revenue, up 161% year-over-year, beating consensus of ~$12.7 billion; net income was $4.4 billion versus a $1.1 billion loss a year ago, its first profitable quarter since IPO, though only ~$2.8 billion came from operating activity per CNBC.
- SI (compute) segment revenue hit $7.56 billion (+830% QoQ), exceeding Starlink's $5.25 billion (+69%) for the first time; launch revenue was $1.77 billion (+6%), per Sawyer Merritt's breakdown of the call.
- CFO Bret Johnson reaffirmed $100 billion annualized revenue run-rate by December and guided Q1 2027 revenue to $33-37 billion; Musk's separate claim of $500B-$1T annual revenue by end of 2027 was self-described as a "crazy assessment" and not echoed by Johnson.
- SpaceX is completing a $40 billion debt raise led by Apollo and Goldman Sachs (~8.3% and ~7.75% tranches), funding Q4 capex guidance of $35-40 billion; JPMorgan reported no equity raise currently under consideration.
- Tesla Q3 EPS came in at 22 cents vs. 23-cent consensus, but free cash flow was positive $1.5 billion with $43 billion cash on hand; next year's capex guidance was cut from $25 billion to $20 billion, and ZEV credits are reportedly nearly exhausted.
- Tesla's less-polished earnings call delivery drew investor criticism versus SpaceX's produced broadcast; Tesla said a video format is planned for next quarter.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla registered 11 more Cybercabs with the Texas DMV, bringing cumulative registrations to 433 — a DMV filing count, distinct from operational fleet figures.
- Cybercab rides gained a rearview camera view and a "quiet mode" that dims the interior display.
- Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy indicated unsupervised customer-owned cars could join the robotaxi fleet by Q2 next year, starting geofenced in Austin — faster than one commentator's prior expectation of late next year or 2028.
- Cybercab sales to customers are expected to begin this year, with a significant production ramp anticipated in Q3/Q4 next year; management reportedly frames regulatory approval, not software or hardware readiness, as the main scaling bottleneck.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's FSD subscription "connection rate" — new buyers taking a paid subscription after trial — reached 75%, per the CFO.
- Tesla reportedly announced zero accidents, zero airbag deployments, and zero injuries since an unspecified FSD v15 program began, ahead of broader deployment into highways and varied conditions.
- FSD V14.3.13 began a minor rollout with release notes unchanged from the prior version, suggesting a stability patch.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus is already in production, but Tesla said units won't reach customers until end of next year at the earliest; manipulator design, supply chains, and AI are still being iterated.
- Musk reiterated a plan to build roughly 15,000–20,000 Optimus units to learn via real-world task repetition, a "broad learning" approach contrasted with competitor Figure's use of human-recorded demonstration video.
- Industrial sales are targeted to start around Q3 2026 (end of next year), per Musk; speculation about pre-sale industrial leasing to companies was explicitly framed as a commentator's own read, not a management statement.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX is installing roughly 200,000 Nvidia GB300 chips per month and targets 2.5-3 gigawatts of compute by year-end; compute capacity hit 1.7 gigawatts at end of September, above the "2+ gigawatt" guidance given in August.
- Musk said 13 gigawatts could be connected by end of next year with an internal plan for 20 gigawatts of power and cooling capacity, cautioning he doesn't expect everything to go perfectly.
- SpaceX casts its own turbine blades using its rocket-engine foundry, avoiding supplier lead times of 12-18 months quoted to competitors.
- Grok's "Grokbot" product reached 800,000 paid weekly users, more than double the prior month; the Cursor acquisition (closed August 14, $60 billion) generated $608 million in revenue in roughly seven weeks.
- New compute capacity reportedly pays back in under a year, with Musk suggesting next year's payback could fall to as little as six months; SpaceX plans to lease roughly half its compute externally on short-term contracts to capture rising spot prices.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink added 1.6 million consumer subscriptions to reach 13.6 million; operating profit nearly doubled to $2.4 billion.
- Aviation contracts now cover 12,000 aircraft (~3,800 installed as of end-September); Starlink Mobile reached 22 million monthly users (+37% QoQ).
- SpaceX agreed October 8 to buy a nationwide 800 MHz spectrum block valued by Reuters at ~$8 billion, plus FCC approval for three additional TriStar licenses; Starlink Mobile's launch target slipped to early 2028 from late 2027, per Shotwell.
- Bloomberg reported SpaceX is in talks to acquire at least 80,000 cell tower sites (~half of all US towers), including with Crown Castle; AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile jointly lobbied the FCC on October 20 against the move, and AT&T/T-Mobile shares fell 7-8% intraday on the report.
- AT&T's CEO called LEO satellite constellations "fantastic for delivering bandwidth to rural areas" while defending AT&T's network edge.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's 14th flight reached orbit for the first time and deployed 26 new V3 Starlink satellites, which SpaceX says added roughly 10x the Starlink capacity of a Falcon 9 mission.
- Flight 15, expected mid-November, will attempt to catch both booster and ship — the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle; Musk called success "likely" but not guaranteed.
- SpaceX targets weekly Starship flights in H1 next year, rising to twice-weekly by year-end; a second Starbase site in Louisiana carries an official 2029 first-launch target (internal goal 2028).
- A NASA safety committee member estimated ~22 launches needed for lunar refueling to support a Starship Moon landing; Musk countered the real number will be closer to six or eight.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Six banks raised SpaceX price targets following earnings: JPMorgan $240→$500, Barclays $254→$300, Bank of America →$275, Wells Fargo →$274, Citi →$240, UBS →$230; Morgan Stanley held at $300.
- Musk called the SpaceX-Tesla merger question a "strange kabuki dance" given his conflict of interest, pushed back on it being "inevitable," but said he thinks it would be a good idea; SpaceX's Thursday report confirmed no decision or terms yet.
- A separate commentator reiterated skepticism that a merger benefits Tesla shareholders beyond a stock-price premium, noting the two companies' deal committees remain unable to reach a mutually agreeable proposal.
- SpaceX purchased $151 million of Megapacks from Tesla last quarter ($480 million year-to-date); Musk said he plans to give one SpaceX share to every child in America next year.
