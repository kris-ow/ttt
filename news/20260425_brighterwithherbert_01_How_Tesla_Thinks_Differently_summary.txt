This video features host Herbert and guest Cern Basher, a chartered financial analyst and founder of Brilliant Advice wealth management, conducting a detailed line-by-line review of Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call transcript. Their core argument is that the market's negative reaction to the call was largely driven by misaligned expectations rather than any fundamental deterioration in Tesla's business, and that a careful reading of the actual transcript reveals a significantly more positive picture than Wall Street's initial response suggested. The discussion spans capex strategy, Cybercab production, robotaxi expansion, FSD adoption metrics, Terafab, hardware 3 upgrades, and Optimus — consistently framing Tesla's approach as long-term and structurally different from how most public companies communicate.

## Financials

1. **Capex Acceleration and Negative Free Cash Flow Guidance:** The CFO stated that Tesla ended Q1 with just over $1.4 billion in free cash flow and that current expectations for 2026 capex are over $25 billion. He explicitly warned that Tesla expects negative free cash flow for the remaining three quarters of the year as the company enters a "very big capital investment phase" lasting a couple of years. Cern Basher and Herbert both frame this as a positive signal, drawing comparisons to Amazon's historical willingness to run free cash flow negative during expansion phases, and noting that Tesla has historically underspent its stated capex targets, so actual spend could exceed $25 billion.

2. **Six Factories Ramping Simultaneously:** The CFO noted that Tesla is paying for six factories currently going into or coming into operation, with some already started and others coming online later in 2026. This context was offered to explain the magnitude of the capex commitment and its multi-year duration.

3. **Tesla's Capital Efficiency Advantage:** Both hosts emphasized that Tesla gets significantly more output per dollar of capex than most other large technology companies, framing the increased spend as more valuable than a comparable outlay from a competitor. This was cited as a distinguishing characteristic separating Tesla from other Mag 7 companies also increasing capex.

4. **FSD Subscription Revenue Proxy:** Cern Basher calculated that with 1.28 million paid FSD customers globally at $99/month, the implied annualized run rate would be approximately $380 million per quarter — coincidentally the same figure as Tesla's regulatory credit revenue in Q1. He clarified this is a proxy calculation assuming all subscribers pay the subscription rate, not Tesla's actual reported FSD revenue, since some users purchased FSD upfront with revenue recognized over time.

5. **Elon's Communication Style vs. Nvidia's Investor Relations:** The hosts contrasted Tesla's earnings calls unfavorably with Nvidia's approach, noting Nvidia provides specific revenue guidance for the following quarter and then consistently beats it by several billion dollars, while also providing gross margin ranges. Tesla provides neither, which creates expectation management problems with institutional investors and algorithmic trading systems that respond to CEO sentiment.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3 as the Last Architectural Piece for Unsupervised FSD:** Elon was quoted directly on the call saying "I think version 14.3 is the last piece of the puzzle for unsupervised FSD." He hedged with "I think" rather than stating it definitively. The hosts interpreted this as highly meaningful — meaning a new major version is not required before unsupervised FSD can be deployed at meaningful scale, and that the 14.3 series is already running in Austin, Dallas, and Houston unsupervised.

2. **Version 15 — Complete Architecture Overhaul Running on AI4:** Elon said version 15 would be a "complete overhaul of the software architecture" running on AI4 hardware, coming "hopefully later this year" but "certainly by early next year." He stated that at that point the focus would be on raising FSD safety above human safety levels by another magnitude. Ashok confirmed on the call that robotaxi operations in Austin, Dallas, and Houston would continue expanding on the 14.3 base "for a while" until version 15 lands. The hosts noted this is not a prerequisite for initial robotaxi scaling — only for what Elon referred to as "very large scale" global deployment.

3. **Operational Issues, Not Safety Issues, Are the Limiting Factor:** Elon explicitly stated on the call that "a lot of what limits wider deployment of robo taxi are actually not safety issues, but convenience issues" — specifically citing cases where the car gets "paranoid and gets stuck," including literal infinite loop scenarios where the car repeatedly routes to the same blocked road. Ashok confirmed zero safety incidents to date across the robotaxi fleet. Herbert interpreted this to mean that incremental point releases within the 14.3 series (such as adding reasoning to more driving scenarios beyond end-destination parking, where it was already applied in 14.3.1) could resolve these issues without waiting for version 15.

4. **FSD Adoption Reaching 1.28 Million Paid Customers:** The CFO reported nearly 1.3 million paid FSD customers globally at the end of Q1, with the bulk of Q1 growth coming from subscriptions rather than upfront purchases. Upfront purchases increased only 7% as Tesla removed the purchase option in some markets during Q1. Cern Basher calculated the fleetwide take rate at approximately 14% of all vehicles ever delivered (roughly 9.2 million cumulative deliveries), noting the true take rate on eligible active vehicles is likely closer to 15% or higher.

5. **Incremental FSD Take Rate Exceeding 50% in Q1:** Cern Basher's chart analysis showed that the incremental FSD take rate — the percentage of new subscribers relative to vehicles delivered in the quarter (approximately 358,000) — exceeded 50% in Q1 2026. He cautioned this is partly inflated by existing owners subscribing for the first time rather than all being new vehicle buyers, and also potentially spiked by the "last chance to buy" FSD before the purchase option was removed. He expects Q2 to normalize but still expects it to remain above the previous high of 26%.

6. **FSD Usage Per Subscriber Nearly Doubling Year-Over-Year:** Cern Basher presented a calculation showing that in Q1 2025, the average FSD subscriber drove approximately 11 miles per day on FSD. By Q1 2026, that figure had risen to over 18 miles per day — nearly double. He framed this as evidence that FSD is transitioning from an occasional feature to a primary driving mode for subscribers, corroborated by anecdotal reports of users running FSD for 80–100% of their miles.

7. **Hardware 3 Cannot Achieve Unsupervised FSD:** Elon confirmed on the call that hardware 3 "simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD." Tesla's proposed solution includes offering a discounted trade-in for hardware 4 vehicles, and offering a hardware upgrade path involving replacement of both the computer and cameras. To execute upgrades efficiently, Tesla plans to set up micro-factories in major metropolitan areas rather than processing upgrades at service centers, which was described as too slow. In the interim, Tesla will release a distilled version of the V14 software for hardware 3 vehicles that includes all V14 features except unsupervised capability.

8. **FSD Regulatory Progress in Europe and China:** The CFO reported receiving FSD approval in the Netherlands, describing it as setting up "well for EU-wide approval later in Q2." Supervised FSD was stated to be going to Brussels for EU review in May. For China, partial approvals have been received, with broader approval targeted for Q3 (which the host interpreted as a July 1st target). Elon said unsupervised FSD and robotaxi in Europe is a goal but the timeline is "at the mercy of regulators" and he said he does not know the timeframe.

9. **Unsupervised FSD to Customer-Owned Vehicles — Geography-Locked Rollout:** Elon stated he expects unsupervised FSD to reach customer vehicles "probably in the fourth quarter," but clarified it would be rolled out gradually and geography-locked — meaning owners in areas already validated for the robotaxi service would be enabled for unsupervised FSD within that same geography first.

## Robotaxi

1. **Expansion to Dallas and Houston — Unsupervised with No Safety Driver:** The earnings call confirmed Tesla expanded its robotaxi service to Dallas and Houston using the same software running in the Bay Area (Austin). The hosts noted that both new city deployments launched with unsupervised vehicles — no safety driver present — which they framed as a meaningful positive signal given the prior expectation that Tesla would follow a graduated safety driver phase-in. The stated limiting factor for faster expansion is "rigorous validation," not safety incidents.

2. **Cybercab Production Start as the Leading Indicator for Fleet Size:** Both hosts converged on the view that Cybercab production rate is the most meaningful leading indicator for the eventual robotaxi fleet size, since there is no logical reason to produce Cybercabs and warehouse them. Herbert's personal estimate for total robotaxi fleet size by end of 2026 is 10,000–15,000 vehicles, which he described as consistent with his expectations prior to the earnings call. He derived this estimate by assuming roughly 20 cities, approximately 500+ Cybercabs per city.

3. **Robotaxi Revenue — "Not Super Material" in 2026, "Material in a Significant Way" in 2027:** Elon said on the call that "unsupervised FSD or robo taxi revenue would not be super material this year" but "I do think it'll be material, probably in a significant way next year." The hosts spent considerable time discussing the ambiguity of the word "material," with Cern Basher suggesting a standard accounting definition might be approximately 5% of revenue, while Herbert suggested $500 million might qualify, and others in the Tesla community may interpret the threshold differently.

4. **12 States Expansion Target for 2026 — Scale Definition Disputed:** Elon said Tesla would expand robotaxi to "probably a dozen states or more later this year." The hosts debated what this implies in terms of vehicle count, with Herbert arguing that 12 states likely means at least 20 cities (given California alone could have three or four metro areas, and Florida similarly), and that each city would likely have at least 500 Cybercabs, implying a total of approximately 10,000 vehicles — consistent with his prior estimate. The ambiguity of "large scale" versus "very large scale" versus "millions of cars" was identified as the central unresolved communication failure from the call.

5. **QA Fleet Deployed Across the Entire United States:** Ashok confirmed Tesla has "a pretty large QA fleet spread across all of the United States," including rural areas, using engineering prototype vehicles for validation before customer deployment. The hosts observed that sightings of Cybercab engineering prototypes in many cities and rural areas confirm active validation is underway nationwide.

6. **Hardware 3 Retrofit as a Robotaxi Fleet Expansion Tool:** The hosts speculated that Tesla's hardware 3 upgrade program could be structured as a financial incentive to funnel retrofitted vehicles into the robotaxi network — for example, by offering the upgrade at low or no upfront cost in exchange for a commitment to put the car in the robotaxi network for a certain number of hours per month, or by offering a reduced Tesla take rate (cited as an example: reducing Tesla's take from 35% to 20%) as an incentive for owners to contribute their vehicle to the fleet.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Gen 3 Reveal Delayed to August — Not a Production Delay:** The hosts noted that the Optimus Gen 3 reveal, previously expected by April and repeatedly delayed, is now expected in August. Elon's stated rationale was that Tesla would show the robot "as close as possible to production" — meaning the delay in the reveal does not necessarily indicate a delay in production itself. Herbert characterized this framing as an important distinction that many community members missed when interpreting the delay negatively.

2. **Optimus Production Line Replacing S/X Line at Giga Texas:** The earnings call confirmed that Tesla is sunsetting the Model S and Model X production line and converting it to an Optimus production line. Groundbreaking for the Optimus factory on the Giga Texas campus was confirmed as having occurred. Elon addressed this on the call to set expectations around the transition timeline.

3. **No Production Volume Guidance for Optimus:** In contrast to prior earnings calls where Elon gave specific numbers (e.g., 5,000 robots by year-end), he stated on this call that it is "impossible to calculate" how many Optimus units would be produced by year-end. The hosts acknowledged this is frustrating but framed it as Elon responding to criticism for giving numbers he subsequently missed, noting the previous 5,000 target was not met.

4. **Cortex 2 Compute Cluster Ramp — Key for Optimus Development:** The catalyst checklist referenced in the video includes reaching 50% capacity on Cortex 2 as an important upcoming milestone, described as particularly relevant to Optimus development. No specific timeline was given.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Highest Q1 Order Backlog in Over Two Years:** The CFO stated that Tesla ended Q1 with its highest Q1 order backlog in over two years, framing demand as having meaningfully recovered. He cited rising gas prices as an additional tailwind, while noting that the order rate improvement had already begun before gas prices moved higher.

2. **Giga Berlin Record Output of 61,000 Units in Q1:** The CFO specifically highlighted Giga Berlin reaching a record output of over 61,000 units in Q1 2026, and stated that Tesla plans to continue growing volumes not just in Berlin but across all factories.

3. **Battery Pack Capacity as the Binding Production Constraint:** The CFO identified battery pack capacity (not battery cells themselves) as "the biggest limiter" to production volume growth, stating Tesla is "actively working on resolving that."

4. **Vehicle Production Ramp Across All Factories:** Elon stated on the call, "We'll be ramping up production of all vehicles in all factories to the best of our ability through the balance of this year." The CFO added that Tesla is focused on increasing overall production volume, with the Cybercab ramp described as starting slow and going "kind of exponential towards the end of the year."

5. **FSD Now Positioned as the Product; the Car is the Delivery Mechanism:** The CFO explicitly stated that Tesla has "evolved our vehicle sales strategy where we now emphasize FSD as the product and the vehicle is only the delivery mechanism." The hosts interpreted this as a significant strategic pivot, noting it also represents an elevated liability commitment — if FSD is the product being sold, Tesla faces greater accountability if it fails to deliver.
