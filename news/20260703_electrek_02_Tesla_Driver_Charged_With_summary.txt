Electrek reports that Harris County prosecutors have charged Tesla driver Michael David Butler, 44, with manslaughter over a June 19 Katy, Texas crash that killed 76-year-old Martha Avila, and that the criminal complaint reveals he had been Googling that FSD was "too timid" in the weeks before the crash.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Charging document details from telemetry and phone data:** Butler, doing DoorDash deliveries, had FSD engaged; per the complaint he twice pressed the accelerator to override FSD's speed (once through a stop sign, once canceling a left turn FSD had initiated), then held the pedal to the floor for about six seconds, reaching roughly 73 mph before hitting a curb, going airborne, and crashing into Avila's home. No mechanical failure, stuck pedal, or floor-mat issue was found, and medical testing showed no seizure, stroke, heart attack, alcohol, or drugs; Butler told paramedics he recalled changing music and looking at the navigation screen before "passing out."
2. **Google searches cited as evidence of intent:** Prosecutors recovered searches including "Tesla fsd not aggressive enough 2026," "FSD is not aggressive enough for city driving," and "Tesla fsd too timid," which they are using to argue Butler intentionally overrode the vehicle's speed.
3. **Charges and legal status:** Butler was charged July 1 in Harris County's 208th District Court with one count of manslaughter (a second-degree felony carrying two to 20 years), is held on $150,000 bond, and is due in court the following Monday. Avila's family has separately sued Tesla, and the article notes a prior $243 million Florida verdict where a jury assigned 67% blame to a driver misusing Autopilot but still found Tesla 33% liable.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues FSD's behavior is "inconsistent" rather than simply timid — citing Tesla's "Sloth" profile and phantom braking as evidence of overly cautious behavior, versus "Mad Max" mode driving 15-30 mph over the limit — and contends this inconsistency conditions drivers to keep a foot on the accelerator "ready" to correct the car, which he says is a real product problem. He states the driver overriding the accelerator does not exonerate Tesla, since FSD was still steering throughout, and suggests the crash likely would not have happened absent FSD use during a delivery run in a residential area, while also stating he is not absolving the driver of fault.
