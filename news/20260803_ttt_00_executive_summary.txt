## Brief

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first public earnings call as SPCX is scheduled for August 4; stock traded around $108, down 32% from its $135 IPO price, per one source, though a separate source put SPCX at $114.53 (+5.68% on the day). A lockup expiry two days after the call covers roughly 20-30% of insider-held shares (~911 million shares, one source states), which one host suggested may be deliberate sequencing.
- The Wall Street Journal reported Tesla is weighing separating its China business to enable a potential SpaceX merger; Elon Musk publicly called the report "fake news" and said it had never been discussed. Commentators on one source were skeptical the denial is fully accurate, arguing China's resistance to a US military contractor absorbing Tesla's China operations is the key obstacle to any deal.
- Elon Musk responded "few understand" to a claim (originating with commentator Steve Mark Ryan) that SpaceX's AI-compute business could add roughly $100 billion in revenue — "an entire Tesla's worth" — within 12-24 months; this remains speculative and unconfirmed by SpaceX.
- SpaceX's Cursor acquisition was characterized by a guest commentator as a strong, well-timed deal likely to boost Grok's coding capability.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Elon Musk revealed a new data center building, "Mini Hard," said to house the same 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips as the "macro harder" building but in roughly a quarter of the space — called a genuine engineering breakthrough by one commentator. Analyst Brian Wang estimated SpaceX's combined data center buildings hold roughly 990,000 mostly B300-class chips.
- A report (cited secondhand as from "Funda AI") projects SpaceX building 4 gigawatts of compute capacity this year and another 4 next year, potentially generating $70-100+ billion in annual revenue if fully leased — commentary and estimates, not confirmed SpaceX guidance.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Hughes Network Systems filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, with its CEO citing structural competition from low-earth-orbit satellite services; sources gave differing subscriber figures (641,000 per one, 681,000 per another, both down from prior levels). SpaceX reportedly pays Hughes a commission for customers converting to Starlink, part of the prior ~$17 billion EchoStar spectrum deal.
- SpaceX filed plans for Starlink Mobile to grow from roughly 650 satellites currently to "thousands" by end of 2028, targeting speeds up to 150 Mbps on unmodified smartphones, with broad device support expected late 2027.
- First Starlink V3 test satellites successfully deployed solar arrays, laser links, and comms during a ~20-minute suborbital test; Musk called V3 "a game changer."

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's most recent flight ended in a successful soft ocean landing; Musk said SpaceX will attempt a tower catch on the next flight barring data-review issues.
- SpaceX is reportedly near a deal for 130,000 acres of Louisiana Gulf Coast land to expand Starship launch capacity, with an announcement expected from the governor this month.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX won a $1.6 billion Space Force contract for 18 Falcon 9 national-security launches (~$89 million per launch).

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD v14 "Lite," Tesla's HW3-compressed build wide-released July 21, is drawing owner reports of Autopilot computer overheating (thermal faults above 90°C, some at 96°C) and, in some cases, outright computer failures requiring replacement, per Electrek's Fred Lambert. Causation is unconfirmed — the underlying thermal fault predates v14 Lite — but reports are clustering around the update.
- Tesla's FSD test fleet has driven over 121,000 miles in France (per Sawyer Merritt) — one source cited a higher, inconsistent figure of 195,000 km — with more than 60% on urban/rural roads and no notable incidents reported.
- Lemonade Insurance expanded its FSD-linked discount program (~50% lower rates) to Tennessee, its fifth state.
- Tesla executive David Moss called a recent FSD release the company's best yet; Moss and Ashok Elluswamy both defended withholding manual speed override during FSD supervision, with Elluswamy calling it an "anti-pattern."

## Electric Vehicles
- Electrek's review of the 2026 Model Y RWD (Standard) scored it 80.2/100: strong efficiency (463 km/288 mi rated, matched in testing) and NHTSA five-star safety, but weak acceleration (7.2s 0-100km/h, "slowest Tesla yet") and multiple feature cuts versus Premium. FSD (Supervised) now requires a $99/month subscription with no purchase option or standalone Autosteer.
- Tesla will now fully cover Cybertruck PCS2 board (AC/DC inverter) repairs for any Cybertruck within 8 years/150,000 miles regardless of model year, and will 100% reimburse owners who previously paid out of pocket (reported $2,000-$7,000 per repair) — a shift from a formal recall that one commentator called unprecedented for Tesla.
- Model Y led June European D-segment EV sales (34,480 units) and global EV sales (153,000 units); Model Y sales in Colombia rose 61.6% year-over-year.

## Market & Competition
- Uber agreed to invest up to $1.2 billion in Rivian and purchase up to 50,000 R2s through 2031, contingent on Rivian delivering a working robotaxi; one host was skeptical of Rivian's near-term robotaxi timeline.
- Volvo is abandoning its lidar-based approach, refunding Norwegian customers 18,000 kroner each for undelivered promised features.

## Robotaxi

## Humanoid Bots
