## Brief

- SpaceX's first public earnings call set for August 4

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first earnings call as a public company is set for August 4; stock traded near $108-114, still well off its $135 IPO price, with a major insider lockup expiry following two days after the call. Analysts expect low-key guidance, not confirmation of a $100B compute-revenue figure or merger news.
- WSJ reported Tesla is weighing separating its China business to pave a path toward a Tesla-SpaceX merger; Musk publicly called the report "fake news," though hosts on Investing Against the Grain argue a full denial is unlikely to be entirely accurate given the business logic for contingency planning. China's regulatory sensitivity around a US military contractor is seen as the key obstacle.
- Elon Musk, replying to a claim SpaceX could add "an entire Tesla's worth of revenue" (~$100B) via compute rental within 12-24 months, said only "few understand" — commentary treated as unconfirmed by SpaceX itself.
- Jefferies analyst Aniket Shah pushed back on investors avoiding SpaceX over governance/ESG concerns, calling such reluctance overly rigid relative to long-term upside.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's FSD v14 "Lite" build, widely rolled out to HW3 cars via software 2026.20.6.11 on July 21, is linked to rising Autopilot computer thermal-fault reports (APP_w141_ECU_Thermal_Issue, triggering above 90°C, some at 96°C) and outright computer failures requiring replacement, per Electrek reporting from four owner accounts. Causation isn't confirmed — the fault predates v14 Lite — but Electrek's Fred Lambert says the clustering timing makes "it's just old cars" an unsatisfying explanation, especially given Tesla's existing obligation to address ~4 million HW3 cars that can't deliver promised unsupervised self-driving.
- Tesla FSD (Supervised) has surpassed 13 billion cumulative miles including over 5 billion city miles; driverless robotaxis have logged roughly 380,000 miles with zero safety incidents, per figures cited by Jeff Lutz.
- Lemonade Insurance expanded its FSD-linked discount program to Tennessee, its fifth state, offering ~50% lower rates for FSD-supervised miles.
- Tesla logged 121,000+ miles of FSD (Supervised) testing in France with over 60% on urban/rural roads and no notable incidents, per a Tesla post (cited elsewhere as 195,000 km).
- Edge cases persist: FSD v14.3.7 still fails a known "chain test," and a Dallas robotaxi running v15 got stuck in a navigation loop for 3-5 minutes before remote support intervened.
- Tesla executive David Moss called a new FSD release the company's best yet; Moss and Ashok Elluswamy both resisted reintroducing manual speed override, calling it an "anti-pattern."

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck PCS2 board (AC/DC inverter) failures, which have caused charging outages on pre-2026 trucks, will now be covered for 8 years/150,000 miles regardless of model year under a new policy dated July 30, with 100% reimbursement for owners who already paid out of pocket ($2,000-$7,000 per repair, per a Cybertruck-focused creator). The creator calls this an unprecedented move to address a known defect without a formal recall.
- Electrek's review of the 2026 Model Y RWD (Standard, Canadian LFP) scored it 80.2/100: strong range/efficiency (463 km rated, matched in testing) and charging, but the slowest 0-100km/h Tesla yet (7.2s) and reduced features versus Premium; reviewer called FSD (Supervised) the best point-to-point Level 2 system available but flagged two issues and criticized the mandatory $99/month subscription with no Autosteer-only option.
- Model Y led Europe's D-segment (34,480 June units) and global EV sales (153,000 units); Model Y sales in Colombia rose 61.6% year-over-year to 7.9% share.
- A Cybertruck parts supplier disputed Tesla's "hostage" characterization of an equipment dispute, saying it spent $11.9 million tooling up after Tesla's projected orders failed to materialize.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Hughes Network Systems filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy amid a $1.5 billion note coming due, with subscribers falling from roughly 1.5 million (2020) to the 641,000-681,000 range currently (sources vary slightly); its CEO cited structural competition from low-earth-orbit satellite services. Under a prior EchoStar-SpaceX spectrum deal (~$17B), SpaceX pays Hughes a commission for customers who convert to Starlink.
- SpaceX filed plans for Starlink Mobile to grow from ~650 satellites to thousands by end of 2028, part of a second-generation constellation of up to 15,000 satellites, targeting speeds up to 150 Mbps on unmodified smartphones with broad device support expected late 2027.
- First Starlink V3 satellites completed a successful ~20-minute suborbital test (solar arrays, laser links, comms) before planned re-entry; Musk called V3 "a game changer," described as roughly 10x more powerful than V2.
- Starlink direct-to-cell expanded from text to full app support (chat, navigation, maps) for Telstra customers in remote Australia.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's most recent flight achieved a successful ocean splashdown; Musk said SpaceX will attempt a tower catch on the next flight barring issues in data review. ARK's Brent Winton rebutted a Bloomberg piece questioning heat-shield reusability, and Musk said cost to orbit could fall well below $100/kg with full reusability.
- SpaceX is reportedly nearing a deal for 130,000 acres of Louisiana Gulf Coast land to expand Starship launch capacity, tied to its stated goal of thousands of launches per year.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- A report projects SpaceX building 4 gigawatts of AI compute capacity this year and another 4 next year using Nvidia Rubin racks, with estimates (unconfirmed by SpaceX) that full lease-out could generate $70-100B+ in annual revenue, comparable to Tesla's scale. Musk revealed a new, more compact data center building called "Mini Hard" housing 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips in roughly a quarter the space of "macro harder."

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX won a $1.6 billion Space Force contract covering 18 Falcon 9 national-security launches (~$89 million per launch); commentary on the call citing far higher per-launch economics does not reconcile with the stated contract total.

## Humanoid Bots
- Elon Musk said "Optimus will be epic" when asked about a personal robot's potential, with no further detail given. Commentary suggests Optimus V3 is being positioned as a training-data platform ("Optimus University") rather than a near-term consumer product, with a customer-ready V4 not expected until at least H2 next year.

- Uber agreed to invest up to $1.2 billion in Rivian and buy up to 50,000 R2 vehicles through 2031, contingent on Rivian delivering a working robotaxi; one host expressed skepticism about the timeline.
- Volvo abandoned its lidar-based approach, refunding Norwegian customers 18,000 kroner each for undelivered features.
