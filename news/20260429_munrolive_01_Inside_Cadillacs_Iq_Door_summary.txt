This video from the Munro Live channel examines a teardown of the 2025 Cadillac IQ Escalade, focusing specifically on the door manufacturing process. The core point is to highlight an efficient two-shot injection molding technique used to integrate decorative chrome trim into a single door component.

## Market & Competition
1. **Two-shot injection molding for integrated chrome trim:** The Cadillac IQ Escalade's door features a decorative chrome trim that is not a separate part but is fully integrated into the door panel assembly. This is achieved through a two-shot molding process: first, a polycarbonate layer is injection molded, then ABS plastic is injection molded over the top of it. The entire combined part is then submerged in a chrome plating tank. Because chrome only adheres to the ABS portion of the part, the chrome coating is selectively applied only where needed — avoiding the cost of chroming the entire structure while still delivering a seamless, single-component result.

Notable quotes and data:
- The vehicle examined is the **2025 Cadillac IQ Escalade**.
- The manufacturing technique is a **two-shot mold** using **polycarbonate** and **ABS** plastics.
- Chrome selectively adheres **only to the ABS** material, not the polycarbonate, enabling cost-efficient partial chroming of a single integrated part.
