The video covers a cluster of Tesla milestones centered on the Cybercab robotaxi program: the first confirmed production Cybercab (no steering wheel, no pedals) driving autonomously on public roads in Austin, two new regulatory steps in Texas and Florida signaling imminent commercial deployment, and Tesla's first major Terafab leadership hire. Host Herbert (brighterwithherbert) is joined by Brian White of the FutureAzA YouTube channel to analyze what these developments collectively mean for Tesla's near-term robotaxi launch timeline.

## Robotaxi

1. **Production Cybercab confirmed driving autonomously in Austin:** Elon Musk publicly confirmed that a production Cybercab — with no steering wheel and no pedals — is driving on public roads in Austin in what Tesla calls an "engineering test." A safety monitor is present in the vehicle but is not operating it. Ashok Elluswamy separately confirmed the vehicle is driving by itself. The hosts note that multiple Cybercab storage lots and depots have been discovered around the country by community spotters, and that over a thousand units are believed to be out and ready, which the hosts argue is inconsistent with a timeline that is still a year or more from deployment.

2. **Vast majority of produced Cybercabs still in supervised FSD data-collection phase:** Per Brian White, citing his interview with Lars Moravy, the vast majority of Cybercabs currently produced are still in a supervised testing phase accumulating FSD autonomy miles in the new form factor. White estimates this phase will require a few more months before broader commercial deployment, noting the Cybercab's different form factor requires its own dataset before Tesla can confidently scale unsupervised operation.

3. **Texas DPS adds Cybercab to connected autonomous vehicles registry:** The Texas Department of Public Safety officially added the Tesla Cybercab robotaxi to its connected autonomous vehicles first-responder interaction plans page. The hosts describe this as a strict regulatory prerequisite for Level 4 operations at scale, meaning police, fire, and EMS in Texas now have a standardized protocol for interacting with a driverless Cybercab — a concrete operational step ahead of a wider Texas launch.

4. **Miami-area first responders complete Cybercab safety training:** In Coral Gables, Florida, fire personnel participated in a specialized training session with Tesla covering vehicle safety features, battery systems, emergency access points, and best practices for autonomous vehicle incidents. The hosts note Tesla has no active robotaxi service in Miami yet, framing this training as a strong signal of planned near-term deployment in the region at meaningful scale.

5. **Tesla missed its end-of-June five-city robotaxi expansion target:** Tesla had committed on earnings calls and in investor materials to launching robotaxi service in five additional cities by the end of June 2026. That deadline passed without the launches. Brian White characterizes autonomy timeline misses as expected, but the host emphasizes this was a formal investor-deck commitment reiterated as recently as months before the deadline — not a casual Elon prediction — and therefore a meaningful miss. The hosts speculate that a July 7 event teased by Lars Moravy around Giga Texas may be the occasion for announcing those city launches, with the holiday weekend cited as the likely reason for the brief delay.

6. **Lars Moravy teases July 7 announcement related to Giga Texas and Cybercab scaling:** In a recently published interview (discussed in detail in a companion video on the FutureAzA channel), Lars Moravy indicated he has "cool news" about something happening around Giga Texas related to Cybercab scaling. Brian White interprets this as likely a deployment or scaling announcement rather than a manufacturing update, reasoning there is no point in continuing to ramp Cybercab production without a corresponding deployment plan. Moravy also stated that Tesla is not building five or ten Cybercabs — they expect it to outsell the Model Y.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD version 14 "light" rollout to Hardware 3 vehicles began by June 30:** Ashok Elluswamy had committed to delivering FSD v14 light to Hardware 3 vehicles by June 30, 2026. The hosts confirm a small number of Hardware 3 cars received it by that date, including some in Canada. Brian White notes the rollout appears broader than just influencers, since regular owners who receive it typically do not post publicly. The host's own Hardware 3 vehicle had not yet received it as of filming.

2. **Gap between Hardware 3 and Hardware 4 FSD performance may be narrowing with v14 light:** Brian White, who drove a Hardware 3 vehicle for several days last December and noted it was "very good but not Hardware 4," observed in June 2026 that the gap between the two was widening. Based on videos from trusted channels, he now believes v14 light has narrowed that gap again. He is uncertain whether Hardware 3 can reach unsupervised capability and suggests Tesla may offer a discounted FSD subscription tier (around $65–70 vs. the current $99) for Hardware 3 owners who do not need unsupervised driving, alongside a paid Hardware 4 chip upgrade path for those who do.

## Electric Vehicles

## Market & Competition

1. **Terafab hires first major semiconductor executive — Gary Jang from Intel:** Tesla (in a joint venture with SpaceX) hired Gary Jang, a semiconductor manufacturing veteran with nearly 18 years at Intel, as Director of Terafab — the planned chip fabrication facility in Austin. The hosts frame this as Tesla building custom chips optimized for its specific AI and autonomy workloads rather than general-purpose silicon, drawing a parallel to a Swiss Army knife trimmed of unnecessary tools. They note the Intel partnership context makes the hire a natural fit rather than a simple talent raid, and argue it applies competitive pressure on existing chip suppliers — pointing to Samsung announcing a production increase and Intel reactivating mothballed fabs as early signs.
