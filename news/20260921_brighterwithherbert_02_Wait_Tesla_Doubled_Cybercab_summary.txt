This video, hosted by Herbert with guest supply-chain executive Jeff Lutz, covers a cluster of Tesla and SpaceX announcements from the weekend of September 21, 2026: an apparent doubling of Cybercab production based on VIN registration data, a seventh European country approving FSD Supervised ahead of an EU-wide vote, a newly announced Boring Company hyperloop project between Austin and San Antonio, and SpaceX's FCC filing for 100,000 Starlink Gen 3 satellites that reportedly double as orbital AI data centers.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab VIN data suggests production has roughly doubled:** Nine more Cybercabs were registered in Texas, bringing the tracked Robotaxi-registered fleet to 58 Cybercabs within a 448-vehicle total Texas fleet (390 Model Y, 58 Cybercab). Tracking VIN numbers, the host estimates about 321 Cybercab VINs were added between August 9–31 (roughly 100–150/week), versus about 865 added between August 31–September 19 (roughly 300+/week) — a host-derived estimate from VIN sequence tracking, not an official Tesla figure.
2. **Guest assessment: deliberate "pulsed" ramp, not yet a mass ramp:** Jeff Lutz characterized the ramp as slow and steady rather than massive, suggesting Tesla is building batches, pausing to apply fixes, and getting field data before scaling further — likely yielding higher quality with less scrap versus a rushed ramp, but still carrying a fixed-cost absorption penalty from running below full line rate.
3. **Three likely gating factors on faster deployment, per Lutz:** (1) software performance and regulatory approval limits on how many vehicles can be usefully deployed; (2) a hardware/line changeover to integrate Starlink connectivity into the Cybercab, which requires retooling and retraining at that station; (3) possible yield or process issues (e.g., in reaction injection molding) being resolved at low volume before scaling.
4. **Houston fleet visibly growing:** A viewer-submitted parking lot image showed a larger-than-before mix of Model Ys and Cybercabs in Houston, which Lutz said likely reflects a mix of ride-ready and verification/data-collection vehicles (including a LIDAR-rigged unit for ground-truth data), and which he reads as a signal of increasing testing and/or production ahead of scaling.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Czech Republic becomes seventh European country to pre-approve FSD Supervised:** Following Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, and Slovenia, the Czech Republic's approval (reported via Tesla Europe and a source called Roland Percher) brings FSD Supervised to 15 countries worldwide. These seven countries together represent only about 11.8% of the 65% of the EU population needed for automatic approval ahead of an EU-wide vote scheduled for October 6. The host and Lutz both suggest the timing — approving just weeks before the EU vote — implies confidence the EU vote will pass.
2. **Lutz flags localization and political dynamics behind Europe's rollout:** Lutz argued some of the slow, piecemeal European approval process reflects political and localization requirements (UI/software behavior requests from individual countries) rather than safety concerns, drawing a parallel to slow past European adoption of 3G/4G/5G technology.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship enables next-gen direct-to-cell and orbital AI compute:** A SpaceX CFO, interviewed alongside Elon Musk, said Starship's larger payload bay will enable next-generation direct-to-device Starlink connectivity and a configuration of satellites (same bus, more solar, deployable radiators) dedicated to orbital compute, calling Starship "such a catalyst" for the company's AI ambitions.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **100,000-satellite Gen 3 Starlink filing enters FCC review:** SpaceX's application to deploy up to 100,000 Gen 3 Starlink satellites across two very-low-Earth-orbit altitude groups was accepted by the FCC for review on September 18.
2. **Gen 3 satellites reportedly combine Starlink with onboard AI compute ("Starmine"):** Elon Musk said each Gen 3 satellite will carry 250 kW of power, connectivity of roughly 10 terabits per satellite (with a path to 100+ terabits), and an SpaceX-designed Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 computer — suggesting Starlink Gen 3 satellites will double as space-based AI data centers ("Starmine"), rather than being a separate satellite class as previously assumed.
3. **Starlink V5 terminal now in production:** Musk announced the next-generation Starlink V5 terminal — smaller, lighter, more power-efficient, delivering home internet speeds up to 375 megabits per second — is now in production, made largely in a Bastrop, Texas factory Lutz described as highly automated, vertically integrated PCB assembly.
4. **Starlink direct-to-phone V2 network expansion announced for Australia (Sept 17):** Musk said the V2 direct-to-phone satellite network will deliver 5G speeds from space with roughly 100 times the data density/bandwidth of the V1 network.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX CFO targets 100 GW/year of orbital compute deployment by end of decade:** A SpaceX CFO said the company is on track to deploy 100 gigawatts of orbital compute per year by the end of the decade, up from a current base the host estimated at under 5 GW (with 5–10 GW targeted for next year) — a figure the CFO stated and Musk affirmed on the record. Lutz noted terrestrial compute projections for next year are around 40 GW, underscoring the scale of SpaceX's orbital ambition relative to Earth-based buildouts; he cautioned these are company projections that "could be wrong" but reflect the industry's broader capex trajectory.
2. **Grok 4.7 released:** SpaceX/xAI released Grok 4.7, described in the video as SpaceX's most powerful model for coding and knowledge, with claimed strengths in legal and electrical engineering benchmarks and lower cost-per-token than competitors, though weaker in some multi-hour office/software-terminal tasks. Musk reportedly said Grok 4.7 would not surpass all other large language models, with that milestone expected only at Grok 5.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX's NASDAQ index weight more than doubled:** The video states SpaceX's NASDAQ weighting more than doubled to 2.62% as of Monday (date not otherwise specified).

## Market & Competition
1. **Boring Company hyperloop precursor tunnel announced for Austin–San Antonio:** Musk announced the Boring Company is working on what he called a "simple precursor hyperloop tunnel" between Austin and San Antonio, targeting speeds over 200 mph and cutting a roughly 2.5-hour drive to under 30 minutes. Lutz interpreted this as at least preliminary approval to pursue the project and speculated (his own view, not confirmed) about possible future synergies or even an eventual Tesla/SpaceX acquisition of the Boring Company, given overlapping transportation-infrastructure interests.
2. **Musk invited to Trump–Xi state dinner:** Musk was invited by President Trump to a White House state dinner with China's president, alongside other tech leaders including Jeff Bezos, Sam Altman, Sundar Pichai, Michael Dell, and Tim Cook. Lutz speculated AI policy framework discussions between the US and China are likely on the agenda, alongside potential discussion of a joint letter from six major US auto associations calling for a ban on Chinese automakers selling, importing, or building factories in the US; Lutz argued such a ban is unlikely to materially change during the current political climate and that Tesla — as the top-selling vehicle brand in China via the Model Y — "would not be toast" even if Chinese competition increased domestically.
