## Robotaxi

- **Texas DOT Executive Director endorses Cybercab scale-up.** Mark Williams, Executive Director of the Texas Department of Transportation, stated at the 2026 Texas Innovation Invitational that he had ridden in a production Cybercab firsthand, confirmed Tesla is currently operating Model Y robotaxis in Austin, Dallas, and Houston in FSD mode, and said "the introduction of the Cybercab will dramatically scale these operations over the coming months." Bhakdi notes Williams, as the state official overseeing AV operations, almost certainly received a prior briefing from Tesla before speaking publicly.

- **100+ Cybercabs accumulating at Giga Texas.** Drone footage credited to Joe Tegtmeyer and a count attributed to Sawyer Merritt showed more than 100 Cybercabs in the Giga Texas lot. Some units were identified as production-spec vehicles without steering wheels — interpreted as units destined for commercial deployment rather than supervised testing.

- **No public evidence of supervised Cybercab FSD testing yet.** Despite visible production-spec Cybercabs accumulating on-site, Bhakdi notes there is still no strong public evidence of Cybercabs operating under FSD supervision even for testing purposes, which he flags as an open question ahead of any commercial launch.

- **Cybercab EPA certification confirms range and specs.** Official EPA filings — the first third-party-verified technical portrait of the vehicle — list a 48 kWh battery, single front-wheel drive motor producing 219 hp, curb weight of 3,113 lbs, and an equivalent all-electric range of 418 miles with a highway range of 375 miles. The range figure materially exceeds the previously anticipated ~375 miles and outperforms the base Model 3 despite a smaller battery and lower weight. EPA certified useful life is 150,000 miles; the test vehicle had 2,100+ miles at submission.

- **Cybercab vs. Waymo unit economics.** Bhakdi frames the 418-mile range as roughly three times a comparable Waymo vehicle's operational range, meaning one Cybercab provides the uptime equivalent of three Waymos. Combined with the Cybercab costing approximately one-third the price of a Waymo vehicle, he characterizes this as a "one to nine" structural cost advantage before accounting for maintenance.

- **June deployment commitment and end-of-year forecast.** Bhakdi cites Tesla's prior promise of robotaxi service in five cities by end of June 2026 and states his "local probabilistic maximum" for commercial launch is this month, with August as the next likely window. His end-of-year price target of $800–$1,000 is explicitly conditional on deployment scaling to 15,000 Cybercab units.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Four consecutive months of zero FSD-at-fault incidents.** The latest NHTSA filing (covering April 15–May 15) shows only one new incident: a stationary robotaxi rear-ended by a human-driven pickup truck — not Tesla's fault. The two prior incidents in March were also rear-end collisions into parked robotaxis. Tesla has recorded zero at-fault FSD incidents since early February 2026. Elon Musk publicly noted the irony that all recent crashes involving the robotaxi fleet were caused by other human drivers.

- **FSD 14.3.4 described as a major leap.** Bhakdi characterizes version 14.3.4 — which launched the prior week — as significantly more than a point release, calling it a "very big leap" built on the architectural overhaul of 14.3. He cites "crazy positive reviews" and references Elon Musk's earnings call statement that Tesla has "solved safety," with the remaining engineering challenge being convenience — optimizing the tradeoff between safety and ride quality.

- **New Jersey legislative threat; international approvals advancing.** Tesla is actively fighting proposed New Jersey legislation it says would effectively ban its autonomous vehicles from the state, sending emails directly to NJ owners calling the bill an "arbitrary roadblock." On the positive side: the Dutch Transport Minister publicly defended the RDW's provisional FSD (Supervised) approval, citing 24 million miles driven on Dutch roads "without any noteworthy incidents" using independent RDW testing (not Tesla-provided data); and Tesla filed its FSD (Supervised) application documents in Taiwan with the Vehicle Safety Inspection Center, formally entering the regulatory review process there.

## Energy

- **Megapack production at record output.** Drone footage of Tesla's Lathrop Megafactory shows lots at their fullest level observed to date, with Sawyer Merritt estimating approximately $500 million worth of Megapacks on site — a strong indicator for Q2 Energy revenue and deployment metrics.

- **4680 dry electrode process is a durable manufacturing advantage.** Jordan Giesige (The Limiting Factor) argues Tesla's newly achieved fully dry electrode coating is not a chemistry but a fundamental manufacturing process improvement applicable across all battery chemistries and form factors. He estimates ~44% of Battery Day's original ~56% cost reduction target remains achievable and durable — primarily from cell design (14%), dry electrode manufacturing (18%), and in-house cathode/lithium production (12%). Current 4680 cost savings vs. third-party cells are estimated at 10–20%, not the originally projected 50–60%, but Giesige frames Tesla as a first-mover in a process the entire industry will eventually adopt.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Goldman Sachs raises Q2 delivery forecast to 420,000.** Goldman lifted its Q2 2026 Tesla delivery estimate from 405,000 to 420,000 units, stating deliveries are "likely tracking ahead of consensus." Full-year 2026 estimate is 1.72–1.73 million vehicles — slightly below 2025 levels, but the direction of revision confirms an improving demand trend across China and Europe.

- **Six new-design Tesla Semis spotted at production facility.** Fresh drone footage from the Semi factory (FutureAzA/Zanegler) shows six new-design Semis — identifiable by a full-width headlight bar — in a cordoned corral near the assembly line exit: two long-cab (long-range, three battery modules) and four short-cab (standard-range, two battery modules) variants. ABF Freight separately added two Tesla Semis to its fleet as of June 15.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla sells off ~1.3% in line with NASDAQ on Fed day.** Tesla's intraday decline tracked the broad market, bottoming near -1.6% before recovering to close down approximately 1.34%. The Federal Reserve held rates steady at 3.5%–3.75% under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh; a dramatically shortened post-meeting statement and repeated emphasis on "price stability" sent the 2-year Treasury yield up ~14 basis points. Bhakdi attributes Tesla's entire move to macro sentiment and views it as a buying opportunity.

- **SpaceX acquires Cursor for $60 billion.** SpaceX officially announced the acquisition via 8-K filing; the deal is expected to close in Q3 2026. Bhakdi and Herbert Ong argue the deal gives SpaceX all three layers of the "AI holy trinity" simultaneously — harness (Cursor, used by 64% of Fortune 500), LLM (Grok), and compute (Colossus) — a combination no other company currently holds. Cursor's ARR grew from $500M to $3B in roughly 11 months, with $6B projected by year-end 2026.

- **SpaceX lock-up mechanics create near-term downside risk.** Bhakdi argues the post-IPO SpaceX surge is mechanically driven: three index inclusions (CRSP and FTSE Russell adding June 18, triggering an estimated $10–16B in forced passive buying) are compressing the float while lock-ups prevent selling. He estimates ~1.6 billion shares — roughly 20% of outstanding — become eligible for sale starting mid-August, against a current float of ~5%, and predicts the stock will fall below its $135 IPO price before December 2026.
