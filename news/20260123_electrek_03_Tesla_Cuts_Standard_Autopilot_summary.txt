Tesla has removed Basic Autopilot's Autosteer function as a standard feature on new Model 3 and Model Y orders in North America, requiring buyers to subscribe to the $99/month FSD (Supervised) package to get lane-keeping capability that was previously included free.

## Electric Vehicles / Autonomous Driving
1. **Autosteer no longer standard:** For nearly seven years Tesla included "Basic Autopilot" — Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC) and Autosteer (lane-centering) — standard on all vehicles. Under the new 2026 pricing structure, new vehicles ship with only TACC; Autosteer now requires the paid FSD subscription.
2. **Follows shift away from one-time FSD purchase:** The change comes weeks after Tesla announced it will stop selling FSD as a one-time purchase starting in February, moving to a subscription-only model.

## Financials
1. **Aimed at boosting subscription take rate:** The article frames the removal of free Autosteer as a way to push customers toward the $99/month FSD subscription, amid what the article says has been a low subscription take rate (previously reported by the outlet). Elon Musk has reportedly hinted FSD pricing could rise as the software improves, but the current move is characterized as volume-focused.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert calls the move "bad for consumers and confusing for the brand," arguing lane-keeping is a safety feature that should be made more accessible, not gated. He contrasts Tesla's new policy with Toyota Safety Sense and Honda Sensing, which include standard lane-keeping on cheaper economy cars like the Corolla and Civic, saying Tesla now offers "less standard safety tech than economy cars costing half as much." He frames the change as a "desperation move" tied to Tesla's two-year sales decline and recent loss of U.S. subsidies, and a "cash grab" driven by financial struggles. A quoted top reader comment speculates the change is partly driven by a milestone in Musk's compensation plan requiring 10 million FSD subscriptions, calling it a cash grab "by Elon" rather than by Tesla as a company — the article presents this as a reader opinion, not a verified fact.
