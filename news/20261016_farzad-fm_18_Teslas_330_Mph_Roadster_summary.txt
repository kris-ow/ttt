This segment is a physics explainer examining Tesla's claim that the new Roadster can be upgraded from its current top speed to 330 mph "just" by changing gearing, using drag and power-curve calculations to test whether that claim holds up.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster's current vs. target top speed:** A Tesla representative states the Roadster's current top speed is 214 mph, with the company working on a version targeting 330 mph per an internal challenge attributed to "the boss" (implied to be Elon Musk), described as requiring only a gearing change.
2. **Physics check on the "just gearing" claim:** The host explains that aerodynamic drag scales with the square of speed while required power scales with the cube of speed, so doubling speed requires roughly eight times the power. Using this relationship, the host estimates overcoming air resistance takes about 6 kilowatts at 60 mph, roughly 270 kilowatts at 214 mph, and nearly a full megawatt at 330 mph. Since Tesla claims the top-spec Roadster's powertrain produces close to one megawatt, the host concludes that at 214 mph the car is limited by gearing/engine revolutions rather than power, but that the available megawatt would be almost fully consumed right around 330 mph — meaning the 330 mph target is near the physical edge of what the stated power output can support, and any further speed gains beyond that become disproportionately expensive in power terms (the host notes reaching roughly 380 mph, only ~15% faster, would require over 50% more power).
