This short clip presents a narrow argument about Tesla's Cybercab robotaxi rollout: current wait times are long not because of pricing but because the fleet is still too small relative to rider demand, and the speaker argues that scaling up cheaper vehicle availability — rather than just cutting fares — is what will let Tesla compete for time-sensitive riders and build a durable competitive moat.

## Robotaxi
1. **Long Cybercab wait times attributed to fleet size, not price:** The speaker describes personally waiting roughly 30 minutes for a Cybercab, attributing the delay to the fleet being new and too small relative to current demand, rather than to service pricing.
2. **Availability, not just low fares, as the competitive lever:** The speaker argues that even when a cheaper ride is available, riders who need to leave immediately will often choose a pricier but faster-arriving alternative — meaning that improving fleet availability (more cars deployed) lets Tesla capture that segment of demand too, which the speaker frames as the basis of Tesla's moat in robotaxi.
