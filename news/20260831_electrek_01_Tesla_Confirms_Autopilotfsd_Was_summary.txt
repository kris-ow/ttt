Electrek reports that Tesla's own NHTSA Standing General Order (SGO) filing on a fatal May 2026 Model 3 crash in Clute, Texas shows Autopilot or "Full Self-Driving" was "Verified Engaged" and the car was traveling 104 mph — details absent from all police statements and news coverage, which attributed the crash to a possible medical episode.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Tesla's SGO filing details:** Tesla's report on the crash lists a 2025 Model 3 driven by a consumer, marks automation "Engagement Status" as "Verified Engaged" (meaning Tesla's own telematics confirmed Autopilot or FSD was active), records a pre-crash speed of 104 mph on a road categorized as a "Street," and logs the outcome as a fatality. Electrek says it found the report while analyzing the full SGO dataset, which it states now contains nearly 4,000 Tesla driver-assist crashes.

2. **Heavy redaction obscures the picture:** Tesla redacted the crash narrative, the specific software version, and the field indicating whether the road was within the system's approved operating area, marking them "confidential business information" — redactions Electrek says Tesla applies to 99.9% of its crash reports. Because the software-version field is hidden, the public record cannot confirm whether Autopilot or FSD specifically was engaged.

3. **Discrepancy with public reporting:** The driver, identified as Steven Alvarez, 23, died when his car left Brazoswood Drive, crashed through a fence at Clute Municipal Park, and hit a decommissioned pool complex, catching fire, per local outlets. Clute police's preliminary belief was a medical episode; no police statement or news report cited Autopilot/FSD or any speed figure — the 104 mph figure appears only in Tesla's telematics, with the lone public hint of speed being an unverified social-media mention of a "speeding Tesla."

4. **Unresolved competing explanations:** The article lays out several non-exclusive possibilities: the system may have been driving in a way that contributed to the crash (though Electrek notes Autopilot/FSD do not normally reach 104 mph on a residential street on their own); Tesla's self-reported speed figure could be inaccurate and warrants independent verification; or, similar to a prior Katy, Texas fatal crash where Tesla says FSD was on but blames the driver for flooring the accelerator to 73 mph, Alvarez may have suffered a medical emergency and involuntarily pressed the accelerator while the system was engaged.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the redactions prevent the public and investigators from reconciling Tesla's "verified engaged" data with the police's medical-episode theory, and calls for Tesla to stop redacting SGO crash data, noting Tesla is already under NHTSA investigation over its crash reporting and that its own AI trainers have reportedly told Reuters they don't trust its safety statistics.
