This episode of a Canada-focused EV show discusses an anticipated wave of Chinese automaker entries into the Canadian market, alongside smaller items on Nissan Leaf pricing, EV charger rebate programs, and a Canadian military EV motorcycle. Tesla appears only briefly, in the context of strong Canadian sell-through of imported vehicles.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chinese automakers preparing large-scale Canada entry:** A co-host says Canada's federal government expects Chinese EVs to arrive in much larger numbers, with the hosts estimating roughly 6–8 months out based on vehicles currently being spotted in testing with manufacturer plates; vehicles must still pass Canadian regulatory review (referenced as CMVSS-type standards) even if already certified under Euro NCAP, ANCAP, or similar.
2. **BYD scaling logistics and testing four models:** BYD is reported to be testing four models in Canada while continuing local hiring, and has ordered additional car-carrier ships (each with capacity cited at 9,200 vehicles) to support export volume; models mentioned include the Sea Lion 7 (a Model Y rival) and the Shark plug-in hybrid pickup, the latter noted as filling a gap in Canada's limited electrified pickup options.
3. **MG entering with a pickup offering:** A host notes seeing an MG pickup truck at an auto show in Nepal, with the co-host noting MG has sold in Europe for years; both suggest a smaller brand like MG could find good sales in Canada's underserved EV pickup segment.
4. **Nissan Leaf vs. Fiat 500e pricing in Canada:** A new Nissan Leaf trim is highlighted as Canada's most affordable EV, priced under CA$38,000; a viewer flagged the Fiat 500e as a few hundred dollars cheaper, though the hosts note its smaller battery makes the Leaf the better choice for range/longevity (a Canadian Fiat listing at roughly CA$45,000 was also referenced, alongside separate apparent US pricing).
5. **Why Canada hasn't launched a domestic-brand EV:** Responding to a viewer question about using idled auto-worker capacity to build a "made in Canada" EV, a co-host says Canada's ~2 million annual vehicle sales are too small to sustain a dedicated factory without export markets, and a 25% tariff currently blocks profitable export to the US; efforts to find alternative export markets (Europe, South America, Mexico) are described as ongoing but unresolved.
6. **EV charging rebate program can turn a profit for owners:** A new Canadian program lets third parties aggregate Wi-Fi-enabled home charger data and submit it to the government for a rebate, currently paying about 10–13 cents per kilowatt-hour back to consumers; since off-peak home charging can cost around 10–11 cents/kWh, some owners could effectively be paid more than their charging cost.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Canadian inventory reported sold out:** A single Model Y appearing on Tesla's Canadian inventory page was cited as evidence that vehicles shipped in from China and Germany are selling immediately, which the hosts frame as a sign of strong market growth opportunity for Tesla in Canada, driven partly by gasoline prices reported to be higher in Canada than in the US.
