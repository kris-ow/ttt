This video (a discussion between the host and Brian, of the FutureAzA channel) covers several developments the host frames as supporting evidence for Tesla's vision-only autonomy bet: Volvo dropping lidar, new EU safety data for FSD supervised, positive mainstream media coverage of FSD among seniors, a Grok software integration update, and small robotaxi UX changes possibly hinting at a new FSD version.

## Market & Competition
1. **Volvo discontinues lidar:** Volvo has discontinued the lidar sensor on its EX90 and ES90 models; Norwegian customers with lidar-equipped units will receive €1,800 in compensation for features that will no longer be delivered, with no retrofit option to remove the hardware. Elon Musk responded to the news saying "I did try to warn them," reiterating that vision/neural-net based systems are the right approach. The host speculates other automakers may follow Volvo's move away from lidar, and Brian predicts (his own view, unconfirmed) that Waymo will eventually drop lidar too, citing Waymo's research division reportedly exploring vision-only neural networks.
2. **Media sentiment shift on FSD:** Both speakers describe a recent uptick in positive mainstream coverage of FSD, citing a Detroit News piece on seniors using FSD (including a 92-year-old in Maryland/Florida and a retired GM engineer using it roughly 98% of the time, per the article) and comments from Autoline's John McElroy describing FSD as safer than human driving and predicting autonomy will save many lives once widespread. Brian attributes the shift to media "following the clicks" as viral positive-experience videos circulate, rather than a coordinated narrative change.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **EU FSD supervised safety data:** Tesla released data showing FSD supervised has been 5.2 times safer than manual driving across 65 million kilometers driven in five EU countries over the trailing four months, with fewer collisions and reductions in automatic emergency braking, harsh acceleration, harsh braking, and hard swerves. Elon Musk stated that supervised FSD "unequivocally saves lives," implying regulatory approval delays cost lives. The host notes this is FSD supervised only (not driverless), and that France has indicated it will not vote yes on approval pending further study; EU-wide approval requires enough countries to represent 65% of the population, and if enough countries (including France) vote no, the five countries that already approved it could be forced to backtrack.
2. **Host/Brian view on country-level approval patterns:** The host observes what they characterize as a correlation between a country's domestic auto manufacturing base and reluctance to approve FSD, suggesting political/economic motives rather than safety assessment; Brian agrees, arguing regulators voting against approval likely lack firsthand experience with the system.
3. **Whistleblower lawsuit at Houston FSD training operation:** Brian reports (covered on his own channel) a whistleblower lawsuit against Tesla from a supervisor at a Houston operations center for FSD training, claiming he alone oversaw 38 vehicles; Brian disputes this characterization, noting each car had a safety driver, making the supervisor the third line of defense, not the first. Brian states the suit is likely to fail because the plaintiff missed the 90-day filing window.
4. **Reduced FSD nag frequency:** The host reports FSD's "nag" (attention reminders) has been significantly reduced in recent use, with long stretches of driving without any nag. Brian corroborates this, noting only two nags across a roughly 2,000-mile round trip, both occurring before reaching the highway; he also relays that nag frequency reportedly varies by selected driving mode (e.g., more relaxed "sloth" mode nags less).

## Robotaxi
1. **Grok deeper vehicle integration:** A software update (referenced as version 2026.26) integrates Grok further into the car, enabling voice control of features like Spotify via the glove box interface in addition to existing navigation and conversational functions. Brian sees this as an early step toward broader voice-based vehicle control, potentially becoming a key differentiator versus Waymo, which lacks a conversational AI controlling the vehicle.
2. **Upcoming memory and destination-parking features:** New software includes a "memory" capability where the car learns preferred routes home rather than defaulting to routed suggestions; an upcoming feature will allow the car to learn and replicate preferred parking positions (e.g., in a driveway). Both speakers speculate this memory capability could extend to robotaxi rides, allowing the fleet to learn location-specific pickup/drop-off preferences shared across vehicles.
3. **Silent turn signals/hazards in robotaxi:** A recent robotaxi update made turn signal and hazard light sounds silent inside the cabin; some observers online speculated this could hint at a new FSD version (referred to as "version 15"), though the host stresses this is unconfirmed. Brian is ambivalent, noting passengers might still want tactile/audible cues to anticipate vehicle movement.
4. **Lars Moravy comments on ride experience design:** Brian relays that in an interview roughly three weeks prior, Tesla's Lars Moravy said Tesla does not yet know what the ideal experience should be for fully driverless robotaxi rides (e.g., whether focused on entertainment or quiet/work), and that this is still being studied as ride volume grows.
