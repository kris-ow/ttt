## Brief

- Samsung engineer confirms Tesla AI5 chip tape-out at 2nm
- Optimus vacuum-robot feature shows early signs of working
- FSD V14 Lite spotted on HW3 hardware in South Korea
- Cybercab gets 21-inch screen, app-controlled cabin airflow
- Bhakdi: Tesla range-bound $370–420s until Cybercab scales
- Merger chatter: could Tesla-SpaceX combine into one company?

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14 "Lite" was spotted running on HW3 vehicles in South Korea, per Sawyer Merritt's digest, signaling continued international rollout of the newer FSD version on older hardware. A separate single-user anecdote claimed 1,000 miles since last disengagement on FSD (Supervised) — not a company-verified statistic.
- Samsung Foundry's Principal Engineer stated the Tesla-designed AI5 chip has reached tape-out and will be built at the Taylor, Texas fab on Samsung's latest 2nm process for future Tesla products; no mass-production timeline was given.

## Robotaxi
- New Cybercab interior/UX details emerged: a roughly 21-inch display (versus the Model 3's 15.4-inch screen) and app-controlled cabin airflow direction and temperature, per Sawyer Merritt's digest.
- Jo Bhakdi expects no major share-price move until there's tangible Cybercab deployment news, which he says could come "any day" or take another two to three months; he argues scaling from 1,000 to 10,000 Cybercabs would happen quickly once the first threshold is hit.
- Bhakdi separately suggested Tesla stock could reach $1,000/share, even without Optimus, if the company suddenly scales to 10,000 Cybercabs.

## Humanoid Bots
- A Tesla Optimus-related "vacuum robot" feature appears to be operational based on shared media, per Sawyer Merritt's digest — an early qualitative signal, with no technical or performance detail disclosed.
- On the futureaza podcast, Cern Basher speculated SpaceX could become one of Optimus's largest long-term customers for off-planet use, though the host countered that Tesla's addressable Earth market for humanoid robots (potentially hundreds of millions of units) dwarfs any off-planet opportunity (thousands of units).

## Market & Competition
- A futureaza episode explored a hypothetical Tesla-SpaceX merger without reaching a conclusion. Cern Basher noted SpaceX purchased roughly $500 million in Megapacks from Tesla last year plus about $147 million in other goods/services, and floated that a combined entity's market cap could land in the $3–4 trillion range — a level he worries could remove investor appetite for further multiple expansion.
- Basher noted Musk holds roughly 85% voting control at SpaceX but lacks equivalent control at Tesla, framing this as a possible motivation for a merger; he also warned that if shareholders reject a merger, Musk could steer future projects (Terafab, Digital Optimus, Macrohard-related work) disproportionately toward SpaceX.
- Bhakdi flagged a broader "sentiment shift" across AI/mega-cap names (Micron, Nvidia, Meta, and to a lesser extent Tesla) this week, citing Grok 4.5's strong "cursor bench" performance, Meta's new "Muse" model, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 release as a notably strong week for frontier AI.

## Financials
- Bhakdi recapped a volatile month for TSLA: a drop to $375 on June 22 "for no reason," a rally on delivery numbers (480,000 vehicles delivered), another unexplained drop, and a snap-back to $419; he expects range-bound trading between roughly $370–390 and the $420s absent Cybercab news.
- Basher noted the existing Tesla-SpaceX commercial relationship (Megapack and other sales) is modest relative to Tesla's roughly $100 billion in annual revenue, but could gain tax efficiencies under a merger scenario.
