This Monday news roundup from futureaza covers a wide sweep of EV and energy stories with Tesla at the center — European and global EV sales data favoring Model Y/3, a Cybertruck warranty extension and supplier dispute, a Rivian-Uber robotaxi deal the host is skeptical of, a Chinese battery fast-charging "arms race," and a Hughes Network Systems bankruptcy that the host frames as a competitive win for Starlink. The host is notably skeptical of near-term robotaxi/Cybercab scaling claims and downplays several "gotcha" news items (Lucid salvage title cost, a driver suing Tesla after a high-performance test drive crash).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla leads Europe's D-segment EV sales (June data):** Per Clean Technica/Jose, June European D-segment EV sales show Model Y at 34,480 units and Model 3 at roughly 17,000–18,000, far ahead of rivals like the Mercedes E-Class (~4,600). Overall European EV share hit 30% in June, which the host attributes partly to high oil prices.
2. **Model Y tops global EV sales; BYD Song/Seal combined narrows gap:** Model Y led global EV sales with 153,000 units, followed by BYD's Song and Seal combined (~26,000–27,000 in pure electric); the host notes this comparison is misleading since Song and Seal are two distinct models being combined to approach Model 3's tally.
3. **Model Y surges in Colombia:** In a market Tesla entered relatively recently, Model Y sales rose 61.6% year over year and now represent about 7.9% of new car sales in Colombia, per the host.
4. **BYD overseas deliveries hit record:** BYD's July sales rose 21.8% year over year, with overseas deliveries at a fresh record, continuing strong momentum seen in Europe and Colombia.
5. **Free Supercharging for European wildfire evacuees:** Tesla is offering free Supercharging to all EVs (not just Teslas) in European wildfire-affected areas to support evacuation.
6. **Cybertruck gets carbon-fiber skid plate:** Tesla has quietly upgraded the Cybertruck's skid plate to carbon fiber, which the host describes as stronger and lighter, with cost impact uncertain.
7. **Tesla extends Cybertruck PCS electronics warranty:** Tesla acknowledged that PCS electronics in pre-2026 Cybertrucks did not meet reliability standards and has extended the warranty on the affected part (2024–2025 model years, 8-year/150,000-mile), with refunds for owners who already paid for repairs.
8. **Cybertruck supplier disputes "ransom" characterization:** A Cybertruck parts supplier pushed back on Tesla's claim it was holding equipment hostage, stating it spent $11.9 million tooling up its own equipment and that Tesla's projected future orders (which only become binding roughly 90 days out) failed to materialize as Cybertruck volumes stayed low. The host says the truth is likely "somewhere in between" the two sides' claims.
9. **Tesla warehouse leases in Texas (purpose unknown):** Tesla has reportedly leased two large (multi-million-square-foot) buildings in Texas, possibly for warehousing/logistics/parts, though the purpose is unconfirmed.
10. **Lucid salvage-title dispute:** A Lucid owner (via Rich Rebuilds) found that repairing cosmetic damage on a salvage-titled Lucid would require a full body swap costing around $50,000 due to the car's high value, despite no structural damage — the host calls this "not great" but not necessarily wrongdoing.
11. **Lawsuit over Model Y Performance test-drive crash:** Tesla is being sued for $10 million by someone who crashed a Model Y Performance during a test drive after allegedly not being warned about its acceleration; the host is dismissive of the claim's merit.

## Robotaxi
1. **Uber invests in Rivian for robotaxi ambitions:** Uber agreed to invest up to $1.2 billion in Rivian and purchase up to 50,000 R2s (contingent on milestones, through 2031) if Rivian delivers a working robotaxi. The host is skeptical Rivian can achieve full robotaxi capability on its stated near-term timeline, calling the challenge's scale underappreciated.
2. **Host skeptical of near-term robotaxi/Cybercab scaling claims:** The host says he doubts robotaxi and Cybercab will meaningfully scale this year, noting he's heard "unsupervised by end of next year" promises for roughly a decade and wants to see it actually happen before believing it.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chinese battery fast-charging race accelerates:** CATL reported a 42% net profit jump and announced an ultra-fast-charging battery for light commercial vehicles reaching 80% charge in under 7 minutes with a stated 600,000-mile warranty. FAW's Hongqi brand (with Leysan battery) claims 10–70% charge in 3 minutes 41 seconds; BYD's Blade Battery 2.0 claims 10–70% in five minutes and 10–98% in nine minutes; a Lincoln-brand (Volvo sister company) vehicle with 900V architecture claims 10–70% in four minutes. Dongfeng also rolled out a 1,500kW charger to rival BYD's flash charger.
2. **China imposes new battery accuracy/safety rules:** China has set a new battery display accuracy rule capping error at 5%, alongside existing strict rules on battery fire risk and long warranties.
3. **Volvo drops lidar plans, refunds Norwegian customers:** Volvo is abandoning its lidar-based approach and refunding Norwegian customers 18,000 kroner for promised features that will not be delivered.
4. **EV used-car pricing defies typical depreciation:** Analysts note used EV prices are rising rather than falling as federal buyer incentives disappeared, pushing new EV prices up and used prices along with them; EVs are also said to hold value well due to fewer moving parts.
5. **MAN begins production of megawatt-charging-capable electric trucks:** MAN has started series production of MCS (Megawatt Charging System)-ready electric trucks at its Munich plant.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Hughes Network Systems reportedly heading toward Chapter 11 bankruptcy:** EchoStar's Hughes satellite internet unit faces a $1.5 billion note that came due, prompting a bankruptcy filing to postpone payment. Hughes subscribers have fallen from 1.5 million in 2020 to 681,000 currently, which the host attributes to inability to compete with Starlink's superior speed and latency (sub-two-second ping vs. geostationary satellite lag). Hughes has reportedly sold its bandwidth to Starlink and T-Mobile rather than being acquired outright.

## Financials
1. **TSLA up on the day, still well off recent highs:** TSLA rose intraday to $323.69 (a roughly 4% gain), but remains well below its highs from the prior six months; the host says he doesn't know the specific catalyst and expects speculative media coverage to follow.
2. **SpaceX (SPCX) rebuts "too soon to buy" call:** The host cites a CNBC commentator's caution against buying SpaceX shares, then notes SpaceX's share price is up about 1% since that call, framing it as the pundit being wrong.
