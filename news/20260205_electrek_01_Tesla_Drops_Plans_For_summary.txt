Tesla abandoned plans to build an autonomous charging garage for future driverless robotaxis in downtown San Francisco, dropping the project on the day it was due for a public planning commission hearing, following a Teamsters union appeal of its earlier conditional approval.

## Robotaxi

1. **Dropped SF autonomous charging garage:** Tesla had planned to convert a garage at 825 Sansome Street (capacity 150+ vehicles, 600 amps existing power) into a part-public, part-private site, with one section used for Supercharging and another for autonomous charging of driverless taxis. Tesla received conditional approval in November, but the Teamsters union filed and won an appeal; a union spokesman said he believed there was "a path forward" on an agreement that any site workers be unionized, until Tesla abruptly dropped the plan. According to Mission Local, Tesla told the union via email that it canceled due to "significant building constraints unrelated to the appeal" — a claim the article notes it cannot verify and speculates (without evidence) may relate to the site's power capacity.

2. **No current means of autonomous charging:** The article notes Tesla vehicles lack inductive charging (though the Cybercab is said to support it) and that Tesla's previously discussed robotic "snake charger" was never developed, meaning there is currently no way for a Tesla to charge itself without a human plugging it in — raising the question of what charging design the canceled garage would have actually used.

3. **SF robotaxi claims contrasted with reality:** The article asserts Tesla has repeatedly called its Bay Area service "Robotaxis" even though it is a human-driven, invite-only taxi service in which drivers sometimes engage FSD, and states that Tesla cannot legally run a driverless taxi service in California because it has not applied for the necessary permits — a point the article says was confirmed to Electrek by the CPUC the prior week.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the garage cancellation as suspicious in its timing, occurring shortly after Tesla's claims about having "Robotaxis" operating in the Bay Area, and implies scrutiny toward Tesla's public statements versus its actual regulatory and operational status. A top-voted reader comment (not the author's view) argues Tesla's robotaxi rollout is more about appearing active across "several" cities for investor calls than genuine deployment.

Notable quotes and data:
- Garage at 825 Sansome Street: 150+ vehicle capacity, 600 amps existing power (roughly enough for two Superchargers at full power).
