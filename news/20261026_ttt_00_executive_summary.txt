## Brief

- Houston Robotaxi geofence expands ~10x, now ~300 sq mi

- Barclays upgrades Tesla to overweight, PT $450, cites merger
- SpaceX closes $40B debt raise for Nvidia GPU buildout

## Robotaxi
- Houston's service area grew roughly tenfold, from ~25 to ~254-300 square miles (tracker vs. host estimates vary), stretching to downtown and Bellaire in a shape social media compared to a rocket — and, per Electrek, also to a phallic image, echoing a July 2025 Austin geofence stunt. Operating hours extended to 1 a.m.; Tesla's unsupervised Houston fleet stands at 34 Model Ys with no Cybercabs yet, per RobotaxiTracker.com, while Waymo's Houston zone remains ~50 sq mi and has run driverless since February.
- Tesla's Texas Robotaxi fleet reached 970 as of October 26, up from ~803 a week earlier, with 463-465 Cybercabs per Texas DMV registrations and RobotaxiTracker.com; Austin's driverless count is now 397, over half Cybercabs.
- Over Austin's F1 weekend (~120,000 attendance), Tesla logged over 50-1,250 Cybercab sightings depending on source, with Tesla AI lead Ashok Elluswamy saying Robotaxis were visible "as far as the eye can see." Anecdotal fares cited a $77 Cybercab ride from COTA versus $112+ for an equivalent Uber.
- Unsupervised miles per vehicle per day are estimated at ~100 currently, up from the low 40s in July, per analyst Genma JP — still below Waymo's typical ~200/day.
- Munro Live's Cybercab teardown reports Tesla internally graded the Model Y structural battery pack a "D" (hard/costly to build) versus Cybertruck's improved "B-minus," and says the Cybercab line is currently outpacing deployment needs with a target of 500,000+ miles useful life.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk confirmed on SpaceX's earnings call that Tesla and SpaceX have each formed special committees of independent directors to explore a merger; any deal needs 50% approval of all outstanding Tesla shares, not just those voted, which he called "a fairly high barrier." Tesla's 10-Q confirmed the committee formation.
- Analyst premium estimates diverge: Gerstner sees $420-$550/share; Goldberg favors a lower ~30% premium (~same range as Gerstner's calc); another host's source cites $500-$550 floated by a committee member. One options trader calculates that applying comp-package vote patterns to the stricter merger threshold yields only ~56.9% approval — a narrow pass.
- Capital-raise rationale: multiple sources argue a merged entity could borrow more cheaply at scale than either company alone, given capex needs potentially approaching a trillion dollars for data-center buildouts.
- Reported merger timeline: announcement targeted for H1 2027, with completion sought before January 2029.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX announced a binding $40 billion master finance lease for next-generation Nvidia Vera Rubin chips, called the largest GPU financing ever arranged; lease schedules begin on GPU delivery in H1 2027. Musk said debt markets have shut off financing for non-AI-datacenter-revenue companies, framing SpaceX's investment-grade status as a differentiator.
- SpaceX's Anthropic compute deal has escalated repeatedly this year: from ~$45B over 3 years, to $85B in September, to a potential $121B through 2029 per a recent Anthropic filing; current run-rate is ~$3.1B/month (~$37B+/year annualized). Agreements remain cancellable with 90 days' notice.
- SpaceX purchased 444 acres near Brownsville in August, speculated (unconfirmed) to become a new data-center site; a separate gas pipeline is being laid there for power. Existing Colossus 2 "Mini Hard" buildings in Memphis/Mississippi are opening on a rolling basis, each taking ~5 months to build.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA proposed rules easing autonomous-vehicle requirements (removing mandatory manual turn-signal controls, dashboard indicator rules, easing headlight restrictions) seen as clearing regulatory barriers for Cybercab; Tesla's response to NHTSA's outstanding Cybercab questions was due by month-end.
- Latvia became the 10th EU country to approve Tesla's assisted driving system, covering only ~14.3% of EU population versus the ~65% threshold needed for broader rollout; a bloc-wide vote is expected around Dec 3-6, with Musk expecting a decision by Q1.
- Turo will let US renters filter for FSD-enabled Teslas starting October 28 via a direct Tesla API integration, with eligible hosts getting a free one-month FSD trial. Tesla disclosed 1.74 million active FSD subscriptions in Q3; Electrek calculates Tesla would need ~8 more years at that pace to hit Musk's 10-million-subscription compensation milestone, and flagged driver-complacency risk given FSD remains Level 2.

## Financials
- Barclays upgraded Tesla to overweight with a $450 price target (from $370), citing accelerating robotaxi miles, FSD attach rates above 75%, and potential SpaceX merger as a bullish catalyst. Canaccord Genuity raised its target to $442, calling the Q3 call "one of the most unapologetically bullish Tesla calls in many a moon."
- Tesla's Q3 call featured bold claims — taking over "transportation of any kind on Earth," Optimus as "the biggest product on Earth," Megapacks sold out through 2027 — that Musk called "absurdly grandiose"; critics noted Tesla gave no forward revenue guidance, unlike SpaceX's call.
- Tesla described itself as supply-constrained for multiple quarters with low inventory; Jeff Watts expects resolution within roughly six months.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla cancelled Cybertruck reservations for Australia and New Zealand, citing inability to fulfill them, offering refunds plus a free one-year FSD subscription to customers who switch to Model 3 or Model Y.
- Tesla Canada's Model 3 Premium RWD listing now shows specs matching the discontinued Fremont-built Long Range RWD (584 km range, 5.2s 0-100) rather than the Shanghai-built version, which has been unorderable since early October due to import quota limits; Electrek estimates a Fremont-built version could cost $65,000-$67,000 CAD under Canada's 25% counter-tariffs, above the EV Affordability Program's $50,000 cap.
