This episode of brighterwithherbert, joined by guest Brian (host of the YouTube channel FutureAzA), covers four Tesla storylines: a new FSD safety feature called Automatic Collision Evasion, Model Y's continued global sales dominance, a production crunch at Giga Berlin driven by European demand, and mixed China retail/export data alongside a new cash incentive push. The overall framing is bullish — software getting safer, demand outstripping supply in Europe, and China leaning on incentives amid a hyper-competitive local EV market.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Automatic Collision Evasion (ACE) rolling out in FSD 14.3.9:** A new feature, Automatic Collision Evasion, is rolling out with FSD version 14.3.9. Unlike standard automatic emergency braking (which only brakes in a straight line), ACE can steer around an obstacle — including changing lanes or moving onto the shoulder — when steering is more likely to avoid a collision than braking alone. It requires both automatic emergency braking and FSD Supervised to be enabled, engages only on controlled-access highways with no pedestrians, cyclists, or other vulnerable road users detected, at speeds under 85 mph, and not on wet/slippery roads. It can also engage if the system detects an inattentive driver or that FSD Supervised may have been unintentionally disengaged. The host expects Tesla will eventually extend a version of this to non-FSD vehicles as a free safety feature, following past precedent, though the guest could not confirm whether that expansion is planned. Currently the feature does not engage in scenarios involving pedestrians or cyclists — a limitation the host called "a real copout to the trolley problem."
2. **China FSD Supervised approval remains partial and delayed:** Per the guest (based on conversations with engineers, described as unconfirmed/secondhand), a version of FSD Supervised is running in China but only in limited form, comparable to an older US software version, because local training data centers only recently came online after the system was initially trained on generic/US-based data (including YouTube videos). Engineers reportedly told him an apples-to-apples comparison with the US version isn't currently possible. The guest said full approval has been "months away" for roughly a year and a half with no resolution yet, and separately noted Chinese regulators are tightening oversight after autonomous test vehicles across the industry experienced a cellular-outage-related mass stoppage in a city. He explicitly dismissed the idea that the delay relates to a potential SpaceX/Tesla-related corporate matter, calling that theory "absolutely not" correct and noting Giga Shanghai/China operations are effectively run as a self-contained, largely China-sourced business.
3. **European FSD Supervised vote set for October 6:** Six countries have already approved FSD Supervised in Europe; a EU-wide vote is scheduled for October 6, requiring approval representing 65% of the population. The host noted Elon Musk has expressed confidence about the outcome and said he was in direct contact with the French transportation ministry, with Germany's approval seen as less certain.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y extends bestselling streak:** Model Y has been the world's bestselling passenger vehicle for three straight years (2023, 2024, 2025) with over 4 million units sold globally, and is number one again through July of this year with sales up nearly 20% year-over-year, putting a fourth straight year at the top in play. The guest noted that in prior years Model Y was in a near-statistical tie with Toyota's RAV4 (within roughly 5,000–10,000 units, and using non-comparable delivery metrics), but expects clearer separation this year as retooling from last year's refresh is complete and the Model Y L adds incremental volume next year.
2. **Giga Berlin running mandatory extra shifts to meet demand:** Giga Berlin is reportedly unable to keep up with European demand; per Handelsblatt, current production is about 6,500–7,000 vehicles per week (sources cited slightly different current figures), with a target of 7,500 vehicles per week by mid-October, take time reduced to about 45 seconds, and mandatory extra shifts across the full production operation in September per an internal note from plant leadership. The guest linked strong European demand partly to elevated petrol prices and said further upside is expected if EU FSD Supervised approval comes through on October 6.
3. **Tesla China August retail down, exports up sharply:** Tesla China sold 50,000 vehicles at retail in August, down 12.4% year-over-year, while exports rose about 38–39% year-over-year to roughly 36,000 units. The guest attributed the pattern to a broader China-wide trend of softer domestic demand alongside rising exports (citing BYD's domestic-vs-export mix as a parallel example), intensifying domestic competition, and Tesla's expansion into smaller overseas markets (e.g., Korea, Japan, Colombia) where it is achieving strong or leading sales.
4. **China cash incentive through September 30:** Tesla China announced a limited-time cash incentive for in-stock vehicle orders/deliveries through September 30: 5,000 yuan for any Model 3 variant (including Performance) and 10,000 yuan for any Model Y variant (including Model Y L). Both the host and guest framed this as a standard quarter-end push tied to a temporary discount structure rather than a permanent price cut, intended to preserve brand/resale perception.

## Market & Competition
1. **Volkswagen conducting largest layoffs in company history:** The host noted Volkswagen is undergoing the largest layoffs in its history, with the guest attributing this to VW's most competitively sold vehicles carrying low margins that are hurting the company financially.
2. **Chinese EV market described as extremely competitive:** The guest characterized China's EV industry as releasing roughly three new car models per day industry-wide, and cited BYD's best-ever month with around 250,000 battery-electric vehicles sold in August (the vast majority exported), as evidence of intensifying domestic competition alongside a global export surge driven by high petrol prices in markets like Australia and the UK.
