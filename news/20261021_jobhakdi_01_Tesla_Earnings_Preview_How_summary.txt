The host previews Tesla's upcoming earnings, arguing the stock has little near-term catalyst beyond Cybercab progress, while framing SpaceX's compute/AI buildout — revealed on its own earnings call the day before — as now dramatically more attractive than Tesla, shifting his personal portfolio weighting sharply toward SpaceX.

## Financials
1. **Consensus estimates for Tesla's quarter:** Analyst consensus going into earnings: revenue ~$28 billion, adjusted EPS $0.45, GAAP EPS $0.23, gross margin 17.9%, operating income $721 million, capex $7.77 billion (implying a run-rate near $30 billion annualized), and free cash flow of roughly -$4.5 billion driven by that capex.
2. **Host's view on margin quality:** The host expects a "very good quarter," citing high FSD attach rates as a margin driver, solid Model Y/Model 3 sales, elevated oil prices supporting EV demand, reduced political headwinds, and a halo effect where FSD adoption also pulls in more car buyers.
3. **Valuation disconnect:** The host notes Tesla trades around a $1.5 trillion market cap at a price-earnings ratio he describes as "crazy" (cited as 358), and argues that modest beats or misses on gross margin or EPS are immaterial next to the capex scale — making it hard to see a strong earnings catalyst either way.
4. **Trade positioning into earnings:** The host disclosed holding out-of-the-money short puts on Tesla (strikes around 350/355/360) placed earlier in the week, and said he expects the stock to hold above 360-370 post-earnings but sees a modest pullback (he suggested roughly a 2% drop) as more likely than a rally, given the lack of a clear catalyst.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab tracker update:** Per Pioneer Lands' tracker, the Cybercab fleet reached roughly 191, including 16 added in the prior week after what the host called a "surge," with another surge expected; he reiterated a view that reaching 2,000 Cybercabs by year-end is possible and would be a major bullish catalyst, potentially pushing Tesla to new all-time highs (he cited a $500 target) by year-end.
2. **No major Cybercab news expected on the call:** The host does not expect Tesla to give forward guidance on Cybercab scaling during the earnings call, noting the company historically avoids predictions here; he expects at most modest commentary (e.g., on a newer FSD version) rather than concrete deployment numbers.
3. **Optimus as a wildcard:** A commenter raised the possibility of a Tesla robot reveal; the host said he doesn't expect anything like a full Optimus reveal, though a bullish comment on robot production (e.g., a sales target for next year) is possible but unlikely to move the stock much on its own.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX's compute scale framed as outsized vs. robotaxi economics:** The host estimates 1 gigawatt of compute capacity is roughly equivalent in economic value to 1–1.5 million Cybercabs, and says SpaceX currently has about 1.7 gigawatts deployed, is on track for 2 gigawatts by the end of this month, and 2.5–3 gigawatts by year-end — figures he says were stated clearly on SpaceX's earnings call.
2. **Accelerated compute buildout target:** The host says Elon Musk indicated SpaceX is aiming closer to 10 gigawatts than 5 gigawatts of compute by next year, a target the host previously doubted but now believes after the earnings call; Musk reportedly hinted at an undisclosed method for securing the energy needed, without revealing details for competitive reasons.
3. **Implied scale comparison to Tesla's robotaxi business:** Using his own rough gigawatt-to-Cybercab equivalence, the host calculates that roughly 15 million fully deployed Cybercabs would be needed for Tesla's robotaxi business to match the economic value of SpaceX's targeted ~10 gigawatts of compute next year — a comparison he presents as illustrating SpaceX's current scale advantage over Tesla's robotaxi ramp.
4. **Read-through to AI/chip stocks:** The host says SpaceX's earnings call confirmed strong demand for AI infrastructure, which he says lifted other AI-adjacent stocks (he names Micron and Nvidia) on the day, but argues Tesla did not get a comparable boost from the news.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
