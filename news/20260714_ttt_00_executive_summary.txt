## Brief
- Cybercab spotted testing in New Hampshire and Houston
- FSD V14.3.5 rolling out with live Camera Preview feature
- Jefferies hikes TSLA price target to $400 on Q2 delivery beat
- Cybercabs staging heavily in Giga Texas outbound lot
- Optimus "Cortex 2" training data center ~50% operational
- New Optimus factory targets ~10M units/year, breaks ground

## Robotaxi
- Merritt reports what "looks like" 100 Cybercabs testing in Houston — well above RobotaxiTracker.com's 13, so treated as his own independent field observation, not a tracker update.
- Cybercab also spotted testing in New Hampshire per Merritt, suggesting geographic footprint is expanding beyond core markets.
- Drone footage (Tegtmeyer, July 8) shows Cybercabs mixed with Model Ys and Cybertrucks in the Giga Texas outbound lot, loaded onto trucks nearly as fast as they accumulate; sightings reported in nearly every major U.S. market, though no fleet-entry timeline is known.
- Giga Texas's test track is running continuous NVH testing over rumble strips and a new "cobblestone" rough-road section, aimed at Cybercab noise/vibration tuning; Lars Moravy reportedly described using acoustic sensors during autonomous drives to flag rattle issues.
- A guest on brighterwithherbert estimates roughly 1,000 Cybercabs produced to date (unconfirmed, inferred from lot sizes), theorizing the stockpile creates a "forcing function" toward broader deployment within months — his own timing calls for April/May/June already missed.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus "Cortex 2" training data-center at Giga Texas is roughly 50% operational, on schedule since an April start, targeting full operation around fall 2026 with roughly double Cortex 1's cooling capacity and an eventual ~260 Megapacks (Tegtmeyer).
- A next-generation Optimus factory is under construction at Giga Texas, targeted at ~10 million robots/year — roughly 10x Fremont's pilot line — potentially stretching ~4,000 feet; equipment installation could start as early as next spring, with meaningful production not expected until 2028 (Tegtmeyer's estimate).

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14.3.5 began rolling out July 13 with a new Camera Preview feature usable while driving, including the interior cabin camera, per Sawyer Merritt.
- A brighterwithherbert guest argues Tesla's next-gen AI5 chip is not a deployment blocker, citing Musk's statement that AI5 isn't necessary — framing it as a performance upgrade rather than the more fundamental HW3-to-HW4 leap; AI5 expected around November 2026 per the guest.

## Electric Vehicles
- 4680 battery production is scaling up at Giga Texas — new cabinetry, reconfigured concrete slabs, and rising recycling volume suggest new trial lines are being added to the original eight-line-capacity space (versus ~1.4 lines previously in use); Giga Berlin is now adding 4680 lines using lessons from Texas (Tegtmeyer).
- Model Y variants (RWD, Performance, Model Y L) are accumulating in large volumes in the Giga Texas outbound lot alongside Cybertrucks and Cybercabs.
- Debate continues (futureaza) over whether Tesla should build a smaller, cheaper pickup as Cybertruck sales lag; Lars Moravy has previously said Tesla has discussed a smaller pickup, but guest Randy Kirk sees a "cyber SUV" as more likely than a compact truck, citing a discontinued small Tesla-adjacent pickup that sold just over 10,000 units.

## Financials
- Jefferies raised its Tesla price target to $400 (from $375) on Tesla's Q2 delivery beat, and lifted its FY2026 EBIT estimate 4% to $6.2 billion, partly citing higher-value vehicle mix.
- A brighterwithherbert guest expects no major surprises from the July 22 Q2 earnings call — no concrete robotaxi timeline disclosed — and predicts a possible modest pre-earnings run-up followed by a pullback absent Cybercab-specific news; TSLA was trading around $400, roughly 10% down year-to-date.

## Energy
- Tesla has been stockpiling transformers at Giga Texas for grid/data-center buildout due to multi-year lead times, per Tegtmeyer.
- A new Megapack factory is expected online later this year in Houston; the host argues Tesla's roughly 30% Megapack margin gives it a cost advantage building its own data-center power infrastructure.

## Market & Competition
- Volkswagen's CEO told staff the company may cut roughly 50,000 additional jobs (confirming a total of 100,000 planned cuts); VW stock reportedly at a 16-year low.
- California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed a new point-of-sale EV rebate: $3,500 off new EVs up to $50,000 MSRP and $1,750 off used EVs up to $25,000 — a potential demand tailwind for Tesla's lower-priced models.
- Land is being cleared at Giga Texas's south end for a planned Tesla/SpaceX joint-venture chip fab, described by a Tesla executive as smaller than the main factory, with a much larger "Terafab" (~10x the main factory) planned elsewhere (Tegtmeyer).
