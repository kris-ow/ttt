## Brief

- FSD V14.3.5 rolling out with live Camera Preview feature
- Cybercab spotted testing in New Hampshire, per Merritt
- Jefferies hikes TSLA price target to $400 on delivery beat
- Giga Texas Optimus "Cortex 2" data center ~50% built
- Next-gen Optimus factory targeted at 10M units/year
- California signs new $3,500 point-of-sale EV rebate

## Robotaxi
- Cybercabs are visibly staging and loading onto trucks at Giga Texas's outbound lot, intermixed with Model Ys and Cybertrucks, with sightings reported in nearly every major U.S. market; neither Tegtmeyer nor Merritt could confirm when they enter revenue robotaxi service.
- Merritt reported what he called ~100 Cybercabs testing in Houston — well above RobotaxiTracker.com's canonical Houston count (13 as of 7/13) — so this is flagged as Merritt's own independent observation, not a tracker update.
- A guest on brighterwithherbert estimates Tesla has produced roughly 1,000 Cybercabs to date (unconfirmed, inferred from production-lot sightings of 70, 30, 60 units) and argues a growing unused stockpile creates a "forcing function" for deployment scaling, possibly by September, though he concedes his April/May/June deployment predictions already missed.
- Giga Texas's test track is running continuous rough-road/NVH testing (including a new "cobblestone" section) for Cybercab noise cancellation and passenger comfort, with Lars Moravy reportedly using acoustic sensors during autonomous test drives to flag rattle issues.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's "Cortex 2" Optimus training data center at Giga Texas is roughly 50% operational, on schedule since coming online in April, targeting full operation by fall 2026 with about 260 Megapacks and roughly double Cortex 1's cooling capacity.
- A next-generation Optimus factory is under construction at Giga Texas, targeted at ~10 million robots/year (~10x Fremont's pilot line) and potentially ~4,000 feet long; per Tegtmeyer, equipment installation could start as early as next spring, with meaningful production unlikely before 2028.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14.3.5 began rolling out July 13 with a new Camera Preview feature letting drivers view any camera — including the interior cabin camera — while actively driving, not just in Park.
- A brighterwithherbert guest argues Tesla's camera-only approach allows full-system rewrites that keep improving iteration speed, contrasting it with Waymo's LiDAR-based architecture; he also downplays AI5 as a deployment blocker, calling it a performance upgrade rather than a fundamental leap, expected around November 2026.

## Financials
- Jefferies raised its Tesla price target to $400 (from $375) on Tesla's Q2 delivery beat, lifting its FY2026 EBIT estimate 4% to $6.2 billion.
- Ahead of the July 22 Q2 earnings call, a brighterwithherbert guest expects no robotaxi deployment timeline disclosure and only standard commentary on deliveries, margins and capex — with TSLA trading near $400, roughly 10% down year-to-date.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tegtmeyer sees signs of 4680 cell production scale-up at Giga Texas (new cabinetry, reconfigured slabs, more recycling volume) with Tesla reportedly having gotten dry cathode and calendaring processes under control; Giga Berlin is now adding 4680 lines using those lessons.
- Debate continues over whether Tesla should build a smaller/cheaper pickup as Cybertruck sales lag; Lars Moravy has previously said Tesla has discussed a smaller truck, but Randy Kirk (futureaza) sees a "cyber SUV" as more likely than a compact truck, citing a discontinued small Tesla-adjacent pickup that sold just over 10,000 units.
- Ford, Telo and Slate are each advancing low-cost electric trucks ($25K–$41.5K range) as market benchmarks; panelists question whether real U.S. demand exists for cheap compact trucks versus fleet/export use cases.

## Energy
- Tesla has been stockpiling transformers at Giga Texas for grid and data-center buildout given multi-year lead times; a new Megapack factory is expected online later this year in Houston.
- The host argues Tesla's roughly 30% Megapack margin gives it a structural cost advantage in powering its own data centers versus external buyers.

## Market & Competition
- Volkswagen's CEO told staff the company may need to cut roughly 50,000 additional jobs (confirming a total of 100,000 planned cuts) as VW stock sits at a 16-year low.
- California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed a new point-of-sale EV rebate: $3,500 off new EVs up to $50,000 MSRP and $1,750 off used EVs up to $25,000 — a potential demand tailwind for Tesla's lower-priced models.
- A large water retention pond is under construction at Giga Texas ahead of a planned Tesla/SpaceX "advanced technology chip fab," with a separate, much larger "Terafab" (reportedly ~10x the main factory's size) planned elsewhere, per a Tesla executive cited by Tegtmeyer.
