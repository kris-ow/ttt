Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) sent a letter to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy on September 9, giving DOT until September 30 to answer questions about Tesla Autopilot/FSD after an NBC News investigation found 43 videos of drivers appearing asleep at the wheel (17 posted in 2026 alone).

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Congressional letter demands answers on FSD driver monitoring:** Krishnamoorthi's letter cites the NBC News investigation's 43 sleeping-driver videos and ties it to NHTSA's ongoing engineering analysis (EA26002) covering roughly 3.2 million Tesla vehicles with FSD, which has already identified nine incidents in reduced-visibility conditions including one fatality and one injury. He asks NHTSA whether it is aware of the videos and whether they signal a systemic problem, whether it has contacted Tesla, what driver-inattention crash data it has collected since January 1, 2024, its own assessment of the cabin camera's effectiveness, and what findings would trigger a safety recall.
2. **Criticism of Tesla's in-cabin drowsiness message:** The letter flags a message Tesla vehicles display — "Drowsiness detected. Stay focused with FSD" — arguing it encourages continued use of a system requiring active supervision at the exact moment a driver may be least capable of providing it; Krishnamoorthi wrote that the message "appears to encourage use of the system precisely when the vehicle has detected that the driver may be less capable." The article separately notes Tesla has posted social media messaging about being more rested when driving with FSD, and that owners have found simple workarounds (covering the cabin camera, wearing sunglasses) to defeat driver-monitoring checks.
3. **NHTSA probe status:** NHTSA upgraded its FSD investigation to an engineering analysis in March 2026, the last step before the agency either closes the case or orders a recall.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the letter asks the right question but is unlikely to move a DOT he says "hasn't shown much appetite for taking on Tesla"; he contends Tesla's cabin-camera monitoring is easily defeated and that its drowsiness-alert design prioritizes keeping FSD active over keeping drivers alert. He also states, without providing a source in this article, that Tesla has conceded its own software fix would have prevented only three of the nine crashes NHTSA is examining.
