## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX swung sharply post-earnings — up ~10%, then down ~10-11% — which one analyst called an irrational move with no real signal, per jobhakdi. A roughly 900-million-share unlock two days later didn't trigger a selloff; the stock rose ~6% that day and rallied further the next session, which the host reads as confirmation the stock had bottomed. He expects resistance as shares approach the ~$135 IPO price, since early holders who stayed through the unlock may sell near their cost basis.
- Peter Diamandis published an essay arguing SpaceX could become the first $10 trillion company, framing it as five businesses in one (Starlink, AI compute, chips, launch, connective layer); Musk reposted it with two rocket emojis, no comment.
- Terafab, the jointly-branded Tesla/SpaceX chip megafactory in Grimes County, TX, is being read by one host as a merger signal — two corporate names on one factory door.

## Financials
- SpaceX's Q2 report (its first as a public company) showed revenue up 92% YoY and AI revenue up 247% YoY, per jobhakdi. New cloud agreements added $1.6B of incremental Q2 infrastructure revenue, plus a further $6.7B cloud contract signed post-quarter, ramping in October.
- Terafab phase one: $16.8B committed jointly by SpaceX and Tesla, with filings allowing up to $119B across all phases, per brighterwithherbert. Texas is providing a $30M grant and a tax deal with roughly 78% abatement (correcting earlier reports of 100%) and fixed payments of $20M/year for 35 years; SpaceX has already wired the first $10M ahead of schedule.
- A separate ~$3B research/pilot fab built around Intel's newest process is already under construction at Giga Texas, ground broken in April, targeted for completion next year.
- A report claiming Tesla's capex ($25B+) now outpaces revenue generation ($103B trailing-12-month) was pushed back on by futureaza hosts, who note it omits Tesla's ~$30B borrowing capacity and ~$40B cash on hand.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's compute readiness capacity hit 1.4GW at end of Q2, ahead of independent analyst models (~1-1.1GW), per jobhakdi. Guidance calls for 2GW by end-2026 and over 5GW by end-2027; Musk reportedly said capacity would be closer to 10GW, a figure the analyst calls likely exaggerated.
- Power/cooling targets of 20GW by end-2027 (SpaceX) or 15GW (Musk, after delays) were also called vastly exaggerated by jobhakdi, who notes these cover infrastructure, not installed GPU compute.
- SpaceX affirmed Nvidia as its exclusive GPU provider, boosting Nvidia's own stock. SpaceX and Nvidia also announced a partnership to build "Star Mind AI 1," a satellite compute payload described as data-center-class space compute, targeting first launches in 2027.
- One analyst argues orbital compute's scaling timeline — 2028 versus 2030-2035 — is a pivotal question for the entire AI trade; a 2028 delivery would let space-based compute take over just as terrestrial buildout hits power constraints, while a slip to 2030+ risks a broader crash across AI-linked stocks (his own framing, not confirmed guidance).

## Humanoid Bots
- AI5, the inference chip built for FSD and Optimus, taped out in spring with silicon samples expected later this year and volume production in 2027; Musk reportedly called getting it right "existential." AI6, aimed at Optimus-at-scale plus AI workloads, targets a December tape-out with mass production in H2 2027; Samsung signed a $16.5B supply deal for it last summer.
- Musk gave "a very approximate guess" that Terafab's compute output would split roughly 25% Tesla/Optimus and 75% AI/spacecraft — softer toward Optimus than an earlier March framing that had space closer to 80%.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink reported 12 million subscribers, 10,200 satellites in orbit, and coverage in 167 countries in Q2 earnings, per jobhakdi — figures the analyst called unsurprising.
- Starlink Mobile has reportedly had $17B already spent on spectrum, with first service targeted for late next year, per brighterwithherbert. T-Mobile's CEO separately downplayed the competitive threat from satellite-to-mobile, calling it "exaggerated" and complementary rather than disruptive.

## SpaceX — Starship
- A brief, unconfirmed post suggests SpaceX may not be able to recover a Starship from the Indian Ocean — no detail on which test flight or vehicle, per Sawyer Merritt's roundup. Treat as an early, unverified signal rather than a confirmed loss.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla filed for a $10.1B vertically integrated solar cell manufacturing plant ("Project Crystal Sun") in Fort Bend County, TX, near Houston, per a public filing cited by Sawyer Merritt. Construction is targeted to start in 2026 and finish in 2028, with 9,712 permanent jobs projected — filing-stage, not yet confirmed.
- Tesla's China wholesale sales grew for a ninth straight month, though one host attributes this mainly to exports rather than domestic demand strength.
- California's MyFirstEV rebate ($3,500 point-of-sale) allocation for Tesla dropped to 30% remaining, from 50% the day before, suggesting exhaustion within days.
- The new long-wheelbase Model Y L is drawing favorable reviews (cited: Edmunds, Motor Trend) for its "rear comfort" ride mode and thermal upgrades; one host calls it the best Model Y variant to date.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla released a new FSD (Supervised) compilation showing the system avoiding accidents and pedestrian collisions across Spain, France, Germany, Finland, UK, Lithuania, Switzerland, Netherlands, and Italy — no incident counts or version numbers given.

## Market & Competition
- A report claims Cybertruck retail demand fell from 39,000 to 3,500 units, with SpaceX/xAI purchases making up nearly 20% of Q4 units; futureaza hosts agree Cybertruck has underperformed original volume targets (50,000–100,000 units), attributing it mainly to pricing near $80,000–$120,000 versus a targeted ~$60,000.
- A battery-degradation study claiming Hyundai, Kia, and Volvo "beat" Tesla was disputed by futureaza hosts as an apples-to-oranges comparison — Tesla's cited figure (~12% range loss at 200,000 miles) was compared against a competitor tested at only 62,000 miles.
