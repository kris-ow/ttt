This week's episode from The Tesla Space covers a range of technology and space news, with the central Tesla-related focus on the rapid expansion of Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi fleet in Texas and the milestone of FSD surpassing 10 billion supervised miles. The video also covers the Tesla Semi's new charging infrastructure program, Neuralink's automated surgical robot, and SpaceX's Mars colony compensation plan for Elon Musk. The core thesis is that Tesla's data-driven FSD strategy is now bearing fruit in real-world autonomous deployments, with exponential fleet growth beginning to materialize.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Hits 10 Billion Supervised Miles:** Tesla's supervised Full Self-Driving has now accumulated over 10 billion real-world miles. The video frames this as a critical threshold — Elon Musk's 2016 Master Plan Part Two originally estimated that approximately 6 billion miles of data would be needed to demonstrate that FSD is safer than the average human driver and unlock broader regulatory approval globally. With 10 billion miles now logged, Tesla has substantially exceeded that original benchmark.

2. **Regulatory Progress in Europe and China:** In the Netherlands, the RDW (the country's official vehicle regulator) has formally approved Full Self-Driving supervised, stating that when used properly with driver monitoring in place, the system is safer than other driver assistance systems and makes a positive contribution to road safety. In China, Tesla has secured partial approval for a supervised FSD release, with early owner feedback described as very positive and broader rollout still in progress.

3. **California Robotaxi Accountability Rules:** California has clarified how autonomous vehicles will be handled when they violate traffic laws. Starting July 1, 2026, robotaxis in California will be ticketed like any other vehicle — if they run a red light or fail to stop for pedestrians, the company managing the vehicle will be liable for the fine. California DMV Director Steve Gordon framed the new rule as enhancing public safety, transparency, and accountability for AV manufacturers.

## Robotaxi

1. **Rapid Unsupervised Fleet Expansion in Texas:** Tesla has expanded its robotaxi operations beyond Austin to include Houston and Dallas, Texas. According to community tracking data from RobotaxiTracker.com, approximately 36 fully unsupervised Model Y vehicles are now operating across these Texas cities. The video highlights a fleet growth chart showing an initial rollout in early 2026, followed by a period of stagnation, then a resumption of growth in early April 2026, culminating in a near-vertical acceleration in fleet size in the weeks prior to the video — described as "what the beginning of exponential growth looks like."

2. **Night Operations Now Active:** The unsupervised Tesla robotaxi fleet in Texas has extended its operating hours into nighttime driving. The video notes that night driving presents distinct challenges for a vision-only system — reduced visibility, headlight glare, and harder-to-detect objects — making the extension of night operations a meaningful signal of Tesla's confidence in FSD's around-the-clock performance.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi Efficiency and Cost Advantage:** The Tesla Semi is rated at under 1.7 kWh per mile. Real-world tests from companies including DHL and PepsiCo indicate it is approximately three times more efficient than a diesel truck. This translates to an operating cost of roughly 23 cents per mile for the Semi, compared to over 80 cents per mile for a traditional diesel rig — a major cost advantage for high-mileage commercial fleets.

2. **New Semi Charging for Business Program:** Tesla is launching a structured charging infrastructure program for Semi fleet operators. The Megacharger delivers 1.2 megawatts of power, enabling the Semi to recover approximately 60% of its range in 30 minutes — aligning with mandatory rest break requirements for truck drivers. A lower-cost base charger is arriving in early 2027, delivering 125 kilowatts of power and priced at around $40,000 for two units. The base charger is designed for overnight depot charging, and Tesla will handle maintenance, network operations, and driver support for both charger types under the program.
