## Brief

- Tesla files for $10.1B Fort Bend solar cell factory, site not final
- Terafab AI chip mega-factory confirmed for Grimes County, Texas
- SPCX stalls repeatedly at $135 IPO-price resistance level
- Musk: SpaceX AI revenue may exceed all other SpaceX revenue by Q4
- Starlink tops 13M subscribers, 22M monthly Starlink Mobile users
- Tesla patent shows plasma treatment fixes 4680 "core collapse" bottleneck

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX shares have repeatedly rejected at $135 (the IPO price) this week, per Jo Bhakdi, who estimates roughly 160–200 million shares sit as sell orders near that level out of ~900 million outstanding, with an overhang he projects could take ~10 trading days to clear. Some unlocked early shareholders reportedly refuse to sell below $135, recalling a prior peak near $225–$250.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas set a $600/share SpaceX bull case built on Cursor alone ($8B revenue by year-end, $33B by 2030), explicitly excluding Grok, X data, and Starlink upside. Deutsche Bank reportedly sees a $100B revenue run-rate achievable this year, driven partly by $45–50B in cloud deals.
- Speculation continues around SpaceX-Tesla merger timing; Bhakdi argues a near-term deal is unlikely given unresolved share price discovery, estimating "earliest next year." Musk's comment "China's awesome, I strongly encourage people to visit" is being read by some commentators as merger-adjacent signaling, though this remains speculative.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk detailed aggressive compute targets: SpaceX currently has 1.4GW of nameplate power, targeting 10GW by end of 2027, which he said could generate $300–500B/year in revenue if achieved.
- Terafab, the jointly announced Tesla/SpaceX chip mega-factory, will span 100M+ sq ft in Grimes County, Texas — roughly ten times Giga Texas — targeting over 1 terawatt of AI compute annually. First phase costs $16.8B, with total phases potentially reaching $119B; Intel has joined as a partner, and the project is expected to create at least 3,000 jobs.
- Jo Bhakdi argues SpaceX's durable moat is compute infrastructure ownership rather than AI models themselves, predicting Musk keeps Grok's most advanced capability in-house for vertical integration across Optimus, robotaxi, and Terafab rather than broad external licensing.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX confirmed over 13 million Starlink consumer subscribers and 22 million monthly Starlink Mobile (direct-to-cell) users.
- Musk said he expects the total Starlink constellation, including direct-to-cell, to exceed 100,000 satellites (versus ~10,000 today), with future satellites carrying up to 50x current V2 throughput — a target the host notes would require licensing approvals well beyond what's currently filed.
- Musk said "all cars will have Starlink" eventually as the only path to high-bandwidth connectivity for billions of vehicles; Lufthansa's first Starlink-equipped A320 Neo enters service August 19.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Ship 13 achieved its softest splashdown yet and was found floating intact, but Musk said "ship recovery is not looking good right now" after close-up heat shield and engine photos were captured; the ship was later lost at sea.
- An FCC filing indicates Flight 14's booster will return to the launch site; Musk said on the earnings call that Flight 14 will also attempt a ship catch, alongside first operational V3 satellites — the most ambitious Starship flight to date, possibly by end of this month per Musk, though the filing itself gives no date.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Robotaxi account unveiled a gold Cybercab with an integrated Starlink dish, with Musk touting in-ride 4K live sports, movies, and gaming — framed by commentators as a potential recurring connectivity revenue stream, though explicitly speculative and unmodeled.
- Florida Governor Ron DeSantis featured the Cybercab (alongside a Waymo) at a press conference, highlighting its steering-wheel-free interior; Tesla styled the car's light bar with an American flag theme for the event.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla is beginning its first FSD (Supervised) ride-alongs in Switzerland this Thursday, letting prospective customers experience FSD from the passenger seat.
- Tesla owner David Moss became the first reported to reach 25,000 miles on the FSD hands-off streak counter, roughly a lap around Earth without intervention.

## Energy
- Tesla filed plans for a $10.1 billion vertically integrated solar cell factory ("Project Crystal Sun") in Fort Bend County, Texas, projected to create nearly 10,000 permanent jobs; construction would run through 2028 with operations starting early 2029. Tesla says the site is not finalized and is weighing an alternative in another state.
- The plan calls for full vertical integration (wafer/ingot manufacturing, coating/metallization), which commentators tie to Musk's earlier WEF comments about Tesla and SpaceX each targeting ~100GW/year of American-made solar within about three years.

## Electric Vehicles
- A Tesla patent published February 12 describes a plasma treatment process for 4680 dry-electrode manufacturing that addresses the long-flagged "core collapse" and slow electrolyte-wetting bottlenecks; per patent data, treated jellyrolls reached 100% wetting in ~8 hours versus ~72 hours untreated, with a ~4% finished-cell energy improvement (88Wh to 92Wh) at low soak times.
- Tesla ranked #1 in a brand loyalty ranking of owners likely to rebuy the same brand, ahead of Ferrari, Toyota, Subaru, and Honda.

## Market & Competition
- GM and SAIC extended their China joint venture 20 years, through 2047, with stated ambitions to explore export markets (Middle East, Africa, South America, Mexico, Asia-Pacific) — ambitions the hosts view skeptically given scale requirements and the multi-year gap before autonomy becomes competitive there.
- Subaru is reportedly spending roughly 3x its gas-car per-vehicle ad budget on EV marketing (industry EV marketing spend said to be up 40% last quarter) while still paying ~$9,000–$10,000 per unit in incentives on the Solterra, Uncharted, and Trailseeker — read by hosts as a product problem, not an advertising problem.
