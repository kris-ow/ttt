This video is a conversational discussion between Brian (Futuraza) and guest Randy Kirk previewing Tesla's upcoming Q1 2026 earnings call (scheduled for Wednesday, April 23, 2026). The hosts assess whether Tesla stock is likely to pop or drop post-earnings, review known headwinds for the quarter, and discuss broader tailwinds like rising gas prices boosting EV demand. The overall tone is cautiously bearish on Q1 results but optimistic about Q2 and beyond.

## Financials
1. **Q1 Earnings Expectations Are Muted:** Randy Kirk states he is not expecting a beat in Q1 2026. The consensus EPS estimate he references is around 23 cents, with some analysts projecting 32–33 cents, though he expresses skepticism even about hitting 23 cents given the known challenges of the quarter.

2. **Cash Flow Likely Negative:** Randy anticipates negative cash flow for Q1, citing approximately 50,000 vehicles that were being shipped out of Giga Shanghai — an unusually high export volume in the third month of the quarter when domestic sales typically dominate. He also notes that Giga Texas underwent a shutdown/retool lasting a couple of weeks, which may have prompted Tesla to build extra inventory ahead of that pause.

3. **Capex Rising, ZEV Credits Contracting:** The hosts flag that capital expenditure is increasing substantially, with significant spending going into R&D. ZEV credits, while still present, are described as contracting rapidly. These factors together weigh on the bottom line for Q1.

4. **Energy Deployments Down Despite Production:** Randy notes that while Tesla produced a significant volume of energy products, actual deployments were down for the quarter, representing another headwind for reported revenue.

5. **Options Market Pricing a 6% Move Either Way:** As of the morning of recording, options pricing implied a roughly 6% move up or down following the earnings report, reflecting significant uncertainty about direction.

6. **Stock Price Context and Near-Term Prediction:** Randy notes he had predicted Tesla would hit $400 by the prior Friday and it did reach $401. As of recording the stock was at $392, which he attributes to macro war-related concerns. He believes once that uncertainty lifts, the broader market and Tesla will climb. He also flags that some investors may trim positions ahead of earnings after the recent run-up.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Q2 Expected to Be a Record Quarter:** Both hosts express optimism about Q2 2026, citing rising gasoline prices as a meaningful demand catalyst. Randy suggests that by the end of Q2, Tesla could be out of cars and may post a record quarter. He also references a general pattern of demand "lumpiness" — after a down quarter, a bounce-back is historically expected.

2. **High Gas Prices Driving EV Interest:** The hosts discuss gas prices ranging from around $4 in parts of the interior U.S. to $5–6 in the Pacific Northwest and $6–7+ in California (with one location cited at $7.50 and another near Universal City at $9/gallon). Randy notes that lower national gas prices reduce urgency in many markets, but the overall trend is favorable for EV conversions at the margin.

3. **SpaceX Purchased Nearly 20% of Cybertruck Sales in Q4:** The hosts reference a notable data point that SpaceX accounted for nearly 20% of Cybertruck sales in Q4 2025, representing roughly one-third of 1% of Tesla's total vehicle sales. Both hosts treat this as a one-time fleet purchase with no meaningful recurring impact, dismissing media narratives that framed it as propping up Cybertruck demand.

4. **Cybertruck Praised as a Practical Fleet Tool:** Randy notes that the Cybertruck's ability to provide remote power (for welders, compressors, etc.) in areas without grid access makes it a genuinely useful fleet vehicle for companies like SpaceX, independent of any related-party optics.

## Market & Competition
1. **Macro War Concerns Weighing on Stock:** Randy attributes the pullback from $401 to $392 to investor concern about geopolitical conflict — specifically whether a war will resume or be permanently ended. He views this as a temporary macro overhang, not a Tesla-specific issue.

2. **Tesla Diner as a Minor Side Business:** The hosts briefly discuss the Tesla Diner in Los Angeles, noting it is operated by a third party (not Tesla directly), making franchise-style expansion theoretically unlimited. However, both agree the financial contribution is negligible — even 100 locations at $5 million in sales each would only produce $500 million, a rounding error relative to Tesla's core businesses. Randy notes that Tesla's insurance business alone would dwarf any realistic diner expansion scenario.
