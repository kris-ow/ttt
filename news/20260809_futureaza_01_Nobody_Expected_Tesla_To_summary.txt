This "futureaza" episode, co-hosted with a guest from "the Tesla Life," is a casual, comedic roundup of Canada-focused Tesla and EV news: an electric yard-tractor fleet deployment, Model Y order backlogs out of Giga Berlin, a small Tesla vehicle buyback tied to cross-border certification issues, new Canadian EV charging investment, and a Canadian EV rebate program nearing its claims cap. There is no discussion of autonomy, robotaxi, Optimus, or SpaceX; the content is retail/market-oriented and regional in scope.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y demand outstripping Berlin supply in Canada:** According to the hosts, Canadian Model Y orders are being pushed into 2027 for delivery because Giga Berlin — the sole source of Canada's Model Y supply — is stretched thin supplying Europe and other export markets; Model 3s for Canada come from China instead. Berlin has reportedly added extra production shifts to help meet demand.
2. **Small Tesla buyback of Canadian-market vehicles:** Tesla is buying back several Canadian-market Model 3 and Model Y units that were imported into the US but were never certified for US sale; the hosts state the vehicles are not defective, simply non-certified for the US market. The host speculated a specific vehicle count, but immediately corrected himself, so no reliable figure is given.
3. **Canadian EV rebate program (EVAP) nearing its claims limit:** A new Canadian incentive program (EVAP), which replaced a prior credit program, has reportedly logged over 44,000 claims and is approaching capacity as buyers rush to claim rebates before amounts step down over time. Eligibility requires the final transaction price to be under $50,000 Canadian (unlike the prior MSRP-based rule), and Tesla has reportedly adjusted fee bundling so its entry-level Model Y qualifies; the rebate is described as being worth $5,000 this year.
4. **Toyota reportedly leading Canadian EV/vehicle sales, Tesla constrained:** One host says Tesla has been competing "with an arm tied behind its back" in Canada because Model 3 imports from China only recently resumed, limiting available inventory even as demand and delivery backlogs grow.

## Market & Competition
1. **Electric yard tractors gaining fleet-level adoption:** WTC Group has added Calmar Ottawa T2 electric yard tractors (used to shuttle trailers at ports, factories, and distribution centers) to its fleet, which the hosts frame as electrification of yard operations moving from pilot programs to real fleet strategy. The newer unit carries about 280 kWh of onboard energy, roughly 70 kWh more than a prior demo unit from a year earlier, and is charged overnight on-site given the short, low-speed duty cycle.
2. **Canada expands EV charging investment, especially for multi-unit housing:** Canada is investing roughly $10.9 million (with about $9 million for chargers and $2 million for education) across 22 EV charging infrastructure and education projects, with a focus on multi-residential housing to address at-home charging access. One host also cited British Columbia's public charging network expanding to over 8,900 ports, an 87% increase since 2023, though he was visibly unsure how many of those ports are specifically in Vancouver versus the wider province.
