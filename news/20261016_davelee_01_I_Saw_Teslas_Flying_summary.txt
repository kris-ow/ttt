Two Tesla investors/content creators (davelee and a guest identified as Emmett, referred to here generically given naming ambiguity) recap their in-person attendance at Tesla's Roadster unveiling event in Waco, Texas, reacting to the flying-car demo, debating whether the "rocket version" will ever be street legal, and extending into broader Tesla/SpaceX merger speculation, robotaxi economics, Cybertruck demand, and a long freeform debate about Optimus's path to usefulness and the AI intelligence race.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster demo reaction:** Both speakers describe the flying-car demonstration (car ascending a ramp, hovering, using cold-air thrusters) as physics-defying and among the most impressive product demos they've seen; however, they note the demo car had no driver and the website lists the flying/rocket-thruster version as "invite only" and not approved for public roads, raising doubts about whether that capability will ever be publicly sold. They speculate it might only ever be offered in geofenced private areas or special track/amusement settings with legal disclaimers, not on public roads.
2. **Safety signaling at the event:** The speakers note attendees were kept roughly a quarter-mile away during the live demo and the car only flew halfway up the ramp rather than completing a full launch, which they interpret as Tesla itself signaling the technology is not yet safe for close proximity or full deployment.
3. **Roadster as last manual car / future lineup implication:** The speakers note Tesla statements at the event (including from a Tesla executive referred to as "Lars," likely Lars Moravy) framing the Roadster as the company's last manually-driven car, which they interpret as confirmation that future Tesla vehicles will be built without steering wheels, "unboxed" and possibly without paint shops, for a driverless-only future lineup.
4. **Cybertruck demand debate:** One speaker, a Cybertruck owner who daily-drives a Model S, argues Cybertruck's radical design limits demand and that a conventional-pickup version could have sold substantially more units, speculating a figure as high as 500,000/year hypothetically; the other pushes back that charging/range practicality, not styling, is the bigger constraint. They also discuss quarterly Cybertruck volumes loosely in the 10,000–15,000 range (uncertain, not pinned to a specific official figure) and note real-world range performance around 300 miles versus Rivian's reported range shortfall in some reviews.
5. **Founders Roadster order:** One speaker has a Founders Series Roadster reservation, expressing excitement about the design (comparing it to Lamborghini/Transformers styling) but disappointment that no convertible option was shown and that the 2028 delivery timeline is a downside.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab ramp as stock catalyst:** The speakers argue Tesla's stock has stalled because vehicle sales have plateaued (characterized as roughly flat versus levels from several years ago), and that a Cybercab/robotaxi ramp across Texas (Dallas, Houston, San Antonio), plus Miami, Tampa, and Las Vegas, showing up in quarterly revenue and margins would be the catalyst for a valuation re-rating, potentially doubling Tesla's market cap if "transportation as a service" gets priced in.
2. **Rollout pace skepticism:** One speaker expresses skepticism that institutional investors are yet pricing in Cybercab or Optimus, arguing robotaxi rollouts have consistently been slower than the market expects, which could delay the anticipated re-rating.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus usefulness timeline (speaker estimate, not guidance):** One speaker estimates it could take three to four years for Optimus to become genuinely useful across a wide range of household tasks, contingent on Tesla both perfecting hardware/dexterity (potentially by a future Optimus hardware version) and solving what he sees as Tesla's current gap in generalized AI intelligence compared to frontier AI labs.
2. **Grok/generalized intelligence gap:** The speaker argues Tesla has strong real-time "physical world AI" (from FSD) and unmatched humanoid manufacturing capability, but lacks frontier-level generalized intelligence; he says Grok is not currently at the frontier and that Tesla would need it to catch up for Optimus to handle complex, unscripted tasks like cooking or cleanup.
3. **Early Optimus rollout expectations:** The speaker predicts Optimus will initially be used internally in Tesla's own factories, with external factory/partner deployments possibly in the following year, and does not expect consumer sales until the robot can perform tasks beyond "low-value" chores — arguing early units will be expensive and only justify their price once broadly capable.
4. **Competitive positioning vs. Figure AI:** Both speakers believe Tesla is "one or two steps ahead" of competitors like Figure AI on functional humanoid use cases (an assertion, not independently verified), though they debate whether rivals will unveil more impressive general-purpose demos soon.

## Financials
1. **Operating leverage from a sales doubling:** One speaker argues the clearest path to a Tesla stock re-rating would be new vehicle models that double unit sales, which he says would sharply improve operating leverage — citing illustrative figures of roughly 25% gross margins and 15–18% operating margins if volumes doubled (his own estimate, not stated Tesla guidance).

## Market & Competition
1. **AI valuation bubble concerns:** One speaker says he's concerned about a possible AI valuation correction in the next one to six months, citing Anthropic's reported $2 trillion valuation (unconfirmed, as described by the speaker) as "no hardware" software-only exposure that could pop if revenue growth decelerates, which he says would hit AI-adjacent names including SpaceX; the other speaker is more sanguine, expecting volatility and possible repricing in scarcity-priced inputs (e.g., memory, data centers) rather than a catastrophic bubble.
2. **Smartest-model-wins thesis:** One speaker argues businesses will increasingly pay up for the most capable ("frontier") AI models rather than settle for cheaper, "good enough" alternatives, because competitive pressure punishes companies that under-invest in intelligence; the other is more persuaded that cost-efficient, slightly-behind models will suffice for most use cases.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation reignited by Roadster event:** The speakers interpret the Roadster's cold-air thruster technology (developed jointly with SpaceX) as an implicit "merger roadshow," arguing investor sentiment toward a Tesla-SpaceX merger has shifted markedly more favorable over the prior several months, and that both TSLA and SPCX rose roughly 3% in reaction to the event (the speakers' own observation, not independently verified against the reference price table). They speculate an announcement could come as soon as the companies' next earnings events, though no shareholder meeting date has been set, and believe a confirmed merger could drive a sustained double-digit bounce in both stocks.
