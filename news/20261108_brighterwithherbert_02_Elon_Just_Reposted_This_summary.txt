This video from brighterwithherbert covers Tesla's and SpaceX's sharp Thursday selloff amid a broader AI-stock rout, Elon Musk's reposted "don't panic" tweet, the Texas gubernatorial election's implications for data centers, Tesla's robotaxi expansion to a seventh city, the Cybertruck production wind-down at Giga Texas, October regional EV sales, FSD mileage milestones, and SpaceX's Starlink Mobile and Starship 15 flight news. The host's core thesis: the week's stock decline was driven mainly by a broad AI/rate-driven market selloff rather than company-specific problems, while the underlying businesses (robotaxi, FSD, international EV sales, Starlink, Starship) mostly continued to improve, with the Cybertruck as the clear exception.

## Financials & Market Reaction
1. **Thursday selloff:** TSLA closed around $343, down about 10.8%; SPCX closed around $124, down about 9%. The Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500 had their worst day since roughly a year earlier, and the chip index fell about 12.3%, its worst performance since the COVID-era crash, triggered by weak Qualcomm and Arm earnings reports that revived doubts about AI infrastructure spending returns.
2. **Friday rebound:** Chip stocks and Tesla/SpaceX rebounded Friday — TSLA closed up about 4% near $358, SPCX up about 8% near $134 — leaving Tesla down roughly 10% for the week and SpaceX roughly flat.
3. **Fed rate odds rise:** Friday's US jobs report showed about 165,000 jobs added in October versus an expected ~65,000, raising odds (per the host, three-in-four) of a December Fed rate hike — a headwind for long-duration growth stocks like Tesla and SpaceX.
4. **Elon's response — "don't panic" repost:** Elon reposted an April 2022 tweet advising investors to buy shares in companies whose products they believe in and not panic during market selloffs; later that evening he endorsed a Warren Buffett stock-market analogy shared by another account.
5. **AI-stock reframing:** The host argues Tesla is now trading as an AI stock (robotaxi, Optimus, compute) and fell roughly twice the Nasdaq's decline because large funds sell Tesla alongside Nvidia during AI selloffs; SpaceX is also exposed via its Colossus 2 data center in Memphis, leased to companies including Anthropic.
6. **SpaceX revenue concentration risk:** A third-party estimate cited by the host put Anthropic at about 34.5% of SpaceX's revenue in the prior quarter (unconfirmed, third-party figure), a concentration the host flags as a risk given Anthropic could reportedly leave after early 2027.
7. **Buying activity and analyst note:** About 348,000 SPCX shares (~$47 million) were bought on Wednesday despite the selloff. Morgan Stanley analyst Adam Jonas nearly doubled his 2027 SpaceX revenue forecast to about $175 billion and called the stock cheap relative to the S&P 500 (per the host's characterization of the note).
8. **Government revenue exposure:** Elon stated on Friday that SpaceX government revenue would be under 10% of total revenue this quarter and under 5% a year from now, addressing post-election concerns about government-contract exposure.

## Market & Competition — Texas Data Center Risk
1. **Election outcome and data-center policy:** Democrats won the Texas governorship for the first time in 36 years, with governor-elect Gina Hinojosa having campaigned on pausing new data-center construction pending Texan-protection legislation. Analyst Dan Ives estimated roughly 25–30% of planned US data-center gigawatts were slated for Texas.
2. **Conflicting readings of Hinojosa's stance:** Gavin Baker characterized Hinojosa's position as allowing all already-permitted Texas data centers to complete construction (covering most of 2027 and part of 2028), with new centers facing requirements on taxes, electricity price protections, water use, and union labor; Elon called this "interesting." Hinojosa's own remarks, per a video circulated by Sawyer Merritt, were less specific, saying Tesla/Musk companies could operate "on our terms" with new protective laws — the host notes Baker's framing is the optimistic interpretation, not confirmed policy.
3. **Legislative timeline limits near-term risk:** Tesla blogger Alexandra Merz noted the Texas Legislature meets in regular session only once every two years (January–May) with special sessions capped at 30 days, and Republicans still control the Texas Senate — meaning new legislation realistically affects only data centers without permits yet, i.e., mainly 2028-onward projects.
4. **Terafab construction pace:** Elon, in an interview with a podcast host referenced by the host, called data-center opposition a "temporary problem" and said Tesla's manufacturing "Terafab" would be located in Grimes County, Texas; rapid steel-structure progress at the site was reported by a source on the show.
5. **SpaceX's Arizona data center:** SpaceX's next major data center, in Cochise County, Arizona, is the subject of a public hearing scheduled for December 9; SpaceX claims (per its own public statements at the hearing) it will cut electricity bills by 40% for local cooperative members and avoid industrial water and gas turbines — addressing most of the concerns raised for Texas, with union labor unaddressed.

## Robotaxi
1. **Seventh city launch — Fort Worth:** Tesla's robotaxi service began driverless operation in Fort Worth, becoming the service's seventh city with no safety driver. Geofence sizes: Austin remains largest at about 270 square miles, Houston about 250, Fort Worth starting at about 140.
2. **Fleet registration numbers:** Per RobotaxiTracker-style tracking cited by the host, Tesla had 1,244 vehicles registered with the Texas DMV as of last week (up from 59 in June), trailing Waymo by about 15; Waymo subsequently added more vehicles (26, then 41) to extend its lead. A separate tracker (Robotaxi Radar) estimated Tesla added roughly 20 new vehicles per day on average in October. Of the 1,244 registered Texas vehicles, 573 were Cybertrucks (likely robotaxi-configured vehicles rather than the pickup).
3. **Las Vegas expansion signals:** Diego Suarez reportedly counted 43 vehicles parked at a Las Vegas robotaxi lot on Wednesday night; Nevada has already granted Tesla a permit, and per Robotaxi Radar's interpretation of state rules, service could start November 24 or by a December 18 deadline — exact date unconfirmed.
4. **Hiring patterns point to next cities:** Job-posting tracking (Tesla Yoda) noted Fort Worth's fleet-support team was staffed about a month before launch, and similar support roles are now open in 17 cities without active service, including Atlanta, Chicago, Nashville, and suburbs of Boston, Detroit, Philadelphia, DC, and New York; night-technician openings were also spotted in nine Texas cities without service yet (San Antonio, El Paso, Corpus Christi, Lubbock, etc.).
5. **Pricing versus Uber/Waymo mixed:** In Houston, Robotaxi Radar found Tesla's robotaxi beat Uber on a short trip ($14 vs. $32) but lost on some longer cross-city trips during rush hour, with Uber applying a 25% discount. In Tampa, Tesla beat Waymo on price in 12 of 17 tracked comparisons (e.g., $9 vs. $11 on one trip).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production wind-down:** Elon announced Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas around mid-2027 to make room for increased production (implied: Cybercab). Kelley Blue Book data cited shows US Cybertruck sales fell nearly half last year to about 20,000 units, now running around 3,500–3,700 per quarter (an annualized pace of about 15,000) versus Tesla's original 250,000/year target; at current rates, the host estimates Cybertruck generates roughly $2.5 billion annually versus about $50 billion for Model Y and $20 billion for Model 3.
2. **Fremont and Giga Texas space reallocation:** Space previously used for Model S/X at Fremont is reportedly being transferred to the Optimus program; at Giga Texas, the vacated Cybertruck line is intended for Cybercab production, which a commentator (Rob Rader) interprets as a signal Tesla expects FSD to reach sufficient scalability by mid-2027.
3. **Cybercab testing expands to Detroit:** The Detroit News reportedly found about two dozen Cybercabs undergoing winter testing in the Detroit metro area with test/safety drivers.
4. **European and Asian sales strength:** Tracked European markets showed about 6,700 Tesla registrations in October, up 69% year-over-year, with Norway up 131%, Spain up 271%, Germany up 290%, while the Netherlands fell 48%. Giga Berlin reportedly produced 7,000 Model Ys in a single week for the first time a few weeks prior. South Korea registered about 9,100 Teslas in October (+111%), its ninth straight month as the top import brand, bringing year-to-date Korean sales to about 98,300 versus about 60,000 the prior year. Australia saw a 342% October increase, with Model Y becoming the country's best-selling passenger car year-to-date (about 39,000 units vs. about 36,000 for the Toyota RAV4).
5. **US and new-market notes:** Per Kelley Blue Book, more than half of all US electric vehicles sold in Q3 were Model Y or Model 3, with Model Y alone outselling the next 24 EV models combined. Tesla is set to begin sales in Argentina in November.
6. **China softness:** Tesla's China wholesale sales were about 68,000 in October, up 10% year-over-year but down nearly 29% from September — the lowest monthly figure since February; the host cautions this may reflect normal early-quarter export timing rather than a trend.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD mileage milestone:** Tesla's fleet surpassed 17 billion supervised FSD miles, now covering 1,000 miles every 1.8 seconds (an annualized pace of about 18 billion miles/year), with the pace up 35% since July.
2. **European regulatory rename:** The Dutch regulator RDW approved renaming FSD (supervised) to "Tesla assisted driving" in Europe; the system's functionality is unchanged, but the maximum settable speed limit had to be reduced (from a higher percentage to 10%, per the transcript) to satisfy European regulators.
3. **Japan regulatory move:** Nikkei reports Japan's Ministry of Transport plans to approve driverless vehicles without steering wheels or pedals as a separate certification category starting in December, moving away from individual per-vehicle certification — potentially relevant to future Cybercab-type deployments.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink Mobile launch:** Starlink announced "Starlink Mobile," described as the first fully integrated nationwide satellite-and-terrestrial network in the US, combining ground-based 5G base stations using Qualcomm chips with Starlink satellites, targeting deployment starting in 2027; Qualcomm reportedly called it its largest 5G deal yet. The Washington Post also reported SpaceX is in talks with EchoStar about Boost Mobile.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship Flight 15 targeted for November 13:** SpaceX is targeting Friday, November 13 for Starship's 15th flight, per an SpaceX VP of launch quoted by the host (FAA planning materials referenced November 12, with the host treating the 13th as the likely target). Elon said SpaceX will attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage on this flight — if successful, it would be the first time all parts of an orbital rocket returned to Earth intact. SpaceX has also requested an increase in its permitted annual launch cadence from the site, from 25 to 100 per year.
