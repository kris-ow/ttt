This video features host Herbert interviewing Brian Wang, futurist and science blogger behind Next Big Future, in the wake of a Tesla earnings call that Brian describes as mixed — strong financials but some narrative confusion around robotaxi timelines. The core thesis is that Tesla's real bottleneck to robotaxi scale is not safety but operational hesitation (indecision at intersections and traffic scenarios), and that once this is resolved through near-term FSD point releases, a meaningful fleet of 5,000–60,000 unsupervised vehicles could be deployed by end of 2026 or Q1 2027. Brian also presents a bull case where improving FSD adoption, cheaper batteries, higher fuel prices, and Grok voice integration could meaningfully lift car sales, margins, and recurring software revenue — even before robotaxi economics fully materialize.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **The hesitation bottleneck is the real blocker:** Brian and Herbert agree that FSD version 14.3 is already very safe — Herbert states there has not been a true accident, only minor bumper incidents, and those have already stopped. The reason Tesla has not broadly rolled out 14.3 (currently only ~17 cars in Austin, Dallas, and Houston) is not a safety failure but an operational one: the system sometimes hesitates or loops at intersections, gets stuck behind buses or in left lanes, and can cause traffic disruption. This "hesitation" or "indecision" is identified as the true bottleneck to scale.

2. **Point releases are rapidly closing the gap:** Herbert argues that FSD 14.3.2 has already solved many of the issues present in 14.3 and 14.3.1 — including smart summon complaints, left-lane camping, and parking lot behavior. He notes that Tesla explicitly said reasoning has been applied to end-destination parking scenarios (a significant architectural improvement) but has not yet been applied to all edge-case traffic scenarios. Once reasoning is applied to these remaining scenarios, the hesitation problem should be largely resolved without needing to wait for FSD version 15.

3. **FSD version 15 is for global mass scale, not the prerequisite for initial rollout:** Brian clarifies that his earlier comment about needing FSD version 15 for "full-on robotaxi" referred to global, million-vehicle scale — not to initial city-by-city expansion. He and Herbert agree that 5x–10x improvement per version release is achievable, and that version 15 may arrive as soon as Q4 2026, at which point debate about sequencing becomes moot. Unsupervised FSD for personal vehicle owners is expected to begin rolling out region by region in approximately 7 months from the video date (roughly late 2026), across 12 states.

4. **Miles-per-car metric is being held back:** Brian notes that if Tesla's robotaxi cars were logging as many miles as Waymo vehicles, the fleet should already be generating roughly 17 million miles. The fact that they are not signals Tesla is deliberately constraining deployment while the hesitation issues are resolved. He suggests that a sharp jump in miles-per-car at the next earnings call (expected around July 22, 2026) would be a strong signal that Tesla is releasing its operational constraints.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab production is confirmed and underway:** Herbert states that Cybercab is already in production, framing it as a confirmed announcement from the earnings call. He argues strongly against a "slow roll" interpretation, noting that the Cybercab factory is purpose-built for mass scale and that slow-rolling it would mean burning cash unnecessarily. The disagreement between host and guest centers on whether the hesitation problem will constrain Cybercab fleet size to ~15,000 (Brian's estimate) or whether it will be resolved fast enough to allow 30,000+ units by year-end.

2. **Fleet size forecast for end of 2026:** Brian's base case is approximately 15,000 Cybercabs deployed by end of 2026, across roughly 25 cities with 50–100 vehicles per city and exceptional cities like Austin and the Bay Area reaching 200–500 vehicles. His optimistic scenario is 20,000–60,000 vehicles, with 500–1,000 per city across 40–50 cities. Herbert suggests 30,000 as a reasonable midpoint. Both agree that even 5,000 unsupervised vehicles would represent roughly double Waymo's current fleet of ~3,000.

3. **Material revenue threshold and Wall Street definition:** Brian defines "financially material" robotaxi revenue as reaching the legal/financial definition of materiality — roughly a few billion dollars of annual revenue — which he estimates would require approximately 50,000 vehicles at full utilization. Elon Musk was described as uncertain whether material scale would be achieved in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, leading Brian to interpret this as a signal that the 10,000–60,000 vehicle range is the realistic 2026 target. Analyst Warren Redlich is cited as having already pushed meaningful robotaxi earnings impact out to mid-2027 due to prior delays.

4. **Waymo comparison and remote operations contrast:** Brian notes that Waymo currently has approximately 3,000 vehicles and generates a limited amount of robotaxi revenue. He highlights that Waymo experiences 100x more traffic-blocking incidents than Tesla's system, and resolves this using remote human operators — a "crutch" Brian believes Tesla's more AI-heavy approach is designed to avoid. The ability for a passenger to verbally instruct a robotaxi (e.g., "that bus had an accident") is presented as a potential near-term solution to the stuck-vehicle problem.

5. **Grok voice integration as a robotaxi enabler:** Brian and Herbert discuss Grok voice and reasoning as a near-term solution to robotaxi indecision scenarios. If a passenger can verbally tell the vehicle to change lanes, exit, or override a stuck decision, the hesitation problem becomes manageable even before it is fully solved by the autonomy stack. The question of whether this can run locally on AI4 hardware (without cloud latency) or requires AI4+ or AI5 chips is debated; Brian believes it can be squeezed into current hardware with a stripped-down model parameter count.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Gen 3 reveal expected in July, production starting August:** Brian forecasts an Optimus version 3 reveal in July 2026, just ahead of production beginning in August 2026. Initial production volumes are expected to be only hundreds to low thousands of units in Q3 2026, scaling further in Q4, with tens of thousands not expected until 2027. He frames the initial units as doing "simple factory work" and notes the demo's quality will be critical for shifting market narrative.

2. **AI5 chip samples may go into early Optimus units:** Brian argues that while AI5 chips will not be in mass production until next year, sample quantities could be available now and could be placed into the first hundreds of Optimus units as a research and development deployment. This would allow early Optimus robots to run a 10 trillion parameter model locally — the same scale as what Brian describes as a potential near-AGI capability level.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **FSD take rate rising and per-car profit at multi-year high:** Herbert opens by noting that Tesla's FSD take rate hit 13.9%, up from 11% the prior year, and that per-car profit jumped to $8,570 — described as the highest in over two years. Brian frames this as evidence that the narrative of collapsing margins is incorrect, and that the data trend is moving in the opposite direction.

2. **Path to 500,000–550,000 cars per quarter:** Brian models a scenario where improved FSD quality drives ~10% more car sales, higher fuel prices in Europe (cited at €2–2.50 per liter, equivalent to ~$8–$10/gallon) add another ~10% sales lift, and cheaper batteries (from 4680 scaling and LFP procurement including from China) continuously reduce cost of goods sold. Combined, these factors could push quarterly deliveries from a ~450,000 baseline to 500,000–550,000, which Brian argues would generate enough free cash flow to partially offset $7 billion in additional capex.

3. **Sub-$30,000 China vehicle — no confirmation on the earnings call:** Brian mentions rumors (attributed to CNV Post and China sources) of a stripped-down Model 3 or Model Y variant for China, codenamed D41, priced below $30,000. He also references separate rumors of a slightly shorter-than-Model Y new vehicle. However, he explicitly states that neither was mentioned on the earnings call, and he therefore does not expect either to launch in Q2 2026, suggesting Q3 or Q4 as more likely windows if they happen at all.

4. **FSD adoption model and subscription trajectory:** Brian breaks down the current FSD user base: approximately 770,000 outright purchases (no longer available), approximately 510,000 subscriptions, totaling about 1.28 million in Q1 2026. He projects adding ~200,000 subscriptions in Q2 2026, bringing the adoption rate to ~15.6%. With EU and China regulatory approvals potentially arriving in Q3 2026, Brian models a path to 2 million FSD subscribers at ~19–20% adoption, and eventually 4–5 million FSD subscribers at ~$1 billion per year in subscription revenue.

## Financials

1. **Margin improvement path to 25%:** Brian projects margin improvements of 0.5–2% per quarter, driven by FSD revenue mix shift, battery cost reductions, and scale. He estimates current automotive gross margin at approximately 21.1% and sees a path to 23–25% by 2027. He notes that at 4–5 million FSD subscribers, per-vehicle profitability could reach $10,000–$11,000, and that robotaxi margins, once at scale, could push blended margins toward 60%. The transition from asset-company to subscription-company economics is described as Tesla's "AWS moment."

2. **Terafab investment details:** Brian discusses the Terafab initiative, noting a $3 billion investment for 2,000 chips, with SpaceX involved in part of the build-out. He expects the fab/lab to be completed next year (2027), with the first phase of the SpaceX-related component coming around 2028.

3. **Service revenue growth and supercharging:** Brian notes a 42% year-over-year increase in Tesla's service revenue and expects FSD adoption and supercharging expansion to further accelerate this line. He also mentions a separate investment opportunity for third-party ownership of Tesla Supercharger stations, which Tesla is reportedly encouraging.

## Market & Competition

1. **Grok voice as the "iPhone moment" for Tesla vehicles:** Brian draws an analogy between the flip phone-to-smartphone transition and the shift from current Tesla UX to a fully conversational, agentic Grok-powered in-car assistant. He shares personal anecdotes of Grok already responding to verbal commands — redirecting parking, changing lanes — and argues that once this capability is consistently reliable and local (not cloud-dependent), it will become a primary vehicle purchase driver.

2. **Grok model roadmap through late 2026:** Brian outlines a rapid Grok release schedule: Grok 4.3 (late April/early May), Grok 4.4 (early May), Grok 4.5 with 1.5 trillion parameters (late May), Grok 5 with ~6 trillion parameters (June–July), and Grok 5 Heavy with ~10 trillion parameters (August–September). He notes this is partly driven by xAI's desire to demonstrate AI credentials ahead of a SpaceX IPO targeted for around June 2026. He compares the 10 trillion parameter scale to Claude Opus (trained on ~5 trillion parameters) and suggests xAI could reach competitive AGI-level capability within months.
