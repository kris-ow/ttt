The article argues that a potential SpaceX/Tesla merger could allow Elon Musk to collect his trillion-dollar CEO pay package automatically — bypassing all operational milestones — by triggering a "change in control" clause embedded in the 2025 performance-based stock agreement.

## Financials
1. **"Change in Control" clause eliminates operational milestones:** The pay agreement's change-in-control language explicitly states that "the Operational Milestones shall be disregarded" in the event of a merger or buyout, leaving only market capitalization milestones to be evaluated. This means Tesla would not need to hit targets like 20 million vehicle deliveries, 10 million FSD subscriptions, 1 million Optimus bots, or 1 million commercial robotaxis for Musk to receive some or all of the 12 tranches of stock.

2. **Tesla's current valuation complicates the picture:** The article notes Tesla is trading at a price-to-earnings ratio of approximately 400 — described as 20–40x higher than traditional norms even for a growth company — making it an unlikely acquisition target at fair value. However, the author notes that merger/buyout discussions typically push valuations higher, potentially unlocking additional tranches.

3. **Self-dealing precedent with Twitter/xAI:** The article cites two prior instances as precedent: Musk's $44 billion Twitter acquisition in 2022 and a subsequent $45 billion all-stock transfer from himself to xAI in 2025, which was then sold to SpaceX after Tesla invested in xAI. The author characterizes these as a pattern of inflating company valuations through self-dealing rather than operational performance.

## Market & Competition
4. **NASDAQ rule change accelerates SpaceX IPO inclusion:** A NASDAQ rule change — which the article says Musk pushed for — would allow SpaceX to be listed on the exchange just 10 days after IPO rather than the prior one-year waiting period. The author argues this forces passive index funds (and by extension retirement accounts) to buy SpaceX at peak frothy valuations. SpaceX's IPO prospectus is also said to include language permitting a merger within the 180-day post-IPO lockup period.

5. **Shareholder dilution risk for index fund holders:** If an all-stock Tesla/SpaceX merger proceeds at inflated valuations, share issuance to Musk would dilute all other shareholders — including, post-SpaceX IPO, anyone holding a broad index fund. The author estimates this could affect roughly 5% of retirement account net wealth.
