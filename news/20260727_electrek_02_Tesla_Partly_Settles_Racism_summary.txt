Tesla confidentially settled with three of five named plaintiffs (Marcus Vaughn, Titus McCaleb, Monica Chatman) in the long-running Fremont factory racism lawsuit, but two named plaintiffs remain, alongside a class of up to 580 Black workers and a separate case brought by California's Civil Rights Department (CRD).

## Market & Competition
1. **Partial settlement leaves broader litigation intact:** Terms of the settlement with Vaughn, McCaleb, and Chatman weren't disclosed; a June 3 court filing revealed the deal, and an Alameda County Superior Court judge set September 11 to finalize it. The original case number remains open, with plaintiffs Garret Parker and Chanel Hendrix still litigating. In May 2024 the court had certified Vaughn as a class action covering roughly 6,000 Black workers, but Tesla won decertification after plaintiffs' lawyers couldn't line up enough workers for a manageable trial; a state appellate panel later cleared five grouped lawsuits to proceed, and plaintiffs' counsel says hundreds more workers hold unfiled right-to-sue letters.

2. **Worker allegations detailed in filings:** Filings describe a segregated worksite, slurs used by supervisors, nooses, and firings for complainants; workers reportedly called the plant "the plantation" and "the slaveship." CRD's February 2022 complaint (following a three-year probe) documented specific slurs and alleged an internally-known "porch monkey station"; one worker said he heard slurs 50–100 times a day. The EEOC's September 2023 federal complaint adds allegations of swastikas, threats, and nooses dating to at least 2015. Among 200+ worker declarations in the state case, about two-thirds reported seeing anti-Black graffiti, and dozens said complaints led to retaliation, including firings. Tesla disputes the allegations and submitted declarations from 228 employees who said they saw nothing; Musk has posted twice on the matter (2018, 2022) defending Tesla's position.

3. **State case heading to jury; pay and staffing data alleged:** On May 27 a court denied Tesla's motion for summary judgment in the CRD case, clearing it for a jury trial calendared for July 20 in Oakland. CRD alleges Tesla had 33 HR staff for 19,916 California workers in 2016 (about 1 per 604 employees), worsening to roughly 1 per 740 by 2020, and that Black workers averaged $1,533 less in monthly compensation than white workers between June 2018 and June 2024. Separately, Tesla and the EEOC agreed in January to move the federal case into private mediation, which the article notes involves no transcripts and destroyed notes. The article also recalls the Owen Diaz case: a 2021 jury awarded $137 million, reduced to $15 million, then $3.2 million after retrial, before Tesla settled confidentially rather than face a third trial.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla's pattern of confidential settlements contradicts Musk's public claim that Tesla would "never settle" unjust cases, and characterizes the strategy — settling vocal plaintiffs, sealing terms, pushing others to mediation — as an approach that "worked" on smaller cases but cannot scale to 580 workers or a state agency with a trial date. The author calls Tesla's defense (228 declarations of not having seen misconduct, plus a blog post) inadequate given allegations spanning from 2012 to the present.
