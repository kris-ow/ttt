A panel of three Tesla/SpaceX commentators (Joe Bhakdi, Jeff Lutz, and Alexandra Merz) discusses why TSLA stock fell sharply in July 2026, whether an AI-sector rebound could lift Tesla, the mechanics and risks of a possible Tesla-SpaceX merger, evidence of robotaxi rollout progress, and a supplier dispute threatening Cybertruck production.

## Financials / Market & Competition
1. **July was a historic momentum-stock selloff:** Bhakdi says July 2026 was the worst monthly selloff for momentum stocks in 30 years — worse than the dot-com crash and the financial crisis on a monthly basis — and argues Tesla's decline (from roughly $420 to $308) largely tracked this broader market move (which he attributes partly to a hedge-fund buyout/panic involving Ken Griffin and a figure referred to as "Ashenbrenner") rather than being Tesla-specific.
2. **Earnings reaction diverges from AI peers:** Lutz notes Tesla fell about 25% over the trailing month versus Nvidia roughly flat and Google down about 5%, arguing the market punished Tesla specifically for guiding to spend more than free cash flow without clearly tying that spend to accelerated robotaxi revenue.
3. **Capital markets growing wary of AI-related debt:** Merz cites a Goldman Sachs report (per her account) projecting roughly $7 trillion in financing needs over the next few years for AI/data-center buildouts, and notes SpaceX's bond issuances have been harder to place and have seen spreads widen on the secondary market — a signal she reads as capital-market stress that could affect both Tesla and SpaceX, which she estimates may jointly need over $200 billion in the next three to five years.
4. **Institutional ownership rising as retail sells:** Merz presents data (Fintel/NASDAQ) showing institutional ownership of Tesla shares is at its highest level ever (over 2 billion of ~3.95 billion shares outstanding, excluding Elon Musk's ~1.1 billion), while retail ownership has fallen to roughly 20% of the float, down from over 50% previously — she attributes this to retail selling into the robotaxi-guidance disappointment while institutions bought the dip.
5. **Elon Musk's stock motivations discussed:** Panelists agree Musk is not a short-term seller of either stock (he recently exercised options to acquire voting restricted stock) but must actively track both stock prices given active merger planning, since relative valuations would determine merger terms.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX AI compute could be a major earnings catalyst:** Citing VC Gavin Baker's read of an Nvidia report, the panel discusses a claim that SpaceX AI could have roughly 4 gigawatts of compute for lease this year and another ~4 GW next year, with some estimating this could translate to around $100 billion in annual revenue — potentially justifying a trillion-dollar valuation — if confirmed at SpaceX's August 4 earnings call. Bhakdi cautions this remains an unconfirmed report and that the real question is how many gigawatts of Colossus capacity actually comes online and when.
2. **New "Minihard" facility revealed:** Following a social media post showing Colossus 2 and facilities called "Macrohard" and "Macroharder," Musk reportedly confirmed a further facility called "Minihard," which will reportedly house a comparable number of Nvidia GB300 chips in a denser configuration — details the panel treats as still emerging.
3. **SpaceX share unlock mechanics debated:** With the first major post-IPO lockup expiration set for August 6 (two days after SpaceX's August 4 earnings call), the panel debates how much forced selling pressure this will create. Merz argues roughly two-thirds of shares (Musk's ~48-49% plus SPV holdings) remain locked well beyond this tranche, and that many early investors already hedged via options, making the unlock less disruptive than feared. Bhakdi counters that even predictable unlocks create mechanical selling pressure absent from normal price discovery, given the float will roughly double as new tranches unlock through December.
4. **SpaceX lacks a general counsel:** Merz notes SpaceX's IPO documents disclosed no current general counsel (predecessor David Harris retired at the end of 2025), with a VP of legal handling the role — she flags this as unusual for a company that recently IPO'd and relevant to how merger-related legal questions might be handled on the upcoming earnings call.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials (Merger)
1. **Merger overhang analysis:** Panelists agree institutional investors are increasingly modeling a Tesla-SpaceX merger, and that stronger SpaceX AI compute revenue announcements would need to be a genuine signed/paying contract (not just a report) to move sentiment. They argue any realistic merger must be at parity or better for Tesla shareholders (not a sub-1:1 deal), and that a higher SpaceX valuation makes the merger easier to execute; Bhakdi suggests an ideal SpaceX share price near $200 for merger timing purposes.
2. **China exposure flagged as merger risk:** Merz raises Tesla's China manufacturing/regulatory exposure as an unresolved and politically sensitive issue that would need to be addressed early in any merger filings, given US-China trade tensions, though she notes no concrete news has emerged since Musk's trip to China with President Trump in May.

## Robotaxi
1. **Driverless Cybercab sightings expand beyond Giga Texas:** The panel highlights community-tracked sightings (crowdsourced, citing accounts including "Cybercab Spotter" and Joe Tegtmeyer) of driverless Cybercabs — with no occupant at all — operating on public roads in Austin, beyond the Giga Texas facility, which they view as a more significant signal than the previously known unsupervised robotaxi fleet.
2. **Active Robotaxi fleet in Austin:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com-aligned figures cited in discussion, roughly 68 of the Austin robotaxi fleet are now operating unsupervised, with the total Austin count around 76 — Lutz frames this as expected progress rather than a surprise, calling the driverless-Cybercab-on-public-roads sightings the more notable development.
3. **Long-term robotaxi fleet projections:** Asked for a 2030 projection, Bhakdi says he is confident the global Cybercab fleet will exceed one million units, including deployment in Europe and China; he separately estimates Austin alone could reach a roughly 2 million-unit annual production run rate within three years of an inflection point in FSD scaling, and notes Tesla (per an unnamed Tesla figure) has said Cybercab production will eventually exceed that of all other Tesla vehicles combined.
4. **Regulatory uncertainty in China and Europe:** Merz flags that China's stance on approving robotaxis and Chinese-made autonomous vehicles remains unknown amid US-China tech restrictions, and that in Europe, FSD Supervised approval requires 65% of countries representing 65% of EU population to approve; without that threshold, even currently-approved countries (e.g., Netherlands) could see approvals rolled back, delaying any Cybercab production plans at Giga Berlin.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production at risk from supplier dispute:** A dispute with a tooling supplier (Angstrom Automotive Group, which acquired Anderton Casting) is reportedly headed to court after the new owner would not release custom die-casting tooling, trim dies, and fixtures that Tesla says it paid for; Tesla claims replacing the equipment would take five to six months, while component inventory is reportedly dwindling within days. Lutz frames this as a common, typically resolvable OEM tooling-ownership dispute rather than an existential threat to Cybertruck, while Merz notes Cybertruck sales volumes have run well below original supplier expectations, complicating the dispute's context.
