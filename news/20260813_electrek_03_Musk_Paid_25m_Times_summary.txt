The article reports on the AFL-CIO's annual executive paywatch report, which found Elon Musk was paid roughly 2,522,203 times the average Tesla worker's pay in 2025, skewing S&P 500 CEO-to-worker pay ratio data. The piece, framed as opinion/commentary by Electrek's Jameson Dow, argues this compensation is undeserved given Tesla's declining financial performance and Musk's conduct.

## Financials
1. **Pay ratio and compensation figures:** The AFL-CIO report states Musk's compensation as Tesla CEO was $158 billion (reported "last year," i.e., for 2025), exceeding Tesla's 2025 total revenue of $94 billion, which fell 3% year-over-year — Tesla's first-ever annual revenue decline. The average S&P 500 CEO-to-worker pay ratio was 5,387:1 including Musk, versus 312:1 excluding him (up from 285:1 the prior year). Tesla GAAP profits were down 61% in 2025 compared to 2024.
2. **Cost of anti-EV campaign:** The article states Musk spent $288 million of his own money on an anti-EV campaign that contributed to the defeat of US EV incentives, which the article claims cost Tesla $1.4 billion in one quarter alone, with continued misses since.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Jameson Dow) argues Musk's massive pay package is undeserved and that he has been actively harmful to Tesla — citing reputational damage from racially charged public statements, the firing of the Supercharger team, the Cybertruck's poor performance, overpromising on FSD, diverting Tesla resources (e.g., GPU allocation) to xAI, and general distraction from Tesla's core business — concluding it would be hard to find an employee who has done more damage to a company while being paid more than all other employees combined.
