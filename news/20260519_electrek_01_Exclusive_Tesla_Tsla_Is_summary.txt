Tesla is building a large-scale, vertically integrated solar panel manufacturing operation at its Brookshire, Texas facility (Empire West Business Park, ~35 miles west of Houston), co-located with the Megapack Megafactory already under construction there. This is the first confirmed location for Tesla's stated 100 GW annual solar manufacturing ambition.

## Energy

1. **Brookshire facility details:** Tesla leases Buildings 9 and 10 at the site, totaling 1.65 million square feet, with plans for an additional 600,000-square-foot building. The entire site is 100% leased to Tesla under a long-term agreement. Capital expenditure across multiple simultaneous facility builds exceeds $250 million, with cleanroom-grade manufacturing environments under construction.

2. **Full vertical integration planned:** Unlike simple panel assembly, Tesla is building the complete production chain at Brookshire: ingot growth, wafer slicing, photovoltaic cell production, and finished panel assembly. This is consistent with the previously reported $2.9 billion deal (reported by CNBC in March 2026) to purchase Chinese solar manufacturing equipment from suppliers including Suzhou Maxwell Technologies, with equipment destined for Texas.

3. **Context and scale:** Tesla currently produces its TSP-420 panel at Gigafactory New York (Buffalo) at roughly 300 MW/year capacity, launched commercially in January 2026. The Houston operation represents a stated 300x scale-up. For comparison, First Solar — the largest domestic solar manufacturer — projects 17.7 GW of capacity by 2027; total U.S. solar installations in 2023 were ~32 GW. Elon Musk announced the 100 GW target at Davos in January 2026. Co-location with the $200 million Megapack Megafactory offers logistics advantages, and Houston's port infrastructure is critical for receiving the Chinese equipment imports.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert expresses cautious optimism: the $250M+ construction commitment and $2.9B equipment deal represent real capital behind what had been rhetoric, distinguishing this from the Buffalo subsidy-driven playbook — but the 100 GW by 2028 target is characterized as "wildly ambitious to completely delusional," with Chinese export approvals and construction timelines as key risks.
