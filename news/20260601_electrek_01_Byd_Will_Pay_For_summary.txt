BYD has announced it will assume full, uncapped financial liability for at-fault crashes occurring while its "God's Eye" urban driving system is active in China — a commitment Tesla has never made for FSD, inverting the industry's standard Level 2 liability model.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **BYD's liability guarantee terms:** Announced May 28 at BYD's vehicle intelligence strategy event, the policy covers all direct economic losses — repairs, third-party property damage, and personal injury — when a driver is using BYD's urban navigate-on-autopilot function in compliance with regulations. There is no payout cap, no requirement to purchase separate intelligent driving insurance, and claims will not raise commercial insurance premiums. Coverage applies to God's Eye A and B systems for one year from delivery; existing owners receive it upon updating to God's Eye 5.0 OTA and it is not restricted to the first or registered owner.

2. **Precedent from parking feature:** BYD applied a similar liability guarantee to L4 smart parking in July 2025, which it says drove feature usage from 21% to 93%. The new policy extends that model to city driving, which BYD calls a "double guarantee."

3. **Tesla's contrasting position:** Tesla's FSD (Supervised) is a Level 2 system and its owner's manual explicitly states drivers bear full responsibility at all times. A Miami federal jury awarded a $243 million judgment against Tesla over a fatal Autopilot crash, assigning Tesla one-third of the blame — a liability Tesla contested rather than absorbed voluntarily.

## Market & Competition

1. **Price gap underscores the contrast:** BYD's LiDAR-equipped God's Eye B system is a one-time 12,000-yuan (~$1,770) option available across BYD's full lineup. Tesla's equivalent in China ("Tesla Assisted Driving") costs 64,000 yuan (~$9,400) as a one-off purchase with no subscription option — roughly five times more expensive, while placing all crash liability on the driver.

2. **BYD's fleet scale:** BYD states it now has 3.15 million vehicles on the road with assisted driving, generating up to 200 million kilometers of driving data per day.

## Editorial Stance
Lambert argues BYD has answered the liability question the industry has spent a decade avoiding, framing the guarantee as both a credible safety statement and a data-harvesting strategy. He contends Tesla, despite 10 billion FSD miles logged, hides behind Level 2 disclaimers and arbitration clauses, and challenges why Tesla won't make a similar commitment. He also cautions readers that God's Eye, like Tesla FSD, remains a Level 2 system requiring driver attention.
