The core argument is that SpaceX's ability to deploy orbital AI data centers represents a virtually limitless economic opportunity, and that the SpaceX IPO — expected in June 2026 — will be a defining moment for both SpaceX and Tesla investors.

## Market & Competition

Baron's firm holds roughly $15 billion in SpaceX and $5 billion in Tesla. He started investing in SpaceX in 2017, putting in approximately $1.7 billion, which is now worth approximately $15 billion at current market prices — nearly a 10x return.

2. **SpaceX valuation and IPO expectations:** Ron Baron believes SpaceX will be worth $10 trillion, $20 trillion, or even $30 trillion over the next 10 to 15 years, stating he could be "very low" on those projections. The SpaceX IPO is described as coming in late June 2026 on NASDAQ. Betting markets are projecting the SpaceX IPO will close above $2.2 trillion on its first day, with the IPO itself expected to price around $1.7 trillion. Baron also notes that after the IPO, SpaceX will be added to multiple indexes within 5, 10, and 15 days respectively, forcing large institutional investors and pension funds to buy the stock.

3. **Space-based AI compute as the core SpaceX thesis:** Jo Bhakdi argues that space-based AI data centers represent the single most transformative economic opportunity in human history, comparing it to the colonial-era race to capture global resources. The thesis is that there is unlimited demand for AI compute, the only bottleneck is energy, and deploying AI satellites in orbit circumvents terrestrial energy and regulatory constraints. Jo Bhakdi identifies four dominant players as likely owners of this orbital compute layer: SpaceX, Google, Amazon, and Microsoft.

4. **Anthropic and Google partnerships signaling orbital compute buildout:** The video highlights two key recent deals: SpaceX's deal with Anthropic, reportedly generating $5–6 billion per year from leasing the Colossus One terrestrial data center in Memphis, with a planned space-based component as well; and ongoing negotiations between SpaceX and Google to explore orbital data centers under Google's "Project Suncatcher," with Google planning to launch prototype satellites in early 2027. Google is noted to hold approximately a 5–6% stake in SpaceX from a 2015 investment. Jo Bhakdi expects Amazon to announce a similar deal soon.

5. **Jo Bhakdi's tactical caution on the SpaceX IPO:** Despite being "infinitely bullish" on SpaceX's long-term value — calling $30 trillion achievable "sooner than later" — Jo Bhakdi says he will not buy the IPO. His reasoning is that there is significant forward pull into the IPO price, the broader market has run up strongly, and he believes there is a high probability of getting SpaceX at a lower price during a market correction in the following 12–24 months. He estimates the real "forcing function" for SpaceX's exponential trajectory begins around 2028 or 2029.

## Robotaxi

1. **Tesla-SpaceX merger debate among Tesla shareholders:** Betting markets are currently giving a 50% probability that Tesla and SpaceX merge within the next year. A significant debate exists in the Tesla investor community, with one camp arguing a merger would be unfair to Tesla shareholders who have waited years for the robotaxi revenue ramp to materialize, and another camp viewing SpaceX as a merger of equals that brings enormous value. Jo Bhakdi argues that the premise of the anti-merger argument — that Tesla is massively underpriced right now but will be fairly priced in a few months — is flawed because markets have become much more efficient and informed about the Tesla robotaxi thesis.

2. **Merger ratio and "magnet effect" thesis:** Jo Bhakdi develops a qualitative thesis that if a Tesla-SpaceX merger is widely anticipated, the two companies' market caps will converge toward a one-to-one ratio. His reasoning: it would be unusual for Tesla to be valued higher than SpaceX in a merger, and a ratio significantly below one-to-one would likely be rejected by Tesla shareholders. This creates what he calls a "magnet effect," where Tesla's market cap is pulled toward SpaceX's market cap. Tesla's market cap at time of discussion is cited as approximately $1.33 trillion, while SpaceX's IPO valuation is expected around $2.2 trillion, implying meaningful upside for Tesla if the thesis holds.

3. **SpaceX IPO as a potential catalyst for Tesla stock:** Host Herbert relays a view from commentator "Larry" that the SpaceX IPO alone — even without any merger announcement — could push Tesla's stock up 10–15%. Jo Bhakdi is skeptical of this specific claim, arguing the IPO is not new information to markets, though he acknowledges the magnet effect thesis could provide some mechanism for Tesla appreciation if merger speculation remains active.
