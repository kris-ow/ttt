This video features FutureAzA host Brian in conversation with drone journalist Joe Tegtmeyer for an in-depth aerial tour and analysis of Giga Texas, covering ongoing construction, production activity, and infrastructure buildout across the sprawling site. The core thesis is that Giga Texas is rapidly evolving from a car factory into a convergence point for Tesla's most ambitious future technologies — including Cybercab production, Optimus manufacturing, AI data centers, and a joint SpaceX advanced chip fabrication facility — with construction activity suggesting a multi-decade buildout horizon. Joe, who has been flying over the site for nearly six years and over 50,000 miles of drone coverage, provides granular observations about equipment staging, structural progress, and what the physical evidence implies about Tesla's near-term production and infrastructure plans.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Multi-model production lines running simultaneously:** Joe reports that Giga Texas has ramped to 12-hour shifts, with normally off days now running 10-hour shifts. Critically, Cybertruck, Model Y, and Cybercab production lines are all running at the same time and vehicles are being interspersed in the output yard — a sign that all production lines are operational simultaneously, something not consistently seen over the prior six to eight months.

2. **Lower-cost Cybertruck production underway, deliveries expected mid-to-late June 2026:** Production of the lower-cost Cybertruck variant has begun, with suppliers and production lines brought up to meet what Joe describes as "pretty big demand." The earliest customers are beginning to receive VINs, with deliveries expected around mid-to-late June 2026.

3. **Model Y demand signals positive:** Brian notes anecdotal evidence of strong Model Y demand, including a personal example of a buyer choosing a new Model Y over a used Model 3 due to current lease incentives and financing. Joe adds that Tesla has reduced referral credit amounts significantly, which he interprets as a positive signal — Tesla no longer needs to incentivize purchases as heavily. FSD is also being cited by buyers as a purchase motivator.

4. **4680 battery cell production expanding significantly:** The storage yard near the racetrack that was previously almost completely full of 4680 manufacturing equipment is now largely emptied — the equipment has been moved into the factory as part of an extensive production capacity expansion. Concrete slabs have been removed, steel beams added, and new cabinets and equipment installed. Joe notes the factory originally had space for eight production lines, operated with roughly one and a half effective lines for a long time, and is now expanding aggressively. The 4680 is identified as critical for Optimus, Cybercab, Cybertruck, and the Tesla Semi — with the Semi alone requiring roughly 10–11 times the cells of a Model Y.

5. **Roadster to be built at Giga Texas, early signs possibly already visible:** Lars (referenced without a last name) implied that evidence of Roadster production preparation would be visible over the coming months. Joe speculates that permits and large new production-looking equipment being staged near the stamping area on the southeast side of the factory — including a gantry crane, SPMTs, and body-and-white tanks inconsistent with Cybercab needs — may already represent early Roadster production infrastructure. Joe also notes there is still meaningful unused floor space adjacent to the Cybercab production line.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD version 14.3 beginning to roll out:** Joe mentions he received FSD version 14.3 the morning of the recording. Brian notes he is still on the prior version and expects to receive it soon. Joe connects the activity of the on-site data center cooling systems — six fans running simultaneously — to FSD training workloads, suggesting heavy compute activity.

2. **On-site data center dedicated to FSD training:** The cooling towers on the southeast corner of the main building, with all six fans operational, are identified as serving a data center dedicated to Full Self-Driving training. Joe frames the full operation of those cooling systems as a positive indicator of training throughput.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab production visible and interspersed with other vehicles:** Cybercabs are actively being produced at Giga Texas and are visibly interspersed with Model Y and Cybertruck units in the output staging areas, confirming the production line is live and running alongside other vehicle lines.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus factory (North Campus) under active construction:** The "North Campus" north of Cortex 2 is identified as the dedicated Optimus manufacturing facility. The goal for this factory is at least 10 million Optimus units per year — contrasted with the Fremont pilot line, which is targeting approximately 1 million units per year run rate (using space freed up by stopping Model S/X production). The North Campus factory is broken into two phases: Phase 1 (currently under construction on flat land) has progressed from earthwork to footings to first steel erection, with steel beams and columns being staged in an adjacent field. Phase 2 involves reclaiming an old sand and gravel mining site (visible as linear hills and ponds) and will roughly double the factory's footprint. When complete, the factory will be nearly the same length as Giga Texas itself, though narrower.

2. **Second Cortex data center partially operational for Optimus training:** Cortex 2 went partially operational in April 2026 per the Q1 financial results meeting. It is described as having two phases, with approximately half capacity now live. Tesla has stated the facility is intended for Optimus robot training. Temporary cooling units are augmenting the permanent chillers while construction of the second phase continues, targeting completion around end of summer or fall 2026.

## Energy

1. **~400 total Megapacks planned for Giga Texas across multiple sites:** Approximately 130 Megapacks have already been installed at Cortex 2. An additional ~18 had been recently delivered, with ~8 installed and ~10 staged on the east side. Joe estimates roughly 28 Megapacks on the east side and approximately 262 more on the west side, for a Cortex 2 total of roughly 250–260. Two additional Megapack sites on the property total approximately 140 units, bringing the grand total to approximately 400 Megapacks across Giga Texas when fully installed.

## Market & Competition

1. **Joint SpaceX/Tesla advanced technology chip fab under development at Giga Texas:** South of Cortex 2 and south of the Optimus factory, a joint SpaceX-Tesla advanced technology chip fabrication facility is in early development. Soil testing completed approximately one month prior to recording. The facility will be used to develop and test AI chips (described as generation five — nearly complete — and generation six and beyond) for SpaceX data centers and future products, as a precursor to a larger volume production fab ("Terafab") planned for East Texas near a substation. Joe estimates that given the current construction state, the chip fab at Giga Texas will likely not be ready for meaningful production until 2028. Because of those long lead times, groundwork is beginning now in 2026. Brian and Joe note this facility will always be one generation ahead of the volume fab, as it is where advanced development occurs.

2. **Giga Texas as convergence point for Tesla/SpaceX ecosystem:** Joe observes that when he began covering the site nearly six years ago, it was a car factory; it has since become the convergence point for Tesla's headquarters, SpaceX Starbase proximity, Boring Company operations, Starlink terminal manufacturing, Optimus production, AI data centers, and now chip fabrication — a scale and scope that would have been nearly unimaginable at the outset.
