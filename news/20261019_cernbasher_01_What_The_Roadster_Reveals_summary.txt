This cernbasher episode, featuring a visit from fellow analyst Phil Beisel, dissects Tesla's new Roadster reveal and its downstream technology implications, tours Tesla/SpaceX/Boring Company/Neuralink construction sites around Austin and Bastrop, reports firsthand Cybercab ride impressions from the Austin X Takeover event, and covers newly identified SpaceX data center construction near Brownsville, Texas — tying it all together with speculation about an eventual Tesla-SpaceX merger.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster reveal showcases cold-gas thruster/fan suction system:** Tesla's new Roadster demo featured the car hovering upside down and flipping using cold gas thrusters and active fan suction, technology the hosts say is AI-controlled at a very high inference rate (speculated 60-120 Hz) since no human could actuate thrusters that fast.
2. **Drive unit efficiency breakthrough:** The Roadster's new two-motor drive unit delivers 33% more power at 50% less weight than the Plaid's three-motor unit (correcting an earlier Motor Trend report that said 50% more power/30% less weight), a ~2.6x power-to-weight improvement the hosts believe will migrate into Cybertruck, Model Y, and Cybercab powertrains for efficiency gains.
3. **Roadster specs and weight-saving design:** The new Roadster is stated at roughly 1,700 kg (vs. a heavier Cybertruck), generates about a megawatt of power (1,341 horsepower, ~4x the original Roadster), and uses a carbon-fiber-wrapped pressure vessel (COPV) for compressed air that weighs about 500 lb and dissipates during a run, with Tesla engineers citing weight reduction (F=ma) as central to the performance design.
4. **Optimus actuators used in Roadster hardware:** Tesla used Optimus leg actuators for the Roadster's rear wing/spoiler and throttle valve system because no automotive actuator was strong enough, illustrating cross-pollination between the Optimus and vehicle engineering teams.
5. **Simulated track performance claims:** Elon Musk said the Roadster (even without rocket thrusters) would beat the best Formula 1 track times using suction and active control surfaces (banned in F1); a fan-made simulation on the COTA track showed a Roadster with 2G of fan suction beating Max Verstappen's qualifying pole time by about 1.8 seconds, while 1G of suction (just enough to hold it upside down) was about 9.5 seconds slower — implying roughly 11 seconds of lap time per G of suction force.
6. **Porsche's poorly timed Mission E rebrand:** Porsche posted a "Mission E" campaign emphasizing driver-focused, analog design hours before Tesla's Roadster demo; hosts mocked the timing and noted Porsche's car has only one rear fan versus the Roadster's four.
7. **Austin area buildout, Terafab and Optimus factory:** Touring Giga Texas, the hosts observed major construction including an Optimus factory (~7 million sq ft) and the Terafab research center (~2–2.5 million sq ft) under construction on the Gigafactory property, plus a Samsung fab in Taylor, Texas (~3 million sq ft, with room to expand) whose entire output is reportedly captured by a Tesla contract.

## Robotaxi
1. **Firsthand Cybercab rides at Austin X Takeover:** Phil Beisel took roughly five to six Cybercab rides during the Austin event and described the experience as polished and "done," with purple/teal ambient lighting that let riders instantly identify their assigned car, functional exit-camera indicators, and tipping options including a no-tip, $0.69, or $4.20 selection.
2. **Fleet growth in Texas:** Hosts observed a parking lot with roughly 200+ Model Ys and Cybercabs (some newer units with no front plates) and said about 150-158 additional vehicles were deployed into service that same night; per Tesla's state registration data cited, Texas has 769 registered vehicles total — 420 Model Ys and 349 Cybercabs (hosts estimate "slightly over 300" Cybercabs currently operating, cautioning not all may be in Austin specifically).
3. **Week-over-week growth estimate:** Based on their own math, the host estimated the fleet is achieving around 17% week-over-week growth normally, possibly as high as 50% during the X Takeover week due to high usage, though this is the host's own estimate rather than a confirmed figure.
4. **Pricing and consumer preference dynamics:** The host predicts Cybercab will command higher fares than Waymo in the near term due to demand outstripping a still-small fleet, with prices expected to fall once Tesla scales up; both hosts say they would clearly prefer Cybercab over Waymo for comfort, aesthetics, and (unproven but expected) safety.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus actuator reuse validates design under extreme stress:** The Roadster's rear wing and throttle-valve mechanisms use Optimus leg actuators, which the hosts say gives the Optimus team real-world stress-test feedback on actuator durability under repeated extreme loads.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **New data center site identified near Brownsville:** SpaceX has built on a 444-acre tract outside Brownsville, Texas (de-annexed from the city, now part of Starbase), which the host identifies — based on aerial photography from RGV Aerial and All Starbase — as a new "Colossus"-style compute complex (potential naming not confirmed), SpaceX's third such site after two in Tennessee/Mississippi. Buildings lack doors/windows/bays typical of warehouses and the site has roughly 400–500 MW of on-site power (via Mitsubishi F8 mobile power packs, GE Vernova TM2500 turbines, and APR Energy equipment — APR being a ~$1 billion SpaceX acquisition from Jacksonville, Florida made in a prior period), supporting the data-center conclusion.
2. **Revenue potential of the site:** The host estimates each "mini hard" data-center unit filled with Nvidia GB300 chips could generate about $22 billion/year in revenue at a build cost of $12–15 billion, and estimates roughly six such units could fit on the Brownsville site (Grok's estimate was six to ten) for a total of about $132 billion in potential revenue; using newer Vera Rubin NVL72 systems, SemiAnalysis estimates a 39-40% revenue uplift per unit, implying around $183 billion if six units were built — which the host says could exceed Tesla's total company revenue from a single site.
3. **Elon Musk's compute buildout targets:** Musk has stated a stretch goal of reaching 10 gigawatts of compute by the end of 2027 and responded "maybe more than 10" when asked; the host frames this alongside Musk's stated longer-term Starlink-related compute ambitions of around 100 gigawatts (initial build) and an eventual 1,000 gigawatts, though these larger figures are presented as the host's interpretation of Musk's stated multiplier-of-ten thinking rather than a formal roadmap.
4. **Power, not chips, as the binding constraint:** The host argues the primary bottleneck for compute buildout is on-site power generation (all power at the Brownsville site is independently generated, not grid-sourced) and suggests this favors eventually moving compute to space, where power is less constrained, though the pace may then be limited by how fast satellites/Starships can be manufactured and launched.
5. **"Sentient Sun" and "Atom shaping" explained:** Referencing Musk's Tesla Takeover remarks, the host explains "Sentient Sun" as shorthand for building very large space-based compute ("Star Mind") to harness solar energy, and "Atom shaping" as Musk's term for the broader shift of energy/manufacturing effort toward building intelligence itself (including Optimus) rather than only physical goods — these are the host's interpretive gloss on Musk's terminology, not direct quotes of a defined roadmap.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation tied to earnings timing:** With SpaceX and Tesla earnings calls scheduled back-to-back the following week (SpaceX first, Tesla second), the host believes this is "one of the most telegraphed mergers... of all time" and speculates it will pressure Musk to finalize a merger since he reportedly dislikes doing two earnings calls; the host also states that after SpaceX's second earnings release, 83% of shares will be unlocked, which some see as aiding price discovery for SpaceX's valuation.
