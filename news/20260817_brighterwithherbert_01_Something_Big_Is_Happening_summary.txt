The video argues that while retail Tesla and SpaceX shareholders are growing impatient with delayed timelines (robotaxi scaling, Optimus), institutional "whale" investors are aggressively buying both stocks based on recent 13F filings and fresh analyst upgrades. Host Herbert interviews investor Larry Goldberg, who argues both companies are set for major long-term upside, framing Tesla and SpaceX as increasingly complementary "one entity" whose products will reinforce each other's success.

## Financials
1. **Major institutions increased Tesla stakes last quarter:** BlackRock added $2.5 billion in Tesla stock last quarter, bringing its total stake to $90 billion. Deutsche Bank increased its Tesla position by $622 million in Q2, adding 1.48 million shares to reach a $4 billion stake. Société Générale's US arm poured in roughly $4 billion (9.5 million shares), moving from under 1% to nearly 3% of its portfolio. State Street bought 2.76 million more shares, building a roughly $49 billion position and remaining Tesla's third-largest institutional investor.
2. **Deutsche Bank sees SpaceX approaching $100B run-rate:** Deutsche Bank believes SpaceX's push toward a $100 billion annual recurring revenue run rate by year end is achievable, with analysts estimating cloud/AI computing deals with Anthropic, Google, and others could generate roughly $45–50 billion alone, and Cursor cited as a further, not-yet-modeled growth driver.

## Market & Competition
1. **Retail vs. institutional divergence on Tesla:** The host notes institutional/analyst ownership of Tesla now exceeds 55% while retail ownership has fallen to around 22%, reflecting retail fatigue over robotaxi delays even as institutions continue buying.
2. **Goldberg's framework for AI value chain timing:** Goldberg argues Tesla is tackling a harder, more valuable "real-world AI" problem than pure-play AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) or even SpaceX's AI efforts, which he views as more comparable to those labs; he believes Tesla's slower path could ultimately be more highly leveraged and rewarding, though it requires more patience than the market has shown.
3. **Goldberg's Tesla/SpaceX convergence thesis:** Goldberg said he does not trade one company against the other over his two-to-three-year investment horizon, framing Tesla and SpaceX as heading toward functioning "as one entity" through product-level integration (citing Terafab as an example), and drew a parallel to Nvidia becoming similarly interconnected with both.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
1. **Morgan Stanley flags robotaxi scaling metrics:** Analyst Andrew Percoco (new Tesla coverage at Morgan Stanley) said investors want clear evidence robotaxi is scaling, citing vehicle count, city density, growing weekly unsupervised miles, and unit economics as key metrics; he called FSD adoption reportedly hitting 55% a bright spot, above expectations, and framed robotaxi as the first real test of Tesla's physical AI strategy.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Argus upgrades SpaceX to buy with $160 price target:** Argus upgraded SpaceX to buy, setting a $160 price target and arguing that its heavy AI infrastructure spending could pay back in under a year.
2. **Morgan Stanley reiterates SpaceX targets, cites Cursor upside:** Adam Jonas of Morgan Stanley kept a $300 base price target with a $600 bull case, saying Cursor's growth (leveraging Grok as an intelligence layer, X for real-time data, SpaceX AI for compute, Starlink for connectivity, and Tesla for physical AI) could be a major factor bridging to the bull case; Morgan Stanley estimates Cursor's annual recurring revenue alone could reach $8 billion by year end.
3. **New disclosed SpaceX stakes from sovereign wealth fund and board member:** Norway's sovereign wealth fund disclosed a $1.2 billion stake (about 0.5% ownership); SpaceX board member Antonio Gracias (Valor Equity Partners, an early backer of both SpaceX and Tesla) disclosed a 6.5% stake.
4. **Semi Analysis reportedly validates Musk's compute target:** The host cites a Semi Analysis research report saying Musk's target of reaching 10 gigawatts of compute by next year is achievable and "real," identifying Microsoft as a major buyer; the host calculates that at 10 gigawatts, this could imply a roughly $500 billion annual revenue run rate (versus Musk's earlier ~$100 billion figure), though this extrapolation is the host's own calculation, not one attributed to Semi Analysis directly.
5. **Goldberg previews his own SpaceX valuation:** Goldberg said he is about to publish a valuation of SpaceX using conservative assumptions and high skepticism toward Musk's stated timelines (while respecting Musk's judgment), concluding that SpaceX has surprised to the upside versus current market pricing and that both SpaceX and Tesla share prices could move sharply higher over the next one to two years; he said he expects to publish it within the next week or so (tentative).
