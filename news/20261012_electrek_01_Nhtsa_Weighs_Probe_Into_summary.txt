NHTSA has opened a petition file (DP26010) to decide whether to formally investigate claims that Tesla Robotaxis speed through active school zones, marking the third federal action against Tesla's driverless fleet in about five weeks.

## Robotaxi
1. **Petition details and timeline:** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened file DP26010 on October 9, after receiving the petition on September 23. It targets model year 2026 Robotaxis (Model Y and Cybercab) operating with Tesla's Automated Driving System engaged. The evidence cited is limited: three linked social media videos and four incidents in NHTSA's own records, with no crashes, injuries, or deaths reported. Under federal rules (49 CFR Part 552), NHTSA has 120 days from receipt to grant or deny the petition, putting the deadline around January 21, 2027; a grant would trigger a formal defect investigation that could lead to a recall.

2. **New fleet size estimate from NHTSA:** NHTSA's filing estimates the affected fleet at about 1,000 vehicles — described by the article as the first federal number given for Tesla's Robotaxi fleet size, since Tesla does not disclose this figure and community trackers had counted only a few dozen active cars as of June. The article speculates NHTSA's figure may reflect vehicles Tesla has registered through Texas DMV certification rather than vehicles actually active in commercial service, and notes Tesla added Cybercab to commercial service in Austin on September 3.

3. **Pattern of prior speed-related scrutiny:** Videos from the Austin Robotaxi launch in June 2025 showed cars exceeding speed limits, prompting NHTSA contact with Tesla at the time. In October 2025, NHTSA opened a separate investigation into Full Self-Driving over alleged traffic-law violations (running red lights, wrong-lane driving); a week later Tesla released a "Mad Max" FSD profile that ignores speed limits. The article distinguishes this new petition by noting that in a driverless Robotaxi, there is no human driver responsible for speed choices — the ADS itself, and thus Tesla, makes that call.

4. **Concurrent Cybercab certification dispute:** The petition comes alongside an existing NHTSA Special Order requiring Tesla to prove under oath that the Cybercab (which lacks pedals or a steering wheel) meets federal safety standards, with Tesla's sworn answers due September 30; NHTSA has not made those answers public.

5. **Comparison to Waymo's school-zone incident:** The article notes Waymo faced a similar issue when Austin ISD reported Waymo vehicles illegally passing stopped school buses roughly 20 times late last year, which Waymo addressed via a voluntary software recall filed with NHTSA in December 2025.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the petition as part of an escalating pattern of federal scrutiny on Tesla's driverless fleet and poses an open question of whether Tesla will follow Waymo's path of a voluntary recall or wait for NHTSA to act.
