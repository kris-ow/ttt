## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Starmind constellation concept:** SpaceX is developing Starmind, a planned constellation separate from Starlink, designed to put AI supercomputers in low Earth orbit (sun-synchronous polar orbit) to capture space solar energy for low-cost AI compute delivered to Earth. Elon Musk confirmed the Starmind name in June 2026. Nothing has launched yet; SpaceX's FCC application for up to 1 million orbital data center satellites was accepted for filing in February 2026 and remains pending.
2. **AI1 satellite specs:** SpaceX's Starmind web page describes the AI1 satellite as 98 ft tall and 246 ft across when unfolded. A June 2026 pre-IPO briefing (per Reuters) showed a draft spec of 150 kW peak / 120 kW sustained power; by the November 2026 update, SpaceX raised the spec to 250 kW peak / 175 kW average, generating 75 kW of compute per ton. SpaceX's June 2026 IPO prospectus said early AI compute satellites targeting 100 kW per ton would need roughly five times the solar array output of V3 satellite designs.
3. **Scale targets and Starship dependency:** SpaceX's June 2026 IPO prospectus states a goal of launching 100 gigawatts of AI compute capacity annually on solar-powered satellites — equivalent to roughly one-fifth of total 2025 US annual power production — while noting even 10 GW/year would be an attractive business. Elon Musk posted in January 2026 that his 50th-percentile estimate is reaching 100 GW/year launched from Earth within 3-4 years. At 250 kW per satellite, that implies building roughly 400,000 AI satellites annually, requiring on the order of 10,000 Starship flights per year at 100 tons per flight (reusable Starship capacity figure per SpaceX).

## Energy
5. **Singulus supply contract:** SpaceX signed a major supply contract with Singulus Technologies (Kahl am Main, Germany; ~312 employees; ~$48.3 million total 2025 revenue) for solar cell manufacturing equipment to support over 100 gigawatts of production capacity in Bastrop, Texas. The first turnkey heterojunction solar cell production line has a disclosed order volume of over €60 million, to be delivered in the first half of 2027, with additional lines planned under a strategic partnership. Singulus shares jumped roughly 31% in after-hours German trading following disclosure, from a prior market value of roughly €113 million.
6. **Why heterojunction silicon over gallium arsenide:** Traditional high-radiation-orbit satellites use multi-junction gallium arsenide cells (35%+ efficiency) that cost tens to hundreds of dollars per watt — too expensive at the scale SpaceX needs. Heterojunction silicon cells, per Singulus and Swift Solar data (November 2026), achieve roughly 25% efficiency (vs. ~23% for older PERC cells), cost pennies to dimes per watt, require lower-temperature processing (~400°F vs. 1,300-1,500°F for PERC/TOPCon), and deliver roughly 10% more power at mission start while degrading less in direct sunlight.
7. **SpaceX financed Singulus's exit from Chinese state-backed debt:** Per Singulus's 2026 financial filings, its largest shareholder and customer was Triumph Science and Technology Group (a China National Building Material subsidiary, ~16.75% stake), to whom Singulus owed a €12 million bond, a €9 million Chinese bank loan, and project advances. An unnamed partner (confirmed by the November 10, 2026 press release to be SpaceX) provided a €29 million five-year loan plus a €3.558 million convertible bond in March 2026 to retire that debt, then provided an additional €22.75 million subordinated secured loan in October 2026 to clear all remaining obligations to Triumph. On the same day in October 2026, Triumph sold its entire stake to a German investor and its board representative resigned.
8. **China's solar dominance and US supply gap:** Per Elon Musk's January 2026 World Economic Forum remarks, China's annual solar manufacturing capacity is around 1,500 GW, having installed a record 315 GW in 2025. A February 2026 World Energy News report citing the Solar Energy Industries Association put total US module assembly capacity at about 65 GW but domestic solar cell manufacturing capacity at only about 3.2 GW; the largest US vertically integrated cell plant (Qcells, Cartersville, Georgia, $2.5 billion, cell production began June 2026) has 3.3 GW/year nameplate capacity — roughly 1/30th of SpaceX's Bastrop target.
9. **Bastrop, Texas buildout:** SpaceX's existing Starlink dish factory in Bastrop has expanded past 1.2 million square feet, with an $855 million expansion launched in May 2026 targeting 10 million Starlink kits/year. Building permits reported in May 2026 show a 1.1 million square foot solar cell factory under construction nearby, part of a reported ~11 million square foot "Gigasat" campus across roughly 1,000 acres. SpaceX's Starmind page targets production starting as soon as late 2027, while its June 2026 IPO prospectus gives a more conservative window of as early as 2028. SpaceX SVP of Starlink Mike Nichols confirmed the Singulus partnership supports solar cell manufacturing for Starmind satellites at the Texas facility.
10. **Tesla and SpaceX combined solar push:** At the January 2026 World Economic Forum, Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla are each separately working toward 100 GW/year of US solar manufacturing (200 GW combined), estimating it would take about three years. Tesla separately filed plans in August 2026 for a $10.1 billion solar cell plant in Fort Bend County, Texas, targeting commercial operations in early 2029.
