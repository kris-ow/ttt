## Autonomous Driving
- Two Chinese automotive influencers who tested FSD 14 in Los Angeles reportedly concluded it now outperforms domestic systems (Huawei ADS, XPeng VLA, Li Auto ADAS) and is the benchmark to catch, per cernbasher's segment.
- A guest on that show argued Tesla's autonomy lead over the broader industry is widening, not closing, putting XPeng, Huawei, and Nvidia's Alpamayo-linked effort a couple of years behind even where "keeping up."
- Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov said at Y Combinator's Startup School that camera-only sensing (Tesla's approach, unnamed) can match human driving but hits a safety curve that "flattens out way too early" for full driverless reliability; Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this undercuts Tesla's scaling story.
- Germany is bringing in a dedicated Tesla vehicle for independent FSD road testing ahead of an EU-wide approval vote expected around October, which could slip into 2027 per industry trackers cited by cernbasher.
- Tesla's global fleet crossed 13 billion cumulative FSD (Supervised) miles, up from 12 billion on July 17 — about 1 billion miles in two weeks, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Redline Reviews (1.1M subscribers) tested FSD for the first time, calling it "the future" and rating it above GM's Super Cruise, a notable mainstream third-party validation moment.

## Robotaxi
- Electrek reports Tesla has confirmed 380,000 driverless robotaxi miles over the past year since the Austin launch, versus Waymo's 220 million rider-only miles and roughly half a million weekly paid trips across 15 cities; Waymo's safety report claims 94% fewer serious-injury crashes than human drivers.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert characterizes Tesla's robotaxi service as still an effectively geo-fenced demo given the mileage gap, and cites an unsourced claim that Waymo's fully-equipped vehicle cost is nearing $60,000.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first quarterly report as a public company: revenue $7.88B (+92% YoY), net loss $541M (improved $467M YoY), adjusted EBITDA $3.5B (+191% YoY), beating the roughly $7B Wall Street estimate.
- Balance sheet: $100B in cash/equivalents/securities and $47.5B backlog; the quarter included the IPO (~$85.7B net proceeds) and a $25B investment-grade bond offering (5.855% weighted average rate) that partly repaid a $20B bridge loan.
- Shares swung from up ~1% after hours to down roughly 7% by call's end — a reaction multiple hosts called irrational given the results.
- Management guided to $100B+ annualized revenue run rate by December, driven mainly by AI cloud services; Musk said internal projections for $1 trillion in annual revenue moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" in 2029.
- SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's Rubin architecture going forward and expects "a very significant percentage" of Nvidia's GPUs next year; the pending ~$60B Cursor acquisition is reportedly through "almost all" regulatory hurdles.
- Ahead of earnings, SpaceX had fallen roughly 37% from its IPO peak — the largest US monthly market-cap loss since 1970 per Axios — which multiple hosts attributed to an overheated post-IPO runup and a small tradeable float rather than fundamentals.
- Share unlocks begin around August 6 (roughly 20% of locked shares eligible regardless of price; a further tranche unlocks early only above $175), with hosts split on how much selling pressure this will create.
- No Tesla–SpaceX merger was announced or substantively discussed on the call, despite a cited prediction-market probability (~40%) that "merger" would be mentioned.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- AI segment revenue hit $2.6B (+247% YoY, +213% sequentially), turning adjusted EBITDA positive at $1.1B, driven by new Colossus/Colossus 2 cloud services deals contributing $1.6B incremental revenue.
- Nameplate compute reached 1.4 GW at Q2-end (up from 1 GW in Q1, 400 MW a year earlier), with guidance to exceed 2 GW by year-end 2026 and a tentative 20 GW power/cooling target by end of 2027 (management viewing ~15 GW as more realistic, cumulative compute "closer to 10 than 5" GW).
- One preview (jobhakdi) had cited a surprise ~$26B/year compute revenue run rate via Anthropic and Google on roughly 1 GW live capacity, warning that ~500 MW of that runs on unpermitted temporary turbines requiring replacement by mid-2027, and modeling a more realistic 2.5–4 GW buildout by 2027 versus circulating claims of 8 GW, which he called "complete bogus."
- New Star Mind AI satellite, built on Nvidia's NVL72/Rubin architecture, will launch starting next year and be deployed terrestrially as well; Musk called it a simplified, cheaper alternative to standard racks.
- Grok roadmap: version 4.6 expected within about a week, 4.7 in three-to-four weeks, and Grok 5 by year-end incorporating SpaceX's full data corpus; only ~10% of compute is expected to go to Grok training long-term, with the rest for third-party inference (Anthropic, Google).
- CFO Brett said new AI compute capital deployments are seeing under one-year payback, with $6.7B of additional cloud services revenue already contracted in early Q3.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink added 1.7 million net subscribers in Q2 (its best quarter, up from 1.4 million in Q1), holding ARPU flat at $66/month, with ~10,200 satellites in orbit and service in 167 markets.
- Connectivity segment revenue rose to $4.3B (+66% YoY), with enterprise/government revenue up 108% YoY; aviation penetration remains under 10% despite new deals with American Airlines, Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus.
- FCC-approved EchoStar spectrum transfer expands Starlink Mobile bandwidth from ~5 MHz to 65 MHz, which management said could make the service roughly 100x more capable; V2 mobile satellites launch next year with commercial service targeted by end of 2027, and Musk floated eventually competing for share of the ~$650B US mobile carrier market.
- Musk said V3 satellites are roughly an order of magnitude more capable than V2, with ~10x as many planned, implying up to a 100x bandwidth increase; SpaceX separately said Starlink Mobile aims for continuous global direct-to-device coverage by end of 2028.
- Hughesnet parent EchoStar filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy after losing 59,000 broadband subscribers in Q2 (down to 622,000), a concrete data point on Starlink's share gains, per Sawyer Merritt.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk said the heat shield problem is likely solved based on inspection of the recovered flight-13 ship, though the team wants to confirm once the next returned stage lands in Australia.
- Flight 14 will be the first to deploy operational V3 Starlink satellites; a tower catch of the ship is tentatively targeted for the next flight (end of this month), with both booster and ship catches expected within this year or next.
- Space segment revenue grew to $962M (+29% YoY) with segment adjusted EBITDA a loss of $205M on elevated R&D; total capex was ~$18.4B, of which ~$15.8B went to AI compute infrastructure rather than Starship.
- Management targets at least one Starship flight per day within about a year; Artemis 3 (Orion docking) is planned for next year, with a "boots on the moon" goal in 2028.

## Electric Vehicles
- Giga Berlin battery cell capacity plans raised from 8 GWh to 18 GWh/year, pushing total cell manufacturing investment to nearly $1.4 billion, per translated management report cited by cernbasher.
- Giga Berlin vehicle production capacity rises to 7,500 units/week starting October 2026 (up 20% twice), adding 3,500 jobs and supplying more than 30 markets.
- Tesla registrations up 86% YoY in France (second-best July ever) and 52% YoY in Denmark, suggesting Tesla is prioritizing incentive-rich European markets, per Sawyer Merritt.
