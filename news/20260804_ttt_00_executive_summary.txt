## Autonomous Driving
- Two Chinese automotive influencers who tested FSD 14 in LA reportedly reversed their prior view that Huawei/Xiaopeng/Li Auto led the world, concluding FSD is now the benchmark and urging domestic firms to close the gap, per cernbasher.
- A guest on the same show argued Tesla's autonomy lead over the broader industry is widening, not narrowing, with XPeng, Huawei, and Nvidia's Alpamayo project still "a couple of years behind."
- Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov said at Y Combinator's Startup School that camera-only sensing (without naming Tesla) can match human driving but hits a safety "ceiling" before reaching the superhuman reliability full autonomy requires; he said Waymo's fused camera/lidar/radar approach is "vastly superior" to any single sensor. Electrek's Fred Lambert asserted Tesla's own robotaxi data shows a crash rate about three times worse than human drivers even with an in-car safety monitor, and argued Musk's safety claims haven't been borne out by data.
- Tesla's FSD (Supervised) fleet crossed 13 billion cumulative miles, up from 12 billion on July 17 — roughly 1 billion miles in two weeks, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Redline Reviews (1.1M subscribers) tested FSD for the first time, calling it "the future" and comparing it favorably to GM's Super Cruise, which the reviewer had previously rated best-in-class.
- Germany's motor transport authority is reviewing FSD safety documents forwarded by Dutch regulators and will receive a dedicated Tesla test vehicle in July 2026 for independent road evaluation; an EU-wide approval vote is expected around October but could slip into 2027 amid member-state feature disputes, per cernbasher's sourcing.

## Robotaxi
- Electrek reports Tesla has confirmed 380,000 driverless robotaxi miles over the past year since Austin launch, versus Waymo's 220 million rider-only miles and ~500,000 weekly paid trips across 15 cities; Waymo's safety report claims 94% fewer serious-injury crashes than human drivers over that mileage. Lambert argues this makes Tesla's robotaxi service effectively a geo-fenced demo given the mileage gap.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first public earnings report: Q2 revenue $7.88B (+92% YoY), beating the ~$7B Street estimate; net loss narrowed to $541M (improved $467M YoY); adjusted EBITDA $3.5B (+191% YoY). Cash and marketable securities stood at $100B with $47.5B in backlog.
- The quarter included the IPO (~$85.7B net proceeds) and a $25B investment-grade bond offering (5.855% weighted average rate) used partly to repay a $20B bridge loan.
- Despite the beat, SPCX shares initially popped ~1% after-hours before reversing to close down roughly 7% — a reaction hosts on futureaza called disconnected from the results.
- Management guided to a $100B annualized revenue run rate by December, driven largely by AI cloud services; Musk said internal projections for $1 trillion in annual revenue have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" in 2029.
- Share unlock mechanics: roughly 911 million shares (~20% of locked shares) become eligible for sale on the second trading day after earnings regardless of price; a further ~455 million shares unlock early only if the stock trades at/above $175. Separately, futureaza's pre-earnings hosts noted SpaceX's decline from its IPO peak was the largest US month-over-month market-cap loss since 1970 per Axios, attributing it to an overheated post-IPO run (IPO price ~$150 to ~$220–225 in a day) and a thin tradeable float (~4–5%) rather than any change in fundamentals.
- The ~$60B Cursor acquisition is through "almost all" regulatory hurdles and should close soon, per Musk; combined product roadmap details are being withheld until closing.
- Kalshi betting markets showed roughly 40% odds a Tesla–SpaceX merger would be "mentioned" on the call; no merger was announced or substantively addressed.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's AI segment (Colossus) posted $2.6B in revenue, up 213% sequentially and 247% YoY, with segment adjusted EBITDA turning positive at $1.1B; new cloud-service deals at Colossus and Colossus 2 contributed $1.6B of incremental quarterly revenue.
- Compute online rose to 1.4 GW at end of Q2 (from 1 GW in Q1, 400 MW a year ago); management targets exceeding 2 GW by year-end and 5–10 GW by end of 2027, with a tentative 20 GW power/cooling target Musk called unlikely to fully execute (~15 GW seen as realistic).
- SpaceX and Nvidia will jointly build the "Star Mind" AI satellite compute payload on an optimized NVL72 Rubin architecture, with the same design also deployed terrestrially; launches begin next year. Musk said SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's Rubin architecture and expects "a very significant percentage" of Nvidia's GPU supply next year.
- Musk said only about 10% of compute will ultimately be needed for Grok training, with the bulk serving CSA deals with Anthropic and Google at what management called increasingly favorable, sub-one-year-payback economics.
- Independent pre-earnings analysis (jobhakdi) had flagged Colossus's ~$26B/year run-rate (on ~1 GW live capacity) as the key valuation driver, warning that ~500 MW of current capacity runs on unpermitted temporary turbines that must be replaced by mid-2027 under a legal settlement, and calling circulating claims of 8 GW by 2027 "complete bogus" versus his own 2.5–3 GW base case.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink added a net 1.7 million subscribers in Q2 (best quarter yet, up from 1.4 million in Q1), holding ARPU flat at $66/month; the constellation reached ~10,200 satellites across 167 markets. Connectivity segment revenue rose 32% sequentially and 66% YoY to $4.3B.
- Enterprise/government revenue grew over 108% YoY; SpaceX says it has never lost an enterprise customer, with aviation penetration still under 10%. New deals include American Airlines, Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus.
- FCC-approved EchoStar spectrum transfer expands Starlink Mobile bandwidth from ~5 MHz to 65 MHz, which Shotwell said targets the $650B combined annual revenue of Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile; Mobile V2 satellites launch next year with commercial service targeted for end of 2027, full global direct-to-device coverage by end of 2028.
- Rival EchoStar (Hughesnet) filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy after Q2 broadband subscribers fell to 622,000, a loss of 59,000 in the quarter, as customers moved to Starlink.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk said the heat shield problem is largely solved based on data and inspection of the recovered flight-13 ship, calling it the single biggest remaining obstacle to full reusability, though the team still wants to examine a returned stage once it lands in Australia to confirm.
- Flight 14 will be the first to deploy operational V3 Starlink satellites; SpaceX hopes to catch both booster and ship this year, possibly as early as flight 14, with a tower catch attempt tentatively targeted for end of this month pending regulatory approval.
- Space segment revenue grew 55% sequentially and 29% YoY to $962M, with segment adjusted EBITDA a loss of $205M reflecting elevated Starship R&D spend; total Q2 capex was ~$18.4B, of which $15.8B went to AI compute infrastructure.

## Electric Vehicles
- Giga Berlin raised planned battery cell capacity to 18 GWh/year (from 8 GWh), bringing total cell manufacturing investment to nearly $1.4 billion; vehicle production capacity is set to hit 7,500 units/week starting October 2026, adding 3,500 jobs, per translated management-report details cited by cernbasher.
- Tesla France registrations rose 86% YoY in July (second-best July ever); Denmark rose 52% YoY, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Market & Competition
- Musk described AI as a "supersonic tsunami" and said "we're already in the singularity," reposting a 12-year-old warning that AI could be more dangerous than nukes; commentators on brighterwithherbert debated whether frontier models retain a meaningful edge over open-source alternatives.
- Grok roadmap: version 4.6 expected within about a week, 4.7 in three to four weeks, and Grok 5 by year-end incorporating SpaceX's full historical data corpus, per multiple sources.
