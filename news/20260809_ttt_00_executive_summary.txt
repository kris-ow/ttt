## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Merger timeline debated: Yashu Chararma argues no serious shareholder vote until roughly mid-2027, citing post-IPO price discovery, lockup expirations, and unresolved Tesla-China/SpaceX-defense questions; he calls merger overhang the single biggest factor suppressing TSLA short-term.
- Both host and guest agree a straight 1-for-1 exchange ratio would likely fail a Tesla shareholder vote; Yashu estimates a plausible range of roughly one to just over three SpaceX shares per Tesla share based on typical M&A premiums, stressing this is speculative.
- A rebuttal case relayed from analyst Alexandra Merz suggests Musk and Gwynne Shotwell comments hint at openness to an earlier merger announcement, and that lockups don't require full completion first — Yashu disputes the political-timing argument specifically.
- xAI and X were folded into SpaceX pre-IPO; the AI compute segment, previously loss-making, is now framed as fast-growing and EBITDA-positive.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Cloud/compute deals worth $14.1B signed in Q2 (Colossus/Colossus 2, customers including Google and Anthropic), with $6.7B more signed in early July; AI segment swung to ~$1.1B EBITDA-positive from a $600M loss.
- Compute capacity: 1.4 gigawatts by end of June, targeting over 2GW by year-end and ~10GW by end of next year per Musk, with infrastructure for 20GW already lined up.
- SpaceX said it has gone exclusive with Nvidia for a very significant share of next year's GPU output; new compute capital is guided to pay back in under a year, a figure Brian Wang notes depends on today's pricing holding.
- 2027 revenue estimates diverge sharply: Goldman Sachs models ~$34.5B for AI compute alone; Brian Wang's base case is $163B total (bull case $256B); Morgan Stanley doesn't see $160B until 2028. Musk called the $100B run-rate floor already contracted, "not a question mark."
- Grok 4.5 tripled token consumption in July; the $60B Cursor acquisition is expected to close within weeks, per a report cited from The Information.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- 12 million subscribers at end of June, up 1.7 million in the quarter (a record), pricing steady at $66/month, generating $1.7B operating income (39% margin).
- New V3 satellite is ~10x more capable than V2 per Musk, with ~10x as many launching, implying roughly 100x total constellation bandwidth per a third-party analysis; first V3 expected on the next Starship flight, tentatively end of this month.
- Enterprise/government revenue up 108% YoY; Shotwell says the company has never lost an enterprise customer. Aviation wins this quarter include American, Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus.
- FCC approved transfer of EchoStar spectrum to SpaceX (65 MHz, 13x current spectrum); next-gen mobile satellites (~10x current count) begin flying next year, targeting the ~$600B U.S. carrier market via rooftop-mounted small cells rather than towers.
- United Airlines reportedly told sources that business travelers specifically book Starlink-equipped flights, with Wi-Fi satisfaction scores more than double the rest of the fleet — a secondhand account, not an official United statement.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 13 in July deployed 20 production V3 Starlink satellites, re-lit a Raptor engine in space, and achieved the softest splashdown yet with the ship intact; Musk says he considers the heat shield problem solved with no remaining technical obstacles to full reusability, while hedging against jinxing it.
- Next flight tentatively targeted for end of this month, aiming for a tower catch pending regulatory approval. Additional roadmap items — flight-a-day cadence within a year, lunar factories, boots on the moon around 2028 — are flagged as high-uncertainty and not required for the 2027 revenue floor.

## Robotaxi
