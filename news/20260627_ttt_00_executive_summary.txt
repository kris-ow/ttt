## Robotaxi

- **Demand throttling is deliberate, not capacity-limited by regulation.** Current pricing is $3 drop fee plus $1.40–$1.50/mile — up from early promotional rates of $0.42 then $0.69 per mile. Per FutureAzA guest John, Tesla is intentionally keeping prices elevated to hold wait times under ~10 minutes given fleet constraints; he believes Tesla could profitably price as low as $0.15/mile but is avoiding demand it cannot yet service. Prices are expected to fall as fleet size scales.

- **Texas regulation is no longer the bottleneck.** Texas recently changed law to allow Tesla to self-declare as a Level 4 autonomous operator, which would theoretically permit immediate deployment across Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and other Texas cities. Yet large-scale deployment has not followed — parking lots are reportedly filling with undeployed Cybercabs — confirming the constraint is operational readiness, not regulatory approval.

- **Cybercab deployment strategy: Model Y leads new cities, Cybercabs inherit proven markets.** Because seating a human monitor in the two-seat Cybercab alongside a passenger is impractical, Model Y vehicles are better suited for the monitored rollout phase in new cities. The predicted pattern: once a Texas market goes unsupervised, the Model Y fleet migrates to a new city while Cybercabs take over the proven Texas routes.

- **~30 U.S. cities with monitored service within one year — 80–90% probability per John.** Tesla is reportedly hiring monitors in approximately 30 cities, including Atlanta. Fully unsupervised operation outside Texas and possibly the Bay Area is assigned much lower odds. NYC is flagged as a separate, complex regulatory case.

- **Waymo leads on driverless rides today — by a wide margin.** Per brighterwithherbert, Waymo is running approximately 500,000 paid driverless rides per week across roughly 10 metros. The host is direct: "Waymo is winning by a lot" on this metric right now. Tesla's structural cost advantage — a Cybercab engineered for 500,000-mile fleet life rolling off the same line as a consumer car for $30,000–$40,000 — is the long-term thesis, not the current scoreboard.

- **Waymo faces an innovator's dilemma on neural nets.** FutureAzA's John argues Waymo must now build a competing neural-net stack to stay long-term competitive while protecting an existing revenue-generating heuristic product — a difficult internal resource allocation problem, particularly given Google's compute commitments elsewhere.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Tesla's FSD data flywheel: 10B+ miles collected, ~30M fresh miles/day.** Brighterwithherbert cites 7M+ Tesla cars on road continuously feeding data, with over 10 billion FSD miles accumulated as of 2026. No other automaker has a comparable closed-loop system where fleet data immediately improves the next software version pushed OTA fleet-wide.

- **FSD lawsuit from 2023 fatal Arizona crash settled; NHTSA investigation remains open.** Tesla settled litigation stemming from a 2023 crash in which a pedestrian was struck by a Tesla operating on FSD; sun glare was cited as a contributing factor. Financial terms were not disclosed. Per Electrified, Tesla's own SGO reporting confirmed FSD was active, the investigation was upgraded to an engineering analysis, and it remains open.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus missed 2025 production targets; ramp now a 2026 story.** Brighterwithherbert states explicitly that Tesla missed its 2025 Optimus unit numbers and that most Optimus robots currently work inside Tesla's own factories. China's Unitree shipped thousands of cheaper humanoids in 2025; Figure has a live factory pilot with BMW. The concrete milestone to watch, per the host: Optimus Gen 3 actually hitting a production line with units shipped — not stage appearances.

- **"Macrohard" demo expected by ~September 2026.** Brian Wang (Next Big Future, via brighterwithherbert) describes Macrohard as a joint Tesla/SpaceX AI software product — Tesla providing hardware, SpaceX/xAI providing models — targeting enterprise workflows via vision agents. A demo is reportedly expected within three months. Wang characterizes it as "the thing no one's talking about" and flags it as a potential near-term revenue catalyst if it proves competitive with Anthropic and OpenAI products.

- **Digital Optimus is a bet, not yet a moat.** Brighterwithherbert describes Digital Optimus as a vision agent that watches a screen and drives mouse and keyboard as a human would, running on the Tesla AI4 chip (~$650). The claimed advantage over API-dependent agents is that it can run any software without vendor permissions. The host's caveat: Anthropic shipped computer use in 2024, holds ~40% of enterprise API spend and over half the coding market, and Digital Optimus has no public benchmark.

## Energy

- **Tesla deployed ~47 GWh of storage in 2025, up ~49% YoY — but BYD is now #1 by volume.** Brighterwithherbert cites record margins on Tesla's 2025 energy storage deployment alongside the new Megablock (20 MWh unit bundling four Megapacks plus transformer and switchgear as a pre-built drop-in). However, the host explicitly states Tesla has been passed by BYD in volume.

- **Tesla–Sunrun virtual power plant: ~16 GW across ~4 million homes.** Brian Wang references a recently announced deal aggregating ~16 GW of distributed resources at ~$400 per participant. Direct revenue is described as a few hundred million dollars — not a needle-mover on its own. Wang's larger point: the deal gives Tesla/SpaceX negotiating leverage with utilities for prioritized power allocation and permitting for new AI data centers.

- **Megapod trademark filed: modular AI compute at Supercharger sites.** Tesla's ~80,000 Supercharger stations carry ~7 GW of already-permitted grid connections. The "Megapod" trademark describes a modular AI data center designed to plug directly into that existing infrastructure. Wang estimates ~$100M per year per megawatt of deployed capacity at Rubin-chip pricing. Brighterwithherbert holds this loosely — "a trademark and a plan" — but frames it as one moat handing a structural head start to another.

Simultaneously, Tesla highlighted stackable California programs: $120,000 off per semi via HVIP (up to 20 trucks) plus $120,000 off via Clean Fuel Rewards with no truck cap and no scrapping requirement — effective immediately.

Tesla stock closed at $379.71, up 1.22% on the day, outperforming a Nasdaq that fell 1.09%.

## Market & Competition

- **VW considering 100,000 job cuts and closure of four German factories.** Both Electrified and Sawyer Merritt flag this as potentially the largest restructuring in automotive history, driven by weak sales rather than costs alone. VW stock is down ~60% over the last three years. For Tesla, this signals accelerating erosion of legacy European competition.

- **SpaceX planning standalone Starlink mobile service, putting it in direct conflict with T-Mobile.** Per Financial Times and Reuters reporting cited by Electrified, SpaceX president Gwyn Shotwell disclosed the plan during the IPO roadshow. SpaceX holds AWS4 and H-Block spectrum purchased from Echostar (~$17B + $2.6B) and expects next-gen V2 mobile satellites to begin deploying on Starship in 2027. The move would put SpaceX in direct retail competition with its current T-Mobile direct-to-cell partner.

- **SpaceX acquires Mesh Optical Technologies (FTC approved).** The startup, founded by former SpaceX engineers, develops optical transceivers using light instead of copper for intra-data-center communication. No financial terms disclosed. Electrified notes ~60% of optical transceiver manufacturing is currently in China, making the acquisition strategically significant for AI infrastructure supply chain security.
