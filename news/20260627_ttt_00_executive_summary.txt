## Robotaxi
- **Demand throttling as deliberate strategy:** Current robotaxi pricing sits at $3 drop fee plus $1.40–$1.50/mile, up from early promotional rates of $0.42 and then $0.69 per mile. Per Dr. Knowitall (FutureAzA), Tesla is intentionally keeping prices elevated to cap wait times below ~10 minutes given fleet constraints — he estimates Tesla could profitably charge as little as $0.15/mile but is avoiding demand it cannot yet meet.
- **Regulatory is not the bottleneck:** Texas recently changed law to allow Tesla to self-declare as a Level 4 autonomous operator, theoretically enabling immediate deployment across all major Texas cities. Yet large-scale expansion has not occurred. Parking lots are reportedly filling with undeployed Cybercabs, confirming that manufacturing is outpacing operational readiness, not the reverse.
- **30-city monitored rollout probable within a year:** Dr. Knowitall assigns 80–90% odds that monitored robotaxi service reaches ~30 major U.S. cities by roughly mid-2027; Tesla is reportedly already hiring monitors in ~30 cities including Atlanta. He assigns much lower probability to fully driverless operation outside Texas and possibly the Bay Area within that window.
- **Cybercab deployment sequencing:** The two-seat Cybercab is poorly suited for the monitored phase (no practical seat for a monitor alongside a passenger). Predicted strategy: Model Y handles new-city monitored launches; once a Texas market goes unsupervised, Cybercabs take over and Model Ys shift to the next city.
- **Scale skepticism from Brian Wang:** Wang (Next Big Future, via brighterwithherbert) expresses doubt about a rapid ramp to one million robotaxis, calling his bull case "perhaps 100,000 if things really start popping off" — which he says has not yet occurred. He frames the key investor question as how many more months or years Tesla remains in the early-ramp phase before meaningful takeoff.
- **Waymo ahead today; Tesla built for scale:** Waymo is running ~500,000 paid driverless rides per week across ~10 metros (per brighterwithherbert). Both sources agree Waymo currently loses money on every ride while Tesla's camera-only, no-spinning-parts architecture — combined with a Cybercab engineered for 500,000-mile fleet life versus ~150,000 miles for a consumer car — is structurally cheaper to operate at scale.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD lawsuit settled, investigation open:** Tesla settled a lawsuit arising from a 2023 fatal crash in Arizona where a pedestrian was struck by a Tesla on FSD; sun glare was cited as a contributing factor. Financial terms were not disclosed. Tesla's SGO reporting confirmed FSD was active; the NHTSA engineering analysis remains open (per Electrified).
- **Tesla's data flywheel scale:** Per brighterwithherbert, Tesla has collected 10+ billion miles of FSD data as of 2026, with ~30 million fresh miles added per day across 7+ million cars on the road. No other automaker has a comparable active learning loop.
- **Neural net vs. heuristic feel:** Dr. Knowitall (FutureAzA), who rode both services back-to-back at launch, describes Tesla's FSD as feeling human because it is trained on human behavior, while Waymo felt "slightly robotic" in starts, stops, and turns due to its heuristic codebase. He argues Waymo faces a classic innovator's dilemma in transitioning to neural net while defending an existing revenue-generating product.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Macrohard demo expected ~September 2026:** Wang (via brighterwithherbert) flags "Macrohard" — a joint Tesla/SpaceX AI software product — as a potential near-term catalyst, with a demo reportedly expected within three months. He calls it "the thing no one's talking about" and argues that if it proves materially superior to Anthropic and OpenAI, it could generate Anthropic-scale revenues quickly given Cursor's existing Fortune 500 distribution.
- **Digital Optimus framed as a bet, not a moat:** Brighterwithherbert describes Digital Optimus as a vision-based computer-use agent running on the Tesla AI4 chip (~$650) with Grok as the reasoning brain. The claimed advantage is running any software a human can run without API integrations. The host explicitly flags this as unproven: Anthropic shipped computer use in 2024, holds ~40% of enterprise API spend and over half the coding market, and Digital Optimus has no public benchmark.
- **Optimus missed 2025 targets; China ahead on units:** Per brighterwithherbert, Tesla's Optimus production ramp slipped from a 2025 target into 2026, and China's Unitree shipped thousands of cheaper humanoids in 2025. Tesla's Optimus robots are currently working primarily inside Tesla's own factories. The concrete milestone to watch is Gen 3 units actually shipped externally, not stage appearances.

## Energy
- **Tesla–Sunrun virtual power plant deal:** Wang references a deal between Tesla and Sunrun aggregating ~16 GW of distributed resources across ~4 million homes at roughly $400 per participant. Wang characterizes the direct revenue impact as modest (a few hundred million dollars) but argues the strategic value lies in giving Tesla/SpaceX leverage with utilities for prioritized data center permitting and power allocation.
- **Megapod trademark filed for Supercharger sites:** Tesla filed a trademark for "Megapod," described as a modular AI data center designed to plug into existing Supercharger power infrastructure. Tesla's ~80,000 Supercharger stations already carry ~7 GW of permitted grid connections. Per brighterwithherbert, Wang estimates ~$100 million per year per megawatt of deployed capacity at Rubin-chip-level pricing — though brighterwithherbert explicitly holds this as "a trademark and a plan," not an operating business.
- **Q2 energy storage consensus: 13.8 GWh:** Analyst consensus compiled by Tesla expects 13.8 GWh of energy storage deployments in Q2 2026, per Sawyer Merritt. Context: Tesla deployed ~47 GWh in full-year 2025 (up ~49% YoY at record margins), though BYD has since passed Tesla in volume per brighterwithherbert.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Semi: up to $240K in stackable California incentives:** California's HVIP program offers $120,000 off per Semi for up to 20 trucks; the California Clean Fuel Rewards adds another $120,000 with no truck limit and no scrapping requirement — stackable, effective immediately. Tesla VP of Vehicle Dynamics Dan Priestley separately posted about cold-weather traction control testing in Alaska with maximum-stress loading configurations (per Electrified).
- **Q2 2026 delivery consensus: 406K vehicles:** Tesla's company-compiled analyst consensus sets the Q2 delivery expectation at 406,000 vehicles, per Sawyer Merritt. This is the formal benchmark against which actual Q2 results will be judged.
- **Tesla stock outperformed on a down day:** Tesla closed at $379.71, +1.22%, on a day the Nasdaq fell 1.09% (per Electrified/Sawyer Merritt).

## Market & Competition
- **VW considering 100,000 job cuts, four factory closures:** Volkswagen is reportedly weighing the largest restructuring in automotive history, driven by weak sales rather than costs alone. VW stock is down ~60% over the last three years (per Sawyer Merritt and Electrified). Both sources frame this as evidence of accelerating legacy automaker deterioration under EV transition pressure.
- **SpaceX planning standalone Starlink mobile service:** Per Financial Times and Reuters reporting cited by Electrified, SpaceX told IPO roadshow investors it intends to launch a direct retail mobile service for U.S. consumers, competing directly with Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile. SpaceX holds AWS4 and H-Block spectrum from two Echostar purchases (~$17B + $2.6B) and has ~650 V1 mobile satellites serving ~7.4 million monthly unique devices across ~30 countries as of March 31, 2026.
- **SpaceX acquires Mesh Optical Technologies (FTC approved):** Mesh, founded by former SpaceX engineers, makes optical transceivers replacing copper for intra-data-center communication. No financial terms disclosed. Electrified notes ~60% of optical transceiver manufacturing is currently in China, making the acquisition strategically significant for AI infrastructure supply chain security.
