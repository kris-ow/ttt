Tesla postponed its next-gen Roadster "flying" demo from October 1 to October 15, citing severe weather forecast for the McGregor, Texas test site, in the latest of many delays to the vehicle's launch.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster demo rescheduled two weeks:** Tesla announced on X that it moved the Roadster demo event from October 1 to October 15, citing "severe conditions predicted" and the fact that the event can only be held outdoors. The demo is expected to occur at SpaceX's rocket test site in McGregor, Texas, and involves making the Roadster hover using a SpaceX-developed cold gas thruster package, with no one in the car and spectators kept several hundred yards back.

2. **Long history of delays:** The article recounts that Tesla unveiled the Roadster prototype in November 2017 with an original production promise for 2020; the flying demo itself has slipped through multiple prior target dates (end of 2025, April 1, 2026, late April, "maybe in a month or so," August, then October 1) before the latest push to October 15. Production is still guided by Tesla for "2027 or 2028."

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the weather explanation doesn't hold up, since the article's own weather-forecast data shows the storm clearing within days (dry by the following weekend) while Tesla pushed the event a full two weeks. He speculates Tesla may be running into technical issues with the hover demo itself, noting Musk reportedly told staff the stunt "will be hard to pull off and could go wrong," and expresses frustration that reservation holders just want a real sports car with real specs and pricing rather than a hover stunt.

Notable quotes and data:
- Tesla's X post: "We've been tracking the weather closely with local meteorologists, but given the severe conditions predicted & because this event can only be held outdoors, we've made the difficult decision to reschedule. New date is October 15."
