Tesla's new $39,490 CAD Model 3 Premium RWD in Canada has had its 0-100 km/h acceleration spec changed a second time in under three weeks — from the launch figure of 4.2 seconds, to 5.2 seconds, and now to 6.2 seconds — raising consumer trust concerns.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Repeated spec revisions on Canadian Model 3 launch:** Tesla launched the Shanghai-built Model 3 Premium RWD in Canada on May 1 at $39,490 CAD (~$29,000 USD) with specs of 4.2s 0-100 km/h, 250 kW DC charging, and 192,000 km battery warranty. Within 48 hours, Tesla revised acceleration to 5.2s, charging to 175 kW, and warranty to 160,000 km, citing a "website error." Around May 19, the acceleration was revised again to 6.2s, with no further explanation.

2. **Technical explanation for slower performance:** The 6.2-second figure matches Tesla's European spec for the same LFP-equipped, Shanghai-built variant, suggesting it is the accurate number. The Canadian car uses Tesla's 3D7 motor (194 kW peak, 340 Nm) rather than the previous Fremont-built model's 3D6 motor (220 kW, 440 Nm) — a reduction of 26 kW and 100 Nm of torque. The LFP battery chemistry, which enables the lower price point, also delivers lower peak discharge rates than NMC cells, contributing to the slower acceleration.

## Market & Competition
1. **BYD competitive pressure cited:** The author notes that BYD is preparing to open 20 dealerships in Canada and offers vehicles that are correctly specced from day one and often out-spec Tesla on comparable metrics — framing Tesla's spec errors as a reputational liability at a critical competitive moment.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's author states the repeated corrections make Tesla's "website error" explanation increasingly implausible, and argues Tesla needs to improve spec accuracy — particularly as BYD expands aggressively into the Canadian market. The author notes 6.2 seconds is adequate for a commuter sedan, and the price remains a record low for the Model 3 in Canada.
