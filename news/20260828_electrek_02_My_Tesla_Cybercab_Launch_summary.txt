This is an opinion/prediction piece by Electrek's Fred Lambert previewing Tesla's September 3 Cybercab launch event in Austin, arguing it will mirror the October 2024 unveiling rather than mark a genuine milestone, and that the real bottleneck — driving safety/validation, not vehicle supply — will go unaddressed.

## Robotaxi
1. **Predicted event content:** The author expects Tesla to relaunch the Cybercab (first unveiled October 2024 as a two-seater with no steering wheel/pedals, butterfly doors, promised sub-$30,000 price) into its existing robotaxi service, likely starting in Austin and expanding to Tampa, Orlando, Dallas, and Houston, possibly with a new city announcement.
2. **Steering wheel and safety driver removal predicted:** The author predicts Tesla will finally run Cybercabs without a steering wheel, pedals, or driver-seat occupant — a visual milestone — but stresses this uses the same FSD software stack and hardware already running in Model Y robotaxis, meaning no underlying technology change.
3. **Predicted headline fleet number vs. actual fleet run:** The author predicts Tesla will announce a fleet deployment "on the order of 1,000-plus" Cybercabs, but argues (citing "our tracking and independent community trackers") that Tesla has never run more than about 50 vehicles active in a single week across all markets, with daily figures closer to 20; the unsupervised/driverless subset reportedly peaked around 25 in spring and has since drifted sideways or down. The author expects the same pattern with Cybercab — a large announced number against a small number actually operating.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Validation cited as the real constraint:** The article notes Elon Musk has told investors that "rigorous validation" is the constraint on scaling and that Tesla is waiting on an FSD rewrite before scaling further — framed as the actual bottleneck rather than vehicle production or efficiency.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues the Cybercab is a genuine efficiency improvement (citing 165 Wh/mile as Tesla's most efficient vehicle and lower running cost than a Model Y) but says this doesn't address the program's real problem: driving quality/safety. He asserts the supervised fleet has been crashing at roughly four times the human driver rate, cites rider-reported missed pickups/drop-offs, and notes remote operators still finish rides the car can't complete. His verdict: watch the fleet Tesla is actually running a month after launch (October), not the announced figure, to judge whether the event represents real progress.
