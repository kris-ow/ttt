## Robotaxi

- **Greater Austin expansion confirmed by Tesla VP:** Ashok Kaliswami officially announced unsupervised robotaxi service now covers the entire greater Austin metro area, no longer confined to a smaller geofenced zone. The exact vehicle count operating in the expanded area is unknown; the RobotaxiTracker app was reported to be lagging more than at launch, possibly reflecting increased activity.

- **Texas Motor Carrier VIN registrations at 51:** The Texas Motor Carrier registry shows 51 registered Tesla robotaxi VINs — up from 42 just days prior — representing vehicles registered and eligible for deployment, not necessarily all active simultaneously.

- **Community reports of unsupervised activity paused in North Austin:** Tracker "Cyber Owl" reported zero unsupervised robotaxis in North Austin as of Thursday, with her own ride including a safety monitor, no robotaxi badge, long wait times, and pickup/drop-off logic still needing improvement. The operational picture remains mixed despite the official expansion announcement.

- **Cybercab steering-wheel removal still unresolved:** All Cybercabs currently spotted in Austin, Houston, and Dallas retain steering wheels. The process for removing the wheel and pedals — whether at any service center, select locations, or via mobile service — has not been addressed publicly, suggesting the true driverless Cybercab form factor may be weeks away from deployment.

- **Six-city expansion timeline under pressure:** Tesla had indicated six cities by end of Q2 2026. As of June 5, supervised and unsupervised service remains in Austin, Dallas, and Houston only; California has no operational robotaxi service. Approximately 21–26 days remain in Q2. The panel on brighterwithherbert broadly expects a more concerted ramp through July–September.

- **Infrastructure buildout signals scale intent:** Three facilities totaling approximately 36,000–151,000 square feet for car washing and charging are under construction or approved in the Dallas/Austin corridor, alongside a new 51,000-square-foot delivery center in Grand Prairie, Texas (between Dallas and Fort Worth). There are currently 36 open job listings for robotaxi-related roles.

- **Cybercab "Gleaming Gold" production unit spotted:** A production Cybercab in a new color called "Gleaming Gold" was shown featuring McLaren-style self-opening doors, two seats, no rear-view mirrors, no sunroof, and no rear window. The unconventional door mechanism was flagged as a potential manufacturing risk at scale.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Belgium FSD homologation test complete:** Tesla has finished the required 5,000-kilometer supervised FSD test in Belgium; the regulatory burden now shifts to the administration for a formal homologation advice. The Belgian mobility minister expressed support for the fastest possible approval in Flanders and Belgium. Belgium had been cited as likely the next European country after Estonia to receive FSD supervised.

- **FSD supervised now approved in 11 countries:** Ashok Kaliswami presented a slide showing approvals in the US, Canada, Mexico, Netherlands, Estonia, broader Europe, China (limited city rollout), South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, and Puerto Rico. Estonia was added the same week. China's inclusion was noted as technically accurate but potentially misleading — reflecting shipment to customers in certain areas, not a nationwide formal approval.

- **FSD pending in 13+ countries covering 36% of world population:** Pending markets include Japan, India, Israel, Bangladesh, Thailand, Taiwan, Malaysia, Philippines, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Ethiopia, Colombia, Chile, and the rest of Europe. Ethiopia was flagged as a surprise given Tesla currently operates only in Morocco on the continent.

- **FSD v14 confirmed coming to Australia and New Zealand:** Tesla communicated to Australian customers that FSD v14 is in its "final stages of local development testing" and is "coming soon," with no specific date given. Most countries outside the US are currently on older versions.

- **Lemonade insurance FSD discount expands to Indiana:** Lemonade now offers a 50% insurance rate reduction for FSD miles in Oregon, Arizona, and Indiana, based on internal data showing FSD drivers have approximately 50% fewer accidents. The CEO has suggested rates could approach near-zero over time; expansion to 10 states total is planned.

- **Tesla AI team presented at CVPR:** Tesla's AI team presented at CVPR this week; Ashok's full presentation is expected to be uploaded within a few days.

- **FSD contract manipulation allegation surfaced:** Electrek's Quick Charge podcast referenced reports that Tesla retroactively added the word "supervised" to FSD contracts already signed by owners, and separately deleted a blog post claiming all Tesla cars contain self-driving hardware. The framing connects this to Tesla avoiding a multibillion-dollar legal liability. Tesla did not respond to the referenced reporting.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus facility at Giga Texas approaching main factory scale:** A dedicated Optimus manufacturing facility is reportedly being built at Giga Texas and is described as nearly as large as the main Giga Texas factory. Tesla currently has 202 open job listings for Optimus-related roles versus 36 for robotaxi — a ratio that signals where internal resource allocation is concentrated.

- **Optimus Gen 3 reveal repeatedly delayed; now tied to production readiness:** Demo dates have slipped from December 2025 to January 2026 to March 2026; Musk has now indicated the reveal will come as close to production readiness as possible rather than as a standalone event. Panel predictions: Jeff Lutz — Q3 2026; Cern Basher — late September to early October 2026.

- **Terafab land acquisition confirmed:** An entity called Terafab Wit LLC, sharing an address with xAI in Palo Alto, has acquired 3,000 acres in Grimes County, Texas near Gibbons Creek Reservoir. The facility is described as a multi-phase vertically integrated semiconductor and advanced computing fab, with an estimated cost of $55–119 billion. Herbert of brighterwithherbert cautions production is at least three or more years away; a partnership with Intel was cited as meaningfully reducing execution risk.

- **Munro Live begins Unitree G1 humanoid teardown:** Munro Associates is undertaking a full teardown of the Unitree G1 base model (132 cm, 35 kg, 23 degrees of freedom, ~0.5 kWh battery, ~2 m/s walking speed). Munro's thesis: humanoid robots are engineering analogs to EVs — actuators, battery systems, thermal management — making its 36 years of EV teardown expertise directly applicable to robot cost analysis.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y #1 overall in South Korea and Australia, May 2026:** In South Korea, Tesla led all imported brands with 10,866 registrations in May, ahead of BMW's 6,555. In Australia, the Model Y became the first EV ever to top the overall sales chart across all fuel types; Tesla ranked 6th by brand, with Model Y comprising 87% of its Australian sales. Australian BEV penetration reached 19.9% in May.

- **China wholesale hits 85,982 units in May, up 39% year-over-year:** Giga Shanghai May 2026 wholesale (domestic plus exports) came in at 85,982 units, +39.4% year-over-year and +8.2% month-over-month. Year-to-date through May: 378,000 units, +29% year-over-year — described as the highest YTD pace after two months into a quarter since 2022.

- **Japan registrations up 182% year-over-year in May:** Tesla registered approximately 2,000 vehicles in Japan in May 2026; year-to-date through May stands at 8,000 units, up roughly 157% versus the same period in 2025. Tesla moved from fifth to fourth place among imported brands in Japan year-to-date.

- **Germany May 2026: ~5,000 sales, +200% YTD versus 2025:** Germany recorded approximately 5,000 Tesla sales in May, representing 2.1% overall market share and 8.5% of the BEV segment. YTD sales are up 200% versus the same period in 2025 and 109% above all of 2025's full-year total. Giga Berlin has announced a 20% production increase.

- **Model Y North America standard equipment upgraded:** All Model Y trims sold in North America now come standard with a black headliner and the larger 16-inch center touchscreen. The 16-inch display is also described as improved in quality, not just size.

- **Tesla Roadster demo pushed to August or later:** Per The Information citing four sources, the planned Texas Roadster demo has slipped to August or later — at least the eighth delay since the 2017 prototype reveal. The demo is intended to showcase the SpaceX-developed cold gas thruster system (internal codename "A71"), which replaces the rear seats with approximately 10 thrusters and enables a claimed 0–60 mph time of 1.1 seconds. A limited SpaceX edition and a scaled-down standard version are both planned; production has been pushed to 2027–2028.

## Market & Competition

- **JP Morgan raises Tesla price target to $475:** New analyst Rajat Gupta replaced longtime bear Ryan Brinkman and lifted the price target from $145 to $475. Commentators noted a potential conflict of interest: JP Morgan is a co-lead underwriter on the SpaceX IPO, giving the bank a direct financial incentive to maintain a positive relationship with Elon Musk.

- **Tesla stock down ~7% on broad macro weakness:** At time of recording on June 5, Tesla was down nearly 7%, largely attributed to the NASDAQ falling over 4% and Tesla's historical beta of 1.5–2.3. Stronger-than-expected labor data was cited as reducing the likelihood of near-term Fed rate cuts — a headwind for long-duration growth equities.

- **Q2 2026 deliveries expected ~July 3; earnings in third or fourth week of July:** Global Tesla sales excluding the US were reported up approximately 27–28% year-over-year through May 2026. The delivery report is expected around July 3, 2026.

- **S&P Dow Jones will not change index inclusion rules:** S&P Dow Jones officially confirmed it will not fast-track the profitability requirement from 12 months to 6 months, meaning SpaceX — if it IPOs in June — cannot join the S&P 500 until approximately June 2027.

## Financials

- **SpaceX Google cloud deal: $920 million/month starting October 2026:** An SEC filing revealed Google agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million per month for approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs and related services from October 2026 through June 2029, with a termination right for either party after December 31, 2026 with 90 days' notice. Annualized, this exceeds $11 billion — more than half of SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue of just over $18 billion. Combined with a previously disclosed Anthropic arrangement running at roughly $15 billion annually, these two contracts alone surpass SpaceX's full-year 2025 revenue.

- **SpaceX IPO pricing June 11, trading June 12:** The roadshow launched June 4; final IPO price is set for June 11 with shares trading June 12. E*TRADE announced a supplemental allocation reserved for Tesla shareholders who have held TSLA for at least 10 years. Fidelity opened access to retail customers with $2,000 or more; SpaceX reserved up to 30% of the offering for retail investors.

- **Alexandra Merz merger scenario: announcement window end of June to mid-August 2026:** Merz outlined a thesis in which a Tesla-SpaceX merger is announced between end of June and mid-August 2026, with a shareholder vote in November 2026 and merger completion in Q2 2027. She estimates S&P 500 index-forced buying at the time of merger consummation could exceed $60–100 billion from S&P alone, with an additional $15–20 billion each from FTSE, CRSP, and NASDAQ 100 — driven by the merged entity replacing the Tesla ticker in the S&P 500.
