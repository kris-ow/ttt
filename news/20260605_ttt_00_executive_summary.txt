## Robotaxi

- **Greater Austin unsupervised expansion official.** Tesla VP of AI Ashok Kaliswami announced that unsupervised robotaxi service now covers the entire greater Austin metro area, no longer confined to its original geofenced zone. The precise number of vehicles in the expanded area remains unknown; the RobotaxiTracker app showed increased lag, possibly reflecting higher activity.

- **Texas Motor Carrier VINs at 51, up from 42.** The Texas Motor Carrier registry shows 51 Tesla robotaxi VINs registered and eligible for deployment, per community tracking — an increase of nine in a matter of days. Registered VINs represent eligibility, not guaranteed simultaneous operation.

- **Unsupervised activity spotty in North Austin.** Community reporter Cyber Owl noted zero unsupervised robotaxis observed in North Austin on Thursday, with her own ride still staffed by a safety monitor, no robotaxi badge on the vehicle, and wait times and pickup/drop-off logic still needing improvement.

- **Cybercab steering-wheel removal is an open operational question.** All Cybercabs currently observed in Austin, Houston, and Dallas retain steering wheels. The logistics of removing them — whether at any service center, select facilities, or via mobile service — remain unconfirmed, and the timeline for deploying the true driverless Cybercab form factor is unclear.

- **Six-city Q2 target under pressure.** Tesla had previously guided to six cities by end of Q2 2026. As of June 5, active service remains in Austin, Dallas, and Houston only. The panel on brighterwithherbert is split: Cern Basher believes three more cities could still arrive before June 30; others expect the more meaningful ramp in July–September 2026.

- **Infrastructure buildout signals scale ambitions.** Three facilities totaling roughly 36,000–151,000 square feet for car-wash and charging are under construction or permitted in the Dallas/Austin corridor, alongside a new 51,000-square-foot delivery center in Grand Prairie, Texas. Per Jeff Lutz: "If Tesla builds out this OPEX-heavy infrastructure and then doesn't deploy high volume, it would be a serious financial drag" — which he reads as evidence of large-scale deployment intent.

- **Production Cybercab spotted in "Gleaming Gold."** Footage showed a production Cybercab in a new color with self-opening McLaren-style doors, two seats, large center console, no rear-view mirrors, no sunroof, and no rear window. The panel flagged the door mechanism as a potential manufacturing risk at higher volumes.

- **Edge cases still unresolved.** Recent testing footage showed a Model Y repeatedly practicing recognition of a chain in a parking lot; a separate incident captured FSD failing to detect a lane-closure gate and striking it. The panel agreed these must be resolved before wide deployment, noting Tesla has speed-profile "dials" it can adjust post-launch to manage risk during edge conditions.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Belgium homologation test complete.** Tesla has finished the required 5,000-kilometer supervised FSD test in Belgium; the case now moves to the administration for a formal homologation opinion. The Belgian mobility minister has stated support for the fastest possible homologation in Flanders and nationally.

- **FSD v14 confirmed coming to Australia and New Zealand.** Tesla told Australian customers that FSD version 14 is in "final stages of local development testing" and "coming soon," with no specific date attached. Most non-US markets currently run older 13-series software.

- **Lemonade FSD discount expands to Indiana.** The insurer now offers a 50% rate reduction for FSD miles in Oregon, Arizona, and Indiana, based on internal data showing FSD drivers have approximately 50% fewer accidents. Lemonade's CEO has suggested rates could approach near-zero over time; the company plans to expand to 10 states total. The panel flagged a compounding dynamic: low-risk FSD drivers concentrating at Lemonade could leave competing insurers with a higher-risk pool.

- **FSD approved in 11 countries; 36% of world population in pending markets.** Kaliswami presented a slide showing supervised FSD live in 11 countries including Estonia, newly approved this week. Pending approvals cover Japan, India, Saudi Arabia, UAE, and 9+ other markets representing 36% of global population. Belgium is reportedly next in Europe, awaiting Dutch homologation data.

- **Tesla AI team presented at CVPR.** Ashok gave a presentation at the computer vision conference; a full upload is expected within days.

- **FSD contract alteration allegation resurfaces.** Per Electrek, Tesla reportedly retroactively added the word "supervised" to FSD contracts owners had already signed, and separately deleted a blog post in which Tesla had claimed all its vehicles contain self-driving hardware. Tesla has not commented.

## Humanoid Bots

- **202 Optimus job listings vs. 36 robotaxi listings.** The gap in open headcount signals where Tesla is concentrating internal resources. An Optimus manufacturing facility is reportedly under construction at Giga Texas, described as nearly as large as the main factory.

- **Optimus Gen 3 reveal timing: Q3–early Q4 2026.** Demo dates slipped from December 2025 through March 2026; Elon Musk has said the reveal will now be tied as closely as possible to production readiness rather than staged as a standalone event. Jeff Lutz predicts Q3 2026; Cern Basher sees second half of September to first half of October.

- **Terafab land acquired in Texas.** Terafab Wit LLC — sharing an address with xAI in Palo Alto — has acquired 3,000 acres in Grimes County, Texas near the Gibbons Creek Reservoir. The estimated facility cost is $55–119 billion across phases. Herbert (brighterwithherbert) cautions that production is at least three or more years out but frames the land acquisition as real forward momentum, drawing a parallel to Musk's Giga Nevada battery rationale: no external supplier can meet projected chip demand for bots, cars, and AI data centers combined.

- **Munro Live begins Unitree G1 humanoid teardown.** Munro Associates has taken delivery of a Unitree G1 base unit (132 cm, 35 kg, 23 degrees of freedom, ~0.5 kWh battery, ~2 m/s walking speed) ahead of a full teardown and cost-structure analysis series. The team frames humanoid robots as "reconfigured electric vehicles" — sharing actuators, battery systems, and thermal management — positioning their 36 years of EV teardown experience as directly transferable.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y #1 overall in South Korea, May 2026.** Tesla led all imported brands with 10,866 total registrations, ahead of BMW (6,555) and Mercedes. Model Y Premium alone accounted for 7,195 units.

- **Model Y #1 overall in Australia, May 2026 — first EV ever to top the full market.** BEV adoption in Australia reached 19.9% in May; Tesla ranked 6th brand overall. The surge is attributed partly to sharply rising petrol prices.

- **China wholesale volume: 85,982 units in May, +39% YoY.** Giga Shanghai's May 2026 output (domestic + export) rose 8.2% month-over-month. YTD through May: 378,000 units, +29% YoY — the strongest pace after two months into a quarter since 2022.

- **Japan registrations +182% YoY in May.** Tesla registered approximately 2,000 vehicles in May and 8,000 year-to-date, moving from 5th to 4th among imported brands. Tesla's Asia sales head Tom Drew: "three more to go," signaling a goal of reaching #1 imported brand.

- **Germany: ~5,000 sales in May, YTD +200% vs. same period 2025.** BEV penetration in Germany is 25%; Tesla holds 8.5% of the BEV segment. Giga Berlin has announced a 20% production increase.

- **Model Y North America: 16-inch screen and black headliner now standard across all trims.** The upgrade applies to all North American Model Y variants; the larger screen is also described as improved in quality, not just size.

- **Tesla Roadster demo pushed to August.** Per The Information citing four sources, the planned Texas demo has slipped to August or later — at least the eighth delay since the 2017 prototype reveal. The demo centers on a cold gas thruster system (internal code "A71") co-developed with SpaceX, targeting a claimed 0–60 mph of 1.1 seconds. A full production SpaceX edition and a scaled-down standard variant are planned; production has been pushed to 2027–2028. Tesla did not respond to comment requests.

## Financials

- **JP Morgan raises Tesla target to $475 from $145.** New analyst Rajat Gupta replaced the long-bearish Ryan Brinkman. Dillon Loomis (Electrified) flagged a structural conflict: JP Morgan is co-lead underwriter on the SpaceX IPO, creating a financial incentive to maintain a positive relationship with Musk.

- **Tesla stock down ~7% on macro, not fundamentals.** The move occurred against a NASDAQ decline of over 4%; Tesla's historical beta of 1.5–2.3 amplified the broader sell-off. Stronger-than-expected labor data reduced near-term Fed easing odds, a headwind for long-duration growth stocks.

- **Q2 2026 deliveries expected ~July 3; earnings third/fourth week of July.** Global Tesla sales (ex-US) running approximately 27–28% above year-ago levels as of May 2026.

- **SpaceX S&P 500 inclusion blocked until June 2027.** S&P Dow Jones officially confirmed it will not change its profitability-duration rules for index inclusion; SpaceX cannot enter the S&P 500 until approximately one year after IPO. Alexandra Merz's Tesla-SpaceX merger thesis — which centers on the merged entity replacing Tesla in the S&P 500 and triggering an estimated $60–100 billion in forced index buying — was discussed extensively across two brighterwithherbert panels, with a proposed merger announcement window of end of June to mid-August 2026.
