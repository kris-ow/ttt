## Robotaxi

- **Official Cybercab first-responder documentation published June 28** — ~30-page PDFs covering vehicle ID, camera placement, battery caution zones, and emergency QR codes, with an Arizona-specific appendix. Per Bhakdi, these filings are legally required before commercial AV operations begin in a state; Sawyer Merritt characterized the release as signaling "first public Cybercab rides are going to start very soon." Production spec confirmed in the filings: no steering wheel, no accelerator, no brake pedal. Vehicles observed with a wheel are explicitly classified as SAE Level 2 test units, not production configuration.

- **Cybercab staging inventory at record levels across multiple cities.** More than 150 units were counted at Giga Texas by June 26, up from prior milestones of 30, 50, and 100. Houston staging reached approximately 84 units per Bhakdi. Separately, a content creator documented Cybercabs arriving in the Washington D.C. area, the first confirmed sighting on the East Coast. Jeff Lutz noted that shipping units away from the factory — where rework is cheapest — is an implicit hardware-readiness signal.

- **June 30 IR commitment is definitively broken.** Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings deck committed to paid robotaxi service in Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix by June 30; zero of those five cities are operational as the deadline expires. The Q1 2026 earnings deck quietly downgraded remaining target cities from date-specific language to "preparations underway." Bhakdi, who had held June as his primary window, now targets a two-to-eight-week horizon and stated explicitly: "As long as [Cybercab scale] doesn't happen, we have a problem… this stock will remain under pressure and trend down."

- **Demand is deliberately throttled; pricing at $3 drop + $1.40–$1.50/mile.** Per FutureAzA guest John, Tesla is intentionally holding prices high — up from launch-era $0.42/mile — to keep wait times under ~10 minutes given fleet constraints. John estimates Tesla could profitably price as low as $0.15/mile but is avoiding demand it cannot yet service. Waymo, for comparison, is running approximately 500,000 paid driverless rides per week across ~10 metros.

- **Cybercab engineered for 500,000-mile fleet duty life** versus approximately 150,000 miles for a consumer vehicle, and contains roughly 4,000 total parts — approximately half the Model 3's count. Waymo's sensor suite alone reportedly consists of ~4,000 parts, illustrating the structural cost gap between purpose-built and sensor-bolted-on approaches. Conversion from steering-wheel-equipped to pedal-free configuration can be performed in the field in hours.

## Autonomous Driving

- **NHTSA removed the manual brake pedal requirement for vehicles designed exclusively for automated operation**, opening a 30-day public comment period before a final DOT ruling. The agency framed the move as an effort to "safely unleash American innovation." For Tesla, it reduces the risk of NHTSA challenging the Cybercab's existing self-certification under FMVSS. Separately, the UNECE adopted the first-ever global AV framework — hardware-agnostic, no LiDAR mandate — backed by the US, EU, China, Japan, Canada, and the UK; binding only after domestic incorporation.

- **FSD 14.3.4 demonstrated zero disengagements across New York City**, including a full unsupervised trip from New Haven to NYC per Sawyer Merritt. A sideswipe-avoidance maneuver — briefly crossing a double yellow line to avoid a drifting vehicle — circulated widely and was cited by multiple commentators as evidence of reactive capability beyond conventional coded responses. FSD cumulative miles crossed 11.3 billion; the fleet now uploads approximately 30 million fresh miles daily.

- **Spain approval expected around June 30** per employees cited by the FutureAzA host; the Spanish testing fleet has grown from 19 to ~30 vehicles and covered ~275,000 km since November 2025 with zero serious incidents. Finland's Traficom stated FSD "is considered to have significant potential to reduce accidents" and may approve independently ahead of the EU-wide vote, now pushed to October 2026 at the earliest. China FSD full-fleet approval targeted Q3 2026; 200-vehicle test fleet is active, uploading ~1.7 TB/day.

## Optimus

- **Optimus V3 supply chain activating in Taiwan and Southeast Asia.** Tesla signed agreements with Merl Automation (harmonic reducers and joint modules, with a new Thailand joint venture) and Asia Optical (spherical and aspherical lens components for the vision system). Parts are slated for mass production H2 2026 into 2027 — supplier-level ramp preceding Tesla's own production line.

- **V3 reveal targeted late July to early August; Fremont line conversion on track for late summer.** V3's headline upgrade is 22 degrees of freedom per hand, doubled from 11, with actuators relocated to the forearm to reduce hand weight and increase speed. The former Model S/X line at Fremont is being converted for Optimus production, expected to complete around late summer 2026. First ~5,000 units are expected to be deployed across five to eight Tesla-owned facilities in California and Las Vegas.

- **Giga Texas Optimus factory steel now four stories tall**, projected at ~4,000+ feet in length at full build, targeting ~10 million units per year at full ramp. Realistic production-ready estimate from on-site observers remains end-2027 to 2028. Approximately 208 Optimus job listings are open across North America and Greece, led by 65 manufacturing/industrialization roles.

- **Digital Optimus emerging as a distinct near-term software product.** Ashok Elluswamy showed Digital Optimus on a slide during the week. Six of 17 AI/ML Optimus job listings explicitly name "Digital Optimus" — an agent running on the AI4 chip that operates computers via screen vision rather than APIs. Brian Wang (Next Big Future) expects a Macrohard/Digital Optimus demo by ~September 2026 and frames it as a potential near-term revenue catalyst. Caveat from brighterwithherbert: Anthropic shipped computer-use agents in 2024 and holds ~40% of enterprise API spend; Digital Optimus has no public benchmark.

## Energy

- **Tesla–NatPower deal is the largest energy storage contract in Tesla history**: 25+ GWh phase one across Italy and the UK, approximately $5 billion in construction value, with the full program targeting 100+ GWh and over $15 billion in revenues over 20 years. The prior largest single-source battery storage installation was approximately 12.3 GWh, making phase one alone more than twice that scale. The deal also embeds Tesla's Autobidder AI energy trading software — the stickier, higher-margin component.

- **Tesla–Giga Storage LOI for 2.8 GWh Megablock project in Limburg, Belgium** — the first Megablock deal at GWh scale, estimated value ~$700 million per Sawyer Merritt. Final contracts expected summer 2026. Combined with the NatPower deal and a $1 GWh Scotland project, Sawyer Merritt cited more than $6 billion in new Megapack announcements over recent weeks.

- **Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home VPP aggregates 16.8 GW of flexible capacity** across hundreds of thousands of residential Powerwalls, EVs, and 8 million+ smart thermostats. Over 300 MW is immediately deployable in Virginia's Data Center Alley. Q2 2026 analyst consensus for energy storage is 13.8 GWh; Q1 came in at 8.8 GWh with record 39.5% gross margin, though management flagged some margin compression ahead from tariffs on Chinese cells.

- **Megapod trademark filed June 18**: modular AI inference data centers co-located at Supercharger stations, which already carry approximately 7 GW of permitted grid connections. Elon Musk confirmed the name and described eventual scale as millions of units. The AI4 chip ($650) serves as shared silicon across FSD vehicles, Optimus, and Megapod — a single inference platform across three form factors.

## Bear Case of the Week

- **Katy, TX fatal crash: FSD was confirmed active.** Ashok Elluswamy confirmed via public statement that FSD was engaged when driver Michael Butler manually overrode the system by pressing the accelerator to 100% pedal travel, reaching 73–75 mph in a residential 25 mph zone and striking a home, killing 76-year-old Martha Avila Mantilla. NHTSA opened a Special Crash Investigation and maintains a separate Engineering Analysis covering ~3.2 million vehicles. Elon initially posted "FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets" — a claim his own company's telemetry then contradicted. Electrek argues the "driver overrode it" defense does not constitute exoneration: the most plausible behavioral scenario is an out-of-the-loop emergency response where the driver hit the accelerator instead of the brake — identical to the grounds on which a Florida jury found Tesla 33% liable in a $243 million judgment upheld February 2026.

- **June 30 IR commitment missed with no credible successor date.** Tesla's binding Q4 2025 earnings deck commitment to paid robotaxi service in Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix by June 30 expires this week with zero of five cities operational. The Q1 2026 earnings deck quietly replaced date-specific language with "preparations underway." Bhakdi — who had defended June as his primary window across multiple prior briefs — has now shifted to a two-to-eight-week horizon. The miss is the third consecutive weekly brief in which this deadline has appeared; it is now factually closed.

- **Paid robotaxi miles remain negligible.** The Q1 2026 earnings deck disclosed paid robotaxi miles "nearly doubled sequentially" in Q1 — from approximately one million to approximately 1.7 million cumulative miles. That figure represents roughly three to four days of Waymo's current weekly output of 500,000 paid driverless rides. Musk's original Austin target of 500 unsupervised vehicles by end of 2025 and Bhakdi's prior projection of 1,800 unsupervised units by April–May 2026 have both been missed by a wide margin. The unsupervised fleet count tracked by RobotaxiTracker.com has not updated since May 9, 2026 — now 51 days stale.

- **Two FSD 14.3.4 edge-case failures documented this week.** In one incident, FSD drove over a gas can that had fallen from a trailer without detecting it; Tesla's Yun Tasai acknowledged the clip. In a second, FSD prepared to proceed through a lowered railroad crossing arm. Loomis noted the inconsistency with FSD's demonstrated ability to brake hard for small animals — the same object-detection pipeline. Both clips circulated publicly during the same week Tesla is seeking EU-wide regulatory approval.

- **Optimus missed 2025 production targets; V3 hand design previously failed.** Brighterwithherbert explicitly states Tesla missed its 2025 Optimus unit numbers and that most current Optimus robots work inside Tesla's own factories. Elon reportedly acknowledged in ~May 2026 that the most recently patented hand design "didn't work," implying the program is on its fourth or fifth hand iteration. China's Unitree shipped thousands of cheaper humanoids in 2025; Figure has a live factory pilot with BMW. The 2026 targets of 50,000–100,000 units are described by the FutureAzA host as definitively unachievable: "it's going to be much less."
