## Brief

- Tesla Q2 earnings: record revenue, EPS miss, stock falls 12-13%
- Free cash flow negative $1.09B on capex surge
- Robotaxi miles up 380,000 driverless, zero notable incidents claimed
- Musk dodges Tesla-SpaceX merger question, cites "appropriate process"
- Optimus production line now live at Fremont, replacing Model S/X
- No concrete HW3 upgrade plan given despite years of promises

## Financials
- Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $28.24B (up 26% YoY, beating the ~$27.6B estimate), on record deliveries of 480,126 vehicles. Non-GAAP EPS came in at $0.33, well below the ~$0.53-0.55 Wall Street estimate; GAAP net income was $1.11B, of which roughly $750M was a mark-to-market gain on Tesla's SpaceX stake, per Electrek's analysis.
- Operating income fell 57% to $398M (1.4% margin, down from 4.1% a year ago); free cash flow was negative $1.09B as capex jumped 142% to $5.79B. Regulatory credit revenue fell 67% to $146M.
- Tesla's cash balance fell only about $1B to roughly $39-40B despite the capex ramp, per Jo Bhakdi — evidence he says the core balance sheet remains sound.
- Terafab (chip/memory fab) location and plans will be announced at a dedicated future event, not folded into the earnings call; equipment orders have been placed for a development fab in Austin covering mask production, logic, memory, packaging and chip testing. Musk thanked Micron for a multi-year memory supply commitment at what he called a reasonable price.
- Stock fell 12-13.4% the day after earnings, shedding over $140B in market value; commentators (Bhakdi, Electrek) partly attribute this to a bigger-than-expected capex signal with no hard numbers given, plus a broader market repricing of AI/growth names (Google also fell ~7% on a capex raise despite beating estimates).

## Robotaxi
- Tesla says the Robotaxi fleet has driven over 380,000 driverless miles across six cities with "zero notable incidents" (Tesla's term); paid cumulative robotaxi miles reached 2.4M in Q2, up from ~1.7M in Q1, per Ashok Elluswamy. Electrek counters that paid miles were flat quarter-over-quarter at roughly 900,000 and the unsupervised fleet has shrunk to about 21 cars — a direct conflict in how the same dataset is being read.
- Musk and Elluswamy both said miles are compounding "more than 10% per week," a pace they expect to hold through year-end; Brian Wang calculates Tesla's cumulative paid miles are still roughly 1% of Waymo's cited 220 million.
- Active Robotaxi fleet stands at 791 per RobotaxiTracker.com (as of 2026-07-24; Bay Area 643, Austin 109, Dallas 20, Houston 19).
- Coverage now spans Austin, Dallas, Houston, plus newly added Miami, Orlando and Tampa, with Phoenix and Las Vegas in prep; California operations still use safety drivers. Tesla confirmed it will not partner with Uber or Lyft, relying on its own app.
- Cybercab production began at Giga Texas with installed annual capacity now listed above 125,000 units; engineering test drives on public roads and employee rides at the Giga Texas campus started this quarter. Estimates for Cybercabs in service by year-end diverge sharply: Wang gives only ~20% odds of a 20,000-40,000 "big number," the host guesses 10,000-15,000, and Bhakdi ties a $1,000 share-price scenario to roughly the same 10,000-15,000 threshold, calling it "increasingly unlikely" given macro conditions.
- Tesla received zoning approval for a 56-stall Robotaxi charging facility in San Antonio for its private fleet, pending a final City Council vote.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD v15 (roughly a 10x parameter increase enabling reasoning) is already running in parts of the Robotaxi fleet; Wang cites 38,000 miles driven under an early version with zero incidents. Seven features, including destination/parking memory, are still in development.
- Musk gave no concrete plan for Hardware 3 upgrades on the call, per Electrek, only saying an upgrade "would make sense" and be "financially sensible at some point" — with no timing, method or cost committed. He floated a future AI chip (a moderate AI4 upgrade around mid-2027, or AI5) rather than Hardware 4 as the eventual fix, but said AI5 "will initially go into Optimus," not HW3 cars.
- France's Transportation Minister said FSD's "safety trade-offs are not yet sufficient" for authorization, stating explicitly it is "not a self-driving system" — a regulatory headwind in France/EU.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla shared photos of its first Optimus production line, now occupying floor space at Fremont formerly used for Model S/X manufacturing; Musk called it "stunning to see" and described Optimus as the hardest manufacturing scale-up Tesla has attempted, given its almost entirely new supply chain.
- Near-term Optimus units (Gen 3, in the thousands) are being used for training and data collection rather than sold commercially; Wang cautions a mass-market, 10-million-unit-scale version is likely still several years out.
- "Digital Optimus" — applying Tesla's self-driving vision stack to computer/screen operation — is being built in partnership with SpaceX, with Grok directing tasks. Tesla is also building "Megapod" compute boxes pairing AI4 chips with x86 hardware for deployment at power-dense sites like Superchargers.
- AI5 chips remain on track for volume production around the middle of next year, per the earnings call, with AI6 also described as on track.

## Market & Competition
- Uber's ride-hailing model, built on ~1 million active U.S. drivers generating ~$190B/year in fares and fees, faces a structural threat from Tesla's owned-fleet approach, argues Farzad's video; cited robotaxi cost targets of $0.20-0.40/mile compare to Uber's >$2.80/mile average.
- Broader market selloff hit AI/growth names market-wide the same day (NASDAQ down ~2.2-2.5%, Amazon down ~4.5%, Google down ~7%), which Bhakdi frames as a systemic repricing rather than Tesla-specific weakness, citing rising 10-year Treasury yields (above 4.7%) and Iran-conflict risk as contributing macro pressures.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk acknowledged "more and more overlap" between Tesla and SpaceX, especially around Terafab, but said combining the companies "can't" be discussed on an earnings call and requires "the appropriate process." Tesla general counsel Brandon Ehrhart said the companies "deepened" their relationship earlier this year via an investment and a framework agreement covering future Terafab development.
- SpaceX's S-1 disclosed Tesla's earlier $2B xAI investment converted into 18,990,195 SpaceX Class A shares (under 1% ownership), and that SpaceX/xAI entities bought roughly $650M in goods and services from Tesla in 2025.
- SpaceX's implied share price fell to around $111, down from about $220 a few weeks earlier, per Bhakdi, who says he's advising against buying SpaceX for now despite long-term bullishness. Electrek cites unnamed Wall Street analysts putting merger odds at 80-90% by early 2027; Wang separately offers his own unconfirmed guess of a possible SpaceX earnings announcement in early August, a Terafab event after, and a shareholder vote around November.
