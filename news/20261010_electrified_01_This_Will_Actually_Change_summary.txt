2. **Elon distinguished Digital Optimus from Grokbot (~2 months before video):** Responding to a comment calling Grokbot "digital Optimus," Elon said Digital Optimus is different — described as "real-time video human emulation." The host treats this as the clearest recent confirmation the two are separate programs, while acknowledging Elon himself said in March that Digital Optimus (aka "Macrohard," a joint xAI/Tesla project under Tesla's $2 billion investment in xAI) is a system-one "instinctive" layer that processes real-time screen/keyboard/mouse video, directed by Grok as the system-two "thinking" layer — intended to run primarily on Tesla's AI4 chip (said to cost around $650) rather than expensive cloud GPUs.

4. **Megapod referenced on Tesla's Q2 call as physical infrastructure for the program:** On the Q2 earnings call (held July 22), per the host's recounting, Elon described a "Megapod" design pairing an x86 computer with a Tesla AI4 computer in a box, with many such boxes deployable at Supercharger sites — citing roughly 7 GW of power Tesla already has at superchargers — to provide distributed compute for Digital Optimus; Tesla separately filed a trademark application earlier this year for "Megapod" covering modular AI-compute data center hardware.
5. **Cost/architecture rationale versus Grokbot's current computer-use approach:** The host explains Grokbot's current computer-use fallback (used when no built-in connector exists) relies on repeated cloud screenshot-analysis loops, which he says cost roughly $1 per 100 screenshots processed (per data he attributes to Anthropic) and can total thousands of turns per task — costs he says could become significant at scale. Digital Optimus is designed to process a rolling ~5-second video buffer locally on AI4, reducing cloud calls (potentially from many thousands to as few as one per task, per the host's framing) and avoiding the "brittleness" of static screenshots.
