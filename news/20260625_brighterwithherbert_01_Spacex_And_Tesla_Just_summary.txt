This video from brighterwithherbert covers several major business developments across SpaceX and Tesla that the hosts argue are being overlooked by mainstream media and analysts. The core thesis is that while public attention remains fixed on rockets and EV sales figures, SpaceX and Tesla are quietly building AI compute and energy businesses — through orbital AI satellites, terrestrial data center deals, large-scale Megapack contracts, and distributed grid partnerships — that could generate tens of billions in new revenue. Host Herbert is joined by Brian from the YouTube channel FutureAzA, who contributes factory-floor insight and broader context on Tesla's energy business.

## Energy
1. **Tesla–Nat Power Megapack Partnership (Phase One):** Tesla signed what the hosts describe as a landmark energy storage partnership with Nat Power. Phase one covers 25 gigawatt-hours of battery storage across sites in Italy and Britain, representing roughly $5 billion in revenue. The full buildout could potentially exceed 100 gigawatt-hours and generate up to $15 billion in revenue over 20 years. The hosts note this is the first time battery energy storage has been procured, financed, and executed across multiple locations under a single integrated framework. Brian points out that the previous largest single-source installation of this type was reportedly 12.3 gigawatt-hours, making phase one alone more than twice that size.

2. **Scale Relative to Tesla's Megapack Production Capacity:** The hosts contextualize the 25 gigawatt-hour contract against Tesla's existing production footprint. Tesla's Megapack factory in Lathrop is said to have approximately 40 gigawatt-hours of annual capacity, and a new Megapack factory in Houston is reportedly planned at 50 gigawatt-hours when it comes online. Brian notes that Tesla's Megapack factory in Shanghai is still ramping and has not yet reached its final run rate, contributing to a backlog of deployments that could cause a future quarter where deployed gigawatt-hours substantially exceed production. Brian also confirms — based on personal factory tours — that Tesla's Megapack battery supplier is a major manufacturer with no supply concerns, though he declines to name them on-record.

3. **Expectation of Strong Q2 Energy Numbers:** The hosts discuss the upcoming Tesla Q2 earnings call, expected in late July 2026. They note that Q1 energy deployments came in below expectations, but attribute this to pipeline timing rather than slowing demand. Brian expresses confidence that a large catch-up deployment quarter is coming — potentially showing 25–30 gigawatt-hours deployed in a single quarter — though he does not commit to Q2 specifically. Some observers the host references expect the energy segment to "really surprise" on the upside.

4. **Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home Distributed Virtual Power Plant:** Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced a partnership to create what is described as the largest distributed power plant in the United States, aggregating more than 16 gigawatt-hours of flexible power capacity. The system draws on hundreds of thousands of residential battery systems (including Powerwalls), EVs, and more than 8 million smart thermostats. Brian explains the mechanism: rather than pulling energy back from vehicles or homes (vehicle-to-grid), the system primarily works by deferring charging and adjusting thermostat setpoints during peak demand — a demand-response model. Participants receive compensation incentives for participating. The hosts note that the data center corridor in Virginia alone reportedly has 300 megawatts available for immediate use under this framework. The partnership is projected to potentially reduce overall U.S. electricity bills by up to $170 billion over the next decade, per the hosts' cited figures.

5. **Case for a European Megapack Factory:** Brian argues that the scale of the Nat Power deal — effectively requiring output comparable to an entire factory — makes a strong case for Tesla building a dedicated Megapack manufacturing facility in Europe to serve both this contract and broader continental demand.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX AI Compute Business — Reflection AI Deal:** SpaceX signed a new AI compute agreement with Reflection AI, who will pay approximately $150 million per month — or up to $6.3 billion through 2029 — for access to SpaceX's Colossus 2 data center and Nvidia GB300 chips. This joins previously announced deals with Google and Anthropic (each reportedly paying $950 million to $1.25 billion, per the hosts' figures). A table compiled by commentator Steve Mark Ryan, as cited by the host, puts SpaceX's total AI compute annual run rate across all signed deals at approximately $27.84 billion, or roughly $2.32 billion per month. The hosts note these figures assume deals run to their stated end dates, which they consider unlikely.

2. **SpaceX "Star Mind" Orbital AI Satellite Brand:** Elon Musk confirmed (per a social media exchange cited by the host) that the name "Star Mind" — a trademark pending application filed by SpaceX — will be the official brand for SpaceX's orbital AI satellite constellation. The hosts note SpaceX has reportedly requested regulatory approval for up to 1 million AI satellites. The branding joins a growing "Star" product family (Starlink, Star Pipe, etc.). Brian and the host discuss the concept's defensibility against skeptics, noting that even if the orbital concept fails, much of the underlying technology could be repurposed for ground-based standalone closed-loop systems.

3. **SpaceX "Star Pipe" Natural Gas Pipeline:** SpaceX has notified the Texas Railroad Commission of plans to build an 8.1-mile, 16-inch natural gas pipeline at Starbase, Texas, dubbed "Star Pipe," with construction scheduled to begin July 7, 2026. Brian explains this is critical for scaling Starship launch cadence — currently, fuel must be trucked in on a single congested road, which limits rapid reuse capability. A 16-inch diameter pipeline is described as substantial for natural gas flow, and an 8-mile stretch is relatively low cost compared to typical pipeline projects.

4. **Tesla "Megapods" — Distributed AI Data Centers:** The hosts briefly reference Tesla's trademarked "Megapods" concept — portable, packaged AI data center units designed to be deployed at or near Supercharger locations. The idea combines existing electrical infrastructure with Megapack storage and compute chips (H100s, B100s, B300s are mentioned). Brian frames this as consistent with Tesla's product philosophy of pre-packaging complexity inside the unit, similar to Megapack 2. He notes potential use cases including data-security-sensitive customers who need on-premises compute, and arbitrage opportunities in municipalities with very low overnight electricity rates.

5. **Contrast with GM and Ford:** Brian expresses skepticism toward legacy automakers, noting that announcements of electrification pullbacks are often followed almost immediately by new electrification commitment announcements, making it difficult to take their strategic direction seriously compared to Tesla's demonstrated scaling in both EVs and energy.
