This panel discussion (hosts Herbert, Jeff Lutz, Cern Basher, and Phil Beisel) covers xAI's Grok bot routing strategy to third-party AI models, Tesla's expanding robotaxi fleet in Texas, Germany's softened stance on approving Tesla's FSD, the structure of the planned Terafab chip facility, and SpaceX's new satellite spectrum deal — alongside a first-hand account of riding in a Cybercab.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok bot now routes queries to best-in-class third-party models:** xAI's Grok bot will direct queries to whichever model performs best for a given task — including Claude Opus 5.5, Midjourney, and Suno — rather than exclusively using Grok's own models; a project lead (referred to in the transcript as "Lauren Tan" per the speaker, name unconfirmed) said this applies even though Grok 4.8 is "still being worked on," which panelists took as a signal that Opus 5.5 currently outperforms it. Panelists characterized this as xAI prioritizing distribution/customer capture over owning the best underlying model, while using its hardware/compute leverage (renting capacity to companies like Anthropic) to stay central to the ecosystem.
2. **Panel debates device/distribution strategy for Grok bot:** Hosts discussed whether SpaceX/xAI needs its own hardware device to distribute Grok bot broadly, given that Apple and Google control phone distribution and Meta/OpenAI have billions of existing users; panelists speculated SpaceX could leverage Starlink as a distribution channel or eventually build its own device, though Jeff Lutz cautioned this remains speculative ("if someone else doesn't build an effective device... I see them taking it upon themselves").
3. **$40 billion debt raise reportedly planned for Nvidia chip purchases:** Per the host, SpaceX is reportedly ("allegedly") looking to raise $40 billion via debt to buy Nvidia chips, in addition to prior IPO proceeds and existing debt; panelists framed this as part of a broader industry-wide compute buildout they do not view as a bubble, given observed GPU rental rates and utilization.
4. **SpaceX targeting roughly 10 gigawatts of compute capacity next year:** A panelist relayed from a conversation with Brian Wang that Elon Musk/SpaceX intend to secure around 10 gigawatts of computing power next year, which would require building "mini-hubs" and locking in Nvidia's next-generation ("Vera Rubin") chips expected in the first or second quarter of next year.
5. **Musk claims SpaceX could eventually be "orders of magnitude" larger than world GDP:** Musk reportedly posted that he sees a path for SpaceX's valuation to exceed the entire current global economy "by orders of magnitude" (not a single order of magnitude). Panelists noted world GDP is roughly $126 trillion, meaning Musk's claim implies a figure in the quadrillions or higher; panelists treated this as a very long-term, speculative claim ("a decade away, guys... it's not going to happen anytime soon") rather than a near-term target.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **SpaceX wins FCC approval for direct-to-cell satellite constellation and mobile spectrum:** SpaceX received FCC approval for a constellation of 15,000 satellites for direct-to-cell communication, plus a separate permit allowing SpaceX to operate as a mobile communications/telephone provider without partnering with an existing carrier. Panelists noted SpaceX's stock rose a few percent after the news while Verizon and T-Mobile shares fell roughly 7%. Panelists view this as a multi-year infrastructure buildout (ground infrastructure and device manufacturing still need to be solved) rather than an immediate product launch, with potential for SpaceX to eventually build its own communications-focused device.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab to be independently owned and operated by Tesla/SpaceX, not TSMC:** Following a report that TSMC was in talks to be involved with Terafab, Musk clarified that Tesla and SpaceX will build and operate the facility themselves; TSMC may lease a portion of capacity but nothing more. Panelists said they don't believe any formal partnership agreements are signed yet (possibly excluding an Intel process arrangement), and described Terafab as roughly 50x existing chip capacity, giving Musk's companies significant near-term leverage over chip supply deals, costs, and priority access while the facility ramps up over the coming years.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas robotaxi fleet grows to 589 vehicles:** Per tracking data (RobotaxiTracker.com), the Cybercab count rose from 45 (as of August 31) to 169, while Model Y robotaxis grew from 269 to 420, totaling 589 vehicles. A separate tracker figure cited this week showed a record 868 Cybercabs driving around Austin, with 670 operating driverless; roughly 300 additional Cybercabs were spotted in Dallas. (Note: given the context is robotaxi fleet deployment, "Cybercab" is used throughout for vehicles the transcript's auto-captions rendered as "Cybertruck.")
2. **Musk addresses nighttime operating hours and pet safety:** Musk said robotaxis would operate until 11 p.m. (not stopping at 10 p.m. as previously suggested), explaining that the main technical focus is avoiding collisions with pets that are hard to see at night ("trying to avoid gray kittens on gray asphalt in the dark").
3. **First-hand Cybercab ride described as highly polished:** Panelist Phil Beisel and host Cern Basher both took rides in Austin, describing the experience as remarkably complete and mature — citing a responsive app with real-time car tracking, confident low-latency navigation in a crowded parking lot, and no steering wheel or pedals. Both contrasted it favorably with Waymo vehicles seen nearby, which they felt looked and felt less polished and more hesitant at intersections.
4. **Panelists speculate AI-accelerated development benefits platform as a whole:** Jeff Lutz argued that Tesla is likely using AI tools not just for FSD driving software but across fleet operations, routing, and quality management, suggesting this acceleration explains the polished state of the robotaxi product; he contrasted this with Waymo, which he believes may be moving toward a vision-only stack but faces platform limitations.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Germany signals openness to approving Tesla's FSD across the EU:** A German Ministry of Transport official (referred to as Stefan Bilger) reportedly shifted position to support approving Tesla's FSD across Europe, with Musk thanking him. Per the discussion, conditions include rebranding the feature (tentatively "TAD," for Tesla Assisted Driving) and limiting speed-limit overages to 10% (down from a prior allowance of 50% over the limit). Panelists noted this could help pave the way for approval in other key EU markets (Italy, France, Spain).
2. **Tesla European sales reportedly rose sharply in September:** The host noted that Tesla sales in certain European countries have risen sharply this September; panelists attributed this to a mix of factors including fuel prices, regional supply chain positioning (local manufacturing in Berlin), and potential EU restrictions on Chinese vehicle imports, rather than FSD approval itself (which has not yet happened).

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Panel discusses clothing/skin for Optimus and social acceptance:** Panelists discussed a host-authored piece arguing Optimus's social acceptance would improve if it wore clothing (branded to context, e.g. hotel uniforms), drawing comparisons to smartphone cases and car wraps; they agreed functional skin (e.g., for safe human contact) could make sense but that making Optimus look too human risks an "uncanny valley" effect.
