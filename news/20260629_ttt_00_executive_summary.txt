## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14 Light now rolling out to Hardware 3 (AI3) fleet.** Ashok Elluswamy hit his self-imposed end-of-June deadline, distilling Hardware 4's V14 driving behavior into AI3's camera and compute configuration. The update adds reinforcement learning, destination parking options, speed profiles, smoother steering, improved merges, pedestrian handling, and new parking/unparking capabilities — features previously exclusive to Hardware 4. Elon Musk publicly acknowledged the release: "Nice work."

- **Hardware 3 engineering achievement is significant.** AI3 carries only ~15% of Hardware 4's effective memory bandwidth. The estimated 3–4 million AI3 vehicles in the fleet make this commercially important, potentially unlocking subscription revenue and fulfilling FSD purchase obligations. Whether AI3 will ever reach unsupervised capability remains an open question.

- **Early tester feedback strongly positive.** Tester Kristen described V14 Light as feeling "as good as version 14 to me on an AI4 vehicle." Tester Zach called it "a big leap in capability and feature set from version 12.6."

- **FSD four-month at-fault-incident-free streak noted.** Per Cern Basher, FSD has gone approximately four months since February 2026 without an at-fault incident. Elon Musk separately confirmed that upcoming FSD releases will incorporate persistent parking memory — learning preferred spots at home, office, and school drop-off — calling destination parking "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD."

- **Grok natural-language vehicle control coming in ~3 months.** Elon replied on X that the ability to give conversational commands directly to FSD — directing drop-off location, remote parking — would arrive "in about 3 months or so."

- **NHTSA eliminates manual brake pedal requirement for fully autonomous vehicles.** The agency announced it will remove the mandate for hand- or foot-operated brake controls on vehicles designed never to have a human operator, while preserving performance-based stopping distance standards. This builds on a 2022 rule loosening that had already removed steering wheel and pedal requirements in principle.

- **UN ECE adopts first global technical regulation for autonomous driving.** The framework incorporates elements of the U.S. DOT's AV framework and is intended to streamline approvals across Europe and participating countries. Commentators caution Europe has a history of layering additional complexity onto global frameworks, and Tesla's political profile in Europe adds further uncertainty.

- **Texas crash under NTSB and NHTSA investigation; Tesla disputes FSD involvement.** A woman was killed when a vehicle struck her house. Tesla and Ashok Elluswamy stated telemetry shows the driver had the accelerator floored throughout, reaching 73 mph, and the car was not operating under FSD as portrayed. The victim's family has filed suit against the driver and Tesla.

- **Waymo accumulated serious operational incidents while receiving limited scrutiny.** Cited incidents include: hitting a child; illegally passing school buses; blocking first responders during a mass shooting; six vehicles entering active Texas freeway construction zones; seven vehicles entering California construction lanes — leading Waymo to voluntarily restrict its entire fleet from freeway and highway operations. NHTSA recalled approximately 3,800 Waymo vehicles for driving into flooded roads. Waymo also suspended service in Atlanta, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and Nashville.

## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab first responder guide officially released.** Tesla distributed the guide to police, fire departments, and city officials, confirming the production Cybercab operates as a SAE Level 4 robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals. Key specs: 10 airbags, active hood for pedestrian protection, exterior microphones and speakers for two-way communication, 400V and 48V dual-voltage battery system, operates on all public and private roadways at all times of day and in light-to-moderate precipitation. In extreme weather, the vehicle pulls over and notifies Tesla's robotaxi operations team. Vehicles equipped with steering wheels and pedals are designated engineering or test units operating at Level 2.

- **Lars Moravy confirms Cybercab production on schedule and teases July 7 Giga Texas announcement.** In a preview of an exclusive interview (full version due June 30), Moravy stated the factory automation ramp "went more smoothly than expected," crediting accumulated launch experience. He volunteered unprompted that on Tuesday, July 7, 2026, there will be "cool news about things happening on the campus of Giga Texas… as part of the scaling effort." Moravy also reportedly said "people have no idea what the volume of this thing is going to be." Bhakdi, who also played the clip, cautions the July 7 news could be a factory expansion update rather than a commercial launch.

- **Hundreds of Cybercabs staged at previously unknown Fremont factory lot.** Drone footage from John of Tesla Own Silicon Valley revealed a newly identified lot containing hundreds of Cybercabs and Model Ys apparently staged for launch — a lot not previously publicly observed.

- **102 Cybercabs spotted in open lot at Giga Texas — described as a record.** Per Cern Basher, this is up from prior counts of approximately 20, 30, and then 50 units. One host with manufacturing experience interprets the outward staging movement as a signal that Tesla has passed a meaningful early-reliability validation threshold.

- **~80 Cybercabs now present in Houston as a test fleet.** Per Sawyer Merritt and corroborated by independent observer SE Robinson's count of 78 units, with additional vehicles out on active test runs at the time of the count. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Houston has 11 active supervised robotaxis — the bulk of the staged vehicles are in a pre-revenue testing phase.

- **Over 70 Cybercabs reported in Dallas lot.** Sawyer Merritt reported this figure; Bhakdi cited it alongside Houston as evidence of multi-city inventory accumulation ahead of a deployment event.

- **Texas robotaxi fleet reaches 84 registered Model Ys.** Texas DMV records show 15 new 2026 Model Y vehicles were added, bringing the Texas total to 84. The host of brighterwithherbert argues continued Model Y additions alongside Cybercab production signals the program is operationally ready.

- **Cybercabs now being fitted with service decals at the factory.** Per Cern Basher, Cybercabs coming off the line are receiving Tesla robotaxi decals on sides and back — interpreted as preparation for revenue-service vehicles rather than internal test units.

- **Cybercabs spotted in New Zealand.** Multiple units were reportedly observed, attributed to cold-weather and left-hand-traffic testing for FSD.

- **Tesla hiring for robotaxi service operations in Amsterdam.** A vehicle software engineer internship role posted in Amsterdam focuses on robotaxi and service operations, signaling European localization work is underway.

- **Texas officially integrated Cybercab first-responder interaction plans into the Department of Public Safety page.** Per Bhakdi, this mirrors similar documentation already filed for Arizona and is described as a necessary regulatory precondition for commercial launch.

- **Q2 city-expansion deadline is upon Tesla.** Tesla had previously stated robotaxi service would expand to additional cities before end of Q2 2026. As of June 29, commentators note the deadline has arrived and that staging activity across multiple cities is circumstantial evidence the timeline remains intact.

- **Zoox unveiled refined robotaxi with a target of up to 100 units per week.** The redesigned vehicle features elevator-style sliding doors, face-to-face bench seating, no steering wheel or pedals, and improved ergonomics, to be produced at Hayward. Larry Goldberg dismissed 100 units per week as "not a business," contrasting with Tesla's stated Cybercab ambition of approximately one unit every two minutes (~30/hour). Zoox's self-certification is also capped at approximately 2,500–2,600 vehicles.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Morgan Stanley raised Q2 2026 Tesla delivery estimate to ~413,000 vehicles**, up from ~373,000, citing stronger-than-expected sales in Europe (up ~100% year-over-year per Bhakdi) and China. The consensus Q2 delivery figure across analysts stands at approximately 406,000, roughly 50,000 above Q1. Bhakdi attributes the day's stock move primarily to this upgrade and warns of a potential "sell the news" dynamic once the official print arrives.

- **Tesla stock surged approximately 8–9% on June 29**, reaching around $412–$413. The NASDAQ was up approximately 2–2.8% on the day, with the excess attributed to the Morgan Stanley delivery upgrade.

- **Tesla topped the Cars.com American-Made Index again**, with Model 3 and Model Y taking first and second spots.

- **Lucid cut ~18% of its workforce**, saving approximately $158 million per year. The COO role was eliminated. The departing COO acknowledged Lucid cannot build an autonomous vehicle stack in-house.

- **VW faces potential job cuts doubling to 100,000** and plans to close at least four factories — Zwickau, Emden, Hanover, and Neckarsulm — as current EV model runs end, affecting the ID.3, ID.4, ID.5, and SEAT/Cupra models.

- **BYD launched the Great Tang flagship SUV** at approximately $35,000 in China, securing over 150,000 pre-orders. Key specs: up to 135 kWh battery, ~590 miles CLTC range, 10C battery charging 10–70% in five minutes, 1,000-volt architecture, megawatt charging capability. BYD already has 6,600 megawatt chargers in China.

- **GM is considering dropping LFP batteries** from its future EV portfolio due to China's export restrictions on manufacturing equipment for latest-generation cells, pivoting toward lithium manganese-rich (LMR) batteries — which do not yet exist at commercial scale. GM simultaneously earmarked $150 million of a $275 million Spring Hill investment for a future ICE Cadillac product.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla AI5 chip tape-out completed April 2026; volume ramp expected 2027.** Per farzad-fm, Tesla's in-house chip program — AI4, AI5, and subsequent generations — is framed as the computational backbone for FSD, Optimus, and SpaceX satellite inference, with the investment value being margin leverage by removing Nvidia from Tesla's cost structure rather than a standalone chip business.

- **Nvidia data center revenue reached approximately $75 billion in the quarter ending April 2026**, up ~92% year-over-year, with gross margins near 75% and market cap surpassing $5 trillion. The host at farzad-fm argues Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem — 18 years in development — is the actual moat, and that margin compression is a structural inevitability as cloud providers build proprietary inference chips.

- **Inference now accounts for roughly two-thirds of all AI compute globally** and is trending toward 70–80%, per farzad-fm. This shift — training as a one-time cost, inference as recurring — is framed as the central variable for evaluating chip company long-term positioning.

- **Nvidia struck a ~$20 billion deal on Christmas Eve 2025** for a perpetual, non-exclusive license to Groq's patent portfolio and software stack, hiring Jonathan Ross, Sunny Madra, and approximately 80% of Groq's staff — structured as a license rather than acquisition to avoid antitrust scrutiny.

- **S&P 500 historical June weakness is seasonal, not structural.** Per investingagainstthegrain, June has averaged only a 0.22% return since 1950. Year-to-date through late June 2026: Tesla −16%, Meta −16%, Amazon +2%, Nvidia +5%, Apple +6%, Google +8%.
