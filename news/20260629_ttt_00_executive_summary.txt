## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14 Lite is live on Hardware 3 as of June 29, hitting Ashok Elluswamy's self-imposed end-of-June deadline.** The build distills V14's driving behavior — reinforcement learning, improved merges, pedestrian handling, smoother steering, new parking/unparking/reversing, arrival options, and speed profiles — from Hardware 4 into Hardware 3's camera and compute configuration. Elon Musk acknowledged the release with "Nice work." Early testers describe it as comparable in feel to V14 on a Hardware 4 vehicle; one tester called it "a big leap in capability and feature set from version 12.6."

- **Hardware 3's constraints made this a significant engineering achievement.** HW3 carries only approximately 15% of the effective memory bandwidth of HW4. With an estimated 3–4 million HW3 vehicles in the fleet, the update is commercially important for both subscription revenue and existing FSD purchase obligations. Whether HW3 can ever reach unsupervised capability remains an open question.

- **NHTSA eliminated the manual brake pedal requirement for fully autonomous vehicles** — vehicles designed never to be operated by a human. All vehicles must still meet the same stopping distance criteria via alternative testing procedures. The rule builds on a 2022 framework that had already removed steering wheel and pedal requirements in principle.

- **The UN Economic Commission for Europe adopted the world's first global technical regulation for autonomous driving systems**, with a positive vote confirmed in June 2026. The framework incorporates elements of the U.S. DOT's AV framework and is intended to streamline AV approvals across Europe and participating countries. Observers caution that Europe has a history of layering additional complexity onto global frameworks, and Tesla's association with Elon Musk adds political uncertainty in European markets.

- **FSD has gone approximately four months since February without an at-fault incident**, per Cern Basher. Elon Musk separately stated that upcoming FSD releases will incorporate persistent memory of parking preferences — home, office, school drop-off — calling destination parking "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD." On Grok integration, Musk said natural-language vehicle control will arrive "in about 3 months or so."

- **NTSB has joined NHTSA in investigating a Texas crash** in which a vehicle struck a home, killing a woman inside. Tesla and Ashok Elluswamy stated telemetry shows the driver had the accelerator floored at 73 mph and FSD was not operating as alleged. The driver's family has filed suit against both the driver and Tesla.

- **Waymo voluntarily restricted its entire fleet from freeway and highway operations** following repeated incidents of vehicles driving into active construction zones — six vehicles failed to recognize ramp closure signs in Texas; seven drove between cones in California. NHTSA separately recalled approximately 3,800 Waymo vehicles after they drove into flooded roads. Waymo has also suspended service in Atlanta, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and Nashville.

## Robotaxi

- **Lars Moravy confirmed Cybercab production is on schedule** and said factory automation ramp went more smoothly than expected, crediting experience from prior vehicle launches. Unprompted, he teased that on **July 7, 2026**, there will be "cool news about things happening on the campus of Giga Texas" related to the scaling effort. He also said "people have no idea what the volume of this thing is going to be." The full Moravy interview publishes June 30.

- **Large-scale Cybercab staging is underway across multiple sites.** A drone survey of Fremont found a previously unobserved lot containing hundreds of Cybercabs and Model Ys staged for launch. At Giga Texas, 102 Cybercabs were counted in the open lot — a record, up from prior counts of ~20, ~30, and ~50. Per Sawyer Merritt and multiple observers, approximately 70–80 Cybercabs are present in a Dallas lot and a similar count in Houston. Joe Tegtmeyer footage shows units rolling off the Giga Texas line daily.

- **Cybercabs exiting the factory are now being fitted with Tesla robotaxi service decals**, which observers interpret as preparation for revenue-service vehicles rather than internal test units. The Cybercab first responder guide — Tesla's first official such document, distributed to police, fire departments, and city officials — confirms production Cybercabs operate as SAE Level 4 with no steering wheel or pedals. Confirmed specs: 10 airbags, active hood for pedestrian protection, exterior two-way audio, dual 400V/48V battery system, two first-responder emergency loops, and autonomous operation capability on all public and private roadways, day and night, in light-to-moderate precipitation.

- **Texas DMV records show 15 new 2026 Model Ys added to Tesla's registered robotaxi fleet**, bringing the Texas total to 84. Texas has also officially integrated connected autonomous vehicle first-responder interaction plans into the Texas Department of Public Safety page, mirroring filings already in place for Arizona — a regulatory prerequisite observers frame as a meaningful pre-launch signal.

- **Tesla posted a vehicle software engineer internship in Amsterdam focused on robotaxi service operations**, signaling localization work for European market expansion.

- **The Q2 2026 deadline for robotaxi expansion to additional cities is effectively upon Tesla** as of June 29. Multiple commentators note that decal application, large-scale vehicle staging, and distributed fleet movement are circumstantially consistent with Tesla remaining on that timeline, though no formal launch announcement has been made.

- **Zoox unveiled a refined robotaxi vehicle targeting up to 100 units per week** at its Hayward facility, with elevator-style doors, face-to-face seating, and no steering wheel or pedals. Commentators note Zoox's self-certification volume cap of approximately 2,500–2,600 vehicles severely constrains its economics, and contrast this with Tesla's stated ambition of producing one Cybercab roughly every two minutes.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Lars Moravy gave a detailed update on Optimus production line status** in the exclusive interview previewed by brighterwithherbert, including the level of automation currently in place and whether the approach follows a serial modular manufacturing model. Jeff Lutz, who conducted the interview, characterized Moravy's answer — particularly the specific stage of automation described — as signaling Tesla is "getting very close" on the Optimus production line. ## Electric Vehicles

- **Morgan Stanley raised its Q2 2026 Tesla delivery estimate from ~373,000 to ~413,000 vehicles**, citing stronger-than-expected sales trends in Europe (reportedly up ~100% year-over-year) and China. Per Jo Bhakdi, Morgan Stanley's institutional distribution reach translated the upgrade directly into buy orders, driving much of the day's move. The analyst consensus for Q2 2026 deliveries stands at approximately 406,000 — roughly 50,000 above Q1 2026.

- **Tesla stock closed up approximately 8–9% on June 29**, reaching around $412–$413. Bhakdi cautions a "sell the news" dynamic is possible once official delivery numbers are released, as the upgrade is largely front-run. Year-to-date, Tesla remains down approximately 15–16%.

- **Tesla again topped the Cars.com American-Made Index**, with Model 3 and Model Y taking the top two spots.

- **Tesla announced it will launch vehicle sales in Uruguay**, making it the third South American country where Tesla vehicles are available. No launch date, models, or pricing were specified.

- **Lucid announced layoffs of approximately 18% of its workforce**, expected to save $158 million per year, and eliminated the COO role. The departing COO acknowledged Lucid cannot build an autonomous vehicle stack in-house and described a partnership with Uber structured around approximately 35,000 vehicles including 10,000 Gravity SUVs.

- **VW faces potential cuts of up to 100,000 jobs** — double earlier estimates — and plans to close at least four factories (Zwickau, Emden, Hanover, Neckarsulm) as current model runs end. Affected models include the ID.3, ID.4, ID.5, and several SEAT/Cupra vehicles.

- **GM is reportedly considering dropping LFP batteries** from its future EV portfolio, citing China's export restrictions on manufacturing equipment for the latest-generation cells. The alternative under consideration is lithium manganese-rich (LMR) chemistry, which does not yet exist at commercial scale. GM simultaneously earmarked $150 million of a $275 million Spring Hill investment for a future ICE Cadillac product.

- **BYD launched the Great Tang full-size flagship SUV** at approximately $35,000 in China, securing over 150,000 pre-orders. Key specs: up to 130–135 kWh battery, ~590 miles CLTC range, 10C battery capable of charging 10–70% in five minutes, 1,000-volt architecture, megawatt charging. BYD has 6,600 megawatt chargers already installed in China.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla's AI 5 chip completed tape-out in April 2026** and is expected to ramp to meaningful volume in 2027. The chip is being developed exclusively for internal use across FSD, Optimus, and SpaceX applications. Success would give Tesla margin leverage by removing Nvidia from its cost structure; the program's value is framed as derivative of the FSD and Optimus thesis rather than a standalone business.

- **Nvidia's data center business generated approximately $75 billion in revenue** in the quarter ending April 2026, up ~92% year-over-year, with gross margins near ~75%. Its market cap surpassed $5 trillion in 2026. Inference now accounts for roughly two-thirds of all AI compute globally and is growing toward 70–80%, shifting the competitive battleground away from Nvidia's training stronghold.

- **Xpeng's CEO announced XNGP 2.0 is going global, including Europe.** One commentator who claims to have been the first Western journalist to drive XNGP 2.0 assessed it as "at least equivalent to a late version of FSD version 12" at the time of the test, with likely further improvement since.
