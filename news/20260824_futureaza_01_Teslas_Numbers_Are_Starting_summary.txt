Two Tesla-focused analysts (the host and James Stephenson) run through Tesla's business lines to argue the underlying numbers are trending positively despite last year's factory retooling disruption, covering vehicle sales, FSD subscription growth and margins, robotaxi and Semi ramps, Megapack, solar, insurance, the loss of ZEV credits, rising capex, and the long-term bets on Optimus and Terafab.

## Financials
1. **Revenue pacing ahead of prior record year:** Stephenson said Tesla's year-to-date revenue through Q2 this year already exceeds the equivalent period in the company's previous record year (cited as 2023), despite last year's factory retooling slowdown; he said this supports his forecast for another full-year revenue record in 2026.
2. **ZEV/regulatory credit revenue collapsing but not existential:** Stephenson estimates regulatory credit revenue, once roughly $400,000/quarter with heavy volatility (correcting himself to clarify he meant hundreds of millions, not thousands), is now modeled at around $100 million — roughly a quarter of prior levels — due to expired U.S. tax credit programs. Both speakers argued Tesla's profitability was never structurally dependent on these credits, pushing back on long-standing short-seller claims (naming Gordon Johnson) that Tesla was "structurally unprofitable" without them.
3. **CapEx increase was pre-telegraphed:** Both speakers noted Wall Street reacted with surprise to increased Tesla capital spending, arguing the company had signaled this spending increase roughly four quarters in advance; they framed rising capex as an investment in future growth (new factories, Optimus, Terafab) rather than a warning sign.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Q2 delivery strength:** The host cited a quarterly delivery figure of 358 (thousand, implied) for the most recent Q2, describing it as one of the strongest Q2 results on record.
2. **10 millionth vehicle produced:** Tesla produced its 10 millionth vehicle around July 27–29, though the exact date wasn't disclosed by the company; the host and Cern Basher had an informal bet on the exact timing that went unresolved.
3. **Solar roof pricing remains a weak point:** Stephenson said he personally got a Tesla solar roof quote comparable to what he paid for his house, calling it uncompetitive; he noted Tesla is expanding traditional solar panel manufacturing instead, aiming to undercut Chinese panel imports once tariffs and shipping costs are factored in.
4. **Tesla Insurance remains a minor business:** Both speakers agreed Tesla Insurance has not scaled to all 50 states and has never been high-margin, comparing it unfavorably to insurance-focused competitor Lemonade; neither expects it to be a meaningful revenue or profit driver.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscription growth is accelerating:** Stephenson cited roughly 170,000 net-new paying FSD subscribers added in Q1, with continued growth into Q2, emphasizing these are customers paying ongoing $99/month fees by choice rather than trial or bundled users. He and the host argued the marginal cost of each subscription is near zero (software already resides on the car; primary cost is payment processing/credit card fees), making FSD revenue growth almost pure margin flow-through to the bottom line.
2. **FSD word-of-mouth growth analogized to a "ChatGPT moment":** The host pointed to rising Google search interest in Tesla FSD and viral social media clips (TikTok, Instagram) of hands-off driving as evidence of organic demand growth, and cited a viewer claim that ~1–2% of the entire car market's purchases are now FSD-driven — which the host estimated would equate to roughly 140,000 vehicles if it were 1% of the total U.S. market.
3. **Ashok Elluswamy's FSD cited as outperforming robotaxi rivals in complex environments:** The host referenced videos from a third party (Omar) showing FSD navigating dense European urban environments in Amsterdam with heavy pedestrian/bike traffic, claiming it outperformed Waymo and Zoox, which he said would "halt and wait" in such conditions.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas driverless robotaxi registrations growing:** Stephenson said that when Texas's law changed at the end of May, only about 33 vehicles were registered as driverless robotaxis in the state; he said that figure has since grown to over 100. He noted Florida has also been added, while California vehicles still require a human safety monitor in the driver's seat under current law, so they aren't counted as driverless.
2. **Debate over "double digit growth" framing:** The host and Stephenson recounted an X debate over whether growth from 33 to 43 units (10 units) counts as "double-digit growth"; Stephenson affirmed it does by percentage-growth definition, contrasting with an unnamed commenter who argued it required a 10x increase.

## Electric Vehicles (Tesla Semi)
1. **Tesla Semi ramp aided by California EV credits:** Stephenson noted two California EV tractor credits worth $125,000 each (a combined $250,000) could reduce the effective cost of a Tesla Semi — cited around $300,000 list — down to roughly $50,000 for qualifying buyers, which he expects to drive strong sales. He noted a standard-range version is priced at $240,000 and speculated a cheaper, shorter-range "port range" version could follow.
2. **Competing diesel-replacement eCascadia struggling:** Stephenson said Daimler's eCascadia electric semi line is shrinking from roughly 1,000 units last year to a pace of about 100 units this year, partly due to pricing in the $400,000–$500,000 range versus the roughly $300,000 breakeven sweet spot he says Tesla is targeting with the Semi.
3. **Semi downtime data favorable in early study:** The host cited a Canadian study comparing Tesla Semi downtime to legacy trucks (some years or decades old); despite the comparison being skewed toward older fleet vehicles, the Tesla Semi reportedly had less downtime and returned to service faster.

## Energy
1. **Megapack production expanding with a third U.S. factory:** The host said Lathrop is at capacity and Shanghai has likely also reached capacity (though reporting lag obscures this), with a third Megapack factory coming online near Katy, Texas (described elsewhere in the discussion as tied to Berkshire). Stephenson pushed back on past X commentators who predicted 60–80% Megapack margins, calling Megapack a commodity-like product where Tesla lacks strong pricing power; current margins were described as in the 20–30% range despite falling prices, with growth expected to come from market expansion (including data center demand) rather than margin expansion.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus and Terafab framed as high-risk, high-reward bets:** Both speakers characterized large-scale humanoid robot production (Optimus) and the Terafab chip venture (noted as being pursued with Intel's help) as genuinely uncertain, expensive new frontiers whose payoff is unclear today, distinguishing them from lower-uncertainty growth areas like FSD, robotaxi, and Cybercab.
