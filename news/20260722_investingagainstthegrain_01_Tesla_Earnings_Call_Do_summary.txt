This is a pre-show, live coverage, and post-show analysis of Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call by a panel of retail analysts (James/Invest Answers, Alexandra Merz, Nick Gibbs, Jeff Lutz, and later Cern Basher), plus the actual earnings call transcript featuring Elon Musk, CFO commentary, Ashok Elluswamy, and Lars Moravy. The core storylines were weak EPS and margins overshadowed by record revenue, a smaller-than-feared free cash flow miss, a muted robotaxi/Cybercab update that nonetheless included new disclosures (V15 software, 10%/week mile growth), and a notable moment where Musk fielded a Tesla-SpaceX merger question and passed it to general counsel rather than deflecting outright.

## Financials
1. **Revenue record but EPS/margin miss:** Tesla posted trailing-12-month revenue over $100 billion for the first time, with quarterly revenue around $28.2 billion (up ~26% year-over-year), but GAAP diluted EPS of $0.32 came in well below expectations, and automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits fell sequentially from 19.2% to 16.3% (CFO attributed most of the sequential decline to non-repeating warranty/tariff benefits from the prior quarter, saying margins would have been roughly flat excluding those items).
2. **Free cash flow negative but better than feared:** Free cash flow was negative roughly $1.1 billion, much smaller than the panel's expectations (estimates ranged from negative $3–5 billion), aided by operating cash flow of just under $5 billion. Capex roughly doubled sequentially to about $5.7–5.8 billion; management reaffirmed full-year capex guidance of "more than $25 billion" and said capex will keep growing over the next two to three years, and disclosed the company is securing debt facilities allowing borrowing of up to $30 billion to help fund investment.
3. **SpaceX stake swings hit net income:** A $1 billion unrealized mark-to-market gain on Tesla's SpaceX equity stake boosted net income, but was largely offset by roughly $300 million in FX losses and about $100 million in Bitcoin losses; panelists noted that without the SpaceX gain, EPS would have been far lower (one estimate: around $0.10 versus the reported $0.32).
4. **Regulatory credits shrank sharply:** Regulatory credit revenue fell to about $146 million from roughly $380–440 million previously, removing a cushion that had previously helped margins.
5. **Energy and services margins moved in opposite directions:** Energy storage deployments grew 53% sequentially to 13.5 GWh (second-largest quarter), but energy gross margin fell from 39.5% to 20.4%, driven by a roughly $240 million warranty true-up tied to legacy vendor cell issues and non-repeating tariff benefits; management said energy margins should normalize in the "mid to low 20%" range long-term. Services and other margin hit an all-time high of 14.1%, with quarterly services revenue over $4.5 billion.

## Robotaxi
1. **Ashok Elluswamy detailed robotaxi safety and scaling data:** Ashok stated the robotaxi program has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised miles across six cities in two states with zero notable safety incidents, and said the fleet has been growing at double-digit week-over-week compounding growth in unsupervised miles, which management expects to continue for the rest of the year. Panelist Cern Basher calculated this compounding rate would imply roughly 142x annual mile growth if sustained, and panelists noted management did not specify vehicle counts, only mile growth.
2. **Cybercab production underway but no rollout timeline given:** The earnings deck confirmed Cybercab (referred to as "cyber cab" in production language) began production at Giga Texas, with engineering test drives on public roads starting in Q2 and employee rides in Cybercabs now beginning at Giga Texas; management said manufacturing targets are being aligned to match projected unsupervised-mile growth rates, but gave no explicit date for public robotaxi deployment of Cybercab.
3. **City-by-city expansion strategy explained:** Multiple executives (Ashok, Lars Moravy, and others) said expanding across many cities rather than concentrating volume in one city is deliberate — to validate that the software generalizes without heavy per-city tuning, to work through city/state-specific regulatory requirements, and to accumulate Cybercab-specific driving data (since it's a new chassis lacking the fleet history of Model 3/Y). They said time-to-launch-a-new-city is trending toward zero, eventually enabling state-wide rather than city-by-city operation.
4. **V15 software running in early form in the robotaxi fleet:** Ashok said the currently operating robotaxi fleet runs early versions of "V15" FSD software, with roughly 40% of seven planned major improvement tracks already merged and running, part of what the panel called the "march of nines" reliability approach where vehicle growth is throttled to keep incident rates near zero.
5. **Panel split on communication quality:** Several panelists (Nick Gibbs, Jeff Lutz) argued Tesla failed to give shareholders clear KPIs or timelines despite having missed prior robotaxi guidance (e.g., "50% of US population by year end," seven-city target) three quarters running, while others (Alexandra Merz, James) argued the Ashok segment provided meaningful incremental data (mile counts, growth rate, V15 progress) that should be read as bullish breadcrumbs.
6. **Canonical fleet data note:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active Robotaxi fleet stood at 773 vehicles (Bay Area 634, Austin 105, Dallas 18, Houston 16) as of the video's date, with the unsupervised fleet last recorded at 39 vehicles (stale since May).

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production line under construction, no new hard dates:** Musk confirmed the Fremont factory line (built where the Model S/X line was decommissioned) is under construction, calling the production-scaling challenge "the hardest product to scale manufacturing" Tesla has ever attempted due to the lack of an existing supply chain; he said the ramp will start "quite flat and long" before scaling.
2. **Optimus 4 targeted for far higher volume and Austin build:** Musk said Optimus 4 (built in Austin) is targeted for roughly 10 million units per year, an "order of magnitude" more than Optimus 3's roughly 1 million/year aspiration, and will be much more vertically integrated in its electronics/PCB production, with the caveat that scaling is "insanely difficult."
3. **Data strategy relies on human and bot-collected video:** Ashok said Optimus training leverages factory worker observation, a dedicated data collection team for high-quality demonstrations, broad internet video data, and eventually a reinforcement-learning "Optimus Academy" flywheel where bots learn from their own attempts; one panelist (Nick) speculated Tesla may eventually use AI glasses/wearables to crowdsource training video, though this was framed as his own theory, not company statement.
4. **Digital Optimus partnership with SpaceX's Grok:** Musk described "Digital Optimus" — an AI system for generalized computer/touchscreen use — built using the same "photons in, controls out" approach as FSD, done in partnership with SpaceX's Grok model, which acts as a manager assigning tasks to Digital Optimus.
5. **Supplier investment in Optimus components confirmed:** Musk and Tesla execs said Samsung (Texas) and TSMC (Arizona) are investing tens of billions in AI compute capacity supporting Optimus/robotaxi, Panasonic has invested in battery cell capacity, and Micron is providing a "very significant" memory allocation on favorable terms — memory demand tied partly to Optimus, which panelists noted requires roughly 4x the memory of a car.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscription/attach metrics highlighted as a bright spot:** Tesla reported over 55% of Q2 North America deliveries included an FSD subscription at delivery, and total paid FSD customers (purchases plus subscriptions) reached nearly 1.5 million globally, up 56% year-over-year, with 55% of that total being upfront purchases and 45% subscriptions. Cern Basher's independent analysis pegged roughly 930,000 FSD purchasers and 550,000 subscribers, a roughly 200,000 quarter-over-quarter increase, and calculated daily FSD miles per subscriber rose to 23 miles from just under 19 the prior quarter.
2. **Hardware upgrade path and future AI chips discussed:** Musk said Tesla plans to eventually upgrade hardware for vehicles with cameras but below AI4-equivalent compute, likely to a "moderate improvement" AI4 chip reaching production around mid-next-year, and separately to AI5 (targeted for volume production around next year, initially going into Optimus); he expressed strong confidence in the in-development AI6 chip, calling it potentially "the best edge computing chip in the world."
3. **Tesla Semi autonomy prioritized for later:** Musk said full self-driving for the Tesla Semi is expected around the end of this year or early next year, deliberately deprioritized behind Model 3/Y and Cybercab to avoid distracting from the "march of nines" safety validation work on higher-volume vehicles.

## Energy
1. **Megapack tied to AI data center power smoothing:** Musk said SpaceX has purchased large numbers of Megapacks specifically to smooth power fluctuations during AI training runs, where power draw can drop by roughly 70% for 100 milliseconds, and argued batteries paired with solar could theoretically double total US energy output given the gap between total installed generation capacity (roughly 1.2–1.3 terawatts) and average usage (about half a terawatt).

## Market & Competition
1. **Regulatory credit phase-down and EV subsidy trends:** Panelist Jeff Lutz noted regulatory credits are being reduced across the industry (with Ford and GM "celebrating" the change), and multiple regions (US, China, EU) have pulled back EV subsidies, yet EV demand continues growing, which he framed as a natural transition away from subsidy dependence just as Tesla's higher-margin robotaxi business is set to ramp.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger question fielded, not dismissed:** When asked directly whether Tesla and SpaceX might combine, Musk cited growing collaboration/overlap (especially around the planned Terafab chip facility, Starlink integration in Cybercab, and Grok in vehicles) but said such decisions "can't be talked about... on an earnings call" and handed the question to General Counsel Brandon, who confirmed Tesla's existing investment and framework agreement with SpaceX but gave no new details. Panelist Alexandra Merz (who maintains a personal, self-described "stubborn" merger thesis timed to SpaceX's August 4 earnings call) called this handling significant and said she felt more validated but cautioned her theory is unconfirmed speculation.
2. **SpaceX share price decline complicates merger math:** Alexandra Merz noted SpaceX trading near $115–120 (down from a scenario where she'd previously expected ~$200) undermines her theorized ~4.2x multiplier merger scenario that would have implied a much higher Tesla valuation; she attributed part of the SpaceX price pressure to option-driven hedging by holders facing lockup expirations, per her own review of SpaceX's S-1 filing.
3. **Terafab location announcement coming as standalone event:** Musk said Tesla will announce a Terafab location "soon" and provide more details in a dedicated announcement rather than folding it into an earnings call, describing it as crucial to avoid being AI-chip constrained for scaling Optimus production (citing needs in memory, logic, and packaging).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Tesla-SpaceX AI compute buildout ("Megapod") described:** Musk described a "mega pod" design combining Tesla AI4 computers with x86 hardware in large, distributed boxes that can be placed anywhere, including at Tesla Superchargers (which he said have roughly 7 gigawatts of power capacity and growing), to provide distributed AI compute using aggregated electricity infrastructure.
