The article reports that Tesla's active Austin robotaxi count, per community tracker Robotaxi Tracker, briefly spiked above 100 unique vehicles after the September 3 Cybercab launch event, then fell back to just 8 active vehicles by September 22 — roughly the same level as when the service launched in June 2025.

## Robotaxi
1. **Post-launch spike collapses:** Following Tesla's September 3 Cybercab launch event in Austin, where influencers and supporters were invited for rides, the seven-day count of unique Tesla robotaxis seen carrying riders spiked past 100, up from a 20-to-40 range that had persisted for most of the year. By September 22, that figure had dropped back to 8 active vehicles, matching levels from the June 2025 launch.

2. **Registered fleet vs. active fleet gap:** Tesla's Texas DMV-registered autonomous vehicle fleet in Austin has grown to 476 VINs (409 Model Ys and 67 Cybercabs), up 285 in the last 30 days. However, trackers have matched only 265 of those to license plates spotted on the road, just 179 were seen at all in the last 30 days, and only 8 were seen carrying passengers in the last 7 days — indicating registration growth does not reflect actual operating fleet size.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the September 3 event was a marketing moment rather than evidence of a real product ramp, contending Tesla deliberately limits deployment scale (geo-fenced, low-speed, few vehicles at a time) due to safety/accident risk, and that the company is misleadingly presenting itself as leading the autonomous market despite actual ride-carrying activity being flat since June 2025.

Notable quotes and data:
- Active 7-day robotaxi count in Austin: 8 as of September 22 (per Robotaxi Tracker), down from a post-launch spike past 100 in early September.
- Registered AV fleet in Texas: 476 VINs (409 Model Ys, 67 Cybercabs), up 285 in 30 days.
- Of those, only 265 matched to spotted license plates; 179 seen in last 30 days; 8 seen carrying passengers in last 7 days.
