This video is a joint discussion between the host of FutureAzA and Herbert from Brighter, reacting to widespread media coverage blaming Tesla's Full Self-Driving system for a fatal crash in Texas. Their core argument is that the crash was caused by a driver pressing the accelerator pedal — not by FSD — and that journalists are irresponsibly amplifying unverified claims while ignoring Tesla's same-day rebuttal from Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy. The hosts situate this incident within a long pattern of FSD-related media misrepresentation that they argue actively harms public safety by discouraging adoption of technology they believe saves lives.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **The Texas crash was driver-caused, not FSD-caused:** The hosts describe footage of a Tesla traveling at approximately 70 mph in a 25 mph zone — roughly double to triple the speed limit — and going airborne before crashing through a house. They argue the accelerator pedal was pressed the entire time, including after the car hit a wall, which they say definitively rules out FSD as the cause. The host states that pressing the gas pedal too long or moving the steering wheel causes FSD to disengage, reverting control to the human driver.

2. **Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy responded with data the same day:** The hosts note that both Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy publicly rebutted the FSD narrative within the same day using telemetry data showing the pedal was depressed throughout the incident. Despite this, subsequent media articles — published hours after the rebuttal — continued to frame the story as an FSD failure without using the word "allegedly."

3. **FSD's known failure mode is caution, not acceleration:** The host argues that when FSD fails, it fails by stopping, hesitating, or slowing down — not by accelerating uncontrollably to 70 mph through a residential area. He frames the Texas incident as inconsistent with any documented FSD failure mode, comparing it to a computer blue-screening in an unrecognized way.

4. **Tesla FSD is claimed to be seven times safer than human drivers (supervised):** The hosts assert that Tesla's supervised FSD has been shown to be seven times safer than human drivers, and reference reporting — attributed generally to doctors writing in the New York Times and other outlets — suggesting that Waymo's autonomous driving data is so compelling that, were it a clinical trial, it would be unethical to continue a control group. The hosts use this to argue the media narrative actively delays life-saving technology.

5. **A pattern of debunked FSD crash claims:** The hosts cite more than ten prior cases where articles claimed Tesla's FSD caused crashes or deaths, all of which were subsequently found not to be caused by FSD. Examples mentioned include: a 2022 fatal crash where the driver was reportedly three times the legal limit; a San Francisco incident (from 2025 per the headline shown); and a Chinese case where a woman made brake-failure claims, lost a lawsuit, was fined, and was required to publicly apologize after protests at Tesla showrooms and car shows.

6. **January 2026 NHTSA robotaxi incident report misrepresented:** The hosts describe a NHTSA report from January 2026 citing five robotaxi incidents in one month, which media outlets used to claim Tesla's robotaxi was four times worse than human drivers. The hosts say not a single article disclosed what those five incidents actually were. Upon investigation, the hosts say all five turned out to be low-speed contacts — described as under 4 mph, occurring in parking lots, essentially just touching another vehicle while parking — and that none constituted meaningful crashes.

7. **Tesla's full robotaxi incident disclosure:** The hosts reference a disclosure by Tesla — described as occurring approximately one to two months before the video, so roughly April–May 2026 — in which Tesla released unredacted details of all 17 robotaxi incidents since June of the prior year (i.e., June 2025). The hosts say a review of all 17 showed Tesla's automated system was not at fault: incidents included cars hitting a parked Tesla (which Tesla is still required to report), a tire touching a curb, and the worst incidents being caused by human remote operators — which the hosts argue proves autonomous operation is safer than human remote control.

## Market & Competition

1. **Media bias in crash reporting by brand:** The hosts argue that when non-Tesla vehicles crash into homes or cause fatalities, articles do not mention the manufacturer. One host describes a user on X who posted multiple examples of cars crashing into houses where no brand was named. Another example given: a Reddit post blaming Tesla's Dog Mode for a child's death was actually, per the host's research, a Hyundai Sonata where a child was left in a running gas car that ran out of fuel.

2. **Anti-Elon sentiment driving coverage:** The hosts acknowledge that dislike of Elon Musk as a political figure is understandable, but argue that weaponizing crash narratives to harm him personally results in delaying life-saving technology — which they frame as a net harm to humanity regardless of the motivation.
