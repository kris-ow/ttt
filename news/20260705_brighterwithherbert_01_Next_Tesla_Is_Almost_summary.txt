This video, hosted by brighterwithherbert with guest investor Larry Goldberg, reflects on Tesla's 23-year milestones and debates the pace of its next growth phase across Cybercab/robotaxi expansion, Optimus, AI infrastructure, and energy, arguing each business is nearing (but not yet at) its "S-curve" inflection point.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Strong quarterly deliveries:** Tesla delivered over 480,000 vehicles last quarter, which the host called a "blowout," driven especially by a jump in Europe, with China also holding up and the US outlook uncertain beforehand.
2. **Model Y L expected to expand TAM:** Goldberg expects a new Model Y variant (referred to as the "Y L") to be announced soon and estimates it could expand Tesla's addressable market by roughly a third or more; he expects it to drive a significant US sales jump, building on strength already seen in China and Europe.
3. **Cybercab as a standalone platform, not a mass-market hatchback:** Citing a conversation with Tesla VP Lars Moravy, the host says Tesla views Cybercab as a platform "on its own" with potential volume "probably 2 million plus" (no official number given), rather than pursuing a smaller/cheaper hatchback model for volume, though Goldberg still sees a four-seat Cybercab variant as a potential future volume play, especially in Europe.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab/robotaxi ramp pushed later than expected:** Goldberg says he originally expected ramping to start earlier this year but now expects it to begin around August–September, citing regulatory (local and national), infrastructure, unproven production quality/quantity, and safety/PR scrutiny as remaining obstacles ("hills or even mountains").
2. **Training bottleneck seen as pickup/drop-off behavior, not vehicle form factor:** Goldberg believes the biggest impediment to Cybercab training data isn't the car's dynamics but handling pickup/drop-off logistics; the host relayed a personal experience with a driverless ride-hail vehicle mishandling hotel pickup, and both agreed Waymo has similar unresolved issues, with Goldberg doubting Waymo has the compute architecture to solve it despite extensive mileage.
3. **Cybercab automation reportedly ahead of expectations:** Per Lars Moravy (via the host), roughly 90% of Cybercab automation is already in place, and the vehicle had fewer problems than Moravy anticipated.
4. **Missed mid-year expansion target:** Tesla had said it would expand robotaxi service to seven cities by June 30 but is currently in three; the host speculates an announcement of expansion to additional cities (with small vehicle counts) could partially offset the miss, while Goldberg is skeptical Tesla tries to "redeem" missed commitments and suspects any imminent announcement is more likely the Model Y L than robotaxi expansion.
5. **Outlook: gradual scale into next year:** Goldberg expects steady robotaxi ramping through next year, calling it "anybody's guess" on timing, and cautions that Tesla-optimistic predictions have historically run ahead of reality.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production line automation still early:** Per Lars Moravy (via the host), Tesla brought in one automated production line from its German automation subsidiary (acquired years ago, one of Tesla's earliest acquisitions), taking about two weeks to get running, with more than 40 additional sublines planned — implying full Optimus line automation could take considerably longer than expected.
2. **Fremont is prototype, Texas is the real factory:** The current Optimus buildout in Fremont is described as a prototype factory; the full production factility is being built in Texas and is roughly a year from being fully operational, after which bots produced there will be tested.
3. **Historical pattern of delay:** Goldberg notes it took about five years from Tesla's "unboxed" manufacturing process announcement to actual implementation, reinforcing his view that bot production timelines will also stretch out.

## Energy
1. **Energy business expected to reaccelerate:** Goldberg expects Tesla's energy segment to pick up again due to some very large projects in the pipeline, though it will take time for that revenue to show up on the balance sheet.

## Financials
1. **FSD subscriptions seen as upside surprise:** Goldberg expects FSD subscription revenue to surprise to the upside and continue growing, citing increased public acceptance of Full Self-Driving in general conversation.
2. **AI5 and Terafab timelines pushed out:** Goldberg estimates AI5 is more than a year away and Terafab is roughly two to three years away.
3. **Macrohard flagged as a "dark horse" revenue opportunity:** Goldberg raises Macrohard — a joint Tesla/SpaceX project — as a potential new revenue stream, drawing a comparison to Anthropic's rapid revenue conversion and noting Cursor's distribution across roughly 70% of Fortune 500 companies could accelerate enterprise adoption. However, he believes most resulting revenue would likely accrue to SpaceX/xAI (hardware, Grok, Cursor) rather than Tesla, with Tesla's role limited to higher, enterprise-level layers of the stack; he does not expect meaningful revenue from Macrohard in the next 12 months and expects next year to be "a tough slug" for it.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo's pickup/drop-off experience called unresolved:** The host describes a personally disappointing Waymo pickup/drop-off experience in California, and Goldberg argues Waymo likely lacks the compute architecture to substantially improve this despite its scale of miles and rides.
