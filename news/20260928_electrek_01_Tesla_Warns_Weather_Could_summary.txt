Electrek reports that Tesla has warned invitees to its October 1 Roadster demo event that it may need to be rescheduled due to weather, since the outdoor event cannot be moved indoors.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Weather threatens Roadster event:** Tesla emailed guests invited to the October 1, 2026 Roadster demo saying the event "must be held outdoors" and "there is a possibility we may need to reschedule due to weather," with no backup date given. Open-Meteo forecast data for McGregor, Texas pulled Monday showed an 82% chance of precipitation with thunderstorms and wind gusts up to 32 mph on October 1; Friday's chance of rain was 75% with lower gusts (~13 mph), dropping to 42% chance of rain by Saturday.

2. **Nine years of delays:** Tesla unveiled the next-gen Roadster prototype in November 2017 with a promised 2020 production date; the timeline has slipped at least eight times since. The demo itself has been repeatedly pushed: a "flying" demo promised by end of 2025 became April 1, 2026, then "maybe in a month or so," then August, then October 1. Reservation holders put down $50,000 (standard) or $250,000 (Founders Series) deposits with no car delivered.

## SpaceX — Starship
3. **SpaceX thruster hardware central to demo:** As previously reported by Electrek, the centerpiece of the event is a hover demonstration using a SpaceX-developed cold gas thruster package (code-named A71) at SpaceX's rocket test site in McGregor, Texas. The car is expected to run remotely with no one inside, and spectators are to be kept several hundred yards back because the thrusters are loud enough to potentially damage eardrums — explaining why the event cannot be moved indoors.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this delay (the 12th in nine years) isn't Musk's fault since weather is uncontrollable, and postponing given the forecast would be the responsible choice. However, he notes it's telling that a car meant to be sold and driven needs a weather-dependent stunt with SpaceX hardware to be shown off, and questions whether the eventual event will reveal a production-intent car with final specs, price, and delivery date, or just another video that resets expectations again.
