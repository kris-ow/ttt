## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab now testing in Colorado**, marking the first confirmed deployment outside Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Sawyer Merritt notes Tesla has been "shipping a lot of them around" and expects Cybercab to "start to pop up in a lot of new places" — signaling active geographic stress-testing ahead of broader commercial rollout.
- **Rerating threshold per Munster: 5,000+ vehicles and 5–10% of ride-hailing.** He describes the current trajectory from ~39 unsupervised vehicles as "incrementally positive" but likely already priced in; a step-function re-valuation requires the average consumer to viscerally understand the opportunity at scale.
- **Waymo is running ~4 million miles/day vs. Tesla's ~80,000** — a roughly 50-to-1 ratio per Munster, who nonetheless argues Tesla holds the superior long-term cost structure. Waymo's last valuation was approximately $120–$150 billion earlier in 2026.
- **Cybercab pricing likely modest, not disruptive:** Munster expects Tesla to price robotaxi rides approximately 5–10% below Lyft/Uber rather than aggressively undercutting — consistent with Tesla's historical pattern of monetizing scale advantages rather than passing savings to consumers.
- **Bhakdi scenario: 10,000 robotaxis by end of 2026 would push Tesla toward $1,000.** He frames it as Tesla pulling itself up independently of broader market tailwinds.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.33 released today.** Bhakdi confirmed the release and noted the Mars Catalog gave it an "extremely positive" review. He describes the broader 14.3 cycle as a complete revamp of the stack with meaningfully higher iteration velocity going forward.
- **Cortex 2 now training both FSD and Optimus**, per brighterwithherbert: 250 MW came online approximately April 2026, targeting 500 MW by mid-2026, adding ~100,000 GPUs. Cortex 1 — which trained FSD v14 — started at 50,000 H100s and is scaling toward 100,000 H100s/H200s at ~130 MW.
- **Munster's personal FSD inflection:** Interventions dropped to near-zero over the past two months versus multiple per day six months prior. He estimates FSD-driven miles represent only ~0.15–0.2% of total U.S. light-vehicle miles driven as of Q1 2026, underscoring how early the penetration curve remains.
- **Elon Musk speaks at the Samson International Smart Mobility Summit on May 18** on "scaling smart mobility." Watch for any forward guidance on FSD, robotaxi timelines, or Optimus deployment.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Model Y first U.S. price increase in approximately two years.** New pricing: base RWD unchanged at $39,990; Premium RWD +$1,000 to $45,990; AWD +$1,000 to $49,990; Performance +$500 to $57,990. The increases signal sufficient order flow to support modest margin expansion.
- **Munster expects ~10% Tesla delivery growth in 2026**, with potential re-acceleration to 20–30% annually within five years as legacy automakers' EV investment cuts (~one-third reduction from peak commitments) compound into a structural competitive deficit.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus carries more optionality than robotaxi near-term, per Munster**, precisely because analyst models price in virtually nothing — any credible progress is upside. The true rerating catalyst will be a third-party commercial customer taking delivery at scale, not factory deployment announcements.
- **Market disbelieves the 1 million unit target:** Munster says very few investors believe the stated goal; even 50,000 units would not be challenged. He rates meaningful Optimus scale as 5–10 years out, adding "if you hear people like me saying 5 to 10 years, it means they have no idea."
- **Optimus training requires more compute than FSD**, per brighterwithherbert, due to higher-dimensional action and perception space — the stated reason Cortex 2 was built at the scale it was and is dedicated to both products simultaneously.

## Financials
- **Tesla raised 2026 capex guidance from >$20B to >$25B**; Munster calls the market's negative reaction a "headscratcher" given Tesla holds more than $30 billion in cash and is running roughly break-even on cash flow. He frames the spend as appropriate infrastructure investment for a paradigm shift.
- **Tesla holds ~9% of XAI and invested $2 billion**, per brighterwithherbert, giving shareholders indirect exposure to XAI's compute buildout — a valuation angle Munster says almost no analyst is pricing. Munster's near-term price target: $600 by end of 2027.
- **Bhakdi's targets:** ~$490 near-term all-time high, $700 possible by November 2026, $4,000 by 2030 (standalone Tesla). He was ~20% over-leveraged heading into Friday's ~4.75% Tesla selloff and warned of potential forced deriski Monday.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla dropped ~4.75% Friday vs. NASDAQ -1.54%**, with excess decline attributed to prior run-up rather than new fundamental news. Tesla was roughly flat for the week overall after strong earlier gains.
- **Bond yields are the primary macro risk:** The 10-year moved from 4.46% to 4.60% in one week; 30-year approaching 5%. Bhakdi flags Japanese government bond dynamics and potential yen carry-trade reversal as structural risks to U.S. equity flows.
- **Strait of Hormuz blockade driving inflation fears:** Over 20% of global oil transits the strait. EIA estimates losses peak at 10.8 million barrels/day in May; JP Morgan flags early June as when commercial inventories approach operational stress. UBS projects OECD inventories could near record lows of 7.6 billion barrels by end of May.
- **SpaceX IPO: June 12 on NASDAQ under ticker SPCX**, with pricing expected June 11. Listing target: $70–75 billion; Goldberg and Bhakdi both expect market cap near $2 trillion on day one. IPO is reportedly 4x oversubscribed after first pricing round, with second-round expectations of 12–20x.
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger: Munster assigns >50% odds within five years.** A combined entity would be ~$3 trillion today; a fully integrated sovereign AI company — controlling energy, chips, manufacturing, Starlink distribution, and Grok — could become the most valuable company in the world, with physical AI via Tesla as the one missing piece in SpaceX's current stack.
- **Starship V3 first flight: Wednesday May 20, 6:30 p.m. ET.** Bhakdi frames the key question as cost-per-kilogram to orbit: current ~$2,500/kg; the ~$200/kg threshold is where space-based data centers become ~60% cheaper over 10 years than terrestrial equivalents.
