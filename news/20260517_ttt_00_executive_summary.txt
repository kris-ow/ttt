## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab now testing in Colorado**, marking geographic expansion beyond the Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Per Sawyer Merritt, Tesla has been "shipping a lot of them around" and Cybercab is expected to "start to pop up in a lot of new places," suggesting active hardware stress-testing across diverse regulatory environments ahead of broader commercial rollout.
- **Rerating threshold framed at 5,000 vehicles and 5–10% ride-hailing share.** Gene Munster (Deepwater Asset Management) says reaching ~60 unsupervised vehicles to ~1,000 is "incrementally positive but likely already priced in." A true step-function rerating requires 5,000+ vehicles demonstrating that autonomous rides are a visible fraction of the market. At current Waymo dominance (~4M miles/day vs. Tesla's ~80,000/day), Munster describes the score as "50 to zip" — while still favoring Tesla's long-term cost structure.
- **10,000 robotaxis by end of 2026 cited as a $700–$1,000 stock catalyst** by Bhakdi, who frames it as a scenario where Tesla "pulls itself up by its own shoelaces" independent of any macro tailwind.
- **Cybercab pricing expected ~5–10% below Lyft/Uber**, per Munster, who draws a parallel to Tesla's EV history: despite enormous scale advantages, blended Model Y/3 ASP sits at $38,000–$42,000, well above the long-anticipated $25,000 vehicle. He expects Tesla to monetize scale rather than pass all savings to consumers.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.33 released.** Bhakdi confirms the release and notes the Mars Catalog rated it "extremely positive." He characterizes the broader FSD 14.3 branch as a complete stack revamp that will accelerate iteration speed — and argues the market broadly underestimates what is coming.
- **Cortex 2 came online at 250 MW in approximately April 2026**, with 500 MW targeted by mid-2026 and ~100,000 additional GPUs. Critically, Cortex 2 trains both FSD and Optimus — with the humanoid workload described as larger than FSD due to higher-dimensional perception and action spaces. Cortex 1 (50,000–100,000 H100/H200s, ~130 MW, online Q4 2024) already produced FSD v14 and is currently training unsupervised FSD.
- **Munster reports near-zero FSD interventions** over the past two months versus multiple per day six months prior. He highlights that FSD-driven miles represent only ~0.15%–0.2% of total US light vehicle miles driven (~3.3 trillion in 2025), underscoring the scale of the untapped addressable market.
- **FSD safety case:** Munster calculates that if Autopilot/FSD is ~7x safer than human driving (per Tesla's own data), the technology could prevent ~37,000 of the 43,000 US traffic fatalities recorded in 2024.
- **Legacy OEMs declined FSD licensing** when Musk reportedly reached out approximately one year ago, per Munster — a strategic error he believes leaves them in an increasingly difficult competitive position as autonomy compounds.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus carries more optionality than robotaxi near-term** because virtually no analyst has it in their financial models, per Munster. He notes that if Tesla shut Optimus down today, the stock would fall not on lost earnings but on what it signals about innovation health. He rates meaningful scale as genuinely 5–10 years away.
- **Third-party commercial customer sale remains the true rerating catalyst** for Optimus, per Munster and Loomis. Factory deployment announcements are insufficient — a credible external customer taking delivery at scale is what moves the stock. Munster cautions against "head fakes" from small pilot orders.
- **Elon Musk's 1-million-unit target for next year is not credible to the market**, per Munster. He notes that if Tesla announced 50,000 units, the market wouldn't object — expectations are that low. Internally, people close to Tesla confirm the company views Optimus as its most important long-term business, with physical labor (~50% of global GDP) as the addressable market.
- **Macrohard** — an XAI software project pairing Grok with a Tesla-built screen-watching agent — is described as a potential multi-trillion-dollar wedge if it works, but has experienced leadership turnover and a paused data annotation effort after researchers found model flaws. The host characterizes this as normal for frontier development.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Model Y price increases — first in approximately two years.** New structure: base RWD unchanged at $39,990; Premium RWD +$1,000 to $45,990; AWD +$1,000 to $49,990; Performance +$500 to $57,990. Price increases rather than cuts signal sufficient order flow and carry positive near-term margin implications.
- **Tesla delivery growth expected ~10% in 2026** (current consensus per Munster), with a scenario for re-acceleration to 20–30% within five years as electrification compounds and legacy OEM underinvestment in EVs (~one-third cut from peak commitments on average) creates a structural gap.

## Financials
- **Tesla capex guidance raised from >$20B to >$25B for calendar 2026.** Munster calls the market's negative reaction a "headscratcher" given Tesla holds >$30B in cash and is roughly break-even on cash flow. He argues near-term profitability concerns are irrelevant when building infrastructure for a paradigm shift.
- **Tesla owns ~9% of XAI and has invested ~$2B**, per the brighterwithherbert compute analysis. The host argues this stake — combined with Cortex clusters training FSD and Optimus — gives Tesla shareholders indirect exposure to the compute race that the market is not yet pricing. Current market pricing is characterized as "a car company that maybe is building a robot."

- **Pioneer Alpha had its worst week of 2026** (estimated ~-3% alpha vs. Tesla) driven by short puts at the $440 strike caught by Friday's macro-driven selloff.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla dropped ~4.75% Friday** vs. NASDAQ -1.54%; beta-implied decline was ~3%, with the excess attributed to the stock's large prior run-up. Tesla was roughly flat for the week overall.
- **Macro headwinds sharpening:** 10-year Treasury yield moved from 4.46% to 4.6% in one week; 30-year approaching 5%. Bhakdi flags the yen carry trade reversal risk and rising yields across German, UK, French, and Japanese bonds as structural threats to US equity positioning.
- **Strait of Hormuz disruption driving inflation risk.** Bhakdi cites EIA projecting losses peaking at 10.8M barrels/day in May, UBS flagging OECD oil inventories could hit near-record lows of 7.6B barrels by end of May, and JP Morgan warning commercial inventory stress by early June. 250M barrels were drawn from reserves in March–April 2026 alone.
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger assigned >50% probability within five years** by Munster, who estimates a combined entity at ~$3 trillion today. His thesis: SpaceX is becoming a sovereign AI company controlling the full stack from energy to satellite distribution to AI models, and Tesla's physical AI layer (Optimus, robotaxi) is the one structural gap. Bhakdi's merger modeling shows it is a net negative for Tesla shareholders in year one (~38% return vs. 50% standalone) but a significant long-term winner (~80–88% in years two and three vs. 60–66% standalone).
- **SpaceX IPO: ticker SPCX on NASDAQ, pricing June 11, first trade June 12**, targeting $70–75B listing valuation. Goldberg (via brighterwithherbert) expects market cap to reach or exceed $2 trillion on IPO day, with the book reportedly 4x oversubscribed in round one and projections of 12–20x in round two. Ron Baron is reportedly seeking another $1B allocation.
- **XAI's Colossus 2 is the world's first 1-gigawatt coherent AI training cluster**: 555,000 Nvidia GPUs purchased, $18B in hardware, targeting 1 million GPUs at a single site. Colossus 1 (~220,000 GPUs, ~300 MW) is fully rented to Anthropic — the same company Musk called "anti-western" roughly three months prior. His explanation: *"No one set off my evil detector."*
- **Starship V3 first flight: Wednesday May 20, 6:30 p.m. ET.** Bhakdi frames the central question not as whether Starship reaches orbit (it already does) but the cost curve — current ~$2,500/kg, with ~$200/kg as the inflection point at which space-based data centers become ~60% cheaper to operate over 10 years than terrestrial equivalents.
