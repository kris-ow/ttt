## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab now testing in Colorado**, marking the first confirmed deployment outside Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Per Sawyer Merritt, Tesla has been "shipping a lot of them around" and Cybercab is expected to "start to pop up in a lot of new places" — signaling active geographic stress-testing ahead of broader commercial rollout.
- **Rerating threshold, per Munster:** reaching ~60 unsupervised vehicles today is "incrementally positive" but likely already priced in. A genuine step-function re-valuation requires 5,000+ vehicles and autonomous rides representing 5–10% of the US ride-hailing market — at which point the average person viscerally grasps the opportunity.
- **Waymo vs. Tesla miles gap:** Munster estimates Waymo at ~4 million miles/day versus Tesla robotaxi at ~80,000 miles/day — roughly a 50-to-1 ratio. He nonetheless maintains Tesla holds the superior long-term cost structure.
- **Robotaxi pricing expectation:** Munster projects Cybercab fares approximately 5–10% cheaper than Lyft/Uber rather than a dramatic price slash, consistent with Tesla's historical pattern of monetizing scale rather than passing all savings to consumers.
- **Scale as stock catalyst:** Bhakdi argues that 10,000 robotaxis deployed by end of 2026 would push Tesla through $700 toward $1,000, independent of broader market tailwinds.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.33 released.** Bhakdi confirms the Mars Catalog rated it "extremely positive" and characterizes the broader 14.3 stack as a complete revamp that will support faster iteration going forward.
- **Cortex 2 is live at 250 MW** as of approximately April 2026, targeting 500 MW by mid-2026 with ~100,000 additional GPUs. Critically, Cortex 2 trains both FSD and Optimus — the first Tesla cluster dedicated to both workloads simultaneously.
- **Cortex 1 trains FSD including unsupervised FSD:** the cluster came online Q4 2024, starting at 50,000 H100s and scaling toward 100,000 H100s/H200s at ~130 MW growing toward 500 MW. Every Tesla fleet mile feeds into it.
- **Munster on FSD inflection:** interventions have dropped to "almost none" over the past two months versus multiple per day six months ago. He recommends Tesla's two-day test drive for skeptics and notes FSD miles are still only ~0.15–0.2% of total US light-vehicle miles driven as of Q1 2026 — the addressable market is nearly untapped.
- **Elon Musk speaks at the 2026 Samson International Smart Mobility Summit on May 18** (virtually, topic: "scaling smart mobility") — a potential source of forward guidance on autonomy and deployment timelines.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus as the largest compute workload in the Musk stack:** training Optimus requires more compute than FSD due to higher-dimensional action and perception space — the explicit rationale for Cortex 2's scale. The host at brighterwithherbert frames this as underappreciated by the market.
- **Munster: Optimus carries more optionality than robotaxi near-term** precisely because virtually no analyst has it in financial models. The true rerating event will not be factory deployment but a credible third-party commercial customer taking delivery at scale. He cautions against "head fakes" from small pilot announcements.
- **1 million unit target not credible to the market:** Munster says "very few people" believe Elon's stated goal. An announcement of 50,000 units would not disappoint. He rates meaningful scale as genuinely 5–10 years out — a range he acknowledges signals he has "no idea" on the exact timing.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Model Y price increase — first in approximately two years.** New US structure: base RWD unchanged at $39,990; Premium RWD +$1,000 to $45,990; AWD +$1,000 to $49,990; Performance +$500 to $57,990. A demand signal with positive near-term margin implications.
- **Tesla delivery growth consensus:** Munster expects ~10% growth in 2026, with potential for 20–30% annually within five years as legacy auto's reduced EV investment compounds into a structural competitive disadvantage.

## Financials
- **Tesla capex raised from >$20B to >$25B for calendar 2026.** Munster views the market's negative reaction as a "headscratcher" given Tesla holds >$30 billion in cash and is roughly breakeven on cash flow — arguing near-term profitability concerns are largely irrelevant when building paradigm-shifting infrastructure.
- **Tesla owns ~9% of XAI and has invested $2 billion** in the entity. The brighterwithherbert analysis argues Tesla shareholders have indirect exposure to the compute buildout (XAI/Colossus) and direct exposure via Cortex — but the market is still pricing Tesla as a car company with a robotaxi premium and almost no compute premium.
- **Bhakdi price targets:** ~$490 near-term (prior all-time high), $700 possible by November 2026, $4,000 by 2030 on a standalone basis. He estimates Tesla's 2029 market cap at ~$8 trillion (~$2,500–$3,000/share). Munster's anchor: $600 by end of 2027.
- **Bhakdi risk flag:** 30-year US bond yields approaching 5%; 10-year moved 14 bps in a single week. He identifies a potential yen carry-trade unwind as a structural risk to US equity flows. Tesla dropped ~4.75% Friday vs. NASDAQ's 1.54%.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger probability:** Munster assigns >50% odds within five years. His thesis: SpaceX is becoming a sovereign AI company (energy → chips → manufacturing → Starlink → Grok) with physical AI as the one gap Tesla fills. Combined entity would be ~$3 trillion today; fully integrated, potentially the most valuable company in the world.
- **SpaceX IPO:** SPCX on NASDAQ, pricing June 11, first trade June 12, listing target $70–75 billion. Early chatter suggests 4x oversubscribed after round one, with 12–20x projected for round two — implying ~$1 trillion in demand at the $75B target per Goldberg. He expects IPO-day market cap to reach or exceed $2 trillion.
- **XAI compute dominance thesis (brighterwithherbert):** Colossus 1 (220,000 GPUs, 300 MW) is fully rented to Anthropic. Colossus 2 is the world's first 1-gigawatt coherent AI training cluster — 555,000 GPUs purchased, $18B in hardware, targeting 1 million GPUs. "Coherent" means every GPU is networked near-zero latency into one unified training machine; most competitor clusters at scale are non-coherent. The host argues most investors are not pricing this into Tesla.
- **Starship V3 first flight scheduled May 20 at 6:30 p.m. ET.** Bhakdi frames the key question not as whether Starship works but as the cost curve: current ~$2,500/kg to orbit; ~$200/kg is the inflection point at which space-based data centers become ~60% cheaper over 10 years than terrestrial equivalents. Reliability (failure rate and its insurance cost implications) is flagged as equally important as reusability.
- **Strait of Hormuz disruption:** Iran's blockade — with 20%+ of global oil transiting the strait — is cited by Bhakdi as the root macro risk driving inflation fears and bond yield pressure. EIA estimates losses peaking at 10.8 million barrels/day in May; JP Morgan flags early June as when OECD commercial inventories reach operational stress.
