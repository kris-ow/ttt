Electrek's coverage today centers on Tesla's decision to end Cybertruck production, with a companion piece on price hikes that followed the announcement.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla to wind down Cybertruck production:** Fred Lambert reports that Elon Musk announced via X that Tesla will "wind down" Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027, citing a need for more Cybercab capacity, with no detail given on how long Tesla will sell remaining inventory or what happens to parts and service. Tesla doesn't break out Cybertruck deliveries, folding them into an "Other Models" category (with Model S, Model X, and Semi) that fell 48% year-over-year to 8,295 units in Q3 2026, the lowest since Q2 2021; the author estimates 7,000–8,000 of those were Cybertrucks, implying a pace of about 30,000 trucks a year against a planned 250,000-unit annual line and over 125,000 units of installed capacity per Tesla's shareholder letter. The author cites Cox Automotive estimates of 3,519 Cybertruck sales in Q1 2026 and 3,744 in Q2, down from 14,416 in Q3 2024, and S&P Global Mobility data showing SpaceX bought 1,279 Cybertrucks and Musk's companies bought 1,339 total in Q4 2025 (about 19% of US registrations that quarter). The author argues the "make room" rationale doesn't hold up given Giga Texas's over 10 million square feet of floor space and Cybercab's low registered volume (463 in Texas as of late October), and contends Tesla is ending the truck because it never sold near planned volumes, estimating Tesla may have lost "a couple of billions" on the program and comparing the framing to how Tesla described ending Model S/X production for Optimus space. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/06/tesla-ending-cybertruck-production-sales-10-percent-plan/)

2. **Tesla hikes Cybertruck prices after announcing the end of production:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla raised US Cybertruck prices by up to 10% and stopped taking orders for the base Dual Motor AWD trim, per Tesla's online configurator: Dual Motor AWD rose from $74,990 to $82,490 (no longer orderable), Premium AWD rose from $84,990 to $92,490, and Cyberbeast rose from $99,990 to $102,490. This is the second hike in under three months after an August increase, making the Premium AWD $12,500 (15.6%) more expensive than early August and pushing the orderable entry price up 32% in about ten weeks, against the 2019 unveiling's promised $39,900 starting price. The author notes Tesla previously discontinued a $59,990 "limited-time" dual-motor offer in February and a single-motor RWD version after 173 sales. The author's take: this reflects a wind-down strategy of maximizing revenue on remaining build slots and burning down parts inventory rather than pursuing volume, and argues the pricing history from $59,990 to $92,490 shows "a company that never figured out who the truck was for." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/06/tesla-raises-cybertruck-prices-after-ending-production/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert frames the Cybertruck's end as evidence of a failed, oversized bet rather than a capacity-driven pivot, and portrays the subsequent price hikes as an opportunistic effort to extract maximum revenue from a dying product line.
