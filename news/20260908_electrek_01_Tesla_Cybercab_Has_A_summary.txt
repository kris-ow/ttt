A Tesla Cybercab rider discovered and filmed a hidden manual driving mode using an on-screen virtual joystick, despite the vehicle having no steering wheel or pedals.

## Robotaxi
1. **Hidden virtual joystick discovered:** Matthew Skrzypczak posted a video on X on September 7 showing his Cybercab's touchscreen split into two panels: one side with a virtual joystick interface for manual driving (with prompts to close cabin doors, open doors, or call support), and the other showing a live surround-camera view, though the side cameras appeared frozen on an earlier frame from a Supercharger location. He said he had a feeling he wasn't supposed to see this but tried driving with it anyway. Tesla has never publicly shown this mode.
2. **Purpose inferred by the article:** The article states this appears to be Tesla's workaround for repositioning a Cybercab (e.g., in a depot or tight spot) given it has no physical controls.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **NHTSA audit context:** Tesla launched the Cybercab in Austin on September 3; that same day NHTSA opened an audit into how Tesla certified a vehicle lacking a steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors, questioning whether certain federal safety standards apply to a driverless car. The audit covers roughly 1,000 vehicles.
2. **Chair's past comment on controls:** In October, Tesla chair Robin Denholm said the car could get real controls if required, stating, "If we have to have a steering wheel, it can have a steering wheel and pedals." The article notes many Cybercab prototypes were seen with a steering wheel and pedals during testing.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the touchscreen joystick looks impractical and doesn't meaningfully address regulators' core question of how a human can take over a driverless car when needed. He contends Tesla removed the wheel and pedals to symbolically assert that full autonomy is here, yet the robotaxi service (running over a year with Model Ys using the same autonomous hardware/software as the Cybercab) has not scaled.
