This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur, discussing several major catalysts driving Tesla's stock higher over the previous five days. The conversation covers Tesla's stock trajectory, Elon Musk's invitation to join President Trump on a China trip, robotaxi fleet expansion, Waymo's recall and competitive weaknesses, and Giga Berlin's production expansion. The core thesis is that Tesla's stock recovery toward all-time highs is justified by fundamentals alone — with robotaxi and Optimus representing additional upside catalysts layered on top.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla stock up $42 (11%) in five days:** Tesla's stock rose over $45 in the five days preceding the video, with Jo Bhakdi arguing the move is not driven by any specific catalyst but rather a logical mean-reversion after an unjustified post-earnings selloff. Bhakdi contends that Tesla's fundamentals are at least as strong as they were when the stock hit its all-time high of $490 on December 16, 2025, and with the broader market also near all-time highs, there is a natural "magnet" pulling Tesla back toward that level.

2. **All-time high of $490 as base target, larger repricing contingent on robotaxi scale:** Bhakdi separates his analysis into layers: a baseline return to ~$490 independent of any robotaxi or Optimus catalyst, and a potential "great repricing" to somewhere between $600 and $1,000 once the market becomes convinced that robotaxi is scaling to multiple millions of vehicles. He argues the market will cross that threshold well before the fleet reaches 10,000 units, at which point a dramatic repricing could occur at any moment.

3. **Elon Musk invited to join Trump's China trip alongside major U.S. executives:** President Trump invited Elon Musk, Tim Cook (Apple), and representatives from Boeing, Goldman Sachs, Blackstone, BlackRock, Citigroup, Meta, General Electric, Cargill, Micron, Qualcomm, Visa, Alumina, and Coherent Corp on his trip to China. Bhakdi interprets the inclusion of top corporate leaders as a strong signal that the U.S.-China trade war is de-escalating behind the scenes and that deal announcements are imminent. Notably, Jensen Huang (Nvidia) was not invited, which Bhakdi reads as evidence that semiconductor export restrictions remain a sticking point.

4. **FSD approval in China signaled by owner's manual update:** The Chinese owner's manual has been updated, with the FSD supervised version now renamed "Tesla Assisted Driving." Tesla has also been hiring Autopilot engineers in China. Herbert suggests this points toward an imminent, expanded FSD launch in China, potentially accelerated by Elon Musk's presence on the China trip.

5. **Optimus supply chain dependency on China:** Bhakdi notes that major key components for Optimus come from China, making a de-escalation of trade tensions particularly important for Tesla shareholders. He frames the warming U.S.-China relationship as directly beneficial to Optimus manufacturing economics.

6. **Waymo loses $5–$6 billion per year, approximately $100 per ride:** Herbert states that Waymo is losing $5 to $6 billion annually, which translates to roughly $100 in losses per ride. He predicts Waymo will eventually announce plans to acquire a car factory and move to a vision-only system, abandoning lidar. Bhakdi argues Waymo's lidar dependency represents a fundamental architectural dead end — transitioning to vision-only would take roughly a year of rework — and that the earliest Waymo could solve its core technology problems is around 2028.

7. **Waymo recalls 3,800 robotaxis over flooded road software issue:** Waymo issued a recall of nearly 3,800 robotaxis (fifth and sixth generation automated driving systems) after a software issue caused vehicles to drive onto flooded roads. The recall reveals the true scale of the Waymo fleet. Waymo's response includes a software fix and narrowing its operating scope to exclude areas prone to flash flooding during intense rain. A separate NHTSA investigation involves a Waymo vehicle striking a child near an elementary school, resulting in minor injuries.

8. **AV Ride (Uber partner) under NHTSA investigation for 16 crashes:** AV Ride, which partners with Uber, is under NHTSA investigation for 16 self-driving crashes involving failures to properly change lanes, respond to vehicles in the same lane, and respond to stationary objects. Herbert notes these incidents occurred even with a safety monitor present in the driver's seat.

9. **Competitors structurally far behind Tesla on data, compute, and time:** Bhakdi argues that autonomous driving is not magic — it requires data, compute, time, and talent. When comparing companies on these metrics, he asserts that competitors outside of Waymo are at roughly 1% or less of Tesla's infrastructure stack, and that even Waymo does not have access to Google's full compute resources. He concludes there is effectively no meaningful competition for Tesla in the near term.

10. **Global auto market consolidation favors Tesla:** Bhakdi predicts that by 2027, the broader auto industry will see widespread bankruptcies among legacy OEMs (German, Japanese, American), with Tesla as the last major profitable player. He notes that in China, over 40% of all cars sold are now battery EVs, and if PHEVs are included, the figure exceeds 50%. He describes a clear EV inflection point in Europe, particularly among Scandinavian countries, benefiting Tesla as the market leader.

## Robotaxi

1. **39 unsupervised Tesla robotaxi vehicles across Austin, Houston, and Dallas:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, there are 39 unsupervised (driverless) Tesla robotaxi vehicles deployed across Austin, Houston, and Dallas. Herbert notes Tesla appears to be converting supervised vehicles to unsupervised and expects approximately 20 more conversions to fill out the next tier. He also notes the overall active fleet across all modes stands at 668 per RobotaxiTracker.com.

2. **64% of Austin robotaxi rides unsupervised in the last 7 days:** Over the seven days prior to the video, 64% of all Tesla robotaxi rides in Austin were unsupervised, signaling a meaningful operational transition. Herbert cautions that the total number of rides and vehicles remains small, characterizing the current phase as a test pilot.

3. **Cybercab production estimate: 300–600 units per month currently, targeting 1,800 as inflection point:** Jo Tegtmeyer spotted 37 new Cybercabs, and Herbert references a prior sighting of 40. Bhakdi estimates that if the ~30–40 Cybercabs visible in the parking lot cycle out even twice a week, minimum production is approximately 10 per day, implying 300 to 600 Cybercabs per month. Bhakdi states his personal inflection point for the thesis is 1,800 total produced Cybercabs, which he believes will be reached within roughly one month at current trajectory.

4. **Cybercab fleet projected to reach ~10,000 by end of 2026:** Bhakdi projects that at the current ramp rate, Tesla will be producing over 1,000 Cybercabs per month within three months, putting the cumulative fleet at approximately 10,000 by end of 2026. He frames this as nearly impossible for bears to dispute, arguing the only bear case is that Tesla builds the vehicles but never deploys them — a bet he considers very unlikely.

5. **Fleet deployment structure: ~1,000 Cybercabs plus ~100 Model Y's per city:** Herbert describes the expected deployment structure as approximately 1,000 Cybercabs per city supplemented by roughly 100 Model Y's per city to handle trips requiring more than two passengers (estimated at ~10% of rides). This is described as the current observable rollout pattern.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Giga Berlin hits 750,000 cars produced, planning 20% production increase:** Tesla's Giga Berlin plant announced it has produced 750,000 vehicles, with plant manager André stating the goal is to reach the first million cars as quickly as possible. Tesla is planning to scale production at Giga Berlin by 20%, which will require new hiring.

2. **$250 million investment to expand 4680 battery cell production at Giga Berlin:** Tesla announced a $250 million investment to expand 4680 battery cell production at Giga Berlin. The expansion will more than double existing capacity, with an annual production capacity target of 18 GWh — more than twice the previously stated 8 GWh target for the site. The expansion is expected to create over 1,500 new jobs.

3. **Affordable Cybertruck variant spotted at Giga Texas:** Joe Tegtmeyer observed what appears to be early production of a new $59,000 Cybertruck variant at Giga Texas, though he notes it is difficult to visually distinguish from the premium all-wheel drive version.
