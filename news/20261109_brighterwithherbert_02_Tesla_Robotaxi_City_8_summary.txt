This episode of a Tesla robotaxi-tracking show covers Tesla's expansion into an eighth robotaxi city (San Antonio), a major Austin service-area expansion, Tesla nearly closing the fleet-size gap with Waymo in Texas, Elon Musk's confirmation that Cybertruck production will wind down to free capacity for Cybercab, Saudi Arabia fast-tracking Tesla robotaxi approval, and groundbreaking on a new $10 billion Tesla solar factory. Host Herbert and guest Jeff Lutz (manufacturing/supply-chain consultant) frame the weekend as Tesla's most significant robotaxi expansion push yet, while debating the strategic logic and risks behind retiring Cybertruck for Cybercab.

## Robotaxi
1. **San Antonio becomes robotaxi city #8:** Tesla opened robotaxi rides in San Antonio at 5:00 a.m. Sunday, covering roughly 275 square miles on day one — larger than the initial launch zones of Austin or Houston — just six days after Fort Worth (city #7) launched. The host noted the geofence as the largest first-day zone Tesla has opened to date.
2. **Austin expands 62% to retake the size crown:** Austin's service area grew by about 170 square miles (62%) to roughly 442 square miles, once again making it Tesla's largest robotaxi zone; the expanded area appeared to cover more suburban/neighborhood coverage beyond the city center.
3. **Texas fleet nearly even with Waymo:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla added 38 Model Y robotaxis on November 8th, bringing its registered Texas robotaxi fleet to 1,282 (breakdown: 573 Cybercabs, remainder Model Ys/other), just three vehicles behind Waymo's 1,285 in the state. Jeff Lutz argued the growth-rate trajectory (slope of registrations over time) favors Tesla scaling past Waymo in the coming months, given Tesla's later start.
4. **Possible unannounced overnight testing in Austin:** A commentator (Ethan, per the host) reported sightings of apparently unsupervised Cybercabs operating between 2 a.m. and 6 a.m. in Austin, outside Tesla's posted 5 a.m.–2 a.m. service hours — suggesting undisclosed round-the-clock testing. Highway driving capability has also reportedly been added, which the host speculated could eventually let Tesla link robotaxi service across Dallas, San Antonio, Fort Worth, and Austin.
5. **Saudi Arabia fast-tracking Tesla robotaxi approval:** Per the host, Saudi Arabia's transport ministry has agreed a Cybercab testing plan for Q3 2027, with data export approval underway and supervised FSD planned as an on-ramp; a Saudi minister reportedly visited Tesla in the US to secure deployment plans, as Saudi Arabia aims to be an early global robotaxi market and reach the top 15 in AI by 2030.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production line to wind down for Cybercab:** Elon Musk said Cybertruck's order backlog currently exceeds what Tesla can build before the line is dismantled for Cybercab expansion around June/July of next year (approximately 8–9 months from now); Tesla will add an extra production shift to try to fulfill existing orders and will return deposits where unable to. Musk stated the decision is not because Cybertruck is unprofitable (he said this has been "misreported by legacy media") but because the opportunity cost of maintaining Cybertruck's complexity outweighs the value of incremental Cybercab production. Jeff Lutz was skeptical of the "not unprofitable" framing, noting Cybertruck's promised lower-priced ~$40,000 trim never materialized at volume, while premium trims (Cyber Beast, all-wheel drive) sold well within their smaller addressable market; he views the shift as a resource-reallocation decision reflecting constrained skilled labor and possibly 4680 cell/module supply shared between Cybertruck and Cybercab.
2. **Cybercab efficiency and color updates:** Musk announced coming efficiency improvements enabling a 40 kWh Cybercab battery pack (down from the current pack, cited as about 16% smaller) and a new "liquid silver" color option, in addition to an existing silver-green shade. The host cited a claim (attributed to Sawyer Merritt) that Cybercab is currently the most efficient production vehicle at 6 miles per kWh, and speculated the smaller pack could push curb weight below 3,000 lbs and lower production cost.
3. **Morgan Stanley reads Cybertruck pullback as bullish for robotaxi:** Morgan Stanley characterized Tesla's move to scale back Cybertruck production in favor of Cybercab as a positive signal reflecting growing confidence in the robotaxi business's long-term potential, framing it as Tesla leveraging existing Giga Texas infrastructure and shifting strategic focus from EV sales toward autonomous mobility.

## Energy
1. **$10 billion solar factory breaks ground near Houston:** Tesla officially began construction on a vertically integrated solar manufacturing facility near Houston, with construction expected to finish around 2028 and commercial operations targeted for Q1 2029. Musk is calling the project "Project Crystal Sun." Jeff Lutz framed this alongside Tesla/SpaceX's broader manufacturing buildout as reducing US dependence on Chinese and Southeast Asian solar supply chains.

## Market & Competition
1. **FSD-linked insurance discounts expand:** Lemonade car insurance now offers a 50% discount for drivers using Tesla FSD (and 30% for FSD-light on Hardware 3/"SI3" vehicles), newly expanded to Ohio among what the host said is now about seven states.
