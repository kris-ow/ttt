Electrek's lone article today examines Tesla's response to NHTSA's Special Order on Cybercab safety certification, with Fred Lambert arguing Tesla is improperly shielding its compliance rationale from public view.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla asks NHTSA to seal its Cybercab safety certification answers:** Per Electrek, Tesla filed its sworn response to NHTSA's Special Order on the October 30 deadline, but requested the entire filing be kept confidential, leaving only a 30-page first-responder guide (dated September 3, version 1.2) publicly readable in the docket; a placeholder exists for Tesla's answer to Request 8 on standard-by-standard certification, also marked confidential in full. The piece recaps that NHTSA's September order followed Tesla's September 3 start of paid Cybercab rides in Austin, posed 21 questions, and carries civil penalties up to $139 million for an incomplete answer; Tesla had sought an eight-week extension citing the "voluminous nature of the request" but NHTSA granted only 30 days. Electrek notes NHTSA named specific rules the Cybercab appears not to meet outright — FMVSS 135 (foot-control brakes), FMVSS 108 (self-canceling turn signals), FMVSS 111 (mirrors) — and questioned whether Tesla self-certified without the Part 555 exemption that Zoox obtained (capped at 2,500 vehicles/year) for its own steering-wheel-less robotaxi. The first-responder document describes an operational design domain covering "all public roadways" in multiple weather conditions and references an "Arizona Areas of Operation Appendix," broader than Austin's current geofence, and confirms first responders can access touchscreen controls to move the vehicle after a badge scan. Fred Lambert's take: Tesla's claimed legal theory for complying with a foot-brake rule without a foot brake "doesn't sound like a trade secret," contrasting it with Zoox's and Waymo's public safety filings; he notes NHTSA previously let Tesla's answers about the original Robotaxi launch stay hidden in June 2025, and that roughly 1,000 Cybercabs remain in service in Austin while the certification basis stays sealed. ## Editorial Stance
Electrek, via Fred Lambert, is sharply critical of Tesla's confidentiality request, framing it as an attempt to avoid public scrutiny of how the Cybercab meets federal safety standards, and contrasts Tesla's approach unfavorably with competitors' transparency.
