The article reports that BYD outsold Tesla roughly 10-to-1 in Australia in January, citing Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries data, as Chinese EV brands continue gaining share in the Australian market.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla's January decline:** Tesla sold 501 vehicles in Australia in January, down 32% year-over-year, placing it 24th among all brands. The article attributes part of the weakness to stock constraints following a solid December, a pattern seen in other markets, but notes the year-over-year decline remains over 30% regardless.

## Market & Competition
1. **BYD's surge:** BYD sold 5,001 vehicles in January, a 641% year-over-year increase, making it Australia's sixth best-selling brand overall and outselling Tesla by roughly 10-to-1. Sales were led by the Sealion 7 (1,171 units) and Atto 2 (562 units), both new to the Australian market within the past year. BYD has also launched a sub-$30,000 EV, the Dolphin, as part of an aggressive pricing strategy.
2. **Chinese brands' broader dominance:** Four Chinese brands — BYD (6th), GWM (7th), Chery (9th), and MG (10th) — now rank in Australia's top 10 best-selling brands. Chinese-made vehicles collectively added 8,014 sales versus January 2025, offsetting declines at established brands like Toyota.
3. **Overall EV market growth:** Australia's total EV sales reached 7,409 in January, up 93.3% year-over-year, representing 8.4% of the new car market; the article states this growth is coming almost entirely from non-Tesla brands.
4. **Global context:** The article references BYD's 2025 global sales victory over Tesla in pure battery-electric vehicles on an annual basis, without providing specific figures.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's editorial take argues Australia illustrates what happens to Tesla when it faces unprotected Chinese competition (no tariffs on Chinese EVs, unlike the US and EU). The author attributes BYD's success to a strategy of flooding the market with competitively priced vehicles across segments, while characterizing Tesla's Australian lineup as limited (only Model 3 and Model Y, no Cybertruck or affordable compact) and not price-competitive. The author expects some recovery as Tesla's stock normalizes but views the broader trend of eroding Tesla dominance as clear, while noting Tesla retains advantages in Supercharger network and brand recognition.
