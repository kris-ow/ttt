Jo Bhakdi responds directly to a viral X post by Rebellionaire (211,000 views) arguing that Tesla shareholders are being treated unfairly in a potential SpaceX merger, having endured years of drawdowns and missed FSD timelines without yet receiving the financial payoff from robotaxi scaling. Bhakdi pushes back on both the "merger is unfair" camp and the uncritical "support Elon, we're going to the moon" camp, arguing instead that rational investors must understand how markets price stocks — and that Tesla, given the level of public attention and information transparency, is unlikely to be as dramatically mispriced as Rebellionaire implies. His core thesis is that any merger price, assuming adequate premium, is by definition a reasonable reflection of known risks and probabilities, and that the assumption Tesla is obviously worth far more is structurally unsound.

## Market & Competition

1. **The Rebellionaire Argument — Tesla Shareholders Deserve More:** Rebellionaire's viral video argues that Tesla shareholders endured years of missed FSD timelines, ridicule, and drawdowns while believing in the robotaxi vision, and that merging Tesla into SpaceX before robotaxi revenue scales to hundreds of thousands or millions of vehicles would cheat them out of the payoff. Bhakdi acknowledges this is emotionally compelling and represents a real concern within the Tesla shareholder community, but ultimately rejects its logical foundation.

2. **Bhakdi's Core Rebuttal — Markets Are Not Dramatically Mispriced:** Bhakdi argues that with this level of YouTuber, analyst (Dan Ives, Adam Jonas/Morgan Stanley), and public attention on Tesla's robotaxi and Optimus verticals, there is no credible case for a lack of information transparency. He contends that over 50% of Tesla's current stock price already reflects anticipated future value from robotaxi and Optimus, discounted for risk. Therefore, the claim that Tesla is obviously and dramatically undervalued — and that a merger would be categorically unfair — is what he calls "structurally unsound."

3. **SpaceX Can Make the Same Argument in Reverse:** Bhakdi notes that SpaceX shareholders could equally argue they are being undervalued in a merger, pointing to SpaceX's theoretically infinite total addressable market (Starlink, Starship, space colonization) versus Tesla's more bounded TAM. He expects SpaceX may be somewhat overhyped at IPO and Tesla slightly undervalued, but says the gap is not "worlds apart." He does not believe Tesla shareholders require a significant merger premium beyond a roughly equal exchange, though he acknowledges the merger price is not yet known.

4. **Alexandra Merz and the Uncritical Bull Camp:** Bhakdi also pushes back on commentators like Alexandra Merz who dismiss shareholder valuation concerns entirely in favor of enthusiasm about SpaceX and Elon Musk's broader mission. He frames this as equally flawed — rational investors must assess intrinsic value, not defer to mission-driven sentiment.

## Financials

1. **Bhakdi's DCF Model — Fair Value of ~$600 for 2026:** Bhakdi shares his discounted cash flow model, which he describes as the most conservative valuation approach available. After pushing his original timelines back by 12–18 months to account for delays, his model produces a 2026 fair value (intrinsic value) of approximately $600 per share — contingent on Tesla deploying 10,000 robotaxis by end of 2026 and scaling from there. He emphasizes this is intrinsic value, not a price target, and that the actual market price could significantly exceed or fall below this figure in any given year.

2. **Risk-Discounting Methodology:** Bhakdi applies a probability discount on top of his raw DCF output. As an example, if robotaxi generates a raw model value of $300–$400 per share but carries a 20% risk of failure, that value is further discounted by 20%. This layered risk discount is how he arrives at the ~$600 intrinsic value figure for 2026, making it explicitly conditional on deployment milestones being met.

3. **Timeline Shift Lowers Near-Term Price Targets:** Bhakdi notes he pushed his original model timelines back by 12 to 18 months, which reduces his 2030 price target relative to earlier estimates. He does not give a specific revised 2030 number in the transcript but characterizes it as "still pretty good" though "a little lower" than before.

## Robotaxi

1. **Conservative 2026 Deployment Assumption of 10,000 Robotaxis:** Bhakdi's base model assumes 10,000 robotaxis deployed by end of 2026, which he describes as conservative — noting that other analysts (he references "Herbert") believe the number will be higher, and that he himself thinks it will likely be more. His $600 intrinsic value is directly contingent on hitting this 10,000 figure.

2. **Scaling Trajectory — 100K in 2027, Millions by Mid-2030s:** From the 2026 base of 10,000, Bhakdi projects 100,000 robotaxis in 2027 (an inflection point), 200,000 by 2028, and then aggressive growth toward millions — reaching approximately 10 million by 2034–2035. He frames 2028 as the year when the technical problem should be "thoroughly solved" and regulatory readiness across jurisdictions should enable true mass scaling.

3. **Stock Price Could Significantly Exceed Intrinsic Value:** Bhakdi believes that if Tesla does deploy 10,000+ robotaxis in 2026, the actual market price will likely run well above the $600 intrinsic value — potentially dramatically so — but warns investors to be prepared for a subsequent correction, since intrinsic value remains the true long-run anchor.
