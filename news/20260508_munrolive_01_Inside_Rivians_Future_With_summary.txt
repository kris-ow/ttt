This video is an interview conducted at the ACT Expo 2026 between a Munro Live representative and Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe. The conversation covers Rivian's fleet strategy across commercial and consumer vehicles, its software-defined vehicle architecture, and key partnership deals. The core thesis is that Rivian is positioning itself as a horizontally integrated technology platform company — not just a vehicle manufacturer — with its software, connectivity, and autonomy stacks deployable across very different vehicle types and brands.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EDV fleet scaling with Amazon:** Rivian's Electric Delivery Van (EDV), developed in deep partnership with Amazon specifically for last-mile delivery, now has well in excess of 30,000 units on the road. Scaringe noted that output is expected to ramp significantly over the coming year, driven by Amazon's recognition of a meaningful total cost of ownership (TCO) and operational cost advantage. Beyond electrification, the value proposition includes vehicle connectivity, built-in prognostics, predictive service interval capabilities, and deep integration into Amazon's software architecture.

2. **R2 as Rivian's first mass-market vehicle:** Scaringe described the R2 as Rivian's first high-volume, mass-market vehicle, targeting the mid-size SUV segment — which he characterized as the largest segment in the market. He contrasted it with the R1, which was successful but limited to the premium/flagship space. Scaringe also teased that R2 represents a significant engineering improvement over R1 and expressed excitement about having it torn down by Munro Live.

3. **Software-defined architecture enabling lower price points:** Scaringe attributed Rivian's ability to offer vehicles at lower price points to its software-defined, zonal ECU architecture, which consolidates compute into a small number of zonal computers, dramatically simplifies the wiring harness, and brings software development fully in-house. He noted this platform is scalable across different price points, content levels, form factors, and brands.

4. **Volkswagen partnership and the ~$20,000 ID1:** Rivian completed a $5.8 billion software licensing deal with Volkswagen approximately a year and a half ago. The first vehicle to emerge from this partnership is the VW ID1, priced at around $20,000 — an EV not intended for the U.S. market — which Scaringe said dramatically demonstrates the simplification of the electronic stack and wiring harness made possible by Rivian's technology.

## Market & Competition
1. **Robotaxi deal with Uber on the R2 platform:** Rivian recently signed a large deal with Uber to build a robotaxi on its R2 platform. Scaringe positioned this as a distinct type of fleet business focused on moving people, with self-driving technology as a central element. He framed this alongside the Amazon delivery van as part of a broader fleet strategy encompassing both goods movement and people movement.

2. **"Also" micromobility company and DoorDash deal:** Rivian created a micromobility subsidiary called "Also," which recently announced a deal with DoorDash to operate a fleet of small delivery bots for food delivery. Scaringe described this as another expression of the same fleet-oriented, autonomy-forward, AI-defined platform strategy.

3. **Horizontal technology platforms as the organizing principle:** Scaringe described Rivian's strategic organization around horizontal technology platforms — including its autonomous driving platform, connected vehicle platform, and diagnostics/prognostics platform — that are shared across very different vehicle types (vans, R1, R2, R3, micromobility, VW products). He said the company is shifting its language from "software-defined vehicles" to "AI-defined vehicles," reflecting a focus on leveraging data collection across vehicles for better decisioning.
