A Quebec court ordered Tesla Canada to pay C$161,700 to insurer Intact after rejecting Tesla's defense that a 2020 Model X fire was likely due to misuse or vandalism, a claim the judge found unsupported by any data.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Case background and ruling:** On March 5, 2023, a 2020 Model X caught fire in Montreal about five minutes after the heat was turned on while owner Sacha De Santis was driving; police ruled out criminal involvement, and an expert report filed with the court found the fire started on the dashboard's passenger side, calling a premature electrical failure "probable" (per the article's translation). The vehicle was still under Tesla's Canadian factory warranty (4 years/80,000 km), roughly three years into service. Insurer Intact paid De Santis C$161,700 and sued Tesla to recover the amount, winning a default judgment in June after Tesla failed to respond in time.
2. **Tesla's failed defense:** When Tesla sought to overturn the default judgment, a Tesla employee testified that vehicle fires are generally caused by misuse or vandalism, but presented no data to support the claim. Justice Ian Demers rejected Tesla's request, writing that Tesla raised no serious defense and made its claim "without relying on any data whatsoever" (per the article's translation). Tesla Canada did not respond to a request for comment; Intact declined to comment and noted the appeal deadline has not passed.
3. **Tesla's published fire statistics (contextual, not used in the case):** Tesla's Vehicle Fire Safety Report claims about one Tesla vehicle fire per 140 million miles traveled from 2012–2024, versus one per 17 million miles for the average US vehicle (per NFPA/DOT data cited by Tesla). The article notes Tesla's own figure was one fire per 170 million miles when first reported in 2019, meaning the rate has worsened over time, though it remains far better than the gas-vehicle average cited.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues this single case doesn't indicate EVs or the Model X are unsafe, since EVs catch fire far less often than gas cars, but criticizes Tesla for contesting a warranty claim with an employee's unsupported opinion rather than data or a counter-expert, calling it surprising for "a $1 trillion company."
