This episode of brighterwithherbert features private investor Phil Trouy discussing The Boring Company as a potential "third giant" alongside Tesla and SpaceX. The conversation covers Boring's Loop tunnel system (built with Tesla vehicles and safety drivers), its cost advantages over traditional subway/rail infrastructure, current deployments in Las Vegas and Nashville, a recent funding round, and speculation about future integration with Tesla's robotaxi network — but Boring itself remains a separate private company, not Tesla or SpaceX stock.

## Market & Competition
1. **Boring Company positioned as transportation infrastructure play:** Trouy argues Boring should be understood not as a tunneling company but as an urban/intercity transportation infrastructure company, targeting a US TAM of roughly $200 billion/year in road spending plus roughly $20 billion/year in regional air travel between nearby cities — with international TAM described as even larger.
2. **Loop system design and cost economics:** The Loop uses individual Tesla vehicles in point-to-point tunnels (not trains), with station spacing as tight as 100 feet versus roughly a quarter mile for typical subway stations. Trouy states Boring's current tunneling cost is about $10 million per mile (single tunnel; ~$20 million for a bidirectional pair), versus $200 million per mile cited for a recent San Diego light-rail extension — roughly one-tenth the cost — with a stated goal of eventually halving that further. He also noted the San Diego project took about 30 years from initial planning to completion and cost about $2.2 billion total, while Vegas's Loop was built with no public funding.
3. **Current deployments and ridership:** Vegas Loop currently has about eight stations operating (with roughly 120 entitlements secured for future stations along the route, and a mapped/projected network of around 50-104 stations across an ~11-mile corridor). Busy-day ridership is currently around 35,000 people/day, expected to rise to as many as 100,000/day once an airport extension opens (expected within a few months of the interview) with 16-18 stations at that point. Fares were cited as $4.50 one-way or $14 for a day pass, versus a roughly $15 minimum taxi fare in Vegas. Nashville and Dubai are cited as Boring's second and third major city projects (Dubai about to break ground); Orlando was mentioned as under political consideration.
4. **Engineering innovations driving cost down:** Boring uses smaller-diameter twin tunnels rather than one large bore, remote/automated control of tunnel boring machines from a Texas control room, continuous tunneling with 5-foot precast concrete segments applied via a moving shield, real-time conveyor-belt muck removal, and purpose-built "liner trucks" (reportedly using three Tesla Model 3 drivetrains) to move segments to the machine. Trouy says the current machine is about six times faster/more efficient than a conventional commercial boring machine of the same tunnel size, and that Boring is building a new machine roughly every two months, per Boring CEO Steve Davis, with plans for four more machines by year-end (from a current fleet of seven).
5. **Safety systems:** Tunnels reportedly include emergency ventilation to clear smoke, cameras throughout, standpipes for firefighter hose access (roughly every 150 feet, per Trouy), and periodic emergency exits to the surface, with fire department training drills cited as an example.
6. **Regulatory/land-rights model:** Boring must obtain easement permissions from individual property owners or cities (property rights are described as extending underground in the US), and has secured roughly 120 entitlements along the Vegas route from businesses/hotels for potential future stations at no cost to those property owners; Nashville's process was streamlined to essentially two major permits after lessons learned from Vegas's piecemeal permitting.
7. **Recent funding round and valuation strategy:** Boring recently raised a $3 billion round; Trouy says the round was originally structured as $2.5 billion at a $20 billion valuation (with $2 billion from strategic investors and $500 million from Elon-linked VCs/institutional investors), which was later increased to roughly $1 billion in that tranche after being oversubscribed by roughly 8x, while the valuation itself was deliberately held at $20 billion rather than raised — a strategy Trouy says mirrors SpaceX's approach of keeping early investors' returns strong across successive rounds.
8. **Future speculation — Hyperloop, asteroid/lunar mining, Tesla acquisition:** Trouy says Boring has tested a Hyperloop tunnel achieving very low internal air pressure using current pumps (without optimization), suggesting city-to-city pod travel potentially faster than airplane speeds is technically feasible as a future project, though not currently active. The host floated a personal theory that Boring's tunneling tech could eventually be applied to lunar/Mars resource extraction; Trouy agreed this is plausible longer-term. Trouy also stated his own personal view (explicitly unofficial) that Tesla will likely eventually acquire The Boring Company, drawing an analogy to SpaceX's acquisition of xAI/X.
