The host lays out his rationale for why Tesla might sell or lease Cybercabs to third-party fleet operators rather than owning 100% of the robotaxi fleet itself, arguing this offloads capital-intensive, low-value operational work while Tesla focuses on its proprietary software and platform. The video also covers Cybercab regulatory progress in Japan and Germany, a Powerwall/Cybertruck integration update, European Tesla Semi specs, Terafab construction progress, and SpaceX compute-leasing and Boring Company funding news.

## Robotaxi
1. **Rationale for third-party Cybercab fleet ownership:** The host argues Tesla's truly scarce, proprietary assets are FSD software/training data, the rider app, dynamic pricing algorithms, customer data, and remote operator infrastructure — not the "grunt work" of vehicle ownership, insurance claims, depots, cleaning, and city permitting. Selling or leasing Cybercabs to fleet operators while taking a revenue cut would convert a capital-heavy, multi-year operating expense into a faster-payback, asset-light income stream, similar to how Tesla now sells Supercharger hardware to third parties without abandoning its own charging business.
2. **Tesla's history of shifting robotaxi ownership plans:** Dating back to Master Plan Part 2, Tesla planned for customer-owned vehicles to join a shared autonomous network; more recently, leases with no buyout option suggested Tesla wanted to reclaim cars for its own robotaxi fleet, but roughly two years ago Tesla abandoned this and sold those vehicles as used cars instead — the host says this was mainly because robotaxi software wasn't ready, and secondarily to manage the balance sheet.
3. **Fleet buyers could offload demand generation and infrastructure:** Tesla's fleet-buyer signup form lists mobility hubs and infrastructure alongside fleet purchases, suggesting Tesla wants fleet operators (airports, hotels, hospitals, colleges, sports venues, municipalities) to handle demand generation and physical infrastructure buildout (zoning, permitting, overnight parking/charging/cleaning lots) rather than Tesla doing it city by city. Waymo is cited as already outsourcing depot/fleet work to Avis for similar reasons.
4. **Timeline caveat and infrastructure surprise:** The host doesn't expect fleet-ownership deals at scale until FSD V15 is broadly deployed and until NHTSA resolves its audit query into the Cybercab. He references Ashok Elluswamy having said on a recent earnings call that Tesla was surprised by how infrastructure-intensive the robotaxi business actually is; Tesla's capex is set around $25 billion this year and free cash flow has turned negative, which the host frames as a constraint (not a red flag) on fully self-funding fleet scale.
5. **Cybercab safety-testing stunts and a rider ban:** A user known as "Dirty Tesla" was banned from the robotaxi service for seven days for stepping in front of a Cybercab to test its response; a separate widely-viewed incident involved someone running into the street in front of a Cybercab, which stopped. The host says he agrees with critics calling out this behavior, noting no autonomous Tesla vehicle has struck a pedestrian and calling this kind of stunt testing unproductive.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **V15 upgrade previewed:** Ashok Elluswamy said the upcoming V15 FSD update will bring earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction time, and significantly better safety and collision avoidance. The host says he now believes V15 (or a subsequent point release like 15.2–15.4) needs to be the version that unlocks serious robotaxi scale, given the Cybercab's lack of a steering wheel and pedals and its ramping production.
2. **Japan Cybercab regulatory path:** Tesla Japan showcased a Cybercab display unit; a Tesla Japan executive said the Cybercab uses the same FSD technology as the current Model Y, that Japan's regulatory environment for driverless Level 4 operation is "not yet in place," and that Tesla's near-term priority is getting FSD itself legalized in Japan this year — full Cybercab approval would be a separate, later step.
3. **Germany considering FSD approval ahead of vote:** Germany's KBA regulator sent an email indicating it's considering an exemption route to recognize FSD approval ahead of a vote later this year at the TCMBB meeting (roughly a month out per the host). The host is skeptical regulators will move before the scheduled vote, though he notes prior reporting that German authorities want Tesla robotaxis operating driverless in certain rural areas.
4. **Dashcam disputes a speeding ticket:** A Tesla owner's wife was ticketed for going 82 mph in a 70 zone; the car's dashcam footage showed she was actually going 77 mph, and police dismissed the ticket after reviewing the footage.

## Energy
1. **Powerwall–Cybertruck integration announced:** Tesla said Powerwall 3 and the Cybertruck can now work together via Powershare, extending home backup by over three days — equivalent to roughly nine additional Powerwalls, based on the Cybertruck's ~123 kWh battery pack versus the Powerwall 3's ~13.5 kWh capacity. Powerwall 2 Plus compatibility is coming later this year. A Cybertruck engineer said the delay was due to certification requirements for two grid-forming devices negotiating home management during an outage across multiple Powerwall generations.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck sales assessment:** The host reiterates his view that Cybertruck sales have been disappointing to date, attributing this partly to the vehicle becoming politically charged following Elon Musk's political activities in recent years, while maintaining hope broader understanding of the vehicle's capabilities could improve perception over time.
2. **European Tesla Semi specs revealed:** Ahead of a Tesla Semi Europe event, specs show 342 miles of range at over 88,000 lb gross combination weight, energy consumption of 1.61 kWh/mile, and Megacharger speeds up to 800 kW (versus 1.2 MW in the US). The host notes Europe's heavier GCW rating versus the US 82,000 lb rating means efficiency is still in line with US figures, and that a long-range Semi has not yet been listed for Europe. European deliveries are slated from 2027 via a Germany web page; initial semis will come from Nevada with hoped-for future production at Giga Berlin. The European variant uses camera monitoring systems instead of side mirrors (allowed under EU but not US regulations) and has standalone headlights instead of a continuous light bar.
3. **Terafab construction underway:** A drone video showed construction officially started at the Terafab site in Grimes County (distinct from the Austin research fab), with main foundation work, footing excavations, rebar gauges, and grading underway.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **New $1.1B/month compute leasing deal, $100B revenue run rate target:** Brett Johnson of SpaceX announced a new compute leasing deal with an unnamed company worth $1.1 billion per month, carrying the same 90-day out clause as prior deals, and confirmed SpaceX is on track for a $100 billion recurring revenue run rate by the end of this year — a large increase versus SpaceX's companywide 2025 revenue of under $19 billion.
2. **Compute pricing trending toward higher end of range:** Johnson said compute leasing deals are priced between $30–$50 per watt of capacity, with SpaceX currently toward the higher end, and he expects SpaceX to eventually earn more than that as internal models (the Grok family, including Grok bot and Grok build) scale to consumer and enterprise use, which would reduce reliance on external lease deals. He also said multi-year-old compute is holding lease rates better than expected, supporting strong residual chip values.
3. **Grok 4.7 delayed slightly, per Elon Musk:** Elon Musk said Grok 4.7 needs a few more days of tuning, citing over-penalization of response length in reinforcement learning causing the model to give up on solvable hard tasks too early and insufficient rigor in checking its own work. This model is expected to incorporate a large amount of SpaceX engineering training data.
