## Robotaxi
- **Reuters investigation documents severe service reliability failures across all three cities.** In Austin, cars were unavailable in 27% of daily checks over three weeks in April; wait times exceeded 15 minutes roughly half the time. In Dallas, a 5-mile trip took nearly two hours, including a car that failed to make a left turn four times before a remote attendant intervened.
- **Tesla's Austin crash rate is approximately 4x worse than the human driver average.** Tesla has reported 15 crashes to NHTSA since August, yielding roughly one crash per 57,000 miles versus the human baseline of one per 229,000 miles per Tesla's own vehicle safety data. Tesla has requested NHTSA redact all crash details.
- **Musk identified safety validation — not software or manufacturing capacity — as the primary scaling bottleneck** on the Q1 2026 earnings call (April 22). He also walked back his mid-2025 prediction that robotaxis would cover half the U.S. population by end of 2025, downgrading to "a dozen or so states" by end of 2026. The planned five-city expansion originally slated for H1 2026 has been delayed.
- **64% of Austin robotaxi rides in the past seven days were unsupervised**, per Herbert Ong. Bhakdi estimates current Cybercab production at 300–600 units per month based on parking lot sightings of 37–40 units cycling out of Giga Texas, and sets 1,800 cumulative Cybercabs as his personal inflection milestone, which he believes is roughly one month away at current trajectory.
- **Dillon Loomis projects Cybercab's first paid deployment around September 15, 2026** — not the 1–2 month window many observers expect. He cites the Cybertruck FSD retraining precedent and flags novel Cybercab-specific challenges: first front-wheel-drive Tesla, no steering wheel or pedals requiring entirely new control command outputs, different camera angles, and the higher stakes of launching unsupervised for paid rides from day one.
- **Piper Sandler's robotaxi revenue model is conservative and back-loaded:** $400M in 2027, $2.2B in 2028, $18.6B by 2030, with meaningful revenue not appearing until 2028 and sharp acceleration from 2031 onward. ARK Invest estimates Tesla's robotaxi cost will reach $0.25 per mile at scale versus Waymo remaining approximately 50% higher in 2030 due to third-party supply chain dependence.
- **Waymo recalled nearly 3,800 robotaxis** (fifth and sixth generation automated driving systems) after a software issue caused vehicles to drive onto flooded roads. A separate NHTSA investigation involves a Waymo vehicle striking a child near an elementary school. The recall incidentally confirms Waymo's true fleet size. Waymo is delivering over 500,000 paid rides per week across 10 U.S. cities, fully driverless, 24 hours a day — a scale contrast with Tesla's current deployment.
- **AV Ride, an Uber partner, is under NHTSA investigation for 16 self-driving crashes** involving failures to properly change lanes, respond to vehicles in the same lane, and respond to stationary objects — all occurring with a safety monitor present.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus V3 has cleared engineering verification and entered small-scale trial production in May 2026**, per Chinese supply chain account Ming. Core suppliers are expected to receive first mass production orders in mid-July, targeting a large-scale production ramp in August. Seven key Chinese partners have already passed Tesla's PPA certification. This corroborates Tesla's earnings guidance and aligns with Jeff Lutz's observation that electromechanical suppliers would have had to begin production in April for a July–August target to be credible.
- **China supply chain reports confirm Tesla has placed its first bulk purchase orders with humanoid robot component suppliers**, moving beyond sample testing and validation into small-scale mass production. The orders involve actual payments and are described as laying groundwork for the July–August 2026 production ramp.
- **Piper Sandler's Alexander Potter argues Optimus is valued at zero in the current stock price.** His 17-business-line model projecting $1.8 trillion in annual Tesla revenue by 2045 deliberately excludes Optimus because including it produces numbers too large for most investors to accept. Potter's price target is $500/share; at $400/share he describes Optimus as "free."
- **Cortex 2 at Giga Texas is approximately 50% operational**, per Joe Tegtmeyer footage, with Megapacks, chiller systems, and superstructure all visible. The future Optimus factory — planned for 10 million units per year capacity — is sited just north of Cortex 2.

## Autonomous Driving
- **2026 Model Y became the first vehicle to pass all four of NHTSA's new advanced driver assistance tests**: pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention. NHTSA's administrator said the Model Y "sets a high bar for the industry." This is a federal-level validation of Tesla's vision-only system at a moment when congressional debates over LiDAR versus vision-only remain active.
- **FSD expansion in China is accelerating on two fronts.** The Chinese owner's manual has been updated with FSD Supervised renamed "Tesla Assisted Driving," and Tesla is hiring Autopilot test engineers across Shanghai, Shenzhen, Chongqing, and Wuhan — the first time city-level specificity has appeared in these postings. Elon Musk's inclusion on the Trump-China trade trip is widely interpreted as a potential catalyst for broader FSD regulatory approval, echoing his April 2024 Beijing visit that preceded Tesla's data privacy clearance and BYD mapping deal.
- **The Netherlands became the first European market where buyers must pay for FSD to access any Autosteer functionality.** Dutch RDW granted FSD Supervised type approval under UN R-171 in mid-April 2026, enabling Tesla to remove Basic Autopilot from the Dutch configurator. FSD is priced at €99/month or €7,500 one-time; a base Model 3 (€36,990) now ships with only the GSR2 emergency lane-keeping nudge as standard. A May 21, 2026 deadline for one-time FSD purchases has been set across other European markets, signaling an imminent broader rollout. Competitors including VW and Hyundai include lane-centering as standard on vehicles at comparable price points.
- **Giga Berlin's production line Model Ys have autonomously driven a combined 93,000 miles on FSD** from the end of the production line to the outbound lot — a real-world unsupervised deployment in a controlled operational environment.
- **Piper Sandler projects FSD licensing revenue at zero through 2031, then ramping to $400 billion by 2045.** FSD subscriptions are modeled at ~$600M in 2026 growing to $10.6B by 2030. Jeff Lutz noted two potential accelerants for the licensing timeline: competitive pressure as FSD becomes a purchase decision driver, and potential regulatory mandates given FSD's claimed safety advantage.

## Humanoid Bots / Market & Competition
- **Tesla and SpaceX are partnering with Intel to build the "Terafab" semiconductor fabrication facility** at Intel's Hillsborough, Oregon site. Phase 1 is estimated at $55 billion, split between Tesla and SpaceX. Tesla previously approached Samsung and TSMC; TSMC declined. Samsung subsequently announced additional chip capacity, suggesting competitive pressure from the Intel deal. The fab is interpreted as de-risking a key question ahead of SpaceX's anticipated IPO and is cited as potentially more significant for SpaceX than Tesla.

## Energy
- **Tesla raised Giga Berlin's 4680 battery cell production target from 8 GWh to 18 GWh**, backed by an additional ~$250 million investment. Total cumulative investment in Grünheide cell production now approaches €1 billion (~$1.2 billion). At 18 GWh, the facility would produce cells for an estimated 250,000–350,000 vehicles per year. Cell production is expected to begin in H1 2027. Tesla has already begun recruiting for 1,500+ new battery production roles.
- **Tesla's Q1 2026 energy business missed consensus by approximately 38–39%**, per Jeff Lutz — a magnitude that would have caused severe market reactions for other energy storage companies. Lutz flagged this as potentially setting up a large Q2 rebound alongside recovering auto sales.
- **Ford launched "Ford Energy,"** a new subsidiary planning to deploy at least 20 GWh of battery storage annually using 512 Ah LFP prismatic cells in standardized 20-foot containerized systems, with first customer deliveries planned for late 2027. Tesla's Megapack business holds a substantial head start in scale and deployment maturity.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Giga Berlin has produced 750,000 vehicles** since opening in 2022, with plant management targeting the first million as quickly as possible. A 20% production increase is planned alongside the cell capacity expansion, requiring new hiring. The factory was reportedly running at ~40% capacity earlier in 2026 following a 28% collapse in European Tesla sales in 2025 (German registrations down 48%); the refreshed Model Y has reversed the trajectory.
- **Giga Shanghai recorded its strongest April ever**, delivering approximately 80,000 vehicles — an annualized run rate approaching one million units. Growth is being driven partly by exports to Canada, Europe, and South Korea as domestic China demand remains softer.
- **The $59,000 Cybertruck variant may have entered production at Giga Texas**, per Joe Tegtmeyer observation, though it is visually difficult to distinguish from the premium AWD version. The Model S/X Signature Edition delivery event has been rescheduled to May 20, 2026 in Fremont, California.
- **Tesla launched a Supercharger virtual queue pilot at five locations** (Los Gatos, Mountain View, San Francisco, San Jose, and the Bronx) — arriving one year behind the originally announced Q2 2025 timeline. The system relies on an honor-system prompt rather than enforced queue order. Approximately 70% of Tesla's 80,000+ stalls are now open to non-Tesla EVs, making congestion management increasingly material.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla stock closed at $445 on May 12**, up 3.89% on the day and up approximately 32–33% over the prior 35 days, sitting roughly 10–11.5% below its all-time high of ~$490–$495. Bhakdi separates the thesis into two layers: a baseline return to ~$490 independent of any new catalyst, and a potential repricing to $600–$1,000 contingent on the market becoming convinced robotaxi is scaling toward multiple millions of vehicles — a threshold he believes could be crossed before the fleet reaches 10,000 units.
- **Elon Musk was invited to join President Trump's China trade trip** alongside Tim Cook, Boeing, Goldman Sachs, Blackstone, BlackRock, Citigroup, Meta, GE, Cargill, Micron, Qualcomm, Visa, and others. Bhakdi interprets the roster as evidence that U.S.-China trade de-escalation and deal announcements are imminent. Jensen Huang's absence is read as confirmation that semiconductor export restrictions remain unresolved. Optimus supply chain dependence on Chinese components makes this trip directly material to Tesla manufacturing economics.
- **The Giga Berlin union vote sequence drew scrutiny.** IG Metall's vote share fell from 39.4% to 31.1% in the March 2026 election after Musk sent workers a video warning expansion would not proceed if IG Metall won a works council majority; management-aligned Giga United won 24 of 37 seats. IG Metall has filed a legal challenge alleging unlawful interference. The $250M investment announcement followed immediately after the union's defeat.
