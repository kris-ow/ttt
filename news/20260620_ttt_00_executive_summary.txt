## Autonomous Driving

- **Elon clarifies the FSD intervention breakdown:** In a public post, Elon stated that destination parking is "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD" and that "critical safety interventions are extremely rare." Per Herbert Ong, this is Elon's clearest public statement yet on the intervention mix — the safety bucket is already small, and the upcoming FSD release adding parking-preference memory will shrink the remaining intervention count further. NHTSA, not Tesla, defines what counts as a critical intervention, so this remains a directional signal rather than a confirmed regulatory milestone.

- **Spain's DGT publishes third-party FSD safety data:** Spain's Dirección General de Tráfico confirmed Tesla's supervised FSD fleet of 30 cars has completed 275,471 km (171,170 miles) of public-road testing since November 2025 with no serious incidents. The data comes from the government regulator, not Tesla, lending independent credibility to FSD's European safety record roughly seven months into the program.

- **FSD V14 reaches Australia and New Zealand:** Tesla began rolling out FSD Supervised V14 to Australia and New Zealand for the first time, extending its most current software version beyond North America and Europe and expanding the global training dataset.

- **Zero-intervention NH-to-NYC drive logged:** Sawyer Merritt reported completing a full drive from New Hampshire to New York City with no steering wheel or pedal input, followed by continued zero-intervention driving through the Financial District, Lower Manhattan, Midtown, and Greenwich Village. Anecdotal, but high-visibility.

- **AI4 sufficient for FSD; AI5 targeted at Optimus:** Per FutureAzA guest Dr. Knowit All, Elon has indicated AI4 (Hardware 4) is already adequate for on-road autonomous driving, and AI5 is being designed primarily for Optimus rather than vehicles. Robotaxi upgrades to AI5 are possible but not an immediate priority.

## Robotaxi

- **Elon publicly confirms fleet is never the at-fault vehicle:** Sawyer Merritt posted that the only crashes involving Tesla's robotaxi fleet in recent months have been other vehicles rear-ending them; Elon replied "Indeed." Herbert Ong notes this mirrors the core metric AV safety reports use to demonstrate the stack outperforms human drivers, and that fault-free operation compresses insurance costs — a direct input to robotaxi unit economics, given Tesla self-insures through Tesla Insurance.

- **Unsupervised approval read-through:** Herbert Ong argues the combination of shrinking safety interventions (per Elon's parking post) and a fault-free crash record constitutes a meaningful setup for an unsupervised approval headline before year-end 2026. He flags an official NHTSA confirmation of the rear-ended-only pattern as the key watch item that would most accelerate that timeline.

## Humanoid Bots

- **~18 months since a meaningful Optimus public update:** FutureAzA host Brian and guest Dr. Knowit All note the last substantive Optimus demo was approximately October 2024. Their interpretation of the silence is that the program is in active development rather than near-complete — if Tesla had something compelling to show, it would show it.

- **Hand dexterity remains the central unsolved problem:** Dr. Knowit All identifies the robot hand as the primary bottleneck for the industry and Tesla specifically — no consensus exists on whether tendons, miniature per-joint actuators, or a novel approach is correct. He references Elon responding roughly one month ago (implying ~May 2026) to newly published Tesla hand patents by acknowledging the patented design "didn't work," suggesting Tesla is already on its fourth or fifth hand iteration.

- **Fremont line conversion and Austin factory read as firm commitment:** The hosts view Tesla's decision to halt Model S/X production at Fremont — converting that line to Optimus pilot production — and the large-scale Optimus factory under construction in Austin as the strongest positive signals in the absence of public demos. Their read: Tesla would not stop a revenue-generating vehicle line or build a facility of that scale without something real to build.

- **AI5 is the Optimus brain; Nvidia platforms used everywhere else:** Dr. Knowit All notes the broader humanoid robot industry has standardized on Nvidia's Jetson and Thor compute platforms, while Tesla is building AI5 (and eventually AI6) specifically for the humanoid form factor. He suggests AI5 may not be prioritized for vehicles at all given AI4's adequacy for driving.

- **Early units will use virtual safety envelopes, not physical cages:** Dr. Knowit All expects early Optimus deployments to rely on proximity-based virtual stop zones rather than hard physical barriers, given U.S. product liability exposure. He points to recent Chinese robot incidents striking bystanders as the commercial risk Tesla is designing against.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla sales rose after $7,500 EV tax credit removal:** Elon posted that when President Trump removed the federal EV tax credit, Tesla sales actually increased as buyers shifted from other EV makers to Tesla. He confirmed the same point with a one-word "true" reply on X. Herbert Ong reads this as disproving a central 2025 bear case, though he notes one quarter of post-credit share gain is a signal, not proof — he will watch whether the pattern holds over a full year.

- **Green Bay PD reports under $500/year operating cost per Tesla patrol car:** Green Bay Police Department is adding two 2026 Model Y patrol vehicles after running two 2024 units since 2024. The Chief of Police cited annual operating costs below $500 per vehicle, with windshield wiper replacements as the only maintenance required — a concrete TCO data point from a public agency.

## Financials

- **Near-term financials expected to look stressed:** FutureAzA guest Dr. Knowit All explicitly cautions against expecting positive short-term results, citing simultaneous capital deployment across Cybercab/robotaxi fleet build-out (pre-revenue), Optimus factory construction, and Cortex compute infrastructure expansion (Cortex 1 and 2). He frames this as intentional, not distress.

- **Tesla's XAI stake gets a cleaner mark post-SpaceX IPO:** Herbert Ong notes Tesla holds a board-approved equity stake in XAI. As XAI lands enterprise distribution deals — including Grok models now available on Databricks' AgentBricks platform — Tesla's balance sheet line on that investment is remarked higher. Tesla also holds a small SpaceX equity stake, which now has a public market reference price.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX closes day one at ~$2.1 trillion, up ~19%:** SpaceX's IPO priced at $135 and closed its first trading day at roughly $2.1 trillion market cap in approximately 6.5 hours. Herbert Ong argues this gives analysts an undeniable public comp when pricing Tesla's AI, robotaxi, and Optimus businesses — previously, SpaceX as a private benchmark could be disputed.

- **Elon targets ~$1T SpaceX revenue by 2030:** Responding to Morgan Stanley's $330 billion 2030 revenue estimate (vs. $18.7 billion actual in 2025), Elon posted he thinks SpaceX might reach approximately $1 trillion in 2030 and added he "would be surprised if revenues not greater than $1 trillion in 2031." Herbert Ong notes that implies roughly a 53x increase in five years and flags that Elon's timelines historically slip.

- **Elon engaged with $30–50T valuation thesis:** An analyst argued SpaceX's real 5-year comp is $30–50 trillion, predicated on Starship reducing cost-per-kg to orbit by 100x and spawning entirely new industries (orbital data centers, microgravity manufacturing, point-to-point Earth transit, asteroid mining). Elon replied the $30T figure was "not impossible, but definitely requires factories on the moon and Mars." Herbert Ong treats this as Elon signaling which comp he wants in circulation.

- **XAI / Grok lands on Databricks enterprise platform:** Elon posted that Grok models are now available on Databricks' AgentBricks platform — described by Herbert Ong as XAI's largest enterprise distribution move to date, analogous to Anthropic's AWS Bedrock deal. The commercial copy used "SpaceX AI's latest models," marking the first appearance of the post-merger SpaceX/XAI combined branding in marketing material (merger closed February 2026 per the host).

- **Elon sues German public broadcaster ZDF for defamation:** Per Jo Bhakdi, a ZDF presenter stated that Elon Musk "called upon" a mob to hunt down foreigners in Ireland — a claim Bhakdi characterizes as fabricated. Musk's German lawyer, Joachim Steinhöwel, has already secured a cease and desist undertaking, which the lawyer publicly called a "great victory" and described as "journalistically completely unjustifiable." The lawyer has stated he intends to investigate every alleged defamation of Musk across ARD and ZDF over the past approximately three years. Bhakdi notes ZDF and ARD together hold roughly 28% of the German TV market, funded by a mandatory €18/month household fee generating over €10 billion per year.
