## Autonomous Driving

- **Spain government data: 275,471 km, zero serious incidents.** Spain's Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT) reported Tesla's 30-car FSD Supervised fleet has completed 275,471 km (171,170 miles) of public-road testing since November 2025 with no serious incidents — a third-party government source, not a Tesla claim, directly relevant to European commercialization and regulatory approval timelines.

- **Elon redefines the intervention mix.** Elon posted that upcoming FSD releases will remember parking preferences, and stated that "destination parking is by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD" while "critical safety interventions are extremely rare." Per the brighterwithherbert host, this is the first clean public statement parsing convenience interventions from safety interventions — the safety bucket regulators examine before unsupervised approval is already very small.

- **Caveat:** "Critical safety interventions are extremely rare" is Elon's framing. NHTSA holds authority to define what qualifies, and unsupervised approval timing remains unconfirmed.

- **FSD V14 reaches Australia and New Zealand.** Tesla officially began rolling out FSD Supervised V14 to Australia and New Zealand for the first time, extending the most current software version outside North America and Europe and expanding the global training dataset.

- **Sawyer Merritt: New Hampshire to NYC, zero interventions.** Merritt reported a full New Hampshire-to-NYC drive with no steering wheel or pedal input, followed by continued zero-intervention driving through the Financial District, Lower Manhattan, Midtown, Greenwich Village, and the East Village. Anecdotal but high-visibility.

- **AI4 sufficient for FSD; AI5 targeted at Optimus.** Per the FutureAzA guest, Elon has indicated AI4 (Hardware 4) is already adequate for on-road autonomous driving, and AI5 — specifically designed for the humanoid form factor's three-dimensional sensing and dexterity demands — may not be prioritized for vehicles in the near term.

## Robotaxi

- **Elon confirms rear-end-only crash pattern.** Sawyer Merritt posted that the only crashes involving Tesla's robotaxi fleet in recent months have been other vehicles rear-ending them; Elon replied "indeed." Per the brighterwithherbert host, this mirrors the exact data point AV safety reports use to demonstrate AV-at-fault rates below human drivers — and, because Tesla runs its own insurance, lower fault rates feed directly into robotaxi unit economics.

- **Unsupervised approval setup thesis.** The brighterwithherbert host argues the combination of shrinking parking-preference interventions and the rear-ended-only crash pattern constitutes a meaningful setup for an unsupervised approval headline before year-end 2026. He attributes this view to himself and flags NHTSA formally confirming the rear-end pattern on the record as the key watch item.

## Humanoid Bots

- **~18 months of Optimus silence; hand dexterity is the bottleneck.** FutureAzA host and guest note the last substantive Optimus demo was approximately October 2024. The guest identifies the robot hand as the primary unsolved problem across the entire humanoid industry — no consensus exists on tendons vs. miniature joint actuators vs. novel approaches. Elon reportedly acknowledged in approximately May 2026 that a recently patented hand design "didn't work," implying Tesla is already on a fourth or fifth hand iteration.

- **Fremont Model S/X line converted; Austin mega-factory under construction.** Tesla stopped producing the Model S and Model X at Fremont to free that line for Optimus pilot production. A large-scale Optimus factory is separately under construction in Austin. The FutureAzA hosts interpret both moves as deliberate, high-conviction commitments — Tesla would not halt a revenue-generating vehicle line or build at that scale without something real to build.

- **AI5 is Optimus's brain; Tesla diverging from industry standard.** The broader humanoid robot industry has largely standardized on Nvidia's Jetson and Thor compute platforms. Tesla is committed to its own silicon, with AI5 (and eventually AI6) designed specifically for the humanoid form factor's complexity. The guest suggests AI5 may not even reach vehicles soon given AI4's adequacy for driving.

- **Early deployment: virtual safety envelopes, not physical cages.** The industry goal is to avoid hard lockout zones; early Optimus units are expected to use virtual proximity stops — pausing if a human enters a defined envelope. The guest cites recent Chinese competitor incidents injuring people and notes U.S. product liability law makes such events commercially unacceptable.

- **Silence reframed as competitive discipline.** The hosts interpret Tesla's absence of public demos as consistent with having recruited the talent it needed and wanting to minimize competitive intelligence leakage — consistent with Elon's stated concern that demos give competitors a blueprint.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla sales rose after $7,500 EV tax credit removal.** Elon posted that when President Trump removed the federal EV tax credit, Tesla sales actually increased as buyers shifted from other EV makers to Tesla. He separately confirmed the point with a one-word reply ("true") on X. The brighterwithherbert host reads this as a fundamental bear-case error from 2025 — Tesla's unit economics absorbed the $7,500 headwind more easily than competitors whose margins were more dependent on the credit.

- **Caveat:** The host flags this as a short-window data point; one quarter of post-credit-removal share gain is a signal, not proof. He will watch whether the pattern holds over four quarters.

- **Green Bay PD: sub-$500 annual operating cost per vehicle.** Green Bay Police Department is adding two 2026 Tesla Model Y patrol vehicles after deploying two 2024 units since 2024. The Chief of Police cited operating costs under $500 per year per vehicle, with windshield wiper replacements as the only maintenance. A concrete, attributable TCO data point from a public agency.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO closes day one at ~$2.1 trillion, up ~19%.** SpaceX went public and closed its first trading day at approximately $2.1 trillion, up roughly 19% from its $135 IPO price in approximately 6.5 hours. The brighterwithherbert host argues this directly benefits Tesla's valuation narrative: anyone pricing Tesla's AI, robotaxi, and Optimus businesses now has an undeniable public comp where previously SpaceX was a private benchmark analysts could dispute.

- **Elon projects ~$1 trillion SpaceX revenue by 2030.** Responding to Morgan Stanley's $330 billion 2030 revenue model, Elon posted he thinks SpaceX "might be able to reach approximately $1 trillion revenue in 2030" and would "be surprised if revenues not greater than $1 trillion in 2031." Starting from $18.7 billion actual 2025 revenue, that implies roughly a 53x increase in five years. The host flags this as Elon's stated view; his timelines historically slip.

- **Elon endorses $30–50 trillion SpaceX valuation thesis.** An analyst post arguing Starship's 100x cost-per-kg reduction would spawn entirely new industries — orbital data centers, microgravity manufacturing, point-to-point Earth transit, asteroid mining — and position SpaceX as "the toll booth on all of it — AWS but for civilization" received Elon's "interesting analysis" endorsement. Separately, Elon called a $30 trillion valuation "not impossible, but definitely requires factories on the moon and Mars."

- **XAI / Grok enters Fortune 1000 via Databricks AgentBricks.** Elon posted that Grok models are now available on Databricks' AgentBricks platform — XAI's largest enterprise distribution move to date, compared by the host to Anthropic's AWS Bedrock playbook. The official XAI copy used the branding "SpaceX AI's latest models," marking the first appearance of post-merger SpaceX/XAI combined entity branding in commercial marketing material (merger closed February 2026 per the host).

- **Musk wins first legal round against German broadcaster ZDF.** Musk sued ZDF for defamation after a presenter stated he "called upon" a mob to hunt down foreigners in Ireland. His German lawyer has secured a successful cease and desist undertaking, publicly labeled a "great victory." The lawyer states he intends to investigate every instance of alleged Musk defamation across German media over approximately the past three years. ZDF and ARD together hold roughly 28% of German TV market share and are funded by a mandatory ~€18/month household fee generating over €10 billion per year.
