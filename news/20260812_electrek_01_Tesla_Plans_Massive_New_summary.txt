Tesla has filed planning permits for a 124-stall V4 Supercharger station in San Francisco, an unusually large build for a dense urban site, spotted and shared by Supercharger tracker MarcoRP on X.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Site and scale:** The proposed station would sit on a former yellow-cab storage lot at Alemany and Bayshore in San Francisco, next to US-101, with permits listing 124 V4 charging posts, switchgear, and 28 parking-lot lights. The author notes this stall count is typical of highway rest stops between major metros (e.g., I-5 sites, and ~200-stall builds in the California desert) but far larger than San Francisco's existing urban Superchargers, which top out at a handful to a couple dozen stalls.

2. **V4 hardware significance:** V4 is the Supercharger platform enabling Tesla's 500 kW charging cabinets, first switched on at full capacity last year (per the article, no specific date given); the author frames a 124-post V4 build as Tesla positioning for long-term urban charging demand, particularly for Bay Area apartment/condo dwellers without home charging.

3. **Amenity building details:** The permit includes a 400-square-foot "micro-amenity" building with two gender-neutral ADA restrooms with diaper-changing stations, a vending vestibule (including Cotti-branded coffee/hot drink machines and snack machines), and a back-of-house storage/IT/security room — described as a restroom-and-vending pavilion rather than a dining facility, contrasted with Tesla's Hollywood Tesla Diner concept.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the filing as a notable bet on urban charging density rather than road-trip traffic, and views the dedicated restroom facilities as a meaningful upgrade over typical Supercharger sites, even if less ambitious than the Tesla Diner concept.
