## Robotaxi
- Tesla launched unsupervised Robotaxi service in Fort Worth at 6 a.m. on November 2, a 143-144 sq mi geofence — its third-largest Texas zone behind Austin (272 sq mi) and Houston (254 sq mi), and the first new Robotaxi city in roughly three months. Fort Worth is the first Tesla-covered city where Waymo isn't active.
- Texas registered Robotaxi fleet hit 1,244 vehicles (671 Model Y, 573 Cybercab) versus Waymo's 1,259 — a 15-vehicle gap — with 164 added in the prior four days versus just 59 cars in June; multiple hosts project Tesla could pass Waymo's Texas count within a week or two.
- Population coverage still favors Waymo: ~9.7M people versus Tesla's ~3.4M, even though Tesla's combined Texas geofence area (~751 sq mi) is roughly 2.4x Waymo's (~307 sq mi). A 19-mile gap between the Fort Worth and Dallas zones is flagged as the likely next connection point.
- Waymo's Texas fleet leans on 863 discontinued Jaguar I-PACE vehicles plus Zeekr vans and planned Hyundai Ioniq 5s (Georgia-built); Waymo also announced a new Toyota partnership adding BZ4X EVs, with Japan testing not starting until 2027.
- App updates: operating hours extended to 6 a.m.-2 a.m., trip-sharing, rear-view camera feed, "calm mode," Grok now live in Model Y Robotaxis, improved vehicle-finding arrow with haptic feedback.
- Japan is finalizing driverless-certification safety standards (meeting November 4, effective December), targeting 10,000 Level 4 vehicles by 2030; Tesla's Japan president says FSD approval push continues but a Japan Robotaxi launch hasn't been decided.
- Tesla hiring self-driving data collection supervisors across 37 cities in 20 states, up from 26 cities/12 states a week earlier; European Cybercab tour resumes November 5 across a dozen cities through January.

## Autonomous Driving
- October Austin NHTSA incident logs: Tesla 8 reports (highest month yet, up from 5 in September) versus Waymo's 12, zero collisions for either; Tesla's most serious incidents involved Robotaxis not yielding to a fire truck and one stopping past a crosswalk and attempting to back up near pedestrians.
- FSD continues to misjudge closed express-lane crossing arms; a reported incident had a teen's FSD-engaged Model Y strike one, with a state trooper reportedly saying it was the fourth Model Y that week to hit the same arm — an unresolved edge case pending a future software version.
- Tesla launched its first owner-to-owner FSD referral program in the US: one free month of FSD (or $99 credit) per successful referral, capped at 12 uses/year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX is reportedly in talks with EchoStar to buy or gain network access to Boost Mobile (7.38M subscribers, $38.60 average monthly revenue per subscriber), per a Washington Post report; the strategic logic is owning the customer billing relationship rather than just supplying satellite capacity inside a carrier's plan. AT&T's network agreement with Boost runs through 2031.
- T-Mobile CEO Srini Gopalan said on the October 28 earnings call he sees no room for a network-rental arrangement with SpaceX, calling it a direct conflict of interest; SpaceX separately targets deploying its second-gen mobile constellation through 2027 with service turning on in early 2028.
- Starlink is introducing quarterly dynamic pricing in select US markets (parts of Kansas and Missouri), with plan prices adjustable up or down based on local supply/demand, reportedly capped at a 20% move per adjustment.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Confidence in a Tesla-SpaceX merger is softening: commentator Alexandra ("Tesla Boomer Mama") reportedly dropped her estimate from fully convinced to 85% in October to 50% now, citing the shareholder-vote bar and institutional voting-rights concerns.
- Marvell's all-stock acquisition of Lumentum is cited as a cautionary comp: the implied deal value fell from ~$1,132 to ~$849 per share after Marvell stock dropped 25% since October 28 ($55B in lost value), illustrating execution/dilution risk in any stock-based Tesla-SpaceX deal.
- Separately, one host speculates merger terms could emerge in December after the current token/equity unlock schedule concludes, floating his own $500-550 "fair price" view versus a reported $670-690 floor sought by shareholder advocate Alexandra Merz — both framed as unconfirmed speculation.
- Bloomberg reportedly ranked SpaceX first among major companies for projected net income growth (~8,000% over four years); Elon Musk replied that Tesla's growth "will be very high also."

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's "Gun Range" training compute site is tracking operational by end of November, and "Spam" (inference) by December — both renamed versions of previously disclosed projects, within SpaceX's stated 2.5-3 GW year-end compute goal.
- Grokbot usage caps were tightened mid-cycle over the weekend after Musk said serving costs were unsustainable relative to revenue; some usage has since been partially restored.

## Energy
- Tesla launched Heat Pump Optimization for Powerwall 3/3P in Europe, sending "normal"/"boost" signals via the SG-Ready standard to store surplus solar as hot water; Europe-only, requires a Tesla-certified wired installation. Electrek's Fred Lambert called it "smart" and "cheap" but noted Tesla is "late to this" versus sonnen's 2023 equivalent feature.
