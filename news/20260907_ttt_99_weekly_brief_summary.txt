## Robotaxi
- Cybercab's commercial debut landed September 3 at Giga Texas (invite-only, NDA'd, no livestream); Texas DMV registrations jumped from 1 to 45 vehicles within hours. Public rides opened Austin-wide September 5, moved up to 2pm CST on surging demand, with wait times spiking past an hour within 20 minutes.
- NHTSA opened audit AQ26002 on September 3 covering ~1,000 Cybercabs, examining how Tesla certified a vehicle lacking human-usable brakes, pedals, and mirrors under FMVSS. Bhakdi argues Tesla's certification is likely to fail on current rules but sees a path via a pending NHTSA rule change; a separate Bloomberg report claiming Tesla needs a 2,500-vehicle exemption cap was disputed by multiple hosts citing Tesla's self-certification stance.
- Tesla disclosed 1 million cumulative unsupervised Robotaxi miles (up from 380,000 at the July 22 Q2 call), growing roughly 17% week-over-week — Waymo has logged 200 million miles across 14 cities by comparison. Cybercab became hailable anywhere in Austin (no geofence) the Friday after launch; by Sept 6 a tracker put deployed count at 37-38 units, with 35 more staged near Miami airport.
- Early fares ran 20-54% cheaper than Uber/Model Y trips ($9.65-$17.12 vs. $16.99-$22.30); Tesla's fleet-purchase interest form drew 73,000 clicks, though commentators see third-party fleet sales a year-plus out.
- Nevada issued Tesla a statewide permit for up to 5,000 robotaxis in Clark County, five times Waymo's 1,000-vehicle permit granted the same day.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek matched Tesla's own NHTSA filings to three fatal crashes — a March 2026 Batavia, IL crash (unprotected left turn, driver killed) and two October 2025 crashes (Florida, Arizona) where cars sat stopped at 0 mph in live freeway lanes and were rear-ended — all marked "Verified Engaged," none reported as Tesla-related by police or press, with Tesla redacting narrative, software version, and operating-area data as it does in a reported 99.9% of filings.
- FSD Supervised fleet crossed 14 billion cumulative miles, the most recent billion added in just 28 days versus 3.5 years for the first billion.
- FSD v14.3.9 and a new always-on active-safety feature let the system take steering control to avoid a collision or intervene on apparent driver distraction, independent of FSD engagement; unclear if it's disableable. FSD v15 is now running fleet-wide on Model Y and Cybercab.
- Tesla released EU safety data ahead of an October 6 approval vote (4x fewer crashes than manual driving across 100M km, five countries approved); Musk met French officials on FSD approval, and Sweden's infrastructure minister publicly endorsed a robotaxi pilot after a demo ride.

## Financials
- Debate continues on merger valuation: one view puts Tesla near $1T and SpaceX near $2T, calling SpaceX "more proven" via Starship and compute revenue; the other notes Tesla is already profitable while SpaceX's AI-compute durability is unproven. No consensus.
- TSLA closed up ~5.4% on Cybercab launch day on above-average volume, though hosts note product events historically don't durably move the stock; Deutsche Bank reportedly bought $4B of TSLA shares around Sept 1.
- Tesla's roughly flat share price over four-to-five years is attributed by one analyst to stagnant near-term profitability, with capital redirected to autonomy, Terafab, and Optimus.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX completed a full 33-engine static fire of the Flight 14 Super Heavy booster, which Musk called "the most powerful moving object ever made by humans." Flight 14 will carry ~20 Starlink V3 satellites, targeted for mid-September; a tower catch remains a few months out.
- Morgan Stanley modeled Starship revenue reaching $3.5 trillion by 2040 based on 5,800 annual launches; Musk countered his own guess for that revenue figure is 2033.
- SpaceX secured 444 additional acres near Port of Brownsville and a reported $220M water infrastructure deal, use unconfirmed.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's compute-leasing business hit a ~$31B run rate at end of Q2, with one projection seeing $100B+ by December; Google and Anthropic remain customers even while competing with Grok, flagged by one host as a conflict of interest.
- SpaceX is building an in-house gas turbine blade foundry near Bastrop, TX to bypass an industry bottleneck booked through 2030, which Musk said let SpaceX turn on AI compute faster than rivals.
- Grok 4.6 scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at $2/$6 per million tokens versus $5/$30 for GPT 5.6.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla discontinued the Solar Roof after ~3,000 total US installs over seven years, peaking at 21-32/week versus Musk's original 1,000/week target; certified installers report six-figure losses from stranded training and tooling investment, with doubts over long-term parts availability.
- A rare-earth-free permanent-magnet motor debuted in Cybercab (18% smaller, 25% lighter), framed by Electrek as incremental given EV motors are already 90%+ efficient.
- Chinese Model Y L owners are reporting rear suspension collapse, concentrated in the first delivery batch (Sept-Oct 2025); Tesla China has not commented, and it's unclear if the US-built Model Y L is affected.
