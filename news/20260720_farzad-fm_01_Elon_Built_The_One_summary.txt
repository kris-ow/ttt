This video argues that SpaceX's durable competitive advantage over Blue Origin isn't capital but flight frequency — a reusability "practice loop" that money alone cannot buy — and surveys Falcon 9's launch cadence, Starlink's role as a built-in launch customer, Blue Origin's New Glenn setbacks, and where Blue Origin still matters strategically (national security launch and Artemis moon landers).

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9 cadence dwarfs New Glenn:** In 2025, SpaceX flew Falcon 9 about 167 times versus roughly two New Glenn flights that year, despite Blue Origin being founded two years before SpaceX (in 2000).
2. **Reuse practice compounds over a decade:** SpaceX first landed an orbital-class Falcon first stage in 2015 and first reflew a booster in 2017; by mid-2026 individual boosters were flying into the mid-30s, with some exceeding 36 flights, giving the team repeated practice at turnaround and landing.
3. **New Glenn's early flight history is mixed:** NG1 (January 2025) reached orbit but did not recover its booster; NG2 (November 2025) achieved Blue Origin's first booster landing; NG3 (April 2026) reused a booster for the first time but lost the upper stage and the commercial payload.
4. **Launch Complex 36 explosion:** On May 28, 2026, a static-fire test ended in an explosion that damaged Blue Origin's only Florida pad for New Glenn, a single point of failure that can pause the whole program, in contrast to SpaceX's multiple pads and standing booster inventory.
5. **National security launch contracts:** Under the Phase 3 Lane 2 National Security Space Launch program, maximum contract values are about $5.9B for SpaceX, about $5.4B for United Launch Alliance, and about $2.4B for Blue Origin — positioning Blue Origin as a funded government backup launcher despite its lower flight cadence.
6. **Blue Origin's engine supply role:** Blue Origin's BE4 engine powers both ULA's Vulcan rocket (two engines per first stage, first flown January 2024) and New Glenn's first stage (seven engines), splitting Blue's factory attention between external engine deliveries and its own rocket program.
7. **Artemis moonlander dual-track:** SpaceX won NASA's first lunar lander contract in 2021 (about $2.9B); NASA awarded Blue Origin about $3.4B in 2023 for its Blue Moon lander as a second Artemis path, giving NASA a schedule-risk hedge across both providers.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink as SpaceX's built-in launch customer:** By mid-2026, Starlink had more than 10,000 working satellites and about 12 million customers; in 2025 roughly 120 of about 160 Falcon flights were Starlink missions, giving SpaceX's factory a steady internal demand stream independent of outside contracts.

## Market & Competition
1. **Amazon Leo vs. Blue Origin dependency:** Amazon's Leo satellite network (formerly Project Kuiper) reported about 396 production satellites in orbit after 14 missions as of an early-July 2026 update — far behind Starlink's scale — and launches on multiple providers (New Glenn, Falcon 9, ULA, Ariane), meaning Blue Origin's factory isn't guaranteed to capture all of Leo's launch demand the way SpaceX captures 100% of Starlink launches.
2. **Blue Origin's deliberate "step by step" culture:** Blue Origin operates under a stated philosophy of proving each engineering stage before advancing, and hired Dave Limp (from Amazon operations) as CEO in 2023 to push for faster tempo after years of slow launch cadence.
