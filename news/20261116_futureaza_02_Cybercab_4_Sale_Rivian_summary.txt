This Monday news roundup from futureaza covers Tesla's new Cybercab personal-use subscription launch and related reactions, a quietly discontinued Model Y trim, SpaceX's Starship catch and the resulting media and investor response, SpaceX-linked solar manufacturing moves, and a grab-bag of EV market and competitor stories (BYD, Lucid, Rivian, EV range/tire economics), closing with audience Q&A.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab personal-ownership subscription opens:** Tesla opened Cybercab pre-orders for personal (non-commercial) use, requiring a $300/month subscription fee, geofenced delivery to specific Texas cities, one vehicle per customer, no wraps or customizations, and estimated December delivery; the host notes Elon Musk has said the fee is too high and should come down, with the host citing a lower figure (~$115/month) as what Musk has suggested it should be instead.
2. **MKBHD orders a Cybercab over a prior bet:** After reportedly being called "a chicken" by Musk, tech reviewer MKBHD placed his own Cybercab order tied to an earlier bet that he'd shave his head if Tesla delivered a $30,000 Cybercab this year; the host frames this as notable because many had doubted the vehicle would ship at all.
3. **Efficiency and battery-size claims:** The host relays a claim (attributed to Tesla/Musk commentary) that the Cybercab uses roughly a 20% smaller battery pack than previously expected due to efficiency gains, framed as allowing roughly three Cybercabs' worth of production for the battery supply of one Cybertruck, and describes the car as using inexpensive injection-molded plastic body panels available in various colors.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Entry-level all-wheel-drive Model Y discontinued:** Tesla quietly removed the most affordable all-wheel-drive Model Y trim, raising the entry AWD price by about $8,000; the host attributes this partly to a production/inventory shortage he personally observed (no Model Y inventory within 200 miles of his location as of the prior Saturday) rather than demand weakness.
2. **EV range and depreciation commentary:** The host discusses a report suggesting ~43 EV models with 300+ miles of range were on sale in the US for the 2025 model year, up from four in 2020, with a projection of around 60 by year-end; he also pushes back on claims that EVs depreciate badly, arguing used Teslas hold value well compared to other EVs like the Cadillac Escalade IQ.
3. **EV tire cost/wear discussion:** The host attributes high EV tire wear mainly to driving style and torque usage rather than any inherent EV weight penalty, citing that a Model 3's weight is comparable to a BMW 3 Series and a Model S to a Mercedes S-Class.

## Market & Competition
1. **BYD raided by Jaguar Land Rover over design similarity:** A court-ordered raid of BYD's Dutch office, initiated by Jaguar Land Rover over alleged design similarities between a BYD vehicle (around $19,000) and the Land Rover Defender, was later followed by an order for JLR to destroy seized documents because the warrant was deemed overly broad; the host characterizes the raid as an attempt to improperly obtain a competitor's information.
2. **Germany's EV sales milestone:** Per a cited report, battery electric vehicles outsold combined petrol and diesel vehicles in Germany for three consecutive months, with BEVs at 37% of October new car sales versus 24.5% for petrol plus diesel combined.
3. **Lucid Q3 results:** Lucid missed Wall Street revenue expectations (reported revenue $350 million vs. $407 million expected) and increased its credit line from Saudi Arabia's PIF fund by $1.75 billion; loss per share was $2.74 versus $2.28 expected, net loss for the quarter was $830 million, down from $978 million a year earlier, and adjusted operating loss of $726 million beat the Street's expected loss of $611 million.
4. **Rivian Autonomy Day announced:** Rivian will hold an "Autonomy Day" on December 9th; the host says he won't attend due to travel plans and describes himself as "cautiously optimistic" about Rivian's autonomy progress.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship catch draws favorable mainstream coverage:** Following a successful Starship catch (compared by the host to a prior animation of the maneuver, with the real landing described as smoother), the host cites Sawyer Merritt's roundup of headlines from CNN and Reuters describing it as a historic/unprecedented test milestone, and argues this reflects genuine reader interest in space news rather than paid influence.
2. **Starship payload-to-orbit comparison and ULA failure:** A social media post cited by the host claims that if Starship were counted as its own launch company, it would already rank second by payload to orbit, having surpassed ULA by 30%; separately, ULA's Vulcan rocket reportedly failed to deliver roughly 40 Amazon LEO satellites to orbit, with the cause not yet known.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Alphabet reportedly increasing SpaceX stake amid share weakness:** The host describes a report that Alphabet (Google) has been buying more SpaceX shares even as the stock has slid recently, contrasting this with retail sentiment he characterizes as more bearish following the Starship catch and the ULA Vulcan failure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Russian Starlink competitor struggling:** The host discusses reports of a Russian satellite constellation project (described as a competitor to Starlink) running into trouble in orbit, with a planned total around 374 satellites — a scale he notes is near the practical limit for expendable rockets; he also notes China is developing its own Starlink-style constellation.

## Energy
1. **SpaceX solar equipment order:** SpaceX reportedly ordered 100 gigawatts of solar equipment from German manufacturer Singulus in a turnkey production-line supply deal valued at over $69 million, intended to support a planned solar/compute initiative; the host also notes Tesla has begun groundwork on a roughly 3,000-acre solar-related project site near Fort Bend County, Texas.
