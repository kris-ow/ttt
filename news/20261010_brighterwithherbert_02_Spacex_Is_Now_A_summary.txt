This weekly roundup from brighterwithherbert covers a cluster of simultaneous Tesla and SpaceX developments: Germany softening its stance on FSD approval, Cybercab fleet expansion in Texas and new cities, SpaceX's Terafab chip plant ownership clarified by Elon Musk, a reported $40B SpaceX chip financing push, Grok's new multi-model backend strategy, and strong September EV sales across Europe. The host's core thesis is that Tesla and SpaceX both moved from "testing/negotiating" mode into "deployment mode" within a single week, with Musk personally resolving several ownership and strategy ambiguities (Terafab, Grok's model sourcing).

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Germany shifts toward backing EU-wide FSD approval:** Germany's transport ministry, after September talks with Tesla, said it will push for EU-wide approval of FSD "as soon as possible." The agreed basis: FSD speeding margin in Europe is capped at 10% (not the previous 50%), the driver remains fully responsible, and Tesla itself proposed renaming the European system from "FSD Supervised" to "Tesla Assisted Driving." The host stresses this is a conference statement, not a formal national approval, and the EU committee's December meeting still requires 15 countries covering 65% of EU population to vote yes (currently 8 countries, ~12.5% of population have approved); Sweden and France have not withdrawn concerns.
2. **Slovakia reportedly set to approve FSD "within days":** Per Sawyer Merritt, Slovakia's transport minister said the country will approve FSD within days, citing accident-rate data, which would make it the ninth approving country — though the host notes this is a minister's remark, not yet a formal vote.
3. **Reuters investigation into Dutch regulator pressure:** Reuters reported Tesla pressured the Dutch regulator to narrow and speed up its FSD review; the regulator says approval rests on 3,000 hours of test. The host expects this article to be cited by countries voting "no" in December.
4. **FSD 14.3.11 rollout:** A new FSD version (14.3.11) began rolling out this week.
5. **FSD continued operating through Austin flood warning:** Per tracker operator Ethan McKenna, Waymo reportedly suspended Austin operations during a flood warning while Tesla's Robotaxi/FSD paused briefly then continued; Ashok Elluswamy commented that "Tesla doesn't stop in the rain."

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab fleet growth in Texas:** Texas Cybercab registrations rose from 126 last week to 169, with 43 registered in the latest 18 hours; total Tesla autonomous fleet in Texas is 589 (420 Model Y, 169 Cybercab), up from 45 Cybercabs at the September 3 presentation. A Texas autonomous-vehicle tracker shows Waymo at 1,168 vehicles versus Tesla's 589.
2. **Austin trip volume:** Austin logged a reported record 868 Cybercab trips in a day, 670 without passengers (register unchanged since the 2nd per the host, other figures are fresher trip-count data).
3. **Dallas and Houston fleet buildup, not yet in service:** Observers estimate roughly 300 Cybercabs now in Dallas (per a local poster) and 190–200 at the Houston site via drone count, up from ~50 in mid-September; Model Y robotaxis already operate in both cities, but Cybercabs are reportedly still parked, not carrying passengers.
4. **Las Vegas buildup:** A local observer (Diego Suarez) reported the Las Vegas Strip lot nearly empty of Model Ys (only ~10 left) and roughly 30 Cybercabs present, suggesting repositioning — unconfirmed, photo-based.
5. **Permit deadlines:** Per a tracker account, permit holders must begin operations within 120 days of permit issuance, putting deadlines for Dallas/Houston/Vegas launches between roughly November 24 and December 18, though the host says the sequencing is uncertain.
6. **Austin hours extended:** Austin Robotaxi service hours extended to 11 p.m. (from 10 p.m.); Musk said the earlier cutoff was to avoid "gray kittens on gray asphalt in the dark."
7. **Robotaxi app appears in new country app stores:** The official Tesla Robotaxi app appeared in Singapore, Australia, and Germany app stores — app listing only, not live service.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX calls itself a "superintelligence company":** Following a White House directive to use "superintelligence" terminology, Musk said SpaceX is a superintelligence company and confirmed "SpaceX AI" will be rebranded "SpaceX SI."
2. **Grok to use best-available backend models, including competitors':** Musk said Grok will route tasks to whichever model performs best for the job, including Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 and other third-party APIs (Midjourney, Suno), with simple queries going to small/fast models and complex ones to large models; a fast Grok 4.8 version is planned to handle most requests once released. The host notes Anthropic is also reportedly SpaceX's largest compute customer, creating a supplier/customer dynamic.
3. **Terafab ownership clarified by Musk:** After reports (Bloomberg's Tim Culpan) that TSMC might own/operate the Terafab chip plant with SpaceX providing funding, Musk publicly stated SpaceX "will build and operate this plant ourselves," adding TSMC might sublease part of it but nothing more. Intel's CEO said Intel remains a development partner.
4. **Conflicting reports on SpaceX data center pace:** The Information reported SpaceX may be slowing new AI data-center construction; Musk denied this, saying SpaceX is "accelerating rapidly." The host flags these as contradictory given the same-week report of SpaceX seeking major chip financing.
5. **Unverified claim on compute allocation:** A poster (referred to by the host as "Djukany") claimed, without SpaceX confirmation, that 60% of SpaceX's reported 10 gigawatts of compute capacity goes to internal needs and only 40% to external customers — the host treats this as unconfirmed but notes it would imply most new chip capacity serves Grok internally.

## Financials
1. **SpaceX seeking ~$40B in financing for Nvidia chips:** FT and WSJ reported SpaceX is seeking roughly $40 billion in bank loans and bonds to buy Nvidia chips, with Oracle and Broadcom reportedly pursuing similar deals; as of the recording, unconfirmed and described by Bloomberg as preliminary. SpaceX's five-year credit default swaps hit a reported record high (~195 basis points) and SPCX shares fell 2.5% on the news.
2. **Morgan Stanley SpaceX valuation note:** Adam Jonas priced SpaceX shares around $159 (noting the stock had since moved toward ~$171 before pulling back on the debt-financing story), rated it "overweight" with a $300 price target, and said a sum-of-the-parts valuation assigns about $127/share to rockets and Starlink — implying the market was pricing SpaceX's AI business at roughly $32/share, which Jonas views as too cheap relative to large AI companies. Susquehanna raised its SpaceX price target from $144 to $173.
3. **Dan Ives and Goldman Sachs on Tesla:** Dan Ives (CNBC) said 2027 could be a "golden year" for Tesla driven by robotaxi, and separately put an 80% probability on the Tesla-SpaceX merger question while cautioning deal terms are unknown. Goldman Sachs kept a neutral rating with a $360 price target, saying the stock's direction depends more on AI/robotics updates than near-term earnings, flagging the October 21 earnings call's robotaxi/FSD/Optimus commentary as the key driver.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15 targeted for October 19:** Flight 15 is planned for October 19 per a slide, pending license approval; the flight's goal is the first attempt at catching the ship (described by a commentator as a "catch," potentially the biggest positive catalyst since the SpaceX IPO). The host cautions the date often shifts.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Airline Starlink expansion:** United Airlines said over 880 aircraft will have Starlink by end of 2026, with the full fleet converted by end of 2027. American Airlines expanded its Starlink deal to its entire core fleet (1,000+ aircraft), with installation planned starting early 2027.
2. **FCC approves next-gen Starlink satellites and direct wireless service:** The FCC approved up to 15,000 next-generation Starlink satellites and granted SpaceX permission to sell wireless communications directly from satellites without leasing spectrum from carriers; Musk said this would provide 100x more throughput than the current V1 system. The host notes this directly threatens the carrier-dependent model T-Mobile's CFO had cited as protection.
3. **India licensing dispute:** Musk said unnamed "oligarchs" are blocking Starlink to protect a monopoly, calling it a "crime against the people of India," and noted Starlink is licensed in 165+ countries and has met Indian regulatory requirements for five years without receiving a license; India's ministry called the claim baseless.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Strong September European sales:** Germany registered 12,552 Teslas (reported up 269% year-over-year, the best month since December 2022, though the host notes September of the prior year was a weak baseline of ~3,400 cars). UK sales reached 15,875 (Model 3 became the top-selling EV and second-best-selling car overall). Model 3 became Spain's best-selling car overall (first time an EV topped Spain's monthly ranking). Model Y took the #1 spot in France, in all five Nordic countries, in Korea for a third straight month, and in New Zealand EVs (up 186%); in Norway more than 1 in 4 new registered cars is a Model Y.
2. **Giga Berlin ramping output:** Giga Berlin is increasing production to 7,500 units per week starting in October.
3. **Model 3 refresh:** The Model 3 received a new 16-inch screen across all configurations with no price change.

## Energy
1. **Power Share extended to Model 3/Model Y with Powerwall 3:** Tesla Energy announced Power Share for home backup, allowing new Model 3 and Model Y vehicles (US and Puerto Rico, orders from October 1) paired with Powerwall 3 to extend home backup power beyond two days.
2. **Virtual power plant event in California:** Tesla reported 85,774 California homes with Powerwall delivered 601 megawatts to the grid during peak load, with owners compensated — described as Tesla's largest such event to date.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus labor-replacement framing:** A third-party calculation (cited by the host, attributed to "Rothman") estimated a human works ~2,000 hours/year versus a robot's ~8,700 operating hours, implying each robot could replace about four workers, and that a billion such robots would exceed the entire global workforce; Musk responded "Exactly."
