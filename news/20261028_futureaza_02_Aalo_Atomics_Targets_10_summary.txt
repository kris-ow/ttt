This video is an interview with Yasser Arafat, co-founder, CTO, and president of Aalo Atomics, a small nuclear reactor startup, about its approach to building modular "extra-small modular reactors" (XMRs). The content is almost entirely about Aalo's technology, regulatory strategy, and timeline, with only incidental, non-substantive references to Tesla and SpaceX used as analogies rather than reporting on either company's business.

## Market & Competition
1. **Aalo Atomics' modular reactor pitch:** Aalo Atomics, founded about three years ago and now employing roughly 200 people, is mass-producing small nuclear reactors it calls "XMRs" — larger than typical microreactors but smaller than traditional small modular reactors — designed to be fully modular (reactor, equipment, and even the building) so they can be transported by truck and assembled on-site, with a stated goal of cutting deployment time from a decade to under 12 months.
2. **Fuel, funding, and regulatory approach:** The company says it uses already-certified uranium dioxide ceramic fuel (the same type used in about 20% of U.S. electricity generation) rather than waiting on novel fuel types, sources uranium from a European supplier, and says it has taken no federal government funding, relying instead on private institutional and individual investors (a funding round was mentioned as ongoing but not detailed). Arafat credits Department of Energy cooperation (including at Idaho National Laboratory) and a more supportive NRC and executive-branch stance under the current administration — including executive orders aiming for new reactors operational by the U.S. 250th anniversary (July 4, 2026) — with accelerating its timeline, while maintaining that safety standards were not lowered.
3. **Sodium coolant engineering:** The company uses sodium as a coolant for its high-temperature reactor design, which Arafat argues is chemically more compatible with structural steel than water despite sodium's reactivity with water, air, and concrete; Aalo addresses this with tube-in-tube designs using micron-scale gaps and redundant monitoring systems to prevent dangerous reactions.
4. **Stated milestones:** Arafat gave three target milestones: demonstrating a full-scale, full-power 10 MW electrical reactor by the end of 2027; scaling to produce enough reactors to add a gigawatt or more of capacity annually starting in 2030; and a longer-term, undated goal of using distributed nuclear power to make currently uninhabitable or underdeveloped land habitable, noting roughly 50% of people currently live on about 3% of land.
5. **Passing Tesla/SpaceX references:** The host compared Aalo's modular, pre-fabricated deployment model to Tesla's Supercharger installation approach, and Arafat noted (without specifics) that skilled people have been leaving companies including Tesla, Apple, SpaceX, and the Boring Company to join the nuclear energy sector — both mentions are incidental rather than substantive Tesla/SpaceX reporting.
