The article covers Elon Musk's Q2 2026 earnings call comments on Tesla Semi self-driving timing, and Electrek's skeptical take on Musk's track record for such predictions.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Semi self-driving timeline given on earnings call:** Responding to a question from LightShed analyst Walter Piecyk on Tesla's July 22 Q2 2026 earnings call, Musk said Tesla expects self-driving to work on the Tesla Semi "probably around the end of this year or early next year," framing it as addressing a truck-driver labor shortage and improving safety/comfort for drivers. He also said Tesla's self-driving engineering focus is on high-volume vehicles (Model 3, Model Y, Cybercab), with Semi autonomy "taking a bit of a backseat for the next six months or so."
2. **Track record on self-driving deadlines:** The article, citing MuskOmeter data (94 tracked public Musk predictions, 8 delivered on time = ~9%, 46 failed outright), states that roughly 10 self-driving/robotaxi deadlines since 2013 have all been missed once due, including a 2016 promise of an LA-to-NY self-driving trip by end of 2017, "feature complete" FSD by end of 2019, 1 million robotaxis by 2020, "100% confidence" on Level 5 by 2021, robotaxi service for half the US population by December (last year), and Europe/China FSD approval "next month" stated in January. The current standing promise is unsupervised FSD on customer cars in Q4 2026, which Musk reiterated in May.
3. **Semi autonomy history:** Tesla unveiled the Semi in November 2017 with "Enhanced Autopilot" and a convoy-mode autonomous-following demo, promising 2019 production; this slipped to 2020, 2021, then 2022. A small batch went to PepsiCo at the end of 2022 with no mention of Autopilot/self-driving at the delivery event, and convoy mode was removed from Tesla's website. The first Semi built on Tesla's high-volume line at Giga Nevada (designed for 50,000 trucks/year) rolled out in April; Tesla told investors this week the Semi will still be "a very small percentage" of its total fleet by end of 2026.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Musk's trucker-safety framing contradicts Tesla's earlier 2022 decision to strip autonomy messaging from Semi marketing, and views the new Semi autonomy comment as an offhand answer to an analyst's question rather than a real product timeline, calling it "a mood" rather than a roadmap. He contrasts this with Aurora, which he says has driverless trucks running on Texas highways with no one in the cab, having crossed 250,000 driverless miles in March with a target of over 200 trucks this year, while Tesla has logged only 380,000 unsupervised miles in a year of robotaxi testing in Texas within a limited, low-speed geofenced area — leading him to doubt Tesla will soon expand speed/geofence enough to make Semi autonomy practical.
