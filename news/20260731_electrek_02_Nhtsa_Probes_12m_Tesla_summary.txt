NHTSA has opened a preliminary evaluation into roughly 1.2 million Tesla Model 3 (2018-2020) and Model Y (2021-2023) vehicles after 156 complaints that the front lower lateral link — a core front suspension part — detached while driving, sometimes without warning, causing loss of steering control and requiring towing.

## Autonomous Driving / Market & Competition (Regulatory)
1. **New suspension probe scope and findings:** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened a preliminary evaluation covering about 1.2 million 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y vehicles over front lower lateral link separation. Most of the 156 complainants reported no advance warning before failure, though some noted prior noises. NHTSA says it is not aware of any crashes, injuries, or fatalities linked to the issue so far.
2. **Prior related recalls:** Tesla recalled about 2,800 Model 3 vehicles in 2021 and 422 Model 3 vehicles in 2023 for suspension issues tied to production problems with the same part. NHTSA states the failures now under investigation extend beyond those earlier recalls and do not appear to stem from the same production issue.
3. **History with Model S/X suspension:** In 2020, NHTSA scrutinized Model S and Model X suspension after Tesla disputed a related recall in China; the agency closed that investigation in 2024 without mandating a recall, though it recommended a part change.
4. **Other open NHTSA scrutiny:** The article notes Tesla is separately facing an NHTSA investigation into Full Self-Driving over reduced-visibility crashes, which was upgraded earlier this year to cover 3.2 million vehicles, moving closer to a possible recall.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the no-warning failure mode is serious even though no injuries have been reported, and views the combination of 156 complaints and sudden failure as the type of pattern that historically leads to escalation into a full engineering analysis rather than case closure. He states this is the point at which regulators are supposed to act, before injuries occur.
