Tesla announced the start of high-volume production of the Tesla Semi at a new dedicated factory in Sparks, Nevada, seven years after the truck's original 2019 production target, alongside details on the redesigned truck, customer orders, and economics.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Dedicated Semi factory launched:** Tesla held a launch event at its new Semi factory next to Gigafactory Nevada, where the first high-volume unit came off the line in April. Tesla says the 1.8-million-square-foot plant can build 50,000 trucks per year, produces all variants (Standard, Long Range, and a European spec) on one line, and is heavily vertically integrated — Tesla says it builds batteries, drive axles, high-voltage distribution, cooling modules, and seats in-house, and claims the first powder-coated Class 8 truck and one of its most automated battery lines.
2. **Redesigned truck for volume production:** Tesla says it spent four years redesigning Semi "for true volume." Key changes: switch from 2170 to in-house 4680 battery cells (no new pack size given); a cheaper steel-caged rotor motor replacing the carbon-fiber-sleeved rotor; a bar-wound stator shared with Cybertruck; electric steering (first for a heavy-duty truck, removing hydraulic components); and a "Megamanifold" heat-pump thermal system shared with Cybercab. Tesla previously specified (in February) 325 miles range for Standard Range and 500 miles for Long Range at 82,000 lbs, with 1.7 kWh/mile consumption.
3. **Uptime and charging claims:** Tesla claims its Semi fleet has had over 98% uptime in 2026 and says the truck needs almost no maintenance. On a Megacharger, Tesla says drivers can regain up to 60% of range in 30 minutes; Tesla shared a Megacharger map showing coverage concentrated in California, Texas, and the East Coast, with gaps in the Mountain West and Plains.
4. **Diesel vs. electric cost comparison:** Tesla presented a chart showing diesel truck fuel costs (at 7 mpg) have risen to roughly $0.80 per mile in 2026, versus electricity costs in the $0.20–$0.30 per mile range for Semi, a gap the article says has widened this year.

## Market & Competition / Financials
5. **Customer orders and deliveries:** Tesla says Semi deliveries "start now" and it will also use Semis to haul its own freight. Trucks bearing logos from PepsiCo, DHL, US Foods, ABF, Einride, IMC Logistics, and WattEV were present at the event. The ZET SCALE alliance recently named Tesla primary supplier for 2,500 electric trucks — an order the article says would nearly double the number of electric Class 8 trucks on US roads — following Einride's 500-truck order in August.
6. **Pricing:** Tesla has not confirmed official pricing since the event; Electrek had previously reported Tesla quoting about $290,000 for the 500-mile Semi and about $260,000 for the Standard Range — up roughly 60% from the $180,000 price advertised in 2017, but said to be about $145,000 below the average price of a competing electric Class 8 truck.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert calls Semi the last Tesla vehicle program that still excites him, arguing it's now the best electric Class 8 truck available and cheaper than competitors, with efficiency claims holding up in fleets like PepsiCo's. He says Tesla can clearly build the trucks but must now sell them and expand Megacharger coverage outside California and Texas; he frames 5,000 Semi deliveries in 2027 as indicating a "real business," while 1,500 would make the factory look oversized.
