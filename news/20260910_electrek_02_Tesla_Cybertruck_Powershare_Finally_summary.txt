Tesla announced that Cybertruck's Powershare feature can now provide home backup power simultaneously with a Powerwall 3, closing a gap that previously forced owners to choose one backup source or the other.

## Energy
1. **Combined backup capacity:** Running together, the Cybertruck and Powerwall 3 provide more than three days of backup power, which Tesla says is equivalent to nine additional Powerwalls; the article notes this math checks out since a single Powerwall holds 13.5 kWh and the Cybertruck's pack is 123 kWh.
2. **Hardware requirements and cost:** Powershare pushes up to 11.5 kW into a home and requires a Universal Wall Connector and a Powershare Gateway; this hardware runs around $4,000 installed per Electrek's prior coverage, though the article notes some owners were quoted much higher, with one quoted over $30,000.
3. **Multi-year delay:** Tesla launched Powershare with Cybertruck in late 2023 and in October 2024 said Powerwall integration was coming "sometime in 2025." That slipped to a mid-2026 target announced in December 2025, which Tesla itself acknowledged was nearly a year later than promised. As recently as late August, Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill reportedly still had no firm date, telling owners only he'd share updates "when there is something new." Morrill has attributed the delay to the engineering challenge of coordinating two grid-forming devices (truck and Powerwall) across different hardware generations without a network, plus required grid-safety certifications.
4. **Broader V2G push:** Tesla launched a Cybertruck vehicle-to-grid program in Texas in February 2026 and made Cybertruck the first AC vehicle-to-grid asset in California via PG&E in April 2026; this Powerwall-compatible home backup feature is described as the last missing consumer piece of that push.
