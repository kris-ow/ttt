MDB Transportation, a Compton-based drayage operator, has launched a three-week pilot running a Tesla Semi on active port freight lanes at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, making it the second port trucking company to deploy the electric Class 8 truck in commercial service.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **MDB pilot details and Semi specs:** MDB is tracking energy efficiency, cycle time, and driver experience during the three-week deployment. The production Semi being piloted is rated for 82,000 lbs gross combination weight, up to 500 miles of range, under 2 kWh per mile energy consumption, and can recover up to 60% of range in 30 minutes via Tesla's Megacharger network.

2. **Tesla Semi production ramp:** Tesla is ramping Semi production at a new 1.7-million-square-foot factory adjacent to Gigafactory Nevada. Two trims are available: a 325-mile Standard Range and a 500-mile Long Range priced at $290,000. Analysts project 5,000–15,000 deliveries in 2026, with annual output eventually reaching 50,000 trucks. Tesla claims a one-million-mile battery lifespan.

3. **Megacharger infrastructure:** Tesla opened its first Megacharger station in Ontario, California. The company has mapped 66 Megacharger locations across 15 states, with the first Pilot truck stop locations expected to open by summer 2026.

## Market & Competition
4. **Competing operators and hardware:** Hight Logistics (Long Beach) took delivery of a Tesla Semi earlier in 2026 and plans to expand its fleet; it already operates 25 electric trucks, mostly Volvo VNR Electrics, with six dual-charging stations capable of serving 12 trucks simultaneously. NFI Industries is running 50 electric Class 8 trucks — 30 Freightliner eCascadias and 20 Volvo VNR Electrics — for port drayage out of its Ontario facility under the $27 million JETSI initiative backed by CARB and the California Energy Commission.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that MDB's pilot is a meaningful signal precisely because MDB is a traditional, commercially driven carrier rather than a sustainability showcase — suggesting the economics of electric drayage are close enough for serious evaluation. He notes Tesla's main competitive advantage is the Megacharger network and 500-mile range, but flags that Freightliner and Volvo have a head start in service networks and fleet familiarity.
