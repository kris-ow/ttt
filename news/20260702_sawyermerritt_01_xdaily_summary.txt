Critically, he described it as a modular line — designed so Tesla can easily reconfigure it as Optimus's design evolves. He indicated there will be dozens of such lines and that they can be set up much more quickly than traditional manufacturing lines. Separately, Lars shared that new Tesla vehicles now autonomously drive themselves through the factory's bumps, squeaks, and rattles test section, with cabin microphones reporting any issues back to the Tesla engineering team — a signal of increasing factory automation.

The program will feature two distinct safety scores: the standard Safety Score and a "Safety Score with FSD (Supervised)" variant — suggesting Tesla is beginning to differentiate insurance pricing based on FSD engagement, which could drive FSD adoption and create a vertically integrated data flywheel.
