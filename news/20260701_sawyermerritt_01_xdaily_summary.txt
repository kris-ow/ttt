## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Production Cybercab testing on public roads — a key robotaxi milestone:** Tesla officially announced it has begun testing production Cybercabs — with no steering wheel or pedals — on public roads in Austin. This was confirmed both by Tesla's own announcement (post [8], 8.8K likes / 795 retweets, the day's highest-engagement post) and by a follow-up sighting of the vehicle testing in Austin (post [1]). A separate post (post [7]) shared an image of the Cybercab's interior screen. The significance here is the transition from prototype/development vehicles to production-spec hardware operating on open public roads without traditional manual controls — a regulatory and engineering threshold that directly precedes commercial robotaxi deployment. This is distinct from the supervised robotaxi fleet already operating in Austin; these are production-configuration vehicles being validated on public roads.

2. **Tesla's Terafab chip-fab project lands first major hire:** Tesla has made its first prominent hire for its in-house semiconductor fabrication project, dubbed "Terafab," based in Austin. Gary Jiang — a semiconductor manufacturing veteran with nearly 18 years at Intel — has joined as Director of Tera Fab (post [2], 3.3K likes / 292 retweets). This is notable for the Tesla investment thesis because vertical integration into chip manufacturing would reduce dependency on third-party suppliers (e.g., Nvidia) for AI training and inference hardware powering FSD and Optimus. The hire of an Intel-pedigreed manufacturing executive signals this project is moving from planning into execution.

3. **Tesla Megapack: 415 MW / 1.66 GWh Australia facility reaches commercial operation:** A new Tesla Megapack battery energy storage facility — the Orana BESS — has reached commercial operation in New South Wales, Australia (post [10]). The project has a capacity of 415 MW and can deliver up to four hours of storage, totaling 1.66 GWh. The facility is valued at $415 million. This represents a meaningful revenue and deployment data point for Tesla's Energy division, which has become an increasingly important contributor to Tesla's financials.

4. **Market & Competition — BMW iX5 unveiled; Ford CEO on Chinese EV dominance:** BMW officially unveiled the all-electric iX5, priced at $81,250 with a 435-mile EPA range, 144 kWh battery, 460 kW peak charging, 800V architecture, bidirectional charging, and a 0–60 time of 4.4 seconds (post [5]). This represents a well-spec'd premium competitor to Tesla's lineup in the high-end segment. Separately, Ford CEO Jim Farley made pointed comments in a new interview, stating that Chinese automakers are "completely dominating the EV landscape globally" and that "there's no real competition from Tesla, GM or Ford" with what's coming from China (post [11]). Sawyer Merritt noted pointedly that the Tesla Model Y is one of the best-selling cars in China — implying Farley's framing may be overstated or inaccurate with respect to Tesla specifically. Farley also discussed Ford's upcoming $30K all-electric pickup truck in the same interview (post [12]).

5. **Tesla expands EV sales to Uruguay:** Tesla officially announced that Model 3 and Model Y sales will begin soon in Uruguay (post [13]), representing incremental international market expansion in Latin America.
