## Robotaxi

- **Nevada permit filing is the week's clearest scale signal.** Tesla formally applied for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Clark County covering up to 5,000 vehicles in the first 12 months post-approval — roughly 1.4x Waymo's entire current U.S. fleet. The filing specifically names Harry Reid and Henderson Executive airports. A separate Reno permit was filed near Giga Nevada; no vehicle count disclosed for Reno. A 36,000 sq ft Cybercab maintenance facility on Mohawk Street in Las Vegas is already under renovation.

- **Austin unsupervised zone expanded to full metro** — the fifth expansion since June 2025 — now including Pflugerville, Manor, I-35, and Austin-Bergstrom International Airport. Texas DMV Level 4 registrations climbed from ~40 to ~69 through the week, growing at roughly 10 new filings per day; all are currently Model Ys. Arizona's Level 4 operating plan, dated April 2026, covers nine cities across Maricopa County (4.8M people) — versus the two cities most observers expected.

- **Cybercab production crossed ~1,000 cumulative units by mid-June per Bhakdi's drone-footage model** (April ~220, May ~440, June partial ~360). Units fitted with decorative wheel caps were spotted in Houston and Las Vegas lots on June 13 — a cosmetic preparation signal Bhakdi reads as public-deployment imminent. Cybercab testing now spans approximately 20 U.S. states.

- **June 30 IR commitment is effectively missed.** The Q4 2025 earnings deck committed to paid service in Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix by June 30; as of June 14, only Austin, Dallas, and Houston are operational. RobotaxiTracker.com's community maintainer was hired by Tesla as a summer intern and cannot update the tracker through at least August 2026. A viewer cited Tesla saying four to five new cities would launch within two weeks; Bhakdi expressed uncertainty about the reliability of that claim.

- **Waymo entered mass-market brand competition during the World Cup.** Waymo aired its first national TV commercial claiming "10x safer" driving and launched an invite-only Premier membership at $29.99/month with 10% Waymo Cash back per trip — signaling it is investing in customer retention infrastructure as Cybercab ramps.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Belgium became the fifth European country to approve FSD Supervised**, signing territory-wide approval after completing ~5,000 km of internal testing. Approval cadence: Netherlands → Lithuania → Estonia → Denmark → Belgium, now averaging roughly every two days. FSD is approved in 12 countries/territories globally (~6% of all countries). Belgium and Greece are reportedly moving quickly; Germany, France, Italy, and Spain remain in review.

- **Netherlands released the first full European real-world FSD safety dataset** (April 10–June 5, 2026): 23.6 million km total, 3.5x fewer collisions than manual driving overall; 3.4x safer on highways across 16.6M km with zero collisions; 15x fewer automatic emergency braking events; 8.8x less harsh acceleration; 7.3x less harsh braking. Denmark's data showed 3.5x safer across hard-braking and hard-turn metrics after four months of use.

- **EU-wide vote slipped to October 2026 at earliest.** The June 30 TCMV meeting agenda listed only continued discussion of the Dutch authorization request — no vote. A surprise June vote is near-zero probability given required two-week advance draft circulation. Rollout could slip to early 2027. Critical downside risk: if the EU Commission formally rejects the Dutch authorization, all four provisional national approvals become invalid after six months.

- **FSD 14.3.4 released June 13** with robotaxi-facing UI strings — "Vehicle will pull over near destination," "Approaching destination. Will pull over near destination," "Driving to Market Basket" — indicating active preparation of the passenger-facing software stack. Early community review called it "by far the best overall FSD build by a wide margin." The 14.3.x series represents an architectural rebuild enabling accelerating iteration cadence. Elon confirmed on the Q1 earnings call that deployment proceeds on v14; v15 is targeted end-2026 for "super scale" (millions of vehicles, not tens of thousands).

- **Piper Sandler's Alex Potter declared FSD achieves Level 4 autonomy in most conditions**, citing a personal April drive from Missoula to Minneapolis. His note: "Tesla has solved the self-driving puzzle and FSD is ready for dissemination beyond early adopters." FSD cumulative miles crossed 11 billion, adding ~1 billion every 37 days — pace accelerating as European markets come online.

## Optimus

- **Giga Texas North Campus Optimus factory first steel went up May 27, 2026.** Permitted footprint is 5.2 million square feet — nearly the full length of the main building. Long-term output target is 10 million units/year from this single site; Apple's global iPhone output is ~230 million/year across multiple campuses. A realistic production-ready estimate from on-site observers is end-2027 to 2028, not 2026.

- **Cortex 2's 250 MW Phase 1 is live** as of April 2026; full 500 MW targeted mid-2026. Combined with Cortex 1, the Giga Texas campus now exceeds 230,000 H100-equivalent GPUs — explicitly removing compute as a ceiling on both FSD and Optimus training. The cluster dwarfs El Capitan and Frontier (each ~40,000 GPUs), the largest U.S. national lab supercomputers.

- **AI 5 chip deploys to Optimus first, not vehicles.** Taped out April 15, 2026; a single AI 5 is claimed to match ~H100 inference performance, two chips approach B200 Blackwell, and delivers ~10x performance per dollar versus AI 4. AI 4 is characterized as already sufficient for autonomous driving at a superhuman level — the sequencing is a deliberate architecture decision.

## Energy

- **Houston Megapack factory exterior construction is complete.** The Brookshire, Texas facility carries a $200M initial investment, targets 50 GWh/year of output, and will employ up to 1,500 people, with production targeted for late 2026. Adding Houston to Lathrop (40 GWh) and Giga Shanghai (20 GWh) pushes combined global Megapack manufacturing capacity past 130 GWh/year — against ~200 GWh of total global battery storage deployed in all of 2024.

- **New Mega Block product: 20 MWh AC per unit**, claiming 23% faster installation and up to 40% lower construction cost versus traditional builds. Q2 2026 Megapack deployments tracking ~2x Q1's 8.8 GWh. A $150M deployment completed in New South Wales; a 96-stall Supercharger with solar canopies and Megapack is planned for Willows, California.

- **Competing grid storage architectures emerging.** GM and Redwood Materials launched a second-life battery repurposing program with UL-certified Pack Manager hardware manufacturing underway in Sparks, Nevada; GM plans deployment at its Michigan plant beginning 2027. GM and Peak Energy announced a sodium-ion grid storage partnership targeting 10,000–20,000 cycles and 20-year life with no liquid cooling — a structurally different chemistry eliminating copper anode costs.

Bhakdi interprets the acquisition as evidence Waymo may be transitioning from LiDAR to a vision-only neural network stack — a company with genuine full unsupervised autonomy across its current operational fleet would not need proving-ground infrastructure at this scale.

## Bear Case of the Week

- **Tesla's own Denmark FSD promotional video — reshared by Elon Musk — documents five traffic violations.** Danish newspaper Politiken analyzed the Tesla Europe clip frame-by-frame and found FSD driving in a bus-only lane, making an illegal right turn, ignoring a "no entry" sign, driving on a street closed to cars, and driving on a bicycle path. Denmark's FDM (equivalent to AAA) called the findings "worrying" and "quite critical." Tesla declined to comment. The video compounds existing litigation exposure: a May 26 Tesla post showing a driver making espresso hands-free while FSD Supervised drives has 5.6 million views and directly contradicts Tesla's courtroom argument that drivers bear full supervisory responsibility. Active FSD/Autopilot litigation stands at $14.5B across 21+ tracks; NHTSA's Engineering Analysis covers 3.2 million vehicles with 80+ documented violations on file.

- **June 30 IR commitment is effectively broken.** Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings deck committed to paid robotaxi service in Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix by June 30 — 16 days away as of the close of this brief's period, with zero of those five cities operational. The RobotaxiTracker.com maintainer was simultaneously hired by Tesla and cannot update the tracker through August, reducing third-party visibility precisely as deployment is supposed to accelerate. A viewer cited Tesla claiming four to five new cities within two weeks; Bhakdi was openly uncertain about that timeline.

- **Unsupervised fleet count has not grown since May 9.** RobotaxiTracker.com shows no change across all three Texas cities for the entirety of this weekly period and the preceding five weeks — despite Texas Level 4 self-certification being live, Cybercab production estimated above 1,000 units, and infrastructure pre-positioned in multiple cities. All Cybercabs publicly observed in Texas this week still retain steering wheels. Brighterwithherbert acknowledged that robotaxi scale "is still mostly just Austin and Phoenix" as of mid-June.

- **EU-wide FSD approval now unlikely before October 2026, with rollout possibly slipping to early 2027.** The June 30 TCMV meeting will not vote; a structural downside risk exists: if the EU Commission formally rejects the Dutch authorization, all four existing national provisional approvals become invalid simultaneously after six months, removing FSD from the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, and Denmark in one regulatory action.

- **SpaceX IPO creates direct structural financial pressure on Tesla.** Tesla dropped to an intraday low of $387.31 at the exact moment SpaceX began trading June 12 — the clearest empirical evidence of forced SpaceX-rotation selling from Tesla holders. With Elon Musk's ~303 million Tesla options expiring August 15 (associated tax obligation ~$7B due January 2028), the merger speculation timeline is partly driven by his personal financial deadline rather than operational readiness — a structural conflict between Musk's incentive and Tesla shareholders' interest in operational milestones being met before any merger-dilution event.
