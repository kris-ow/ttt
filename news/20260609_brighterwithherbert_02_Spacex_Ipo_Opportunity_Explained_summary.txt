## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO Launch Details:** SpaceX is set to begin trading on Friday, June 12, 2026, under the ticker SPCX at an IPO price of $135 per share, implying a market cap of $1.75 trillion. The IPO involves issuing 555 million new Class A shares, representing approximately 4.3% of the market cap as the initial float. Underwriters have a 30-day greenshoe option that could expand the offering from approximately $75 billion to approximately $86 billion if demand warrants it. The total authorized Class A shares will rise from 6.8 billion pre-IPO to approximately 7.38 billion at minimum, or nearly 7.5 billion if the full greenshoe is exercised. Class B shares remain approximately 97% controlled by Elon Musk, and Class C shares (non-voting) remain at zero.

2. **Index Inclusion Timeline and Passive Buying:** Alexandra lays out a detailed timeline for index fund inclusions. The first wave — CRSP (Vanguard) and FTSE Russell — is expected either Thursday, June 18 or Monday, June 21, 2026, due to the Juneteenth holiday on June 19 closing markets. The second and larger wave is NASDAQ 100 inclusion on approximately July 7, 2026 (delayed from the July 4 holiday weekend). Alexandra estimates total index passive buying of between $8 billion and $18 billion, with approximately $15 billion being her central estimate, noting that purchases are float-adjusted and therefore lower than some commentators expect given only 4.3% float. Further rebalancing inclusion rounds are expected in September and December 2026 for CRSP, FTSE, and NASDAQ. The S&P 500 declined to change its 12-month profitability rule and therefore the earliest S&P 500 inclusion under current rules would be June 2027, unless a Tesla-SpaceX merger is consummated first — at which point the combined entity would be included immediately.

3. **Lockup Unlock Structure and Stock Price Projections:** Alexandra describes an unusual and deliberately engineered lockup structure where the largest early-investor unlocks are tied to SpaceX quarterly earnings calls rather than a simple 180-day period. The first major unlock — up to 30% of eligible early investor shares — occurs two business days after the Q2 earnings call, which Herbert guesses will fall on July 22, 2026. Importantly, Elon Musk personally cannot sell for 366 days from the IPO date, meaning he represents roughly 50% of the early investor pool and is excluded from near-term unlocks, so effective unlock exposure is approximately 10–15% of total shares rather than 20–30%. A Grok-generated chart (based on Alexandra's published assumptions, fed in by a community member named Aurelius) projects SpaceX stock price peaking between $275 and $300 around the July 7 NASDAQ 100 inclusion date — more than double the $135 IPO price — before declining as early investors unlock. Even in the most bearish scenario modeled (all non-Elon early investors sell 100% of holdings), Grok projects the stock does not fall below the $135 IPO price, supported by the floor of staggered index purchases.

4. **Proposed SpaceX–Tesla Merger Thesis:** Alexandra's central investment thesis is that a SpaceX acquisition of Tesla will be announced sometime between July 7 and approximately July 28, 2026 — the window between peak SpaceX stock price (at NASDAQ 100 inclusion) and the first major early investor unlock at the Q2 earnings call. Her rationale is that this window offers the highest SpaceX market cap (potentially $3–3.5 trillion), giving SpaceX maximum stock-based acquisition currency. The proposed structure is a stock-for-stock "merger of equals," meaning no cash changes hands. She speculates the new combined entity ticker would be "X," replacing both TSLA and SPCX. Alexandra holds Tesla call options expiring in August 2026, purchased at a $385 strike, as her primary expression of this thesis — she chose Tesla options over buying SpaceX IPO shares because she believes Tesla stock would double to match SpaceX's market cap if a merger of equals is announced.

5. **Tesla Shareholder Vote Math:** If a merger is announced, a Tesla shareholder vote would likely occur in approximately November 2026, with deal consummation (regulatory approvals including HSR, SEC, and Department of Defense review for SpaceX government contract firewalling) expected in Q1 2027 at best, more likely Q2 2027. To reach the required 50% plus one vote of Tesla's approximately 4 billion shares, Alexandra's math requires approximately 2 billion yes votes. Elon Musk holds approximately 17.5% post-option-exercise (~700 million shares). She estimates Vanguard and BlackRock together represent approximately 15%, and with Ron Baron and others, total friendly institutional votes reach approximately 35% — leaving approximately 15% still needed. With retail shareholders at 31% of shares, approximately half of all retail would need to vote yes. Alexandra notes that abstaining is equivalent to a no vote, making retail mobilization critical. She also theorizes that SpaceX chose Charles Schwab, Morgan Stanley, and JP Morgan as IPO underwriters/distributors specifically to obligate them to vote in favor of a Tesla acquisition, given those institutions had previously voted against Tesla shareholder proposals.

6. **Elon's August 15, 2026 Stock Option Exercise:** Alexandra flags a significant near-term financial event: Elon Musk must exercise his 2018 Tesla compensation package stock options by August 15, 2026, described as a "$7 billion event" that triggers a taxable event in January 2028. She argues this creates a strong personal financial incentive for Elon to maximize Tesla's stock price by August 15, further reinforcing her thesis that a merger announcement — which would cause Tesla stock to double — would be timed before that date.

7. **Retail Shareholder Allocation and Broker Restrictions:** Elon reportedly pushed for retail shareholders to receive priority allocation in the SpaceX IPO, requesting that every retail investor who applied receive at least one share, and specifically asked that large institutional orders not crowd out small investors. Fidelity has a firm recommendation (with consequences for future IPO access) that retail IPO buyers hold for 15 trading days — which would expire around July 7, 2026, the same date as NASDAQ 100 inclusion, which Alexandra finds notable. Schwab's minimum account threshold for SpaceX IPO participation has reportedly been reduced from $500,000 to $2,000 after pressure. Options on SPCX are not expected to be available on the first trading day; Alexandra expects them to become tradable the following Tuesday or Wednesday.
