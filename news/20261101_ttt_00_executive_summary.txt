## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Two unnamed modelers project SpaceX capex near $470 billion over the next five quarters, with quarterly revenue reaching roughly $122 billion by Q4 2027 and about $150 billion in debt; Musk pushed back, posting "our capex efficiency is greatly underestimated," per Jo Bhakdi's reporting.
- Bhakdi argues SpaceX's six-month data-center payback period (versus ~18 months for peers like Nebius and CoreWeave) makes its capital efficiency understated by the market, and pegs SpaceX's market cap at $1.86 trillion with a potential forward P/E in the 7–7.5x range if revenue targets hit — his own framework, not a SpaceX-confirmed figure.
- A Tesla–SpaceX merger announcement is "getting close" per Bhakdi, though he stresses it remains process-dependent; he expects Tesla to get a 30–40% valuation premium in any deal.
- Investor Ron Baron predicts SpaceX could be worth $20–50 trillion within a decade, per farzad-fm, resting on Starlink and orbital AI data center revenue.
- Anthropic's S-1 reportedly discloses a $125 billion compute commitment to SpaceX through 2030, per Bhakdi (citing a forthcoming separate video).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk named two new SpaceX supercomputer clusters: "Gun Range," targeted operational by end of November, and "Spam," targeted for December. Combined, per Sawyer Merritt, they represent roughly $35+ billion in GPU capacity and about 420,000 Nvidia GB300 GPUs.
- Per Bhakdi relaying Musk, "Macrohard" and "Macrohard-er" each run 220,000 Blackwell GB300 chips for training; "Gun Range" mirrors "Spam's" configuration, with Spam running 200,000 GB300s for inference. Musk said he walked the full Colossus 2 site at midnight on Halloween and reported good progress.
- Bhakdi's own extrapolation: the combined buildout could reach ~3 gigawatts and ~1.2 million Blackwell chips by year-end (up from ~450 MW earlier in the year), implying a potential ~$150 billion revenue run rate versus a cited ~$100 billion market expectation — his estimate, not a company figure.
- SpaceX's Starlink roadmap includes orbital AI data centers — satellites running AI processing in constant sunlight with laser links to Starlink — with deployment tied directly to Starship's reusable payload capacity, per farzad-fm.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Ship 42 completed static fire testing, clearing Starship Flight 15 for a November attempt to catch both booster and ship; Flight 16 is targeted from Florida's pad 39A (potentially December), with booster 23 running parallel flows, per brighterwithherbert.
- Raptor 4 is targeted at ~300 tons of sea-level thrust per Musk — a modest gain over Raptor 3's rated spec but roughly a 30% real-world jump versus Raptor 3's reliable ~230 tons; a guest speculated Raptor 4 could be roughly two years out.
- NASA posted an updated Artemis/lunar lander timeline: propellant transfer and cabin testing in Q1, landing leg/airlock demos in Q2, uncrewed tanker and lander flights in Q3, an uncrewed lunar landing in Q4, and a crewed Artemis mission targeted for 2028; a guest called Blue Origin currently ahead despite its own pad-rebuild setback.
- Hexagonal heat shield tiles exist because, per Musk, there's "no direct path for hot gas to accelerate through the gaps" — square or shingle-style overlapping tiles were rejected for seam and airflow-direction reasons, per farzad-fm.
- Flight 10 showed heat leaking through tile edge gaps despite tiles staying in place; SpaceX's Bill Gerstenmaier said a white ablative backup coating eroded to protect the structure, and a "crunch wrap" edge-sealing fix is planned for Flight 11. Metal tiles tested on Flight 10 oxidized and underperformed.
- Flight 13 gave engineers a fully intact post-splashdown heat shield to inspect; by Flight 14 the ship completed its first full orbital mission with a controlled splashdown. A heat shield researcher argued tiles still need too much inspection/repair for rapid reuse — a view Musk has directly disputed, per farzad-fm.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink's subscriber base (~13.6 million) has surpassed AT&T Fiber (~12.6 million), adding roughly 1.6 million subscribers per quarter versus AT&T's ~400,000, per brighterwithherbert.
- Starlink Mobile launched nationwide in Brazil, reaching an estimated 50 million potential customers with 7 million phones already connected for data/messaging (voice/video not yet supported).
- The FCC approved roughly 482 MHz of additional spectrum for direct-to-device satellite services over objections from AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile.
- Starship's 14th flight deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, adding roughly 10x the network capacity of a single Falcon 9 Starlink launch, per SpaceX.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX told the FAA it wants to reach 10,000 launches per year, primarily from Starbase Louisiana (designed for 30 launches/re-entries per day), and said rockets are no longer the bottleneck — FAA air traffic processes are. The FAA licensed just 182 total launches in all of 2025.
- SpaceX has built its own airspace/air-traffic management software, already used free by most major US airlines, rather than waiting on government system upgrades.

## Humanoid Bots
- A leak (per Robert Scoble, unconfirmed by Tesla) describes Tesla employees at Palo Alto wearing ~24-pound suits with 40 cameras for months, collecting home and shop-floor task data around the clock, with similar efforts reportedly at a Texas plant; Giga Berlin's camera-based data collection for "global training for Optimus" was separately confirmed by the plant in September.
- Some Fremont and Austin workers reportedly refused to wear the data-collection gear, objecting to training a robot that could replace them; in Germany, the works council reportedly has not yet been consulted on the camera program.
- Tesla's careers site shows ~45 Optimus data-collection roles across 15 US cities (most without a factory), requiring operators to wear motion-capture gear and walk 7+ hours daily; separate "Implementation Program Manager" and "fleet coordinator" listings suggest undisclosed new collection sites.
- Musk described an "Optimus Academy" of 10,000–20,000 self-learning robots plus millions of simulated robots; Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is "very close to getting great results" in its reinforcement learning loop, and dismissed rival humanoid demos as remote-controlled.
- Giga Texas's new Optimus factory (7M+ sq ft) is about 40% complete, with small-scale production targeted late 2027 and scale-up in 2028, per drone-footage tracker Joe Tegtmeyer citing Tesla.
- Tesla targets 400 MW of AI training capacity by year-end (up from ~225 MW combined today); the AI5 chip is expected in Optimus around April 2027 with an order-of-magnitude more compute than AI4, per Musk.
- John Carmack noted no robot today can do a task as simple as changing jackhammer batteries; Musk countered with a far higher bar (one-handed watch assembly while catching a baseball) that he acknowledged no robot, including Optimus, has approached. The robot arm remains the hardest unsolved engineering problem, per Musk.
