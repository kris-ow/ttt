## Brief

- Starship Flight 13: booster landing burn failed, ship survived splashdown
- SpaceX wins $1.6B Space Force launch contract
- FAA proposes waiving 13 environmental laws for launch licensing
- Q2 earnings: $28B record revenue, FCF beat, margins missed
- Bhakdi pushes 15,000-Robotaxi target to March 2027
- Tesla hits 10 millionth vehicle; Electrek flags stalled EV growth
- Cybertruck production at risk from supplier tooling lawsuit

## Financials
- Q2 revenue hit a company record ~$28 billion, beating ~$26 billion consensus, with trailing-12-month revenue topping $100 billion for the first time and automotive revenue up 23% y/y, per futureaza (Herbert/Brian).
- Free cash flow came in around negative $1 billion versus a feared negative $3 billion; cash on hand was $43.5 billion and capex ~$25 billion, per the same source.
- Margins missed expectations, which hosts attribute largely to a sharp drop in ZEV/regulatory credit revenue after a CAFE standards change; they expect margin recovery toward 22%+ in Q3/Q4 as FSD subscription revenue scales.
- Jo Bhakdi relayed a claim (his own, unverified) that only ~2 percentage points of Tesla's ~13%+ post-earnings stock decline is attributable to the actual financials, with the rest driven by a broader AI-sector selloff.
- TSLA closed at $298.32, down 2.97% on 2026-07-29, amid Fed commentary on elevated inflation; SpaceX private shares fell 3.32% to an implied $112.55, per Electrified.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla built its 10 millionth vehicle, but Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this masks two straight years of delivery declines (1.81M in 2023, ~1.79M in 2024, 1,636,129 in 2025, down 9% y/y) and a "second growth wave" vehicle still absent since it was promised in Q1 2024.
- Installed manufacturing capacity now exceeds 2.375 million units/year, but Tesla is on pace for only ~1.8 million in 2026 — over half a million units of unused capacity, per Electrek.
- Elon Musk said Tesla will open-source the discontinued Model S/X design and software, mirroring the original Roadster; per futureaza's Brian, this is plausible for niche production (likely China) but unlikely to scale meaningfully.
- Canadian Model Y delivery estimates have stretched into early 2027, attributed mainly to Giga Berlin (now the sole Canadian source) ramping capacity rather than pure demand, per futureaza.
- Tesla filed an emergency lawsuit alleging a tooling dispute with Angstrom Automotive Group is blocking access to custom Cybertruck manufacturing equipment; the filing reportedly warns component inventory could run out within days and replacement tooling would take five to six months.
- In a 70 mph real-world range test, Model Y beat its EPA estimate (310 mi actual vs. 306 mi rated), while Rivian R2 underperformed its EPA rating (253 mi actual vs. 330 mi rated), per Electrified.

## Robotaxi
- Bhakdi is pushing back his prior forecast of 15,000 Robotaxis by year-end to roughly March 2027 for "10,000 plus," though he still expects some inflection point before December, citing a slowing ramp.
- Herbert (futureaza) argues the post-earnings stock drop reflects markets wrongly extrapolating Musk/Elluswamy's ~10%+ weekly growth commentary flatly, when Tesla's real strategy is to perfect a safer FSD version ("v15") before shifting from city-by-city to state-by-state scaling.
- Software updates deepen Grok voice control (Spotify via glove box) and add route/parking memory features; silent turn-signal/hazard sounds in the cabin have sparked unconfirmed speculation about a new FSD version, per brighterwithherbert.
- Lars Moravy reportedly said (relayed secondhand, ~3 weeks old) Tesla still doesn't know the ideal driverless ride experience and is studying it as volume grows.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla data shows FSD supervised was 5.2x safer than manual driving over 65 million km across five EU countries in the trailing four months; Musk said supervised FSD "unequivocally saves lives." France has signaled it won't yet approve, and EU-wide approval requires countries representing 65% of the population.
- A Houston whistleblower lawsuit alleges a supervisor oversaw 38 FSD training vehicles alone; Brian (FutureAzA) disputes the framing (each car had a safety driver) and says the suit likely missed its 90-day filing window.
- An unverified Reddit post shows a vehicle apparently drifting into a concrete barrier captioned "FSD14 light hits a wall"; origin and cause are unconfirmed, per Electrified.
- Volvo discontinued lidar on the EX90/ES90, compensating Norwegian lidar-equipped owners €1,800 with no retrofit option; Musk said "I did try to warn them."

## Humanoid Bots
- Herbert (futureaza) expects Optimus to follow a "slow then sudden" trajectory like Robotaxi: a near-term hardware version used mainly for training data (possibly placed in employee homes), followed by a mass-production version he places in second-half 2027 targeting 10 million units/year once the factory ramps.

## Energy
- Tesla signed a $225 million Megapack contract with Neoen in Australia — Neoen's 12th Tesla battery project, pushing their combined Tesla storage capacity past 2GW, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Q2 energy deployment was 13.5 GWh, which futureaza's hosts call low in isolation but say reflects a large multi-year contracted backlog.

## Market & Competition
- Ford's EV unit lost $900 million in Q2 on ~$1 billion revenue (down 58% y/y) from reduced Mach-E output and Lightning discontinuation, even as Ford beat overall EPS estimates and raised full-year EBIT guidance to $10–11 billion; Ford plans a sub-$30,000 EV pickup with NACS and LFP in H2.
- Waymo relaunched its Ohio UI with Google Gemini integration and resumed freeway routes in Phoenix after a construction-zone-related pause, per Electrified.
- Mercedes unveiled the electric GLA (up to ~350 mi EPA, 800V, 320kW charging); Ferrari reportedly hit its 2026 Luce EV sales target (500 units, ~$320M) just two months post-unveil.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 13's booster completed a full 33-engine boost-back but its landing burn relit only 8 of 13 engines, causing a hard splashdown; the ship itself made a soft Indian Ocean splashdown, kept transmitting, deployed 20 functional next-gen Starlink V3 satellites, and completed an in-space engine relight that had aborted on Flight 12.
- Musk called the test successful, saying higher acceleration was used intentionally to test heat-shield tile retention; he confirmed SpaceX is recovering the floating S40 and said the splashdown was precise enough to have been caught by tower arms.
- Across 13 flights in 39 months, average turnaround is 99 days; Flight 14 will attempt a tower catch of the ship and a booster water landing, with SpaceX aiming for its first orbital insertion attempt.
- Musk reiterated a roughly 5–7 year timeline for people on Mars, with an uncrewed lander a few years sooner.
- Third-party analysis (cited by Electrified, attributed to an analyst called Brett) estimates ship reuse could cut cost-per-kg-to-orbit ~45–58% versus expendable Starship/Falcon 9, potentially tripling 2031 SpaceX revenue/EBIT estimates.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX won a $1.6 billion Space Force task order for 18 Falcon 9 launches from Vandenberg through end-2027, supporting missile detection and battlefield-data-relay satellites — the largest publicly announced task order under NSSL Phase 3 Lane One so far.
- The FAA proposed waiving 13 environmental/historic-preservation statutes for commercial launch licensing (safety and national-security reviews unaffected); a 30-day comment period and likely litigation lie ahead.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 13 deployed 20 fully functional next-gen V3 satellites, with two-way RF/laser communication and telemetry confirmed before they burned up after ~20 minutes; SpaceX's own summary did not claim full integration into the operational constellation, per Electrified.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Bhakdi gave SpaceX a $65–75 per-share price target for December, arguing it would be undervalued even at $70 given potential Starlink-adjacent "Colossus" progress and tens of billions in incoming capital.
- Bhakdi said Tesla's Terafab/Intel foundry deal was likely driven by Intel being the sole domestic option and financially desperate rather than technically superior, and doubts Tesla will succeed building its own memory (e.g., HBM).
