This episode of brighterwithherbert, featuring guest Jo Bhakdi (economist and host of the Pioneer Lens channel), covers a shift in Germany's stance on EU approval of Tesla's supervised FSD, growing Cybercab fleet sightings, Dan Ives' bullish 2027 outlook and merger odds, several bank notes ahead of Tesla's Q3 earnings, and Elon Musk's net worth crossing back above $1 trillion.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **EU FSD vote pushed to December; Germany shifts tone:** The EU's technical committee (TCMV) met in Brussels on October 6th to discuss Tesla's FSD Supervised but did not vote; the agenda item was continued discussion of the Netherlands' request for an Article 39 authorization, with a decision now expected in December (unconfirmed whether a vote will actually occur then). Separately, per reports cited by the host (not independently confirmed), Germany's transport minister, a member of Chancellor Merz's CDU party, is now publicly pushing for Tesla FSD Supervised to be deployed across Europe as soon as possible and has suggested Germany could act unilaterally if the EU cannot reach agreement. Bhakdi frames this as likely connected to CDU's political struggles against a rising AfD and a broader German push toward pro-innovation, EU-critical positioning, though he presents this as his own interpretation rather than established fact.
2. **Oppenheimer sees FSD as a margin and demand driver:** Oppenheimer's note argues FSD could become a key driver of both vehicle demand and higher margins simultaneously, rather than just an added feature. Bhakdi adds that at roughly $600/year in FSD subscription revenue, take-rate increases could meaningfully lift per-vehicle margin (he estimates a hypothetical 10%+ margin boost on a Model Y sale from FSD attach alone), though he does not cite a specific current take rate.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab fleet growth observed in Texas:** A third-party source (SE Robinson) reported the Cybercab fleet lot in Houston was extremely busy, estimating roughly 190–200 cabs there, with vehicles moving in and out overnight; Bhakdi notes the Pioneer Lens tracker also showed a notable daily increase in deployed units and reiterated his expectation of reaching multiple thousands of Cybercabs deployed this year, acknowledging this is a minority view.
2. **Dan Ives' 2027 "golden year" call and robotaxi/Optimus timeline:** In a CNBC interview, Dan Ives (Yorkville Ives and Company, formerly Wedbush) said he expects robotaxi expansion into more cities by early-to-mid 2027, with Optimus rollout following in the second half of the year, framing 2027 as a potential "golden year" for Tesla as it transitions from an EV company into an AI company.
3. **Robotaxi unit economics expected to surface on upcoming earnings calls:** Bhakdi expects Tesla to begin sharing robotaxi-specific profitability metrics on this quarter's earnings call or, more likely, a subsequent one, as operations scale toward full-time service. He claims widely circulated analyst estimates of robotaxi profit per vehicle (citing figures as low as roughly $2,500/year on the low end and up to roughly $15,000 on the high end, attributed to unnamed others) are "completely wrong" and expects actual figures to surprise observers; this is Bhakdi's opinion, not a disclosed Tesla figure.
4. **Weekly growth rate contextualizes Cybercab expansion:** The host notes a reported 17–20% week-over-week growth rate in Cybercab deployment, framing fleet increases (including a reported single-day addition of 40 new cabs) as consistent with that trend rather than a one-off surprise.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Ives expects Optimus rollout in H2 2027:** Dan Ives said he has not seen a clear rollout timeline from Tesla but estimates Optimus launches will come in the second half of 2027, following earlier robotaxi expansion. Bhakdi separately stated his own belief that 2027 will be "the year of the humanoid" across the industry, with Tesla among the companies launching products, calling this his opinion rather than confirmed guidance.

## Market & Competition
1. **Dan Ives puts SpaceX-Tesla merger odds above 80% by end of 2027:** Ives said he continues to believe there is "very possible... over an 80% chance" that SpaceX and Tesla merge by the end of 2027. Bhakdi said he personally would put the odds "maybe a little higher than 80%."
2. **Analyst reactions to Tesla's delivery beat are mixed:** UBS maintained a neutral rating with a $385 price target, noting deliveries beat consensus by roughly 5% but flagging that energy deployment misses could modestly weigh on the earnings model. Goldman Sachs reiterated a neutral rating with a $360 price target, expecting improving vehicle revenue from the Model Y long-range ramp but cautioning that higher costs and capex could limit 2026 EPS upside; Goldman separately said robotaxi, FSD, and humanoid developments will matter more for the stock than Q3 earnings itself. Bhakdi pushed back on treating higher capex as a negative, arguing it should not be lumped with EPS as if it were a bad sign.
3. **Bhakdi's competitive thesis for Tesla heading into 2027:** Bhakdi argues (restating a view he says he held roughly 18 months prior) that 2027 will be a reckoning year for legacy automakers in Europe, the U.S., and Japan, and that Chinese EV makers face intensifying domestic competition and reduced government support, leaving Tesla relatively well positioned due to U.S./Europe tariff protection and its core-business cash generation — his own analysis, not sourced to a bank or filing.

## Financials
1. **Tesla Q3 earnings set for October 21; Bhakdi expects a beat on margins:** Bhakdi said he expects strong Q3 results given low discounting, favorable pricing, and higher FSD take rates, arguing that if capex is treated as a positive investment rather than an earnings drag, Tesla will "strongly beat" on an adjusted basis; he flagged potential additional upside from updated FSD take-rate disclosures or bullish robotaxi ramp commentary on the call.
2. **Elon Musk's net worth crosses $1 trillion again:** Musk's net worth rose to just over $1 trillion (stated as $1.02 trillion), up $41.5 billion in a single day, as both Tesla and SpaceX shares rose.
