This is a live-streamed reaction show (futureaza with Dr. Know It All and the Tesla Life crew) covering Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings release and subsequent earnings call, mixing financial analysis with extended banter, Halloween decorations, and audience Q&A. The core thesis discussed: Tesla missed EPS estimates by a penny but showed a blowout FSD attach rate and robotaxi mileage growth, while Optimus and Terafab timelines remain multi-year and "early stage."

## Financials
1. **Q3 GAAP EPS misses by a penny:** Tesla reported GAAP EPS of $0.22 versus a Wall Street consensus of $0.23. GAAP operating income was $2.7 billion (a 56% year-over-year decrease) yielding a 2.6% operating margin; GAAP net income was reported alongside non-GAAP income figures, with free cash flow positive at over $1.5 billion and operating cash flow at $6.3 billion. Total cash and investments ended the quarter above $45 billion.
2. **Capex guidance revised down; new borrowing facility secured:** CFO Vaibhav Taneja revised full-year capex guidance down to approximately $20 billion, to be funded primarily from operating cash flow. Tesla also secured capacity to borrow up to $30 billion to support expected capex growth but does not plan to draw on it this calendar year.
3. **Revenue roughly flat; margin puzzle pieces:** Quarterly revenue was roughly flat at $28 billion year-over-year. Automotive margins excluding credits rose sequentially from 16.3% to 18.1%, aided by a higher APAC delivery mix. Energy gross margins jumped from 20.4% to 33.1%, driven by tariff refunds and warranty true-ups from a vendor cost settlement. Services and other margins hit an all-time high of 15.9% (up from 14.1%), helped by supercharging, insurance, and robotaxi. Net income included a $142 million mark-to-market loss on Tesla's SpaceX holdings, offset by a roughly $290 million Bitcoin mark-to-market gain.
4. **Backlog and deliveries:** Tesla ended the quarter with its highest vehicle backlog in four years, with deliveries constrained primarily by production rather than demand; it posted two consecutive quarters of vehicle deliveries over 480,000. Model Y was said to be the top-selling BEV in Europe and top-selling passenger car in South Korea, per preliminary data, with records also set in Australia, Japan, and Taiwan.
5. **Panel questions the 30-cent robotaxi target:** Panelists were skeptical Tesla will ever offer retail robotaxi rides near the oft-cited 30-cents-per-mile target, speculating it may instead refer to internal operating cost rather than consumer pricing; on the call, Taneja said Tesla is not at 30 cents today but sees "a path" to it via scaling.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate surges to over 75% in North America:** Tesla reported FSD attach rate reached over 75% of new North American deliveries, up roughly 20 percentage points from the prior quarter, with cumulative paid FSD customers surpassing 1.7 million globally — over half now subscription-based. Management described this as the primary driver of vehicle demand, with customers reportedly asking showroom staff for "an FSD drive" rather than a specific model.
2. **Hardware 3 upgrade path remains undefined:** Tesla has acknowledged Hardware 3 vehicles cannot support unsupervised FSD; panelists estimated the eligible pool (owners who purchased FSD on HW3 vehicles) at perhaps 100,000–400,000 cars, based on an assumed historical ~10% take rate, but no clear free-upgrade plan or timeline was given on the call.
3. **Real-world FSD glitches persist:** Panelists shared firsthand anecdotes of FSD misbehaving — looping in parking lots, attempting to back into occupied spots or chains, failing at angled versus pull-through charger stalls, and one host describing the car nearly reversing into another vehicle at a supercharger. These were cited as evidence Tesla is still doing cautious, localized validation before wider unsupervised rollout.
4. **Tesla building proprietary AI-based mapping/navigation:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is building its own mapping and navigation architecture (not reliant on third-party map data) integrated with voice control and personalized/fleet learning, with initial elements rolling out as part of the V15 release.
5. **China and competitor dynamics:** On the call, Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy argued no competitor is close to matching FSD's capability, including in China, and said Tesla is working with Chinese regulators for broader approval; they also referenced Tesla's recently released robotaxi safety report showing extremely low incident rates.
6. **EU-wide FSD approval expected within months:** Musk said Tesla is approved in many European countries already and hopes for EU-wide approval "within a few months," saying he'd be surprised if it takes longer than Q1.

## Robotaxi
1. **Austin Cybercab fleet up 10x since launch; cumulative unsupervised miles up 8x quarter-over-quarter:** Musk said the Austin Cybercab fleet has grown roughly 10x since its ~month-ago launch, with cumulative unsupervised robotaxi miles growing 8-fold quarter-over-quarter on sustained double-digit weekly growth, nearing 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles by quarter end. Austin service hours have expanded to 6 a.m.–midnight, with plans toward 24/7 operation and higher-speed roads by year-end.
2. **Dallas expansion confirmed; multi-city rollout continues:** Ashok Elluswamy confirmed Tesla will launch robotaxi service in Dallas next month, extending the same software stack (also used in customer Model Ys) to additional cities; the company said it is already operating/preparing in six cities.
3. **Customer-owned Cybercab deliveries targeted by end of 2026 but limited:** Lars Moravy said Tesla is trying to get Cybercabs to individual customers by end of 2026, though nearly all current production is going to Tesla's own robotaxi fleet; Musk reiterated Cybercab will primarily be "transport as a service," not an owned product like the Model 3/Y. The panel was skeptical, with one host doubting anyone receives a Cybercab before the end of 2027 and comparing it to a prior public bet/prediction by a tech reviewer (unnamed on the call).
4. **Customer FSD-unsupervised robotaxi fleet entry targeted ~Q2 next year:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla suspects "somewhere around Q2 next year" is when the first customer-owned vehicles could be turned on as part of the robotaxi fleet, pending geofencing and fleet management requirements.
5. **Cybercab production line has 50% fewer parts than any prior Tesla vehicle:** Lars Moravy said the Cybercab line has 50% fewer parts than any previous Tesla vehicle, calling it one of the easiest Tesla products to scale manufacturing-wise; he said listed production capacity (previously cited elsewhere as over 125,000 units/year) reflects current operating rate, not the line's true ceiling.
6. **Robotaxi profitability and revenue impact expected to build through 2027:** Management said robotaxi revenue/profitability won't move the needle this quarter but should become "noticeable" around Q2 next year and a "big deal" by Q3/Q4 2027, using exponential growth math on unsupervised miles.
7. **Zero safety incidents claimed in Tesla robotaxi safety bulletin:** A Tesla executive posted a safety bulletin during the call (per the host, reading from X) citing zero robotaxi-related injuries, zero airbag deployments, and zero pedestrian/cyclist collisions to date — figures the host called impressive though unverified beyond the bulletin itself.
8. **Pricing positioned below rideshare competitors, not yet near 30-cent target:** Musk said Tesla's robotaxi cost base differs from ride-share competitors because Tesla has no driver and manufactures its own vehicles, and stated a long-term ambition for Tesla to capture "a majority of all passenger miles on Earth" where permitted to operate freely.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Fremont high-volume line now in early production:** Musk said Tesla has begun "early production" on a high-volume Optimus line in Fremont, with most equipment for a roughly million-unit-per-line capacity already installed, but said reaching an annualized rate of 20,000 units/week will likely take at least a year, possibly longer.
2. **Hand remains the primary hardware bottleneck:** Both Musk and an unnamed Tesla engineering voice on the call identified the hand — particularly bespoke gears and squishy, sensor-laden fingers that must withstand wear without damage — as the most difficult unsolved mechanical problem, more complex than the robot's locomotion actuators.
3. **Optimus training relies on self-play, not crowdsourcing:** Ashok Elluswamy and Musk said Optimus lacks the vast fleet data advantage Tesla has with cars, so training requires building thousands of physical Optimus units (on the order of 10,000–20,000) to generate self-play data in an "Optimus Academy," bootstrapped initially from human demonstration data.
4. **Non-scripted Optimus demo video expected in roughly one to two months:** Musk said Tesla aims to release an Optimus video demonstrating non-pre-programmed, AI-driven behavior within about a month or two, explicitly distinguishing it from "canned demo" humanoid robot videos from competitors.
5. **AI5 chip integration into Optimus targeted around April next year:** Musk said integrating Tesla's AI5 chip into Optimus — offering an order of magnitude more compute than AI4 — is targeted for roughly April 2027 (next year, per publish date), calling it a major capability step-change given Optimus's far greater degrees of freedom versus a car.
6. **Optimus external shipping timeline: end of next year:** Musk reiterated he expects the combination of hand design, AI software, and AI5 chip integration to converge "around the end of next year," which he set as the target for delivering Optimus to companies outside Tesla, with industrial/dangerous-task use cases prioritized first.
7. **Second Optimus production line under construction in Austin:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla has also started constructing an Optimus factory in Austin in addition to Fremont, intended to produce significantly more robots than the Fremont line.
8. **Analyst-sourced timeline uncertainty:** Independent of the call, one panelist (John) said unnamed internal sources indicated Optimus scaling could land anywhere between Q1 and Q4 of next year, citing persistent hand and "brains" (software) issues — explicitly flagged by the speaker as a wide, uncertain range from unverified internal sources.

## Energy
1. **Megapack demand near sellout for 2027:** Musk said Tesla is "almost sold out" of all Megapacks for 2027 and is ramping production as fast as possible, with gross margins expected to increase meaningfully; energy storage deployment for the quarter was 13.7 GWh, which panelists noted was below some expectations of over 20 GWh.
2. **Megapack 3 and in-house LFP batteries:** Vaibhav Taneja said Tesla began deliveries of Megapacks using in-house-manufactured LFP battery cells in Q3, and R&D spending included pre-production ramp costs for Megapack 3.
3. **Data-center-driven energy demand and future "megapod" product left vague:** In response to an analyst question about a previously mentioned "megapod" product tied to distributed compute/power, Musk said it's "still early days" with no clear sense of scale yet, suggesting more clarity by Q1 or Q2.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi entered production in Q3:** Musk confirmed Tesla began production of the Tesla Semi truck during the quarter, with semi-truck sales expected to start hitting financials in Q4 per a chat reference (flagged by the host as an AI-sourced and thus unreliable claim).
2. **5 million vehicles/year target by ~2030 reiterated; factory expansion continues:** Musk reiterated a target of approximately 5 million vehicles per year by around 2030. Lars Moravy said factories are running at record rates, Tesla applied for permits to expand Giga Berlin, and the company started a new Semi factory in Reno. Panelists debated and dismissed a separate in-chat claim about "30 million vehicles by 2030," concluding it likely referred to a cumulative figure or was a mishearing.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab capital needs and timeline:** Musk said Terafab capital investment will be in the "tens of billions" through early 2028, potentially exceeding $100 billion long-term, with the cost split between SpaceX and Tesla still being determined. First test chip production is targeted for around May/June next year, with useful-volume chip production aspirationally targeted for around Q3 2028. Tesla's first (research) fab equipment orders have mostly already been placed.
2. **No merger commentary permitted:** Management explicitly stated they would not address any questions about a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger on this call, after Musk said the prior day's SpaceX call had already covered what needed to be said on the topic.
