Electrek's coverage centers on Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings release and the newly announced Tesla-SpaceX merger review, with several follow-on pieces dissecting specific claims in Tesla's shareholder letter and earnings call.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Tesla and SpaceX form merger-review committees:** Jameson Dow reports that Elon Musk announced, during SpaceX's Q3 earnings call, that Tesla and SpaceX have each formed a "special committee" of "independent board members" to explore a potential merger — coming just months after SpaceX's IPO and its second-ever quarterly report. Musk said he thinks a merger would be a good idea because "we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity," a claim the author disputes as both untrue and irrelevant to an automaker. The author argues, as his own view, that the merger would let Musk collect stock from his 2025 pay packages (at both companies) regardless of whether operational milestones like Mars colonization or robotaxi/robot targets are ever met, since the packages reportedly auto-vest on a merger; he also contends Tesla's new "auto-vote" shareholder procedure and Musk's majority SpaceX voting control make shareholder approval likely, and he compares the move to the SolarCity and Twitter-debt transactions, which he calls similarly disadvantageous to shareholders. He further argues the dilution would affect ordinary retirement savers because both companies sit in major stock indices, noting SpaceX was added to the Nasdaq 100 shortly after its IPO. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/20/tesla-and-spacex-take-first-step-towards-merger-would-dilute-all-shareholders/)

## Financials
2. **Tesla Q3 2026 results: margins up, profit still down:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla posted $28.05 billion revenue (flat year-over-year, in line with the $28.06B consensus) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.48, beating the $0.45 consensus but down 4% from $0.50 a year ago. GAAP net income fell 43% to $787 million ($0.22/share), including a $288 million unrealized bitcoin gain and a $142 million unrealized loss on Tesla's SpaceX equity stake. Total gross margin was 19.6% (up from 18.0% a year ago); operating income fell 56% to $718 million (2.6% operating margin vs. 5.8% a year ago) as opex rose 39% to $4.78 billion, with R&D up 69% to $2.76 billion and stock-based comp up to $1.17 billion. Capex was $4.73 billion, free cash flow was $1.53 billion, and cash/investments stood at $45.4 billion. Services and other revenue grew 33% to $4.61 billion with a record $734 million gross profit. The article notes capex through nine months ($13.01B) is only about half of Tesla's "more than $25 billion" full-year guidance, meaning Q4 spending would need to more than double its largest quarter yet — leading Lambert to suggest the 2026 capex plan may be "quietly slipping into 2027." Tesla also said Robotaxi reached nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles by quarter-end. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-tsla-q3-2026-financial-results/)

3. **FSD "subscription" count includes outright buyers, tied to pay-package definition:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's Q3 letter states 1.74 million active FSD "subscriptions," up 67% year-over-year, under a footnote definition that follows the 2025 CEO Performance Award and "includes both upfront payment and monthly subscriptions" — meaning owners who bought FSD outright (some years ago, at prices up to $15,000) count toward the figure needed for Musk's 10-million-subscription pay-package milestone. Tesla stopped selling FSD outright on February 14, 2026, moving to a $99/month subscription for new buyers; Tesla added 260,000 net subscriptions in Q3, its best quarter yet. Lambert calculates that at the Q3 pace it would take about eight years to reach 10 million, or about 12 years at the trailing-12-month pace of roughly 700,000/year (the award runs until 2035). Tesla also reported more than 75% of North American Q3 deliveries included FSD subscriptions, a figure Lambert notes Tesla doesn't define (unclear if it includes introductory offers or reflects ongoing payment). Lambert's take: counting outright buyers isn't inherently wrong, but calling the metric "momentum" obscures that many of those buyers were sold unsupervised self-driving Tesla hasn't delivered, and Tesla won't disclose how many people actually pay monthly. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-fsd-subscriptions-1-74-million-musk-pay-package/)

4. **Battery-supply explanation doesn't match falling prices, Lambert argues:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's Q3 letter states "battery availability continued to be the limiting factor in increasing auto volumes in the quarter" and claims "the largest backlog for our passenger vehicles in four years," without quantifying the backlog. Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles (down 2% YoY from 497,099), with Model 3/Y down 1% and "other models" (Cybertruck, Semi, remaining Model S/X) down 48% to 8,295 units. Tesla built 464,391 vehicles, delivering 22,141 more than it produced — the second straight quarter of drawing down inventory, which fell to 11 days of supply from 27 in Q1 and 15 in Q2. Lambert's own calculation: Model 3/Y production ran at roughly 1.83 million units annualized against Tesla's listed 2.25+ million units of installed capacity, or about 81% utilization. Tesla's letter also cited "lower vehicle average selling price" as a reason revenue didn't grow. Lambert's take: a genuinely supply-constrained automaker would raise prices rather than cut them, so the more likely explanation, in his view, is a mix of partial demand recovery and a deliberate Tesla decision to route battery cells toward Megapack, Cybercab and Semi instead of car sales. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-battery-supply-limiting-sales-prices-falling/)

## Energy
5. **Energy revenue falls 16% on tariff refunds despite higher deployments:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's energy generation and storage revenue fell 16% year-over-year to $2.88 billion in Q3 even as deployments rose 10% to 13.7 GWh (short of the roughly 15.9 GWh analysts expected). Tesla's letter attributes the drop "partially" to "tariff-related customer refunds" without disclosing the amount, recipients, or accounting treatment. Segment gross margin rebounded to 33.1% from 20.4% in Q2, but gross profit still fell to $954 million from $1.07 billion a year ago. Year-to-date deployments are up 11% to 36 GWh while revenue is down 6% to $8.43 billion. Tesla said storage demand is at its "highest-ever order rate and backlog," driven by data centers; xAI (now part of SpaceX, per the article) bought another $269 million of Megapacks this year after $430 million in 2025. Tesla also shipped its first Megapacks built entirely with its own Nevada-made LFP cells, and started production at Megafactory Texas (50 GWh planned capacity). Lambert's take: the energy segment's margin has swung sharply all year (39.5% in Q1, 20.4% in Q2, 33.1% in Q3) largely due to unquantified tariff effects, and while refunding customers for tariffs is commendable if true, he notes pointedly that Tesla's own CEO backed the official who "installed those illegal tariffs in the first place." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-energy-revenue-drops-tariff-customer-refunds/)

## Autonomous Driving
6. **Unsupervised FSD delayed to Q2 2027, redefined as joining Tesla's Robotaxi fleet:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla AI chief Ashok Elluswamy told the Q3 earnings call that unsupervised FSD for customer-owned cars is now targeted for Q2 2027, but described it as owners adding their cars to Tesla's managed Robotaxi network rather than a car that can drive anywhere. This would restrict unsupervised operation to Tesla's existing small Robotaxi geofences across six US metros, which the article says stick to low-speed routes and avoid highways; in the Bay Area the service remains supervised because Tesla won't release data required by the California DMV. Tesla reported nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised Robotaxi miles by end of Q3, a month after Cybercab joined the Austin fleet; the article notes third-party tracking this spring showed only dozens of fully driverless Teslas on the road, far short of Musk's prior predictions, while Waymo already operated in Orlando and Tampa before Tesla and has surpassed 3 million miles. Lambert's take: treats the Q2 2027 date skeptically given a decade of missed deadlines, and argues the promise has been narrowed from "drives you anywhere" to "works inside a Tesla-controlled geofence," calling it "the goalpost moving of all goalpost moving"; he also notes Elluswamy admitted safety remains the limiting factor and Tesla doesn't actually know the real timeline. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-unsupervised-fsd-q2-2027-robotaxi-fleet/)

## Robotaxi
7. **Cybercab to reach customers this year, before software that can drive it:** Jameson Dow reports Tesla VP of Engineering Lars Moravy said on the Q3 call that Tesla aims to get Cybercabs into customer hands by the end of 2026, while AI chief Ashok Elluswamy said unsupervised FSD for customer cars won't arrive until Q2 2027 — leaving what the author calculates as a roughly half-year gap during which Cybercabs, which have no steering wheel or pedals, would be inoperable by their owners. Musk also said on the call that Cybercabs would mostly function as Tesla-owned transport-as-a-service vehicles, which the author notes appears to contradict the call for Cybercab fleet buyers Tesla put out on the day of its Cybercab launch event. The author's take: either Tesla delivers cars "usable by precisely nobody" for six months, or the vehicles will be confined to the same small six-city/two-state Robotaxi zones already in operation (far short of Musk's prior prediction that Robotaxi would cover half the US population by the end of last year), or — as the author suggests — the timelines are simply unreliable. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-wants-to-sell-you-a-useless-car-with-no-steering-wheel-this-year/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's authors are uniformly skeptical of Tesla management's Q3 narrative: they argue the "battery constrained" explanation for flat sales doesn't square with falling prices, that the 1.74-million FSD "subscription" figure is defined to flatter Musk's pay-package milestones rather than inform investors, that the Q2 2027 unsupervised-FSD promise represents a narrowed and still-unreliable goalpost, and that the proposed Tesla-SpaceX merger — paired with Tesla's new auto-vote mechanism — is framed by Jameson Dow as a vehicle for Musk to secure pay-package stock regardless of performance, at shareholders' and even outside retirement savers' expense.
