Herbert and Jo Bhakdi discuss a burst of evidence — Texas DMV registrations, geofence expansion, hiring posts, and street footage — that they argue shows Tesla's Cybercab robotaxi ramp accelerating ahead of a Cybercab launch event on Thursday, September 3rd. The core thesis is that these operational signals (production, hiring, capex commitments) are more reliable leading indicators for the stock than public perception, and that the market has not yet priced in how fast the fleet could scale.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas DMV registrations surge:** Tesla added 124 robotaxis to its Texas fleet over 5 days, including 45 Cybercabs registered in a single day (up from roughly 6-7 previously), plus 79 Model Ys added on August 27th. This brings the total Texas-registered robotaxi fleet to 314 (45 Cybercabs, 269 Model Ys), per the hosts.
2. **VIN numbering suggests large existing Cybercab count:** Bhakdi says VIN numbers observed (e.g., in the 2,400 range) suggest that, if VINs count from zero as is typical, over 2,000 Cybercabs may already exist in the wild — a claim he says he made earlier and was doubted on, now feeling more confident about (unconfirmed, inferred from VIN sequencing).
3. **Austin geofence expansion:** The Austin robotaxi service area expanded for the first time in about 10 months, growing roughly 9% to about 264 square miles, extending further north and west toward Flugerville; Dallas's geofence was also reported expanded around the same time.
4. **Street testing footage:** Multiple videos show driverless Cybercabs (no one inside, no steering wheel/pedals) performing pickup/drop-off practice runs in Austin, including one navigating a Whole Foods parking lot; Tesla's Robotaxi account posted "Getting my shopping done" in response.
5. **Cybercab structural safety guide released:** Tesla published a guide detailing Cybercab's high-strength zones — ultra-high-strength steel on A/B pillars and door rings, high-strength steel overhead rail, with plastic/glass-fiber-reinforced exterior panels — aimed at reassuring the public about occupant safety.
6. **Night-shift fleet hiring expands to new metros:** Tesla posted night-shift robotaxi fleet supervisor job listings in roughly 11 new metro areas beyond current Texas, Arizona, Florida, and Bay Area operations, including Atlanta, Nashville, Bellevue, San Diego, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Michigan, Washington DC, and Georgia; these roles cover overnight charging, cleaning, inspections, and maintenance to prep vehicles for next-day operation.
7. **Bhakdi's stock/ramp thesis:** Bhakdi argues Cybercab production ramp and the scale of hiring are the two most reliable "central indicators" of Tesla's trajectory, since large capital and HR commitments signal high internal certainty rather than speculative hype. He states a cumulative probability of over 90% that the fleet reaches 10,000 live Cybercabs within the next nine months, and separately notes he and the host have previously floated a (non-guaranteed) estimate of 10,000 Cybercabs produced by year-end. He predicts a possible "sell the news" dip after Thursday's launch (expecting only a small number of cars, likely under 25, in service by that Friday) followed by a ramp that outpaces last year's slower pattern.
8. **Morgan Stanley comparison:** Bhakdi contrasts his own bullish projection (north of a million Cybacabs eventually) against a Morgan Stanley estimate of 30,000 Cybercabs by 2030, framing this as the key disagreement investors need to resolve.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comparison to Waymo:** Bhakdi argues Waymo's reliance on visible sensor arrays ("stick" bumpers) undermines public perception of its intelligence versus Cybercab's sensor-minimal design, and cites an unnamed OpenAI-affiliated commentator who reportedly said self-driving would need AGI and wouldn't arrive until the early 2030s — which Bhakdi characterizes as representative of mainstream skepticism.
2. **Elon Musk's "glow" narrative and Optimus:** Bhakdi frames Musk's public reputation as cyclical, currently in a trough, and argues a visibly successful Cybercab public launch could trigger a broad reputational "glow up" that lifts sentiment toward Optimus and other ventures as well, though this is his own interpretive framing rather than a factual claim about the products themselves.
