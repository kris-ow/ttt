Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call featured opening remarks from Elon Musk and CFO Vaibhav Taneja, a robotaxi/Optimus deep-dive from VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy, and an analyst Q&A covering capex, robotaxi scaling, Optimus supply chain, energy storage demand, and chip roadmap. The core narrative: record Q2 deliveries driven partly by FSD attach rates, a heavy investment cycle across robotaxi, Optimus, Terafab, and energy, with margins pressured by one-time items and rising costs, and management framing 2026 as a peak-capex year ahead of expected outsized returns.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries and demand resurgence:** Musk said Model Y is now the best-selling car of any kind in the world. Taneja said Q2 deliveries were a record with sequential growth of 60% in Americas, 27% in APAC, and 12% in AMIA, with Model Y setting records in the Netherlands, Australia, and New Zealand; Tesla exited Q2 with its largest order backlog since 2023.
2. **FSD as a demand driver:** Musk said many customers are effectively "buying FSD with a car attached." Taneja said ~55% of North America deliveries in Q2 had an FSD subscription enabled at delivery, and paid FSD customers reached nearly 1.5 million globally (55% upfront purchases, 45% subscriptions), with future FSD monetization growth expected to come mainly from subscriptions.

## Financials
1. **Automotive gross margins declined:** Automotive margin excluding regulatory credits fell sequentially from 19.2% to 16.3%. Taneja attributed this partly to a $230 million warranty/tariff benefit in Q1 that didn't repeat; excluding that, margins would have been roughly flat, plus rising interest-rate subvention costs and commodity price increases.
2. **Energy margins fell sharply on one-time items:** Energy gross margin declined from 39.5% to 20.4%, driven by a ~$240 million warranty true-up for legacy cell issues, non-repeat of a $200M+ Q1 tariff benefit, and declining industrial storage ASPs amid competition; management expects energy margins to normalize in the mid-to-low 20% range long-term.
3. **Service and other margins hit an all-time high:** Service and other margin improved from 9.2% to 14.1%, driven by volume growth and better fleet cost management across used cars, supercharging, service centers, and insurance, with some investment embedded for future robotaxi scaling.
4. **Capex guidance and financing:** Taneja reiterated capex for 2026 will exceed $25 billion and will keep growing over the next two-to-three years for robotaxi, Optimus, semiconductor fab, solar manufacturing, and AI compute; Tesla is securing debt facilities allowing borrowing up to $30 billion to help fund this investment cycle.
5. **Net income and free cash flow items:** Net income benefited from a $1 billion mark-to-market gain on SpaceX holdings, offset by ~$300 million in FX losses and ~$100 million in Bitcoin losses. Free cash flow was negative in the quarter as capex more than doubled sequentially, with further increases expected in H2 2026.
6. **Operating expenses rising on R&D and litigation:** OpEx increased sequentially due to pre-production ramp costs for the semi-truck, Optimus, Cybercab, and other AI initiatives, additional compute depreciation, and litigation expense charges; management expects R&D-driven OpEx growth to continue into 2026 and beyond.
7. **Capex philosophy — speed over maximal efficiency:** Musk said Tesla is deliberately not optimizing for maximum capital efficiency, favoring faster execution even if somewhat less efficient, arguing this yields higher NPV; he characterized the scale of simultaneous manufacturing buildouts as possibly the fastest industrial scale-up in the US since World War II.

## Robotaxi
1. **Safety record and scale:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents (any reported incidents involved other actors hitting stationary Tesla vehicles), which he framed as validation of Tesla's camera-only approach versus lidar/HD-map-based competitors.
2. **Exponential growth trajectory:** Unsupervised miles driven have grown at double-digit weekly compounding rates since early 2026 (Musk separately cited "more than 10% a week" in miles driven), and Elluswamy expects this rate to continue through the rest of the year; expansion time to new cities is trending toward near-zero, with an eventual goal of state-wide rather than city-by-city operation.
3. **V15 FSD software underpinning the fleet:** The current robotaxi fleet runs early versions of V15 FSD, with roughly 40% of ~7 planned major improvement tracks already merged; management expects continued safety/capability gains as V15 development completes.
4. **Cybercab production tied to demand, requires dedicated driving data:** Manufacturing targets for Cybercab are aligned to the growth rate of unsupervised miles. Because Cybercab is an entirely new chassis, Tesla must first accumulate driving data using modified robotaxi vehicles retrofitted with steering wheels and pedals before scaling Cybercab deployment; the fleet also spans seven US markets currently, per Taneja.
5. **Vertical integration and reliability as the binding constraint:** Musk said Tesla plans to remain fully vertically integrated in robotaxi rather than pursue third-party ride-share distribution partnerships, and that the primary constraint on scaling is achieving very high ("nines of") reliability rather than demand, which he expects to outstrip supply.
6. **City-by-city expansion strategy explained:** Executives (Elluswamy, Lars Moravy, Tom Zhu) said expanding across multiple cities rather than concentrating in one is deliberate — to prove the software stack generalizes, to work through operational (not just software) issues in a controlled way, and because transportation regulations vary by city/state with no single federal framework.
7. **Regulatory environment:** Moravy said Tesla has made progress with NHTSA on FMVSS rules moving toward acceptance of purely autonomous vehicles, calling recent developments in New Jersey "a little bit disheartening," and emphasized Tesla prefers regulations that set performance goals rather than prescribe specific technical solutions.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus manufacturing is the hardest scaling challenge Tesla has faced:** Musk said Optimus lacks an existing supply chain (unlike EVs, which could tap existing non-powertrain automotive suppliers), forcing significant in-house/insourced production; the Fremont production line (former Model S/X plant) is being built out. The ramp will follow an S-curve with an unusually long, flat initial phase due to the newness of nearly every part.
2. **Optimus 4 will be far more vertically integrated:** Musk said Optimus 4, to be built in Austin, will have much more in-house/vertically integrated production (including PCB work) than Optimus 3, with an aspirational goal of roughly 10 million units/year versus roughly 1 million/year for Optimus 3 — while cautioning this scale-up is "insanely difficult."
3. **Dexterity as the key design goal:** Musk emphasized Optimus is designed for full human-level (and eventually superhuman) hand dexterity, describing the human hand as an extraordinarily complex benchmark to match.
4. **Data flywheel strategy:** Ashok Elluswamy described a multi-source Optimus training approach: observing Tesla factory workers performing tasks, a dedicated small high-quality demonstration data team, broader internet video data, and eventually a reinforcement-learning loop ("Optimus Academy") where bots practice and self-improve, using the same end-to-end AI approach (V12–V15) developed for FSD.
5. **Supplier investment in Optimus/robotaxi compute:** Musk thanked TSMC (building a fab in Arizona) and Samsung (fab in Texas) for multi-billion-dollar investments in AI compute capacity, Panasonic for battery cell investment, and Micron for prioritizing memory allocation to Tesla amid tight industry supply.

## Market & Competition
1. **Terafab and AI chip roadmap:** Musk said Tesla will announce the Terafab location and details separately from the earnings call, calling it crucial to avoid being constrained by AI chip supply for Optimus production; equipment orders have been placed for a development fab in Austin combining lithography, logic, memory, packaging, and chip testing under one roof. He said an upgraded AI4 chip is expected in production around mid-2027, AI5 in volume production around the second half of 2027 (initially for Optimus), and expressed strong confidence in the in-development AI6 chip design.
2. **Digital Optimus / Grok partnership and Megapod compute:** Tesla is developing "Digital Optimus," an AI system for generalized computer/screen use built on the same end-to-end approach as FSD, done in partnership with SpaceX/Grok, which acts as a higher-level task manager. Tesla is also building "Megapod" units pairing Tesla AI4 chips with x86 compute, deployable at sites including Superchargers (citing roughly seven gigawatts of Supercharger power capacity) to distribute AI compute.
3. **SpaceX–Tesla collaboration deepening (not a merger discussion):** Asked directly about combining SpaceX and Tesla, Musk said he couldn't discuss such a topic on the earnings call and deferred to general counsel Brandon, who noted Tesla deepened its SpaceX relationship earlier in 2026 via an investment and framework agreement covering projects like Terafab and Digital Optimus; Starlink connectivity is being integrated into Cybercab and other vehicles.
4. **Tesla Semi autonomy timeline:** Musk said Tesla expects to get self-driving working on the Tesla Semi around the end of 2026 or early 2027, but is deprioritizing it for the next roughly six months in favor of focusing self-driving resources on high-volume Model 3/Y and Cybercab.

## Energy
1. **Energy storage deployment growth:** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of energy storage in Q2, a 53% sequential increase and the second-largest quarter for the energy business, per Taneja.
2. **Data center demand and grid balancing thesis:** Musk described strong future demand for Megapack driven by AI data center power smoothing (citing training runs where power draw can drop ~70% in about 100 milliseconds) and grid balancing for intermittent solar/wind; he said SpaceX has purchased many Megapacks for this purpose, and floated that batteries alone could roughly double effective US power availability given an estimated ~1.2–1.3 terawatts of installed capacity versus roughly 0.5 terawatts average usage.
