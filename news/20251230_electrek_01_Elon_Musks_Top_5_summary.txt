Electrek reviews five Elon Musk predictions for 2025 that it says failed to materialize, framing them as part of a recurring pattern of missed Tesla timelines.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Delivery growth reversed into a second annual decline:** Musk said in late 2024 he foresaw 20-30% volume growth in 2025; instead Tesla's annual deliveries fell to approximately 1.64 million vehicles, down from the prior year per Tesla's latest Q4 estimates, marking a second consecutive annual decline even as global EV sales grew 25% this year.

## Robotaxi
2. **Robotaxi coverage and fleet size fell far short of stated targets:** Musk said in July 2025 he expected autonomous ride-hailing covering roughly half the US population by year-end, and had previously said Tesla would have "over a million robotaxis" on the road in 2025; he later scaled back to "500 Robotaxis" in Austin by October, then to about 60 in November. The article states Tesla's actual Austin Robotaxi fleet is only about 30 vehicles, most not in operation most of the time and still requiring in-car safety monitors.

3. **Promised "epic" demo did not appear:** Musk said in summer 2025 Tesla would deliver the "most epic demo ever" by year-end; the article says this never happened and reports it is tied to the new Roadster, which has already been delayed five years, with Musk now reportedly discussing pushing the demo to April 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
4. **Tesla Semi production slips again:** Tesla had officially guided to start-of-production for the Semi in 2025, but the company confirmed in late 2025 that volume production slipped into 2026; the article notes the Semi was originally promised for 2019.

## Humanoid Bots
5. **Optimus production goal unmet:** Musk had targeted "thousands" of Optimus robots working in Tesla factories by end of 2025, and separately cited a goal of producing 5,000 to 10,000 Optimus units in 2025. The article states there's no official count but no evidence Tesla produced thousands or even hundreds in 2025, citing supply-chain reports of program delays and noting Tesla has not unveiled the next robot version; demonstrated capability remains limited to simple tasks like handing out water bottles, often via teleoperation.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's own commentary is minimal beyond framing the predictions as a repeating pattern, quoting Musk's own line that "the ability to predict the future is the best measure of intelligence" as an implicit rebuke. The piece also includes a highlighted reader comment (not the author's own view) arguing Musk is primarily motivated by Tesla's stock price, that shareholders care more about promises than results, and calling Tesla's valuation (cited as a P/E of 300 and 17x price/sales) "completely ridiculous," plus a comment suggesting the Robotaxi program is more about creating an impression of progress than substance due to a high crash rate among safety-monitored vehicles.
