The host reads Anthropic's IPO filing as evidence for the SpaceX thesis. The filing shows frontier-lab revenue surging and compute economics turning profitable, and it discloses a very large compute commitment to SpaceX. His core argument is that this demand supports SpaceX reaching a $100B annualized run rate by the end of 2026 and makes the stock, at a roughly $2 trillion market cap, look undervalued.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Anthropic's growth and compute economics:** The host cites the filing as showing Anthropic revenue up 31x in five quarters, from $0.4B to $11.6B in Q2 2026. He calls the compute-cost chart the most important in the AI buildout. Since Q1 2026 Anthropic has spent less than $1 on compute per $1 of revenue, falling from above $1 to 72 cents and now 48 cents. He says compute cost per revenue dollar fell about 50% in one quarter and then about 30% in the next. Operating margin has also turned positive. He treats Anthropic as representative of other frontier labs.
2. **$120.5B Anthropic commitment to SpaceX:** Citing Cern Basher's reporting, the host says Anthropic disclosed it expects to spend up to $120.5B on SpaceX's compute infrastructure through 2029. He puts that at roughly $40B a year (perhaps $30B depending on how this year is counted). It would mean about $3.1B per month in SpaceX revenue (about $37.2B annualized) once all capacity is delivered, up from the $1.25B per month previously disclosed. All contracted capacity is expected to be delivered by December 2026.
3. **90-day termination clause:** After initial non-cancelable periods expire in early 2027, either party can terminate on 90 days' notice. The host argues this favors SpaceX, because Musk believes compute spot prices will rise, so SpaceX could reprice upward. He concedes the commitment is not guaranteed but says the surrounding evidence gives conviction.
4. **Run-rate projection and demand chain:** Cern Basher's projections, which include Alphabet, Reflection AI and an undisclosed customer, put SpaceX's compute rental revenue at about $8.3B per month. That implies a $100B annualized run rate by the end of December. In the host's breakdown, an unannounced customer is about $3B per month and Anthropic is $3.1B. He argues demand flows from frontier models to unlimited compute needs at SpaceX, hyperscalers and neoclouds, then to chips, memory and fabs. He says SpaceX has cornered the ability to build compute on Earth and, starting in earnest in 2028, in space.
