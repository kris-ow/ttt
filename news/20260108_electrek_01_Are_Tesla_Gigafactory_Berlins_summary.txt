The article (including an editorial "Take" section by Fred Lambert) argues that collapsing European sales combined with an escalating labor dispute could be setting up Tesla to scale back or close Gigafactory Berlin while blaming the union rather than demand weakness.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **European sales have reportedly fallen below pre-Berlin levels:** The article cites "new data from 2025" claiming Tesla's European sales have fallen below levels seen in 2022, before Giga Berlin had fully ramped production, when Tesla relied entirely on imports. The author argues this undercuts the original rationale for building the factory (localization, tariff avoidance, logistics savings).
2. **Capacity vs. demand mismatch:** The author states Giga Berlin has capacity to produce more than 375,000 Model Ys annually, while Tesla's total European sales (including imported Model 3s and a small number of Model S/X units) were only about 235,000 vehicles in 2025 — a gap the author characterizes as a "financial bleed." Canada's counter-tariffs on the US reportedly pushed some Model Y shipments to come from Berlin, which the author calls only a minor offset.
3. **BYD opening a new European factory:** The article notes, without detail, that BYD is launching a new factory in Europe at the same time Tesla's European demand is weakening.

## Financials / Market & Competition
1. **Tesla–IG Metall tensions ahead of works council elections:** Per a Handelsblatt report cited in the article, Gigafactory Berlin director André Thierig has drawn a "red line" against the IG Metall union's demand for a 35-hour workweek, and management reportedly warned that if IG Metall gains influence in upcoming works council elections, future investment in the plant would be halted. Handelsblatt is quoted: "If the German trade union IG Metall were to expand its influence in works council elections, investments would be halted." Thierig is also cited comparing Grünheide's productivity unfavorably to Tesla's U.S. and Chinese plants and suggesting the company would prioritize other locations if a "hostile" union takes over the works council.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues this situation looks like a setup: he contends the sales data shows Giga Berlin is financially redundant, and speculates Tesla is positioning a union victory as a scapegoat so it can frame a future closure or downsizing as a response to "anti-business" labor practices rather than an admission that its demand growth story has stalled. He suggests this would let Tesla reduce European overcapacity and resume importing cheaper cars from China while avoiding the appearance of failure to investors.
