This video from theteslaspace covers a behind-the-scenes look at the Boring Company's Las Vegas Loop tunneling operations (based on footage from independent creators Farzad and Dirty Tesla), plus updates on Starlink's new portable receiver, an upcoming Starship test flight, a Tesla FSD variant for older hardware, and Tesla's expanding Semi charging network.

## Market & Competition
1. **Boring Company automates tunnel construction:** The Boring Company's Las Vegas Loop, which connects the Convention Center, the city's airport, and three major hotels, has reportedly produced roughly six times more transportation tunnel length than the rest of the US combined over the past 25 years, according to the host. The company has moved from twelve on-site workers per boring machine down to two, with the next-generation Prufrock 6 machine designed for full automation with zero people in the tunnel, controlled remotely from Boring Company headquarters in Bastrop, Texas — including a machine currently tunneling in Dubai.
2. **Tesla Model 3 repurposed as underground hauler:** The Boring Company uses a modified Tesla Model 3 — stripped of standard car features and converted into an industrial buggy — to haul heavy concrete tunnel segments underground. Because of the load, its range drops from over 300 miles to just 15–20 miles, but Tesla's self-driving hardware allows it to be remotely operated from Texas.
3. **Continuous mining machine in testing:** The Boring Company's Prufrock 5 machine is the first to achieve simultaneous digging and tunnel-wall construction (rather than the traditional stop-and-start process), which the host says could at least double tunneling speed. It's currently running test tunnels at Bastrop ahead of deployment to a full-scale loop project later this year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink Mini Dish 2 launches:** SpaceX introduced the Starlink Mini Dish 2, a compact, low-power receiver with a built-in battery designed for mobile, grab-and-go use in areas without reliable power (disaster zones, war zones, protests). Early tests show speeds of roughly 1–20 megabits per second — much slower than standard Starlink — with the host suggesting speeds could improve once Starlink's V3 satellites, to be launched via Starship, come online.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Next Starship test flight scheduled:** SpaceX's next Starship test flight (its 13th) is scheduled for Thursday, July 23rd.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD V14 Lite brings newer AI to older hardware:** Tesla is beta testing FSD V14 Lite, a version of its self-driving software adapted for Hardware 3 vehicles that cannot be upgraded to Hardware 4. An X user reviewing the beta reported an improved interface, smoother highway driving with better lane-change and passing decisions, more confident city-street handling, and less hesitation pulling away from stop signs, though self-parking remains weak. Two drivers reportedly completed a 4,200-mile trip from Key West, Florida to Cape Flattery, Washington on V14 Lite in a Hardware 3 car with zero manual driving inputs, and continued on toward Alaska. Tesla VP of AI software Ashok Elluswamy has said this FSD version is likely close to wide release.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Tesla Semi Megacharger station opens:** Tesla opened its first public Megacharger station for the Tesla Semi in Bloomington, California (near San Bernardino, roughly an hour east of Los Angeles), featuring six stalls capable of delivering over 1 megawatt of peak charging power. This supersedes an earlier, lower-powered (750 kW) station near Ontario International Airport shown off in March, which is no longer listed on Tesla's charging locator.
