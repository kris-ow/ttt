Electrek reports that South Korean battery material supplier L&F Co. slashed the disclosed value of its cathode-material supply deal with Tesla from $2.9 billion to $7,386 — a cut of over 99% — which the article argues is strong evidence that Tesla's 4680 battery program and the Cybertruck, its only vehicle using 4680 cells, are in serious trouble.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **L&F contract gutted:** L&F announced in early 2023 a $2.9 billion contract to supply high-nickel cathode materials to Tesla for its 4680 cells; in a regulatory filing, L&F revealed the contract's value has been written down to $7,386, citing only a "change in supply quantity" without explicitly naming the cause.
2. **Cybertruck demand weakness cited as context:** The article recaps its own prior reporting on Cybertruck demand problems through 2025 — discounted financing in March, 0% APR incentives by June amid piling inventory, and discontinuation of the cheapest Cybertruck trim in September. It states Giga Texas has production capacity of 250,000 units/year but the Cybertruck is currently selling at a run rate of roughly 20,000 to 25,000 units annually (these figures are the article's own reported numbers, not newly sourced in this piece).
3. **4680 cell's only current application:** The article states the Cybertruck is currently the only Tesla vehicle using Tesla's own 4680 cells, and reasons that declining Cybertruck production reduces or eliminates demand for 4680 cells and thus for L&F's cathode material.

## Robotaxi
4. **Cybercab dependency noted:** The planned Cybercab is also intended to use 4680 cells; the article notes Musk has said it will launch without a steering wheel in early 2026, but flags that Tesla has not yet solved level 4 autonomous driving, and warns the program could face volume limitations similar to or worse than the Cybertruck's.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's own editorial section argues the 99% contract reduction effectively means the deal was cancelled, implying Tesla is not ramping but possibly winding down 4680 production, and calls this "not a good look" for a program originally marketed (per a Battery Day) as a "holy grail" for low-cost EVs — a promise the author says remains unfulfilled five years later. A quoted top reader comment (from user stetrain, not verified by the author) lists several original Battery Day promises — a standardized 4680 format adopted industry-wide, 54% more range, 56% lower $/kWh, a 520-plus mile Plaid Plus, and a $25k car — asserting none have materialized, and claims competitors BMW and Rivian are using 46XX-format cells from other suppliers in mass-market vehicles.
