This video, hosted by cernbasher with a Tesla-affiliated guest, walks through a series of user-submitted and Tesla-published FSD clips illustrating real-world capability: an elderly driver regaining independence, first-time riders reacting to hands-off driving, an off-road parking-escape torture test, night-time deer avoidance, and FSD correctly interpreting a police officer's hand signals in traffic. The framing throughout is that FSD is handling edge cases — sensory, social, and physical — that are difficult or impossible for human drivers to match.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Accessibility case study — 93-year-old driver:** A Tesla-produced video features a 93-year-old woman in San Diego County who had been driving for 75 years but was tiring on daily drives and going out less; FSD is credited with restoring her independence to visit family without waiting on others. The Tesla guest shared a personal anecdote that FSD served as a "lifesaver" for his wife during a period when a retinal tear made night driving very difficult for her.
2. **First-time rider reactions:** Three separate clips show women trying FSD (in supervised/hands-off demo mode) for the first time, reacting with nervousness, laughter, and eventual comfort — including one rider testing a highway on/off-ramp merge and a subsequent automated parking-spot selection in a lot. The guest noted this reaction pattern repeats consistently across many customers he has personally ridden with, and recalled his own wife adopting FSD after a stalk-based engagement roughly two years prior (around the version 12 release).
3. **Off-road parking-escape torture test:** A Cybertruck owner (channel: Electric Cyber Solar Farmer) ran an FSD "parking escape" test on a steep hill and tight gap next to a house; the vehicle initially struggled, backed up, and reconsidered a route before successfully navigating through a narrow gap that the host said he "would not have tried."
4. **Night-time deer avoidance:** Dashcam footage shows a Tesla traveling at speed (initially ~47 mph, later cited around 35 mph at detection) swerving into the other lane to avoid a deer that appeared suddenly at night with degraded headlights. The guest argued the car's camera height and always-on, simultaneously-stitched multi-camera processing likely let it spot the deer before a human driver's eye would, though he acknowledged a trained human eye could sometimes match it.
5. **Police hand-signal recognition:** In a clip from Nate (Tesla Driven Lifestyle), a police officer manually directing traffic waved a Tesla forward with a small hand gesture while in FSD/self-driving mode, and the car correctly interpreted the signal and proceeded. The guest called this "remarkable," noting the gesture was subtle and barely visible even to the viewer, yet FSD correctly parsed roughly three or four arm waves as the cue to move.
