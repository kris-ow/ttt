A Tesla Model YL test mule has been spotted driving on Interstate 280 in the San Francisco Bay Area — the first time the extended-wheelbase, 6-seat SUV has been photographed on American roads, strongly suggesting Tesla is preparing the vehicle for the North American market.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **First US road sighting of Model YL prototype:** The vehicle was photographed on I-280 Northbound near Tesla's Palo Alto headquarters and Fremont factory by user Michael R Carroll, who shared the image on X. This follows earlier drone footage from Gigafactory Texas showing what appeared to be Model YL bodies-in-white shipped from China, but a prototype on public roads represents a more advanced stage of US preparation.

2. **Model YL specs and market context:** Tesla launched the Model YL in China in August 2025 at approximately $47,000. It features a 150mm longer wheelbase, 180mm more overall length, and a 2-2-2 captain's chair configuration for six passengers. The vehicle has since expanded to Australia, New Zealand, Southeast Asia, and secured European type approval. In China, the Model YL accounts for roughly one-third of all Model Y sales.

3. **Musk's prior skepticism vs. current evidence:** When the Model YL was announced, Musk said US production wouldn't begin until late 2026 and added it "might not ever" come, citing the "advent of self-driving in America." Tesla is also discontinuing the Model S and Model X later in 2026, which would leave the Model YL as the only Tesla capable of comfortably seating six adults.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitive gap and lineup pressure:** With the Model S and X discontinuation, Tesla faces a meaningful gap for buyers needing more than five seats. Three-row crossovers from Hyundai, Kia, and Volkswagen are gaining EV market share in the segment a US Model YL would target.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek argues the US Model YL launch was always a matter of when, not if, given strong global demand and the coming discontinuation of the Model S and X. The author estimates a US price point of $51,000–$53,000, calls Musk's "end of 2026" production timeline "increasingly conservative," and suggests Tesla could begin taking US orders before the end of 2026, with a late 2026 or early 2027 launch realistic.
