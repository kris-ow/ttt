This is a live panel recap of Tesla's Q3 earnings, combining pre-show speculation about a Tesla–SpaceX merger, a line-by-line read of Tesla's financial deck, and reaction to the live Q&A call featuring Elon Musk, Vaibhav Taneja, Ashok Elluswamy, Lars Moravy, and other executives. The panel's core thesis: Tesla's underlying operating results (robotaxi mileage growth, FSD attach rate, cash flow, backlog) were strong, but Tesla's communication/presentation was viewed as weak next to SpaceX's polished call the prior day, and the panel sees the SpaceX–Tesla merger — not quarterly earnings — as the dominant near-term catalyst for TSLA.

## Financials
1. **Free cash flow and cash position beat expectations:** Tesla reported free cash flow of $1.5 billion for the quarter, versus a consensus the panel described as expecting roughly negative $4.5 billion; cash and investments ended the quarter at over $45 billion (Vaibhav Taneja cited "over 45 billion" on the call), which the panel called an increase of roughly $1.9–2 billion.
2. **Margins improved broadly:** Automotive gross margin (excluding credits) rose sequentially from 16.3% to 18.1% per Taneja; the panel's own read of the deck cited a figure near 19.6%, up from about 16% last quarter. Energy gross margin rose from 20.4% to 33.1%, and services/other margin hit an all-time high of 15.9% (up from 14.1%).
3. **R&D and capex:** R&D spend rose 69% year-over-year to nearly $3 billion, attributed largely to AI/compute, chip development, and pre-production costs for Optimus, Megapack 3, and the Semi. Full-year capex guidance was revised down to approximately $20 billion; Tesla also secured up to $30 billion in new borrowing capacity but said it does not plan to draw on it this calendar year.
4. **Bitcoin and SpaceX mark-to-market items:** Net income included a $142 million mark-to-market reduction from Tesla's SpaceX equity holding, offset by an approximately $290 million mark-to-market gain on Bitcoin.
5. **Panel reaction — strong quarter, poor delivery:** Several panelists (Herbert, Nick, James) argued the substantive results (reduced capex, positive FCF, record backlog, margin expansion) were among Tesla's best quarters, but that Musk's and executives' muted, unscripted delivery on the earnings call failed to communicate this clearly, unlike SpaceX's more polished call the day before.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet and mileage growth:** Elon Musk said the Cybercab fleet in Austin had grown by roughly a factor of 10 since launch about a month earlier, with cumulative unsupervised miles described as growing exponentially; Ashok Elluswamy separately cited roughly 3 million cumulative unsupervised miles to date, up from the 1.82 million figure in the Q3 deck (dated September 30), implying growth the panel estimated at around 14–20% week-over-week (panelists differed on the exact rate). Taneja said cumulative unsupervised miles grew 8x during the quarter with weekly double-digit growth throughout.
2. **Expansion plans:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla will launch robotaxi service in Dallas next month, with the same software scaling to additional cities alongside Model Y robotaxis; Tesla's safety report separately named Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio as additional cities, though no firm dates were given on the earnings call itself. Lars Moravy said Tesla aims to get Cybercabs to customers (for ownership, not just fleet use) by the end of 2026.
3. **Unit economics:** Taneja said Tesla is not yet at its target cost of 30 cents per mile for robotaxi but sees "a path" via scaling operations; management reiterated that Cybercab will primarily be offered as transport-as-a-service rather than sold to individual owners, unlike the Model 3/Y/Roadster lineup.
4. **Unsupervised FSD timing for customer cars:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla expects to enable some first customer vehicles to join the robotaxi fleet in unsupervised mode around Q2 of next year, contingent on geofencing and fleet management readiness.
5. **Cybercab production capacity:** The Q3 deck listed Cybercab production capacity at greater than 125,000 units/year; Lars Moravy clarified this reflects current operating rate rather than the line's full capacity, which he said is significantly higher, and noted the vehicle has 50% fewer parts than any other Tesla vehicle built to date.
6. **Operating hours and safety-driven expansion:** Tesla said robotaxi operating hours have expanded from an initial 6am–9pm window toward 24/7 operation by year-end, contingent on safety validation rather than regulation per se; a Tesla executive said the company is deliberately risk-averse and that remaining bottlenecks are largely technological before becoming regulatory around Q1–Q2 next year.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate jumped:** FSD attach rate on new North American deliveries reached over 75%, up from roughly 55% previously per the panel's reading of the deck; Taneja said this was approximately 20% higher than Q2 and noted over 1.7 million paid FSD customers globally, with more than 50% now on subscriptions for the first time.
2. **New regulatory approvals:** Tesla received four additional FSD regulatory approvals in Europe (Croatia, Slovenia, Czech Republic, Slovakia); Musk said he expects EU-wide approval possibly within a few months, and would be surprised if it takes longer than Q1.
3. **New AI-based mapping system:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is building its own AI-based mapping and navigation architecture (rather than relying on third-party maps), integrated with voice control and personalized/fleet-learned preferences, with initial trip start/end features rolling out as part of the V15 release and expanding over subsequent months.
4. **FSD as the primary demand driver:** Musk and Taneja both said FSD is now the key driver of vehicle demand, with Elluswamy noting showroom customers now ask specifically "can we get an FSD drive" rather than asking about specific models.
5. **FSD revenue scale:** In response to an analyst's estimate that FSD annual recurring revenue is "approaching a billion dollars," Taneja confirmed the order of magnitude was correct and said Tesla will share more FSD-specific metrics (including churn/retention) as it scales further.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production started, but a year+ from customer shipments:** Musk confirmed Optimus "early production" has begun on a high-volume line in Fremont, said the ramp is an S-curve with a shallow, bumpy initial slope, and said he expects it will take at least a year (possibly longer) to reach an annualized rate of 20,000 units/week. He said he expects design, AI, and reliability issues to be solved around the end of next year, which is the target timeframe for delivering Optimus to companies outside Tesla.
2. **Training approach — "Optimus Academy":** Musk and Elluswamy described a plan to build roughly 10,000–20,000 Optimus units to generate self-play training data in a dedicated "Optimus Academy," since (unlike cars) there is no existing fleet generating relevant data; an Optimus demo video showing non-pre-programmed capabilities was said to be coming in about a month or two.
3. **AI5 chip integration:** Musk said integrating the Tesla AI5 chip into Optimus — providing roughly an order-of-magnitude compute increase over AI4 — is targeted for approximately April next year and is viewed as a major capability unlock.
4. **Hand design remains the hardest hardware problem:** An Ashok Elluswamy and Musk both said the hand/fingers remain the most challenging electromechanical component, more complex than locomotion actuators, due to the need for durability combined with delicate, sensor-laden dexterity.
5. **Initial use cases:** Musk said the earliest commercial Optimus deployments will likely be industrial, repetitive/dangerous tasks requiring round-the-clock operation, before expanding to consumer/home use as production scales.
6. **New Austin Optimus factory:** A Tesla executive said Tesla has started constructing an additional Optimus factory in Austin, intended to produce significantly more robots than the Fremont line.

## Energy
1. **Megapack sold out and margin improvement:** Musk said Tesla is "almost sold out" of all Megapacks for 2027 and is ramping production to meet demand, expecting gross margins on energy storage to increase meaningfully; Taneja reported energy deployments of 13.7 GWh for the quarter (second-best on record) with gross margin improving from 20.4% to 33.1%, aided by tariff-related fund recognition and a warranty true-up from a vendor settlement.
2. **Data center demand driving growth:** Taneja said the energy business is seeing "extreme demand from data centers" and that Tesla is working to increase production accordingly.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record delivery backlog, production-constrained:** Taneja said Tesla had two consecutive quarters of vehicle deliveries over 480,000 and exited the quarter with its highest backlog in four years, with deliveries constrained primarily by production rather than demand; battery supply remains a limiting factor on production according to the panel's reading of the deck.
2. **Model Y leadership in key markets:** Taneja said preliminary data indicates Model Y was the top-selling BEV in Europe and the top-selling passenger car in South Korea, with new records in Australia, Japan, and Taiwan.
3. **5 million vehicles/year target:** Musk reiterated a goal of reaching approximately 5 million vehicles per year by around 2030, a target Lars Moravy also referenced in discussing factory expansion (new permits applied for in Berlin, a new Semi factory started in Reno).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger process status and panel speculation:** Musk said each side has formed special committees of independent directors to evaluate a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger, and stated there is no agreement yet; panelists (particularly Alexandra Merz) identified likely independent committee members (e.g., Jack Hartung, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson, with Robin Denholm, James Murdoch, Joe Gebbia, and JB Straubel flagged as having potential conflicts or having previously recused themselves). Panelists speculated — without confirmation from the companies — about possible exchange ratios (ranging roughly from 3x to 4.2x SpaceX shares per Tesla share) and merger-closing timing (various panelists estimated announcement possibly before year-end, with closing anywhere from mid/late 2027 to Q4 2027), tied partly to Alexandra Merz's theory linking the timing to Musk's large tax obligation in January 2028 tied to his 2018 compensation package and to a potential SpaceX S&P 500 inclusion.
2. **Shareholder vote mechanics:** Alexandra Merz explained that reaching the needed 50%+ of Tesla's outstanding shares for merger approval is difficult because non-voted shares (roughly 15%) and broker non-votes (roughly 10–11%) effectively count against approval, making retail shareholder turnout significant; the December 3rd annual shareholder vote (routine director re-election, no merger item) is being used by the panel/shareholder advocacy effort as a "stress test" of voting participation ahead of an eventual merger vote.
2. **No merger discussion on Tesla's Q3 call:** Travis Axelrod stated upfront that Tesla would not address merger questions on the earnings call, which several panelists (notably Alexandra Merz) criticized as unfair to Tesla shareholders given that Musk discussed the merger openly on SpaceX's call the prior day.
