A Sacramento auto parts shop owner spent approximately $40,000 and two years converting a 1966 Ford Mustang into a fully functional Tesla-powered vehicle — including a working installation of "Full Self-Driving" (Supervised), reportedly making it the first non-Tesla vehicle to run FSD.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **First non-Tesla vehicle running FSD:** Yaro Shcherbanyuk of Calimotive Auto Recycling in Rancho Cordova, California retrofitted Tesla's full camera array onto the Mustang, enabling Autopilot, Sentry Mode, and FSD (Supervised). The system reportedly functions despite every camera being mounted at different angles and heights than Tesla's trained configuration — a notable demonstration of the neural network's adaptability to non-standard camera placements.

2. **FSD licensing context:** The article notes that Elon Musk has sought to license FSD to other automakers for years without success. Ford CEO Jim Farley publicly rejected the idea, citing Waymo's system as superior, and Musk has acknowledged legacy automakers don't want FSD. The build is presented as evidence that Tesla's hardware/software stack is more portable than its licensing struggles imply.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Build specifications:** The conversion uses a 2024 Tesla Model 3 dual-motor drivetrain — approximately 400 hp and 471 lb-ft of torque, 0–60 mph in ~3.5 seconds. Three sections of the Model 3's floor and battery case were grafted into the Mustang's body, with the battery case shortened to fit without altering external dimensions. Interior includes the Model 3's 15-inch touchscreen (with OTA updates), Cybertruck yoke steering wheel, and Tesla-sourced heated/cooled seats. The charging port replaces the original gas cap location.

2. **Efficiency and range:** Shcherbanyuk reported 258 Wh/mi efficiency — matching or beating a standard Model 3 — despite the Mustang's less aerodynamic body. A Business Insider test drive showed approximately 194 miles of remaining range at ~80% battery.

3. **Conversion market context:** The article notes Arc Motor Company offers Tesla-battery-based classic car conversions starting at $75,000, making this $40,000 DIY build comparatively affordable. The global vehicle conversion market was valued at $5.9 billion in 2024 and is projected to grow at 9% annually through 2034.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert calls it "one of the coolest EV conversion projects we've seen" and argues the FSD functionality despite non-standard camera geometry is "genuinely impressive," adding it has meaningful implications for Tesla's potential to eventually license FSD to automakers with different vehicle geometries.
