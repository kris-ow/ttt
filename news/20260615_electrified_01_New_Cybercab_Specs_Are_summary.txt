This video from Dillon Loomis of the Electrified channel covers several Tesla developments including the Cybercab receiving its EPA certificate of conformity, new Cybercab specs from federal filings, ongoing issues with FSD version 14.3, the European controversy over Tesla's FSD approval, and a brief update on Tesla Semi charging infrastructure. The video's core thesis is that the Cybercab is progressing well toward commercialization on the federal level, while external forces — particularly incumbent automaker-aligned advocacy groups — are working to slow FSD adoption in Europe.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab EPA Certificate of Conformity:** Tesla announced that the Cybercab has received the EPA certificate of conformity, meaning the vehicle now meets all federal Clean Air Act emission standards and can legally be sold or introduced into US commerce. Dillon Loomis notes this clears every federal emissions hurdle, though state-level regulatory requirements for fully autonomous vehicles may still apply.

2. **Cybercab EPA Filing Specs:** Federal filings revealed several notable Cybercab specifications. The charge-depleting range was listed at over 418 miles, with highway miles for charge depletion just over 375 miles. The EPA-listed range in the documents is 280 miles. The battery pack size is listed at around 50 kWh, with a recharge event energy of 53 kWh, yielding over 7.8 miles per kWh in lab conditions — described as "unheard of." The vehicle's expected useful life is listed at 150,000 miles (Loomis believes this will be on the low side), and weight is listed at 3,113 lbs, roughly 1,200 lbs lighter than some Model Y variants. The filing listed the vehicle as front-wheel drive, though some entries noted an all-wheel drive test mode; regenerative braking was confirmed as front wheels only, flagged as a discrepancy to watch.

3. **Cybercab Production and Testing Activity:** Videos from Spencer in Dallas show approximately 50 Cybercabs sitting in a lot, though all reportedly still have steering wheels. A flyover shared by Airwave of the parking lot at Giga Texas showed over 80 Cybercabs. Per Joe Tegtmeyer, there may be over 1,000 Cybercabs now out testing in the wild. Loomis concludes that the presence of steering wheels and pedals suggests the testing phase will continue for at least another few weeks.

4. **Robotaxi Tracker Update — Texas DMV Data:** Loomis updated his robotaxi tracker to pull unsupervised-only vehicle counts daily from the Texas DMV website, removing Bay Area figures to enable an apples-to-apples comparison with Waymo. Per the Texas DMV, Tesla currently has 69 vehicles registered for the robotaxi service in Austin. The tracker also covers incident data: Tesla reported one new incident for May, bringing the total to 18 since service launch, with 8 incidents where the Tesla vehicle was stopped. The May incident involved a Tesla stopped in a left-turn-only lane at a red light, which was rear-ended by an approaching pickup truck; a safety monitor was present with no passengers.

5. **Elon Musk on Unsupervised FSD for Customer Vehicles:** Elon Musk was quoted saying FSD unsupervised will likely reach customer cars in the US in Q4 of this year (2026), emphasizing a gradual geographic rollout as each area is confirmed safe — raising the question of whether robotaxi-level testing must precede customer deployment in each geography.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Leaked Tesla FSD Video in China:** A video apparently leaked showing Tesla's FSD operating in China, reportedly intended for Tesla's official Chinese page. Loomis speculates Tesla is waiting for broader regulatory FSD approval in China before posting it officially, which the Tesla team has indicated could happen as soon as Q3 2026.

2. **Reuters Article on FSD Safety Data in Europe:** A Reuters article alleged that Tesla presented misleading FSD safety data to European regulators. The claims in question — that FSD could have saved 32,000 lives and prevented 1.9 million injuries — were criticized by researchers as based on unrealistic assumptions, including that every US vehicle (including freight trucks and motorcycles) would be replaced by an FSD-enabled Tesla and that every such Tesla would be at least seven times safer than what it replaced. Loomis acknowledges Tesla cherry-picks favorable data but characterizes the broader attack as a defensive move by incumbents.

3. **ETSC Opposition to FSD Approval Across EU:** The European Transport Safety Council (ETSC) sent letters to transport ministers of nearly every EU member state that had not yet approved FSD, urging them not to recognize Tesla's FSD until key safety questions are publicly answered by a party other than Tesla, and until there is real-world safety evidence from Dutch roads. Loomis counters that the RDW (Dutch vehicle authority) already conducted over 1.5 years of independent testing on public roads and test tracks and concluded that FSD makes a positive contribution to road safety. He notes the ETSC's corporate partners include Toyota Motor Europe and the German Road Safety Council, the latter closely tied to Mercedes, BMW, and VW — all of which have competing autonomous driving programs — and argues these relationships reveal a conflict of interest.

4. **FSD Version 14.3 Complaints and 14.3.4 Rollout:** Loomis shares several clips illustrating problems he experienced with FSD 14.3, including: hard braking for a school bus merely signaling a left turn; driving onto a golf course in a parking lot; stopping mid-intersection at a four-way stop with no obstacle present; and brake-stabbing from 44 mph down to under 30 mph due to a bird. The most frustrating issue was navigation in parking garages — the system circled an entire garage multiple times before finding an exit. He notes that FSD 14.3.4 is now rolling out with early positive reviews, but suspects some users are still experiencing regressions in highway navigation. He also states Elon Musk called version 14.3 "a banger" but that his personal experience did not match that characterization.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi Megacharger Prefab Infrastructure:** Tesla filed details for a pre-assembled Megacharger unit for Tesla Semi charging. Similar to its approach for regular Superchargers, Tesla will complete most of the assembly — including high-voltage bus bars and concrete work — at the factory. On site, the unit is dropped into a slot, connected to the grid, and turned on. Loomis notes this approach reduces time and cost, which is critical for broader Tesla Semi adoption.

## Market & Competition

1. **Incumbent Automaker Influence on FSD Regulation:** Loomis argues that the ETSC's campaign against FSD approval across the EU is primarily motivated by competitive pressure from legacy automakers — specifically Toyota Motor Europe, and German OEMs (Mercedes, BMW, VW) represented through the German Road Safety Council — whose autonomous driving programs cannot match Tesla's FSD capability. He contends that if Tesla's vision-only FSD receives broad EU approval, those manufacturers will face difficult questions about their sensor-redundancy-heavy approaches.
