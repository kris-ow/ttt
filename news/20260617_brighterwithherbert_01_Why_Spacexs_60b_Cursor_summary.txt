This video discusses SpaceX's $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor, the AI coding tool company, framing it not as a simple software purchase but as the completion of a full-stack AI empire. Host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi argue that Cursor provides SpaceX/XAI with the critical missing piece — developer distribution and enterprise access — to compete directly with Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in the enterprise AI market. While both are long-term bulls on SpaceX, Bhakdi warns that the current stock price is mechanically inflated by IPO lock-ups and forced index buying, and will likely correct significantly before genuine price discovery occurs.

## Market & Competition

1. **The "Holy Trinity" of AI — Harness, LLM, and Compute:** Jo Bhakdi frames the Cursor acquisition through what he calls the "holy trinity" of AI: the harness (the interface that translates user intent into LLM-readable instructions), the LLM itself, and compute. He argues Cursor is the best harness in the world — with a larger install base than Anthropic — while Grok provides the LLM and Colossus provides compute. Before the deal, SpaceX/XAI had world-class compute but lacked a strong harness and enterprise distribution. Now, Bhakdi argues, SpaceX holds all three layers simultaneously, something no other company — not Microsoft, not Google, not Anthropic — currently does. Anthropic, by comparison, has only two of the three (harness and LLM) but no proprietary compute, yet reached a trillion-dollar valuation on that basis alone.

2. **Cursor's Enterprise Penetration as the Core Value Driver:** The video emphasizes that Cursor is used by 64% of Fortune 500 companies and 70% of Fortune 1000 companies, with approximately 50,000 enterprise customers including Nvidia, Samsung, and Salesforce. Salesforce alone has 20,000 engineers using Cursor with over 90% daily adoption. Bhakdi stresses that this pre-existing enterprise footprint — which took Cursor roughly 18 months to build from launch to $1 billion in revenue (compared to Slack's seven years) — is the real strategic asset, giving SpaceX a fast-track distribution channel into the white-collar workforce that is the core of the 22.7 trillion enterprise AI TAM outlined in the SpaceX IPO deck.

3. **Cursor's Revenue Trajectory and the Deal's Valuation Context:** Cursor's ARR reportedly grew from $500 million to $3 billion in approximately 11 months, with projections of $6 billion by year-end 2026. The video notes that SpaceX paid roughly 10 times forward revenue for Cursor, contextualizing this against Microsoft paying 13 times forward revenue for its OpenAI stake. Bhakdi notes that despite these growth metrics, the $60 billion price tag represents a very high multiple even by the standards of comparably frothy companies like Palantir and Anthropic, and he argues that if SpaceX were valued on Tesla's revenue multiple it would be at $35 per share — a fraction of its current price.

4. **Colossus + Cursor Data: A Potential Leap to Frontier AI:** The video plays a clip from investor Gavin Baker, who explains that Cursor and Anthropic hold more tokens of proprietary coding training data than any other lab — more than exists on the public internet. Baker describes how Cursor's internal coding model (Composer 2) reached near-frontier performance with minimal training, and after just three weeks on the Colossus 2 cluster it became benchmark-dominant on Cursor's internal coding benchmark (CursorBench). Baker frames this as a signal that once XAI/SpaceX trains Cursor's data chinchilla-optimally with reinforcement learning, Grok has a genuine shot at becoming a leading frontier model in coding.

5. **SpaceX IPO Mechanics and Near-Term Stock Risk:** Bhakdi argues the post-IPO stock surge is largely mechanical rather than organic. He explains that most pre-IPO shareholders face lock-up periods ranging from 15–30 days (retail, broker-side) up to 180 days and 366 days for large institutional holders, meaning effectively no one is allowed to sell. Simultaneously, three indices are scheduled to include SpaceX at 5, 10, and 15 days post-IPO, creating forced buying pressure. Options trading also began at the time of the video. Bhakdi's view is that once these forced-buying mechanics fade and lock-ups expire, the stock will drop sharply into genuine price discovery — potentially to around $80 per share. He states he is waiting to buy at a lower price and is not currently purchasing.

6. **SpaceX Revenue Trajectory and Valuation Concerns:** The video states SpaceX's 2025 annual revenue was approximately $18.7 billion. Following compute-leasing deals with Anthropic, Google, and now the Cursor acquisition, its estimated annual revenue run rate has risen to approximately $55 billion. The video suggests SpaceX could exit 2026 with a run rate exceeding $60 billion. Bhakdi is more conservative, estimating SpaceX will end 2026 with $30 billion in actual (not run-rate) revenue, and could reach $120–$150 billion — possibly $200 billion — in actual revenue in 2027. However, he argues that even at $200 billion in revenue, at a Tesla-equivalent valuation multiple the stock would be worth roughly $2 trillion by end of 2027, which at current prices (~$2.8–3 trillion market cap) still implies significant downside. SpaceX raised $85.7 billion in its IPO (initially targeting $75 billion).

7. **Potential Tesla–SpaceX Merger Hinted:** Bhakdi and Herbert briefly touch on the idea that SpaceX may eventually acquire Tesla, referencing it as something Elon Musk needs SpaceX stock to be "strong enough" to execute. Bhakdi suggests the stock ratio (one-to-one, one-to-two, etc.) matters less to Elon than getting the deal done, and predicts there will be community drama around it — referencing a future debate between "Rebellionire versus Alexandra" — but that the merger will ultimately happen.
