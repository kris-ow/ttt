## Robotaxi
- **Record Cybercab count at Giga Texas:** Sawyer Merritt spotted 245 Cybercabs in the outbound lot, shared by Tesla's Robotaxi account with the label "gold rush"; vehicles lacked Cybercab decals and some appeared to have steering wheels, which one commentator (Jeff, on brighterwithherbert) attributed to concurrent test-vehicle builds during a ramping production line.
- **Steering-wheel-less Cybercabs arrive in California:** Cybercabs with Cybercab decals and no steering wheels were photographed near San Bernardino — a state where Tesla doesn't yet run unsupervised robotaxis — which a commentator read as consistent with a broad geographic rollout rather than Waymo's city-by-city approach.
- **Phoenix hiring across all shifts:** Tesla is hiring robotaxi fleet support specialists in Phoenix for morning, afternoon, and night shifts covering prep, inspections, and lot management; a commentator called all-shift hiring a signal leadership sees hardware/software as close to ready.
- **New Orleans testing begins:** Tesla's robotaxi service has started testing in New Orleans, per futureaza, expanding beyond existing markets.
- **Cybercab accounting debate:** Commentators discussed whether Tesla will book Cybercabs as fixed assets (enabling first-year tax deductions) versus inventory buildup; one estimated Tesla could carry ~10,000 Cybercabs for roughly $200 million in capex, though most agreed large inventory holds aren't typical of Tesla's approach.
- **Bull case reaffirmed despite market skepticism:** One commentator (jobhakdi) maintained a projection of roughly 15,000 unsupervised robotaxis this year, versus Morgan Stanley's estimate of 30,000 by 2030, while noting TSLA was trading near $370 ahead of earnings.

## Autonomous Driving
- **Houston remote-operator crash disclosed:** Electrek reports Tesla filed an NHTSA crash report where a remote operator, while extracting a stuck Model Y from a Houston dead-end road, struck a hidden tree stump at 2 mph — the first time Tesla has formally coded a crash as caused by a remote (commercial/test) operator. It's the third disclosed remote-operator crash following July 2025 and January 2026 Austin incidents; Electrek notes every disclosed remote intervention so far has ended in a collision, contrasting with Waymo's stated policy of not using human-performed remote driving.
- **Additional NHTSA crash reports filed:** Tesla also reported crashes in Austin (SUV reversed into a stopped Tesla) and Dallas (first Dallas crash: hit a metal chain at 5 mph blocking a lot entrance, echoing a similar September 2025 Austin incident); two of the four new reports were attributed to the other party.
- **Florida AG seeks to overturn $243M Autopilot verdict:** Florida AG James Uthmeier, joined by Alabama and Georgia, filed a brief asking the 11th Circuit to reverse or reduce the verdict (33% liability, $43M compensatory, $200M punitive) from the 2019 Key Largo fatal crash; Electrek's Fred Lambert flags donation-timing questions involving a lobbying firm tied to Tesla, though calls it "suggestive and nothing more."
- **Speed-offset removal criticized:** Electrek's Fred Lambert reports getting a speeding ticket (78 km/h in a 50 km/h zone) while demoing FSD Standard mode, and argues Tesla's replacement of the old custom speed-offset control with fixed presets (Sloth/Chill/Standard/Hurry/Mad Max) removed driver judgment needed for local conditions.
- **Chinese influencers say FSD leads China's systems:** Testers of FSD 14 reportedly concluded Chinese systems (Huawei ADS, Xpeng, Li Auto) still trail Tesla, per a clip discussed on brighterwithherbert; a commentator argued Tesla's lead may be widening rather than narrowing, with Xpeng, Huawei and Nvidia's autonomy efforts "keeping up" but a couple years behind.
- **Germany slow-walking FSD approval:** Germany's motor transport authority is reviewing Dutch-forwarded safety documentation and receiving a dedicated Tesla test vehicle in July 2026; an EU-wide technical committee vote is expected around October 2026, with disputes over details like a speed-offset feature risking slippage into late 2026 or 2027.
- **FSD memory feature coming:** Elon Musk said upcoming releases will let FSD remember individual driver preferences and specific past interventions (e.g., parking spots, lane preferences), which a commentator called a potential major reduction in convenience-related interventions.
- **Trial lawyers lobbying against AV legislation:** The American Association for Justice has opposed AV legislation for nearly a decade; Musk reacted "terrible" to a Paul Graham comment suggesting trial lawyers oppose self-driving cars to preserve lawsuit volume.
