## Robotaxi
- **Record Cybercab build at Giga Texas:** Sawyer Merritt spotted a record 245 Cybercabs (no decals, some with steering wheels) in the outbound lot, labeled "gold rush" by Tesla's Robotaxi account; commentators frame it as evidence of a rapidly ramping production line rather than a deployment signal.
- **Steering-wheel-less Cybercabs staged in California:** Decaled, wheel-less Cybercabs were photographed near San Bernardino — a state without active unsupervised robotaxi operations — read by one commentator as consistent with a broad geographic rollout rather than Waymo's city-by-city model.
- **Phoenix hiring across all shifts:** Tesla is hiring robotaxi fleet-support specialists in Phoenix for morning, afternoon, and night shifts, seen by one commentator as a sign leadership expects hardware/software readiness soon.
- **New Orleans testing begins:** Tesla's robotaxi service has started testing in New Orleans, per one source.
- **Cybercab inventory accounting debate:** Commentators disagree on treatment — one argues Tesla could hold ~10,000 Cybercabs in inventory for ~$200M capex; another says Cybercabs will likely book as fixed assets, generating first-year tax deductions.
- One channel reaffirmed a bullish (non-consensus) projection of ~15,000 unsupervised robotaxis this year, versus Morgan Stanley's 30,000-by-2030 estimate; framed explicitly as the host's own model, not verified data.

## Autonomous Driving
- **NHTSA crash reports:** Tesla filed four new Robotaxi crash reports, including a first-of-its-kind Houston incident where a remote operator, freeing a stuck Model Y, hit a hidden tree stump at 2 mph (no injuries). Per Electrek, all three disclosed Tesla remote-operator interventions to date have ended in collisions, contrasting with Waymo's stated policy of not using human-performed remote driving.
- **Florida AG intervenes in Autopilot verdict:** Florida's AG, joined by Alabama and Georgia, filed a brief asking the 11th Circuit to vacate or reduce Tesla's $243M verdict in the fatal 2019 Key Largo Autopilot crash, citing Florida's 3:1 punitive-damages cap; Electrek notes donation-timing questions around the AG tied to a Tesla-linked lobbying firm, which the outlet calls suggestive but not conclusive.
- **Speed-offset removal draws criticism:** Electrek's Fred Lambert received a speeding ticket (78 km/h in a 50 km/h zone) during an FSD Standard-mode demo, and argues Tesla's shift from a custom speed-offset control to five fixed profiles (Sloth/Chill/Standard/Hurry/Mad Max) removed driver control despite drivers remaining liable.
- **Chinese influencers say FSD leads China's systems:** Two Chinese auto influencers testing FSD 14 in LA reportedly concluded Huawei ADS, Xiaopeng, and Li Auto still trail Tesla, urging domestic catch-up; one commentator argued Tesla's lead may be widening rather than narrowing.
- **Germany still reviewing FSD approval:** Germany's transport authority is evaluating Dutch-forwarded safety data and receiving a dedicated Tesla test vehicle in July 2026; an EU-wide technical vote is expected around an October 2026 meeting, with risk of slipping to late 2026 or 2027, per one source.
- **FSD memory feature coming:** Elon Musk said upcoming releases will let FSD remember individual driver preferences and past interventions (e.g., parking spots, lane choices).
- **Trial lawyer lobbying against AVs:** A lobby representing trial lawyers has opposed AV legislation for years; Musk reacted to posts on the topic, calling one "terrible."

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus Gen 3 ramp questioned by shareholders:** Retail investors submitted top-voted questions on Gen 3 production ramp and 2027 sales volume; one channel's hosts said the relevant factory floor remains empty/in prep, with current units still hand-built rather than in production ramp.

## Financials
- **ASML confirms Terafab in 2027 plans:** ASML CFO Roger Dassen said Terafab is part of customer building-plan discussions; ASML is raising EUV lithography output from ~40–60 to ~80 machines/year, partly to meet demand from customers including Terafab.
- **Retail shareholder questions signal anxiety:** Ahead of an upcoming shareholder meeting, top-voted questions (only ~1M votes cast vs. ~1B retail shares) pressed management on missed Robotaxi guidance (three straight quarters), Hardware 3's FSD ceiling, and Optimus timelines — described by one channel's hosts as confrontational and unlikely to draw substantive answers.
- **Micron cited as valuation cautionary tale:** One channel argues an "inverse bubble" could suppress TSLA even after strong robotaxi results, citing Micron trading near 6x earnings despite 100%+ growth as a comparable case; this is explicitly framed as the host's own hypothetical, not a forecast.
