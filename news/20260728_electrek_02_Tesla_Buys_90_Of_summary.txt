Tesla has signed a long-term power purchase agreement to buy 90% of the output from Project Sterling, a 509 MW solar plant paired with a 360 MW battery being built in Arizona by ContourGlobal (owned by KKR), expected online in 2028.

## Energy
1. **Project Sterling deal terms:** Tesla will buy 90% of the output of the 509 MW solar project and its 360 MW/four-hour (~1,440 MWh) battery storage system; ContourGlobal keeps and trades the remaining 10%. Financial terms were not disclosed. The plant connects to the Western Area Power Administration grid, giving access to California markets. ContourGlobal calls it the largest renewable asset in its portfolio; it acquired the project in late 2024. Deals of this type typically run 10-15 years.

2. **Second solar deal same day:** Earlier the same day, Tesla and Spanish developer Zelestra confirmed a deal for the entire output of a 140 MW solar farm in Texas, meaning Tesla contracted over 640 MW of third-party solar plus 360 MW of storage in one day. The article attributes this to rising electricity demand from AI data centers tightening US power markets and Tesla's own growing load, including Gigafactory Texas and expanding AI compute.

3. **Buying rather than building:** Despite manufacturing the Megapack (described as the best-selling grid battery) and operating one of Arizona's largest Megapack installations, plus building a 100-GW solar panel factory in Texas, Tesla is buying both solar and storage from a third-party developer for Project Sterling rather than building it itself. Neither company disclosed whose batteries will be used in the plant.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the two same-day PPAs a smart move to secure power for Tesla's growing electricity needs, but views it as ironic that Tesla — a battery and solar panel manufacturer — is buying storage capacity from a third party instead of building it with its own products; he speculates (without evidence) that the project could end up using Megapacks, which would mean Tesla buying back capacity it manufactured. He attributes the reliance on PPAs to Tesla having gutted its solar business years ago and only now rebuilding it.
