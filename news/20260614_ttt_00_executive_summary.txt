## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.3.4 released June 13, 2026**, receiving standout community reviews. A prominent tester drove 250 miles and described it as "by far the best overall FSD build by a wide margin" — more refined than 14.3.3, with natural animal-avoidance behavior replacing hard braking, improved parking destination logic, and a reviewer characterization of "one step closer to robotaxi."
- **Robotaxi-oriented UI strings** appeared in 14.3.4 per Sawyer Merritt: "Vehicle will pull over near destination," "Approaching destination. Will pull over near destination," and "Driving to Market Basket" — messaging designed for a passenger-facing, driverless experience rather than a monitored driver.
- **The 14.3.x series represents a fundamental architectural rebuild**, stripping legacy components and enabling faster iteration. Bhakdi notes major upgrades are now arriving roughly every two to three weeks, and the pace is reportedly accelerating.
- **Elon Musk stated on the most recent earnings call** that robotaxi rollout does not require waiting for FSD v15 — deployment will occur "at huge scale" on v14, with v15 targeted for approximately end of 2026.
- **Cortex 2 at Giga Texas removes the training bottleneck** underpinning FSD improvement. The first 250 MW phase came online April 2026; full 500 MW activation is targeted for mid-2026. Combined with Cortex 1, the campus exceeds 230,000 H100-equivalent GPUs — dwarfing the largest national lab supercomputers at ~40,000 GPUs each.

## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab production has crossed 1,000 units** per Bhakdi's conservative estimates from drone footage: ~10/day in April (~220 units), ~20/day in May (~440 units), ~30/day in June to date (~360 units). He projects roughly 2,000 units by the first week of July and 3,000–4,000 by August.
- **Cybercabs spotted in Houston and Las Vegas on June 13, 2026**, both batches fitted with decorative wheel caps absent during earlier testing phases — which Bhakdi interprets as a cosmetic preparation signal indicating public deployment is near. Sightings also reported in Palo Alto, Austin, Chicago, Boston, and Florida.
- **Bhakdi's near-term deployment call**: he remains convinced "multiple dozens" of unsupervised Cybercabs will deploy in June 2026. He estimates 40–50 unsupervised units would be a 10–20% short-term stock catalyst, pushing Tesla toward its prior all-time high of ~$490. At 1,800 unsupervised robotaxis with accelerating growth, he sees a fundamental repricing toward $600–$800+.
- **A viewer noted Tesla indicated four to five new robotaxi cities** going online in the next two weeks; Bhakdi acknowledged the comment but flagged uncertainty about whether Tesla's stated timelines can be relied upon.
- **The Brighterwithherbert analysis concedes** that robotaxi scale remains "still mostly just Austin and Phoenix" as of mid-2026 — a legitimate criticism alongside timeline slippage across Tesla's broader industrial buildout.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus factory at Giga Texas North Campus broke ground in earnest**: first steel went up May 27, 2026, across a 5.2 million square foot footprint per permit filings — nearly the full length of the main Giga Texas building. Low-volume production could begin as early as summer 2026, with the primary ramp running through 2027.
- **Long-term output target is 10 million Optimus units per year** from the North Campus site alone — a figure the source explicitly labels a target, not existing capacity. For scale: Hyundai's Asan complex produces ~1.5 million vehicles/year; Apple's iPhone output runs ~230 million/year across multiple Foxconn campuses.
- **Cortex 2's scale directly enables Optimus neural net training** alongside FSD. The cluster runs 130,000+ H100-equivalent GPUs, with the campus total past 230,000 combined with Cortex 1.

## Energy
- **Houston Megapack factory in Brookshire, Texas**: $200 million initial investment, 50 GWh/year target, production targeted for late 2026 (flagged as likely to slip). The flagship product, "Mega Block," packs 20 MWh AC capacity per unit with Tesla claiming 23% faster installation and up to 40% lower construction costs versus traditional builds.
- **Combined Tesla grid storage manufacturing capacity exceeds 130 GWh/year** across Lathrop, Giga Shanghai, and Houston once the latter is online — roughly two-thirds of the ~200 GWh of global battery storage deployed in all of 2024.
- **A $1.4 million Tesla Solar Tile Roof** — claimed to be the largest residential installation in the U.S. — was disclosed on a $26.5 million Maui home listing, per Sawyer Merritt. No kilowatt capacity was specified.

## Market & Competition
- **SpaceX President Gwyn Shotwell declined to deny a Tesla-SpaceX merger** in a CNBC interview, saying it "might make Elon's life a little easier," acknowledged "there's no question that there are synergies," and cited "definitely a convergence of what we're all trying to accomplish." Wedbush's Dan Ives puts merger probability above 80%, targeting 2027. Commentator Larry Goldberg, who published a merger white paper in February 2026, argues an offer won't materialize until early 2027, citing Tesla's need for robotaxi clarity, SpaceX shareholder lockups, and near-term operational load.
- **SpaceX's S-1 amendment** references language indicating the company may be increasing available shares to conduct large acquisitions — cited as a structural signal supporting the merger thesis.
- **After SpaceX absorbed xAI in February 2026**, Tesla's $2 billion xAI investment converted into a small SpaceX stake. Tesla holds no direct xAI equity, though FSD and the in-vehicle Grok assistant run on Colossus infrastructure.
- **SpaceX IPO context**: stock rose ~20% in its first week to ~$165 vs. a ~$135 IPO price. Index inclusion catalysts: Russell US fast-entry on June 26, NASDAQ fast-entry rebalance ~July 6, Russell semiannual reconstitution December 2026. S&P 500 inclusion is at least one year away after a rules change was rejected. Bhakdi is not buying at current prices, predicting a classic IPO "valley of tears" lasting one to two years before re-acceleration.
- **Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe stated** the company will launch "full supervised point-to-point" autonomy (explicitly compared to Tesla FSD) across Gen 2 vehicles and R2 later in 2026, with unsupervised capability targeted for 2027 — notable as a competitive benchmark, though Rivian remains far behind Tesla in training data and compute scale.
- **Rivian R2 faces compounding headwinds**: launch edition priced at ~$58,000–$60,000+ vs. the originally announced $45,000 ($44,900 target now pushed to spring 2027 at earliest); Consumer Reports placed Rivian last in reliability across all surveyed brands; cumulative cash burn across pure-play EVs tracked by analyst AJ reached $24.4 billion as of February 2026; ZEV credits (~$590M for Rivian), the $7,500 federal EV tax credit, and charging infrastructure grants have all been eliminated. Volkswagen's new software development agreement with Xpeng also casts doubt on the durability of the Rivian-VW software partnership.
