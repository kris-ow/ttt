On Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call, Elon Musk addressed a question about Tesla-SpaceX synergies by describing "more and more overlap" between the companies, but said combining companies "can't" be discussed on the call and would require "the appropriate process," deferring to Tesla general counsel Brandon Ehrhart.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Musk avoids confirming or denying a merger:** Responding to Wells Fargo analyst Colin Rusch's question about synergies from combining Tesla and SpaceX, Musk cited growing overlap between the companies, especially around the Terafab project, but said the topic requires a formal process rather than discussion on an earnings call.
2. **Framework agreement disclosed by Tesla's general counsel:** Ehrhart said Tesla "deepened" its SpaceX relationship earlier in the year "through an investment and a framework agreement," which per SpaceX's S-1 filing covers only a general framework for future Terafab development, with specific projects subject to separate negotiations. Ehrhart said this will let the companies continue working on Terafab and Digital Optimus.
3. **Tesla's xAI investment converted to SpaceX shares:** SpaceX's S-1 filing disclosed that Tesla's $2 billion investment in xAI was converted into 18,990,195 shares of SpaceX Class A common stock, representing under 1% ownership; the filing also showed SpaceX and xAI entities purchased roughly $650 million in goods and services from Tesla in 2025.
4. **Musk catalogs cross-company overlaps:** Musk listed Grok (xAI's model) being integrated into Tesla vehicles and helping "drive digital Optimus," Starlink connectivity being added to the Cybercab and planned for all Tesla vehicles in markets where Starlink operates (to avoid robotaxi connectivity dead zones), and Terafab as a joint SpaceX/Tesla semiconductor project he said Tesla needs to avoid being "constrained" in scaling Optimus production.
5. **CFO ties connectivity to robotaxi operations:** CFO Vaibhav Taneja noted Tesla has begun offering Cybercab rides at its Austin factory, saying this illustrates why Starlink connectivity "becomes so important."

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the described overlaps (SpaceX equity stake, framework agreement, Grok, Starlink, Terafab) constitute the "connective tissue" typically built before a merger, and suggests Musk is dangling a large-scale combination partly to distract from weak underlying results. He notes that of Tesla's $1.1 billion GAAP net income this quarter, roughly $750 million was a mark-to-market gain on Tesla's SpaceX stake (with actual operating income just $398 million, a 1.4% operating margin), and that this gain is likely to reverse next quarter given SpaceX stock has fallen roughly 30% since the prior valuation mark. He frames a potential merger as a fourth Musk "self-deal" (after SolarCity, Twitter/X, and the xAI arrangement), noting Musk holds roughly 20% of Tesla but 85% of SpaceX's voting power, and that a merger near SpaceX's reported $1.75 trillion valuation versus Tesla's ~$1.6 trillion market cap would disadvantage Tesla shareholders. He cites unnamed Wall Street analysts putting merger odds at 80-90% by early 2027, and speculates a merger could help trigger Musk's compensation plan giving him control of the combined company.
