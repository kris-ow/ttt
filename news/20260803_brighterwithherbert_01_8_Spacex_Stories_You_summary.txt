This video (a discussion between the host and guest Larry Goldberg) covers eight SpaceX news items ahead of SpaceX's earnings, centered on a report projecting SpaceX's AI compute business could rival Tesla's revenue, a newly revealed data center building called "Mini Hard," Starship's ocean landing and heat-shield debate, early Starlink V3 test satellites, a large Space Force launch contract, and a new Starlink hire the host speculates points toward device partnerships.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Report projects SpaceX AI compute revenue near Tesla's scale:** A report (from an outlet the host calls Funda AI) reportedly projects SpaceX building 4 gigawatts of compute capacity this year and another 4 gigawatts next year using Nvidia Rubin racks; VC Gavin Baker is cited noting this reading of the report. The host cites unnamed estimates that if SpaceX leased out all this capacity (across its Colossus/"macro hard," "macro harder," and new "mini hard" buildings) it could generate roughly $70–100+ billion in annual revenue — comparable to Tesla's current annual revenue. Guest Larry Goldberg says he believes "a market" of this thesis is accurate but thinks the timeline is more likely to play out over the next two-plus years rather than immediately, and separately states his own view that SpaceX will exceed $100 billion in revenue from this business regardless of full lease-out.
2. **New "Mini Hard" data center building revealed by Elon Musk:** Responding to a photo of the Colossus/"macro hard" and "macro harder" site (which the poster noted includes six blocks of Megapacks, 720 units on site), Elon Musk revealed a previously unannounced building called "Mini Hard," said to house the same 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips with upgraded network interface cards as "macro harder" but packed into roughly a quarter of the space. Goldberg calls this a genuine engineering breakthrough that will lower implementation cost and speed, reinforcing SpaceX's position as the low-cost compute producer; a third-party estimate cited in the video pegs the chip cost alone for 220,000 GB300s at about $12 billion, requiring roughly 0.4 gigawatts of power. Separately, analyst Brian Wang is cited noting SpaceX's combined data center buildings hold roughly 990,000 mostly B300-class chips.
3. **SpaceX–Tesla valuation gap narrowing:** Goldberg argues that because Starlink and the AI compute business run on much higher margins ("electrons" vs. Tesla's "molecules"), and because SpaceX's share price has recently fallen more than Tesla's, the two companies are now roughly comparable in capital value — a dynamic he says would make a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger easier, though he declines to elaborate further.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **$1.6 billion Space Force contract:** SpaceX won a competitively bid U.S. Space Force contract worth $1.6 billion covering 18 Falcon 9 launches of national security satellites — roughly $89 million per launch. Goldberg notes SpaceX won on lowest cost and best technical solution, and calls it highly profitable; note that his stated per-launch economics ("that's almost a billion dollars a launch", repeated) are inconsistent with the $1.6 billion total for 18 launches cited in the same segment.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Successful ocean splashdown; tower catch attempt planned next flight:** Starship's most recent flight ended in a soft ocean landing, and Elon Musk said that barring issues found in mission data review, SpaceX will attempt to catch the ship with the launch tower on the next flight. Goldberg calls this "the most amazing news" and says the heat shield's post-landing condition, despite some visible surface wear, exceeded expectations for only about the fifth or sixth ship recovery attempt.
2. **Heat shield bear case rebutted:** Bloomberg published a piece featuring former NASA-affiliated sources arguing Starship's heat shield could be a major obstacle to full reusability. ARK Invest's Brent Winton countered that heat shield tiles are not a dead end, since SpaceX can trade off refurbishment cost against landing performance and reduce launch costs over time; Elon Musk stated that cost to orbit could drop well below $100/kg with full, rapid reusability and local production of methane/oxygen propellant. Goldberg dismisses the Bloomberg experts' credibility, drawing a parallel to a past ULA executive's skepticism about Falcon 9 reusability that proved wrong, and says SpaceX could become significantly profitable and launch orbital data centers within two to three years even before achieving very rapid reusability.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **First Starlink V3 satellites tested in orbit:** The first Starlink V3 satellites were launched and deployed solar arrays, laser inter-satellite links, and communications systems successfully during a roughly 20-minute suborbital test before all 20 units re-entered and burned up as planned (unlike Starship, which does not burn up on return). Elon Musk called V3 "a game changer"; the host notes V3 satellites are described as roughly 10 times more powerful than V2. The next flight is expected to carry at least 20 V3 satellites into actual orbit.
2. **Direct-to-cell expands from text to apps in Australia:** Telstra customers in remote parts of Australia can now use apps (chat, navigation, trail directions, weather, offline maps) over Starlink direct-to-cell connectivity, an upgrade from text-only service — described as a first for Australia. The host expects further improvement in this capability as Starlink V3 satellites come online.
3. **New Starlink hire fuels device-partnership speculation:** SpaceX hired Cole Young, previously Microsoft's director of gaming business development for Xbox (6 years), as director of business development for Starlink product growth. The host speculates (his own theory, unconfirmed) this signals SpaceX may pursue Starlink integrations with gaming/device platforms similar to Xbox's past app and hardware partnerships. Goldberg pushes back, suggesting SpaceX doesn't need to build devices itself but stands to profit by providing the underlying connectivity that such devices pay for.
