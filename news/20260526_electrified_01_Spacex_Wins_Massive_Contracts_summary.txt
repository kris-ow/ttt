This episode of "electrified," hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers a wide range of SpaceX and Tesla news, framed around the growing public conversation about a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger, major new SpaceX/Starlink contracts, and several Tesla product and fleet updates. The video's core thesis is that SpaceX's expanding role in military and commercial infrastructure has direct long-term implications for Tesla, regardless of whether a formal merger occurs.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla-SpaceX Merger Speculation Reaches Prediction Markets:** CNBC reported that Elon Musk has engaged in "thought experiments" around folding Tesla into SpaceX's ecosystem, citing unnamed sources. Dillon Loomis dismissed the reporting quality but noted the signal: these discussions are now entering public discourse, even if anonymously. On the prediction market Kalshi, the odds of a Tesla-SpaceX merger within the next year were cited as high as 70% in recent days, though Loomis clarified this was a peer-to-peer market driven by trader activity, not an institutional odds-setter. As of recording, the contract for a merger before May 1st sat at 44%.

2. **Starlink's American Airlines Deal and Airline Expansion:** Starlink announced a deal with American Airlines to install terminals on more than 500 narrowbody aircraft, including the A321 XLR and A321 Neo deliveries, starting Q1 2027. Starlink is now approaching 40 airline customers and is already deployed on roughly 1,000 aircraft. Delta remains the notable holdout, having chosen Amazon's Project Kuiper instead, with installation not set to begin until 2028.

3. **Starlink Speed Improvements in Europe:** New industry research showed Starlink's median download speed across all 27 EU countries rose from 114 Mbps in Q1 2025 to nearly 166 Mbps in Q1 2026 — a 45% increase — with improvement in 26 of the 27 markets.

4. **Starlink Military Use Controversy Clarified:** Elon Musk disputed Reuters reporting that the Pentagon used Starlink's civilian system for military operations in violation of its terms of service. Musk stated the responsible party was Spectre Works, a US-based Arizona defense company that manufactures kamikaze drones, which incorrectly used the civilian Starlink system instead of Star Shield — SpaceX's separate, encrypted, government-grade satellite network. Musk stated this was a breach of contract by that company, not authorized Pentagon use.

5. **Space Force Awards SpaceX ~$2.3 Billion SDN Contract:** The US Space Force awarded SpaceX a just-under-$2.3 billion firm fixed-price agreement to build and operate the Space Data Network (SDN) backbone — a core communications layer for US Space Force warfighting systems connecting sensors and shooters globally and securely. SpaceX is the sole provider using Star Shield technology with inter-satellite lasers. A fully operational prototype capability is required by end of 2027. Loomis characterized this as cementing SpaceX as central to US defense infrastructure for years to come.

6. **StarCloud Signs Starlink Laser Terminal Integration Contract:** StarCloud, founded in 2024 and valued above $1 billion, signed a contract with SpaceX to integrate Starlink mini laser terminals onto its satellites for up to 25 Gbps of continuous inter-satellite connectivity at distances up to 4,000 km. The first hardware is expected on orbit within one year. StarCloud 2 will feature a GPU cluster, persistent storage, 24/7 access, and proprietary thermal/power systems, fully operational in sun-synchronous orbit by 2027.

7. **European Mobile Satellite Spectrum Opening:** Viasat and EchoStar hold EU satellite spectrum licenses expiring in May 2027 covering direct-to-cell capability. Reports indicate Starlink and Amazon's Project Kuiper may be able to acquire some of this spectrum next year, though two-thirds would be reserved for European companies. This would give Starlink a realistic path to direct-to-mobile connectivity for texting, calls, and data in remote areas.

8. **Elon Comments on SpaceX AI Ambitions:** Responding to a post arguing Elon doesn't need the best AI model but rather the best hardware (citing Nvidia's dominance), Elon agreed the hyperscaler play works but stated SpaceX AI is only 3 years old — half the age of Anthropic and a quarter the age of OpenAI — and vowed never to give up. He noted SpaceX itself was written off as dead after six years with three consecutive launch failures.

9. **Ferrari EV Reveal Criticized:** The former longtime chairman of Ferrari publicly criticized the company's new EV, calling the design "hideous" and warning it risked "the destruction of a myth." Loomis added that at approximately $640,000, the vehicle represents what he called 2012-era technology at an embarrassing price point, predicting it will be discontinued by 2028 with Ferrari blaming lack of customer demand for EVs rather than the product itself.

10. **Voltera and Revel Merge to Build AV-Focused Charging Infrastructure:** Voltera and Revel announced a definitive agreement to combine businesses, creating a charging infrastructure platform purpose-built for autonomous vehicles, electric fleets, and ride-hail deployment in dense urban markets. The combined company will operate more than 1,000 charging stalls across 11 major US metro markets and will not be open to public charging. Loomis noted this would primarily serve non-Tesla AV companies, as Tesla robotaxis would likely use Superchargers.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Granted Patent for Autonomous Camera Lens Cleaning System:** Tesla received a new patent for a camera lens cleaning system that uses a controller to detect debris via image quality degradation, then automatically triggers a fluid dispenser and wiper to clean the lens — without requiring human intervention. The camera lens is spherical, requiring a contoured wiper blade. Loomis noted this is critical for unsupervised autonomy in snowy and dirty climates and that Optimus is not named in the patent but could also benefit from the technology. He speculated this could potentially be tied to a future FSD version Elon mentioned as possibly coming next year.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Sightings in Houston with Starlink Equipment:** The Cybercab Spotter account reported multiple tandem Cybercab sightings, all appearing to be equipped with Starlink — confirming this is not a one-off. SC Robinson shared new footage from Houston showing 14 Cybercabs on site.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Figure Announces Commercial Deployment with Catalyst Brands:** Figure signed a commercial agreement with Catalyst Brands — which operates JC Penney, Aeropostale, and Brooks Brothers — to deploy humanoid robots at scale, starting in Reno, Nevada, within Catalyst's distribution and logistics network. The initial focus will be on the facility's Joey Pouch sorting system. No pricing, timeline specifics, or deployment scale details were disclosed.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybertruck Dual Motor AWD VINs Now Being Assigned:** Sawyer confirmed Tesla has officially started assigning VINs to customers who ordered the $59,000 Cybertruck dual motor all-wheel drive, with first deliveries expected in the coming weeks.

2. **Tesla Roadster to Be Built at Giga Texas:** Lars confirmed on the Ride the Lightning podcast that the Roadster will be built at Giga Texas, with "a lot of things unfolding in the next months." The unveil event will not take place in May 2026.

3. **Volvo Expands Tesla Supercharger Access in Europe:** Starting Q4 2026, Volvo owners will be able to use the Volvo app to charge at more than 20,000 Tesla Supercharger stations across 29 European countries. Volvo is also planning to transition selected models to NACS in Japan and South Korea by 2029.

4. **Tesla Opens Supercharger Prepaid Passes to Non-Tesla EVs in China via WeChat:** Tesla expanded Supercharger network access in China to non-Tesla EV owners through prepaid charging passes purchasable via WeChat, usable at V3 and V4 Supercharger stations. The trial pass is approximately $12 for 50 kWh (~$0.23/kWh) and the enjoy pass is $88 for 400 kWh (~$0.22/kWh). Loomis noted this improves asset utilization during off-peak periods and generates upfront cash flow.

5. **First Tesla Supercharger Goes Live in Colombia:** Tesla Charging announced the first Supercharger is now live in Bogotá, Colombia.

6. **Elon Corrects Overstated Battery Patent Claim:** Elon Musk corrected a viral post claiming Tesla's new patent would cut battery production costs by nearly half, clarifying it will "significantly reduce the cost of battery cathode production" — not halve total battery costs. The patent relates to Tesla's full dry battery electrode (DBE) process for the cathode, which also accelerates 4680 cell cathode production.
