## Brief

- Tesla sues Cybertruck tooling supplier, production at risk

- Tesla buys 90% of 509MW Arizona solar+storage project

- Starship Flight 14 may attempt first tower catch

## Robotaxi
- Driverless Cybercab sightings around Giga Texas rose roughly 2.5x from early to late July, per Joe Tegtmeyer footage cited by Bhakdi, read as evidence of a shift from supervised to driverless public-road testing.
- Bhakdi pushes his ~15,000-Cybercab year-end estimate back about one quarter, toward Q1, citing slower-than-hoped commentary on Tesla's earnings call, though he says his 6-12 month bullish thesis is unchanged.
- Tesla has disabled the audible turn-signal sound inside unsupervised-mode Model Y Robotaxis, a minor UX refinement to the driverless ride experience, per Sawyer Merritt.
- A back-of-envelope estimate (jobhakdi) pegs robotaxi margin at $20,000–$50,000/vehicle/year versus roughly $6,000 profit per Model Y, implying each robotaxi equals about seven Model Y sales in annual profit; the same analysis frames timing of true unsupervised FSD, not unit economics, as the key open question for the stock.
- Waymo has been fined roughly $9,325 in Austin parking tickets since 2024, cited on Herbert's show as an early friction point as fleets scale.
- Uber lobbyists reportedly pushed New Jersey legislation requiring human drivers for 85% of driverless rides over three years; the provision didn't survive into the final bill. Uber's CFO said the company is investing more than anyone in AV progress and was responding to a bill that would have banned both Tesla and Zoox in NJ.
- Waymo is reportedly exploring launching independently in Austin and Atlanta once its Uber contract expires (cited variously as January 2028 or "early 2018" in one garbled account), amid a soured Waymo-Uber relationship over cleanliness, routing, and economics disputes.

## Autonomous Driving
- Wrongful-termination suit from ex-Houston FSD test-fleet manager Javier Medrano alleges Tesla pushed his operator ratio to 38:1 against an internal 15:1 safety baseline, with test drivers working 60–80 hour weeks. He alleges a March 30, 2025 crash involving a member of the public was mishandled because he was asleep when the emergency call came in; he was fired May 1, 2025 and is seeking reinstatement and damages. Tesla has not responded to the claims, which Electrek notes are one-sided and unproven.
- A post cited by Sawyer Merritt claims 20,000 miles driven on FSD without needing to touch the wheel — an anecdotal, unverified data point.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla sued Cybertruck tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive on July 23 in Texas federal court, seeking emergency access to a closed Troy, Texas plant to retrieve specialized die-cast tooling. Angstrom allegedly demanded $250,000/week to keep the plant running; Tesla says its parts supply will be exhausted within days and rebuilding tooling would take five to six months, putting thousands of committed Cybertruck builds at risk. Angstrom has not commented.
- Tesla won a UK court bid to revive a London patent lawsuit against InterDigital and Avanci ahead of its planned 5G vehicle launch in Britain, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Energy
- Tesla signed a PPA to buy 90% of output from Project Sterling, a 509 MW Arizona solar plant with 360 MW/four-hour (~1,440 MWh) battery storage built by ContourGlobal (KKR-owned), online in 2028; terms undisclosed.
- Same day, Tesla confirmed a deal for the full output of a 140 MW Texas solar farm with developer Zelestra — over 640 MW of solar and 360 MW of storage contracted in one day, which Electrek ties to rising AI-data-center power demand and Tesla's own growing load (Gigafactory Texas, AI compute). Electrek notes the irony that Tesla, a battery/solar manufacturer, is buying capacity from third parties rather than building it.

## Financials
- TSLA fell roughly 17% over five days into and after earnings; Bhakdi estimates only 2-3% of that was earnings-specific, with the rest driven by a broader AI-stock selloff (Google, Micron, Nvidia) tied to China lithography news and a South Korea market plunge.
- Retail stock inflows are at a five-year low, below pandemic-panic levels, per Bhakdi's research — framed as market-wide nervousness rather than a Tesla-specific signal.
- Tesla closed down only slightly amid the broader tech crash; SpaceX private shares rose about 2.56% on the day after initial pressure, though still down roughly 50% from peak since IPO, per jobhakdi.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Bhakdi says SpaceX's lower valuation plus upcoming share unlocks complicates near-term Tesla-SpaceX merger timing; he floats a hypothetical high per-share floor Musk could set to guarantee shareholder approval. Analysts diverge: Alexandra Merz reportedly still sees a deal possible soon after Tesla's August earnings call, while Bhakdi, Larry Goldberg, and Cathie Wood see it as more likely next year or later.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas flagged the possibility SpaceX attempts its first-ever Starship tower catch on Flight 14, remarking bears "may need to consider cryogenic hibernation" if successful — bullish framing from a major sell-side analyst, per Sawyer Merritt.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- A report cited by Sawyer Merritt says Starlink is increasingly winning customers who already have terrestrial broadband, expanding its addressable market beyond rural users.
- Amazon has requested FCC approval for a 5,100-satellite direct-to-device network targeting 2028 deployment, a emerging competitive threat to Starlink's direct-to-cell business.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said of SpaceX's orbital data center ambitions, "there is no reason to believe this will not come to existence," citing Musk/SpaceX's capitalization — third-party validation cited by Sawyer Merritt.
