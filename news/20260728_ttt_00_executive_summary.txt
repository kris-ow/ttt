## Brief

- Tesla sues Cybertruck tooling supplier, seeking emergency plant access
- Tesla buys 90% of 509 MW Arizona solar-plus-storage project
- Driverless Cybercab sightings in Austin up ~2.5x in July per Bhakdi
- Ex-manager lawsuit alleges 38:1 staffing ratio, understaffed Houston FSD fleet
- TSLA fell ~17% over five days amid broad AI-stock selloff
- Starship Flight 14 may attempt first tower catch

## Robotaxi
- Driverless Cybercab sightings around Giga Texas rose roughly 2.5x from early to late July, per Joe Tegtmeyer footage cited by Bhakdi, suggesting progress toward driverless public-road operation beyond employee-only testing.
- Bhakdi pushes his ~15,000-Cybercab year-end estimate back about a quarter, toward Q1, citing slower-than-hoped rollout commentary on the earnings call, though he says his 6-12 month bullish thesis is unchanged. A separate commentator (jobhakdi) similarly flagged a slip toward early 2027 as immaterial to the long-term thesis.
- Tesla disabled the audible turn-signal sound inside Model Y Robotaxis operating in unsupervised mode, a minor UX refinement to the driverless ride experience.
- Uber reportedly lobbied for New Jersey rules requiring human drivers for 85% of driverless rides over three years; the provision didn't make the final bill. Uber's CFO says the company was actually responding to legislation that would have banned both Tesla and Zoox.
- Waymo is reportedly exploring launching independently in Austin and Atlanta once its Uber contract expires in January 2028, amid a soured Uber relationship; separately, Waymo has been fined roughly $9,325 in Austin parking tickets since 2024.
- A Tesla investor commentary (jobhakdi) argues each deployed robotaxi could generate $20,000-$50,000/year in margin at scale versus a Model Y's roughly $6,000 profit "on a lucky day" — framing the real open question as timing of true unsupervised FSD, not unit economics.

## Autonomous Driving
- Wrongful-termination lawsuit from ex-Houston FSD test-fleet manager Javier Medrano alleges Tesla pushed his operator-to-manager ratio to 38:1 versus an internal 15:1 baseline, with test drivers averaging 60-80 hour weeks. He alleges a March 30, 2025 crash involving a member of the public occurred while he was sleep-deprived, and that he was fired May 1, 2025 after raising staffing concerns; Tesla has not commented. Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the claims unproven but consistent with Tesla's known approach.
- A social post cited by Sawyer Merritt claims a driver went 20,000 miles on FSD without touching the wheel — an unverified but notable anecdotal reliability data point.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla sued Cybertruck tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group in federal court (filed July 23), seeking emergency access to a Troy, Texas plant to retrieve tooling after Angstrom closed the plant and allegedly demanded $250,000/week to keep it running. Tesla says its parts supply is "dwindling and will be exhausted in mere days" and rebuilding the tooling would take five to six months, putting several thousand committed Cybertruck builds at risk. Electrek notes only Tesla's side is public so far.

## Energy
- Tesla signed a PPA to buy 90% of output from Project Sterling, a 509 MW Arizona solar plant with 360 MW/four-hour battery storage (ContourGlobal/KKR), online in 2028; terms undisclosed. The same day, Tesla also confirmed a deal for the full output of a 140 MW Texas solar farm from Zelestra — over 640 MW of solar and 360 MW of storage contracted in one day, driven by AI data-center demand tightening power markets.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert notes the irony that Tesla is buying storage from a third party despite manufacturing Megapack, speculating (without evidence) the project could ultimately use Tesla's own batteries.

## Financials
- TSLA fell roughly 17% over five days into and after earnings, per Bhakdi, who estimates the earnings-specific drag was only 2-3%, with the rest tied to a broader AI-stock selloff (Google, Micron, Nvidia) and record-low retail stock inflows.
- Tesla and SpaceX (private shares) were relatively resilient during the broader semiconductor/momentum-stock crash, with Tesla closing down only slightly and SpaceX shares up ~2.56% on the day after earlier weakness, per jobhakdi.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Bhakdi flags the SpaceX-Tesla merger overhang as complicated by SpaceX's declining valuation and upcoming share unlocks; he floats a hypothetical high per-share floor Musk could set to guarantee shareholder approval. Analysts diverge on timing: one (Alexandra Merz) reportedly still expects a deal soon after SpaceX's August earnings, while others (Bhakdi, Larry Goldberg, Cathie Wood) see it as unlikely this year.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas flagged the possibility of SpaceX's first-ever Starship tower catch on the upcoming Flight 14, calling it a milestone bears may need to reckon with.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- A report cited by Sawyer Merritt says Starlink is increasingly winning customers who already have traditional broadband, broadening its addressable market beyond rural/underserved users.
- Amazon has requested FCC approval for a 5,100-satellite direct-to-device network targeting 2028 deployment — an emerging competitive threat to Starlink's direct-to-cell business.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said he sees "no reason to believe" SpaceX's orbital data center ambitions won't materialize, citing Musk/SpaceX's capitalization.
