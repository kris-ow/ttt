This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and venture capital investor, breaking down SpaceX's S1 IPO filing. The core thesis is that SpaceX is far more than a rocket or satellite company — it is rapidly becoming an AI compute-as-a-service business, with a self-described total addressable market of $28.5 trillion, the vast majority of which is in artificial intelligence enterprise applications. The discussion centers on the Anthropic compute deal, Starlink's growth trajectory, and the long-term vision for orbital data centers and beyond.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX's TAM Claim — $28.5 Trillion, Mostly AI:** The SpaceX S1 filing claims what it calls "the largest actionable total addressable market in human history," estimated at $28.5 trillion. Of that, $26.5 trillion is attributed to AI, broken down as $2.5 trillion in AI infrastructure, approximately $760 billion in consumer subscriptions, $600 billion in digital advertising, and $22.7 trillion in enterprise applications. Only $370 billion is attributed to space-enabled solutions, and $1.6 trillion to connectivity (including $870 billion in Starlink broadband and $7–40 billion in Starlink mobile). China and Russia are excluded from these global estimates. Larry Goldberg notes the $22.7 trillion enterprise applications figure is consistent with figures he tracked during his own career in that industry, and argues the precise number matters less than the sheer magnitude of the opportunity.

2. **AI Compute as a Service — The "Real Business":** Both host and guest argue that the most underappreciated revelation in the SpaceX filing is that Elon Musk explicitly stated SpaceX is "offering AI compute as a service at significant scale" and is "in discussions with other companies to do the same." This positions SpaceX not merely as a launch or connectivity provider but as a hyperscaler — analogous to AWS — renting GPU compute capacity to frontier AI labs. Goldberg argues this business, enabled by SpaceX's own data center infrastructure (Colossus, Macrohard, and "Macro Harder"), is the central value driver going forward, particularly as orbital data centers come online.

3. **Anthropic Deal — $1.25 Billion Per Month:** The S1 filing discloses that Anthropic has agreed to pay SpaceX $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 for access to AI compute capacity across both Colossus and Colossus 2, with capacity ramping in May and June at a reduced fee. Either party may terminate the agreement with 90 days' notice. Anthropic publicly confirmed the deal, stating it would be scaling up on GB200 capacity through June. Goldberg claims he predicted the deal would be worth approximately $15 billion annually when analysts were citing figures of $6–8 billion, reasoning that Anthropic was losing $18–20 billion per month in potential revenue by throttling customers due to compute constraints.

4. **Competitive Landscape — AI Frontier Models:** Goldberg offers his assessment of the competitive positioning of frontier AI companies. He argues Anthropic's Claude found the right strategic path by focusing on enterprise and coding (agentic AI), while Grok focused on consumer content and OpenAI on chatbots. He contends enterprise applications represent the most important near-term battleground, and that all frontier players — Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, and xAI — will find niches in what he describes as a market that has "barely scratched the surface." He warns that coding, currently the focus of competitive benchmarking, is "not even the tip of the iceberg" relative to the full AI TAM.

5. **Cursor Partnership and xAI Competitive Position:** The S1 filing includes a disclosed right for SpaceX/xAI to acquire Cursor for $60 billion. Cursor's Composer 2.5 is described as approaching the performance of Anthropic's Opus and GPT-5.5 while being significantly cheaper. The host notes Elon Musk commented that Cursor is "an important piece of the puzzle to make Grok much better" and that Anthropic "will not be destroyed" — framing Anthropic as a customer and partner, not a target for elimination. Goldberg acknowledges xAI started three to five years behind leading frontier labs and initially focused on the wrong product direction, but believes it is now "very close to the frontier."

6. **Starlink as Enabling Infrastructure:** Goldberg argues Starlink's value should not be assessed in isolation. With 9,600 satellites (75% active and maneuverable), 164 countries served, and 10.3 million subscribers, Starlink is already a substantial revenue business. More importantly, Goldberg says Starlink bandwidth is the prerequisite for orbital data centers — without a low-latency, high-bandwidth global constellation, AI inference from space-based compute is not viable. He contends that once Starship enters production, each launch could deliver roughly the equivalent bandwidth of an entire year of Falcon 9 satellite launches, causing Starlink capacity to "go vertical." He predicts competitors (citing AST SpaceMobile and Amazon) will face structurally higher launch costs, making it very difficult to compete.

7. **Terafab — The Overlooked "Other Bet":** Near the end of the conversation, Goldberg flags that Brad Gerstner's otherwise praised BG2 podcast analysis mentioned "Terafab" only as a throwaway item under "other bets." Goldberg strongly disagrees with that framing, calling Terafab "the biggest of them all" and saying he has been doing a deep dive into it with significant findings, though the discussion does not elaborate further in this episode.

8. **Predicted Next Move — Microsoft Deal:** The host predicts Microsoft will be the next company to sign an AI compute agreement with SpaceX, noting that Google is already reportedly in talks and Anthropic has already signed. No specific terms or timeline are provided for a potential Microsoft deal.

## Financials

1. **SpaceX S1 Financial Highlights:** The filing discloses the following figures: $18.7 billion in revenue, $6.8 billion in operating cash flow, and $20 billion in capex — of which $7.2 billion is attributed to Starlink and $12.7 billion to AI capex. The connectivity segment generated $3.25 billion in its first quarter with $1 billion (described also as $2 billion in one reference) in income from operations, primarily driven by Starlink, with $11.3 billion in income for operations cited at another point. Goldberg notes the company is already profitable and frames the current moment as the inflection point after 20 years of investment.

2. **Analyst Revenue Estimates and Brad Gerstner's Projection:** Brad Gerstner (BG2 podcast), quoted in the video, estimated that the Anthropic compute deal would generate an incremental $4–5 billion of revenue in the current year, on top of analyst consensus estimates in the "mid-20s" (billions). Goldberg criticizes Gerstner's estimate as too low, arguing Gerstner underestimated both the scope of the deal (not realizing Blackwell GPUs were included) and the broader opportunity by "at least an order of magnitude." Gerstner's framing — that the deal "takes pressure off xAI delivering immediate revenue" and establishes SpaceX as an immediate hyperscaler competitor — is described as directionally correct but insufficiently ambitious.
