This video from the "cernbasher" channel features a discussion between two hosts analyzing key statements from a Tesla earnings/shareholder call regarding FSD software versioning, the robotaxi expansion roadmap, and the Hardware 3 upgrade situation. The core thesis is that FSD 14.3 is already sufficient for near-term unsupervised robotaxi expansion across multiple U.S. cities and states, and that Version 15 — while a major upgrade — is not a prerequisite for meaningful scale. The hosts also explore Tesla's strategy for Hardware 3 vehicles, speculating on how upgrade incentives could funnel those cars into the robotaxi network.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 as the "last piece of the puzzle":** Elon Musk stated on the call, "I think version 14.3 is the last piece of the puzzle for unsupervised FSD," though he qualified it with "I think" rather than a definitive assertion. He clarified that known major architectural improvements exist that could significantly improve safety, and Tesla intends to finish writing, validating, and releasing those improvements before going large-scale with unsupervised FSD. The hosts note this creates ambiguity but does not mean robotaxi expansion is blocked pending Version 15.

2. **Version 15 as a major but not mandatory upgrade:** A Tesla executive (referred to as "A Shock" in the transcript) noted that the robotaxi deployments currently running in Austin, Dallas, and Houston are essentially Version 14.3 variants and are "obviously safe." He indicated Tesla will continue expanding on the 14.3 base for a while until Version 15 lands, describing Version 15 as "a major upgrade." The hosts interpret this to mean gradual geographic expansion can and will continue under the 14.x series without waiting for Version 15.

3. **Unsupervised FSD rollout to customer vehicles — geography-gated approach:** Elon indicated he expects unsupervised FSD to reach customer cars probably in Q4 2026, but walked it back to a geography-by-geography approach. His exact framing was: "I think we would release unsupervised gradually to the customer fleet as we feel like a particular geography is confirmed to be safe." The hosts interpret this to mean that customers who live within an already-approved robotaxi geography could be enabled for unsupervised FSD without requiring a broader Version 15 rollout.

4. **Hardware 3 cannot achieve unsupervised FSD:** Tesla confirmed on the call that Hardware 3 "simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD." This reversed earlier hints of possibility from one or two prior quarterly calls. Tesla is offering Hardware 3 owners a discounted trade-in toward a car with Hardware 4, or the ability to upgrade their existing vehicle by replacing both the computer and cameras to Hardware 4 spec. In the interim, Tesla will release a "distilled" version of the Version 14 software for Hardware 3, described as having all the features of Version 14 for Hardware 4 except unsupervised capability. Hardware 3 upgrade timing was referenced as "this summer" by Tesla, though one host pushed back and said "next year."

## Robotaxi

1. **12 states does not mean 12 cities — hosts project 15–20+ cities:** Tesla indicated robotaxi expansion to 12 states. The hosts argued this translates to significantly more than 12 cities, noting Texas already has three cities and Florida would likely have at least three major metro areas. One host estimated a minimum of 15 cities, more likely 20. At roughly 300–500+ vehicles per city, the hosts projected a total of approximately 10,000 to 15,000 unsupervised robotaxi vehicles by end of 2026, which one host noted was consistent with his pre-call estimate.

2. **Scale definition remains frustratingly vague:** Both hosts expressed frustration that Elon did not quantify what "large-scale" deployment means — whether it is 1,000, 10,000, 100,000, or millions of cars. They noted Elon likely avoids specific numbers due to past backlash from missed projections, but wished he had at least given an order-of-magnitude signal.

3. **Hardware 3 upgrade as a robotaxi fleet recruitment strategy:** The hosts speculated that Tesla's true motivation for offering Hardware 3 upgrades is to accelerate robotaxi fleet growth. They discussed potential incentive structures: Tesla could cover the upgrade cost in exchange for a minimum number of robotaxi network hours per month, or reduce Tesla's revenue take rate (cited as currently 35%) down to around 20% for owners who enroll their upgraded vehicles. The hosts characterized this as a net money-maker for Tesla rather than a pure liability, since upgraded vehicles generating robotaxi revenue would allow Tesla to recoup the upgrade cost and more.

4. **Estimated Hardware 3 FSD liability:** The hosts discussed estimates of how many Hardware 3 vehicles have purchased FSD that has not been transferred. One host suggested the number might be around half a million, while another expressed skepticism it was that high. At an estimated $5,000–$7,000 per vehicle retrofit cost, the potential liability is significant, but the hosts argued Tesla views this as an investment in fleet expansion rather than a sunk cost.
