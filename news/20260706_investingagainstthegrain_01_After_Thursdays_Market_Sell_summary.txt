The host, from the channel investingagainstthegrain, argues that Thursday's market sell-off — including Tesla's drop despite record deliveries — was driven by spillover fear from a South Korean market crash and overblown "AI bubble" narratives, not fundamentals, and that both the broader market and Tesla remain mispriced to the downside. He frames this as a buying opportunity for Tesla ahead of an anticipated announcement at the Giga Texas/Austin campus.

## Market & Competition
1. **South Korean market crash seen as sell-off trigger:** The host attributes Thursday's US sell-off largely to a crash in South Korea's Kospi index (down 8%, triggering trading halts) tied to concentration in memory/chip names like Samsung and SK Hynix, which he says has recently tended to spill over into US tech/AI-infrastructure stocks (Micron, SanDisk, Nvidia, Marvell, Bloom Energy).
2. **Meta's cloud-rental plan reframed as bullish, not bearish:** Reports that Meta will lease out excess data center capacity spooked markets over fears AI demand is overstated, but the host argues Meta (like SpaceX and Tesla) is monetizing excess compute while awaiting newer Nvidia chips — evidence of supply constraints (citing Anthropic and Google renting compute from Elon Musk/SpaceX), not weak demand.
3. **Dot-com bubble comparison rejected:** The host contrasts today's market — low P/E multiples, strong earnings, GPUs running at 70-90% utilization, no rush of unprofitable IPOs — with the 1999-2000 dot-com bubble's PE expansion without revenue and "dark fiber" overbuild, concluding current AI infrastructure spending is fundamentally different and not a bubble.
4. **Broadening market as bullish signal:** He notes the Magnificent 7 are down for the year while the S&P 500 overall and its equal-weight version are up and outperforming the cap-weighted index — a sign of market breadth that he says typically precedes continued bull markets rather than a top.

## Electric Vehicles

The host argues the more important story than headline deliveries will be how many buyers signed up for FSD.
2. **Model Y L launched in the US:** Tesla launched the Model Y L variant domestically, a version the host says has already been highly successful in China and Europe; he considers this a significant product event for the US market given consumer preference for larger vehicles.
