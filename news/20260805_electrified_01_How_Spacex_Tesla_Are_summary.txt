## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Host now open to a Tesla–SpaceX merger:** The host states he is now supportive of a SpaceX acquisition of or merger with Tesla, conditional on a "reasonable offer," citing reassurance from the SpaceX earnings call that capex is behaving more like cost of goods sold with roughly one-year payback periods on cloud service agreements (CSAs). He flags that Elon and SpaceX executives may have incentive to talk up the company ahead of an insider share-lockup expiration and a possible Tesla acquisition offer, since a higher SpaceX share price would reduce dilution for SpaceX in an all-stock deal.
2. **SPCX share move tied to lockup expiration:** SpaceX shares closed down 13.6% at $108.27, which the host attributes primarily to market uncertainty ahead of an unlock of over 900 million shares becoming sellable, rather than to earnings-call substance (which he characterizes as strong), though he notes some Wall Street concern about capex spend.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Path to $1 trillion revenue assessed as improbable but not impossible:** SpaceX did about $19 billion in total 2025 revenue (space $4B, connectivity/Starlink $11B, AI $3B) and roughly $12.5 billion in the first half of 2026, with the host projecting an exit-2026 run rate near $38 billion. To reach $1 trillion by 2030, he models AI/compute revenue at roughly $820 billion, connectivity around $158 billion, and space around $38 billion — implying 5-year CAGRs of about 180% for AI, 69% for Starlink/Enterprise, and 65% for the launch business. He stresses no company has ever grown at 180% sustained from this base, and none has ever hit $1 trillion in annual revenue (Amazon is closest at just over $700 billion), calling the AI growth requirement "unprecedented."
2. **Compute capacity build-out modeled to ~26 GW by 2030:** Using a $40/watt monetization assumption (Musk's stated midpoint for the Vera Rubin platform), 85% utilization, and Musk's disclosed plan that about 90% of SpaceX compute will be rented via CSAs (only ~10% used internally for Grok training/inference), the host calculates SpaceX would need about 26 GW of operational compute by 2030 to hit ~$800 billion in AI revenue. Stated compute targets: exit-2026 at 2 GW, Musk's guidance of 5–10 GW by end of 2027 (host uses 7 GW, ~250% growth), 12 GW by end of 2028, 18 GW by end of 2029, and ~26 GW by 2030 (growth rates of 71%, 50%, and 45% in those later years).
3. **Orbital compute buildout still small relative to total need:** Musk has targeted roughly 1 GW per year run rate for orbital compute by late 2027. Based on prior host estimates, reaching 1 GW in orbit would require about 6,000 AI1 satellites, roughly 40 per Starship launch, or about 150 launches — which the host thinks is most plausible by 2028 given Musk's call comment that Starship could reach roughly daily launch cadence within about a year (a claim the host treats with skepticism, though more credible than Musk's early robotaxi timeline predictions). His best-case estimate is 1–4 GW of orbital compute by 2030, meaning the bulk of the projected 26 GW would need to come from terrestrial data centers — which he argues will be the primary driver of SpaceX's near-term valuation and revenue, more than Starship or rockets themselves.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink Mobile detailed as a hybrid satellite/terrestrial service:** SpaceX plans a hybrid direct-to-cell system combining next-generation "V2" satellites (expected up next year) for outdoor coverage with new SpaceX-built femtocell devices for improved indoor/dense-urban capacity, using acquired EchoStar spectrum paired with existing Starlink KU/KA-band terminals for backhaul. The host personally speculates SpaceX may not launch its own independent mobile plan until around early 2028, and raises an open question of whether existing Starlink customers would get a retrofit/add-on device rather than needing an entirely new terminal. He also speculates that a future combined Starlink device could eventually be integrated into Tesla vehicles alongside the Cybercab's existing native Starlink support.
2. **Explicit confirmation SpaceX is entering the mobile carrier market:** The host notes that prior reporting suggested SpaceX would not offer its own competing mobile service, but Gwynne Shotwell's public comments targeting AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile signal explicit intent to compete directly, which he frames as another industry Musk enterprises are set to disrupt — though not expected to be a primary revenue driver for SpaceX in the near term.

## Robotaxi
1. **Samsung and LG secure Cybercab camera supply contracts:** Samsung has reportedly secured a contract to supply cameras for Tesla's Cybercab and began mass production of camera modules last month; LG will join the supply chain with production starting later this year. The combined initial order from both suppliers is enough cameras to cover between 20,000 and 30,000 vehicles, which the host takes as confirmation of camera supply for up to 30,000 Cybercabs, without confirming a production timeline.

## Autonomous Driving

The host stresses this is early speculation with no official data on causation, and flags a third party's recommendation against installing FSD v14 Light on Hardware 3 vehicles.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EVgo adding V4 Superchargers to its network:** EVgo announced it will add V4 Superchargers to its Metropolitan network, appearing in Tesla in-car navigation with automatic routing; deployment starts this fall with first sites expected operational in the second half of this year. The chargers will include Tesla's Magic Dock for CCS/NACS charging, with installations described only as "hundreds" of chargers at roughly 20 per site; EVgo said consumer demand on its network has grown over 700% over the past three years.
