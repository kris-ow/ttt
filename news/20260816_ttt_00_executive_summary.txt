## Robotaxi
- Cybercab distribution is ramping fast but customer rides aren't confirmed live yet; futureaza puts perhaps 1,500 units sent out awaiting activation with VINs spotted as high as ~2,300.
- Leaked footage revealed Cybercab's removable steering wheel/pedal setup for Tesla's "Project Rodeo" test-driver program, plus an operator interface with "short oops," "long oops," "win," and voice-note controls for logging interventions.
- Jobhakdi estimates Cybercab production nearing 2,000/month by September, citing sightings in Chicago and Dallas, and puts 90%+ odds on 10,000 Cybercabs existing by year-end but only 40–50% odds that many are commercially deployed by then.
- Las Vegas granted Tesla an interim operating order as part of robotaxi permitting, ahead of a full autonomous-network application before the Nevada Transportation Authority — an incremental step, not final approval.
- New Jersey lawmakers are advancing a bill that would require driverless commercial vehicles to carry sensors beyond cameras, effectively banning camera-only robotaxis; one host called it "ridiculous" but uncertain to pass.
- Adam Jonas reportedly values Tesla at roughly 34% core auto/energy, 41% robotaxi hype, 25% Optimus — a split cited to explain why robotaxi delays hit the stock disproportionately.

## Autonomous Driving
- Regulatory and data bottlenecks remain unclear for Cybercab's driverless rollout; Ashok Elluswamy reportedly said on X that Cybercab's different form factor from Model 3/Y requires additional data collection to fine-tune FSD, though engineers reportedly expect a smaller lift than Cybertruck's.
- FSD v15 and the AI5 chip (targeting 2027 production) were flagged by one host as possibly more consequential to robotaxi scaling than near-term deployment numbers, though claims about v15 already running in the commercial fleet remain unverified.

## Humanoid Bots
- A large-scale Optimus manufacturing factory is under construction, expected complete by end of next year, cited as a signal Tesla intends mass production rather than a side project; no formal public "reveal" is expected, with a commercial launch possibly around December 2027.

## Financials
- Tesla's free cash flow reportedly turned negative for the first time as capex outpaces cash generation, with cash cited around $42 billion and shrinking slightly; hosts frame this as an intentional reinvestment choice, not distress.
- Margin softening is attributed to loss of ZEV/CAFE regulatory credits rather than core business erosion, per one analysis citing strong Q2 revenue.
- Waymo has logged over 200 million autonomous miles but is estimated (unconfirmed) to lose $2–5 billion/year while pricing above human rideshare — cited to question robotaxi-capex-justifies-valuation arguments broadly.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla posted its best-ever July in South Korea: 33% of imported car sales were Teslas, Model Y was top imported model for a sixth straight month, and YTD deliveries are up 150% year-over-year.
- Tesla sales in Japan reported strong and growing, with new sales/service centers opening despite domestic-brand dominance.

## Market & Competition

- One host argues Tesla is now more attractive than SpaceX near-term, citing rising institutional ownership (up ~20–25%, per a chart from Alexandra Merz) even as TSLA sits well below its ~$490 prior high.
- Rivian's claim to match Tesla FSD capability next year was dismissed by one host as unrealistic given Rivian lacks comparable AI training infrastructure.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Grok 4.6 launched, positioned by Musk as top-tier and highest "intelligence per dollar"; Grok 4.7 is teased for three-to-four weeks out, targeting engineering-task dominance.
- Grokbot, a consumer AI agent product, launched in beta gated behind Super Grok Heavy ($300/month) or select Cursor tiers; Musk distinguished it from the separate, higher-capability "Digital Optimus" project.
- A new SpaceX/"Macrohard"-linked data center was revealed containing a reported 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips.
- Musk said in a companywide presentation that AI revenue could exceed all other SpaceX revenue as soon as September.
- Musk reportedly said large-scale AI compute will move to space within 2–3 years, framed around terrestrial power/cooling constraints; one commentator linked this to broader SpaceX-Tesla merger speculation.
- The Tesla/SpaceX/xAI "Terafab" chip project, targeting a terawatt of annual chip output, was cited alongside speculation (unconfirmed) about Nvidia's ~$500 billion financing commitment partly flowing to SpaceX.

## SpaceX — Starlink
