## Key Posts / Themes

1. **FSD Homologation Progress in Belgium — Regulatory Milestone Reached:** Tesla has completed the 5,000 kilometers (3,107 miles) of FSD testing required on Belgian public roads, as confirmed by Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder. The test file has now moved to De Ridder's administration for formal homologation advice. This is a procedural step forward for FSD approval in Belgium and, by extension, potentially broader European regulatory momentum — though the post notes the process is not yet complete and formal approval has not been granted.

2. **Model Y U.S. Spec Upgrades — Standard Features Expanded:** Tesla has upgraded the base $39,990 Model Y RWD and the $41,990 Model Y AWD in the U.S. to now include the all-black headliner and the larger 16" center screen as standard equipment. This is a meaningful value-add for buyers at those price points without a price increase, potentially supporting demand.

3. **Model Y Europe — New Coastal Blue Color Option:** Tesla introduced an all-new "Coastal Blue" exterior color exclusively for the base Model Y in Europe, priced at €1,300 (~$1,500 USD). This brings the total number of blue Model Y color variants to four: Coastal Blue (new), Marine Blue, Frost Blue Metallic, and Glacier Blue — the latter three introduced in 2025. The Europe-exclusive positioning suggests a targeted demand stimulus in a key market.

4. **U.S. Pricing & Incentive Adjustments for Model 3:** Tesla made several pricing tweaks for U.S. Model 3 customers: the 1-year free Supercharging promotion for Model 3 Premium orders ends June 15, 2026; the $36,990 Model 3 RWD lease price increased from $299/month to $329/month; and the $41,990 Model Y AWD financing rate rose from 0% to 0.99% APR. These adjustments suggest Tesla is modestly tightening incentives, which could reflect improved demand or margin management heading into Q2 2026.

5. **Ferrari CEO Rules Out Full Autonomy — Competitive Context:** Ferrari's CEO stated emphatically in a new interview that Ferrari will not make fully autonomous cars, emphasizing driver engagement as core to the brand. While not a direct Tesla datapoint, this reinforces the competitive divergence in strategy: legacy luxury brands are explicitly ceding the autonomy space, which structurally benefits Tesla's long-term autonomous positioning.

6. **SpaceX Compute Deals & IPO — Indirect Tesla Investor Context:** SpaceX announced a $920 million/month Cloud Service Agreement with Google for compute capacity. Combined with a prior Anthropic deal, SpaceX is now generating a $2.17 billion/month ($26 billion annualized) revenue run rate from compute alone. SpaceX is also upsizing its Japan IPO by 25% to $2.5 billion (14.8–18.5 million shares at $135 each) due to strong retail demand. Morgan Stanley projects SpaceX revenue could reach $3.4 trillion by 2040 with adjusted EBITA topping $2.7 trillion; both Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs project ~$160 billion in SpaceX revenue by 2028. These items are not directly Tesla-relevant but matter to Tesla investors who hold SpaceX exposure or track Elon Musk's capital allocation attention.
