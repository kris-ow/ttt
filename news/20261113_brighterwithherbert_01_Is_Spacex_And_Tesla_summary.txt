This panel discussion (hosted by Herbert with guests Jeff Lutz, Alexandra Merz, and Cern Basher) covers Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's abrupt departure (replaced on an interim basis by CFO Colette Kress) and debates whether this threatens SpaceX and Tesla's chip supply, including speculation Elon Musk could recruit Huang for Tesla's in-house chip plant, TerraFab. The second half covers Tesla Robotaxi's rapid 24/7 multi-city expansion and fresh reporting on Tesla-SpaceX merger timing.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia CEO departure sparks governance and geopolitical debate:** Jensen Huang stepped down/was removed as Nvidia CEO following allegations involving personal relationships (one panelist characterized as "possibly a Chinese spy," speculative); CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO. Elon Musk publicly said he'd be "honored" to hire Huang in response to a Zero Hedge post. Panelists (Jeff, Alexandra, Cern) broadly argued Tesla and SpaceX are insulated short-term because they have existing multi-year chip allocations with Nvidia and don't rely on the vendor-financing structures (GPU-for-future-payment deals) that make neo-cloud and LLM providers more exposed; Oracle was named as a company whose financial stability could be more exposed to this fallout. Cern Basher speculated (explicitly framed as speculation) that China may have had a hand in destabilizing Nvidia as a form of economic warfare, though he acknowledged this is unproven.
2. **Alexandra Merz on Delaware litigation risk:** Alexandra Merz argued Nvidia's Delaware incorporation and Huang's low voting-share ownership (~3.5%, single share class) leave the company exposed to shareholder derivative lawsuits questioning the board's and Huang's conduct — contrasting this with Tesla's shareholder-friendly structure after its 2018/Texas reincorporation. She cited Palantir's Class F share structure (created at its 2003 Delaware incorporation) as a device giving founders ~49.99% voting control, noting new Delaware law lowers that threshold to 33%, and urged shareholders of Delaware-incorporated companies to file reincorporation proposals before year-end shareholder-meeting deadlines.

## Financials
1. **Bloomberg report on Tesla-SpaceX merger timeline:** Per an Bloomberg article (by journalists named Ryan Gold, Siddhartha Philip, and Dinesh Nair), merger negotiations are reportedly progressing with an announcement possibly coming in December, potentially slipping to early January 2027; terms are reportedly still being finalized. Alexandra Merz theorized the announcement could land near Tesla's December 3 annual shareholder meeting or, more likely in her view, shortly after the SpaceX early-investor lock-up expiration (which she believes falls around December 8–12, unconfirmed), to maximize free float before any public rollout.
2. **Google board member's SpaceX board departure flagged as possible conflict-of-interest signal:** Alexandra Merz noted Don Harrison (a longtime SpaceX board member representing Google, which she says owns roughly 4% of SpaceX and invested about $900 million) recently left SpaceX's board. She attributed this to growing conflicts of interest as SpaceX, Tesla, and Google/Waymo increasingly compete (robotaxis, AI data centers, space communications), speculating this is tied to merger negotiations, though she characterized this reasoning as her own inference.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla Robotaxi expands to 24/7 operation and new cities:** Tesla announced 24/7 Robotaxi operation across all its service cities (Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) this week, alongside a large new San Antonio geofence and expanded Fort Worth, Houston, and Austin zones. Panelists linked this to progress on FSD v15 components previously flagged by Ashok Elluswamy. Panelists noted (citing a tracker, unspecified) that Tesla's Texas footprint may have temporarily pulled ahead of or fallen slightly behind Waymo depending on the day, with figures changing rapidly and acknowledged as possibly already stale.
2. **Florida geofence expansion lagging, cause unclear:** Panelists flagged that Tesla's Florida robotaxi geofences (Tampa, Orlando) remain comparatively small despite Florida's flat, uniform road geography making expansion technically easier; they attributed this to likely "operational readiness" (staffing, local validation work) rather than software limitations, per Ashok Elluswamy's prior comment on an earnings call that city launch times are trending toward zero as Tesla moves toward state-wide rather than city-by-city management.
3. **Cybercab fleet additions in Texas reportedly paused since October 28:** A panelist noted Tesla had not added Cybercabs to Texas registrations since around October 27–28, speculating this could relate to NHTSA requiring Tesla to respond to self-certification questions (Tesla submitted answers around October 20) rather than a regulatory block; Jeff Lutz said he doubted NHTSA had actually restricted Cybercab additions and suggested new-city launches using Model Y vehicles was a more likely explanation.
4. **Robotaxi-related hiring spans 42 U.S. cities:** Citing a tracker of Tesla job postings (attributed to "Tessie Yoda"), the panel noted Tesla is hiring for robotaxi-related roles across 42 U.S. cities, with Texas leading at 14 cities; California and Southwest postings were flagged as less likely to translate into near-term launches, with Elon Musk having said California robotaxi service is unlikely before roughly mid-2027.
