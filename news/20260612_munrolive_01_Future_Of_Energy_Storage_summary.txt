This video is an interview conducted by Jordan Roach of Munro Live with Cal Lton, Chief Commercial Officer at Redwood Materials, recorded at a GM Energy event in San Francisco. The conversation covers Redwood Materials' expanding partnership with GM, focusing on second-life battery repurposing for grid-scale energy storage, battery recycling, and how the circular battery economy can ultimately reduce costs for EV consumers.

## Energy

1. **Redwood Materials' circular battery economy model:** Redwood Materials positions itself as the infrastructure layer beneath battery cell manufacturing and vehicle production — handling battery reuse/repurposing, recycling and refining critical minerals, and remanufacturing those minerals back into battery-grade materials for reintroduction into the supply chain. The company does not make cells or cars, but covers everything below that level in the value chain.

2. **GM partnership scope and today's announcement:** GM represents Redwood's broadest automotive partnership. Redwood has been GM's exclusive recycler for production scrap from GM's LG joint venture (Altium Cells) for many years, and has also handled recycling for GM facilities with material return into GM's value chain. The new announcement at the event focused on the upstream repurposing of EV battery packs for stationary energy storage — an extension of the already-established recycling relationship.

3. **Second-life battery deployment plans:** GM Energy announced that its bidirectional chargers are now ready to support vehicle-to-grid (V2G) applications. Starting next year (2027), GM plans to deploy second-life battery packs at its own operating plants, beginning at a site in Michigan. Redwood stated it has already repurposed "gigawatt hours" of storage in partnership with GM and others, meeting significant near-term demand.

4. **The "Pack Manager" hardware solution:** Redwood has developed a proprietary piece of hardware called the "Pack Manager," designed to integrate a heterogeneous mix of repurposed EV packs — varying in form factor, chemistry, age, and communication protocols — into a single consistent DC output, functionally comparable to a brand-new containerized LFP battery solution. The Pack Manager monitors down to the individual cell level for voltage, amperage, and temperature, and handles power conversion onto a common DC bus. Manufacturing of the custom power electronics for the Pack Manager is now underway at Redwood's factory in Sparks, Nevada (outside Reno), and the product has gone through UL certification.

5. **Thermal management and safety through duty-cycle reduction:** A key technical insight shared is that repurposed EV packs, originally designed for high-C-rate (high discharge) applications, are now being used in grid-scale storage at much lower C rates and longer durations. Cal Lton used the analogy of a sprinter accustomed to 100-meter dashes now running a 5K. This dramatically reduced duty cycle, combined with the Pack Manager's cell-level monitoring, creates very wide safety margins from thermal limits, enabling higher-density arrays.

6. **Design philosophy — adaptability over prescription:** Rather than requiring OEMs to design battery packs to a specific "recyclability" standard, Redwood's philosophy is to let OEMs optimize their pack designs for cost reduction, manufacturability, and energy density — then engineer Redwood's own disassembly and integration processes to handle whatever form factors result. Cal Lton noted that a cell-to-pack architecture reducing vehicle cost and increasing EV adoption is preferable to a less optimal pack that happens to be easier to recycle.

7. **Recycled materials already cheaper than newly mined materials domestically:** Redwood stated that recycled critical minerals, refined to battery-grade quality, can already be produced domestically at a cost lower than newly mined and imported equivalents. These materials are being passed back to OEMs, reducing cell costs, which in turn lowers EV prices for consumers given how large a proportion of EV cost is attributable to the cell.

8. **Higher battery residual values reducing consumer lease costs:** Redwood is working with the captive financing arms of OEMs to incorporate higher battery residual values — backed by Redwood's repurposing and recycling commitments — into vehicle financing models. This allows OEMs to offer lower net lease payments to consumers, and is described as already happening today, both for fleet customers and individual consumers.

9. **NASA selects Redwood's battery technology for lunar rover:** At the same GM Energy event, it was announced that NASA has selected Redwood's battery technology to power the lunar outpost rover for the Artemis mission, which is stated to launch in two years (approximately 2028).

## Electric Vehicles

1. **EV adoption flywheel and charging infrastructure progress:** Cal Lton expressed optimism about continued EV adoption growth, citing progress on charging infrastructure ubiquity, ease of use, and standards integration. He specifically highlighted the broader industry adoption of the Tesla charging standard as a significant positive step toward a more seamless charging experience for consumers.
