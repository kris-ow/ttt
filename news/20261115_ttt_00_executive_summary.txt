## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 15 (Nov 13–14): ship 42 completed ~14 orbits and became the first orbital rocket upper stage ever caught by a launch tower, at Starbase on Nov 14 at 3:21pm CT. Super Heavy booster 22 aborted its catch roughly 6.5 minutes in after running low on fuel — one of 33 engines failed to relight during the return burn — and instead performed an offshore water landing where it later exploded/overheated.
- Musk attributed the booster issue to slight engine underperformance on ascent causing a copper-fueled green flame from regenerative-cooling-circuit depletion; he said a fix is coming via Raptor V3.2 and an earlier ascent cutoff, and that the loss doesn't change SpaceX's timeline to high-volume reusable Starship flights.
- Ship 42 deployed all 26 onboard Starlink V3 satellites during the flight; SpaceX says heat shield condition looked markedly improved versus earlier test flights.
- SpaceX has asked the FAA to approve scaling Starbase flight cadence from 100 launches/200 landings per year up toward 456 launches/912 landings annually; Musk has cited a longer-term target exceeding 10,000 liftoffs/year by 2030.
- SpaceX is planning a Starbase facility in Louisiana on the Gulf Coast, which Musk has said could eventually handle 30+ Starship flights per day; one account projects ~3,000 direct jobs in Vermilion Parish over 10 years.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla opened invitation-only personal ownership of Cybercab (no steering wheel/pedals) at $29,990 plus $299/month, requiring a $1,000 deposit, Texas delivery estimated December; the car works only inside existing Robotaxi geofences and cannot be used for ride-hailing. Electrek calculates a five-year total of $47,631 before insurance, registration, and charging, and argues a $99/month Model Y with FSD offers more capability for less money.
- Musk said the subscription fee is too high and should be closer to the ~$115/month FSD-and-connectivity fee on other models, though the $299 fee bundles maintenance, tires, and windshields beyond FSD alone.
- The launch is tied to a bet with YouTuber MKBHD, who said in September Tesla wouldn't ship a $30k personal Cybercab in 2026; Musk called him "a chicken" after the order page went live.
- Ashok Elluswamy said owners will likely be able to add cars to the ride-hailing network around Q2 2027; personal Cybercabs today can only be parked, not monetized.
- San Antonio became Tesla's eighth Robotaxi city with a ~275 sq mi footprint, the largest day-one zone yet; Austin's zone grew ~62.5% to ~442 sq mi the same day, pushing Tesla's combined Texas footprint from ~751 to ~1,194 sq mi overnight (+59%), reportedly ~4x Waymo's Texas footprint.
- Tesla announced 24/7 Robotaxi service across all eight live cities.
- Tesla briefly edged ahead of Waymo in Texas registered vehicles (1,302 vs 1,297) before Waymo pulled back ahead (1,321 vs 1,303) by Friday.
- Signals of further expansion: robotaxi-related job postings in 42 US cities; a Las Vegas test fleet reportedly at 53 vehicles with a Nevada 120-day commercial-service deadline looming; reports of accelerating Saudi Arabia interest with Tesla a leading candidate.

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck production at Giga Texas was set to wind down mid-2027 to free capacity for Cybercab, then Musk added a shift, floated refunding some deposits, and ultimately extended production to end of summer 2027 to work through the order backlog.
- Cybertruck prices rose 9%; only the Cyberbeast remains orderable at $109,990, with deliveries pushed to March–June 2027. Musk said a future version will get a ~16% smaller battery pack and a "liquid silver" color option.
- Per Kelley Blue Book, Model Y and Model 3 combined outsold all other EVs in the US combined last quarter; Model Y was reported as Australia's top-selling passenger car year-to-date.

## Autonomous Driving
- Finland approved FSD Supervised (branded "Tesla Assisted Driving" in Europe), the 11th European country to do so, with immediate use ahead of an expected Europe-wide vote this winter. UK regulators reportedly expect new UN rules on supervised autonomous driving effective December 24, potentially auto-adopted by ~60 countries. Tesla also plans an Argentina launch this month.
- Tesla VP Lars Moravy told Jay Leno that vehicle owners bear liability under FSD Supervised, while Tesla is the "driver" of record for robotaxi and future company-operated driverless deployments.
- Tesla's first Times Square billboard promotes FSD with Tesla's own marketing claims of "7x safer than a human when engaged" and "15+ billion miles driven globally."

## Humanoid Bots

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk said SpaceX is on track for $100 billion in annualized revenue as soon as December and put odds of $1 trillion annualized revenue next year at ~2% (up from zero); he said this quarter's SpaceX revenue alone would exceed NASA's ~$18.8 billion annual budget.
- Deutsche Bank estimates SpaceX's compute business alone could end the year with ~$77 billion in contracted annual revenue, separate from launch or Starlink.
- New projects disclosed: a $50 billion, up to 6-gigawatt AI data center complex in Cochise County, Arizona (construction starting 2027); a $24 billion, 444-acre AI data center near Port of Brownsville, annexed into Starbase jurisdiction after a local moratorium. Memphis's mayor said SpaceX's local tax payments more than doubled to $54 million.
- SpaceX's "Star Mind"/AI1 orbital compute program with Nvidia is targeting a satellite carrying an Nvidia GPU rack, with specs of ~30m deployed height, ~75m wingspan, up to 250kW peak compute payload; SpaceX is building a satellite manufacturing facility in Bastrop, Texas.

## SpaceX — Starlink

- Vivo's Starlink-based phone service in Brazil reportedly gained 10 million users within two weeks of launch. SpaceX released the Starlink Mini 2 ($999, ~4.5-hour battery), shipping in December. United Airlines says Starlink is now installed on over half its daily flights.
