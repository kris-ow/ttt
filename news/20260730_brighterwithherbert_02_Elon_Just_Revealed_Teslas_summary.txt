This episode of a Tesla-focused discussion covers four Tesla-related developments — Elon Musk's plan to open-source the Model S and Model X design, stretched Model Y delivery timelines in Canada, a legal dispute threatening Cybertruck production, and a look at Ford's EV losses — with commentary from guest Brian (of the YouTube channel FutureAzA). The core thread is that Tesla's near-term EV story is mixed: strong-looking demand signals are entangled with supply constraints, and Cybertruck faces a fresh production risk just as its price cuts had begun working.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Musk plans to open-source Model S/X design and software:** Elon Musk announced Tesla will open-source the design and software of the discontinued Model S and Model X, mirroring the approach taken with the original Roadster. The guest called this consistent with Tesla's philosophy and plausible to help a niche market (e.g., a company continuing production, most likely in China) rather than a promotional stunt, though he doubts it will result in large-scale production given the vehicles' cost and limited market versus Tesla's own economics of scale.
2. **Model Y delivery dates in Canada pushed to late 2026/early 2027:** All Model Y variants in Canada are now showing delivery estimates stretching to the end of 2026 and into early 2027, despite five months remaining in the year. The guest attributed this primarily to Giga Berlin — now the sole source of Canadian Model Ys after Tesla shifted away from Giga Shanghai and then U.S.-based shipments due to tariffs — running at present capacity while adding and training new shifts, rather than purely a demand story, though he noted demand is also genuinely strong (citing faster-selling used EVs in Canada amid high gas prices).
3. **Canada import quota context for Model 3:** Model 3s sold in Canada come from China under a quota allowing a set number of EV imports (with roughly half required to be under a specified wholesale price threshold), while Model Ys face no such cap since they now originate from Germany; Tesla has so far captured the large majority of the import allocation used by all brands.
4. **Cybertruck production at risk from supplier/tooling lawsuit:** Tesla filed an emergency lawsuit alleging a dispute with a supplier (Angstrom Automotive Group, which acquired a Texas manufacturing facility along with tooling from Anderson Casting) is blocking Tesla's access to custom tooling — including large diecast equipment, trim dies, fixtures, cutting tools, gauges, and X-ray inspection equipment — needed to manufacture certain Cybertruck components. The filing reportedly states remaining component inventory could run out within days, that no alternate supplier can make the affected parts, and that reproducing the equipment would take five to six months; the guest said he doesn't know the specific part involved but called the situation potentially serious enough to halt Cybertruck production if the affected part is critical.
5. **Cybertruck pricing and demand history:** The guest recapped that Cybertruck pricing fell from an original ~$120,000/$100,000 level down to $80,000, then $60,000, with sales reportedly surging when the price dropped to $60,000. He argued Tesla did not increase production to match that demand surge, which he found surprising, and expressed hope Tesla returns to a $60,000 price point to drive volume once the tooling dispute is resolved. He also noted the truck's heavier, costlier structure (steel panels that don't act as a structural exoskeleton) made the originally promised sub-$40,000 price point unachievable in hindsight.
6. **Cybertruck factory footprint tension with Cybercab:** Drawing on an earlier factory tour with a Tesla-focused YouTuber (Joe Tegtmeyer), the host raised concern that Cybertruck occupies a large footprint at Giga Texas relative to its sales volume compared to the Cybercab line, questioning whether that space could be better used for Cybercab. The guest suggested a lower-cost "Cyber SUV" variant using composite panels (avoiding paint-shop expansion) on the same production line could improve economies of scale and broaden appeal, while disagreeing with the idea that Cybertruck's design is needed for Mars/Moon use given its Earth-gravity-optimized suspension and weight.

## Market & Competition
1. **Ford Q2 EV losses despite raised full-year guidance:** Ford's EV unit (Model E) lost $900 million in the quarter on revenue of about $1 billion, down 58% year-over-year, which management attributed to reduced Mustang Mach-E production and discontinuation of the F-150 Lightning. Despite this, Ford beat overall adjusted EPS estimates (42 cents vs. 35 cents expected) and raised full-year adjusted EBIT guidance to a range up from a prior $8.5 billion level, also raising free cash flow expectations. The guest attributed much of the Mach-E weakness to it being tariffed despite Mexican manufacture under USMCA, contrasting with strong Mexican-built EV sales in Canada (e.g., GM's Blazer) where such tariffs don't apply, and noted declining carbon-credit revenue industry-wide (including for Tesla) as a factor no longer offsetting ICE inefficiency.
2. **Ford's upcoming lower-cost EV pickup:** Ford plans to unveil a new, smaller EV pickup truck in the second half of this year with a native NACS charging port, LFP battery, and a starting price around $30,000. The guest expects minimal volume at that starting price but views the LFP chemistry choice as a positive direction, and hopes the product helps Ford re-expand overseas EV sales.
