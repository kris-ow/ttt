This episode of the show (hosted this week by guest Cern Basher in place of the regular host) is a deep dive into SpaceX/xAI's data-center and AI compute buildout following the Grok 4.5 release, plus a discussion of Starship launch economics. Tesla appears only tangentially — mainly through speculation about a Tesla-built "megapod" energy product and AI5 chip supply — making this mostly an xAI/SpaceX story with limited direct relevance to Tesla's investment thesis.

## Market & Competition
1. **Grok 4.5 released, competitive with top-tier AI models:** The guest reports Grok 4.5 launched the day of the show, scoring competitively against Anthropic's Opus-tier models on software engineering and terminal benchmarks (cited around 62% on a software engineering benchmark), putting it in the "second tier" of top models alongside Opus, though still behind the newest "Fable" frontier model.
2. **Grok 4.5 dramatically cheaper per task:** The guest claims Grok 4.5 uses far fewer tokens per task (roughly under 20,000 vs. ~100,000 for a comparison "Fable" model) and is priced around $1.50 versus $17 for that comparison model — roughly one-tenth the price — positioning xAI as the low-cost/high-efficiency leader on the AI pricing "Pareto frontier."
3. **xAI's chip and data-center lead over rivals:** The guest estimates xAI currently controls roughly 40% of all currently-installed (not merely announced) working Nvidia Blackwell chips globally, driven by faster data-center buildout (Colossus 2/3 sites) versus rivals like OpenAI/Oracle and other hyperscalers; he expects this share to gradually decline toward 20-30% as competitors catch up over the next few years, then spike again with next-gen "Reuben" chips.
4. **Power buildout at Colossus sites:** xAI's power capacity is estimated near 2 gigawatts currently (about 60 natural-gas turbines, up from 27 in December), with plans to more than double to over 4 gigawatts by around March 2027 via additional large Korean-made turbines (~380MW units, 12 on order) and continued smaller turbine additions (~5-7/month).
5. **Emissions lawsuit unlikely to be a major setback:** A lawsuit (NAACP/API group) over turbine emissions at the Colossus 2 site is described by the guest as unlikely to halt operations — he expects a slow legal process, no injunction, and possible fines only, partly because the DOJ has classified the infrastructure as critical.
6. **Morgan Stanley (Adam Jonas) SpaceX AI projections discussed:** The guest cites a Morgan Stanley report projecting SpaceX/xAI data center capacity growing from ~2 gigawatts now to 8 gigawatts by 2028 and 16 gigawatts within two years after, roughly aligning with his own estimates, though he disputes the report's assumption of rental prices collapsing over time (e.g., dropping from current levels to $11-12 billion per gigawatt by 2030 and ~$3 billion by 2040).
7. **Starship/Falcon 9 launch economics:** The guest estimates Starship's per-kilogram launch cost (without reuse) around $500/kg, already cheaper than Falcon 9's real cost (~$600-700/kg with reuse), and projects costs could fall to $100-200/kg with full reusability; current Starship production is around one per month, with capacity to scale to about four per month.

## Energy
1. **"Megapod" as modular AI data-center building block:** The guest describes a hypothesized Tesla-made product ("megapod") analogous to Megapack units but designed to package natural gas turbines, racks, and cooling into standardized, scalable blocks for rapid AI data-center deployment — potentially unveiled around Q1-Q2 of next year, with SpaceX/xAI as the likely primary customer.
2. **Supercharger network as latent power source:** The guest speculates Tesla's global Supercharger network may total around 7 gigawatts of provisioned power (with growth of 1-3 GW/year), floating the idea that some of this capacity could eventually be used to power compute infrastructure via the megapod concept.
3. **AI5 chip allocation trade-off between vehicles and data centers:** The guest suggests Tesla's upcoming AI5 chips, needed for both Cybercab/vehicle autonomy and megapod-style data centers, may be more profitable when deployed in data centers than in vehicles, and that memory supply (from Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix) rather than chip production itself is likely the binding constraint over the next few years — tied to the rationale for Tesla's "Terafab" chip initiative.
