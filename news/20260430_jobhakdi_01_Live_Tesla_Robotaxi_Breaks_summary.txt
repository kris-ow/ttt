In this live session, Jo Bhakdi delivers a bullish update on Tesla's robotaxi rollout, arguing that the deployment of Cybercabs and the growth of the unsupervised robotaxi fleet represent a historic inflection in transportation that the market is undervaluing. He frames the current moment as a rare investment opportunity obscured by retail investor pessimism and short-term earnings noise. ## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised Fleet Hitting New Record — 25 Vehicles Across Three Cities:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised robotaxi fleet has reached 25 vehicles: 19 in Austin, 3 in Dallas, and 3 in Houston. Bhakdi highlights this as the most important chart to watch, describing it as a decisive and exciting milestone even if still small in absolute terms.

2. **1,800 Unsupervised Robotaxis as the "Forcing Function":** Bhakdi reiterates his thesis that 1,800 deployed robotaxis — primarily unsupervised — represents a "forcing function," not merely an inflection point. At that scale, he calculates the annualized free cash flow contribution would equal approximately 5% of Tesla's entire 2024 free cash flow, making it impossible for even skeptical analysts to ignore. He now estimates this target may be reached by September–November 2026, acknowledging a potential 6-month delay from his earlier projection, but maintains the thesis is intact.

3. **Cybercab Production Ramping at Giga Texas:** Bhakdi references Joe Tegtmeyer's tracking work, noting that Cybercabs are rolling off the production line at Giga Texas at a rate of roughly 10–20 units per day. He predicts production could reach thousands of Cybercabs per month by June 2026. Vehicles have been spotted in Illinois, Massachusetts, Florida, the Bay Area, Texas, Phoenix, and potentially Georgia, suggesting broad geographic pre-deployment distribution. He notes that some Cybercabs are being manufactured and shipped without a steering wheel, which he treats as meaningful signal about their intended autonomous-only use case.

4. **Regulatory Environment Seen as Non-Issue for Scale:** Responding to a viewer arguing that regulation will block Cybercab scaling, Bhakdi disagrees strongly. He contends that in states like Georgia, Arizona, Florida, and Texas, the environment is largely self-regulated with no major hurdles. He frames Cybercab deployment as purely a function of FSD technology quality, not regulatory barriers — with California and New York being the notable exceptions, which he says Tesla does not need in the near term.

5. **Total Addressable Market Requires Replacing Car Ownership:** Bhakdi argues that for Cybercab to justify a multi-trillion-dollar TAM, it must go beyond replacing Uber, Lyft, and taxis — it must eat deeply into personal car ownership and replace public transport. He treats this as the core economic assumption underpinning the long-term Tesla bull case.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD End-to-End Neural Net as the Competitive Moat:** Bhakdi dismisses Waymo and Chinese autonomous competitors by arguing they cannot scale due to technology limitations — relying on what he characterizes as "bandaging and taping" their way to pseudo-success. He contrasts this with Tesla's vision-only, end-to-end FSD neural net system, which he believes is the only architecture capable of true massive scale.

2. **Tesla Semi Autonomy Predicted for 2027:** Bhakdi flags the Tesla Semi as an underappreciated asset, noting that the first production units are now rolling off the line. He argues that highway autonomy for trucking is technically simpler than urban driving (though higher risk) and predicts Tesla will deploy autonomous Semi trucks by 2027. He describes the trucking and rail market as an enormous, largely uncontested TAM with zero autonomous competition currently, and characterizes the Semi as highly profitable.
