This video is a two-host breakdown of SpaceX's first earnings report since its IPO, focusing on the company's AI/compute buildout, Starlink growth (including a newly announced Starlink Mobile direct-to-cell business), and launch business economics, framed around whether SpaceX can keep growing revenue over the next several decades.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Capex and AI spend beat expectations:** SpaceX spent $18 billion in capex this quarter, with roughly $14 billion attached to AI; the hosts note this is small relative to other "Mag 7"-scale AI spenders. The CFO reportedly said the company expects to recoup this AI capex within about a year — faster than competitors — and that SpaceX is on pace for a $100 billion annualized AI revenue run rate by the end of this year.
2. **Nvidia partnership:** SpaceX announced an exclusive partnership with Nvidia to use its Vera Rubin chips, with Nvidia committing a significant portion of its chip allocation to SpaceX, which the hosts say lets SpaceX build data centers faster than competitors.
3. **Compute capacity targets:** SpaceX expects to reach 2 gigawatts of AI compute by the end of this year (with Colossus 1, Colossus 2, and "Macrohard" already operating) and, per a projection attributed to Elon Musk, could reach roughly 8–10 gigawatts by the end of next year. One host cites an analysis by Cern Basher estimating an 8-gigawatt buildout by end of 2027 could represent a $500–600 billion per year rental opportunity — about five times Tesla's current revenue — while cautioning this is speculative ("a big if").
4. **AI segment financials:** AI revenue came in at $2.5 billion versus $2.1 billion expected (per Street Account), though the AI unit posted a $1.2 billion operating loss for the period; the connectivity segment, by contrast, was profitable with operating income of $1.66 billion.
5. **Return on data center capex and next build location:** The CFO (referred to by the hosts as Brett Johnson) reportedly said return on capex for data center leasing is coming in under a year, prompting continued build-out. One host relays an unconfirmed rumor that the next data center location will be in Texas, noting Texas is working on a moratorium on new data center permits because applications run roughly five times current peak demand — though xAI-style self-generated power could help circumvent that constraint.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Launch/"Space" segment revenue slightly missed:** The launch revenue segment came in about half a billion dollars below expectations per Street Account, which the hosts characterize as a minor miss. They attribute much of SpaceX's roughly $45 billion backlog to launch demand, and note SpaceX recently signed a $6 billion launch contract with the US government, which one host argues reflects being the lowest-cost, most reliable bidder rather than a subsidy. Falcon 9 Block 5 is described as having the best safety/reliability record of any rocket in history.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink subscriber growth and enterprise shift:** Starlink subscribers reportedly doubled to 12 million; Elon Musk and Gwynne Shotwell reportedly said they expect enterprise Starlink demand to eventually exceed consumer demand, citing improved latency/reliability and a new enterprise sales push, alongside strong uptake in maritime and aerospace.
2. **Connectivity revenue beat:** Connectivity revenue was $4 billion versus an expected $3.8 billion.
3. **Starlink Mobile (direct-to-cell) announced for next year:** Gwynne Shotwell announced plans to launch Starlink Mobile next year, built on a distributed terrestrial network potentially leveraging existing dishes rather than large cell towers; she reportedly cited the roughly $600 billion/year US wireless market (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) as a target opportunity, noting SpaceX's advantage of no dead zones and resilience versus terrestrial disasters. The hosts note the company already claims roughly 100x improvement in the underlying technology (10x more satellites, 10x better spectrum usage, now supporting video) but caution the initial mobile rollout will likely be limited in scope and take longer than people expect.
4. **V3 Starlink satellites:** One host relays claims from unnamed prior guests that V3 Starlink satellites offer a substantial capacity improvement over V2, though he could not recall the exact multiple.

## Financials
1. **Quarterly loss and full-year 2025 loss:** SpaceX reported a quarterly net loss described as roughly a half billion dollars, better than an expected loss described as four times larger, with loss-per-share coming in far smaller than forecast. The hosts also cite a prior full-year loss of $4.9 billion, noting this looks reasonable given simultaneous investment in Colossus 1 and 2, Starship development, and a not-yet-profitable Starlink buildout.
2. **First post-IPO 10-K and modeling uncertainty:** The hosts note this is SpaceX's first annual report (10-K) since its IPO, meaning most prior analyst estimates were rough guesses; they single out Aaron Bernett of Mach33 as having the most credible financial model pre-earnings, though even his estimates were said to be conservative relative to actual results.
3. **Stock reaction:** The hosts describe a "buy the rumor, sell the news" pattern in SpaceX's stock around the earnings report, based on a five-day trading chart shown on screen, without citing specific price levels.
