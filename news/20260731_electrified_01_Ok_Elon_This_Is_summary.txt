This episode of Electrified covers a denied WSJ report on a Tesla–SpaceX merger tied to a Giga Shanghai sale, new NHTSA rules easing federal AV hardware requirements (which mostly don't apply to Tesla since Cybercab is already self-certified), several Robotaxi service hiccups, a Cybertruck PCS recall/investigation, new suspension-related NHTSA scrutiny, Tesla energy and manufacturing milestones, and a deep dive into SpaceX's compute infrastructure buildout and associated lease revenue.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger report denied by Musk:** A Wall Street Journal report claimed Tesla was preparing to sell Giga Shanghai to help complete a SpaceX merger/acquisition; Musk called this "absurdly fake news" and said it has never come up in discussions. The host says he'll revisit if any confirmation emerges, noting Tesla selling Giga Shanghai isn't the only path to clear such a merger, but treats the report as unconfirmed for now.
2. **SpaceX earnings call date set:** SpaceX will hold its first earnings call on August 4th at 4:30pm Eastern.

## Robotaxi
1. **NHTSA fast-tracks AV rules, but mostly bypasses Tesla:** NHTSA is implementing five changes to accelerate AV deployment, including allowing Zoox to commercially deploy up to 2,500 robotaxis per year for two years under a Part 555 temporary exemption, funding a three-year $5 million AV performance standards program with SAE, modernizing exemption applications, and updating AV technical guidance. The host stresses Tesla's Cybercab is unaffected by vehicle caps because it is already self-certified as meeting FMVSS standards, so this news benefits other AV companies (like Zoox) more than Tesla. He notes NHTSA could theoretically challenge Tesla's self-certification later, but that likelihood is decreasing as federal AV frameworks mature; a separate federal framework for actual robotaxi operation (vs. hardware) — such as the proposed SELF DRIVE Act — remains stalled, meaning Tesla still must navigate state-by-state deployment rules.
2. **Regulation isn't the bottleneck in Texas:** Per a clarification the host received, Tesla's daily active robotaxi fleet in Austin was around 30 (fleet trackers showing 80+ reflect total, not daily active, vehicles). The host argues Tesla could scale to thousands of robotaxis in Texas with no regulatory hurdles, meaning operational/software issues — not regulation — are the current limiting factor.
3. **Operational hiccups reported by users:** A rider in Houston said support told him to exit and cancel a robotaxi ride around 8:30pm after he was already inside the vehicle. Separately, a robotaxi in Dallas got stuck in a turnaround loop for 3–5 minutes with a "poor connectivity" message before support intervened. The host frames these as evidence Tesla isn't yet ready to scale to thousands of vehicles, though not safety-critical.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD parking garage crash flagged:** A user reported that on the fourth day of ownership, FSD crashed into a pole in a parking garage; the host used this as a reminder to stay vigilant around concrete barriers, shopping cart returns, and chains.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck gets carbon fiber underbody panels:** New Cybertrucks (seen on a Cyber Beast) have carbon fiber underbody panels described by a Tesla-affiliated source as more durable than aluminum, lower weight and cost, and offering a slight efficiency improvement due to better airflow around bolted areas versus stamped aluminum. Retrofit availability for existing trucks is unknown.
2. **Cybertruck PCS failures prompt warranty extension and recall-adjacent NHTSA probe:** Following complaints about Cybertruck's power conversion system (PCS/PCS2) failing and limiting AC charging, Tesla extended PCS warranty to 7 years/70,000 miles for 2026 vehicles and 8 years/150,000 miles for 2024–2025 vehicles, will reimburse prior out-of-pocket PCS replacement costs starting in the coming weeks, pushed a software update to maintain Supercharging despite PCS failure, and is offering free Supercharging for trucks awaiting parts. Separately, NHTSA opened a preliminary investigation into roughly 1.2 million Teslas over suspension failure reports that could cause loss of steering control, covering certain 2018–2020 Model 3 and 2021–2023 Model Y vehicles with 156 complaints; NHTSA said this is distinct from an earlier related investigation.
3. **10 million vehicles produced milestone:** Tesla announced 10 million vehicles produced globally. The host notes this took nearly 20 years, while the next 10 million (relevant to the 20-million-vehicle milestone in Musk's new compensation package) could take an estimated 3–5 years.

## Energy
1. **New solar PPAs confirmed for Supercharger network:** Tesla confirmed it is the buyer in three power purchase agreements with Concord New Energy for US solar projects totaling 469 megawatts AC, expected to supply about 1,200 gigawatt-hours of electricity annually once fully operational, with the first project's power supply expected in the first half of 2027. Musk said these deals support renewable energy supply for the Supercharger network; the host notes these were originally announced in 2025 but confirmed as Tesla deals now, alongside a separate Arizona solar farm PPA and a northern Texas PPA covered earlier in the week.
2. **Domestic Megapack LFP cells begin shipping:** Tesla said its first Megapacks using US-made LFP cells (produced in Sparks, Nevada, integrated into Megapacks in California) are shipping to customers, qualifying for a $35/kWh domestic manufacturing tax credit. The host estimates this could be worth roughly $245 million annually based on the Nevada LFP line's ~7 gigawatt-hour capacity, but cautions that Tesla's guided 20–25% gross energy margins likely already factor this in, and margins are unlikely to return toward 30% barring one-offs.
3. **New "Megapod" hardware role posted:** Tesla is hiring for a Megapod hardware lead role tied to new hardware for AI data centers, with responsibilities including go-to-market strategy; the host expects meaningful developments sometime in 2027 rather than this year.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Mini Hard data center design cuts footprint 75%:** Musk described a new SpaceX AI facility called "mini hard," featuring the same 220,000 GB300 chips and 800-gig NICs as "macro hard" but in a redesigned, much denser layout occupying roughly 25% of the floor space for similar compute capacity. The host argues these efficiency gains in terrestrial data centers will eventually benefit Tesla's Cortex compute efforts.
2. **Compute lease deals already total ~$28B/year, with room to scale toward $100B:** Existing compute lease agreements include Anthropic renting effectively all of Colossus 1 (~$15B/year revenue run rate), Google renting a portion of Colossus 2 (~$11B/year), and Reflection renting a portion of Colossus 2 (~$2B/year), totaling roughly $28B/year already under contract. Colossus 1 is reportedly 100% leased and Colossus 2 about 33% leased. With Macro Hard and Mini Hard targeted for completion around early 2027, the four sites combined could reach roughly 1.2 million GPUs (220K at Colossus 1, 550K at Colossus 2, 220K at Macro Hard, 220K at Mini Hard); the host calculates that if SpaceX AI hypothetically leased out all this compute, it could generate roughly $100 billion annually, though he stresses SpaceX won't lease it all since some compute is needed for Grok training and internal use. For comparison, SpaceX's total 2025 company revenue (including xAI) was roughly $18 billion, meaning a single large lease deal could double that.
3. **Reported demand pipeline could reach ~3 million GPUs:** A third-party report cited by the host claims SpaceX AI has roughly 8 gigawatts of demand-side compute locked in (potentially around 3 million GPUs), with a named investor (Gavin Baker) reportedly citing 4 gigawatts this year and another 4 gigawatts next year per that report; the host emphasizes this is not a guarantee. The same report flagged regulation as the biggest uncertainty, noting every turbine at Colossus 1 operates without a permit, with possible outcomes including fines and retroactive permitting, relocation, or shutdown.
4. **Turbine transition near Memphis:** SpaceX said it will remove unpermitted turbines powering xAI data centers near Memphis as it transitions to a permanent 1.2 gigawatt natural gas power plant, with full turbine removal not expected until July 2027; SpaceX said it currently operates 69 gas turbines powering the Colossus data centers. The host notes unconfirmed word that the recently acquired APR Energy's turbine fleet is not likely to be used for this new power plant and may instead serve an unannounced separate project.
