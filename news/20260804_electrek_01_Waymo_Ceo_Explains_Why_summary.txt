The article reports on Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov's remarks at Y Combinator's Startup School arguing that camera-only sensing (Tesla's approach, though not named) can match human driving and power an assist product, but hits a safety "ceiling" or plateau before reaching the superhuman reliability needed for full driverless autonomy, while contrasting this with Waymo's multi-sensor (camera, lidar, radar) approach and safety/scale data.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Dolgov's sensing argument:** Speaking at Y Combinator's Startup School, Dolgov said cameras alone can approximate human performance and support a driver-assist product, but "weak sensing" leads to a safety curve that "flattens out way too early" for full autonomy requiring strongly superhuman performance. He said Waymo's cameras, lidar, and radar are not backups to each other but fuse into a single view "vastly superior to what you get with any one sensor," citing examples (a Phoenix dust storm, people jumping barriers at night, kids chasing dogs in darkness) where camera feeds were nearly useless but lidar performed well, plus a case where a tree branch on a Waymo's sensor caused it to use other sensors to self-drive back to its depot.
2. **The "nines" reliability framing:** Dolgov described an "exponential ladder of nines," where each additional nine of reliability takes roughly ten times more effort than the last; he warned against extrapolating a steep early demo curve forward, since it typically plateaus before reaching the reliability a real driverless product requires. Elon Musk uses a similar "march of the nines" framing, per the article.
3. **Cost argument on lidar:** Dolgov said Waymo is on its sixth generation of hardware and that each generation has sharply cut sensor costs, arguing that betting against lidar based on today's prices assumes "a number that has a fairly short shelf life."
4. **Tesla's camera-only strategy and results:** Tesla removed radar (2021) and ultrasonic sensors (2022), betting fully on cameras ("Tesla Vision"); Musk has called lidar "a fool's errand." The article states Tesla's own robotaxi data shows a crash rate about three times worse than human drivers even with an in-car safety monitor, and asserts Musk's repeated claims that FSD is safer than humans have not been borne out by data.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet mileage comparison:** The article states Tesla has confirmed accumulating 380,000 driverless miles over the past year since its robotaxi launch in Austin (2025), which the article contrasts with Waymo's 220 million rider-only miles and roughly half a million weekly paid trips across 15 US cities, with Waymo reportedly scaling toward a 1-million-weekly-rides target.
2. **Waymo safety report:** Waymo's latest safety report, based on 220 million rider-only miles, reportedly shows 94% fewer serious-injury crashes than human drivers over comparable distance (described as roughly 17 times better).

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues Tesla's robotaxi service is effectively still a geo-fenced "demo" given its low driverless mileage, and that Dolgov's "nines" framing exposes why Tesla's steadily improving FSD releases won't necessarily extrapolate to safe, scaled driverless operation. He also contends the cost gap between lidar-equipped and camera-only vehicles is shrinking, weakening the cost argument for camera-only systems, and cites an unsourced claim ("we are hearing") that Waymo's full vehicle-plus-sensor-and-driver package is nearing about $60,000.
