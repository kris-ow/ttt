Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that Tesla's clean NHTSA autonomous-vehicle crash record is not a safety achievement but a consequence of its robotaxi program barely operating — with the active fleet shrinking rather than scaling a full year after launch.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet size is far smaller than permits suggest:** Texas's DMV database, updated after a new state law took effect May 28, shows Tesla self-certified its Model Y robotaxis as SAE Level 4 and obtained 42 driverless operation authorizations. For comparison, Waymo holds 577 Texas authorizations and Avride holds 317. Per the latest RobotaxiTracker.com data, Tesla's total active fleet across all markets is just 31 vehicles seen in the past seven days — 16 in Austin, 7 in Dallas, 3 in Houston, and 5 supervised vehicles in the Bay Area — with only 14 operating unsupervised. That means Tesla is running fewer than one-third of its Texas permits in unsupervised mode.

2. **The fleet is contracting, not growing:** Tesla launched in Austin in June 2025. The unsupervised fleet peaked at 25 cumulative vehicles in late April 2026, then declined. Dallas and Houston, which launched in April with small geofences, have barely grown since. Musk originally projected 500 robotaxis in Austin and over 1,000 in the Bay Area by end of 2025, later reduced the Austin target to roughly 60, and the current unsupervised count stands at 14. Musk now cites the FSD v15 software rewrite as the prerequisite for aggressive scaling, pushing any real ramp to late 2026 or 2027.

## Autonomous Driving
3. **Low crash count reflects low exposure, not system superiority:** Tesla's total NHTSA ADS incident count sits at 18 since the Austin launch, versus roughly 697 for Waymo. Lambert attributes the gap entirely to exposure: Waymo operates approximately 3,000 robotaxis and completes more than 500,000 paid trips per week. When Tesla unredacted its 17 crash narratives last month, most incidents were rear-endings and sideswipes by human drivers — the same pattern seen in Waymo's data. The latest report adds one more stopped-and-rear-ended event with no Tesla at-fault finding.

## Market & Competition
4. **Waymo is expanding aggressively while Tesla contracts:** Waymo recently raised $16 billion to fund expansion to London and Tokyo, now covers over 1,400 square miles across 11 U.S. markets, and operates thousands of vehicles. Tesla, meanwhile, has been expanding its geofence — most recently to the entire Austin metro — while the actual vehicle count declines.

## Editorial Stance
Lambert argues that Tesla's growing geofence is a costless metric that obscures a shrinking operational fleet, and that the clean crash record should be read as evidence of how little the program is doing rather than how well the technology works. He states the real test only comes when hundreds or thousands of driverless Teslas are on the road and crashes are measured against actual rides delivered.
