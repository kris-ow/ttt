## Robotaxi

- **One-year anniversary, slow ramp acknowledged.** June 22, 2026 marks exactly one year since Tesla's Austin launch — Elon Musk's opening price was a $4.20 flat fee, with 500,000 people watching the live stream. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active fleet stands at 738 vehicles across four markets. Both the Herbert/Jeff discussion and Loomis independently call the pace "materially behind" expectations.

- **Five-city expansion target effectively missed.** With 7 days left on Tesla's stated timeline for launching in five new cities, Loomis says it "feels very doubtful" and declares he is "now confident Tesla will not catch Waymo this year." He directly challenges any 2026 forecast of 10,000 robotaxis as not credible.

- **Jeff forecasts meaningful acceleration in July.** Tesla's supply-chain guest on Herbert's channel argues the remaining blockers are pickup/drop-off polish and physical Cybercab deployment — not safety — and predicts "meaningful movement" in July 2026 based on what he is observing. His frame: "slow at first, then all at once."

- **California regulatory signals — cautiously positive.** The California DMV updated Tesla's AV permit holder name to "Tesla Robotaxi LLC," and a new limousine permit was issued covering SFO pickups and drop-offs. Loomis clarifies both are administrative only — no new CPUC autonomous vehicle permit has been granted. Jeff argues driverless authorization may be "fairly imminent" given nine-plus months of testing data and no serious incidents on file with NHTSA.

- **Austin safety record: 18 incidents in 12 months, last at-fault in February.** Per the robotaxi dashboard cited by Loomis, 8 of the 18 incidents were attributed to other parties. Tesla has been at-fault-incident-free since February 2026.

- **Houston hub expanding; new construction project registered.** On-the-ground observer Sladoch counted 55 Cybercabs at the Houston hub, up from 45 two days prior. Service hours now begin as early as 7:00 a.m. Tesla separately registered a new $5.2 million renovation project with the Texas TDLR on June 16 — a 38,900 sq ft building near central Houston, with work scheduled October 2026 through April 2027. Purpose (robotaxi vs. Megapack) is unconfirmed.

- **Tesla hiring signals global platform intent.** Analysis of 10 Tesla robotaxi job-posting categories (cernbasher) reveals 24/7 fleet ops roles, field incident-response specialists (with wheel-swap duties), a Palo Alto-based global commercial expansion manager explicitly scoped to "autonomous vehicle fleets across target cities and regions" and "mobile ecosystems around the world," a multi-sector business development role spanning mobility, logistics, and retail, and a dedicated real estate manager for robotaxi infrastructure. A fleet support posting is located in Tampa, Florida — a market not yet announced. Four ~31,000 sq ft support locations in Texas are already identified.

- **Texas AV registrations show Tesla growing fast from a small base.** Per OTM AI scorecard data cited by FutureAzA: Tesla has 69 registered autonomous vehicles in Texas as of the video date, up 64% from 42 in May. Waymo holds 620 registered vehicles in Texas, up ~7.5% from 577 in May.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Spain milestone: 171,000+ miles, zero serious incidents.** Spain's National Road Authority reported Tesla's supervised FSD fleet has driven over 171,000 miles of public road testing since November 2025 with no serious incident. Tesla's 30-car fleet is the most active tester on Spain's registry by a wide margin — the next largest entry is bus operator Alsa at approximately 900 miles. Only one other company tests level-two passenger cars in Spain, with three vehicles.

- **Texas crash: driver override confirmed, not FSD.** A Tesla struck a home at 73 mph in a residential area, fatally injuring the occupant, with initial media reports implicating Autopilot. Post-incident data (per "A Shouk," cited by Loomis) showed the driver manually pressed the accelerator to 100% pedal travel. Elon Musk stated publicly: "This makes no sense. FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets." Sawyer Merritt also addressed the story, noting Autopilot is not self-driving and that Tesla's logs would be determinative.

- **Tesla Semi with LiDAR ground-truth equipment spotted in Sunnyvale.** Community member Danny shared photos; Loomis and FutureAzA both covered it. This is the first confirmed public sighting of a Semi outfitted with autonomous validation equipment. Loomis does not expect unsupervised Semi FSD to be in serious consideration until 2028.

- **Grok voice integration in Tesla vehicles ~3 months out.** Elon Musk stated the ability to converse with Grok inside a Tesla — for commands like drop-off location or parking — will arrive in approximately 3 months, implying roughly September 2026.

- **Lemonade FSD insurance now in 4 states.** Colorado is the latest addition, joining Arizona (January 2026), Oregon (February 2026), and Indiana (earlier June 2026). Lemonade operates car insurance in approximately 11 states. Jeff notes FSD doing the driving improved his personal insurance score enough to save roughly $100 per vehicle per month versus like-for-like coverage.

- **FSD consumer software "almost perfect" per supply chain executive.** Jeff characterizes current consumer FSD as meaningfully better than a year ago and "almost perfect" with the exception of parking lot departure and final drop-off behavior — the same issues he flags as the critical path for robotaxi scaling.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Megapod trademark filed — distributed AI compute at Superchargers.** Tesla filed an intent-to-use trademark for "Megapod," described as modular data center hardware systems for AI computing comprising servers, AI processing hardware, networking hardware, power distribution units, and cooling systems, sold as a unit with monitoring software. Loomis and Herbert both connect this directly to Elon Musk's March 2026 statement that Tesla would deploy "millions of dedicated digital Optimus units in the field at Supercharger locations," where Tesla has approximately 7 gigawatts of available power.

- **Digital Optimus / Macrohard capability exists; material deployment likely 2027–2028.** Elon described the system as Grok ("system two" reasoning) directing digital Optimus ("system one" execution) to process real-time screen video and keyboard/mouse actions. It already works on all AI4-equipped vehicles. Loomis says the software is live but that Tesla's current robotaxi focus pushes material deployment to late 2027 into 2028.

- **Optimus factory construction underway at Giga Texas.** Joe Tegtmeyer shared construction images of the Optimus manufacturing facility planned for 10 million units per year capacity. Loomis estimates completion toward end of 2027.

- **Boston Dynamics fully consolidated under Hyundai — not an Optimus threat.** SoftBank sold its remaining ~10% stake to Hyundai for $325 million, implying a total company valuation just above $3 billion. FutureAzA and Sawyer Merritt both covered it. FutureAzA argues Boston Dynamics lacks the compute infrastructure, data centers, and manufacturing scale to compete with Optimus; the valuation figure is cited as evidence.

## Electric Vehicles

- **80+ Cybercabs visible at Giga Texas; new "Cybercab" decals replace "Robo Taxi."** Joe Tegtmeyer documented vehicles in the outbound lot, at factory exit, and running test pickup/drop-off runs on-site. A new batch carries "Cybercab" door and hatch decals — consistent with Elon's prior statement that labeling will vary by city based on local legal restrictions on the words "taxi" and "cab." Jeff frames production of labeled variants as a meaningful manufacturing milestone indicating multi-city regulatory preparation.

- **Cybercab design details confirmed in Austin field observation.** A Tesla employee simulating drop-off near downtown Austin was observed exiting and re-entering via the passenger door, sliding across an unobstructed seat to the driver side. Butterfly doors open upward to clear the curb. Wheel covers use rubber material designed for low-cost replacement after curb contact, with the substrate reportedly color-matched to minimize visible damage.

- **UK Model Y AWD/Performance deliveries resuming after type-approval gap.** Tesla introduced mid-cycle hardware upgrades at Giga Berlin — more energy-dense cells and rear motor improvements — that invalidated existing UK type approval certificates. Affected vehicles are physically in the UK but could not be registered. As of June 22, Tesla Owners UK reported self-scheduling links being sent to affected customers within 24–48 hours, with free Supercharging and premium connectivity offered as goodwill. Only AWD long-range and performance Model Y variants are affected; RWD and Model 3 are unaffected. VCA standard processing time for type approval updates is reportedly 3–6 weeks.

- **Model YL US production reportedly starting August–September 2026.** FutureAzA cites a source describing Giga Texas production of the larger third-row Model Y Long beginning in August or September 2026. Brian estimates pricing at $549.90 — roughly a $4,000 premium over the standard Model Y, consistent with China market differential. One published article had speculated $539.90.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX acquires Cursor for $60 billion.** SpaceX completed the acquisition of Cursor, an AI coding platform used by 64% of Fortune 500 companies with over 1 million daily active users writing more than 100 million lines of code per day. Cursor is now training a 1.5-trillion-parameter frontier model on 100,000 GPUs, described by co-founder Michael Truel as "deep into training" and releasing "in the next couple of weeks." Elon Musk characterized Cursor as "an important piece of the puzzle to make Grok much better."

- **SpaceX annualized compute revenue run rate: ~$28 billion from three deals.** A new agreement with Reflection AI ($150M/month, July 2026–2029, ~$6.3B total) joins existing deals with Anthropic (~$1.2B/month) and Google (just under $1B/month). Per ARK Invest data cited by Loomis, SpaceX's compute build cost is approximately $26B per gigawatt, while the Google deal implies revenue of over $53B per gigawatt — implying a greater-than-100% return on investment.

- **SpaceX IPO: $100.8 billion cash, investment-grade from all three agencies.** SpaceX's June 19 8-K disclosed approximately $100.8 billion in cash and equivalents including IPO proceeds. Fitch assigned an investment-grade rating on a new notes offering (terms unspecified), making all three major agencies now investment-grade on SpaceX. Tesla closed at $405.05 (+1.14%); SpaceX closed at $154.60 (–16.43%).

- **Tesla-SpaceX merger: Bhakdi proposes ratchet structure, expects offer within 1–3 months.** Bhakdi argues SpaceX must be the surviving acquirer (Musk holds 10x supervoting shares there), that SpaceX's 36 billion authorized vs. 13 billion outstanding shares signals pre-authorization for a large deal, and that float expansion to ~20% around August 2026 will pressure SpaceX's price before any vote. He models a 50% premium offer at $600/share ($1.88T Tesla market cap) and proposes a one-line ratchet: Tesla shareholders receive the greater of $1.88T or the full SpaceX market cap at vote date — ensuring Tesla shareholders are never diluted if SpaceX rises, while retaining a floor if it falls. Bhakdi: "No ratchet, no deal."

- **Waymo and Zoox remain the only scaled driverless ride-hailing operators.** Jeff (Herbert channel) notes no existing robotaxi operator is profitable. The OTM AI benchmarking platform ranks Baidu Apollo Go first, Waymo second, Pony AI third, WeRide fourth, and Tesla fifth across safety records and deployment metrics.

- **Mobileye and Uber both targeting Houston robotaxi entry in 2027.** Mobileye announced a U.S. robotaxi launch in 2027 with a five-year target of 17,000 vehicles. Uber is bringing a premium robotaxi to Houston in 2027 using a Lucid Gravity vehicle with Nuro software. FutureAzA is skeptical of both unit economics.
