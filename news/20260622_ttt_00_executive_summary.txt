## Robotaxi

- **One-year anniversary, slow ramp acknowledged.** June 22, 2026 marks exactly one year since Tesla launched its Austin robotaxi service at a $4.20 flat fee. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active fleet stands at 737 vehicles across four cities. Both Herbert Ong's guest Jeff and Dillon Loomis (Electrified) call the ramp "very disappointing relative to expectations," with Loomis flatly stating Tesla will not catch Waymo in 2026 and expressing serious doubt about the five-city expansion target — with only 7 days remaining on Tesla's own stated deadline.

- **Jeff forecasts July inflection.** Jeff (supply chain executive and guest on Herbert Ong's channel) believes Cybercab is the correct unit to track — not Model Y — and argues the hardware ramp is now on the right product. He predicts "meaningful movement" sometime in July 2026: "Somewhere in the next 2 to 6 weeks, they're really going to start scaling this."

- **Safety record is the standout positive.** Over one year in Austin: 18 total incidents, 8 attributed to other parties, and Tesla's last at-fault incident was in February 2026. Jeff: "It's been issue free since I think February, and even those issues were minor — that's probably the most important thing."

- **California regulatory signals — cautiously positive.** The California DMV updated Tesla's AV permit holder name to "Tesla Robotaxi LLC," and a new limousine permit was issued authorizing pickups and drop-offs at San Francisco International Airport. Loomis clarifies the SFO permit covers only the existing supervised ride-hail service and is not a new CPUC autonomous vehicle permit. Jeff estimates Tesla has "several hundred" cars accumulating miles in California and believes driverless authorization there may be "fairly imminent" given nine-plus months of testing data — adding there are no serious NHTSA-reportable safety incidents to explain the delay.

- **Houston hub expanding; new construction project registered.** On-the-ground observer Sladoch counted 55 Cybercabs at the Houston robotaxi hub, up from 45 two days prior and 31 the prior week (per investingagainstthegrain). Tesla registered a new $5.2 million renovation/alteration project with the Texas TDLR on June 16, 2026 — a 38,900 sq ft building south of central Houston, with a start date of October 19, 2026 and completion date of April 15, 2027. Purpose is unconfirmed.

- **Tesla hiring reveals global platform ambitions.** Analysis of Tesla's robotaxi job postings (cernbasher) shows ten distinct hiring categories: 24/7 fleet operations staff, KPI-driven operations managers, third-party logistics coordinators, field-based incident response specialists (with physical wheel-swap duties), a global commercial expansion strategy lead based in Palo Alto, a multi-sector business development role spanning mobility/logistics/retail/tech, a mobile ecosystem integration mandate (speculation includes X app and WeChat), and a senior real estate manager for site selection and lease negotiation. A fleet support specialist posting is located in Tampa, Florida — the first signal of potential expansion beyond Austin and the Bay Area. Roughly four ~31,000 sq ft robotaxi support locations have already been identified in Texas.

- **Competitive context.** Per the OTM AI benchmarking platform (cited by FutureAzA), Tesla ranks fifth among robotaxi operators globally behind Baidu Apollo Go, Waymo, Pony AI, and WeRide. Texas AV registrations: Tesla had 69 as of the video date (up 64% from 42 in May); Waymo had 620 (up ~7.5% from 577 in May). Mobileye announced a planned U.S. robotaxi launch in 2027 targeting 17,000 vehicles over five years. Uber is bringing a premium robotaxi to Houston in 2027 using a Lucid Gravity vehicle and Nuro software.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Spain milestone: 171,000+ supervised miles, zero serious incidents.** Spain's National Road Authority data shows Tesla's 30-car FSD fleet has driven over 171,000 miles of public road testing since November 2025 without a single serious incident — dwarfing the next largest entry on Spain's registry (Alsa bus group at ~900 miles). The only other Level 2 passenger car tester in Spain is Wave with three vehicles.

- **Texas crash — Autopilot not involved.** A Tesla crashed into a Texas home at high speed, initially blamed on Autopilot in media coverage. Elon Musk stated "FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets and this was a high-speed crash." Community analysis (per "A Shouk," cited by Loomis) showed the driver manually pressed the accelerator to 100% of pedal travel, reaching 73 mph in a residential area, with the accelerator still pressed post-crash. Sawyer Merritt and Loomis both pushed back on the media framing.

- **Tesla Semi spotted with LiDAR ground-truthing equipment.** A Tesla Semi equipped with LiDAR-based ground-truth validation gear was spotted in Sunnyvale, California (per community member Danny, cited by Electrified; separately noted as unspecified location by Herbert Ong's guest). Jeff characterizes this as significant but under-discussed. Loomis does not expect unsupervised FSD for the Semi to be in serious consideration until 2028 at earliest.

- **Grok voice integration in Tesla vehicles arriving ~September 2026.** Elon Musk stated the ability to converse with Grok inside a Tesla vehicle — for commands like directing drop-off locations or parking — will arrive in approximately 3 months, implying roughly September 2026.

- **Lemonade FSD insurance expands to Colorado — now 4 states.** Lemonade's autonomous car insurance (offering a 50% discount on FSD miles) is now live in Colorado, following Arizona (January 2026), Oregon (February 2026), and Indiana (earlier in June 2026). Jeff notes his own Tesla Insurance saves approximately $100 per vehicle per month versus comparable coverage.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Megapod trademark filed — modular AI compute at Supercharger locations.** Tesla filed an intent-to-use trademark for "Megapod," described as modular data center hardware systems for AI computing comprising servers, AI processing hardware, networking hardware, power distribution units, and cooling systems — sold as a unit with downloadable monitoring software. Herbert Ong and Loomis both connect this to Elon Musk's March 2026 statement that Tesla will deploy "millions of dedicated digital Optimus units in the field at Supercharger locations," where Tesla has approximately 7 gigawatts of available power. Loomis describes the form factor as analogous to a Megapack and flags the "sold as a unit" language as raising the possibility of eventual third-party sales — unconfirmed. The filing is intent-to-use; no units are deployed.

- **Digital Optimus / Macrohard — capability confirmed, deployment timeline uncertain.** Elon Musk has described "Macrohard" (also "digital Optimus") as a joint xAI-Tesla project in which Grok acts as the reasoning layer and digital Optimus acts as the instinctive execution layer, processing the past 5 seconds of real-time screen video and performing keyboard/mouse actions. It is operational on all AI4-equipped cars. Loomis says material deployment may not arrive until late 2027 or 2028, given Tesla's current robotaxi focus.

- **Optimus factory construction underway at Giga Texas.** Joe Tegtmeyer images show the Optimus factory under active construction at Giga Texas — the facility planned for an eventual 10 million unit per year capacity. Loomis estimates completion toward end of 2027.

- **Boston Dynamics fully consolidated under Hyundai.** Hyundai acquired SoftBank's remaining ~9.65–10% stake in Boston Dynamics for $325 million, giving Hyundai full ownership and implying a company valuation of just over $3 billion. FutureAzA frames the low valuation as evidence Boston Dynamics lacks the compute infrastructure and manufacturing scale to compete with Optimus.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab production signals accelerating.** Joe Tegtmeyer documented 80+ Cybercabs across Giga Texas — in the outbound lot, near the factory exit, and running what appeared to be test pickup/drop-off runs on-site. Approximately 10 Cybercabs were also visible on the new Giga Texas test track. A new batch of Cybercabs was observed with "Cybercab" door and hatch decals rather than "Robo Taxi" decals — Herbert Ong's guest Jeff characterizes the production of differently-labeled variants as a genuine manufacturing milestone, noting that switching between configurations in a high-volume factory is non-trivial and indicates preparation for multi-city, multi-regulatory deployment.

- **Model YL reportedly starting Texas production August–September 2026.** Per FutureAzA host Brian's source, the Tesla Model Y Long — a larger third-row variant — will begin production at Giga Texas in August or September 2026. Brian estimates a price of $549.90, roughly $4,000 above the standard Model Y, consistent with the China pricing differential.

- **UK Model Y AWD/Performance deliveries resuming after type-approval delay.** Tesla introduced mid-cycle hardware upgrades to Giga Berlin-built all-wheel-drive and performance Model Y variants — more energy-dense battery cells and rear motor improvements — which caused those vehicles to fall out of compliance with existing UK type approval certifications. Vehicles are physically in the UK but could not be registered. As of June 22, Tesla Owners UK reported self-scheduling delivery links being sent to affected customers within 24–48 hours. Standard (RWD) Model Y and all Model 3 variants are unaffected. Goodwill gestures including free Supercharging and free premium connectivity have been offered to some affected customers.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX acquires Cursor for $60 billion.** SpaceX completed the acquisition of Cursor, an AI coding platform used by 64% of Fortune 500 companies with over 1 million daily active users. Elon Musk stated the acquisition is "an important piece of the puzzle to make Grok much better." Cursor is training a new ~1.5-trillion-parameter frontier model on 100,000 GPUs, described as being "deep into training" with a release expected "in the next couple of weeks" from the announcement date.

- **SpaceX compute leasing revenue run rate ~$28 billion annually.** SpaceX signed a new compute agreement with Reflection AI ($150 million/month starting July 1, 2026, through 2029). Combined with existing deals with Anthropic (~$1.2 billion/month) and Google (just under $1 billion/month), Loomis calculates an annualized run rate of approximately $28 billion from three deals alone, with Cursor adding further. ARK Invest data cited by Loomis suggests SpaceX's build cost for compute is approximately $26 billion per gigawatt while the Google deal implies over $53 billion per gigawatt in revenue — implying over 100% return.

- **SpaceX files new notes offering; $100.8 billion cash on hand.** SpaceX's June 19, 2026 8-K discloses approximately $100.8 billion in cash and equivalents. The new notes offering will fully repay its outstanding bridge loan facility. Fitch assigned SpaceX an investment-grade credit rating, making all three major agencies now rating it investment-grade.

- **Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation intensifying.** Multiple commentators (investingagainstthegrain, Jo Bhakdi) argue the merger is likely within 2026. Bhakdi proposes SpaceX must be the surviving legal entity given Musk's supervoting shares, and notes SpaceX has 36 billion authorized shares versus 13 billion outstanding — a ratio he argues is deliberately pre-positioned for a large acquisition. He models a 50% premium offer at $600/share ($1.88 trillion Tesla market cap) and advocates for a "ratchet" mechanism giving Tesla shareholders the greater of the fixed offer or SpaceX's market cap at vote date. Bhakdi expects SpaceX share price to retreat toward or below IPO price ($161) around August 2026 as the float expands from ~5% to ~20%. These remain analyst/commentator views, not confirmed by Tesla or SpaceX.
