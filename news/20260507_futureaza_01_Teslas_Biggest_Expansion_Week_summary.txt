This FutureAzA video, hosted by Brian with guest Herbert from Brighter, covers three major expansion announcements from Elon Musk's companies during what they call Tesla's biggest expansion week. The hosts discuss SpaceX's proposed semiconductor fabrication facility in Grimes County, Texas; a large Megapack installation in Australia; and the Boring Company's first residential tunnel stop in Nashville — examining whether each announcement is real, significant, and strategically sound.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Unsupervised FSD enabling point-to-point Boring Company travel:** The hosts note that once Unsupervised FSD is operational, passengers could work the entire commute from Austin to Grimes County without touching the wheel. This is framed as a practical illustration of how autonomy adds value to longer inter-city trips, not just urban rides.

## Robotaxi
1. **Boring Company Nashville residential stop as robotaxi integration showcase:** The Boring Company announced its first residential tunnel stop, connecting three apartment buildings in Nashville to the downtown/airport trunk line. Residents would take an elevator to a lower parking level, board autonomous Model Ys, and be transported to the airport in approximately 9 minutes — compared to 14 minutes at off-peak hours and 35–40 minutes during rush hour by surface roads. The hosts argue this residential stop sets a precedent that could trigger a chain reaction of private-entity approvals (apartments, malls, city halls), similar to how Las Vegas casinos demanded inclusion once the system proved itself.

2. **Boring Company operations timeline and cost model:** The hosts expect the first Nashville tunnel stops to begin operation by end of 2026 or early 2027. The cost model discussed mirrors Las Vegas: the Boring Company pays for the main trunk, while spur connections are paid by the connecting entity — though for the residential buildings, the exact cost-sharing arrangement was described as uncertain. The hosts highlight that using existing underground parking garages as pickup/drop-off zones dramatically reduces station construction costs.

3. **Autonomous daisy-chaining as a subway substitute:** Herbert describes how autonomous robotaxi vehicles in tunnels could daisy-chain together like train cars, delivering riders point-to-point before rejoining the main corridor — offering the efficiency of a subway without fixed stops, at a fraction of the cost. Subway construction is noted as costing approximately $1 billion per mile, making city-wide coverage financially impossible with traditional infrastructure.

## Energy
1. **Large Megapack installation in Australia:** Tesla is delivering a 200 MW / 800 MWh battery installation called the Kulonga battery in Australia, valued at approximately $225 million worth of Megapacks. Brian notes this is not a new Gigafactory in Australia (a misreading he initially made), but a large grid-scale energy storage project. The hosts emphasize that Australia has repeatedly proven the Megapack business model works, with payback periods of roughly one to two years, and call for more announcements at this scale from other countries.

2. **Megapack 3 and Megablock production timeline:** Brian expects Megapack 3 units out of the new Gigafactory in Texas to ship no later than Q3 2026, with Elon's stated timeline of "this year" characterized as sandbagging. The ramp-up could be slower due to uncertainty around cell supply from LG's new factory in Lansing, Michigan. Herbert confirms he toured the Megapack factory and observed details under NDA.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX semiconductor fab in Grimes County, Texas — scale and strategic rationale:** SpaceX filed for a proposed next-generation, vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing and advanced computing fabrication facility in Grimes County, Texas, approximately two hours from Austin. The proposed capital investment is $55 billion upfront, with a potential ceiling of $119 billion across multiple phases, with a county hearing scheduled within one month. Elon Musk confirmed the announcement is real and noted several locations are under consideration, describing it as the largest and most advanced chip facility of its kind.

2. **Location analysis — proximity to Giga Texas as key criterion:** The hosts argue Grimes County's location between Houston and Austin is strategically sound. Elon Musk previously asked Samsung to build its fab as close as possible to Giga Texas, and the same logic applies here. Giga Texas is cited as the future home of a 10 million Optimus units per year production line and Cybercab manufacturing — making chip supply chain proximity critical. The hosts also cite talent access (proximity to Austin and Houston metro areas), reasonable Texas power rates, and county approval likelihood as key factors.

3. **Domestic chip manufacturing precedent:** The hosts push back on skepticism about building semiconductor fabs in the US, noting that Samsung and TSMC are already doing it, and that Tesla pioneered domestic lithium processing — something others said was impossible. The SpaceX fab is framed as part of a broader reshoring trend accelerated by tariff pressures.
