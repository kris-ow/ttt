Tesla's @robotaxi account posted photos of what it says is the first Cybercab built with a physical Starlink antenna integrated into the roof, moving beyond the concept rendering Tesla and Starlink teased in July.

## Robotaxi
1. **First hardware sighting:** Tesla shared two images of a gold Cybercab at Gigafactory Texas showing a low-profile roof-mounted Starlink antenna alongside the Cybercab badge; the caption only stated "First Cybercab with @Starlink integration" with no specs or timeline given.
2. **Production status:** The Cybercab, unveiled in October 2024, was originally positioned as a $30,000 two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals; production began at Giga Texas earlier this year, and the article notes over 100 units had been spotted in factory lots by July, though Tesla still cannot sell or operate them without a human safety monitor.
3. **Unclear utility:** The article questions the antenna's purpose, noting the Cybercab drives using Tesla's onboard AI4 computer without needing internet connectivity, and that robotaxi service currently runs only in Austin, Houston, Dallas, and Miami — dense markets with strong existing cellular coverage. Tesla is reportedly framing the antenna as an added option for tracking robotaxis in remote areas.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **July integration announcement:** Tesla and Starlink previously announced in July that satellite internet was "directly integrated" into the Cybercab, at that time illustrated only with a cutaway rendering rather than physical hardware.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the Starlink integration is currently not particularly useful for Tesla, calling it primarily another way for Musk to have Tesla pay SpaceX; he notes the antenna is redundant in markets with existing cellular coverage and adds cost, weight, and recurring fees to a vehicle targeted at a low price point, while a Starlink terminal in every Cybercab would provide SpaceX recurring revenue. He frames this within a broader pattern of Musk moving Tesla resources toward his other companies (citing the xAI investment and ongoing SpaceX-Tesla merger talk), and says any real rural/intercity autonomy use case is likely years away.
