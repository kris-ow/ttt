Electrek reports that Tesla's 2025 delivery figures showed a second consecutive annual sales decline, and criticizes Elon Musk for claiming on Twitter that Tesla's production is "~2M cars/year and rising" — a claim the article says is factually wrong on both counts.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla's 2025 deliveries fall, ceding global EV lead:** Tesla delivered 1.63 million vehicles in 2025, placing it second globally in EV deliveries behind China's BYD. The article states this marks the first time in company history that sales have declined for two consecutive years, following a prior-year decline as well; the drop was reportedly worse than Tesla's own lowered expectations and even steeper in some markets, including Europe, despite a rising global EV market.
2. **New/refreshed models failed to reverse the trend:** Despite refreshes like the Model 3 "Highland" and Model Y "Juniper," the article says the Model Y did not reclaim its position as the world's best-selling car in 2025. The Cybertruck, which began deliveries in late 2023 and reportedly had over a million reservations, is described as a commercial flop with low current sales.
3. **Musk's production claim disputed:** Responding to NVIDIA's CES AI announcements, Musk tweeted that Tesla is making "~2M cars/year and rising." The article states Tesla has never produced 2 million cars in a year, peaking at 1.85 million in 2023, and that 2025 production was 1.65 million — down, not rising, for two straight years.

## Financials
1. **Q3 pull-forward and lobbying cost cited:** The article notes Tesla had a record Q3 driven by buyers rushing to purchase EVs before a $7,500 incentive was eliminated under policies the article attributes to Republican legislation Musk supported, but expects a subsequent sales trough. It also states Tesla disclosed a $1.4 billion loss in Q3 tied to the effects of Musk's political/lobbying activity (as previously reported by the company, per the article).

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Musk is personally responsible for Tesla's sales decline, citing his political alignment with policies harming EV incentives, his controversial government advisory stint, and widely criticized public statements (including on Nazi Germany), which the author says have driven away customers and protests. The author frames Musk's "rising" claim as either genuine ignorance of his own company's performance or psychological denial of his responsibility as CEO, and notes Musk said in a December interview that he regretted his time in government and should have focused on his companies instead — a moment of apparent self-awareness the author says did not last.
