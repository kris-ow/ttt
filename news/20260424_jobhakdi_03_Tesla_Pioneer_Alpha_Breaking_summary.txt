## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Deployment Sequencing — Five-Stage Ladder:** Jo outlines a five-stage deployment ladder for humanoid robots. Stage one is household sales to early adopters who will pay for novelty and utility before cost-effectiveness is proven; he believes millions of units could be sold into homes in this phase. Stage two is industrial manufacturing with repetitive tasks. Stage three is mining and agricultural/forestry work. Stage four includes electricians, plumbers, painters, and nursing. Stage five encompasses highly complex tasks such as surgery, which he expects may take until approximately 2035 or beyond.

2. **Optimus Competitive Position — Tesla Likely Ahead Despite Silence:** Jo argues that Tesla's silence on Optimus capabilities reflects a deliberate strategy to prevent copying, not a lack of progress. He contends that given Tesla's capital, talent, and pressure on the project, Tesla is very likely at minimum in the top three humanoid robot programs globally and possibly significantly ahead. He draws a distinction from FSD: Optimus will face real competition, whereas FSD does not.

3. **Fleet Learning Advantage — Knowledge Replication Across All Units:** Jo emphasizes the compounding advantage of a large Optimus fleet where any skill learned by one robot can be replicated across the entire fleet. He frames this as a civilization-scale advantage and notes it is extremely difficult for people to develop intuition for how powerful this becomes at scale.

4. **Optimus Gen 3 Pre-Order Deals Anticipated:** In response to a question, Jo states he believes large companies will enter pilot deals with Tesla for Optimus before version 3 is formally revealed, provided it is "decently good." He sees these early pilot agreements with major corporations as an important near-term catalyst.

5. **Fast Food as an Early High-Value Deployment Vertical:** Jo highlights fast food — specifically citing McDonald's — as a Goldilocks deployment scenario for Optimus: labor cost savings, low worker engagement rates to replace, and a customer attraction effect. He believes companies could profit from Optimus deployment in fast food even before broader economic efficiency is fully established.
