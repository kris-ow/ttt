This video recaps Tesla's third-quarter earnings call commentary on robotaxi expansion, featuring a discussion between the host and guest analyst Brian (of the channel "FutureAzA") on Cybercab fleet growth in Austin, plans to bring customer-owned vehicles into the robotaxi network via FSD v15, and new city launches. The pair also cover FSD subscription uptake, Optimus plans, Tesla's in-house mapping ambitions, and monopoly/competition arguments around Tesla's cost advantage.

## Robotaxi
1. **Austin Cybercab fleet up tenfold in a month:** Elon Musk said the number of autonomous Cybercabs in Austin has grown roughly 10x since operations began about a month prior, with total autonomous driving distance up eightfold during the quarter and showing double-digit weekly growth; Musk described the weekly mileage graph as looking "like a rocket entering orbit."
2. **Dallas launch next month, more cities by year-end:** Tesla plans to launch Cybercab service in Dallas the following month, with expansion to additional cities by the end of the year; Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy said the goal is 24/7 service and highway operation by then. Per a previously released Tesla safety report cited by the host, candidate next cities after Austin/Dallas include Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio.
3. **Customer vehicles to join robotaxi network around Q2 next year:** Ashok Elluswamy said customer-owned vehicles will get the same FSD v15 software as the Cybercab, and once included in Tesla's geofencing and vehicle management system, could begin robotaxi operation around the second quarter of next year — letting owners earn robotaxi income while their car is otherwise idle (e.g., at work). The host and Brian noted this timeline is faster than they expected (they had guessed end of next year or later) but flagged that similar promises have missed deadlines before.
4. **Revenue ramp expected starting Q2 next year:** Elon Musk said he expects robotaxi revenue to start increasing noticeably around Q2 of next year and become a large contributor in Q3 and Q4.
5. **Zero safety incidents reported; cumulative mileage milestone:** Tesla reported zero injuries, zero airbag deployments, and zero pedestrian/cyclist collisions attributable to robotaxi operation, with a cumulative total the host cited as 3.1–3.3 million autonomous miles driven (the host used both figures at different points). Of this, about 2.1 million miles came from Austin and roughly 608,000 miles from Dallas, per the host — with Brian noting Dallas/Houston volumes are larger than he'd assumed.
6. **CFO's 30-cents-per-mile cost target clarified as operating cost, not price:** Brian clarified that CFO Vaibhav Taneja's stated goal of achieving 30 cents per mile refers to operating cost under favorable conditions (e.g., night driving, dedicated low-insurance cargo use), not a passenger fare, and said this could rise to around 33 cents if inflation remains strong.
7. **Limited Cybercab sales to third parties called a low-priority initiative:** Lars Moravy reiterated Tesla will provide Cybercabs to some customers by the end of 2026; Brian was skeptical of the business rationale given production and backend capacity constraints, suggesting it may be a small-scale test/demo (e.g., related to a well-known tech reviewer's wager) rather than a meaningful commercial push. Musk reportedly confirmed Cybercab is not sold as a customer-owned car in the traditional sense but is framed as a pure transportation-as-a-service vehicle.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscription uptake surges:** Tesla reported that 75% of new vehicles delivered in North America in Q3 signed up for paid FSD despite a one-month free trial, up from 55% in Q2. Over 1.7 million people worldwide are paid FSD customers, with about 50% on the subscription model and the rest having purchased outright.
2. **Tesla developing its own in-house mapping system:** Per the discussion, Tesla is building a new AI-based mapping architecture (moving away from reliance on Google Maps) that learns individual driver route preferences and could eventually enable location-based transaction/advertising revenue; Brian argued this is a long-term vertical-integration advantage.
3. **EU-wide FSD approval timeline:** FSD is currently operating in nine EU countries; Elon Musk said he would be surprised if EU-wide approval took longer than the first quarter of 2027, with a relevant vote reportedly expected around December 6.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus demo video planned within 1-2 months:** Elon Musk said a video is scheduled for release within one to two months showing Optimus performing tasks that were not pre-programmed, with external companies expected to be able to adopt Optimus starting around the end of next year.

## Financials
1. **FSD revenue implication:** The host suggested the 75% North American attach rate could translate to roughly $1 billion-plus in additional annual revenue, though Brian cautioned he doesn't know FSD's development costs and the program may still be running at a loss once hardware and compute investment are factored in.

## Market & Competition
1. **Monopoly framing and cost-advantage argument:** Brian argued Tesla's scale and low per-vehicle cost structure make it hard for competitors to catch up within a decade, comparing Tesla's potential market position to Google's historical search share and GM's past US market share — arguing a de facto dominant position isn't illegal unless the dominance is abused.
