This episode of Electrified (published as a hastily reassembled recap after the host lost a prior edit) covers weekend Tesla/SpaceX news: newly spotted Robotaxi hub locations across Texas and Tennessee, Elon Musk's expansive commentary on Starlink's revenue potential, a major new Tesla solar manufacturing filing in Fort Bend County, Texas, SpaceX's power strategy for the Terafab project, and firsthand accounts of Robotaxi pickup/drop-off issues in Austin.

## Robotaxi
1. **New hidden Robotaxi hubs and fleet growth:** Spotters found a new Robotaxi hub in Fort Worth, Texas (roughly 40 minutes from Dallas) with dozens of Cybercabs and Model Ys staged as Robotaxis; separately, sources reported about 105 Cybercabs in Dallas and roughly 15+ Cybercabs/Robotaxi-ready Model Ys in Nashville, Tennessee. Based on VIN tracking, the host estimates Tesla now has over 2,000 Cybercabs (mix of production variants and pre-production "Farzod" units) staged at dozens of locations nationwide, which could be activated for expansion in the future; this is the host's estimate, not from RobotaxiTracker.com.
2. **San Antonio flagged as possible next launch city:** A commentator (David Moss) noted six badged Model Ys spotted in San Antonio, and said this badging is unique compared to other pre-launch cities like Phoenix and Las Vegas, speculating — without certainty — that San Antonio could be the next Robotaxi city to launch.
3. **Cybercab spotted with native Starlink integration:** An image circulated showing a Cybercab with built-in Starlink connectivity, which the host ties to Musk's broader comments about eventually equipping all vehicles with Starlink for high-bandwidth needs.
4. **Austin operating hours and pickup/drop-off issues:** The Tesla Robotaxi app in Austin now lists operating hours of 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. A rider (Chuck Cook) described his first unsupervised ride: a roughly 4-minute pickup wait that stretched to about 10 minutes because the vehicle couldn't find a stopping spot near a hotel, and a drop-off at the same hotel that took nearly 25 minutes after three failed attempts, ultimately dropping him two and a half blocks away at a different location. He concluded terminal (pickup/drop-off) maneuvers remain unrefined.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Musk projects massive Starlink revenue potential:** Responding to a claim that Starlink alone could be a trillion-dollar company, Musk said "far more than that," citing surging AI/robotics bandwidth demand. He said even if the communications market merely doubles, he'd expect Starlink to reach at least 25% market share outside China (and potentially inside China too), which he said would be over half a trillion dollars in annual revenue; if Starlink carries more than half of long-term internet traffic, he said that would probably exceed a trillion dollars a year, adding "there's no obvious impediment to that outcome so far."
2. **Clarification on SpaceX's trillion-dollar revenue projection:** Musk clarified that his earlier remark about a trillion dollars in SpaceX revenue by around 2029–2030 referred to revenue generated in the year 2030 itself, not cumulative revenue — describing this as an internal projection while acknowledging "it's always possible we flounder along the way or are delusional."

## Energy
1. **Tesla files for $10.1 billion solar manufacturing site in Fort Bend County, Texas:** Tesla filed for a fully vertically integrated solar cell/module manufacturing facility ("Project Crystal Sun") near Houston, about 2 hours 15 minutes from Giga Texas. Planned spend: roughly $2.3B in the current year, $3.3B in 2027, and about $4.5B in 2028, with construction starting this year, completion expected 2028, and commercial operations expected 2029. The filing states Tesla is evaluating multiple US states, though Texas is seen as the likely choice. Projected impact includes over 9,700 permanent jobs, about $4 billion in direct state/local tax revenue, $2.3 billion in indirect state/local tax impact, and a claimed $107 billion boost to Texas GDP. The host notes this spending is already part of Tesla's previously stated capex plans and cautions that large near-term capital outlay with no near-term production is unlikely to help the stock in the short run, even though it's positive for long-term investors.
2. **Uncertain status of prior Brookshire, Texas solar plans:** The host notes no update in months on previously reported solar production plans co-located with Tesla's Megapack factory in Brookshire (about 30 minutes from the new Fort Bend site), and speculates — without confirmation — that Tesla may deprioritize that smaller site in favor of the new large Fort Bend facility, or use it as a smaller precursor.
3. **SpaceX's separate solar plans for Terafab:** SpaceX reportedly has its own plans for about 100 gigawatts of solar cell production annually at Bastrop, intended for AI-related orbital compute, distinct from Tesla's Earth-focused utility/commercial/distributed solar filing.
4. **SpaceX's Terafab power strategy avoids the Texas grid:** A SpaceX leader said the company will bring its own power for the Terafab project via large battery arrays and primarily new natural gas power plants, avoiding reliance on the Texas (ERCOT) grid; solar was notably not mentioned as part of this plan. This follows Musk's earlier acquisition of APR Energy (a natural gas power plant company) and SpaceX's previously reported plan to buy $2.8 billion worth of gas turbines over three years.
5. **Speculation on free-electron-laser EUV chipmaking for Terafab:** Based on a social media exchange, Musk replied "FEL FTW" (free electron laser for the win) to a post suggesting a particle accelerator is being built at the Terafab site, hinting at ambitions to build a free-electron-laser-based EUV lithography system. The host discusses this as an emerging, unproven technology (citing Silicon Valley startup Xlite, which raised $350 million and received a $150 million CHIPS Act award, with a first prototype targeted for 2028) but stresses this is speculative and states he does not yet have deep expertise to assess it.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Supervised training scale in Europe:** Tesla said FSD Supervised has been trained and tested on about 2.2 million kilometers (roughly 1.3 million miles) across 19 EU countries, covering edge cases described as rare in a typical lifetime.
