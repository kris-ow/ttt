## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production wind-down confirmed:** Per Elon Musk's X post (cited by the creator), Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas around the middle of next year, with the stated purpose of freeing capacity for increased Cybercab production; the creator states deliveries would be completed by the end of 2027, though it's unclear whether that date came from Musk's post or is the creator's own projection.
2. **Creator's prior prediction and reasoning:** The creator says they told paid subscribers on October 4th that the Cybertruck's end was "inevitable," arguing that once a production line's sales fall below the volume needed to fully load even one assembly shift, the line "loses its meaning" — the same reasoning they say they previously applied, correctly, to predict the end of Model S and X production late last year/earlier this year based on falling Q4/Q1 sales figures.
3. **Cybertruck as commercial failure, engineering stepping-stone:** The creator characterizes the Cybertruck as a commercial failure but not an engineering failure, saying lessons from it (structural battery pack, front/rear castings, seats mounted to the battery before installation) feed into Tesla's broader "unboxed" manufacturing process. They note the vehicle lacked a fully structural exoskeleton — body panels are held by clips rather than structural fasteners — which the creator says kept it roughly 800 pounds heavier than it could have been, limiting range/battery capacity improvements and preventing deeper cost reduction.
4. **No Cyber SUV replacement; Robotaxi van speculation:** The creator says Musk indicated the freed Giga Texas capacity is for more Cybertrucks, not a Cyber SUV, but expresses skepticism that Cybertruck demand could ever fill that capacity. They speculate the real purpose may be production of Tesla's Robotaxi van, noting it would share few components with the Cybertruck (different suspension, no shared castings) but that Cybertruck served as a testbed for 48-volt architecture and other systems applicable to future "unboxed" vehicles.
5. **Mentions pickup truck market context:** A third-party quote (Matt Smith of Rebellionaire, from late July) calls the Cybertruck a likely "commercial failure" given sales below expectations and costs that never came down, describing it as a specialized niche product rather than a mass-market vehicle. The creator also references the Hyundai Santa Cruz as a well-regarded but low-volume truck, suggesting a compact Tesla pickup could fill unmet demand, possibly arriving next month (unconfirmed, creator's expectation).
