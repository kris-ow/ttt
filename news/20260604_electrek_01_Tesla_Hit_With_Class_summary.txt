A Quebec Tesla owner has filed a class action request against Tesla in Quebec's Superior Court, alleging the company's heat pump system constitutes a hidden defect under provincial consumer law. The suit could be worth up to $400 million and covers all Quebec owners of heat pump-equipped Tesla models.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Plaintiff's case and repair history:** Amélie Paquette purchased a 2021 Model 3 in December 2020 for $52,880. Her vehicle suffered three separate heat pump-related failures under warranty — sensor replacements, debris in the fan, and a full compressor/manifold replacement — before a smoke-accompanied failure on January 27, 2026, at 158,220 km rendered the full heat pump inoperable. Out-of-warranty, Tesla charged her $4,476.55. Tesla rejected both an in-person request for shared responsibility and a formal demand letter (refused by email March 17, 2026).

2. **Scope of the class action:** Filed by Perrier Avocats before Quebec's Superior Court, the suit targets all Quebec owners or lessees of heat pump-equipped Teslas: Model S (2021+), Model 3 (2021+), Model X (2021+), Model Y (2020+), and Cybertruck (2023+). It seeks repair cost reimbursement, purchase/lease price reductions, and punitive damages. The lawsuit argues Tesla, as a professional seller, is presumed to have known about the defect and failed to disclose it. It asserts vehicles of Tesla's price should last at least 10 years or 200,000 km without major failure.

3. **Regulatory and legal background:** NHTSA issued a recall covering certain 2021–2022 Model 3, Model Y, Model S, and Model X vehicles after confirming the heat pump problem compromised windshield defrosting, a federal safety violation. Transport Canada launched its own investigation after receiving over 170 complaints from Canadian owners, predominantly from Quebec. Tesla has previously faced authorized class actions in Quebec over paint defects and Premium Connectivity changes, and faces up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits across multiple jurisdictions. Out-of-warranty heat pump repairs in Canada typically cost $3,800–$4,700.

## Editorial Stance
The author calls the lawsuit "inevitable," citing Tesla's persistent heat pump reliability issues since 2021 and a recurring pattern of warranty-covered failures followed by expensive post-warranty bills. The author considers the suit's 10-year/200,000 km durability standard "entirely reasonable" and notes Quebec's consumer protection laws are among the strongest in North America.
