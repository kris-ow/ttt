Citing Tesla's own crash reports to NHTSA's Standing General Order database, Electrek reports that Tesla logged 207 Autopilot/FSD-involved crashes in May 2026, the highest single-month total on record, while noting that Tesla continues to redact nearly all narrative detail from these reports.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Record monthly crash count:** Tesla reported 207 driver-assist crashes in May 2026 per NHTSA SGO data, more than all of 2021 combined (157 crashes). Across the full dataset since 2019, Tesla accounts for 3,763 unique driver-assist crashes, roughly 85% of all industry ADAS reports.
2. **Accelerating year-over-year trend:** 2025 was the first year Tesla crossed 1,000 driver-assist crashes annually (1,043). Through six months of 2026, Tesla has logged 826 crashes, on pace to roughly double 2025's total. Comparing January-through-June periods each year, counts ran 180, 261, 269, 476, and now 826 — a 73% year-over-year jump.
3. **No exposure/mileage data available:** The article notes raw crash counts naturally rise with more miles driven and larger fleets, but Tesla does not publish independently auditable Autopilot/FSD miles-driven data, making it impossible to determine whether the per-mile crash rate is rising or falling; Tesla's own AI trainers have reportedly criticized its quarterly safety report as misleading.
4. **Near-total redaction of crash narratives:** Tesla has redacted narrative detail on 99.9% of its 3,763 crash reports since 2019, citing confidential business information; most severity fields are logged as "Unknown" and software-version fields are redacted, preventing separation of Autopilot from FSD crashes. By contrast, GM, Ford, Honda, and Toyota redact essentially none of their narratives in the same dataset. Tesla has reportedly argued to regulators it would "suffer financial harm" if the data became public, and is under a separate NHTSA probe over its crash reporting practices.
5. **Reporting lag means undercounting:** Due to reporting lag, recent months are undercounted rather than overcounted — the prior "final" May 2026 figure was revised upward by 139 crashes with this latest export, meaning the 207 figure is described as a floor, not a ceiling.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert states FSD (he cites testing v14) is genuinely improving and calls it the most capable Level 2 system available, but argues the improving technology breeds driver complacency (citing observed phone use and gaming of driver monitoring), which he believes is driving the crash surge. He argues Tesla's failure to aggressively scale robotaxi service or ship unsupervised FSD to consumer cars shows the company itself does not believe the technology is ready for unsupervised use, and criticizes Tesla as the only automaker hiding nearly all crash detail while its marketing outpaces the technology's actual readiness.
