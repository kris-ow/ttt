Herbert (brighterwithherbert) and economist-investor Jo Bhakdi (Pioneer Lands) review a week of possible catalysts: Financial Times reporting on the Tesla–SpaceX merger committees, Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia, the FSD v15 rollout, California's proposed robotaxi rule changes, and Tesla's European rebound. Bhakdi's view is that these strengthen both underlying businesses. He argues that only a well-priced merger, Cybercab deployment in the thousands, or SpaceX's Q4 earnings are likely to move the stocks in an indifferent, macro-driven market.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger moves from rumor to process:** The Financial Times reports that both companies have formed special committees to negotiate a deal valued at about $3.5 trillion.
   - SpaceX's committee members are Roelof Botha and Randy Glein. Tesla's are Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung.
   - Named advisors include Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and Evercore.
   - An announcement could come as early as December, with a target of closing by the end of 2026.
   - An insider says the parties are not yet close to a deal, though intensive work keeps a year-end close possible.
   - Tesla approval requires more than 50% of all outstanding shares.
2. **What premium will Tesla holders get:** Cathie Wood expects about a 25% premium for Tesla shareholders, while the FT points to closer to 10%. A community member calculated that a $3.5T combined value is about $170B above the companies' combined market caps of roughly $3.3T, which would be only about a 12% premium for Tesla. Bhakdi calls that conclusion too speculative and sets out bounds instead:
   - **Upper limit:** A premium much above 50% risks lawsuits from SpaceX minority shareholders.
   - **Lower limit:** Winning more than 50% of all outstanding Tesla shares is a "tall request." Abstentions effectively count as no votes, and retail holders must turn out.
   - **His estimate:** He has always seen at least 20% and believes it should exceed 30%, depending on Tesla's price at announcement. If Tesla rallies toward $480–$600, 20% may suffice. At current levels the offer probably needs to imply something above $500 per share.
   - He warns: "If a Tesla costs $380 and you offer 20%, you will fail."
3. **Vote timing and outcome:** Bhakdi notes the merger vote would not take place at the December 3 annual meeting and would need a separate special meeting, possibly around February. He firmly expects the deal to be structured to win a yes vote, because a failed vote would be "extremely shameful, extremely unprofessional."
4. **SpaceX weighed down by the merger:** Bhakdi considers SpaceX the better investment at present. He notes that on Tuesday (November 24) Tesla fell about 1% while SpaceX fell about 5%. He attributes SpaceX's weakness to the merger, arguing the expected Tesla premium is effectively being taken out of SpaceX's valuation.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Q4 earnings as SpaceX's key catalyst:** Bhakdi expects SpaceX's Q4 report in January to be a huge catalyst. He argues it needs no surprise, only confirmation that the guidance is being met.
   - **His figures:** SpaceX reaching about 3 GW of compute and a revenue run rate of about $150B around December. He also projects a path to $500–600B by the end of 2027.
   - **Valuation argument:** He says a roughly $1.8T company on track for about $500B of revenue at around 60% margins is mispriced.
   - **Market doubts:** He believes the market doubts that AI compute demand and token prices will hold, which he calls "extremely stupid" given fast-rising demand and stagnant supply.
   - Herbert adds that Bhakdi has posted that SpaceX will outperform in Q1 2027.
2. **Cathie Wood's robotaxi-funding thesis dismissed:** Wood argues Tesla robotaxi cash flows could help fund SpaceX's orbital data centers. Bhakdi calls this "a little ridiculous."
   - By his estimate, a million Cybercabs would generate about $50B of additional cash flow. That is roughly one to two gigawatts of compute against the roughly 10 GW SpaceX is building.
   - He adds that Tesla needs its own cash for Optimus and other investments.
3. **Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia:** On Monday, November 23, Nvidia announced Huang's return as CEO and board member, less than two weeks after his resignation.
   - Per the host, Nvidia shares jumped about 31% that day, adding almost a trillion dollars in value. Huang told employees, "I'll be a better CEO the second time around."
   - Musk welcomed the reversal, saying the error had been fixed.
   - **Bhakdi's view:** A zero-sum reading would say Huang's absence helps SpaceX, because neoclouds would collapse and SpaceX could charge higher token prices. He rejects that: Tesla, SpaceX and Terafab all need Nvidia supply, and Huang is hard to replace.
   - He calls the return a "giant win" for both stocks because they correlate with a market that had been sliding. He cites the market's fears as an election result favoring Democrats, Huang's exit and recent cyberattacks.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v15 rollout:** FSD v15 began reaching owners over the weekend. Tesla cites 25 times better handling of critical safety cases, and Musk clarified the figure means 25 times, not 25%. Musk called v15 "the beginning of the super part" of Tesla's superintelligence.
   - Chris of Dirty Tesla has said v14 is at least twice as safe as a human, and others estimate two to seven times. Herbert speculates that this could put v15 far above human safety, while noting Tesla has not said so.
   - Early evidence is anecdotal: a two-hour Manhattan drive with no mistakes by one tester, and a clip of the car navigating around a large fallen tree.
   - Both men stress the real gains will be clear only after wider use.
2. **Approaching the deployment inflection:** Bhakdi says the system has been improving exponentially since a practical inflection point in April. He thinks Tesla is close to a deployment tipping point, moving from dozens of Cybercabs deployed at a time to hundreds. In his view, once safety reaches roughly 10–20x human levels, deployment in large numbers follows.
3. **Europe:** Italy approved FSD (Supervised), making it the 12th European country to do so. Bhakdi expects FSD to be widely deployed across Europe in 2027.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab volume forecasts:** Bhakdi admits his earlier call of 10,000–20,000 Cybercabs by the end of 2026 is probably wrong. He still sees 50,000+ in 2027 as on track.
   - On production, he notes that about 2,500 units were known roughly four months ago. He calls over 5,000 built by year-end "guaranteed" and suggests 10,000 is possible, while saying he does not know how many will be deployed.
   - He argues the production trajectory shows what Tesla really expects, because building cars costs money.
2. **California loosening robotaxi rules:** Tesla's California robotaxi service still uses a driver in the seat and runs no Cybercabs. Tesla has published a California addendum to its robotaxi plan covering Model Y and Cybercab, describing SAE Level 4 operation across a significant portion of the San Francisco Bay Area. Musk has said this would happen sometime next year.
   - The CPUC's proposed rules would simplify service-area expansions and allow all autonomous programs, including the driverless pilot, to serve airports.
   - They would also drop unnecessary equipment requirements for purpose-built autonomous vehicles where state or federal law does not require them.
   - The vote is set for December 3, the same day as Tesla's annual meeting.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **European rebound:** Tesla's October registrations in Europe rose 76% year over year, against 56% for the EV market and 3.4% for the overall car market, while many rivals declined.
   - Bhakdi sees 2027 as a turning point for legacy OEMs, with bankruptcies likely. He calls Tesla the clear Western winner, with Chinese makers second.
   - He also cites steady six-monthly improvements to Model Y and Model 3.
   - He calls this a strong cash-flow business rather than a stock driver for a roughly $1.5T company. He praises Tesla and SpaceX as among the best-managed, most capital-efficient companies in history.

## Market & Competition
1. **Macro dominates:** Bhakdi says the market currently ignores company fundamentals and swings on macro events. He believes only thousands of Cybercabs rolling out at once would move Tesla individually.
   - He warns that even huge success could be awarded a price-to-earnings ratio of about 40, noting that valuations are at their lowest relative to earnings growth in 30 years.
   - He flags black-swan risks, including cybersecurity and rapidly advancing AI, and advises investors to give macro factors "enormous weight."
