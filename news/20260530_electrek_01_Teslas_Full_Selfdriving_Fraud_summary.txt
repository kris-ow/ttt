A Beijing court held its first hearing in a consumer fraud lawsuit filed by 10 Tesla owners seeking 3.95 million yuan ($583,000) in damages over Tesla's "Full Self-Driving" software — marking China's first collective legal challenge over FSD promises.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Plaintiffs allege FSD fraud over hardware exclusions and unmet promises:** The 10 plaintiffs each paid 56,000 yuan (~$7,800) for Tesla's FSD package between 2019 and 2021, after Tesla sales staff and Elon Musk allegedly assured them full autonomy was imminent. When Tesla rolled out its driving assistance software in China, it only supported HW4.0 hardware, excluding owners with HW3.0 vehicles — which covers all cars produced between 2019 and 2023. Plaintiffs argue FSD lacks regulatory approval in China, cannot perform marketed functions, and that Tesla concealed hardware limitations. Tesla disputed the claims, saying some FSD functions are "fully operational" while others are "partially functional" or still in development.

2. **Tesla's recent rebranding complicates its legal defense:** Nine days before the hearing, Tesla confirmed FSD (Supervised) is now available in China. One week before the hearing, Tesla renamed the system "Tesla Assisted Driving" in the Chinese market — a move the article characterizes as a tacit admission the original branding was misleading. Since plaintiffs purchased under the original "Full Self-Driving" name, the renaming is unlikely to aid Tesla's defense.

## Financials
3. **Case could set a precedent with massive scale:** Under China's Consumer Rights Protection Law, plaintiffs are seeking full refunds plus triple damages — the standard fraud penalty. Tesla is estimated to have over 1 million HW3-equipped vehicles in China, and hundreds of additional owners are reportedly consulting lawyers. Globally, Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits related to Autopilot and FSD. In the US, a Texas owner recently won a $10,000 judgment against Tesla over FSD promises, which Tesla is still contesting. No ruling timeline was disclosed.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Tesla's premature sales of autonomous driving features represent a massive and growing financial liability across multiple jurisdictions, and that the Chinese rebranding to "Tesla Assisted Driving" is effectively an admission that plaintiffs' core argument is correct. He estimates that triple damages scaled across 1 million+ HW3 vehicles in China could represent billions in exposure.
