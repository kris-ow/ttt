This episode from The Tesla Space covers three distinct technology stories: The Boring Company's progress on its Proof Rock tunnel boring machine in Nashville and Las Vegas, new details about the Tesla Semi's ground-up engineering philosophy shared by the Semi program lead, and a meaningful update to Tesla's Actually Smart Summon feature in FSD version 14.3. The central thesis across all three stories is that each of these efforts represents a fundamental rethinking of existing industrial or automotive categories rather than incremental improvement on legacy designs.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi's ground-up design philosophy:** Tesla Semi program lead Dan Priestley shared details confirming that Tesla never treated the Semi as a traditional truck with a battery added in. Instead, Tesla discarded all design conventions inherited from diesel-era heavy trucking — including engine placement, cooling systems, frame structure, and cab positioning — and started from scratch. The overriding design question for every decision, according to Priestley, was: "Does this make the truck cheaper and better to operate?" The Semi was engineered to simultaneously satisfy three distinct customers: the driver, the fleet owner, and the shipper.

2. **Aerodynamics as the central design driver:** Priestley stated that roughly half of the energy required to move a heavy-duty truck down the highway is spent pushing through air. This insight shaped nearly every design decision on the Semi. A more aerodynamic profile reduces the battery capacity needed, which reduces weight, which reduces structural stress and improves efficiency further — creating a beneficial chain reaction throughout the vehicle. Tesla integrated the battery structure, frame rails, and drivetrain into a single unified platform, conceptually similar to a uni-body passenger car structure. The cab is hard-mounted directly to the frame, which is possible because there is no diesel engine generating the constant vibration that traditional trucks must accommodate.

3. **Massive battery capacity and the EPTO system:** The long-range Tesla Semi carries 822 kWh of usable battery capacity, while the standard-range version carries 548 kWh — roughly 10 times the capacity of a typical electric passenger car (60–100 kWh). Tesla's Electric Power Takeoff (EPTO) system leverages this large battery by powering refrigerated trailers directly through an integrated electric connection capable of delivering up to 25 kW continuously, eliminating the need for a separate diesel-powered refrigeration unit. Because refrigeration systems cycle on and off rather than running continuously at full power, the Semi can realistically support refrigerated cargo for extended periods without meaningful impact on range.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Actually Smart Summon speed increase in FSD 14.3:** Tesla rolled out an update to Actually Smart Summon (ASS) as part of Full Self-Driving version 14.3, raising the feature's maximum speed to 8 mph — approximately 33% faster than the previous limit. Tesla AI confirmed this change. While 8 mph remains a modest speed, the update is significant because parking lots present a uniquely complex autonomous driving environment: chaotic, poorly marked, and full of unpredictable movement at close range. The higher speed makes the feature more practically usable in everyday situations.

2. **History of Summon as a public FSD demonstration:** Tesla introduced the original Summon feature in early 2016 with Autopilot 7.1, initially allowing owners to move the vehicle slowly forward or backward into a garage or parking space via the Tesla app. The feature evolved into Smart Summon, enabling navigation through parking lots toward the owner. Early versions were rough, and performance deteriorated after Tesla removed radar and ultrasonic sensors in favor of a vision-only system. "Actually Smart Summon," released in 2024, rebuilt the feature from the ground up using the same AI and camera systems powering Tesla's broader Full Self-Driving stack, enabling the car to interpret its environment visually rather than relying on proximity sensors.

## Market & Competition

1. **The Boring Company's Proof Rock as reusable industrial equipment:** The Boring Company provided three major updates across its Nashville and Las Vegas projects, all pointing to a single core idea: Proof Rock is not behaving like a conventional tunnel boring machine. Unlike historically bespoke TBMs designed around single projects and often dismantled underground at completion, the Boring Company is operating Proof Rock more like reusable industrial equipment, using the same tunnel design across every project to eliminate reinvention costs. Proof Rock 2 is being transported across Las Vegas on a heavy-haul platform the company calls "the Monster" — one section of the machine alone weighing roughly 465,000 lb, with the Monster adding another 140,000 lb — and will relaunch directly off that platform to begin digging its fifth tunnel in Las Vegas using a process the company calls "porpoising."

2. **Proof Rock's continuous digging and zero-crew underground operation:** Traditional TBMs dig approximately 5 feet, stop, and wait while crews install pre-cast concrete tunnel lining rings, repeating this cycle continuously. Proof Rock is designed to install tunnel lining simultaneously with digging, eliminating those stops entirely. Excavated rock is immediately loaded onto a growing conveyor belt that will eventually stretch roughly 10 miles between downtown Nashville and the airport upon completion of the first phase of the Music City Loop. The machine also features what the Boring Company calls "ZPIT" — Zero People In Tunnel — with most operations monitored and controlled remotely from the surface. Proof Rock also runs on electricity rather than diesel, keeping the underground environment cleaner, quieter, and safer. Additionally, unlike most TBMs that can only move forward, Proof Rock demonstrated the ability to reverse away from the rock face for cutter head inspection. The company's target is to dig faster than 1 mile per week, which would make Proof Rock six times faster than its predecessor.
