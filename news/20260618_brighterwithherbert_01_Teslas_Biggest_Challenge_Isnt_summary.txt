This video, hosted by "brighterwithherbert" featuring guest Brian White of FutureAzA, argues that Tesla's primary challenge in advancing Full Self-Driving and robotaxi deployment has shifted from technology to politics and narrative warfare. The hosts present a series of positive FSD safety and regulatory developments, then contrast them with a growing wave of legislative resistance and media criticism — particularly a Reuters article challenging Tesla's safety claims in Europe and proposed restrictive legislation in New Jersey.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised Submitted for Approval in Taiwan:** Tesla has officially submitted FSD Supervised for regulatory review in Taiwan through the Vehicle Safety Inspection Center. The process requires a technical committee review and road testing, with the committee expected to convene within 2–4 weeks. If deemed feasible, it then moves to the Ministry of Transport for final approval. Separately, Tesla Taiwan has discontinued the one-time FSD purchase option, moving to subscription-only starting July 1st, which the hosts interpret as a signal that FSD launch in Taiwan is imminent.

2. **FSD Now Approved or Available in 12 Countries and Regions:** The hosts enumerate the current markets where FSD is approved or available: the US, Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium. Japan is also conducting road tests in metropolitan areas including Tokyo and Yokohama. Brian White notes that as approval spreads in Europe and Australia, additional Asian countries that defer to other regulators' approvals are likely to follow without conducting their own full review processes.

3. **Dutch Regulators Defend FSD Approval Against Reuters:** Reuters published an article claiming Tesla used misleading US safety statistics when presenting data to European regulators — specifically alleging that Tesla counts only serious crashes for its own vehicles and compares that to all US crashes, and that Tesla's "32,000 lives saved" figure assumes all trucks and motorcycles in America are replaced by FSD-equipped Teslas. The Dutch vehicle authority (RDW) pushed back directly, stating their approval was based on an independent 18-month review that included 1.6 million km of road tests, 4,500 track tests, and 13,000 customer ride-alongs. Post-approval, Tesla recorded zero highway collisions across 23.6 million km of FSD driving in the Netherlands (also cited as 24 million miles by Dutch transportation officials). Brian White argues Reuters wrote a technically defensible but incomplete picture, knowingly omitting context that would make Tesla's comparison methodology reasonable given that no other manufacturer provides equivalent telematics data.

4. **Reuters Senate Inquiry Criticized as Irresponsible:** Reuters publicized on X that a US Senate inquiry had been opened based on their article, which Brian White characterized as journalistically irresponsible — analogous to "standing next to a building you just burned down." He noted Reuters has shifted toward sensationalism since introducing a paywall, citing a prior false report about a Tesla factory near Mexico City airport as an example of the outlet publishing unverified claims for clicks.

5. **New Jersey Legislation Would Effectively Ban Driverless Deployment:** Tesla sent a direct email to New Jersey Tesla owners urging them to contact state legislators to oppose proposed legislation moving through Trenton that would impose restrictions severe enough to keep true driverless deployment illegal in the state. The email noted that 578 people were killed in traffic accidents in New Jersey in the prior year (2025), with more than 94% of serious crashes attributed to human error, impairment, distraction, or fatigue. Brian White and the host suspect taxi or transportation lobbying interests are behind the bill. The hosts note the proposed legislation appears to specifically target Tesla's FSD while leaving other ADAS systems such as Blue Cruise, Super Cruise, and ProPilot unaffected. Brian White argues that legislators actively blocking AV deployment now have "blood on their hands," comparing it to discovering a cancer treatment and refusing to distribute it.

6. **Real-World FSD Life-Safety Use Case:** The hosts highlight a viral story of a Model Y driver who suffered a heart attack mid-drive, called his son, and whose son remotely rerouted the vehicle to a hospital via the Tesla app. The medical team confirmed that minutes of delay could have changed the outcome. The hosts use the story to illustrate a practical life-safety dimension of FSD beyond crash statistics.

## Robotaxi

1. **Four Months Without an At-Fault Robotaxi Incident:** The latest NHTSA robotaxi data for Tesla's Austin fleet shows one new incident in the most recent reporting month: a stopped Tesla robotaxi that was rear-ended by a truck — Tesla not at fault. Combined with two prior incidents in March where the robotaxi was also stationary and rear-ended, Tesla has now gone approximately four months without a robotaxi incident where FSD was at fault. Sawyer Merritt noted the irony that all recent crashes involving Tesla's robotaxi fleet were caused by human drivers in other vehicles rear-ending the stopped Teslas. Elon Musk affirmed this observation.

2. **Robotaxi Fleet Remains Active and Taking Passengers:** In response to skeptics claiming the robotaxi fleet isn't conducting rides, Brian White cited Josh West, who visited Austin the prior week for the Cyber Rodeo event hosted by Tesla Owners of Austin and immediately took a robotaxi ride. The hosts acknowledge ride volume may be lower than peak but argue that any active mileage makes a zero at-fault accident rate meaningful.

## Market & Competition

1. **Regulatory Fragmentation as a Business and Strategic Risk:** The hosts discuss how the patchwork of regulatory environments — ranging from permissive (US, Australia) to proactive independent testing (Netherlands) to potentially hostile (New Jersey, Boston) — creates uneven FSD deployment timelines. Brian White expresses confidence that pockets of resistance like New Jersey and Boston are not a material long-term threat to Tesla's mission, as they represent a small percentage of overall drivers and the legislative prohibition strategy is unlikely to hold once the technology's safety record becomes undeniable. He predicts holdout jurisdictions will eventually flip.

2. **FSD Subscription Revenue as a Rising Revenue Wave:** The hosts briefly discuss the revenue implications of the global FSD subscription rollout. With the one-time purchase option being phased out (most recently in New Zealand/Taiwan markets by July 1st), all new FSD users will be on recurring subscriptions. Brian White references Elon Musk's next compensation plan target of 10 million FSD subscribers as the scale milestone, estimating the revenue potential at roughly $100/month per subscriber across that base.
