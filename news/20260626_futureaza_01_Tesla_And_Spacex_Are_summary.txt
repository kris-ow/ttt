This video features FutureAzA host Brian and guest Randy Kirk debating whether Tesla or SpaceX represents the better investment over a 5–10 year horizon, framed as a "showdown" between Elon Musk's two most prominent companies. The core thesis is that both companies carry enormous and largely unknowable upside — driven by Optimus, autonomous driving, SpaceX's data center ambitions, and Starlink — making a definitive ranking nearly impossible, though the hosts ultimately suggest investors in either (or both) are likely to do well regardless of how a potential merger shakes out.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla vs. SpaceX: The Unknowable Investment Question:** Randy Kirk opens by arguing the question of which company has greater upside over 5–10 years is fundamentally "unknowable," citing three reasons: the uncertain scale of SpaceX's space-based data center concept, the unresolved question of which company benefits more from what Randy calls the "Macrohard" hardware ecosystem, and the open question of whether Optimus reaches 100 million to a billion units per year. He notes that Brian Wong (described as someone who analyzes this in far more detail) believes SpaceX will win out, while Randy leans toward Tesla given the potential scale of Optimus.

2. **New TAMs Expanding the Investment Case:** Randy observes that even a year prior, Optimus was already considered the largest total addressable market in the world. Since then, he claims two additional TAMs — larger than Optimus — have emerged from things Elon Musk is now attempting across his companies, though he does not name them specifically. He cites SpaceX's recent move to rent out data center capacity to now three clients (reportedly unknown to analysts just two months prior) and the concept of space-based compute as examples of entirely new categories that were not publicly anticipated.

3. **SpaceX Data Center Rental and Compute Strategy:** The hosts discuss what they believe is Elon Musk's emerging strategy to build data centers designed for third-party rental from day one — not just for internal use. Randy expresses confidence (attributed to conversations he's had in recent days) that Elon has already identified buildings for this purpose. The hosts argue that Elon's relationship with Nvidia's Jensen Huang gives him priority access to GPU supply, and that many companies that placed chip orders lack the cash or infrastructure to take delivery — making it rational for them to let SpaceX acquire and operate the hardware instead.

4. **Merger Valuation Concerns:** Both hosts acknowledge ongoing investor anxiety about a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger and which side would receive the worse deal. Brian argues this concern is somewhat moot if both companies 10x or more in value — "you should never cry at a profit, especially if the profit is 10x." He also references a separate video the channel published earlier in the week debunking the idea that Tesla is "sandbagging" its valuation, concluding there is no evidence for it on practical, ethical, or legal grounds.

5. **SpaceX IPO and Current Valuation:** Brian notes he was unable to buy SpaceX at its IPO price and says he will likely not purchase shares unless the price falls below the IPO level. At the time of filming, SpaceX shares were trading around $155, which Brian characterizes as still approximately 15% above a level he'd consider fair value — though he acknowledges the float was so small at IPO that the true long-run equilibrium price is unclear.

6. **Starlink Aviation as a Competitive Moat:** Brian shares a personal anecdote contrasting his experience with Starlink on a Hawaii flight the prior year (described as "gamechanging") versus a recent Alaska Airlines flight without Starlink on the same route, where he paid $8 for an intermittent, nearly unusable connection. The hosts discuss that Starlink's advantage is most pronounced on wide-body aircraft with large passenger counts, where competing systems struggle to deliver adequate bandwidth per user, and on transoceanic routes generally.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Scale and "Game Over" Scenario:** Randy and Brian discuss the possibility of Optimus reaching 100 million to 1 billion units per year in production, with Randy characterizing that outcome as potentially "game over" for competitors. Brian adds that Optimus's total addressable market extends beyond Earth — Mars colonization will rely primarily on Optimus units, making it arguably the largest TAM in the solar system. Brian also pushes back on deeply skeptical viewers who believe Optimus will either never happen or take a decade, noting that Tesla is already building a large dedicated factory.

## Financials

1. **Tesla Share Price and Retail Investor Math:** Randy frames the investment decision in concrete retail terms: 100 shares of Tesla are worth approximately $40,000 at the time of filming (implying a share price of roughly $400). He suggests that an investor holding 100 shares each of Tesla and SpaceX — 200 shares total — could see roughly $800,000 in value if both 10x, which he says most market participants expect, or more.
