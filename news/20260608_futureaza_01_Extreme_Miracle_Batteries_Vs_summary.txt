This Monday morning news roundup from FutureAzA, recorded live from Detroit following a summer meetup, covers roughly a dozen news stories spanning EV affordability, battery technology developments, SpaceX/Starlink milestones, and miscellaneous market topics. The host's overarching theme is separating genuine technological progress from hype — particularly around next-generation battery claims — while also highlighting competitive dynamics in EVs, energy storage, and satellite internet.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Slate Auto Truck Reveal Approaching:** The host discusses Slate Auto's upcoming June 24th production-intent truck unveil, at which the actual price will also be announced. A deal with Carvana has already been announced ahead of the sales launch. The host expects the price to land under $40,000 and considers a sub-$30,000 launch price unreasonable at this stage. He also expresses concern about the truck's range and hopes Slate will switch from NMC to LFP batteries, noting that domestic sourcing incentives that originally justified NMC are no longer relevant. With LFP, buyers can charge to 100%, whereas an NMC battery charged only from 20–80% effectively cuts usable capacity by 40%, potentially leaving a range-limited truck with only ~100 usable miles.

2. **Used Cybertruck Demand Surprise:** Tesla recently uploaded a batch of used Cybertrucks to its sales site, with prices starting at $72,900 for a Foundation Series. The host notes that roughly half of the newly listed inventory sold within a day, suggesting meaningful demand at lower price points despite the vehicle's polarizing reputation.

3. **Dodge Charger Daytona Price Hike:** The Dodge Charger Daytona Scat Pack received a $12,000 price increase despite slow sales, with the base model now starting at approximately $50,000. The host is skeptical of the logic behind raising prices on an already slow-selling vehicle.

4. **Canada BYD Expansion and EV Tariff Quota:** Canada has opened an import quota allowing 49,000 EVs from China at preferred tariff rates. Tesla has already consumed roughly 10% of that allotment. BYD is moving to open Canadian showrooms to capture share, and Nissan — which manufactures some models in China — is also positioned to benefit given its existing dealership network.

## Energy

1. **CATL Sodium-Ion Commercial Rollout:** CATL, described as the world's largest lithium-ion battery maker by a significant margin, has announced a concrete roadmap for commercial sodium-ion energy storage systems, with first customer deliveries expected to begin in September 2025 (implied from context as a matter of months away from the June 2026 publish date — though the host presents this as imminent). The host notes sodium-ion is cheaper to produce but historically has had lower energy density, worse roundtrip efficiency, and shorter cycle life than lithium-ion — factors that must be factored into total cost of ownership, especially for grid storage.

2. **Lithium-Air Battery Research Paper — 12,000 Wh/kg Claim:** A research paper claiming a theoretical lithium-air battery energy density of 12,000 watt-hours per kilogram generated significant online buzz over the weekend. The host strongly cautions that this is purely theoretical — not in production, not in pilot, not even in prototype — and that the jump from ~400 Wh/kg (considered very good today) to 12,000 Wh/kg should trigger serious skepticism. He acknowledges that if it were ever real, it would be transformative across aviation, rail, and virtually every energy-intensive application.

3. **BYD Flash-Charge Battery Supply Constraints:** BYD's new fast-charge batteries — described as charging roughly four times faster than standard for a modest price premium — are experiencing supply constraints because demand has outpaced production capacity. A full-size SUV model's launch has been pushed to June as a result.

4. **Tesla Vehicle-to-Grid/Vehicle-to-Load Progress:** Tesla has rolled out vehicle-to-load functionality in Australia, at least for the Model Y Performance variant. The host notes the full vehicle-to-grid vision — where fleet-scale bidirectional charging could meaningfully support grid stability — remains limited by the fact that most Tesla vehicles lack the necessary onboard circuitry.

5. **Battery Energy Storage Bans in U.S. Towns:** Some U.S. municipalities have enacted outright bans or placed pending restrictions on large-scale battery energy storage projects (Megapacks and equivalents). The host highlights Washington State examples, including an outright ban in Black Diamond. He argues the fire concerns driving these bans are largely outdated, noting that thermal runaway incidents have become rarer even as deployments have surged, and that the industry has largely moved away from high-nickel chemistries toward lithium iron phosphate, which is significantly less prone to thermal runaway.

6. **PNNL Domestic Battery Production Line:** The Pacific Northwest National Laboratory has commissioned a 16-piece prismatic battery cell production line inside the Department of Energy's Grid Storage Launchpad, aimed at developing safer and cheaper domestic energy storage solutions. The host is mildly skeptical this represents near-term news, suggesting the research mandate changes slowly year over year.

7. **GM Battery Bet at Warren Tech Center:** GM is investing $900 million in a new battery development facility at its Warren Tech Center outside Detroit, attempting to reboot an EV battery program that was previously written down. The host is skeptical of the accounting logic but notes the investment signals continued commitment to an electric future.

## Market & Competition

1. **EU Trade Defense Against Chinese EVs:** The European Union is expanding its response to Chinese industrial overcapacity, moving beyond tariffs toward broader trade defense and industrial policy measures. The host notes that European automakers have begun producing genuinely competitive EVs (citing the VW ID.Buzz as attractive but roughly 1,000 lbs too heavy and $30,000 too expensive) but have not yet achieved the cost competitiveness needed to displace Chinese imports on price.

2. **Elon Musk / ASML Terafab Chip Plant Talks:** Elon Musk is reportedly in direct talks with ASML regarding a proposed $119 billion chip fabrication facility in Texas. The host clarifies that $119 billion refers to the total factory cost — not the ASML lithography equipment deal alone — and notes that ASML's CEO described Musk as "very serious." The ASML CEO also warned that semiconductor ambitions tied to Tesla and SpaceX could strain an already tight global chip supply, which the host finds logically confused given that building new fab capacity is precisely the solution to supply tightness.

## SpaceX / Starlink (Non-Tesla Context)

1. **Starlink Reaches 12 Million Active Subscribers:** Starlink has announced 12 million active paying customers, each with a dedicated terminal. The host frames this as the beginning of a much larger growth trajectory, citing a former SpaceX engineer (Quincy from Electric Era Tech) who believes 12 million is just the start even with the existing satellite constellation and without Starship ever flying.

2. **British Airways Starlink Pause Explained:** A headline suggested British Airways was suspending Starlink installations due to a problem with the service. The host clarifies that deep in the article, the actual reason is that the summer travel season prevents planes from being taken out of service for maintenance, and the Starlink installations will proceed alongside regular maintenance in the fall. No service issues exist.

3. **Falcon 9 Booster 35th Reuse:** A single Falcon 9 booster completed its 35th launch and landing. The host uses this as a broader point about how reusability enables higher quality rocket engineering — when boosters are expendable, there is no incentive to build them to last.

4. **Russia's Starlink Competitor Loses First Satellite:** Russia's competing constellation lost its first satellite after just 75 days in orbit. The host notes this is less alarming than it sounds for a first batch, but points to Russia's fundamental cost disadvantage from using non-reusable rockets and geopolitical barriers to market adoption.

5. **SpaceX XAI Compute Deal:** Google has signed a $920 million per month compute deal with XAI/SpaceX. The host also references a large deal with Anthropic, framing these as validation that SpaceX/XAI has meaningful, monetizable compute infrastructure.
