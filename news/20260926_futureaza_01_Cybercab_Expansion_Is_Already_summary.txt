This futureaza episode, featuring host Brian and guest Herbert Ong, digs into why Tesla's Cybercab/robotaxi fleet expansion in Texas has appeared to plateau, arguing the bottleneck is an upcoming FSD software release (referred to as version 15) rather than regulatory, battery, or demand constraints. They also discuss NHTSA's posture toward Tesla's self-certified robotaxi program and push back on low third-party fleet-size estimates.

## Robotaxi
1. **Registered fleet vs. active fleet gap:** Herbert cites a post from X user Herbert Ong (@HerbertOng) noting Tesla had added registrations in Texas bringing the total to 476, including 69 Cybertrucks, with two more added subsequently. Brian and Herbert distinguish this registered count from the number of vehicles actually active in service, explaining that Tesla registers cars first, then tests them before adding them to live robotaxi service.
2. **Houston buildout underway:** Herbert says parking lots in Houston are visibly filling with Cybercabs and other robotaxi variants (some with steering wheels, some without), which he interprets as pre-launch staging ahead of Tesla expanding robotaxi service into Houston next.
3. **Disputed simultaneous-fleet-size estimates:** Herbert says a "Tesla critic" claimed only about eight robotaxis were operating at a time, which he disputes based on personally observing roughly 15–20 driving simultaneously in Austin picking up and dropping off passengers. Brian estimates the true peak (excluding the robotaxi launch night, which he believes required "full mobilization" of resources) was around 50 vehicles, and cautions that reported figures like "eight" undermine critics' credibility without being accurate.
4. **Fleet size may have contracted after launch, then plateaued:** Herbert suggests Tesla temporarily added more vehicles around the Cybercab presentation event and has since throttled back, a pattern he says has occurred before when Tesla slows fleet growth while rolling out new FSD features.
5. **Demand outstripping supply, driving dynamic pricing:** Herbert says post-Cybercab-presentation demand is very high, leading Tesla to use dynamic pricing — in some cases making a Cybercab ride pricier than an equivalent Waymo ride — and to tell riders to wait up to an hour, which he frames as evidence this remains a "pilot project" rather than a full-fledged service.
6. **Growth rate and year-end fleet projection:** Herbert states that based on reaching 1 million miles of unattended robotaxi driving, week-over-week growth has been running at about 17%, and around 20% over the most recent month; he projects this pace could hold until FSD version 15 ships, at which point growth could accelerate, putting active robotaxis in a range of roughly 1,000–2,000 by year-end.
7. **NHTSA cap not the real constraint:** Herbert relays from a detailed conversation with Eric Earley (described as the executive responsible for Cybercab and the robotaxi program) that Tesla, being self-certified, is not bound by the 2,500-unit limit that applies to a competitor like Zoox, and that NHTSA documentation reportedly allows for around 3,000 of these vehicles; Herbert frames the current pace as a self-imposed restriction rather than a regulatory one.
8. **Speculative theory on deliberate pacing ahead of EU vote:** Herbert speculates (his own theory, not confirmed by Tesla) that Tesla may be deliberately holding back a rapid fleet scale-up until after an EU regulatory vote he says is scheduled for October 6th, reasoning that a safety incident during a sudden scale-up could jeopardize that vote; he also frames incremental scaling as ultimately faster to reach large fleet numbers than steady 20%-per-week compounding.
9. **Ruling out battery and production bottlenecks:** Both hosts agree that 4680 battery cell supply and the Cybercab production line itself are not limiting factors, noting the line is designed to run faster than the current addition rate and that Tesla can substitute Model Y production if a specific new component runs short.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD version 15 tied to "destination memory" feature:** Herbert says the anticipated version 15 of FSD (also referenced as being close to Ashok Elluswamy's "ridiculously safe" safety bar) is expected in one to two months and will add a "destination memory" feature allowing robotaxis to remember specific pickup/drop-off spots (e.g., a hotel lobby vs. curb, airport zones, mall entrances). Herbert characterizes this, combined with the safety threshold, as the missing piece unlocking faster fleet expansion, and estimates Tesla is "40% of the way" through a set of seven functions he references without detailing the rest.
2. **NHTSA regulatory posture described as favorable:** Herbert cites recent public remarks by NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison as supportive of Autopilot and robotaxis without mandating lidar, instead requiring companies to demonstrate safety regardless of technology used. Herbert states NHTSA has already repealed five of 22 regulations tied to human-driving assumptions, with nine remaining, and calls the ongoing back-and-forth largely "procedural," not a substantive obstacle. A concurrent NHTSA inquiry that began around the robotaxi launch is characterized by Herbert as a standard part of the process rather than a sign of a bloated investigation.

## Market & Competition
1. **Demand and reputational headwinds dismissed as non-binding constraint:** Herbert argues that despite visible anti-Musk sentiment (e.g., people putting stickers on Teslas distancing themselves from Musk while still buying the car), Tesla continues to sell every unit it produces and retains underutilized production capacity — including the decision not to build a Model 2 or launch Giga Mexico — implying demand is not the bottleneck on robotaxi scaling, this being a deliberate capital-efficiency choice rather than a demand shortfall.
