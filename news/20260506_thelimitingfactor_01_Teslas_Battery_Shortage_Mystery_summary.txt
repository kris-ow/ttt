This video by Jordan Giesige of The Limiting Factor investigates the "battery shortage" Tesla has intermittently referenced over the past year, revealing that the constraint is not a cell supply problem but rather battery *pack* assembly capacity. The core thesis is that Tesla is deliberately creating a temporary, self-imposed bottleneck by upgrading and insourcing battery pack production across multiple factories — a strategic move to reduce costs and improve margins in an increasingly competitive EV market.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Battery Pack Assembly — Not Cells — Is the Bottleneck:** In Q1's earnings call, Tesla CFO Vaibhav clarified that the limiting factor is battery pack capacity, not the cells themselves (i.e., not the 4680 or LFP cell production lines). This resolved widespread speculation that the 4680 ramp was causing the constraint. Lars reinforced this, noting that new capacity is actively being added across multiple facilities and that the situation is expected to improve over the coming months.

2. **Giga Berlin Shifting to In-House 4680 Cells:** At Giga Berlin, Tesla has launched a Model Y battery pack using in-house 4680 cells produced in the United States. This is described as ramping up and contributing to Berlin's output, helping meet the demand surge seen in Europe. These cells are cheaper than third-party alternatives both because they eliminate supplier margins and because Tesla receives a $35 per kilowatt-hour production tax credit on them.

3. **Giga Nevada Production Line Upgrades:** At Giga Nevada, Tesla is retooling battery pack production lines that were originally stood up in 2017–2018 — nearly a decade old. The upgrades are expected to yield higher production rates and lower production costs, and may also include improvements to the battery pack design itself.

4. **Giga Shanghai Insourcing LFP Modules from Sunwoda Cells:** At Giga Shanghai, Tesla has begun purchasing battery cells from Sunwoda (rather than CATL) and assembling the modules in-house. Previously, CATL supplied both cells and modules. The Sunwoda cells are expected to be cheaper than CATL's, and in-house module assembly should provide additional cost savings.

5. **Vehicle Backlog and Future Sales Upside:** Tesla exited Q1 with its highest vehicle backlog in over two years. Once the battery pack bottleneck is resolved, Jordan argues this should translate into higher vehicle sales in upcoming quarters and potentially new highs for total vehicle deliveries.

6. **Strategic Cost Reduction Thesis:** Jordan frames all three factory initiatives as part of a unified strategy: by reducing costs on the battery pack — the most expensive assembly in the vehicle — Tesla can either lower vehicle prices to compete more aggressively or capture the savings as higher margin.

## Energy

1. **Megapack Q1 Deployments Down Sharply:** Tesla's energy storage deployments in Q1 came in at 8.8 GWh, a 38% reduction from 14.2 GWh in Q4 2025. Tesla attributed the decline to the lumpy nature of the energy storage business, which is subject to customer project timelines and construction schedules.

2. **Megapack Gross Margins Hit All-Time High — But for the Wrong Reason:** Megapack gross margins reached an all-time high in Q1, but Jordan notes this was driven by adjustments related to tariffs paid in prior quarters rather than organic operational improvement. Tesla is guiding that Megapack gross margins should be expected to decline in future quarters due to increased competition and tariff impacts.

3. **Megapack U.S. Domestic Cell Supply and Future Cost Trajectory:** For U.S.-bound Megapacks, Tesla plans to increasingly use domestic LFP battery cells to avoid tariffs, though production costs will initially be higher domestically. Jordan notes that this cost dynamic could change in the future if Tesla brings prismatic LFP cell production in-house using lithium from its Corpus Christi refinery, LFP cathode material from Austin, and its dry electrode coating process.

4. **Megapack 3 Coming to Houston Later in 2026:** Tesla reaffirmed that Megapack 3 production will begin later this year in Houston, Texas. Jordan characterizes Megapack 3 as a more competitive product than Megapack 2.

5. **Giga Shanghai Megapack Capacity Listing Anomaly:** In Tesla's Q1 quarterly report, Giga Shanghai's Megapack production capacity was listed as 20 GWh, down from the usual 40 GWh figure. Tesla made no comment on this change during the earnings call. Jordan flags it as potentially worth raising at the next earnings call, but notes it doesn't appear significant given Tesla's reaffirmed strong demand and production outlook.
