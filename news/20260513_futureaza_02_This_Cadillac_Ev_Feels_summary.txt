This video is a hands-on test drive review of a used Cadillac Lyriq EV, filmed at Platt Auto in Milwaukie, Oregon with dealer Jack. The hosts evaluate the Lyriq's interior quality, driving dynamics, driver assistance features, and charging specs, ultimately concluding it's a luxury-feel purchase driven by aesthetics and comfort rather than best-in-class EV specs.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cadillac Lyriq Overview and Platform:** The Cadillac Lyriq is built on GM's Ultium platform and is described as the best vehicle GM produces on that platform. It features a 102 kWh battery pack, a stated range of just over 300 miles, and is manufactured in Spring Hill, Tennessee — as is the battery. The battery uses NMCA chemistry. The specific unit on the lot is listed for $45,900 used, with new well-optioned examples having originally sold into the $70,000s.

2. **Driving Dynamics and Performance:** The Lyriq is noted to be front-wheel drive in base configuration, rated at 365 horsepower. The hosts observe that torque delivery is intentionally suppressed at low speeds to avoid wheel spin, with power ramping in more noticeably at higher speeds (around 30–40 mph). The ride is described as composed and well-balanced given the vehicle's weight, with very low road and wind noise inside the cabin.

3. **Interior Quality and Features:** The Lyriq is praised for its premium feel — supple seats, metal door handles, wood trim accents, and a generally upscale tactile experience that surpasses other Ultium-platform vehicles like the Blazer EV. It includes heated and ventilated seats, massaging seats (via a lumbar air bladder that inflates and deflates), an AKG sound system (which the host finds underwhelming for the space), haptic seat vibration alerts for proximity warnings and lane departure, and headrest-integrated speakers. Physical buttons are present in large quantity but criticized for being uniform in size and shape, making them hard to distinguish by feel alone.

4. **Charging Speed Criticism:** The Lyriq charges at a maximum of 190 kW DC, which is the standard rate across all Ultium-platform vehicles (including the Equinox EV, Blazer EV, and others). The hosts note the quoted 10–80% charge time of 30–40 minutes is a best-case figure. They compare this unfavorably to Tesla and the Hyundai/Kia 800-volt architecture vehicles, noting the Lyriq's charge time is roughly double. The large ~102 kWh battery makes the relatively modest charge rate more impactful in real-world use.

5. **Driver Assistance — Super Cruise:** The Lyriq includes GM's Super Cruise system, which operates hands-free on over 400,000 miles of pre-mapped divided highways and supports automatic lane changes (including blinker-initiated lane changes). It does not work on city streets or undivided highways. Super Cruise is a paid subscription service, which the host criticizes, contrasting it with Nissan's ProPilot Assist 2.0 — a comparable pre-mapped hands-free lane-change system that is not subscription-based.

6. **Used Market Availability and Comparable Inventory:** The dealer Platt Auto has been receiving multiple Lyriq units regularly. The lot also has Ford Lightning pickups priced at approximately $36,000 and $42,000 used (Flash and a higher trim), and recently sold six Silverado EVs (large battery variants) to a fleet customer. The hosts note used EV truck inventory has grown as prices have come down to accessible levels.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla FSD vs. Subscription ADAS:** A brief comparison is made between Tesla's Full Self-Driving and GM's Super Cruise subscription model. One host notes they have FSD on one of their two Tesla vehicles because they drive it enough to justify it, while their coworker uses FSD heavily on a 2021 or 2022 Model 3 for a long highway commute in rush-hour traffic. The host expresses a general objection to charging subscriptions for driver assistance features already embedded in the vehicle.

2. **Cadillac's EV Lineup Context:** The hosts briefly survey Cadillac's broader EV lineup, including the Escalade IQ (larger), the Vistiq (mid-size, position relative to Lyriq uncertain), and the Celestiq — described as a hand-built, extremely expensive halo vehicle of which they expect very few to be sold. The Lyriq is positioned as Cadillac's smallest EV.

3. **Charging Rate Competitive Landscape:** The hosts note that while 190 kW is disappointing relative to Tesla and 800-volt Hyundai/Kia products, it is not the worst in the segment — the 2025 Subaru Solterra is still at 100 kW, some Volkswagen ID.4s were at 130 kW for a period, Ford Mustang Mach-E was around 150 kW, and Ford Lightning is 150 kW.
