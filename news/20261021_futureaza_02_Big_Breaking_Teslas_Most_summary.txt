This is Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call Q&A, featuring Elon Musk, CFO Vaibhav Taneja, VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy, and VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy. The call centers on accelerating FSD-driven demand, rapid robotaxi mile growth via the Cybercab fleet, early-stage Optimus production, strong energy storage demand, and capital plans for the Terafab chip facility, alongside improved but still-constrained automotive margins.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate climbing sharply:** Over 75% of North American deliveries in Q3 included a paid FSD subscription sign-up at delivery, up roughly 20 percentage points from the prior quarter, with total paid FSD customers surpassing 1.7 million globally — for the first time, over half on a subscription basis rather than one-time purchase. Taneja noted every delivered car also includes a free month of FSD, yet customers are still paying to subscribe.
2. **European and Chinese regulatory approval still pending:** Musk said Tesla is approved in many European countries and hopes for EU-wide approval within a few months, expecting full European approval no later than Q1 of next year (not a timeline Tesla controls). Elluswamy said broader FSD approval in China remains difficult, with Tesla "working diligently" with regulators there; Tesla has published both its FSD and new unsupervised/robotaxi safety reports to support its regulatory case.
3. **Tesla building proprietary AI-based mapping:** In response to criticism of third-party map dependence, Elluswamy said Tesla is building its own AI-based mapping/navigation system integrated with voice control and personalized/fleet-learned routing, beginning rollout with trip start/end points as part of the V15 release, expanding over several months to cover parking and full destination handling.
4. **FSD revenue estimated near $1 billion annualized:** An analyst estimated FSD's annual recurring revenue at roughly $1 billion based on attach rates and per-car economics; Taneja confirmed the order of magnitude was correct and said Tesla will share more FSD metrics, including retention/churn data, as the business scales.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab fleet and unsupervised miles scaling fast:** Musk said the Cybercab fleet in Austin grew roughly 10x since launch about a month earlier, calling weekly autonomous-mile growth "a rocket going to orbit." Taneja said cumulative unsupervised miles grew 8x in the quarter with double-digit weekly growth throughout, and Elluswamy described a high-teens weekly growth rate in unsupervised miles since late July that management expects to continue, with no identified hurdles ahead.
2. **Expansion beyond Austin to Dallas and other cities:** Elluswamy said Tesla will launch in Dallas next month, with the same software scaling to additional cities alongside Model Y robotaxis; Tesla currently operates six cities and is pursuing both breadth (more cities) and depth (denser fleets, like Austin) with 24/7 operation, higher speeds, and more weather/road conditions targeted by year-end.
3. **Per-mile cost target of $0.30 not yet reached:** Taneja said Tesla is not yet at its target cost of $0.30 per paid robotaxi mile but sees "a very clear path" to get there through scaling operations and learning fleet management (pickup, drop-off, cleaning).
4. **Cybercab line capacity above 125,000 units/year, ramp still early:** Moravy said the Cybercab has 50% fewer parts than any Tesla vehicle built before and that the factory is currently producing only at the rate the robotaxi service needs, well below the line's actual capacity; production is also starting at a new Austin robot factory.
5. **Customer-owned Cybercabs targeted by year-end 2026; customer FSD-unsupervised robotaxi fleet around Q2 next year:** Moravy said Tesla aims to deliver Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, though the vast majority will remain Tesla-owned for transport-as-a-service; Elluswamy said customer-owned Model Ys could begin joining the robotaxi fleet around Q2 of next year once geofencing and fleet management requirements are met.
6. **Robotaxi financial impact expected to build through 2027:** Taneja said robotaxi revenue won't move the needle this quarter but should become noticeable around Q2 of next year and a "big deal" by Q3 or Q4 of next year.
7. **Musk predicts Tesla will dominate global passenger-mile transport:** Musk said that absent government restrictions protecting domestic industries, he expects Tesla to handle the majority of passenger miles in any country where it can operate freely, a prediction he said he feels confident in.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus high-volume production line started in Fremont; full ramp ~1 year out:** Musk said Tesla has begun early production on a high-volume Optimus line in Fremont with most of a planned roughly 1-million-unit-per-line setup already installed, but expects it will take at least a year, possibly longer, to reach an annualized rate of 20,000 units per week, given ongoing design changes.
2. **Bootstrapping training via "Optimus Academy" and self-play:** Musk and Elluswamy said Optimus lacks a large existing fleet (unlike cars) to source training data, so Tesla must build roughly 10,000–20,000 bots to generate self-play data through a new "Optimus Academy," combining human-demonstration-based initial training with reinforcement learning and simulation.
3. **New Optimus demo video planned within one to two months:** Musk said Tesla plans to release an Optimus video showing non-pre-programmed, AI-driven task performance (not a "canned demo") within roughly a month or two.
4. **AI5 chip integration targeted for ~April next year:** Musk said integrating Tesla's AI5 chip into Optimus — offering an order of magnitude more compute than AI4 — is targeted for around April of next year, which he called a major capability step given the far greater degrees of freedom a humanoid robot must handle versus a car.
5. **Hand design remains the hardest engineering problem:** Elluswamy and Musk said most of the electromechanical system has converged to a workable design, but the hand remains the most difficult area due to durability, dexterity, and sensor requirements, with ongoing refinement of bespoke gears and finger design.
6. **External customer deliveries expected around end of next year:** Musk reiterated that AI, electromechanical design, and durability issues are expected to be solved around the end of next year, at which point Tesla would begin delivering Optimus to companies outside Tesla; he said initial use cases will likely be industrial/dangerous/repetitive tasks run around the clock, with Musk stating he'd be "stunned" if any competitor has a remotely competitive humanoid robot by then.
7. **New Austin Optimus factory under construction:** Taneja said Tesla has started building a second Optimus factory in Austin with greater capacity than the Fremont facility.

## Energy
1. **Megapack near sold out through 2027, margins rising:** Musk said Tesla is "almost sold out" of all Megapacks for 2027 and ramping production to meet demand, with gross margins expected to increase meaningfully; Taneja said energy gross margins improved from 20.4% to 33.1% sequentially, aided by tariff-related funds and a vendor warranty true-up on cell costs, with 13.7 GWh deployed in the quarter (second-best quarter on record).
2. **Distributed compute "megapod" concept still early-stage:** In response to an analyst question, Taneja said Tesla's vision for a combined distributed power/compute product is still in early stages, with more clarity expected around Q1 or Q2 next year.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record backlog, Model Y leadership, production-constrained demand:** Taneja said Tesla exited Q3 with its highest backlog in four years, constrained by production rather than demand, with two consecutive quarters of over 480,000 vehicle deliveries. Based on preliminary data, the Model Y was believed to be the top-selling BEV in Europe and top-selling passenger car in South Korea, with new records in Australia, Japan, and Taiwan.
2. **Automotive gross margin improved to 18.1%:** Margins excluding credits rose sequentially from 16.3% to 18.1%, driven by a higher mix of deliveries in APAC, even as commodity costs and interest rates created headwinds partially offset by adjusted financing programs.

## Financials
1. **Capex guidance cut to ~$20 billion; new $30 billion credit facility secured:** Taneja revised full-year capex guidance down to approximately $20 billion due to efficient capital deployment, to be funded primarily from operating cash flow; Tesla separately secured capacity to borrow up to $30 billion to support future capex growth, with no draws planned this calendar year. Capex is expected to rise meaningfully next year and reach the tens of billions by 2028 as semiconductor capacity, Optimus, and robotaxi fleet expansion scale up.
2. **Free cash flow and cash balance solid; SpaceX stake mark-down offset by Bitcoin gain:** Tesla posted positive free cash flow of over $1.5 billion and ended the quarter with cash and investments over $45 billion. Net income was affected by a $142 million mark-to-market reduction on Tesla's SpaceX holding, offset by an approximately $290 million Bitcoin mark-to-market gain.
3. **Services and other margin hit all-time high:** Services and other margins rose sequentially from 14.1% to 15.9%, an all-time high, driven by supercharging, insurance, and other service lines.
4. **Terafab capital needs: tens of billions through 2028, potentially $100B+ long term:** Musk said the Terafab chip facility will require "tens of billions" in capital equipment through early 2028, likely exceeding $100 billion long-term, with cost-sharing between Tesla and SpaceX still to be determined; a research fab is targeted to begin commissioning machinery in the first half of next year, with a first test chip targeted around May or June and useful volume production aspired to around Q3 2028.
5. **SpaceX merger questions explicitly off-limits:** Musk and the IR team declined to address any questions about a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger, with Musk saying he already said what needed to be said and would not elaborate further on the call.
