## Brief

- EU FSD data: 5.2x fewer collisions, zero highway crashes in 26M miles
- Tesla signs two major solar PPAs in Texas and Arizona
- Q2 miss erased ~$140B in market value, capex guidance held at $25.5B
- Houston FSD whistleblower lawsuit filed, called procedurally weak
- SpaceX engineering archive to train next Grok model
- Musk won't deny growing Tesla-SpaceX overlap via "Terafab"
- Model Y Canada deliveries slip to late 2026/2027 on demand

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's first EU FSD safety dataset: supervised FSD was 5.2x safer than manual driving across ~65 million km over four months in five EU countries, with zero collisions across 26 million highway miles and six collisions across 14 million non-highway miles.
- A federal whistleblower lawsuit filed July 27 by former Tesla Houston FSD testing manager Javier Madrono alleges unsafe understaffing (scaling from 15 to 38 vehicle operators under his sole oversight) and a crash tied to exhaustion-driven guidance; one host's review argues the "mandated" ratio claim is inconsistent with internal messaging and the suit likely misses Texas's 90-day whistleblower filing window.
- NHTSA denied a petition to investigate hidden manual door releases on the 2022 Model 3 but will draft a new federal safety standard on emergency door releases for all automakers, following Tesla's prior redesign and the Safe Exit Act.

## Robotaxi
- Active Robotaxi fleet and unsupervised fleet figures continue updating per RobotaxiTracker.com; qualitative signals include Houston service now booking rides after 8:00 p.m. (still ending 8:30 p.m.) and silent turn signals/hazard lights rolled out fleet-wide.
- A third-party license-plate tracker estimated roughly 55 unsupervised robotaxis in Austin, independent of and higher than the host's prior 30 estimate — flagged as unverified.
- Axios Charlotte spotted 14 Cybercabs and 14 Model Ys with Texas plates in a Charlotte-area lot, fueling speculation of extended out-of-state testing before any launch.
- Waymo is reportedly considering ending its Uber partnership over operational and commercial disputes; it already ended in Phoenix and remains active in Austin and Atlanta for now.
- Jo Bhakdi called Morgan Stanley's forecast of 30,000 Cybercab-type vehicles by 2030 "nonsensical," arguing Tesla's economics make the realistic outcomes binary — near-zero or millions of units — with no plausible middle ground.
- On the earnings call, Musk said Tesla wants to "grow as fast as possible with Robotaxi without harm to anyone," language one host reads as more cautious than earlier full-US-coverage guidance.

## Financials
- Q2 revenue hit a record ~$28B (+26%), but EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.53 estimate, operating margin was 1.4%, and free cash flow turned negative; the stock fell ~14% over two days, wiping out ~$140B in market value.
- FSD subscriptions reached 1.48 million, up 56% year-over-year, with roughly 200,000 added in Q2 alone.
- Tesla's SEC filing disclosed a $1.95B all-stock AI hardware acquisition, ~90% contingent on retention/performance milestones; speculation (unconfirmed) points to Destiny AI, founded by former Dojo project leaders.

## Energy
- Tesla signed a PPA for the entire output of Zelestra's 140 MW AC Lumen solar farm in Texas (construction 2027, operational 2029), structured as a financial hedge via ERCOT rather than direct power delivery.
- Tesla signed a ~1 TWh/year hybrid PPA with ContourGlobal's Sterling Renewable Project in Arizona (450 MW AC solar, 1.4+ GWh storage), covering ~90% of output with completion targeted for 2028 — one of the largest solar-plus-storage corporate PPAs in US history.

## Electric Vehicles
- Model Y delivery dates in Canada now stretch into late 2026 and 2027, interpreted as strong demand alongside H1 Canadian Model Y sales up nearly 80%.
- Tesla is in a legal dispute with Cybertruck/Model Y supplier Angstrom Automotive, which is withholding ~700 finished parts and demanding $250,000/week to keep its Troy, Texas plant running; Tesla says on-hand inventory could run out within days.
- Musk announced Tesla will fully open-source the design and software of the discontinued Model S and Model X.
- A Geotab report claiming DC fast charging doubles annual EV battery degradation was contested by one host as methodologically skewed toward fleet-heavy, non-Tesla usage; Recurrent data on ~13,000 Teslas over 8 years showed minimal degradation difference regardless of Supercharger frequency.

## Humanoid Bots
- Jensen Huang called Optimus, Grok, and Tesla self-driving "world class" and said humanoid robots could become the next multi-trillion-dollar industry; Musk called Huang "awesome" in response.
- Musk reiterated Optimus production "will be extremely slow at first as everything is new," tempering near-term volume expectations after prior schedule slips.
- Viral footage of Chinese Engine AI T800 humanoid robots fighting showcased advanced fall-recovery motor control, though likely human-operator-directed rather than fully autonomous; separately, Figure's O2 logged ~1,200 hours at BMW's Spartanburg plant and its Helix system demoed a 4-minute autonomous dishwasher task (company-published, unverified).

## Market & Competition
- Ford's Model e EV division lost $900M in Q2 with a -89.6% EBIT margin as EV revenue fell 58% YoY to $1B, underscoring a profitability gap versus Tesla.
- TSLA traded in step with the broader tech selloff, closing near $298 on one referenced day after touching green intraday; one host argues the decline is market-wide, not Tesla-specific.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk confirmed SpaceX's engineering archive (excluding ITAR-restricted material) will feed a supplemental training run for Grok's next major model, following Grok's move under the SpaceX umbrella post-merger with xAI.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Asked directly about a Tesla-SpaceX merger on the earnings call, Musk said overlap between the companies is growing "especially with the Terafab" but that he "can't talk about combining companies" on an earnings call — a non-denial one host flagged as notable.
- Ron Baron detailed 27 SpaceX investments since 2017 totaling ~$2B, now valued around $25B, plus $1B more at IPO; he projects SpaceX could reach a $20–40 trillion valuation versus its ~$2 trillion IPO valuation.
- Cathie Wood's Ark Invest continued buying both SpaceX and Tesla shares as SpaceX stock fell roughly 50% from a prior high near $225.
- In an Economist interview, Musk predicted AI could exceed combined human intelligence within roughly five years and that humans may not be "in charge" within ten years, repeating his 10–20% estimate of catastrophic AI risk.
