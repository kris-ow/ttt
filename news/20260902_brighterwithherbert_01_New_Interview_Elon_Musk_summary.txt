This video covers clips from a recent Elon Musk interview at the G20 summit, focused on AI's projected economic impact and an emerging global power shortage for AI compute, followed by discussion with guest Jo Bhakdi (economist and investor) on the implications for SpaceX's AI/compute strategy and the OpenAI–Cursor–SpaceX dispute. The core thesis: AI compute growth is outpacing global power supply, and SpaceX's move to build its own power generation (including in-house turbine manufacturing) positions it to capture outsized value from this bottleneck, including via space-based compute.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Musk cites $20–30 trillion AI economic upside, with a 2027 power shortfall:** Musk said AI could increase the global economy by 20–30%, or roughly $20–30 trillion annually, with AI capable of performing almost any digital task by the end of next year (his prediction). He also cited an analyst consensus of at least a 15-gigawatt power shortfall for AI chips in 2027, driven by AI chip production growing 40–50% per year versus power availability outside China growing only 10–20% per year.
2. **SpaceX building its own natural gas turbines to bypass power bottleneck:** Per the host, SpaceX is reportedly building its own natural gas turbine blades in-house (rather than relying on the handful of turbine manufacturers already booked out to 2030), which the host says is expected to accelerate SpaceX's ability to bring data centers online by roughly 18 months. Musk stated that constructing SpaceX's own power plants was "the only way" the company was able to turn on AI compute faster than competitors, and that Google, Anthropic, and other companies are now leasing compute from SpaceX as a result.
3. **NVIDIA–SpaceX space-based compute rack, targeted for Q4 2027:** Musk noted NVIDIA and SpaceX have announced collaboration on a space-ready compute rack (referred to as an "NVL72"-class rack with multiple GPUs), targeted for completion by Q4 2027, tied to a next-generation AI satellite effort. Bhakdi separately predicted (as his own view, not confirmed) that the first gigawatt-scale space compute milestones could arrive by 2028, potentially 2029, with rapid scaling thereafter, and argued space-based inference (rather than terrestrial training) will become the dominant paradigm due to free solar power, no permitting, and unlimited space.
4. **Bhakdi predicts AI training could become tightly restricted or illegal by 2028:** Bhakdi (speculative, his own view) argued that once core model intelligence reaches a very high level, further large-scale training toward superintelligence has "only massive infinite downside," and predicted governments — including in the US — will increasingly restrict or effectively ban large training runs by 2028, while inference/agentic systems (deployable in space) continue scaling without that risk.

## Market & Competition
1. **OpenAI ends Cursor partnership after SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor:** OpenAI announced it is ending its partnership with Cursor (now owned by SpaceX), cutting off direct access to OpenAI models for Cursor developers effective November 12, citing an alleged terms violation by "Elon companies." Musk responded that he "couldn't care less." Cursor CEO Michael Truel said OpenAI models represent only about 5% of Cursor's user traffic and that Cursor is in talks with OpenAI; Anthropic's Tom Brown said Anthropic will increase compute support for Cursor instead.
2. **Host and guest frame this as OpenAI becoming isolated within the AI ecosystem:** Bhakdi characterized the move as "open war" between Musk/SpaceX and Sam Altman, noting Anthropic is growing closer to Musk (buying SpaceX compute) and that NVIDIA (Jensen Huang) is also reportedly pulling away from OpenAI after OpenAI struck a competing deal with AMD. Bhakdi suggested this could leave OpenAI increasingly isolated while a Musk/Anthropic/NVIDIA-aligned ecosystem consolidates, though he stressed this is his own read of the situation, not a confirmed outcome.
