Tesla has launched its first US vehicle-to-grid (V2G) program, "Powershare Grid Support," initially limited to Cybertruck owners in select Texas markets, allowing them to discharge battery energy to the grid during high-demand events in exchange for bill credits.

## Energy
1. **Program mechanics and eligibility:** The program is available to Cybertruck owners served by CenterPoint Energy and Oncor (Houston and Dallas metro areas), requiring a Powershare Gateway and Universal Wall Connector. Owners enroll via Tesla's Electric Drive plan in the Tesla app; during grid events the truck automatically discharges excess energy and the owner earns bill credits. The program is currently invitation-only (early adopter phase), with Tesla planning wider access after initial testing; Tesla states participation will not affect the Cybertruck warranty.
2. **Texas focus and prior VPP history:** Tesla has built virtual power plant (VPP) presence in Texas since 2022, starting with a 200-Powerwall demonstration and gaining Public Utility Commission of Texas approval for two VPPs in August 2023. Tesla paid Powerwall owners $9.9 million through its VPP programs in 2024. The Cybertruck's 123 kWh battery is described as roughly equivalent to nine Powerwalls (13.5 kWh each). California (PG&E, Southern California Edison, San Diego Gas & Electric territories) is named as the next planned expansion market.
3. **Delays and product gaps:** The Cybertruck remains Tesla's only vehicle with official bidirectional charging support; Tesla confirmed in late 2025 that the new Model Y Performance would support bidirectional charging, but it has not yet rolled out broadly. Powershare-Powerwall integration, originally promised for late 2024, has been delayed again and is now scheduled for mid-2026. The article notes then-VP of energy Drew Baglino said in March 2023 that Tesla could have bidirectional charging within two years.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitors ahead on V2G/V2H:** Ford's F-150 Lightning has offered vehicle-to-home capability since its 2022 launch and powers a residential V2G pilot with Baltimore Gas and Electric. General Motors has committed to making every new EV bidirectional-capable by 2026, with Ultium platform vehicles already shipping with 19.2 kW hardware. Hyundai Motor Group is running V2G pilots in Korea, Europe, and the US with the Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, Kia EV6, and EV9. Third-party vendor Sigenergy has built a universal bidirectional DC charger compatible with most Tesla vehicles.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the launch "genuinely important" for Tesla Energy and credits it as useful distributed-energy innovation for the fragile ERCOT grid, but argues Tesla should extend the feature across its full lineup and criticizes the repeated delays to Powershare-Powerwall integration, contrasting Tesla's slow rollout with Ford's and GM's more advanced bidirectional charging commitments.
