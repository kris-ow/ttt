This is a casual year-end livestream ("Tesla Boyz") among several Tesla-focused YouTube hosts recapping a brief Elon Musk appearance on a Twitter/X Spaces call, debating Tesla's 2026 outlook, and making informal predictions on Cybercab, FSD, Optimus, Megapack, and TSLA's stock price. The tone is jokey and speculative throughout, but the substantive core is a set of hedged predictions about Tesla's autonomy and robotics ramp and reaction to a widely shared FSD road-trip data point.

## Robotaxi
1. **Musk's 2026 outlook clip:** In a brief Spaces appearance, Elon Musk said he expects "widespread robotaxi" to be a big thing for Tesla in 2026, alongside an Optimus 3 launch and SpaceX achieving full Starship reusability; he also said volume production of Cybercab is targeted for April (following an already-started preliminary/small-scale production run).
2. **NHTSA regulatory ceiling on driverless Cybercabs:** One host clarified that the relevant NHTSA rule allows up to 2,500 autonomous vehicles without steering wheels, pedals, or mirrors per manufacturer as test beds; beyond that, further approval is required. He noted this cap doesn't apply to Model Y-based robotaxis (which retain steering wheels/pedals), so Tesla could scale those without the same constraint, and speculated Tesla might roll out Cybercabs with steering wheels/pedals if the no-controls approval isn't resolved in time, citing a Tesla chairwoman's comment that they'd add steering wheels/pedals if needed.
3. **Host predictions for Cybercab output by end-2026:** Predictions varied widely and were explicitly flagged as guesses: one host said "thousands" of Cybercabs by year-end; another estimated 20,000–30,000 in Cybercab production for the year while suggesting Model Y-based robotaxi volume could exceed 50,000, with Texas alone potentially able to support up to 100,000 robotaxis; a third suggested "high hundreds a day" printing rate by year-end would be optimistic, and expects only a slow, city-by-city scale-out through most of 2026 with meaningful volume not visible until late Q3/Q4.
4. **Ramp seen as logistics-constrained, not manufacturing-constrained:** One host argued (citing a prior conversation, show unspecified) that Cybercab manufacturing capacity won't be the bottleneck since it's designed for high volume; the constraint will be the remote-operator-to-vehicle ratio and city-by-city rollout logistics.
5. **Driverless delivery reality check:** One host noted that as of now Tesla has completed only one driverless customer delivery as a proof of concept, and said he expected to see this cadence ramp up (e.g., weekly or daily) by now — tempering optimism about an instant nationwide robotaxi ramp even if Cybercab volume production starts in April.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **David Moss's cross-country FSD drive:** A Tesla owner (David Moss) has been driving 100% on FSD version 14.2, surpassing 10,000 miles (host estimates now around 11,000+) with zero disengagements, including all parking. He drove North Carolina to Seattle, then Seattle to Tesla's LA Diner, and is now on a second coast-to-coast trip, having gone from the LA Diner to Myrtle Beach, South Carolina in roughly two days and 20 hours. Elon Musk reposted the milestone; Sawyer Merritt also noted the 10,000-mile mark reached around December 30. Moss reportedly said most of his remaining "disengagements" are just impatience in parking lots, and that he's disappointed maps have gotten worse in recent updates.
2. **Host's own FSD anecdotes:** One host described his personal FSD experience handling a construction zone with a flagger waving vehicles through, and noted a newer behavior where the car avoids blocking cross streets at stop lights — behaviors he said even many human drivers don't reliably follow.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus 3 and unit-count predictions:** Hosts largely reiterated Musk's stated plan for an Optimus 3 launch in 2026, with predictions of roughly 5,000–10,000 Optimus units by end of next year (i.e., 2027) per one host, who said the delay to date was due to Tesla needing to build specialized actuators in-house after redesigning the arms.
2. **Debate/bet over "useful work" in 2026:** Two hosts disagreed on whether Optimus will perform genuinely useful factory work (vs. only data collection) in 2026 and made an informal bet: one predicted Optimus would only be used for data collection this year, not real production work; the other bet that at least ~20 Optimus units would be doing clearly useful, non-demo work at a Tesla factory by end of 2026, citing pick-and-place tasks already demonstrated by other robotics companies (naming Digit at an unspecified facility and Figure's reported round-the-clock BMW trial) as evidence the task isn't a major technical hurdle.
3. **Off-the-record engineer color:** One host said he spoke privately with several unnamed Optimus engineers who were reportedly very enthusiastic about undisclosed upcoming capabilities but declined to give specifics beyond saying it would be "mind-blowing."
4. **Possible Optimus 3 skin design:** One host speculated (his own prediction, not attributed to any Tesla source) that Optimus 3 might feature a human-skin-like rubber covering rather than cloth for at least one version, though other hosts pushed back on the idea.

## Energy
1. **Megapack deployment growth prediction:** One host predicted Megapack deployments would double from current quarterly levels within a year, driven by full ramp of Shanghai and the start of shipments from a Texas facility.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi truck production timeline prediction:** The same host said he's hopeful for pilot production of the Tesla Semi in Q1 2026, with volume production beginning in earnest by Q2, and by year-end a daily output of at least 20 units, possibly up to 50.

## Financials
1. **R&D spending prediction:** One host predicted Tesla's R&D spend will roughly double next year, from an estimated $8–10 billion currently to around $18–20 billion, driven by investment in robotaxi, Optimus, AI data centers, and possibly new Megapack factories.
2. **Stock price predictions for end of 2026:** Predictions were informal and hedged: one host reiterated a "date with 500" framing from the prior week and said he still expects the stock to reach $500+ and possibly $600 next year; another said he'd be happy with the stock near $600 (roughly 25% year-over-year gain) by end of 2026, explicitly rejecting more aggressive targets some commentators have floated; a third said he expects the price to be above 500 but below what more bullish observers expect.
3. **Reasons cited for recent stock weakness:** One host attributed a pullback (~10% off all-time highs, described elsewhere as roughly $2.56/~0.56% down on a particular day) to a combination of low holiday trading volume, anticipation of January 2 delivery numbers, uncertainty around ZEV credit accounting in the upcoming earnings report, unmet milestones Musk previously promised for this year, and macro risk factors including a looming government shutdown; he separately attributed a weak November to Michael Burry's short positions and reports that OpenAI had trouble raising private funding, calling this "more of what it was" than any Tesla-specific driver.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo capacity comparison:** In discussing NHTSA's driverless-vehicle test-bed cap, one host noted Waymo plans to grow its fleet by roughly 2,500 units next year and would face the same regulatory cap if scaling further, unlike Tesla's Model Y-based robotaxis which aren't subject to that rule.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Musk's Starship reusability comment:** In the same Spaces clip, Musk said he hopes SpaceX will achieve full reusability with Starship in 2026, describing this along with widespread robotaxi and Optimus 3 as one of the "giant" milestones expected this year.
2. **Moon manufacturing remarks:** Musk speculated about using the Moon to manufacture solar cells and radiators (with heavier items like chips still shipped from Earth), and described using a mass driver to launch large quantities of AI-powered satellites into orbit annually — framed as a longer-term, speculative vision rather than a near-term plan.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Early Starship cargo will be Starlink satellites:** Musk confirmed, in response to a question, that the first major Starship shipments are expected to be Starlink satellites.
