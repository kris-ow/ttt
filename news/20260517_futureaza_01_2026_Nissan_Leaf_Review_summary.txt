This video from FutureAzA features host Brian and Jack from Plaid Auto reviewing the 2026 Nissan Leaf (S Plus trim) during a test drive in the Portland, Oregon area. The core thesis is that the new Leaf — with over 300 miles of range, NACS charging, liquid cooling, and a price just over $30,000 — is poised to become the best price-to-range value in the EV market, succeeding the Chevy Bolt as the go-to affordable EV for budget-conscious buyers.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **2026 Nissan Leaf S Plus specs and pricing:** The vehicle reviewed is the S Plus trim — base model ("S") with the larger battery ("Plus") — priced at $29,900 at Plaid Auto with only 400 miles on the odometer, compared to a new MSRP of approximately $32,500. It offers just over 300 miles of range and comes standard with a 360-degree parking camera and Nissan ProPilot Assist (adaptive cruise with lane centering). The true base trim with the smaller battery (rumored around $30,000) had not yet been released at the time of filming. The car is built at Nissan's Sunderland plant in the UK, which received $450 million in upgrades — a shift from the previous-generation Leaf, which was manufactured in Tennessee.

2. **Charging infrastructure upgrade as a key selling point:** Unlike previous Leaf generations that were limited to CHAdeMO fast charging and air-cooled batteries, the 2026 Leaf features both a J1772 port (Level 1 and Level 2) and a NACS port (all three levels of charging). The battery is liquid-cooled, which Brian and Jack consider essential — earlier Leaf Plus models could do 100 kW DC fast charging but would overheat if done twice in a day, making road trips impractical. The new platform is described as enabling genuine long-distance travel for the first time in the Leaf's history.

3. **Drive experience and powertrain characteristics:** The Leaf is front-wheel drive, which the hosts note produces noticeable torque steer under hard acceleration in sport mode. This is contrasted with rear-wheel drive EVs (Tesla, Hyundai, Kia, Volkswagen ID.4), which are preferred because weight transfers to the rear wheels under acceleration, improving traction especially on wet roads common in Portland. The car offers D and B modes for regenerative braking, plus an "E-Step" mode for more aggressive regen — though it does not bring the vehicle to a complete stop, requiring brake input at low speeds. Drive modes include Sport, Standard, and Eco.

4. **ProPilot Assist driver assistance system:** The base S Plus trim includes ProPilot Assist, which combines adaptive cruise control and lane centering — a hands-on system. Brian notes that higher trim Leaf models are expected to offer ProPilot Assist 2.0, a hands-free system available on pre-mapped roads that also supports prompted automatic lane changes, comparable to Ford Blue Cruise, Chevy Super Cruise, Tesla's FSD, and Nissan's own 2.0 system as the main hands-free offerings in the market.

5. **Value positioning and market trajectory:** Brian predicts the 2026 Leaf will rapidly become his top recommendation for price-to-range ratio, displacing the Chevy Bolt — which the dealership has historically sold in large numbers due to the same buyer profile: wanting maximum range at minimum cost. Used Bolts are now entering the low $10,000 range, sometimes sub-$10,000. Brian expects the Leaf to become an even stronger used car value in approximately three years when lease returns come back to market.

6. **EV used car pricing trends in 2026:** Jack explains that 2026 is the first full year without federal EV incentives, which had suppressed used EV demand and pricing earlier in the year when gas was also cheaper. Rising gas prices are now pushing used EV auction prices and retail prices upward, with trade-in values also increasing as a result. The dealership prefers to take EV trade-ins directly rather than send them to auction where prices are climbing. Electricity costs in the Pacific Northwest are estimated at approximately $0.80/gallon equivalent, rising to around $1.20/gallon equivalent in other parts of the region.

7. **Legacy Leaf reliability and battery degradation:** Jack describes the Leaf as the "cockroach of EVs" — extremely durable for daily city driving despite its reputation as a poor road trip car. The main historical criticism was battery degradation from heat exposure, with the worst cases settling around 50–60% of original range. However, the Leaf never fully failed and remained usable for urban commuters. As a parallel, Brian mentions a recent story of a Model 3 reaching 380,000 miles on its original battery with 67% battery health remaining, and a personal acquaintance with a Model S exceeding 400,000 miles.

## Market & Competition

1. **Lucid service infrastructure challenges:** The dealership recently acquired its first Lucid vehicle, but it is stuck in service mode and cannot be used to demonstrate Dream Drive (Lucid's driver assistance system). The nearest Lucid service center is approximately three hours away in Seattle — illustrative of a broader problem Lucid faces nationally, where sparse service infrastructure undermines buyer confidence, creating a chicken-and-egg problem with sales growth.

2. **Competitive hands-free driver assistance landscape:** Brian identifies four hands-free driver assistance systems he has personally experienced at this dealership: Ford Blue Cruise, Chevy Super Cruise (referred to as "ProPilot" in transcript, likely a misspeak), Tesla's FSD, and Nissan ProPilot Assist 2.0. He notes that Hyundai and Kia do not currently offer a hands-free system in vehicles he deals with, positioning Nissan's 2.0 as a meaningful differentiator in its segment.
