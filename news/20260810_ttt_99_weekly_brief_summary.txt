## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- SpaceX's first quarterly report as a public company: revenue $7.81B (+92% YoY, +66% QoQ), beating the ~$6.81-7B Street estimate; net loss narrowed to $541M, adjusted EBITDA hit $3.5B (+191% YoY), $100B cash, $47.5B backlog. Management guided to $100B+ annualized revenue run rate by December — which Musk clarified is a floor, "what we'd achieve if we basically did nothing" — with internal $1 trillion targets pulled forward from 2031 to 2030, "non-zero chance" in 2029.
- Shares whipsawed on the print — up as much as 10%, down as much as 13.6% across sessions — which multiple hosts called an irrational reaction given the beat; the roughly 900M-share lockup unlock on August 6 then passed without a selloff, with SPCX rallying 5-6%+ that day, read as a bullish bottoming signal with resistance expected near the ~$135 IPO price.
- Terafab, the jointly-branded Tesla-SpaceX chip megafactory in Grimes County, TX, was officially confirmed: $16.8B initial-phase investment (down from Musk's earlier $25B figure), targeting 100M+ sq ft, 3,000+ jobs, and 1+ terawatt/year of compute for Optimus, Cybercab, and space-based data centers. Sources disagreed sharply on total scope — one filing reportedly allows up to $119B across all phases — and Electrek flagged that SpaceX's own S-1 described Terafab as a non-binding "general framework" with no finalized IP split.
- A poll cited by analyst Alexandra Merz found over 50% of 813 respondents now favor an immediate Tesla-SpaceX merger (under 8% opposed) — a reversal from prior weeks — while a competing view (Yashu Chararma) argues no serious shareholder vote lands before mid-2027, citing unresolved Tesla-China/SpaceX-defense questions and calling merger overhang the single biggest factor suppressing TSLA short-term.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

- AI segment revenue tripled sequentially to $2.5-2.6B (+247% YoY), turning EBITDA-positive at ~$1.1-1.15B from a prior loss, on $14.1B of Q2 cloud agreements (Colossus/Colossus 2, Google, Anthropic) plus a further $6.7B signed in early July. Compute capacity reached 1.4GW at quarter-end, guided to exceed 2GW by year-end.
- SpaceX went exclusive with Nvidia (Vera Rubin/Reuben architecture) for a "very significant percentage" of 2027 GPU supply, and unveiled "Star Mind AI1," a satellite compute payload targeting first launches in 2027.
- 2027 AI-revenue estimates diverge enormously across analysts: Goldman Sachs ~$34.5B, Brian Wang's base case $163B (bull $256B), Morgan Stanley not seeing $160B until 2028 — with compute pricing durability and Musk's history of slipping timelines flagged as the key swing factors.
- Musk said memory, not chips, is now the binding constraint on AI growth, with supply growing ~20%/year against 200%+/year demand.

## SpaceX — Starlink

- Starlink added 1.7M net subscribers in Q2 (best quarter yet), reaching 12 million total across 167 markets, with ARPU flat at $66/month and connectivity revenue up 66% YoY to $4.3B (39% operating margin).
- FCC approved the EchoStar spectrum transfer (5MHz to 65MHz), and Starlink Mobile is targeted for launch end of 2027, aimed at the ~$600B/year US wireless carrier market; competing carrier CEOs (T-Mobile) downplayed the threat as "exaggerated."
- Hughes Network Systems (parent EchoStar) filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, citing LEO competition; Viasat has lost 81% of its US fixed-broadband subscribers since 2020.

## SpaceX — Starship

- Musk declared the Starship heat shield problem "solved" based on inspection of the recovered Flight 13 ship, with "no technical obstacles" remaining to full rapid reusability, though current reuse may be limited to 3-10 flights and he cautioned against "jinxing it."
- Flight 14 will attempt the program's first tower catch of the ship and carry the first operational V3 Starlink satellites to orbit, tentatively end of this month. A separate, unconfirmed report suggested SpaceX may have lost the Flight 13 ship during Indian Ocean recovery — unverified, contradicting Musk's own "intact" characterization from the same week.

## Robotaxi

- Zoox began paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas, priced 20-40% above standard ride-hailing, operating under an NHTSA exemption capped at 5,000 vehicles over two years — a deliberate ceiling Tesla is reportedly avoiding by pushing for broader regulatory approval instead.
- Tesla's unsupervised Austin fleet began running limited night hours (until ~9-9:30 PM), still avoiding highways; a Cybercab was spotted with a new flush-mounted Starlink dish, an unconfirmed hardware iteration.
- Samsung began mass production of Cybercab camera modules (LG joining later this year), with combined initial orders reportedly covering 20,000-30,000 vehicles — no production timeline confirmed.
- Bhakdi expects Tesla to hold off a real paid Cybercab service until FSD reaches a safety-validated version, estimated roughly three months out; robotaxi economics remain opaque across Tesla, Waymo, and Zoox alike.

## Electric Vehicles

- Tesla extended Cybertruck PCS (power conversion system) warranty to 8 years/150,000 miles after admitting pre-2026 units "do not meet our reliability standards"; Electrek's July poll found 41% of surveyed Cybertrucks needed a PCS replacement, with out-of-warranty repairs costing $5,000-$7,200. Owners who already paid will be reimbursed.
- Tesla filed for a $10.1B vertically integrated solar cell plant ("Project Crystal Sun") near Houston, targeting 2028 completion and 9,712 permanent jobs — filing-stage, not yet confirmed.
- A report claims Cybertruck retail demand fell from 39,000 to 3,500 units, with SpaceX/xAI purchases making up nearly 20% of Q4 units; hosts broadly agree Cybertruck has underperformed original 50,000-100,000 unit targets, citing pricing near $80,000-$120,000 versus a targeted ~$60,000.
- California's MyFirstEV rebate ($3,500 point-of-sale) exhausted its Tesla allocation within four days of its August 3 launch, signaling strong entry-level demand.

## Autonomous Driving

- FSD (Supervised) crossed 13 billion cumulative miles, up from 12 billion on July 17 — about 1 billion miles in roughly two weeks.
- Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov argued camera-only sensing can match human driving but hits a safety curve that "flattens out way too early" for full driverless reliability, an explicit challenge to Tesla's scaling story.
- Two Chinese auto influencers testing FSD 14 in Los Angeles concluded it now outperforms domestic systems (Huawei ADS, XPeng VLA, Li Auto ADAS); a 1.1M-subscriber review channel separately called FSD "the future," rating it above GM's Super Cruise.
- Scattered reports continue of HW3 computer overheating/failures tied to FSD "v14 Lite," including thermal faults above 90°C; causation remains unconfirmed and the underlying fault predates the update.

## Energy

- Megapack 3 entered production at the new Brookshire, TX Megafactory (16 months groundbreaking-to-production, 50 GWh/year design capacity); each unit holds ~5 MWh (+28% vs. Megapack 2 XL), bundled into "Megablock" units claiming 248 MWh/acre density and 1 GWh deployed in 20 business days.
- SpaceX disclosed $295M of Megapack purchases in Q2 for its datacenters, bringing cumulative spend to over $800M — a direct Tesla Energy revenue line tied to SpaceX's AI buildout.
- Tesla Megapacks went live powering Ørsted's 250MW/500MWh Old 300 Storage battery in Needville, Texas.

## Financials

- Terafab's compute output is expected to split roughly 25% Tesla/Optimus, 75% AI/spacecraft, per Musk — softer toward Optimus than an earlier March framing that had space closer to 80%.
- AI5 (FSD/Optimus inference chip) taped out in spring with volume production targeted 2027; AI6 targets December tape-out with mass production in H2 2027, under Samsung's existing $16.5B supply deal.

## Bear Case of the Week

- SPCX shares fell as much as 13.6% mid-week on Wall Street concern over ballooning AI capex ($15.8B of Q2's $18.3B total), even as headline results beat estimates.
- An unconfirmed report suggests SpaceX may have lost the Flight 13 Starship during Indian Ocean recovery — directly at odds with Musk's own "intact, softest-yet splashdown" characterization from earlier in the week; the discrepancy is unresolved.
- Cybertruck retail demand reportedly collapsed from 39,000 to 3,500 units, with SpaceX/xAI purchases propping up nearly 20% of Q4 volume — a signal underlying consumer demand has weakened well below original targets.
- Tesla's own admission that pre-2026 Cybertruck PCS electronics "do not meet our reliability standards" follows sustained owner and press pressure, with Electrek noting Tesla acted only after the fact rather than proactively.
- Terafab's disclosed scope is internally inconsistent — a $16.8B phase-one figure from Tesla against separately reported numbers ranging as high as $119B — with SpaceX's own S-1 previously describing the arrangement as a non-binding "general framework."
