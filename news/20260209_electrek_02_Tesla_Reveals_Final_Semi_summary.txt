Tesla updated its official Semi website with final production specs for two trims — Standard Range and Long Range — as customer deliveries and volume production approach in 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Standard Range specs:** 325 miles of range at 82,000 lb gross combination weight, curb weight under 20,000 lbs, 1.7 kWh/mile efficiency, three independent rear-axle motors, 800 kW drive power, MCS 3.2 charging support with 60% charge in 30 minutes, and electric Power Take Off (ePTO) up to 25 kW.

2. **Long Range specs:** 500 miles of range, 23,000 lb curb weight, same 1.7 kWh/mile efficiency and 800 kW drive power, 1.2 MW peak charging speed, MCS 3.2 support, 60% charge in 30 minutes, and ePTO up to 25 kW. The 1.2 MW peak charging figure matches a demonstration Tesla showed in a video late last year.

3. **"Designed for autonomy" framing:** Tesla describes both variants as designed for autonomy, which the article says signals intent to eventually deploy the Semi in an autonomous freight network — something Tesla had previously not mentioned in connection with the Semi.

4. **Production timeline and pricing:** Production was pushed into 2026 while Tesla installed equipment at its Nevada factory, which the company said in April was on track for volume production in H1 2026. Pricing has not been updated from the original 2017 figures ($150,000 for 300-mile, $180,000 for 500-mile range), and the article notes a previously reported claim from a Tesla Semi customer that pricing had seen a "dramatic increase."

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the 325-mile Standard Range is the more practically useful trim for most fleets due to higher payload capacity, while the Long Range trades about 3,000 lbs of payload for extended range. He calculates the 1.7 kWh/mile efficiency implies roughly $0.17/mile energy cost versus $0.50-0.70/mile for diesel, suggesting substantial fuel savings over a truck's operating life, and frames pricing as the key remaining unknown for Tesla's push into the trucking industry.
