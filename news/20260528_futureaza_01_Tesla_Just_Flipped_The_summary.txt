This video from FutureAzA features host Brian and co-host Herbert discussing Tesla's resurgent EV sales in Europe and emerging markets like Colombia and Chile, while also exploring the broader strategic question of whether traditional vehicle sales still matter given Tesla's pivot toward robotaxi and autonomous driving. The core thesis is that Tesla's 13-month European sales losing streak has ended, the rebound is real and accelerating, and the long-term value of selling cars today lies less in unit revenue and more in building the FSD-enabled fleet and autonomous vehicle ecosystem.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Colombia EV Market Surges:** Colombia's BEV market share reached nearly 20% in April 2026, up from 7.4% a year prior, representing over 300% growth year-over-year in EV sales. The hosts attribute early growth (2022–2025) to organic adoption from pent-up demand, while the more recent spike is linked to rising gas prices. Tesla responded by expanding its Supercharger network in Colombia, signaling confidence in continued growth. Total market volume remains small — estimated around 5,000 cars — but the trend is notable.

2. **Chile: Small Market, Meaningful Share:** Roland Percher's data shows Tesla reported 242 sales in Chile in April, capturing 16% of the BEV market, while overall BEV penetration in Chile sits at 5%. The hosts highlight this as an example of Tesla entering smaller markets that legacy automakers typically bypass due to cost and complexity.

3. **Europe Sales Rebound Is Real:** Tesla ended a 13-month European sales losing streak, with 13,740 cars sold across the EU, UK, and European Free Trade Association in the most recently reported month — a 29% increase year-over-year. Weekly data for May 18–24 showed 3,600 sales across tracked European countries, up 32% week-over-week and 67% versus the same week last year. Year-to-date European sales are up approximately 28,000 units, or 45.8%, from the prior year. France and Spain are reportedly at record highs. The hosts attribute the prior-year decline primarily to factory retooling for the refreshed Model Y, not demand destruction.

4. **Third Consecutive Month of European Growth:** According to a report referenced from ACEA (described as "APAC/AA"), Tesla has now posted three consecutive months of year-over-year growth across European markets, with April up 47% compared to April of the prior year. The tracked countries include the UK, Norway, Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, Czech Republic, and Iceland — representing approximately 60% of European Tesla sales.

5. **Norway Standout:** The hosts note Tesla sold approximately 400 units in Norway in a single day, describing it as exceptional for a small country and a sign of strong enthusiasm for the brand there.

6. **Legacy Automakers Losing Ground:** Volkswagen and Stellantis are each reportedly reducing total production capacity by approximately one million cars. The hosts argue that brand loyalty, historically bulletproof for European automakers, is eroding as consumers shift toward technology-first vehicles. BYD is also expanding dealer presence in Europe with aggressive pricing and a reported 185% sales increase, yet legacy OEMs are still the ones losing the most ground.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 13.3 Reduces Nag Frequency:** The hosts discuss a version of FSD (described as version 13.3, with the key change in the point-three update over point-two being reduced driver attention prompts). Users are reportedly driving for extended periods — one user cited over an hour — without receiving an attention nag. Herbert emphasizes this is supervised FSD and recommends all Tesla owners enable it, while both hosts caution against distracted driving.

2. **FSD Approval in Europe as a Demand Catalyst:** FSD has reportedly been approved in the Netherlands and is in test trials across multiple European countries. The hosts speculate that EU-wide approval across all 28 member states is possible and frame it as a significant future demand driver. Herbert argues that once consumers experience what FSD can do, it becomes a compelling purchase reason — one that other EVs cannot match.

3. **FSD Revenue as the Real Value of Current Sales:** Brian argues that the primary value of selling Model 3s and Model Ys today is generating FSD subscription or purchase revenue, rather than the vehicle margin itself. The hosts discuss how Tesla's strategic decision to cancel the Model 2 and prioritize the Cybercab — as described in the Walter Isaacson book — reflects a deliberate shift away from maximizing traditional car sales volume.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Strategy Over Model 2:** The hosts recount the internal Tesla decision, sourced from the Walter Isaacson biography, in which Elon Musk chose to build the Cybercab robotaxi-first vehicle instead of a sub-$30,000 Model 2. Executives reportedly convinced him to design the Cybercab so it could function as a conventional car if FSD was not ready in time. This is framed as a clear signal that Tesla's corporate focus has fundamentally shifted away from traditional volume car sales.

2. **Customer-Owned Vehicles in the Robotaxi Network — Uncertain Future:** Brian raises Elon Musk's repeated promises that Model 3 and Model Y owners would be able to add their vehicles to Tesla's robotaxi network. He notes this model makes business sense for handling peak demand (analogous to Uber surge), but expresses concern that Tesla may be moving away from this plan. Herbert shares the concern, noting that Austin-deployed robotaxis appear to have hardware additions — such as camera washers and Starlink Mini — that existing consumer vehicles lack, which could disqualify them from network participation.

3. **Cybercab Revenue Superiority Over Consumer Vehicle Sales:** The hosts estimate that Tesla earns approximately $4,000–$5,000 profit selling a Model Y, versus potentially $25,000 per year in revenue by keeping one Cybercab in a robotaxi fleet. Brian initially suggests one Cybercab equals five consumer cars in revenue terms; Herbert revises this to three to four, accounting for the concentration of driving hours when roads are actually used. Both agree the Cybercab economics are dramatically superior to consumer vehicle sales.

## Market & Competition

1. **BYD Growing in Europe But Not Dominating:** Despite a 185% sales increase in Europe, BYD is described as not yet selling as much as it wants to in the region. The hosts note that Chinese EV brands broadly have made inroads but have not captured the market share they targeted, leaving room for Tesla's rebound.

2. **Media Coverage Criticized:** The hosts mock an Automotive News story framing the Dacia Sandero reclaiming Europe's monthly sales lead in April as Tesla "tumbling," calling it a failure to understand delivery wave dynamics at the start of a quarter. They also critique an Investor's Business Daily article suggesting investors should wait for Tesla stock to reach a buy point before purchasing, calling it a misunderstanding of how to evaluate a long-term thesis stock.
