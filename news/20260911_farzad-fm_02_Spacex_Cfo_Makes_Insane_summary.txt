This is an interview with SpaceX's CFO (Bryan/Brett, per the conference host's address) covering the company's vertical-integration strategy across rockets, Starlink, and AI infrastructure, with new disclosures on terrestrial/orbital compute economics, a fresh hosting deal, Starship's flight 13 recovery learnings, and Starlink's broadband and mobile roadmap. The core thesis: reusable rocketry (Starship) is the foundational asset enabling SpaceX to underpin AI compute, broadband, and mobile businesses at a cost structure no competitor can match.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 13 delivered key recovery data:** SpaceX's Starship flight 13 delivered demonstration V3 satellite payloads, performed a Raptor engine relight, and achieved what the CFO called the softest, most precise splashdown of the second stage over the ocean; the recovery team towed the vehicle back to shore, letting engineers inspect the heat shield's performance directly, with learnings already being applied to the next flight.
2. **Flight 14 will be the first revenue-generating flight:** Flight 14, planned for later this month, will carry production V3 Starlink satellites rather than demonstration payloads, marking SpaceX's shift from Starship test flights to production cadence; beyond flight 14, the company is targeting a shot at recovering both first and second stages later this year.
3. **Booster reuse track record:** SpaceX has been reflying Falcon 9 first-stage boosters since the year after its first recovery in December 2015, with over 500 rocket boosters reflown to date; the CFO says once Starship reaches routine reuse of both stages (hoped for as soon as next year), its cost curve will fall dramatically, unlike terrestrial infrastructure costs, which are rising with inflation.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Orbital compute targeted for next year:** SpaceX plans to fly its first orbital compute satellites next year, using largely the same V3 satellite bus as Starlink but with larger solar arrays; the CFO argues that while much of the AI industry agrees orbital compute is the future, most players think it is many years away, whereas SpaceX believes its vertical integration lets it hit meaningful scale (tens of gigawatts) by 2028, on a shorter timeline than competitors expect. Cost parity with terrestrial compute, and eventually favorable cost curves via reusability and satellite cost declines, could arrive as soon as next year, per the CFO.
2. **New hosting deal pushes 2026 ARR target higher:** SpaceX previously guided to roughly $100 billion annualized run-rate (ARR) by year-end 2026 (based on annualizing its December terrestrial compute number). The CFO says the company closed an additional hosting deal earlier this month worth about $1.11 billion/month starting December 1, 2026 — roughly $13 billion of incremental ARR — giving added conviction in the $100B ARR target.
3. **Gigawatt deployment guidance:** SpaceX ended this year with a little over 2 gigawatts of terrestrial compute deployed and is guiding to 5–10 gigawatts deployed next year, which the CFO attributes to vertical integration giving line-of-sight into power supply — a constraint he says Musk flagged as early as last year or the year before and that is now playing out industry-wide.
4. **Nvidia exclusivity and compute deal structure:** SpaceX has an exclusive relationship with Nvidia for compute allocation. Its compute-hosting deals (including the one just closed) are structured as roughly six-month commitments (90-day terms with a 90-day out), which the CFO says preserves flexibility to redirect compute to SpaceX's own AI products (Grok/Grokbot) rather than being locked into giving away capacity long-term.
5. **Monetization range and Grok product momentum:** On the earnings call, SpaceX guided to $30–$50 per unit of compute monetization next year and said it is tracking at or near the high end of that range. The CFO cites the recent acquisition of Cursor, the viral reception of Grokbot, and the jump from Grok 4.5 to Grok 4.6 (with 4.7 coming soon) as evidence of improving product cycles; he says SpaceX has strong compute relationships with Anthropic and Google as customers.
6. **Capex framing — "not all capital is the same":** The CFO argues GPU-based compute capex carries less-than-one-year payback and can be resold/refinanced as an asset class (unlike decades-long infrastructure like launch towers), reducing investment risk; he says demand for compute deals is coming from nearly every major AI player.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Enterprise and mobility expansion:** Starlink has moved from a consumer-focused "better than nothing" service (as it was described in 2020–2021) to enterprise-grade offerings with multi-nines uptime and SLAs. The CFO says three of the four largest airlines are now Starlink customers (declining to name the holdout), alongside a large aircraft-terminal production backlog, plus growing adoption on cruise ships, yachts, and trains; Starlink broadband is now live in over 170 countries.
2. **Direct-to-device mobile roadmap:** SpaceX plans to fly next-generation satellites next year (reusing the V3 bus) to build out full 5G-quality direct-to-device service, targeting service launch in the first half of 2028 in the US. Its current version-one direct-to-cell service (used by partners like T-Mobile, referred to as "T-Satellite") offers texting and light voice only. SpaceX has acquired mid-band spectrum from EchoStar and has both direct-from-space and terrestrial FCC-approved capability, and flew roughly 600 version-one satellites within about a year.
3. **AI/robotics connectivity as a new demand driver:** The CFO frames connectivity for humanoid robots, autonomous vehicles, and flying vehicles as a major emerging demand source for Starlink, arguing terrestrial networks cannot meet the always-on connectivity needs of physical AI devices.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Capital allocation philosophy:** The CFO (in the role for 15 years) says SpaceX has historically prioritized organic growth but made its first notable M&A this year (the Cursor acquisition) to accelerate AI product timelines; he frames Starship, terrestrial compute, and orbital compute as the three priority investment areas, with orbital compute still the most underappreciated by the market in terms of near-term feasibility.
