This cernbasher podcast episode (hosts discussing Tesla and SpaceX developments) covers accelerating Tesla Robotaxi fleet growth in Texas with new mile-tracking data tools, a detailed breakdown of SpaceX's Starlink constellation growth and its $40B Nvidia GPU financing deal, and an extended critique of Anthropic's IPO prospectus ahead of its public listing.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab fleet overtakes Model Y in Texas:** One host reports the Robotaxi fleet in Texas now has more Cybercabs than Model Ys — 573 Cybercabs versus 507 Model Ys, with 110 Cybercabs added in a single day — and speculates the eventual ratio could reach 5-6x or even 9:1 given ridership patterns.
2. **Nearing Waymo's Texas fleet size:** The host says Tesla is close to surpassing Waymo's Texas fleet (just over 1,200 cars), with Tesla's robotaxi count near 1,100 and possibly already larger within Austin specifically since Waymo spreads across multiple Texas cities; he suggests Tesla could pass Waymo's statewide total as soon as the following week.
3. **F1 weekend pricing test:** During the Austin F1 race weekend, a host compared ride prices for the same 19-mile trip: a Model Y Robotaxi at $78 (4-minute wait), a Cybercab at $76 (40-minute wait), versus Uber ranging from $112–$172 (roughly $6–$9/mile) under surge pricing — making Tesla's robotaxi over 50% cheaper in some cases. Hosts frame this as a successful real-world test of Tesla's dynamic/surge pricing at a high-demand event.
4. **New independent mile-tracking tool built on Tesla's own data:** A host built a live dashboard (hosted on his NetP site, built with Grok) pulling Tesla's own published Robotaxi/FSD mile-count data, showing that Tesla's self-reported counter is a periodically-adjusted compounding rate rather than a true real-time feed. As of the tool's start on October 24, the company's stated supervised-mile rate was 244 million miles/week, which Tesla raised to 316 million miles/week by October 26 (~30% jump); unsupervised (Robotaxi) miles were separately tracked at 557,000/week and represented a mere 0.023% of total FSD miles.
5. **Week-over-week Robotaxi mile growth accelerating:** Using Tesla's own disclosed mile milestones (380,000 miles on July 22; 1 million miles by September 3 — a 17.1% weekly growth rate; 1.875 million by end of Q3 — 17.7% weekly; 3.3 million by the Tesla earnings call three weeks later — 20.7% weekly), a host shows the week-over-week growth rate itself has been accelerating.
6. **Fleet-size projections based on 17% weekly growth:** Using a 100-miles/vehicle/day assumption and the ~17% weekly growth rate, a host's model estimates the fleet needs roughly 723 vehicles today (versus ~180 actually in Texas, implying Tesla is ahead of near-term demand), growing to ~3,000 vehicles and ~14 million cumulative miles by year-end, ~23,000 vehicles by Q3, ~176,000 by Q2 next year, ~1.35 million by Q3 next year, and ~2.5 million vehicles by this time next year — which at an assumed per-mile revenue would imply roughly $230 billion in annualized Robotaxi revenue; the host stresses this assumes continued 17% compounding, which he does not expect to hold exactly.
7. **Cybercab production running below capacity:** A host cites Lars Moravy's comment that the Robotaxi fleet "can't use as many vehicles as they can make right now," so Tesla is deliberately throttling the Cybercab production line — running it, then pausing it — until fleet demand catches up.
8. **FSD v15 described as the software breakthrough; unified mapping system in development:** A host credits FSD version 15 as the breakthrough that resolved software as the limiting factor earlier this year; separately, Ashok Elluswamy described on the Tesla earnings call (per the hosts) a new unified mapping system merging navigation and the FSD autonomy stack into a single AI-driven system with real-time fleet learnings, rather than two separate systems as today.
9. **Voice-control-for-routing feature expected soon:** Hosts discuss that Tesla (per Ashok Elluswamy) indicated upcoming functionality to let riders talk to the car/Grok in real time to adjust routing (e.g., "pull over here," "take this exit"), though they note this wasn't explicitly tied to a chip generation and note current voice interaction with Grok in the car is cloud-based with latency/connectivity tradeoffs.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **$40B GPU financing deal:** SpaceX secured $40 billion in financing — described as the largest GPU financing deal ever — managed by Apollo Global Management to buy Nvidia chips, with financing partners including Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, and KKR; the host notes the capital is reportedly recovered in as little as six months in some cases over a 5-year term.
2. **Anthropic compute spend with SpaceX rising sharply:** Per the host, Anthropic increased its monthly compute spend commitment with SpaceX from $1.2 billion to $3.1 billion, securing compute through 2029 starting around December 2026, with either party able to terminate with 90 days' notice after an initial contract period ending in early 2027 (a termination right the host says Musk, not Anthropic, had requested).
3. **SpaceX's projected December compute revenue mix:** The host's breakdown of SpaceX's expected monthly compute revenue run rate at end of December shows Anthropic at 37%, an undisclosed customer at 13% ($1.1B/month), Alphabet at ~11% (almost $1B/month), Reflection AI at ~2%, and roughly 37% ($3B/month) from still-unannounced customers.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Constellation scale:** Per a host's independent tracking tool, Starlink has 11,161 active satellites across 423 launches since 2019, with 916 terabits of total network downlink capacity and 5.2 terabits added in the current month alone.
2. **Single Starship launch added more capacity than the entire rest of the industry combined:** A Starlink V3 satellite group launched on a Starship flight (described as only half-full) added 26 terabits of downstream capacity in one launch — about 2.8% of total system capacity — which the hosts say exceeds the combined total capacity of all competing satellite internet providers (excluding Starlink); a full Starship load could add roughly 5% of total capacity per launch.
3. **Starlink availability gap in India:** The hosts note one Starlink connection detected near the India border on the tracking tool despite general unavailability of the service in India, and note no active connections yet detected in China, Greenland, or Antarctica.
4. **AT&T downplays LEO competition while lobbying against it:** The hosts cite an AT&T statement claiming competitors "cannot and will not match" its network performance, framing it as dismissive of Starlink/LEO threats, while noting telecom incumbents are also lobbying the FCC to slow SpaceX's progress — which the hosts view as inconsistent with public confidence.
5. **Musk's timeline claim on Starlink buildout:** A host notes Elon Musk responded to a claim that a build would take five or six years by saying it would take two years instead (specific claim/context not detailed in transcript).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Elon Musk's SpaceX share gift to children via Trump accounts:** The host reports Musk's plan to give one SpaceX share to every child in America starting "next year" via 530A ("Trump") accounts, noting Musk is currently locked up (until around mid-2027) which explains the delayed timing; Musk's post reportedly says "this will be the first of many."

## Market & Competition
1. **Anthropic IPO prospectus detailed by host — growth and risk factors:** A host's article on Anthropic's prospectus highlights: a 900% net revenue retention rate (existing customers spending ~9x after a year); customers paying over $100,000/year growing from 20 (Dec 2023) to 200 (Dec 2024) to 1,500 (Dec 2025) to almost 12,000 (nine months later); 80% of Q3 revenue coming from models released in the prior six months (a concentration risk the host flags, especially given Musk's stated ambition for Grok to be on par with or better than competitors by around April); nearly half of Anthropic's revenue passing through competitor cloud marketplaces (Amazon, Google, Microsoft), with those marketplaces accounting for 58% of net receivables; and 5–7% of revenue in the first nine months coming from customers later found violating usage policies.
2. **Unusual governance structure ahead of IPO:** Per the host, immediately before the IPO Anthropic will issue one Class F share to an entity controlled by its seven co-founders, carrying 50.1% of voting power and thus control of the company; separate Class E employee shares carry no dividend or transfer rights and are forfeited if an employee leaves for a competitor; two irrevocable proxy agreements let Anthropic's CEO vote shares donated to named charitable entities. The hosts call this governance structure unprecedented and flag unresolved risk of internal disagreement among the seven co-founders.
3. **Disclosed AI safety and legal risk factors:** Per the host's reading of the prospectus, Anthropic disclosed its models "might blackmail, manipulate, or resist shutdown" in controlled testing, warned of potential liabilities from an AI-related catastrophe "beyond the capacity... of the insurance industry," is under FTC civil investigative demand (not a finding of wrongdoing) regarding consumer protection and data practices, and had a prior 2026 dispute with the US government over export restrictions (since lifted) on unspecified AI models.
4. **Dilution mechanics disclosed:** The host notes Anthropic allocated roughly 850 million non-voting shares (5% of fully diluted count) to a public benefit fund, with another 2% potentially added each time the share price rises 50%; plus stock plan reserves that could add up to 7% dilution annually across compensation, purchase plans, and charitable-donation matching (including a 3-for-1 company match for some pre-2024 employees).
