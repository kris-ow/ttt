A Swedish used-EV retailer's analysis of nearly 10,000 real-world battery health tests found that the CATL LFP version of the Tesla Model 3 retains more capacity over high mileage than nickel-based versions of the same car, challenging the assumption that pricier nickel chemistry is more durable.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **LFP Model 3 outperforms nickel versions on battery health:** Carla, a Swedish used-EV retailer, analyzed 9,954 battery tests conducted in Sweden between 2022 and 2026 using AVILOO diagnostics. Among Model 3s driven over 62,000 miles (100,000 km), the CATL LFP pack averaged 93.3% battery health, outperforming every nickel-based version, with a roughly five-point gap between the best (LFP) and worst (Panasonic NCA) packs.
2. **Chemistry explains the gap:** LFP batteries are cheaper, heavier per kWh, and lower in energy density but more thermally stable, and can tolerate full 100% charging better than nickel-based cells, which Tesla recommends charging to only 80-90% for daily use — a difference the article says compounds over high mileage. The finding aligns with a Tesla-funded study and independent teardowns previously showing LFP ages more gracefully.
3. **Context on Tesla's LFP shift:** Tesla moved its Standard Range Model 3 and Model Y to LFP primarily to cut costs and reduce nickel use; the article notes this data suggests owners also gained a longevity benefit as a byproduct.
4. **Broader ranking and corroborating data:** In Carla's ranking of the 20 EV models with highest average battery health past 62,000 miles, the Kia e-Niro and Hyundai Kona (sharing a 64 kWh pack) led at over 97%, with every top-20 model averaging above 91%. Separately, fleet-data firm Geotab, using data from more than 22,700 vehicles, found average annual degradation improved to about 1.8% per year, suggesting EV batteries could last 20 years or more. Tesla has reported its own Model 3 and Model Y Long Range packs lose roughly 15% of capacity after 200,000 miles, with most loss occurring early before the curve flattens.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the data overturns the perception of LFP as a "budget" chemistry, framing it instead as the more durable option, and says this matters for used-EV buyers since two identical-mileage Model 3s could differ by five points of battery health with no way to tell from the outside which pack is inside.
