This video from the "electrified" channel covers a wide range of Tesla news in the days leading up to Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call, with particular focus on the robotaxi rollout in Houston and Dallas, the Cybercab's regulatory status, Optimus patent updates, and several ancillary topics including energy products, competitive landscape, and government fleet sales. The host's core argument is that while Tesla's decision to launch unsupervised robotaxi service in brand-new cities is a genuinely historic milestone, the extremely limited vehicle availability and early operational hiccups raise legitimate questions about timing and execution. The video balances honest criticism of Tesla's near-term rollout decisions with long-term optimism about the trajectory of the autonomous driving thesis.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised Launch in Houston and Dallas is a Major Milestone:** Tesla has launched unsupervised robotaxi service in both Houston and Dallas, which the host describes as a dramatic and significant departure from prior expectations. As recently as Q4 2025, Elon Musk explicitly stated that Tesla would deploy safety drivers or safety occupants in any new robotaxi market as a cautious approach, noting that "all it takes is one in 10,000 trips to go wrong and you've got an issue." Austin had supervised operation for seven months before going unsupervised. The fact that Houston and Dallas launched without supervisors from day one signals a major leap in Tesla's confidence in its software, and the host emphasizes this is the most important takeaway from the entire rollout.

2. **Extremely Limited Fleet Size Raises Questions About Timing:** Despite the headline launch, the host notes that as of recording, Tesla appears to have only two unsupervised vehicles active in each of Houston and Dallas. The host draws a parallel to Tesla's behavior ahead of the Q4 2025 earnings call, when Tesla announced unsupervised rides in Austin for the first time just days before that January 28th call — also starting very small. The concern is that launching with such minimal availability ahead of an earnings call could sour first-time users who download the app and cannot get a ride, particularly non-Tesla-enthusiasts who may not understand the context of a soft launch.

3. **Operational Hiccups Documented by Texas Tesla:** The host highlights several real-world incidents documented by a Tesla-supportive content creator called Texas Tesla during the Dallas launch. These include: the robotaxi ending up on the highway when it was not supposed to be there (the screen displayed a message that the vehicle was finding a safe location to pull over, and it eventually exited and rerouted safely); the car being in a left-turn-only lane with the right turn signal on and briefly going the wrong way before correcting; the car refusing to turn right on red despite having the opportunity; and the in-car screen freezing and displaying a "please exit safely" message while the vehicle was traveling at 40 mph, leaving the passenger unable to interact with any features. The host stresses none of these were safety-critical but flags them as significant customer experience problems, especially for first-time riders.

4. **App and Service Consistency Issues:** Additional inconsistencies noted include: the app showing a 14-minute ETA while the real-time widget showed 24 minutes; conflicting information about hours of operation (official sources say 6 a.m. to 8 p.m., but some Houston users report the service not opening until 9 a.m.); and reports of the service being suspended due to rain in Houston, while simultaneously another user shared video of an unsupervised vehicle operating in the rain. The host attributes much of this to the very early-stage nature of the rollout.

5. **Tesla Pricing Significantly Undercuts Waymo:** Pricing data from multiple receipts shared by Sawyer Merritt shows Tesla robotaxi fares dramatically below Waymo's. In one comparison, a Tesla ride cost $6.15 versus a comparable Waymo ride at $13.93 — less than half the price. Another data point showed Tesla at $1.67 per mile versus Waymo at $5.41 per mile for comparable trips. The host notes this pricing advantage is one of Tesla's biggest competitive strengths, but stresses that availability needs to match.

6. **Geofence Comparison with Waymo:** In Dallas, Waymo's geofence covers 48 square miles versus Tesla's current 31 square miles, with significant overlap between the two. In Houston, Waymo's geofence is 23 square miles and Tesla's is 25 square miles with no overlap. The host notes that Tesla's Houston geofence is currently more suburban and easier driving terrain compared to Waymo's more urban, dense downtown coverage area with cyclists and pedestrians.

7. **Elon's Prior Fleet Guidance Not Met:** In Q1 2026, Elon stated Tesla had "well over 500" taxi vehicles carrying paid customers between the Bay Area and Austin, and projected this would "probably double every month — it's on an exponential curve." By that projection, the fleet should have reached roughly 1,000 in February, 2,000 in March, and 4,000 in April. The host notes that even the "all tracked" figure — which is not the count of active vehicles — sits at only 611, far below the exponential trajectory Elon described. The host uses this to caution against placing high confidence in specific short-term guidance from Elon.

8. **Delivery Center Vehicles Spotted:** At a Houston delivery center, approximately 30 Tesla Model Y vehicles were spotted, but only three had the robotaxi badge. The host raises the question of whether the remaining vehicles will eventually be badged and deployed, or whether they will not enter the robotaxi fleet at all.

9. **Austin Scaling Still Uncertain:** The host notes that whether Tesla will also remove safety monitors in Austin and scale all three cities simultaneously or sequentially will be a key signal in the coming weeks. A scenario where 30 unsupervised vehicles are operating in both Dallas and Houston by mid-May, alongside Austin scaling, would be the bullish outcome. Continued stagnation would re-raise the question of whether the launches were primarily for earnings optics.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Cybercab Regulatory Status — No 2,500-Unit Cap Expected:** There has been significant debate about whether the Cybercab is subject to a 2,500-unit annual production cap under FMVSS Part 555 exemption rules. The host argues clearly that this cap does not apply. Because Tesla has self-certified the Cybercab — as evidenced by a sticker on a Cybercab at a pop-up event reading "This vehicle conforms to all applicable US FMVSS in effect on the date of manufacture shown above" — Tesla is asserting the Cybercab meets all Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards without needing a Part 555 exemption. The 2,500-unit cap only applies when a manufacturer uses that exemption process. The host notes that a 2022 Federal Register ruling amended occupant protection FMVSS to account for vehicles without traditional manual controls, including steering wheels, which supports Tesla's self-certification position. NHTSA retains the ability to later challenge Tesla's interpretation, but for now the cap is not in play.

2. **FSD Tested in Amsterdam — Impressive Edge Case Handling:** A video shared by Steven Peters shows supervised FSD navigating complex streets in Amsterdam, handling bike lanes, obstructed sightlines, pedestrians, and other edge cases described by passengers as a "wipeout challenge" or "crazy adventure course." The host notes that this kind of international data is feeding back into FSD training and finds the performance encouraging.

3. **FSD Sign Recognition Still Not Perfect:** Spencer and David returned to test FSD version 14.3.1 in a parking garage where a prior version (14.3) had ignored a "Do Not Enter" sign, and confirmed the issue has still not been fixed in the newer version.

4. **Netherlands RDW Presentation Extended to One Hour:** An upcoming RDW (Netherlands vehicle authority) presentation on May 5th, initially scheduled for 20 minutes, has been extended to one hour and moved to after lunch. The presentation covers Article 39, related to FSD/autonomous vehicle regulation in the EU. The host does not expect a formal vote at this meeting — still anticipating the June 30th meeting — but acknowledges the extension cracks the door open for a possible earlier vote.

5. **Spain FSD Testing Data Shared:** Spain's regulatory response to a supervised FSD approval inquiry was broadly positive. Tesla has been testing 30 FSD-equipped vehicles in Spain with approximately 80,000 kilometers driven since November 2025, with zero reported incidents. Spain appears to be waiting for the broader EU/RDW vote before acting.

6. **Giga Shanghai and FSD Approval in China:** Elon, responding to Ford CEO Jim Farley's comments about Tesla lacking updated vehicles, stated that supervised FSD approval in China is the limiting factor for Shanghai, and that production output is at or near maximum capacity. Giga Shanghai's listed capacity is 950,000 units annually (approximately 80,000/month). The host notes this raises questions about what happens when FSD is approved in China and Tesla begins global Cybercab production — pointing to the likelihood of Giga Shanghai expansion plans or rumors emerging over the next year, especially given reports that Giga Shanghai may also produce Optimus.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Rolling Contact Finger Joint Patent Design Changed:** Elon Musk replied to a viral video about a recent Optimus patent covering rolling contact finger joints, stating "We already changed the design. This one did not actually work." The host takes Elon largely at face value on this, acknowledging the patent's original filing date means some changes were likely. However, the host believes the core innovations of the patent — specifically moving actuators from the hand into the forearm and the cable/tendon wiring design that enables this — are likely still intact. The host also raises the possibility (without endorsing it) that Elon could be intentionally misleading competitors. The host notes that even if an Optimus V3 demo occurs in the coming weeks, the hands will likely be covered with gloves or a covering to conceal design details.

## Energy

1. **Three-Phase Powerwall Launching in Europe This Week:** Tesla is rolling out a three-phase Powerwall in Europe this week. While Powerwall 3 has already been available in parts of Europe including the UK and Germany, it has only been single-phase. Many European residential systems use three-phase power, which distributes load across three separate currents. A single-phase Powerwall can operate in a three-phase environment but often requires multiple units or complex installation setups. The native three-phase Powerwall is expected to unlock broader EU adoption by eliminating this mismatch, which has been a constraint despite strong consumer demand.

2. **Cybertruck and Powerwall Approved for PG&E Vehicle-to-Everything Program:** PG&E and Tesla announced that the Cybertruck, Tesla Powerwall gateway, and universal wall connector have been approved for PG&E's residential vehicle-to-everything (V2X) program in California. This is the first AC vehicle-to-grid application approved for California customers, which is significant because it allows the Cybertruck to send AC power directly to the home without requiring expensive DC-to-AC conversion hardware. Ford's F-150 Lightning was part of a prior pilot but used a DC-based system requiring costly external inverters. Eligible customers can earn money selling power back to the grid during events and may receive up to $4,500 in incentives. The host notes that Powerwall home backup integration for existing Powerwall customers is still pending an OTA update.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Giga Shanghai Near Maximum Capacity:** Giga Shanghai has been operating near its listed maximum capacity of approximately 950,000 units per year (about 80,000/month). Following the new vehicle changeover ramp-up, production has effectively plateaued at that level for the second half of the prior year. The host flags this as a constraint that will become increasingly relevant as Tesla considers Cybercab global production and potential FSD approval in China.

2. **Government Fleet Sales Agreement via Sourcewell:** Tesla has signed a master purchasing agreement with Sourcewell, a Minnesota-based cooperative purchasing program serving more than 50,000 government, education, and nonprofit entities across the US and sometimes Canada. The agreement covers Model 3, Model Y, and Cybertruck, as well as charging infrastructure including wall connectors, mobile connectors, and Supercharger access, plus fleet management tools, training, and third-party upfitting options. The initial term is four years, expiring November 2029, with extension options. Tesla is offering volume discounts: 2% for 6–15 deliveries, 3% for 16–50 deliveries, and 4% for 51 or more deliveries — described as the first publicly known discount structure from Tesla. The host describes the addressable market as significant given that 50,000 public agencies collectively manage hundreds of thousands of vehicles.

3. **Cybertruck V2G Approval in California:** As noted in the Energy section, the Cybertruck's approval for PG&E's V2X program also represents an important product milestone for the vehicle's value proposition in California.

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo Fleet Size and Scaling Reality:** Despite frequent new city launch announcements, Waymo's total fleet size is estimated at approximately 3,000 vehicles, with the most recent public guidance targeting around 3,500 units by year-end 2026. The host points out that Waymo typically starts each new city with only 10–20 vehicles and scales gradually, meaning the headline expansion pace overstates the actual fleet growth rate. The host anticipates Tesla will eventually surpass Waymo's fleet size, estimating this could happen later in 2026 or in the first half of 2027.

2. **Waymo Incident Reported by Former Meta CTO:** The former CTO of Meta publicly described a frightening Waymo ride in which the vehicle suddenly pulled off a highway, stopped short before a major intersection, and began moving erratically — prompting the passenger to exit the vehicle at a red light. The passenger noted it was his first incident across 52 rides and 550 miles and stated he generally loves Waymo, but the host uses this to illustrate that Waymo is not immune to significant incidents, even if the press tends to give Waymo more favorable coverage.

3. **GM Pays $40 Million for Former Tesla Autopilot Director:** GM paid $40 million to hire Sterling Anderson, a former Tesla employee who led the Model X and Autopilot programs (serving as director of Autopilot around 2015) and later co-founded Aurora. Anderson is reportedly among the candidates to succeed GM CEO Mary Barra. The host frames this as evidence that legacy automakers are still struggling to find autonomy answers and continue to look to Tesla's talent pipeline.

4. **Rivian R2 Factory Tornado Damage:** Rivian's R2 factory in Illinois sustained significant structural damage from a tornado. The company said it is too early to assess the impact on R2 production timelines, though no injuries were reported.

5. **Uber Deepens Lucid Investment for Robotaxi Plans:** Uber announced an additional $200 million equity investment in Lucid, bringing total commitment to $500 million and raising Uber's stake to 11.5% (valued at approximately $255 million). Uber also increased its vehicle commitment to at least 35,000 Lucid Gravity SUVs over six years, up from the original 20,000. The planned commercial robotaxi service — using Lucid Gravity fitted with Nuro's Level 4 autonomous stack — remains targeted for launch in San Francisco later in 2026. Nuro recently opened test rides to Uber employees in the Bay Area with safety drivers. The host expresses skepticism, saying he'll believe it when he sees it.

6. **Reuters Tax Story — No Legal Violations Found:** Reuters published an investigation claiming Tesla saved approximately $400 million in taxes over recent years through offshore tax strategies. However, buried in the article is a disclosure that Reuters found no indication Tesla's tax practices violate any laws. The host dismisses the framing as misleading and argues Tesla has a fiduciary duty to minimize tax liability legally.

7. **Tesla Wrongful Death Lawsuit Settled:** A wrongful death lawsuit involving a teenager who died in a crash at 116 mph in a 25 mph zone — with allegations that a Tesla technician had without authorization disabled speed-limiting software — was settled before trial. Terms are sealed. The host notes the settlement closes the loop on a case that had been watched for its potential implications for future cases.

8. **SpaceX IPO Dual-Class Share Structure:** Reuters reports that SpaceX plans to implement a dual-class equity structure post-IPO, with Class B shares (held by Elon and insiders) carrying 10 votes each and Class A shares (sold to public investors) carrying 1 vote each. Elon would remain CEO, CTO, and chairman of a nine-member board. Shareholder disputes would be forced into arbitration.
