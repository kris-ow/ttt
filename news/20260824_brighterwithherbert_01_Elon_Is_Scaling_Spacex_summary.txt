This video (a discussion between host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg, a venture capital investor) rounds up a cluster of recent SpaceX developments: a 100th launch of the year, Starlink surpassing 11,000 satellites with a massive new FCC filing, a possible India direct-to-cell proposal, new spaceport and data-center land deals, Elon Musk's mass-to-orbit growth targets, AI/compute scaling claims, and the recovery of a Starship test vehicle from the ocean. The overall thesis is that SpaceX is simultaneously scaling its launch cadence, satellite constellation, ground infrastructure, and AI compute ambitions at a pace the hosts argue the market has not fully priced in.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **100th launch of the year:** SpaceX completed its 100th launch so far this year, the milestone mission carrying 24 additional Starlink satellites aboard a Falcon 9.
2. **80% of world's mass to orbit:** Elon Musk confirmed SpaceX now launches over 80% of the world's mass to orbit, with a stated target of roughly 2.25 million tons of mass to orbit per year by 2032, versus about 2,000 tons in all of 2025 (Musk's figures, roughly a thousand-fold increase); Goldberg believes Starship achieving mass production will be the key unlock enabling this.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Constellation surpasses 11,000 satellites:** The Starlink constellation has officially crossed 11,000 satellites, with roughly 1,300 satellites added so far this year according to the host; SpaceX has filed with the FCC for approval to eventually launch and operate up to 100,000 satellites (a third-generation proposal).
2. **India direct-to-cell filing:** Starlink has filed a proposal covering nearly 30,000 satellites that includes direct-to-device technology to connect mobile phones directly to Starlink satellites in India. Goldberg expressed skepticism pending more detail, noting such approval would likely require a partnership with an unidentified Indian company, while acknowading the potential quality-of-life impact for India's large rural population.
3. **Sherman, Texas gateway approved:** Sherman, Texas's planning and zoning commission approved converting unused mall parking into a Starlink ground gateway linking satellites to fiber; Goldberg noted gateways remain necessary for rural service today but will become largely redundant as direct-to-cell technology matures.
4. **V3 satellite/Starship bandwidth jump expected:** Goldberg said the roughly 2,000 satellites added via 100-plus Falcon 9 launches this year carry about the same bandwidth as a single Starship launch carrying V3 satellites, which he said could begin as early as next month, implying a coming step-change in bandwidth and revenue growth.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Louisiana spaceport deal:** Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry is scheduled to announce a multi-billion dollar deal for SpaceX to develop a new spaceport on roughly 130,000 acres in Vermilion Parish, following a federal court's dismissal of a coastal-damages lawsuit against ExxonMobil that had been holding up the land. Goldberg called this SpaceX's third U.S. launch site (after Starbase in Texas and Kennedy Space Center in Florida) and said it uniquely enables polar-orbit launches; he expects further global spaceport announcements, including in Australia within the coming year, and speculated about future sites in Europe and South America.
2. **Mississippi land swap:** SpaceX affiliate MCX Tax agreed to a 51-acre land swap with South Haven, Mississippi (SpaceX affiliate receiving 51 acres on Lane Road, the city receiving 69 acres on Swiya Road); Goldberg speculated, without firm evidence, that this is likely tied to an xAI data-center consolidation effort given the pattern of large land assembly in Louisiana, Tennessee, and Mississippi.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Musk's 100x intelligence prediction reportedly realized:** The host cites a January podcast appearance in which Musk predicted 100x gains in intelligence at fixed model size; the host claims this has now occurred, pointing to a chart showing the Qwen model achieving high intelligence-index scores with far fewer parameters than competitors. Musk is said to now be predicting another 100x gain driven by specialized, domain-specific AI models — these are Musk's own stated predictions/claims, not independently verified outcomes.
2. **Token costs falling, volumes rising:** Citing a post from Cathie Wood of ARK Invest that Musk replied "true" to, average AI token costs are said to have fallen from about $2 per million tokens in May to about $1 per million tokens, driven by price cuts to open models and new open-source releases from Kimi and DeepSeek; ARK's view (endorsed by Musk) is that falling prices are driving a virtuous cycle of exploding demand.
3. **SpaceX/xAI compute targets:** The host states SpaceX/xAI is targeting a range of 5–10 gigawatts of compute next year (Musk reportedly confident in at least 5GW), up from about 2 gigawatts by the end of this year, which the host suggests could represent $300–500 billion or more in annual revenue next year — these figures are presented as the host's characterization of industry/Musk targets, not confirmed guidance. Goldberg said he "wouldn't bet against Elon" and believes SpaceX will get close to the 10GW mark despite analyst estimates clustering around 5GW.
4. **Nvidia-backed Ohio data center:** Nvidia is financially backing a large data center project in Ohio led by SB Energy, to be leased to OpenAI, with Nvidia providing $105 billion in financing (scaled back from an initial larger figure) supporting an initial ~4.25 gigawatts, with potential to grow to 10 gigawatts. Musk reportedly said the project will take much longer to bring online than expected; Goldberg noted SoftBank's mixed track record on large investments and expressed skepticism the project will hit its targets on schedule.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 13 recovered from the ocean:** After 24 days adrift at sea, SpaceX's recovery team guided the Starship flight 13 vehicle to a location near Christmas Island; engineers plan to inspect the heat shield for tile damage before determining whether to return it to Starbase. The vehicle will not be reflown; Goldberg said the primary goal is examining heat-shield damage to improve future designs, and that the ship itself (as opposed to the booster) will not be recovered on the next test, Flight 14, expected in early September.
2. **Ship reuse and reflight timeline:** Musk stated SpaceX will likely be able to catch a Starship ship with the tower within a few months, with the first ship reflight expected either at the end of this year or early next year. Goldberg noted that per unspecified analysis ("Mark 33"), SpaceX can hit its revenue numbers even without ship reuse as long as booster reuse is achieved, and expects SpaceX to beat next year's guidance on that basis.
