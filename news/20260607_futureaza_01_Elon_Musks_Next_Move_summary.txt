This episode of FutureAzA features host Brian and guest Randy Kirk discussing the potential SpaceX IPO and its implications for Tesla investors, Wall Street narratives, and the broader Elon Musk tech ecosystem. The central argument is that media and financial analyst fears about SpaceX harming Tesla or robbing retirement accounts are overblown, dishonest, or simply wrong — and that Tesla's fundamentals heading into the rest of 2026 are actually strengthening.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO and its real winner — Anthropic's cloud deal:** The SpaceX S1 disclosure revealed a cloud services agreement with Anthropic worth $1.25 billion per month, with capacity ramping in May and June 2026 at a reduced fee period, and a 90-day cancellation window available to either party after the first 6 months. Randy Kirk argues this deal — combined with a reported ability to train new AI models 10 times faster than a few months prior — positions SpaceX as a serious compute provider, and that the Anthropic and Cursor deals push SpaceX's projected 2026 revenue from a previously estimated $19.7 billion to approximately $40 billion, with solid profitability expected this year. Brian notes the joke thesis of the article is that Mark Zuckerberg is the "real winner" of the SpaceX IPO because he can simply copy SpaceX's cloud monetization playbook, given Meta's massive but arguably less productive AI capex spend (2026 capex guidance raised to $145 billion).

2. **Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation dominating headlines:** Brian and Randy discuss how the Tesla-SpaceX merger narrative has been the dominant headline for roughly a week at time of recording. A Fortune analysis cited a potential combined entity valued at approximately $3.4 trillion — SpaceX at an anticipated $1.75 trillion and Tesla at $1.65 trillion — which would make it nearly three times larger than the biggest merger ever completed. One analyst named Larry Goldberg is cited as predicting Tesla could reach $550 by end of June 2026, driven by investors seeking exposure to a potential SpaceX merger. Both hosts are skeptical that a merger is a "rescue mission" for Tesla, pushing back hard on that framing.

3. **SpaceX IPO impact on Tesla stock and index funds:** The hosts discuss how the SpaceX IPO could cause short-term selling pressure on Tesla, as investors raise cash to participate in the IPO — noting Tesla fell approximately 5% on the Monday of recording (around June 2, 2026), alongside drops in Amazon, Meta, and cryptocurrencies. However, they also note a potential positive offset: SpaceX insiders unlocked on IPO day may rotate some proceeds into Tesla shares. Randy estimates SpaceX could represent approximately 4% of the S&P 500 upon inclusion, requiring index funds to buy in and potentially dilute other holdings marginally. A union spokesperson and a content creator named Zack Nelson are cited as claiming the IPO will "rob" 401k accounts — claims the hosts dismiss as uninformed or dishonest. A portfolio manager named Ian McMillan (at Client First) is quoted calling the situation "100% fraud," a claim the hosts also reject as legally reckless and analytically wrong.

4. **SpaceX revenue trajectory and long-term growth:** Randy projects SpaceX revenue of $40 billion in 2026, $65 billion in 2027 (noting Brian Wong projects $80+ billion), and potentially $130 billion the year after. He argues the company could be profitable today if it stopped expanding Starlink, but that halting expansion would be strategically foolish given competition from Amazon's LEO constellation, New Glenn, and Russia's emerging low Earth orbit network. The hosts frame SpaceX as a company deliberately sacrificing near-term profits for long-term dominance — similar to early Tesla.

## Financials

1. **Tesla's trailing earnings scrutinized and defended:** A Fortune article is cited claiming Tesla's trailing GAAP earnings have collapsed from $15 billion to $3.9 billion, with core operating earnings (excluding regulatory credits and Bitcoin gains) at just $2.3 billion. Brian and Randy forcefully reject this framing, arguing the analysis cherry-picks negative line items while ignoring growing FSD subscription revenue, improving vehicle sales and margins in Q2 2026, and expanding energy (Megapack) deployment. They also note that regulatory credits represent real money paid by competitors to avoid fines, and stripping them out while not applying equivalent adjustments to competitors is intellectually dishonest.

2. **Tesla outlook for 2026 and 2027:** Randy argues Tesla is heading into very substantial profits in 2026, driven by higher Q2 vehicle unit sales (described as "up substantially"), increasing FSD subscription uptake, and approximately double the Megapack deployments in Q2 versus Q1. He projects the trajectory "goes crazy" in 2027 with the scaling of robotaxi and Optimus.

## Robotaxi
