This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel covers a broad update on Tesla's investment thesis, anchored by a new Piper Sandler analyst note arguing that at $400/share, investors are getting Optimus "for free." Host Herbert is joined by Jeff Lutz, a multi-decade supply chain executive and CEO of a high-tech manufacturing consulting firm, to discuss analyst forecasts, technical momentum in the stock, Optimus supply chain signals, Cortex 2 progress, and Cathie Wood's robotaxi and Starlink commentary.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Piper Sandler: Optimus Valued at Zero in Current Stock Price:** Piper Sandler analyst Alexander Potter published a note arguing that at $400 per share, Tesla investors are receiving Optimus for free — meaning the market has not yet assigned any value to the humanoid robot business. Potter's 17-business-line model forecasting $1.8 trillion in annual revenue by 2045 does not include Optimus at all, which the hosts note is a deliberate omission because including it would produce numbers too large for most investors to accept. Jeff Lutz draws a parallel to how Tesla's energy business and FSD were similarly excluded from analyst models until they became undeniable, suggesting Optimus will follow the same pattern and trigger another valuation re-rating when analysts are eventually forced to add it.

2. **China Supply Chain Reports: First Bulk Purchase Orders Placed:** According to chatter from China's humanoid robot supply chain, Tesla has officially placed its first bulk purchase order with humanoid robot component suppliers. The reports characterize this as a move beyond sample testing and validation into small-scale mass production and delivery, involving actual payments, and laying the groundwork for mass production in July and August 2026. Jeff Lutz contextualized this by noting that if Tesla is targeting a July–August production start as stated on its earnings call, mechanical and electromechanical suppliers would have had to begin production in April, and silicon and other externally sourced components would require purchase orders even earlier. He described the convergence of Tesla's public guidance and supplier intelligence as corroborating evidence that the timeline is credible.

3. **Optimus Factory and Cortex 2 Investment Signals:** Joe Tegtmeyer's video footage of Giga Texas shows Cortex 2 making significant progress, with roughly 50% of the facility already operational. Installed Megapacks, chiller system assembly, and superstructure work are all visible. The hosts note that just north of the Cortex 2 facility is the future site of Tesla's planned Optimus factory, described as targeting a production capacity of 10 million units per year. Elon Musk also toured Intel's Oregon fab, interpreted as a sign that Terafab development is moving quickly. The hosts argue that the scale of capital being deployed across Cortex 2, Terafab, and Optimus supply chain orders is itself evidence of Tesla's commitment to mass-scale humanoid robot production.

4. **AND-Gate Framework for Bot Success:** Jeff Lutz articulated a framework for evaluating which humanoid robot companies will succeed: product, AI/engineering capability, and manufacturing must all be present — if any one is absent, the result is zero. He argued Tesla is at "one" on all three dimensions, making it one of very few companies with a realistic path to mass production. He noted that of the roughly ten humanoid robots Jensen Huang displayed on stage at Nvidia, perhaps only one will ship in volume. He also flagged that a wave of Chinese bots is coming, but questioned whether any will achieve Tesla's combination of proven AI, manufacturing scale, and commercial viability.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Licensing and Hardware Sales Included in Piper Sandler Model:** Piper Sandler's 17-business model includes FSD software licensing and FSD hardware sales to other automakers, with both projected to begin around 2032. Jeff Lutz suggested the licensing opportunity could be accelerated by two factors: first, competitive pressure as automakers see FSD becoming a purchase decision driver and risk losing sales without it; second, potential regulatory mandates, given that FSD is claimed to be approximately eight to nine times safer than human driving, which could motivate Congress or the executive branch to push for broad adoption to reduce the approximately 43,000 annual US traffic deaths.

2. **Coast-to-Coast FSD Drive as Competitive Benchmark:** The hosts referenced Tesla's recent coast-to-coast drive from Los Angeles to New York City completed without intervention as a demonstration of FSD's lead. Jeff Lutz framed the competitive question simply: no other vehicle available at any US dealership can be given an address and autonomously navigate there. He noted no competitor — domestic or foreign — has a credible near-term timeline to replicate this, citing it as the second recent example (after profitable global EV manufacturing) of Tesla building a lead that no one has closed.

## Robotaxi
1. **Unsupervised Robotaxi Fleet Growth Cited as Market Signal:** Jeff Lutz noted that Tesla has grown from approximately two cars operating autonomously to nearly 40 in the past month or two, characterizing this as a meaningful inflection — sufficient to offer real public service and signal that the robotaxi program is past the "will it work?" binary question. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised (driverless) fleet stands at 39 vehicles as of 2026-05-09, with the broader active robotaxi fleet at 668 vehicles across Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston. The hosts described Cybercab production startup and Q2 2026 milestones as significant near-term catalysts.

2. **Cathie Wood: Robotaxi Cost Structure at 25 Cents Per Mile vs. Waymo at 50% Higher:** Cathie Wood of ARK Invest was featured in two interview clips arguing that Tesla's vertical integration will produce the lowest robotaxi cost structure by far. She stated Tesla's costs will drop to $0.25 per mile as robotaxis scale, compared to Waymo's cost structure which ARK estimates will remain approximately 50% higher than Tesla's in 2030 due to Waymo's dependence on third-party automakers and supply chain partners. She framed the current pricing umbrella as $3+ per mile using Uber as a reference. Jeff Lutz argued 50% may actually be conservative in Tesla's favor, drawing an analogy to a car manufacturer who can produce at $20,000 what competitors cannot build below $30,000 — effectively locking them out of an entire market segment.

3. **Piper Sandler Robotaxi Revenue Projections — Conservative and Delayed:** Piper Sandler's model does not show robotaxi becoming a meaningful business until 2028, with significant revenue ramp occurring from 2031 onward and accelerating sharply through 2033. The hosts characterized these projections as very conservative relative to Tesla's own pace of deployment, consistent with how Morgan Stanley has also modeled a delayed ramp. They argued analysts are constrained by the information available and will revise upward as Tesla executes faster than modeled.

## Financials
1. **Piper Sandler 17-Business Model: $1.8 Trillion Revenue by 2045:** Alexander Potter of Piper Sandler projects Tesla approaching $1.8 trillion in annual revenue by 2045 across 17 discrete business lines, implying a market capitalization on the order of $17 trillion if a 10x revenue multiple is applied. The model includes FSD licensing, FSD hardware sales to automakers, robotaxi, supercharging, insurance, subscriptions, and vehicle sales, among others. Vehicle sales are projected to be essentially flat or declining as a share of revenue over time, at approximately $48–$50 billion by 2045, illustrating the degree to which software and services dominate the long-term model. Optimus is entirely excluded.

2. **Tesla Energy Miss in Q1 and Potential Q2 Recovery:** Jeff Lutz flagged that Tesla missed consensus estimates for its energy business in Q1 2026 by approximately 38–39%, a miss that would have caused severe market reactions for other energy storage companies. He suggested this sets up a potentially large Q2 rebound in energy, alongside what appears to be recovering auto sales based on April momentum.

3. **Cathie Wood: Starlink Revenue Potential of $160 Billion:** Cathie Wood's ARK Invest research estimates that Starlink alone could generate $160 billion in revenue for SpaceX. ARK's SpaceX position represents a mid-teens percentage of its private fund; Wood indicated ARK would likely reduce this position as SpaceX moves toward a public listing, since a public vehicle would offer a less expensive access point. The hosts noted this figure is not included in any Tesla valuation but is mentioned in the context of Elon Musk's broader infrastructure buildout.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's Competitive Moat: Two Proven Examples, Robotaxi as the Third:** Jeff Lutz structured the competitive argument around two recent precedents where Tesla built a durable lead that no competitor has closed: profitable global EV manufacturing, and Full Self-Driving capability. He argued Chinese EV manufacturers cannot demonstrate profitability even in their domestic market and cannot establish profitable global operations. He predicted robotaxi will become the third such example — other systems will exist and operate but will be limited in scale, unable to reach Tesla's deployment volume. He named Xpeng as a potential second-place competitor in China, possibly one to two years behind Tesla, with Xiaomi also flagged as a credible EV contender following a highly automated design and production model.

2. **Colossus 1 Leased to Anthropic — Framed as Strategic, Not a Concern:** The hosts addressed criticism on X regarding Tesla/xAI leasing Colossus 1 to Anthropic. Jeff Lutz argued this reflects the reality of building compute capacity at speed — timing will not be perfect, and excess capacity will exist at certain points. He framed it as a revenue opportunity for xAI in the near term, with Tesla able to reclaim the capacity when needed for Cybercab fleet inference and Optimus training at scale. He noted Elon Musk is simultaneously building Colossus 1, Colossus 2, Cortex 1, Cortex 2, Macrohard, and Dojo, and suggested the possibility of an "Elon Web Services" offering — terrestrial and space-based compute available to third parties — which he said is not in any current valuation model.
