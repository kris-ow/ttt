Tesla published a small Robotaxi service-area geofence for Miami, but the article argues this is a hollow milestone given Tesla's stalled scaling in Texas a year after launch.

## Robotaxi
1. **Miami geofence is narrow:** Tesla's Robotaxi account shared a Miami coverage map bounded roughly by SR-826 to the north and US-41 to the south, covering only West Miami and a strip toward Doral and Sweetwater — excluding downtown Miami, Miami Beach, the airport, and most of Coral Gables. Miami was one of five cities (with Phoenix, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas) Tesla targeted for launch in "1H 2026," a timeline the article says has since softened to "preparations underway."

2. **Austin fleet remains tiny a year post-launch:** Tesla launched Robotaxi in Austin in June 2025. City officials estimate Tesla's Austin fleet at roughly 50 vehicles, with the unsupervised (no safety driver) portion far smaller. The article cites recent data showing the unsupervised fleet shrinking from a peak of about 25 cumulative vehicles to roughly 14 active cars. [Note: this contradicts the RobotaxiTracker.com canonical stale figure of 28 unsupervised vehicles in Austin as of 2026-05-09; per sourcing rules, the article's more specific/differing figures are attributed to the article's own data rather than overwritten.]

3. **Service area expanded but ridership unchanged:** Tesla widened its map to cover the entire Austin metro, but with only about 20 vehicles running, wait times routinely exceeded 15 minutes, and in more than a quarter of checks no cars were available.

## Autonomous Driving
4. **Musk cites safety as the bottleneck:** On Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call, Musk said safety validation, not mapping, is the limiting factor for Robotaxi expansion, and that Tesla is holding back scaling until a rewritten FSD v15 arrives. Tesla has reported a series of crashes in Austin to NHTSA, and the article notes independent analysis has pointed to a crash rate roughly four times worse than the average human driver.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues that publishing new geofences generates headlines that prop up Tesla's autonomy narrative but says nothing about actual operational capability; he contends Texas evidence shows Tesla cannot yet operate driverless cars safely at scale, and views the Miami map as low-substance until Tesla can field significantly more genuinely driverless vehicles in a single city.
