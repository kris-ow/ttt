This video features a conversation between host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur, focused on Elon Musk's AI strategy through xAI and SpaceX. The central thesis is that Elon's acquisition of Cursor — the leading AI "scaffolding" company — combined with Grok's large language models and SpaceX's massive compute infrastructure, positions xAI to leapfrog Anthropic and OpenAI within months rather than years. The discussion also covers Starship's economics, orbital AI data centers, and xAI's compute-rental business model (dubbed "Elon Web Services").

## Market & Competition

1. **xAI's Strategic Position — Hyperscaler Over Best Model:** Peter Diamandis argued that Elon does not need to run the best AI model — only to control the best hardware, comparing the strategy to Nvidia's dominance despite not having the most popular model. Elon responded by agreeing with Diamandis's framing but doubling down, saying "our AI will be great" and vowing never to give up. He noted xAI is only three years old — half the age of Anthropic and a quarter the age of OpenAI — and said, "Let's see where things stand 3 years from now," implying a competitive horizon around 2029.

2. **SpaceX's Historical Precedent for Long Games:** Elon drew a parallel between xAI's current position and SpaceX's early years, noting that SpaceX had "achieved nothing of note after three years" and was "written off as dead after six years with three consecutive launch failures." The implication is that xAI is following a similar long-game trajectory and should not be counted out.

3. **Cursor Acquisition — The Scaffolding Thesis:** Jo Bhakdi argued that the real competitive advantage in AI is no longer the core LLM but the "scaffolding" — the prompting infrastructure, pipelines, and agent orchestration wrapped around it. He identified Cursor as the world's best scaffolding company, better than Anthropic itself, evidenced by the fact that Cursor using Kimmy (described as a second-tier Chinese model) achieved near-parity with ChatGPT and the latest Claude model in coding benchmarks — proving Cursor's scaffolding alone is more powerful than Anthropic's. Elon reportedly offered a $10 billion call option to acquire Cursor for $60 billion (exercisable 60 days after Cursor's IPO, with an execution deadline of approximately August 11, 2026). OpenAI had previously tried to buy Cursor for $3 billion and was turned down.

4. **Grok Model Lineup — Multiple Models in Parallel:** The transcript describes two Grok models currently in development: a 1.5 trillion parameter model (training complete, evaluations positive, Cursor data incorporated in supplementary training, fine-tuning underway, reinforcement learning beginning imminently, with public release in two to three weeks from the publish date) and a 0.5 trillion parameter model (currently serving all Grok production traffic, especially for difficult coding tasks, with an open release planned toward the end of 2026). Jo Bhakdi argued the multi-model approach — running several frontier models simultaneously — is a structural advantage enabled by xAI's outsized compute capacity.

5. **Compute Rental Revenue — "Elon Web Services":** A financial analyst identified as Phil Duan calculated that Colossus 1, with approximately 220,000 GPUs (broken down into H100s, H200s, and GB200s at typical market pricing), generates approximately $5 billion in annual compute revenue paid by Anthropic to xAI. Because Anthropic applies that compute to inference (charging customers), that $5 billion of compute cost translates to approximately $15 billion in annual revenue for Anthropic at a 60–70% gross margin. Herbert noted that the $15 billion figure implies Anthropic is renting only about 150,000–200,000 GB200s from Colossus 2 — roughly one-third of Colossus 2's 550,000 GB200 capacity — meaning xAI retains two-thirds of Colossus 2 for its own use or further rental. Jo Bhakdi and Herbert compared this flywheel to Amazon AWS: overbuild for internal needs, rent out excess, use that revenue to fund even more buildout.

6. **Cursor's Enterprise Moat:** A key strategic insight raised by Jo Bhakdi is that Cursor is already deeply embedded in every Fortune 500 company through developer adoption. This gives xAI an enterprise sales channel that would otherwise require a partnership with Salesforce, Oracle, or a private equity rollup. Herbert had predicted Elon would need such an enterprise partner; Bhakdi corrected this, saying Cursor already provides that penetration without any additional sales effort.

7. **Orbital AI Data Centers — Timeline and Economics:** The discussion covered Elon's plans for AI satellite data centers in orbit, with a stated deployment target of 2028 (referenced from xAI's S-1 filing). Jo Bhakdi predicted orbital prototype satellites in 2027. A technical question from a user named Pierre Farerugi raised a power budget concern (three to four hundred kilowatts of solar panels for one hundred kilowatts of compute, factoring in panel decay). Elon responded that he will present a more detailed AI satellite design "in a few weeks." Bhakdi rated the engineering complexity of AI satellites as "medium to high" — well within Elon's execution sweet spot — and expressed 100% confidence in success.

8. **Starship Economics and Launch 12 Success:** Jo Bhakdi broke down the risk hierarchy for Starship launches: (1) avoiding an explosion at launch with cargo aboard is the highest priority because it affects insurance/cost reserves for the next ~20 flights; (2) successful cargo deployment is next; (3) booster catch and reuse matters for economics but is less catastrophic if failed; (4) Starship vehicle reuse is the least economically critical because even without reuse, Starship's cost per launch (~$40 million for 150 tons of payload) compares favorably to current alternatives (~$70 million for 15 tons). He concluded that orbital AI data centers are economically feasible even without Starship reuse. Launch 12 was described as a near-complete success — one engine failure out of 33 engines but cargo deployed successfully; booster relanding failed but was deemed economically minor.
