## Robotaxi
1. **Elon's Robotaxi predictions repeatedly missed:** The host cites specific quotes from recent quarterly calls: a claim that half the US population would be covered by Robotaxi by the end of a prior year (reality: not close); a claim of 8–10 metro areas operating by year-end (reality: one); a claim of launches in Nevada, Florida, and Arizona by year-end (reality: none); and a claim of "well over 500" paid robotaxi vehicles across the Bay Area and Austin with numbers expected to roughly double monthly (reality: the figure stayed flat, with the unsupervised count much smaller). The host argues this represents a sustained pattern with insufficient explanation, not a one-off error.
2. **Tampa and Orlando Robotaxi launch timed right before earnings:** Tesla announced live Robotaxi service in Tampa and Orlando one day before the earnings call, with geofences estimated at roughly 15–30 square miles each. The host notes a similar pattern occurred with the Houston/Dallas launch before the prior quarter's call, after which those cities' geofences reportedly remained small with only about 5–10 active vehicles at a time some 94 days later, and cautions Tampa/Orlando could follow the same "pilot/beta" trajectory. One user (bearded Tesla) reportedly could not even request a ride in Orlando shortly after the announcement.
3. **Six active Robotaxi cities, but vehicle counts still opaque:** With Tampa and Orlando added, Tesla now has six active Robotaxi cities (excluding Bay Area), down to two remaining cities (Phoenix, Las Vegas) from the original slide-deck list not yet launched. Texas has 175 Tesla vehicles registered statewide (per state data), but this doesn't indicate how many are actively operating; Florida lacks similar disclosure rules, so active vehicle counts there are unknown. All service is currently on Model Y; Cybercab is not yet active in Robotaxi service. For comparison, Waymo is active in 11 cities.
4. **Robotaxi safety-incident tracker updated:** Of 22 incidents since Robotaxi's launch last year, 3 were attributable to the Tesla safety/teleoperator, with the most recent teleoperator-caused incident in May of this year; 9 of the 22 were caused by other parties (Tesla not at fault). February, March, and April were "clean months" with no Tesla-at-fault incidents; June had one new incident, not Tesla's fault; but May saw two incidents where Tesla was at fault.
5. **SFO airport permit is a stepping stone, not full driverless authorization:** Tesla's Bay Area ride-share now serves San Francisco International Airport, but under a human-driver/safety-monitor permit — California and SFO do not currently classify this as a true robotaxi service. SFO can set rules independently of state-level CPUC decisions; the host notes SFO previously required Waymo to run a safety-driver validation period, which may help Tesla's case, but Tesla will need new or amended approval to drop the safety monitor there. One rider reported a $60 fare for a Tesla ride-share trip to SFO versus $50 on Uber for the same trip (excluding tip).

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Starlink V5 integration confirmed for Cybercab:** Tesla officially confirmed Starlink V5 is directly integrated into Cybercab, with an antenna mounted in the trunk; Tesla's VP of AI stated the connectivity is not required for safe operation but is meant for navigation, customer service, and fleet management. The host argues this gives Cybercab an always-on data uplink even in rural dead zones, aiding fleet training data collection, remote-operator connectivity, regulatory transparency (e.g., telemetry/video in emergencies), and pickup/drop-off coordination. It's unclear whether this will extend to Model 3/Model Y given their panoramic glass roofs make antenna placement less straightforward.
2. **Hardware 3 gets updated FSD "light" version:** A new FSD v14 "light" release for Hardware 3 vehicles no longer requires brake confirmation to start and adds the self-driving app for Hardware 3, though rollout is still in progress and not yet available to all owners.
3. **New 2026 summer software features detailed:** Tesla outlined upcoming features including expanded Grok capabilities (phone calls, music search/play, climate adjustment, glove box control, Tesla-related Q&A), self-driving stats in the mobile app, automatic navigation adapting to routine destinations, new "preferred routes" prioritizing previously-taken routes, and arrival energy settings via the app. Musk stated the car will learn individual intervention preferences over time, and in a reply to a user request responded that setting a specific parking-spot destination and voice commands to FSD for parking are "coming soon." Grok in Tesla vehicles is expanding to more Asian markets and is now available across all of Europe.
