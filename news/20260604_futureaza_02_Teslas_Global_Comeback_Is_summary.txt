This video is a conversation between Brian (FutureAzA) and Herbert from Brighter, covering Tesla's global sales rebound, Cybertruck pricing developments, and a Reuters rumor about a new smaller, cheaper Tesla SUV. The core thesis is that Tesla's global comeback is accelerating faster than many expected, driven by improved products, competitive pricing, and favorable macro conditions like higher gas prices.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla's Global Sales Rebound Across Multiple Markets:** Herbert summarizes data from analyst Roland Percher's charts showing strong year-over-year sales growth across several countries. In China, wholesale volume grew 39% in May year-over-year, described as the highest number so far in 2026 and far outpacing 2024 and 2025 performance. In Australia, the Model Y was the bestselling vehicle of any kind for the month of May — described as the first time in electric car history that an EV led overall sales in Australia. In Germany, sales rebounded and surpassed all prior years except 2023. Both hosts note this positive trend was already underway before the recent rise in gas prices, suggesting it is not purely a fuel-cost phenomenon.

2. **Tesla's EV Market Share in Australia:** Tesla achieved a record high 20% market share in Australia in May, even while carrying less inventory than competitors. Both hosts highlight this as a significant milestone and note that broader EV adoption in Australia continues to surge, with Tesla capturing a large share of that growth.

3. **Cybertruck Pricing and Affordability Efforts:** Tesla officially began selling used Cybertrucks directly from its inventory as of June 1, 2026, with shipping available anywhere in the country for $2,500. The starting price was listed at $66,200 for a used Foundation Series. These used units come with 1 to 3 months of FSD and 1 to 3 months of complimentary charging, but without lifetime perks, in order to keep costs down. The used inventory sold out almost immediately. A separately mentioned new lower-priced Cybertruck trim (a slightly trimmed-down new model) also sold through within roughly a week to ten days after launch. Both hosts conclude that price has been the primary barrier to Cybertruck adoption, not the product itself.

4. **Cybertruck's History of Disappointing Sales and Root Causes:** Herbert and Brian discuss how Cybertruck originally attracted 1–2 million reservations, most of which were placed at the $39,000 price point (which they adjust to approximately $53,000 in inflation-adjusted terms). The vehicle never reached that price point, launching at over $100,000, later coming down to around $80,000 and then to approximately $65,000. A prior attempt to create a cheaper variant — described as cutting $10,000 off the price by removing $30,000 worth of utility — sold only around 600–700 units. Herbert also notes that brand damage associated with Elon Musk's public profile in 2025 disproportionately affected Cybertruck compared to other Tesla models, given its more polarizing design and visual identity. Both hosts believe lower pricing will eventually overcome this lingering brand association issue.

5. **Reuters Rumor: New Smaller, Cheaper SUV:** Reuters reported, citing four anonymous sources, that Tesla is developing an entirely new model — not a variant of the Model 3 or Model Y — that would be approximately 14 feet long (notably shorter than the Model Y at 15.7 feet) and substantially cheaper than the Model 3's current starting price of $37,000. The vehicle would reportedly be manufactured in China, potentially offered with a smaller battery and a single motor. Brian and Herbert are both skeptical. Herbert argues it is highly unlikely Tesla would build a net-new platform from scratch rather than a variant of an existing line, and notes that Elon Musk himself recently stated Tesla is working on "something way cooler than a minivan, but smaller than a Model Y." Both hosts also point out logistical problems: Giga Shanghai is reportedly at capacity, and Fremont has no room. Herbert raises the possibility of a Cyber-SUV variant built on the Cybertruck line at Giga Texas, noting that Joe Tegtmeyer confirmed a very large portion of Giga Texas is dedicated to Cybertruck infrastructure — more than what is allocated to Cybercab production — and that this cannot easily be repurposed. The hosts conclude that while the rumor has some logical appeal for the Chinese market specifically, too many elements of the Reuters story don't add up.

## Market & Competition

1. **EV Cost Curve vs. ICE Pricing Trends:** Brian makes the broader market point that gas cars increase in price every year while gas prices are volatile, whereas electric vehicles have continued to move down the cost curve. He frames this as a structural advantage for Tesla and EVs generally, suggesting the growth story for EVs is far from over.

2. **Brand Damage and Its Market Impact:** Herbert argues that while Tesla's overall global sales have recovered — including in Germany — the Cybertruck remains the model most visibly affected by the brand damage Tesla experienced in 2025. He attributes this to the vehicle's bold, polarizing design, which makes it more symbolically associated with Elon Musk in the public eye than other Tesla models. He believes this effect will diminish as more units reach the road and as prices come down to attract a broader buyer base.
