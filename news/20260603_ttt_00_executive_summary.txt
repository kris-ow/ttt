## Robotaxi

- **Full Austin geofence now covered by unsupervised Robotaxis.** Tesla's official Robotaxi X account confirmed unsupervised service now spans the entire Austin metro — expanded from a small South Austin sub-zone through several phases over the past year. Per electrified's Dillon Loomis, with the full geofence live, safety monitor positions in Austin may effectively be eliminated, and the only remaining scaling bottleneck is backend remote operations infrastructure.

- **Electrek and brighterwithherbert disagree sharply on active fleet size.** Electrek (Fred Lambert) reports the unsupervised Austin fleet has declined from a peak of 25 vehicles in late April to approximately 20 active today, and that the total active fleet across all modes collapsed from 165 to just 34. BrighterWithHerbert references approximately 28 unsupervised vehicles slated for Austin. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised fleet stands at 39 total. Lambert characterizes the geofence expansion as a costless distraction from stagnant fleet counts; Loomis characterizes it as a genuine confidence signal preceding an imminent ramp.

- **30+ Cybercabs photographed in Dallas staging lot.** Two photos circulated on June 3 showing first 26, then over 30, Cybercabs in a Dallas lot — a strong indicator of near-term expansion there. Separately, a ground reporter in Houston confirmed a Model Y added as vehicle nine in the Houston fleet, suggesting fleet additions are not limited to Cybercabs.

- **FSD v15 cited as scaling prerequisite by Musk; ARK disagrees.** Electrek reports Musk has publicly tied aggressive fleet scaling to the FSD v15 software rewrite, pushing any meaningful ramp to late 2026 or early 2027. ARK Invest's Cathie Wood, quoted via brighterwithherbert, counters that FSD v14 already running in Austin and San Francisco is sufficient — AI4 hardware meets the bar, and current safety data shows Tesla FSD is already safer than human drivers.

- **Scale gap vs. Waymo remains stark.** Waymo operates approximately 3,000 robotaxis across 11 US cities, completes over 500,000 paid trips per week across more than 1,400 square miles, and recently raised $16 billion to fund expansion including London and Tokyo. ARK's Wood noted Tesla can produce more vehicles than Waymo's entire fleet in a single day — underscoring manufacturing upside if software and regulatory hurdles clear.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Tesla retroactively made pre-2024 FSD purchase contracts inaccessible.** Owner Oliver Abcarius discovered that his 2019 FSD purchase agreement (2018 Model 3) and his wife's 2020 Model Y agreement — both from the era when Tesla sold "Full Self-Driving Capability" without "supervised" language — now link to invalid pages. Every other document on both accounts opens normally. Electrek confirmed the pattern with multiple owners.

- **Legal exposure context.** Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in active litigation covering FSD false advertising, Autopilot crash liability, and securities fraud. A certified US class action covers statements from October 2016 to August 2024. A California administrative ruling found Tesla engaged in FSD false advertising. An arbitrator previously ordered a $10,600 refund to one FSD buyer. Electrek's Fred Lambert notes that making executed contracts inaccessible during active litigation raises the possibility of evidence spoliation, which courts can treat with adverse inferences or sanctions.

- **Timeline of the FSD language shift.** Tesla sold "Full Self-Driving Capability" for up to $15,000 without "supervised" language from 2016 through early 2024. In March 2024 with FSD v12.3.3, Tesla formally renamed the product "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)." In April 2026, Musk confirmed HW3 vehicles (produced 2016–2023) cannot achieve unsupervised FSD due to hardware limitations, with no concrete retrofit plan.

- **FSD 14.3 update rolling out — mostly bug fixes.** Per electrified, the new FSD 14.3 version is primarily bug fixes, with one highlighted improvement to driver monitoring sensitivity including better eye gaze tracking and eyewear handling. Tesla clarified the feature had actually shipped in a prior update and was simply not noted in those release notes. FSD 14 for HW4 vehicles in Australia is in final stages of local testing with no confirmed release date.

- **FSD 14 already deployed in Las Vegas Loop commercially.** Per farzad-fm, the Boring Company is running Tesla FSD commercially in the Las Vegas Loop with paying customers today — cited as evidence the robotaxi use case has moved beyond pilot testing into revenue-generating deployment.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus factory construction begins at Giga Texas — steel structure standing.** Construction officially started this week on Tesla's dedicated Optimus manufacturing facility at Giga Texas. Drone footage by factory observer Joe Tegtmeyer shows the first steel structure now erected at the site. When complete, the building is expected to stretch nearly the full length of the main Giga Texas factory. Tesla's stated target remains approximately 10 million Optimus units per year from that facility (~27,000/day).

- **Elon described Optimus as a "Von Neumann probe."** Per The Tesla Space, Musk posted on X: "Optimus will be the first Von Neumann machine capable of building civilization by itself on any viable planet" — a self-replicating machine concept from physicist John Von Neumann — explicitly linking the robot program to SpaceX's Mars settlement ambitions.

- **Tesla granted mechanical eye lens-cleaning patent.** Tesla received a patent for a lens-cleaning system featuring a circular lens, eye-socket housing, tear-duct-positioned nozzle, and eyelid-shaped wiper blades — completing a full cleaning cycle in under one second. Since Optimus shares camera hardware with Tesla vehicles, The Tesla Space argues this system will be required for robots operating in uncontrolled real-world environments. Tesla has already been spotted testing camera cleaning systems on Cybercab prototypes.

- **Jason Calcanis Optimus lab visit, January 2026.** Per farzad-fm, Calcanis visited Tesla's Optimus Lab around CES and posted: "I can tell you now, nobody will remember that Tesla ever made a car. They'll only remember the Optimus robot." Musk replied: "probably true."

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Australia May 2026: record 6,433 units, Model Y tops all-fuel sales chart.** Per Sawyer Merritt and electrified, Tesla's May figure surpassed the prior record of 6,017 set in March 2024. The Model Y became the first EV in history to lead Australia's overall new-vehicle sales chart across all fuel types. Year-to-date Australian sales are up 56%.

- **Tesla China May wholesale: 85,982 units, highest Giga Shanghai figure of 2026.** Up 8.2% over April and up 39.4% year-over-year. Year-to-date through May, Tesla China is up 29.4% versus the same period last year — and this is before FSD receives broad approval in China.

- **Tesla Germany and Japan posting exceptional year-over-year growth.** Germany: over 5,100 units in May, up 322% YoY; YTD exceeds 21,000, already 9% above full-year 2025 total. Japan: YTD registrations up 157% (8,100+ vs. 3,178 same period last year), despite overall foreign brand registrations in Japan down 6%.

- **Norway: Tesla held 21.5% of all new car sales in May.** The Model Y alone represented 1 in every 5 new vehicles sold. Norway's overall EV market share reached 97.8% for the month.

- **Cybertruck: used inventory sold out in ~one day; new order lead times extending.** Tesla listed used Cybertrucks starting at $66,200 (Foundation Series, 2,566 miles); the full inventory cleared within roughly 24 hours. New Premium AWD delivery estimates have pushed to September–October 2026, extending from August–September the prior week — implying a 4–5 month wait, attributed to Tesla prioritizing production of another vehicle.

- **Cybertruck new sales remain weak; Kazakhstan purchase a marginal signal.** Cybertruck sales fell 48% in 2025 to approximately 20,000 units; Q1 2026 came in at just 3,519 units, with SpaceX accounting for 1,279 purchases over the past year. Kazakhstan's Ministry of Emergency Situations confirmed plans to expand its Cybertruck fleet following successful mountainous terrain rescue operations around Almaty. Electrek's Fred Lambert views the purchase as evidence of demand desperation rather than a reversal, and identifies the $60,000 AWD trim as the vehicle's "last hope."

- **Camouflaged Model Y L spotted testing at Fremont.** New footage shows a camo-wrapped vehicle on the Fremont test track, theorized by FutureAzA's Brian White and Mark to be the three-row Model Y L. Elon previously said the vehicle "might" appear "at the end of '26, if at all," but White predicted a first-half 2026 US appearance. The Model Y L has driven record sales in markets where it has launched, including Australia.

- **Model Y 4680 "8L" battery pack: charging alarm was overblown.** The Limiting Factor's Jordan Giesige reviewed two charging datasets and concluded the 8L pack (74 kWh usable, 166 Wh/kg) performs roughly in line with Tesla's other packs when properly preconditioned — achieving 250 kW at 7% state of charge in Fabrizio's definitive test. The alarming 130 kW headline came from an unpreconditioned test by Elektro Ronny. Cell-level energy density (~95.4 Wh) matches the Cybertruck's Cybercell, suggesting identical Gen 2 4680 cells. The 8L pack is approximately 6% behind the best available 2170 packs on energy density.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO set for June 12 at $135/share, implying $1.75 trillion valuation.** Reuters reported approximately 555 million shares will be offered, raising $75 billion — over 50 times revenue. Musk responded to valuation questions with: "You shall see." He is subject to a lockup preventing share sales for more than one year post-IPO.

- **Tesla/SpaceX merger thesis: serious internal discussions reported.** Per The Tesla Space, people familiar with the conversations say Musk has seriously discussed a merger with trusted colleagues and the idea is openly discussed inside Tesla. The SpaceX prospectus revealed $697 million in Tesla Megapack purchases and $131 million in Cybertruck purchases by SpaceX in 2024–2025, Tesla's $2 billion investment into XAI (which subsequently merged with SpaceX), and an Nvidia $500 million GPU order redirected from Tesla to XAI at Musk's personal request. Jo Bhakdi's theory, previewed via brighterwithherbert, holds that Tesla shareholders would receive a premium — roughly two-thirds of the combined entity — because SpaceX's prospectus reportedly authorized 36 billion shares.

- **Institutional ownership surpasses retail in Tesla for first time.** Per Alexandra Merz (cited by brighterwithherbert): institutions now hold 47.8% of Tesla stock, Musk holds ~20%, and retail has fallen to 31.9%. Approximately 4,276 institutions have filed 13F forms. Largest holders include Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street, Geode Capital, Jane Street, and JP Morgan.

- **Tesla Semi dominates California EV truck voucher applications.** California voucher data (January 2025–February 2026) shows Tesla Semi accounting for 965 of 1,067 total Class 8 tractor applications. Daimler and Volvo combined for fewer than 100. Per electrified's dashboard: 644 verified Semi orders across 9 confirmed customers, 48 trucks delivered (45 to Pepsi, 2 to WEV, 1 to DHL), 81 known charging depot sites.

- **Nvidia RTX Spark announcement targets PC market reinvention.** Jensen Huang unveiled the N1X/RTX Spark chip: Blackwell RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores, 1 petaflop AI performance, 20-core Grace CPU (MediaTek partnership), 128 GB unified memory, TSMC 3nm, 70 billion transistors. Jo Bhakdi argues this doubles the home computing TAM and will push average PC prices from ~$900 to $2,000+, with Nvidia capturing roughly half the incremental revenue. Developed in a ~3-year collaboration with Microsoft. Nvidia stock was trading around $226 on June 3; Bhakdi's 2026 price target is $300. (Bhakdi discloses Nvidia is his second-largest holding after Tesla.)

- **Terafab semiconductor facility: 3,100+ acres filed in Grimes County, Texas.** Wit Tech LLC — sharing an address with XAI in Palo Alto — filed land acquisition deeds on May 28, 2026. Scope: $55–$119 billion, multi-phase vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing and AI computing fabrication. Public hearing held June 3. Per farzad-fm, Terafab targets 100–200 billion custom AI chips per year; ~80% earmarked for space applications. Bhakdi estimates first chips within 3–4 years.
