This video features FutureAzA host interviewing Will from the Tesla Jigsaw YouTube channel about delivery delays affecting certain Model Y variants in the UK. The core issue is that Tesla introduced mid-cycle hardware upgrades to Giga Berlin-produced all-wheel-drive and performance Model Y vehicles — specifically more energy-dense battery cells and rear motor improvements — which caused those vehicles to fall out of compliance with existing type approval certifications, blocking registration in the UK and parts of Europe. The conversation also touches on UK Tesla demand more broadly and comparisons between the Model 3 and Model Y for European driving conditions.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **UK Delivery Delays Caused by Running-Change Type Approval Gap:** Tesla introduced hardware upgrades mid-production cycle to Giga Berlin-built all-wheel-drive and performance Model Y variants. The changes included more energy-dense battery cells (to improve range) and updates to the rear motor (to improve performance). Because these changes meant the vehicles no longer matched the existing type approval specifications on file, the UK's Vehicle Certification Agency (VCA) had not yet issued updated certificates of conformity, and the DVLA system consequently blocked those vehicles from being registered and handed over to customers. The delays had reportedly been building for a couple of months, with some customers seeing their delivery dates pushed back repeatedly — in some cases to September or later.

2. **Cars Built and In-Country But Undeliverable:** Affected vehicles are physically present in the UK — stockpiled at ports, airport overflow sites, industrial estates, and other temporary locations — but cannot legally be handed over pending the updated certification. Will notes this is unusual in that the vehicles are not defective; they actually exceed the specifications on file, delivering better range and performance than the approved spec.

3. **Resolution Underway — Customers Receiving Self-Schedule Links:** As of the video's recording date (around 2026-06-22), the Tesla Owners UK community had posted that notifications were being sent to affected customers, who would receive self-scheduling links within 24–48 hours to rebook their deliveries. Tesla asked affected customers to wait for those links rather than contacting Tesla directly, to avoid overwhelming support. Will also notes that goodwill gestures — including free Supercharging and free premium connectivity — had been offered to some affected customers.

4. **Only All-Wheel-Drive and Performance Variants Affected:** Standard (rear-wheel-drive) Model Y variants, both long-range and short-range, are unaffected and delivering normally. Model 3 vehicles are also unaffected. The delay is limited to the all-wheel-drive long-range and performance Model Y variants produced at Giga Berlin.

5. **Tesla's Running-Change Manufacturing Model Creates Certification Risk:** Will observes that Tesla continuously updates its vehicles rather than waiting for annual model-year changeovers like most automakers, which should make it well-practised at managing type approval implications of mid-cycle changes. He expresses some surprise that Tesla did not proactively notify the VCA of these specific hardware changes before vehicles were produced and shipped. The VCA's normal processing time for such updates is reportedly 3 to 6 weeks (per Will). Will notes that while this situation is frustrating, mid-cycle technical issues are not unique to Tesla — other manufacturers experience similar problems.

6. **UK Demand and Sales Appear Strong Despite Controversy:** Will describes Tesla as doing "quite well" in the UK, noting (per his belief) that the Model 3 was among the best-selling cars in the market. He also observes that Teslas are increasingly visible on UK roads and that both updated Model Y and Model 3 vehicles are easily distinguishable from older versions. Giga Berlin's production capacity is said to be up 20% as of July (the host attributes this claim to Will without further sourcing). Will also mentions that the UK-market lower-cost rear-wheel-drive standard Model Y retains larger wheels compared to some other markets — reportedly due to UK insurance considerations and customer preference for residual value, particularly for leased vehicles, per a conversation Will had with Tesla UK.

7. **Model Y Practicality vs. Model 3 Dynamics in European Context:** Will, who has driven a Model Y across the UK and into Austria on the autobahn, says the car is no larger than most SUVs already on UK roads and poses no meaningful obstacle on typical routes, including tight roads in rural Wales. He and the host agree that while the Model 3 is sportier and more engaging to drive, the Model Y's higher seating position and ease of ingress/egress make it more practical for everyday family use.
