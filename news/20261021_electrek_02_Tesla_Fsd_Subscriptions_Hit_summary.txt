Tesla's Q3 2026 shareholder letter reports 1.74 million active FSD "subscriptions," up 67% year-over-year, but Electrek argues the metric is defined to match the operational milestones in Musk's 2025 CEO pay package rather than to give investors a clear picture of recurring revenue.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **How Tesla defines "FSD subscriptions":** A footnote ties the metric to "our 2025 CEO Performance Award" definition, which counts both upfront (one-time) FSD purchases and monthly subscriptions as "active subscriptions," excluding only free trials. Tesla stopped selling FSD as a one-time purchase on February 14, 2026 (new US customers now pay $99/month), but anyone who paid upfront before then — at prices that reached $15,000 — remains counted as long as the car is active. Tesla reported 1.28 million under this same definition in April (per the article's earlier coverage) versus 1.74 million now; Tesla does not disclose how many of the 1.74 million actually pay monthly.
2. **75% attach rate on new deliveries:** Tesla says more than 75% of new North American deliveries in Q3 included FSD subscriptions, calling it "a nearly twenty percentage point sequential increase," and states it believes "FSD is driving demand in markets where it is available." The article notes Tesla does not define whether this includes introductory offers at delivery or how many of those buyers remain paying subscribers after a few months.
3. **Unsupervised vs. supervised gap:** The article notes that many of these counted "subscribers" originally paid for unsupervised self-driving, which Tesla has not yet delivered — they are now effectively counted within a supervised-only service.

## Financials
1. **Link to Musk's 2025 pay package:** The 10 million active FSD subscriptions figure is one of the operational milestones in Musk's 2025 CEO performance award, which has 12 tranches each requiring a market-cap milestone plus an operational milestone (also including 20 million vehicles delivered, 1 million Optimus robots, 1 million robotaxis in commercial operation, and adjusted EBITDA targets). As of June 30, Tesla considered only the 20 million cumulative deliveries milestone probable. Tesla's "cumulative deliveries" figure (now 10.2 million) similarly includes "any unsupervised Robotaxis placed into commercial operation" to align with the pay package's 20-million-vehicle milestone.
2. **Pace toward the 10 million subscription milestone:** Tesla added 260,000 net FSD subscriptions in Q3, described as its best quarter yet; at that pace reaching 10 million would take about eight years. At the trailing 12-month pace (roughly 700,000/year), it would take closer to 12 years. The award runs until 2035.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that counting one-time FSD buyers as "subscribers" isn't factually wrong but is misleading when presented as a momentum metric, since the definition was written for Musk's pay package rather than investor clarity; he also criticizes Tesla for not disclosing actual $99/month payer counts or subscriber retention data, and for counting former "unsupervised" buyers within an indefinitely supervised product. He calls the 75% attach rate the more interesting data point "if it holds up," but says Tesla needs to show retention data to substantiate it.
