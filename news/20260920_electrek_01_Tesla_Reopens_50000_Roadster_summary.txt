Tesla has reopened $50,000 deposit reservations for its next-generation Roadster ahead of an October 1 unveil event near Waco, Texas, marking the second time it has collected this deposit amount for a car that still hasn't shipped after nearly nine years.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New reservation terms:** The reservation page requires $5,000 upfront via credit card (described as "fully refundable"), followed by a $45,000 wire transfer due within 10 days; the reservation isn't final until the wire clears.

2. **History of delays:** Tesla first took Roadster deposits in November 2017 at $50,000 for a standard reservation or $250,000 upfront for a "Founders Series," with production promised for 2020. The article states the 2017 Roadster has been delayed at least eight times and has not delivered a single unit; it reports that reservation holders, including Marques Brownlee (who had waited eight years), struggled to get deposits back, and that Sam Altman said he was ghosted when trying to cancel his.

3. **October 1 event plans:** Tesla says the event will reveal pricing, specs, and production targets. Elon Musk has teased a demo involving SpaceX-designed cold gas thrusters that he says will let the car "fly" or hover; the article describes this stunt car as expected to be remote-operated, not street legal, and potentially costing hundreds of thousands of dollars on its own.

4. **Musk's own expectation-setting:** At the All-In Summit this month, Musk said: "It'll be a banger. Excitement guaranteed. Success is not guaranteed, but excitement is," adding that Tesla needs a live audience "to vouch for the fact that this is not AI."

5. **Original vs. new specs:** The 2017 car promised 620-mile range, sub-1.9-second 0-60 mph, 250+ mph top speed, and a $200,000 base price. Teasers for the new version point to a redesign using Tesla's "Cyber" styling and a full-width light bar similar to the robotaxi/Cybertruck line.

6. **Prediction market skepticism:** On Polymarket, a contract on the Roadster reaching customers by end of 2026 sits under 5% (article notes this market is thinly traded), while a separate contract for 2027 delivery prices around 61%.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert, who discloses he holds two Founders Series Roadster referral awards, argues Tesla is repeating its 2017 playbook — collecting $50,000 deposits before showing a production version — and says he won't trust the Roadster is real until cars are actually delivered to customers. He also states his own view that the Roadster "never needed a redesign, it needed to ship."
