Tesla has sued Cybertruck tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group in federal court, seeking emergency court-ordered access to a Troy, Texas plant to retrieve tooling it says it needs to keep building the Cybertruck, after the supplier closed the plant and blocked Tesla's access.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Lawsuit filed to retrieve tooling, not damages:** Tesla filed suit on July 23 in the US District Court for the Western District of Texas (No. 6:26-cv-0477), asking only for retrieval of its own tooling rather than damages or resolution of the broader commercial dispute, per the complaint as first reported by Bloomberg Law.

2. **Timeline of the standoff:** Angstrom notified Tesla on July 13 that the Troy plant was closing, with no cooperation plan for removing Tesla's equipment, according to the complaint. A July 17 shipment of 700 finished Tesla parts never left the plant. Angstrom then billed Tesla for tooling modifications and reportedly offered to keep the plant running only if Tesla paid $250,000 per week on top of amounts already owed on open purchase orders. Tesla sent representatives with law enforcement to the plant on July 21, but was denied entry. Angstrom has not commented publicly.

3. **Specialized tooling creates supply risk:** The tooling in question — large die-cast machinery, trim dies, fixtures, cutting tools, gauges, and X-ray inspection gear — is purpose-built for specific Cybertruck castings. Per Tesla's complaint, no other supplier can currently produce these parts, rebuilding the tooling would take five to six months, and Tesla's on-hand supply of affected components is "dwindling and will be exhausted in mere days," putting several thousand already-committed Cybertruck builds at risk.

4. **Supplier history:** Angstrom, a Southfield, Michigan tier-one supplier founded in 1999, acquired Ontario-based KSR International in July 2025 (reportedly creating a supplier with over $1 billion in annual revenue and 4,500 employees, per Automotive News) and then acquired Anderton Castings on September 9, 2025. Anderton — which ran aluminum die-casting operations in Troy, Texas and Monterrey, Mexico making structural body components and EV propulsion parts such as inverter housings and manifolds — was Tesla's original supplier relationship, inherited by Angstrom.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert notes only Tesla's side of the story is available so far and suspects there is more context to the dispute. He also suggests Tesla's history of over-ordering parts — citing Cybertruck production plans for up to 250,000 units/year against actual sales of about 20,000/year — may have contributed to strained supplier relationships and "screwing some people over."
