Electrek's Tesla coverage today centers on the chaotic Cybertruck wind-down, with secondary pieces on the new Roadster's reception and a rare Semi delivery data point.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Unplugged Performance offers to take over Cybertruck production:** Fred Lambert reports that Tesla tuner Unplugged Performance publicly offered, via X, to take over Cybertruck production at Giga Texas, funded by a consortium of investors, in exchange for a license fee plus FSD/Robotaxi network revenue per vehicle; UP also proposed building a three-row "CyberSUV" and an unmanned "CyberCargo" hauler. The offer came days after Musk said Tesla would wind down Cybertruck production around mid-2027. Lambert notes Tesla delivered an estimated 7,000–8,000 Cybertrucks in Q3 (roughly a 30,000/year pace, about 30% of installed capacity), and argues Musk's stated "floor space" rationale doesn't hold up given Giga Texas's size and the low number of Texas Cybercab registrations; he says he doubts Tesla will agree but wishes UP luck. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/10/unplugged-performance-offers-take-over-tesla-cybertruck-production/)

2. **Tesla extends Cybertruck production to summer 2027 amid backlash:** Fred Lambert reports Musk announced a third, shifting end-date for Cybertruck production — now "through the end of summer 2027," citing backlog — after earlier saying "around the middle of next year" and then "in about 8 months." Tesla simultaneously raised Cybertruck prices up to 10% (cheapest version now $92,490, up from $69,990 in August) and paused base Dual Motor orders. Lambert argues the "backlog" is self-created by the wind-down announcement, disputes Musk's shifting rationales (space, talent, complexity), and notes Tesla has announced no new consumer vehicle as its lineup shrinks toward just Model 3 and Model Y by fall 2027; he also cites China retail sales falling 66% in October to a four-year low and frames the overall pattern as Tesla pivoting from selling cars to funding robotaxi/Optimus bets. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/11/tesla-extends-cybertruck-production-summer-2027-fan-backlash/)

3. **Porsche CEO criticizes Tesla's Roadster stunt reveal:** Fred Lambert reports Porsche CEO Michael Leiters, per Bloomberg, said Porsche wants to "completely steer clear from any kind of effects and shows," contrasting Tesla's thruster-powered Roadster flip reveal with Porsche's Mission S concept unveiled the same day; Leiters said the cars don't target the same customers and reported "overwhelming" response to the Mission S. Lambert recaps the Roadster's three trims (Performance, Thrust, Rocket) and Musk's "2028, actually" timeline, and argues the cold-gas thruster physics (citing 400 liters of air at 10,000 psi and 80 kN thrust) limit the car to brief stunts, not real flight, criticizing Musk for suggesting otherwise on Joe Rogan's show and noting Ferrari's CEO rated the launch "10 out of 10." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/11/porsche-ceo-tesla-flying-roadster-stunt/)

4. **Tesla confirms 50-truck Semi delivery to Chicago carrier:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's Semi account confirmed "Chicago's first 50 Semis" are being delivered, matching an order from HMD Trucking (a 650-truck, mostly-diesel Chicago carrier) placed after Tesla's September production event; HMD's COO said the first truck arrived November 2 and that Tesla "has been transparent... regarding the capabilities of Semi." The deal is reportedly worth about $15 million (~$300,000/truck per the owner, cited to MotorTrend), and HMD's summer pilot truck averaged 1.74 kWh/mile over 4,000+ miles. Lambert notes Tesla doesn't disclose Semi production/delivery figures despite claiming 50,000-unit annual capacity at its Nevada plant, and argues the 50-truck batch is a small fraction of that capacity but suggests the ramp has "clearly started." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/11/tesla-semi-delivery-50-trucks-chicago-hmd/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert takes a consistently skeptical line across today's pieces: he argues Tesla's official explanations for killing Cybertruck production don't withstand scrutiny, frames the shifting end-dates and price hikes as evidence of poor planning, criticizes the Roadster reveal as style over substance bordering on misleading physics, and remains cautious about reading too much into the Semi's opaque production numbers despite seeing real ramp progress.
