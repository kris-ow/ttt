The host (Dillon Loomis) walks through Tesla's Q2 earnings call, arguing that Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy finally gave the market a real metric to track — unsupervised robotaxi mile growth — and uses that data to build his own model for expected fleet size and mileage through Q3, while also flagging why he thinks Tesla stock had further downside even after its recent drop.

## Robotaxi
1. **New official metric: unsupervised miles, not just "paid miles":** Tesla disclosed it has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states. The host argues the previously-tracked "cumulative paid robotaxi miles" chart likely includes Bay Area supervised ride-hailing (not just Austin unsupervised rides), based on Elon Musk's comments grouping the Bay Area under the robotaxi umbrella, and estimates from a pixel analysis of that chart that monthly paid miles peaked around 445,000 in March before declining to roughly 155,000 in June — numbers he stresses are unofficial, derived estimates with wide error bars.
2. **Double-digit weekly growth in unsupervised miles:** Musk said the fleet is scaling at more than 10% weekly growth in miles driven; Ashok Elluswamy separately clarified this refers specifically to unsupervised miles, growing at a high compounding weekly rate since the start of the year, and said Tesla expects this pace to continue through the rest of the year.
3. **Cybercab production tied to unsupervised mile growth:** Elluswamy said Tesla will match Cybercab production/deployment rates to the growth rate of unsupervised miles, making that mileage metric the key number to track going forward.
4. **Host's model for Q3 fleet size:** Applying a sustained 10%-weekly growth assumption from a roughly 3,500-mile/week starting point in January, the host estimates Tesla reached about 38,000 unsupervised miles/week by mid-July (matching the ~380,000 cumulative figure), implying roughly 50 unsupervised vehicles doing just over 100 miles/day each. Extrapolating the same growth rate to late October would imply about 130,000 miles/week and a fleet needing to grow to around 173 unsupervised vehicles (adding ~123 over the quarter); under a decaying growth-rate scenario, the fleet might instead reach around 115 vehicles (adding ~65). The host's own Q3 expectation is up to roughly 200 deployed robotaxis, explicitly rejecting claims that Tesla will reach thousands of unsupervised vehicles this year.
5. **Comparison to Waymo:** A commentator's viral estimate pegs Waymo at roughly 570,000 driverless miles per day, versus Tesla's projected ~130,000 miles per week even under sustained growth — a comparison the host calls largely apples-to-oranges but useful for scale context.
6. **State-by-state expansion "trending toward zero," but not soon:** Elluswamy said time-to-launch in new cities will trend toward zero as Tesla moves toward operating across entire states rather than city-by-city. The host believes full-state approval won't happen this year at the earliest, more likely next year, and could be a multi-year process.
7. **Newer FSD version testing on robotaxi fleet:** The host notes that a newer FSD version is reportedly running on the robotaxi fleet in a deliberately controlled, small-scale environment, which he argues is actually the safest place to validate bleeding-edge autonomy software rather than deploying it broadly across the whole consumer fleet.
8. **Small sample size caveat:** The host emphasizes 380,000 unsupervised miles with zero "notable incidents" (a term Tesla did not define) is too small a sample to extrapolate safety conclusions to a nationally scaled fleet, given the concentrated, favorable-condition areas where robotaxis currently operate.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus remains highly uncertain:** The host argues Optimus deployment timelines, cost of goods sold, and pricing to third-party customers remain essentially unknown, with early production units expected to go first to an internal "Optimus Academy" rather than external customers; he criticizes analysts for not asking Tesla about Optimus Academy deployment timelines or scale during the earnings call.

## Financials
1. **AI hardware acquisition disclosed in 10-Q:** Tesla's Q2 10-Q confirmed an acquisition of an AI hardware company for up to $1.95 billion in common stock/equity, of which about $1.73 billion is contingent on service conditions or performance milestones (~$222 million allocated to patents/technology upfront). The host reiterates his unconfirmed Q1 speculation that this could be Density AI, founded by a former Tesla Autopilot Hardware senior director who worked on Dojo, joined by (per rumor) as many as 20 former Tesla Dojo engineers.
2. **FSD attach rate and recurring revenue:** Tesla's FSD attach rate in North America reached roughly 55% in Q2, spanning 1.48 million active subscriptions — 45% paying monthly (~666,000 users) and 55% purchased outright (~814,000 users). The host calculates this implies roughly $791 million/year in recurring FSD revenue, calling it one of the more encouraging Q2 stats, though he cautions it isn't yet enough to move Tesla's overall financials.
3. **Regulatory credit revenue declined sharply:** Regulatory credits came in at $146 million for the quarter, down from a prior-year low water mark of $380 million, which the host attributes to the expiration of CAFE penalties.

## Energy
1. **Energy gross margins guided lower:** Tesla's energy gross margin fell from about 39% to roughly 20% in Q2, and Tesla's CFO guided that energy gross margins are now expected to settle between 20-25% going forward — down from earlier expectations (per the host) that margins could exceed 30-40%.

## Market & Competition
1. **Valuation and stock outlook:** With TSLA around $313, the host highlights Tesla's forward P/E for fiscal 2027 at roughly 140x, versus Apple (35x), Amazon (23x), Google (22x), Microsoft (20x), Nvidia (17x), and Meta (17x), arguing this shows the market still prices in substantial robotaxi growth and thus retains significant downside risk when expectations are recalibrated lower. He states he sees more downside than upside in the near term, would not be surprised by TSLA testing $260-280, but says he is personally still buying shares and has not sold any, remaining bullish over a 3-5 year horizon.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Starlink and Semi FSD roadmap notes:** Musk said Starlink connectivity will eventually be added across the rest of Tesla's fleet where available, and that FSD for the Tesla Semi is expected to go active either later this year or early next year, with near-term focus remaining on FSD for high-volume passenger vehicles.
