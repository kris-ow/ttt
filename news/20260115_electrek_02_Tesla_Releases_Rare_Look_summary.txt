Tesla released a video featuring Site Manager Jason Bevan giving a tour of its Corpus Christi-area (Robstown, Texas) lithium refinery, confirming the plant has started production after breaking ground in 2023.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Refinery timeline and process:** Bevan states the project went from groundbreaking in 2023, to running rock through the kiln in 2024, to full integrated plant startup "now in 2025" — Electrek notes this phrasing implies the facility likely reached full startup late last year. Tesla describes it as the first spodumene-to-lithium-hydroxide refinery in North America, using hard rock ore (rather than brine) processed through a kiln, cooler, and alkaline leach before crystallization into battery-grade lithium hydroxide.
2. **Acid-free process and byproduct:** Tesla says the refining technology platform is acid-free, avoiding hazardous byproducts like sodium sulfate common in traditional acid-roasting methods. Instead, it produces a benign co-product (Bevan calls it "analy," likely an aluminosilicate such as Analcime) consisting of sand and limestone, which Tesla says is being used in concrete mixes. Bevan: "Our process is more sustainable than traditional methods and eliminates hazardous byproducts." Tesla also claims the "fastest time to market" for this type of project by running feasibility, design, and construction in parallel.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the milestone significant for Tesla and the North American EV market, noting such projects typically take 5-10 years versus Tesla's roughly two years. He adds the refinery is less valuable now than when announced given lower lithium prices, but could become more useful if prices continue recent spikes. The article's top reader comment (54 likes) pushes back, noting mines/refineries of this type typically take 2-3 years from final investment decision (not an outlier), that the underlying process was developed by contractor Metso rather than being Tesla's own technology, and that ramp-up — not startup — is the real challenge, citing other lithium refineries (in Australia) that struggled to scale; the commenter says recent drone footage suggests the Tesla plant may not yet be running at meaningful capacity.
