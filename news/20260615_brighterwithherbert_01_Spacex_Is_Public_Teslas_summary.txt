This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel argues that the SpaceX IPO — described as the biggest IPO in stock market history — has set the stage for Tesla's own stock repricing, driven by a dense calendar of over 20 milestones between now and December 31, 2026. The host Herbert (alongside collaborator Cern Basher) walks through a milestone tracking table covering robotaxi expansion, FSD international rollouts, Optimus development, energy products, financials, and the dominant wild card: a possible SpaceX-Tesla merger as early as late July 2026. The core thesis is that even without a merger, Tesla's second-half 2026 milestone slate is the strongest the company has ever had, and the merger represents an asymmetric option layered on top.

## Robotaxi
1. **Current unsupervised fleet status:** Unsupervised robotaxi service (no safety driver, paying customers) is live in three cities — Austin, Dallas, and Houston. The existing paying-ride fleet is still composed of Model Y vehicles; no Cybercab has yet carried a paying passenger. Five additional cities are pending — Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas — though Tesla's language softened from "first half" to "preparations underway" as of April, suggesting some may slip into Q3 2026.

2. **City-by-city strategic significance:** Each new city launch serves as proof that the FSD system generalizes without city-specific mapping or depot infrastructure. Phoenix is highlighted as a direct competitive signal, as it is Waymo's home turf. Las Vegas offers friendly regulators, year-round weather, and the Boring Company tunnel infrastructure. Miami, Orlando, and Tampa are tourist markets where first-time riders (non-Tesla owners) become word-of-mouth ambassadors.

3. **Scale ramp as the key inflection:** Herbert identifies the Q3 unsupervised ramp — going from dozens of cars per city to hundreds and then thousands — as the milestone that transforms robotaxi from a headline into a modelable revenue line. FSD miles have crossed 11 billion cumulative, with a projection past 17 billion by year-end 2026; the most recent 1 billion miles took just 37 days, with over 4 billion total logged on city streets.

4. **Federal autonomous driving framework:** A national regulatory standard replacing the current state-by-state patchwork is tracked as a Q4 2026 milestone. If it lands, the city-by-city rollout map effectively becomes a national rollout map, though Herbert acknowledges Tesla controls nothing in Washington.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Europe rollout accelerating:** The Netherlands granted the first European approval for FSD (supervised) on April 10, 2026, through the Dutch authority RDW, with rollout to Dutch owners beginning the next day. Denmark followed, and Belgium approved on June 10, 2026 — with Tesla publishing roughly two months of Dutch safety and usage stats alongside the Belgium approval. The count now stands at 13 countries globally with FSD supervised, five of them in Europe. Tesla is targeting broader European rollout through summer 2026, country by country, with the possibility of an EU-wide approval.

2. **FSD China launch and scale potential:** Tesla made FSD supervised officially available in China on May 21, 2026, priced as a one-time purchase of approximately 64,01 RMB (~$9,400 USD). Tesla built a dedicated data center in Shanghai, partnered with BYD for mapping, and is hiring test drivers across nine major cities. Full fleet-wide regulatory approval (over-the-air to all eligible cars) is targeted for Q3 2026. With over 2 million Teslas on Chinese roads, Herbert notes that every 10% of the fleet purchasing FSD at $9,400 represents roughly $2 billion of high-margin software revenue (flagged as rough math with hedged attach rates).

3. **Self-reinforcing regulatory flywheel:** Each country that goes live generates local safety data that serves as evidence for the next country in the queue. Herbert argues this is why five European approvals in two months could look slow compared to the next six months, and why the approval pace is accelerating in pairs.

4. **FSD on customer-owned vehicles (unsupervised) — Q4 target:** Elon Musk stated on the Q1 2026 earnings call that unsupervised FSD reaching customer-owned vehicles (i.e., your own car driving with no one watching) should happen in Q4 2026. Herbert expresses skepticism about on-time delivery of this milestone.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybercab production underway:** Cybercab production began at Giga Texas on April 24, 2026, targeting hundreds of units per week with a ramp in progress. The table tracks two remaining Cybercab milestones for 2026: the first paid Cybercab ride (likely with an event) and Cybercab opening for customer orders in Q4 2026, the latter of which Herbert is skeptical will hit on schedule.

2. **Tesla Semi in production:** Tesla Semi started production in 2026 (already checked off the milestone table), retiring what Herbert describes as the "science project" bear-case narrative.

3. **10 million cumulative deliveries milestone:** Somewhere in Q3 2026, Tesla is expected to cross 10 million cumulative vehicles delivered — a brand milestone Herbert expects to land while the robotaxi narrative is peaking, amplifying headline impact.

4. **Next-gen Roadster demo:** Originally targeted for April 1, 2026, then pushed to late spring, and now pointed at August 2026. The car is reportedly ready; the SpaceX thruster package is described as the long pole. Herbert notes that Franz von Holzhausen stated in an interview just days before the video that the Roadster is "coming very soon" and will "blow people away" — which Herbert treats as more credible than prior schedule promises.

5. **Model S and Model X sunset:** The table tracks the winding down of the Model S and Model X lines, partly to free up tooling capacity for Optimus manufacturing.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus factory construction underway:** Construction started in Q2 2026 on a dedicated Optimus factory in Austin. Tesla's stated plan for the first-generation line is capacity for 1 million robots per year. Optimus units are currently being tested inside Tesla factories, though Tesla has not provided an officially confirmed unit count and has not given a 2026 production target. Wall Street models citing ~50,000 units for 2026 are characterized as analyst math, not company guidance.

2. **Optimus Gen 3 reveal and pilot production — Q3 target:** Gen 3 is penciled for a Q3 2026 reveal, possibly at the annual shareholder meeting. Leaked information on Gen 3 hand design points to approximately 50 actuators of dexterity. Herbert argues that if the reveal demonstrates hands capable of real factory tasks, the million-robot factory transitions from a prototype bet to a manufacturing bet — the thing Tesla has proven it can do. Gen 3 pilot production is also tracked for Q3.

3. **Digital Optimus / Macrohard — Q4 wild card:** A Q4 2026 row on the table tracks what Herbert calls the "digital Optimus reveal" — the Macrohard project — described as a software-only version of the robot brain doing knowledge work.

4. **Compute buildout supporting Optimus:** Cortex 2.0, the AI training cluster in Austin, is hitting 50% capacity in Q2 2026 and full capacity in Q3. Construction on an advanced technology chip fab (the follow-through on the Terafab chip project launched in Q1 2026) is also beginning, with Herbert describing Tesla making its own AI chips at scale as a 5-to-10-year statement.

## Energy
1. **Megapack 3 and Megablock production launch:** Megapack 3 and a new product called Megablock are set to begin production at a new Houston Megapack factory late in 2026. Each unit delivers 5 MWh — 28% more than Megapack 2 in the same footprint — out of a factory built for 50 GWh per year of capacity. Herbert describes energy as Tesla's best-margin hardware business and frames this as its "next gear."

## Financials
1. **Q2 2026 production and delivery report:** Q2 production and delivery numbers are expected to land in the first week of July 2026, providing the first data point on Cybercab ramp and robotaxi fleet growth.

2. **Q2 2026 earnings call — late July:** The Q2 2026 earnings call in late July is flagged as the event where robotaxi unit economics, city-by-city color, Cybercab ramp numbers, and FSD take rates following the Europe and China launches will be disclosed. Herbert frames this as the call where the story moves from narrative to arithmetic.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX IPO as the catalyst:** SpaceX's IPO — described as the biggest in stock market history — put a live public market price on "Elon execution," and Herbert argues the capital that watched SpaceX get repriced will now look directly at Tesla. The two companies entered the IPO week at nearly the same market capitalization.

2. **SpaceX-Tesla merger: the dominant wild card:** A possible merger between SpaceX and Tesla emerged from podcast speculation into mainstream financial discourse after CNBC reported in May 2026, citing a Tesla employee and others familiar with the talks, that the two companies are weighing a combination. Wolf Research says the idea has moved into the Wall Street mainstream. Dan Ives puts the odds at 80%; the prediction market Kalshi (referred to as "Calry" in the transcript) has it around 52% within the next year.

3. **Merger financial math and strategic logic:** Analysts running merger math are publishing a combined company valuation of around $3.4 trillion. Herbert describes a stock-for-stock "merger of equals" scenario where the exchange ratio is the critical variable — a near-par ratio would pull Tesla's price toward SpaceX's trading price at announcement, while a ratio favoring SpaceX holders would shrink Tesla's upside. SpaceX's own S1 filing language — stating the company "may issue a significant amount of equity in connection with future transactions" — is noted as potentially meaningful. Fortune's Sean Tully interpreted this as a forward signal of a large stock-funded acquisition, with Tesla being the only target that fits "significant" at SpaceX's scale.

4. **Why the IPO itself raised merger odds:** Herbert argues that a private SpaceX could not practically acquire Tesla with stock because there was no market price, no liquidity, and no way for a Tesla board to present shareholders with a fairness opinion. The IPO created a ticker, a tape, and a price the lawyers can use — meaning the structural tool to do the deal now exists for the first time.

5. **Bear case on the merger:** A national security review is flagged as a serious obstacle — SpaceX is a major defense contractor while Tesla runs major operations in China. SpaceX is also described as still posting losses (revenue up, profits negative), meaning no positive cash flow on day one of a combined company. Board committees, shareholder votes, and fairness reviews on both sides add further delay risk.

6. **Waymo competitive framing:** Herbert notes that Tesla's ability to light up new robotaxi cities in weeks rather than years — without city-specific maps or depot infrastructure — directly undermines Waymo's cost structure argument, which Herbert describes as "most of the long-term valuation debate."
