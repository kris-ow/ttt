This video from Dillon Loomis of the "Electrified" channel covers several major Tesla topics, with a particular focus on clarifying misunderstood Cybercab engineering details (front-wheel drive, cooling infrastructure, unboxed manufacturing), providing a measured reality check on the pace of Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi expansion, and contextualizing the competitive landscape including Waymo's financials and Xpeng's FSD rival. The core thesis is that while Tesla's progress is real and its cost advantages over competitors are enormous, expectations for near-term exponential scale should be tempered.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised Fleet Size and Realistic Framing:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised robotaxi fleet stands at 26 total vehicles — approximately 20 in Austin and 3 each in Dallas and Houston. Dillon Loomis cautions against framing incremental additions of 6 vehicles as "exponential growth," emphasizing that nearly a year after the robotaxi service launched, the fleet remains very small. He acknowledges the tracker likely captures only a few percent of actual rides logged, meaning there may be more active unsupervised vehicles than reported, but notes that at this small scale, the tracker should at least be in the ballpark.

2. **Near-Term City Expansion Expectations:** With approximately 60 days remaining before the end of June 2026, Tesla would need to launch in one new city every 12 days to meet the five new cities laid out in its earlier slide deck. Loomis considers this unlikely and advises expecting a gradual rollout rather than sudden exponential scaling. He notes that new unsupervised Tesla vehicles were spotted at Tesla Houston in Hempstead, and expects the Dallas and Houston fleets to each reach roughly 10 unsupervised vehicles within the next week or two.

3. **Cybercab FSD Calibration Challenge:** Loomis points out that just as FSD required recalibration time for the Cybertruck due to different camera placements, the Cybercab will similarly require real-world miles to validate its software before it can be deployed on the robotaxi network. He views the test Cybercabs being sent across the country as productively contributing to that validation process.

4. **Waymo's Deteriorating Unit Economics:** Alphabet's Q1 results show the "Other Bets" category — which includes Waymo — generated $411 million in revenue, down from $450 million in Q1 2025, while operating losses grew to $2.1 billion from $1.2 billion the prior year. Loomis estimates that after removing one-time items like stock-based compensation and assuming Waymo represents roughly 70% of the other bets category, Waymo is likely losing approximately $800 million per quarter. Framed another way, for every $1 in ride-fare revenue Waymo earns, its costs are in the neighborhood of $15–$20. Sundar Pichai has said Waymo won't be profitable until 2027 at the earliest. Waymo did raise $16 billion in February at a $126 billion valuation and continues to scale cities and update its fleet away from the Jaguar I-PACE.

5. **Competing Robotaxi Infrastructure Complexity:** The announcement that Hertz (via a new affiliate called Oro Mobility) will manage vehicle assets — charging, maintenance, repairs, cleaning, depot staffing — for the Uber/Lucid/Nuro Bay Area robotaxi service (targeting launch by end of 2026) is used to highlight the coordination costs of multi-party robotaxi models. Loomis draws a parallel to Waymo using Avis for fleet management in Dallas, arguing that each additional company in the chain seeks its own margin and slows approvals, contrasting this with Tesla's vertically integrated approach.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3.2 Rollout Status:** A new version of FSD 14.3.2 has rolled out to a few more users beyond the early access group, but is still not a wide release. As of recording, Loomis estimates roughly 90% of the fleet has yet to receive FSD 14.3. A new feature in this version is a popup dialogue when the driver disengages that now includes navigation as an option, and the screen requires a response — there is no way to exit it.

2. **FSD V14 Lite International Expansion:** Tesla has confirmed that following the rollout of FSD V14 Lite for Hardware 3 vehicles in the US, it plans to expand V14 Lite to additional international markets, ensuring Hardware 3 owners continue to receive updates. However, Tesla stated it cannot provide definitive dates at this time.

3. **EU FSD Approval Timeline:** Supervised FSD approval across the EU appears to be converging on a group decision rather than country-by-country action. Most nations are waiting for the TCMV meeting and an expected vote on June 30, 2026. In Italy, a senator has publicly pushed for fast-track approval, but the government responded that no decision has been taken at the European level. An EU-wide approval this summer remains on the table.

4. **China Suspends New AV Licenses After BYD Apollo Go Incident:** China has suspended issuing new licenses for autonomous vehicles following an incident in which more than 100 Apollo Go robotaxis stalled on city streets in Wuhan at the end of March. Regulators are requiring local governments to conduct safety reviews. The suspension blocks new robotaxi permits, new test projects, and city expansions but does not suspend current operations except for BYD. Loomis notes this has no immediate impact on Tesla since Tesla operates at Level 2, while the licenses specifically pertain to Level 4 vehicles.

5. **Xpeng VLA 2.0 as FSD Competitor:** Xpeng's VLA 2.0 system is receiving attention as a credible FSD rival, particularly in complex Chinese driving scenarios. The Xpeng CEO publicly claimed VLA 2.0 outperforms FSD in Chinese scenarios and made a public wager: if VLA 2.0 achieves within China the same road experience as FSD 14.2 in Silicon Valley by August 30, 2026, he will build a Chinese-style canteen in Silicon Valley; if it falls short, Xpeng's head of intelligent driving will run naked across the Golden Gate Bridge. Loomis contextualizes Xpeng's edge as expected given Tesla's limited local Chinese road data, and notes Xpeng still uses radar unlike Tesla's vision-only approach. A global VLA 2.0 rollout is expected in 2027 with Volkswagen as the inaugural launch partner in the Chinese market. The Xpeng CEO himself stated full unsupervised autonomy will arrive within 1–3 years.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi Production Begins at Giga Nevada:** The first Tesla Semi has officially come off the high-volume production line at the Tesla Semi factory in Giga Nevada. Elon Musk stated on the Q1 earnings call that initial production of the Cybercab and the Semi will be slow before ramping and going exponential toward the end of 2026 and into 2027. Loomis personally sets a modest bar of over 1,000 Semis for the remainder of 2026, and does not expect anything exponential from the Semi line until well into 2027. The long-range Tesla Semi has reportedly been quoted to a customer at $290,000 before destination fees and taxes; the standard-range variant is estimated around $260,000 (approximately 325-mile range) and the long-range at approximately 300,000 (500-mile range). For context, CARB data shows the average cost of a zero-emission Class 8 truck was $435,000 in 2024, and fleet buyers in California may receive incentives exceeding $100,000 per Semi purchase through the HVIP program. Tesla's stated goal is 50,000 Semis per year from the Nevada factory.

2. **4680 Battery Production Potentially Coming to Giga Nevada:** Analyst Hinrich Zayn suggests there is a decent chance the new Tesla Semi factory infrastructure includes 4680 battery cell and pack production, in addition to or alongside Semi vehicle production. This would make Giga Nevada the third 4680 production site alongside Fremont and Giga Texas, potentially streamlining supply chain logistics for the Semi. Loomis notes a 2023 announcement had previously targeted 100 GWh annually of 4680 cells at Giga Nevada, though Tesla later shifted its 4680 focus to Austin.

3. **Tesla Files "Tesla Base Charger" Trademark:** Tesla has filed for the trademark "Tesla Base Charger," with the description covering battery chargers for electric semi-truck vehicles, charging station services for electric semis, and charging/recharging batteries for electric semis. Filed at the beginning of April 2026, Loomis believes this is likely a depot or home-base charging solution targeting fleet operators.

4. **Cybercab Engineering Deep Dive — Front-Wheel Drive Confirmed:** Contrary to speculation, the Cybercab is definitively a front-wheel drive vehicle. Joe Tegtmeyer, who spoke on Brian from Futurazza's channel, confirmed this based on conversations with engineers, explaining that the unboxed process places the drive unit, cooling system, and all electronics in a front subassembly module that is bolted to the rest of the car as a complete unit — eliminating wiring runs, drivelines, and brake lines to the rear. The rear wheels are 21-inch aluminum alloy (for better rolling resistance) while the front wheels are 18-inch steel (lower cost, more durable for potholes). The steel front tires will require more frequent replacement due to carrying more of the vehicle weight.

5. **Cybercab as a Rolling Data Center:** Joe Justice observed that the Cybercab's cooling infrastructure is significantly larger than expected — potentially larger than even the Model S Plaid in certain components — which he theorizes reflects a "data center on wheels" architecture. He believes the vehicle is engineered to run distributed compute workloads when not actively in service (or when not at full processor utilization during rides), with the cooling system likely circulating through the battery pack and electronics. Loomis adds that the heavy daily mileage and charging cycles expected for a commercial robotaxi fleet would independently justify more robust cooling.

6. **Cybercab Unboxed Manufacturing and Paint Shop Elimination:** The Cybercab's injection-molded colored plastic panels eliminate the need for a traditional paint shop. A paint shop can cost $200 million to $1 billion to build, occupy up to 50% of factory footprint, consume approximately 60% of factory energy, and generate approximately 60% of plant CO2 emissions. Ryan McAffrey reported that Lars (Munro) said eliminating the paint shop saves Tesla approximately $500 million, with Lars not pushing back on that figure. Joe Tegtmeyer confirmed Tesla has perfected color matching across all panels. The result is a vehicle that likely costs approximately $20,000–$25,000 to produce and can drive itself — a competitive moat Loomis describes as nearly impossible to replicate.

7. **Cybercab Target Specs:** Tesla has stated the Cybercab will achieve approximately 5.5 miles per kWh in efficiency. Lars Munro has said the target battery pack size is under 50 kWh and the range target is approximately 300 miles — which Loomis notes would require closer to 6 miles per kWh to achieve.

8. **Used Tesla Market Outperforming:** Over the past 90 days per CarGurus, average used Tesla prices are up nearly 18%, versus a 6% increase for the broader CarGurus index. Used EVs without a Tesla badge have lost more than 10% of their value in the past year, while Teslas and hybrids have held stable. Tesla's Model X was the fastest-selling used car in March 2026. Consumer demand is described as sharply focused on hybrids and Teslas over EVs from legacy automakers.
