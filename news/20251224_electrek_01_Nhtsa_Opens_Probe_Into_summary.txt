NHTSA has opened a defect petition (DP25002) into the manual/emergency door release mechanisms on the Model Year 2022 Tesla Model 3, following reports that occupants and first responders struggled to locate them during crashes, including some fatal incidents.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Scope and trigger of the probe:** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation estimates approximately 179,071 Model Year 2022 Tesla Model 3 vehicles are covered. The petition alleges the mechanical door release is "hidden, unlabeled, and not intuitive to locate during an emergency," and the subject is listed as "Emergency egress controls are not readily accessible and clearly identifiable." The ODI's summary report currently associates one injury incident with this specific petition, though the article notes separate Bloomberg and Post investigations have found several fatal crashes in which door handle and opening designs may have played a role.

2. **Process status:** The investigation is in the "Defect Petition" phase, where NHTSA will evaluate the issue and decide whether to grant or deny the petition — this is an early-stage review, not a confirmed defect finding or recall.

3. **Design context:** Tesla's electronic door handles require power to operate; in a total power loss (which can occur in severe crashes), occupants or rescuers must use manual mechanical overrides, which are the subject of the complaint.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the front manual release is well-known to owners (sometimes pulled by mistake), but considers the rear release process — citing a reader comment describing a multi-step, hidden mechanism in a 2024 Model 3 — likely not discoverable by most people in an emergency without having read the owner's manual. He argues that if first responders are struggling to extract occupants, it's a serious problem, and that Tesla's minimalist design approach needs stronger safety redundancy; he speculates a possible outcome could range from added labeling/stickers to retrofitting a more obvious physical lever, if NHTSA finds the design delays egress.
