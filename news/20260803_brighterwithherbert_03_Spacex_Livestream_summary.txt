This video is a two-part livestream (pre-show analyst panel plus live coverage of SpaceX's first earnings call as a public company) covering Q2 2026 results, with a large panel of SpaceX-focused analysts and commentators reacting in real time to the financial deck and management's prepared remarks and Q&A. The core thesis discussed is that SpaceX's business now spans three segments — space/launch, connectivity (Starlink), and AI/compute (xAI/Grok) — with AI compute increasingly seen as the dominant near-term driver of the investment story alongside continued Starship development.

## Financials
1. **Q2 2026 headline results beat expectations:** SpaceX reported quarterly revenue of $7.8 billion (up 92% year-over-year from $4.1 billion), a net loss of $541 million (an improvement of $467 million year-over-year), and adjusted EBITDA of $3.5 billion (up 191% year-over-year from $1.2 billion) — panelists described this as beating both their own internal models and Wall Street consensus.
2. **Balance sheet and capital raises:** SpaceX ended Q2 with $100 billion in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities and $47.5 billion in backlog (spanning data-center leasing, Starlink enterprise/government, and launch). The quarter included an IPO raising approximately $85.7 billion in net proceeds and a $25 billion investment-grade senior notes offering (five tranches, weighted average interest rate of 5.855%, average maturity 11.7 years), partly used to repay a $20 billion bridge loan.
3. **Capex breakdown:** Total company capex in Q2 was approximately $18.4 billion, of which roughly $15.8 billion supported AI compute infrastructure; management indicated the next two quarters should look similar from a capex-level perspective.
4. **Segment profitability shifts:** Space segment adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $205 million; connectivity segment adjusted EBITDA rose 64% year-over-year to $2.6 billion; the AI segment turned adjusted EBITDA positive at $1.1 billion after narrowing its net operating loss to $1.3 billion (a 49% improvement vs. Q1 2026).
5. **Forward revenue targets accelerated:** CFO Brett stated SpaceX expects to reach a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate (ARR) by December 2026 "even if we did nothing further," with Elon Musk saying the figure will likely be higher. Musk also said internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029.
6. **Short interest and stock reaction:** A panelist cited roughly 34% short interest as a percentage of float, calling SpaceX one of the most illiquid and heavily shorted mega-cap stocks, partly attributed to speculative hedging by early/lock-up-restricted shareholders. Despite beating estimates, the stock reportedly rose intraday before falling in after-hours trading, which panelists attributed to the market awaiting management's forward guidance and reacting to broader tech-sector weakness (citing AMD's light guidance).

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Two successful Starship V3 flights in 90 days:** Flight 12 (May) achieved a successful liftoff from the new Starbase pad, precision landing, and deployment of modified V2 Starlink satellites; Flight 13 (July) achieved all objectives including deploying 20 production V3 satellites, an in-space Raptor relight, and what SpaceX called the softest-ever splashdown, leaving an intact heat shield for study.
2. **Heat shield deemed effectively solved:** Musk stated he considers the heat shield problem solved based on data and visual inspection from Flight 13, pushing back on a recent third-party report characterizing it as still a major obstacle; he said SpaceX sees no remaining technical obstacles to achieving full and rapid reusability, though further improvements will continue.
3. **Aggressive cadence and reusability targets:** Management said they will attempt a ship catch as soon as the next flight (tentatively end of this month) and aim to catch both booster and ship by year-end. They target roughly one Starship flight per day within about a year, and Starship aims to quadruple payload capacity and cut launch costs 10x versus Falcon 9.
4. **Human-rating and lunar timeline:** Musk said SpaceX expects to achieve a safety level sufficient for crewed Starship flights by the end of next year, with Artemis 3 (docking with Orion) planned for next year, an uncrewed direct lunar cargo mission to follow, and a goal of "boots on the moon" in 2028.
5. **Falcon 9 transition underway:** A panelist detailed infrastructure evidence (converted launch towers, repurposed drone ships, new towers at Cape Canaveral and Starbase) showing SpaceX is actively transitioning launch infrastructure from Falcon 9 toward Starship.
6. **Scale ambition versus current output:** Musk said Falcon-based launches currently deliver roughly 2,500 tons to orbit per year (80–90% of global mass to orbit), versus an aspiration of "well over a million tons" and ultimately up to 10 million tons per year via Starship.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and revenue growth:** Connectivity segment revenue rose 66% year-over-year to $4.3 billion; Starlink reached 12 million subscribers as of Q2 end (doubling year-over-year, up 1.7 million net adds sequentially — the company's best quarter of net adds to date), while ARPU held stable at $66/month.
2. **V3 satellites framed as a step-change:** Musk said Starlink V3 satellites are roughly an order of magnitude more capable than V2, with plans to launch roughly an order of magnitude more of them — implying a potential ~100x increase in delivered bandwidth, which he said could still mean a 10x revenue increase even if per-bit monetization dropped by a factor of 10.
3. **Enterprise, government, and aviation expansion:** New agreements were signed with American Airlines and other carriers (Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, Aer Lingus), plus mobile partnerships with SoftBank, NTT DoCoMo, and Spark New Zealand. Management said aviation penetration remains under 10%, and expects enterprise/government revenue to eventually exceed consumer revenue; SpaceX won over $6 billion in multi-year US government (Space Force) Star Shield contracts in Q2.
4. **Starlink Mobile buildout strategy:** SpaceX plans to launch next-generation Starlink Mobile (V2) satellites next year, later integrating 65 MHz of Echostar/EchoStar spectrum (FCC-approved) for a service management described as up to 100x better than the current ~5 MHz system. Management outlined a low-capex "outside-in" terrestrial strategy using small cellular base stations attached to existing Starlink dish hardware rather than large traditional cell towers, targeting a share of the roughly $600 billion US mobile carrier market (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute capacity scaling rapidly:** Nameplate compute reached 1.4 gigawatts in Q2 (up from 1.0 GW in Q1 and 400 MW a year earlier), with plans to exceed 2 gigawatts by year-end. Musk said SpaceX aims for "closer to 10" gigawatts of cumulative compute online by the end of next year (versus 5 GW), with a tentative target of 20 gigawatts of power/cooling capacity, acknowledging some projects may slip.
2. **Cloud services contracts and Cursor acquisition:** SpaceX signed cloud services agreements totaling $14.1 billion in contracted sales (generating $1.6 billion in incremental Q2 AI infrastructure revenue) and announced an agreement to acquire Cursor for $60 billion to accelerate AI enterprise offerings, expected to close in Q3 2026. An additional $6.7 billion of cloud services revenue was contracted in the first weeks of Q3, ramping from October.
3. **Exclusive Nvidia partnership:** Management confirmed SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's (Blackwell/Rubin-generation) architecture going forward, citing close partnership and expecting to receive a "very significant percentage" of Nvidia's GPU supply next year; only about 10% of compute is expected to be reserved for internal Grok training, with the rest available for external leasing/inference.
4. **Star Mind AI satellite program:** SpaceX announced an agreement with Nvidia for its first Star Mind AI satellite compute payload, described as an optimized Nvidia NVL72-class computer intended for both orbital and terrestrial deployment; satellite launches are planned to begin next year, ahead of the dedicated Terafab satellite manufacturing facility being completed.
5. **Grok model roadmap:** Grok 4.5 (released July) reportedly tripled token consumption after launch; Grok 4.6 is expected roughly the following week and Grok 4.7 about three-to-four weeks out, with Grok 5 (incorporating SpaceX's full historical data corpus) targeted before year-end.
6. **Compute monetization economics:** Management characterized new AI capital deployments as achieving less than a one-year payback, with Musk offering a rough personal guess of $30–$50 of monetization per watt for Nvidia's next-gen chips, and arguing that AI compute demand (growing roughly 200%+ per year, versus ~20% memory supply growth) should support rising, not falling, prices.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **No merger discussion on the call:** Panelists noted the earnings call did not address a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger; one panelist argued this was appropriate since it wasn't a topic for an earnings call, while others speculated SpaceX would simply decline to comment if asked.
2. **Panel valuation view:** One investor panelist argued the results were "very good" and that the market (similar to Tesla in the past) does not yet know how to properly value SpaceX's new, multi-segment business model, expressing continued bullishness despite the stock's after-hours decline.
