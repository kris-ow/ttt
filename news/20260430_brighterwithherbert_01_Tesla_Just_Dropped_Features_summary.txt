This video from "brighterwithherbert" features host Herbert and guest Brian White (YouTube channel: Future Aza) reviewing several new Tesla software and product features, arguing that some have the potential to go viral with mainstream audiences. The discussion covers FSD v14.3.2 and Smart Summon improvements, the Grok wake-word integration, a new Supercharger virtual queue feature, and the upcoming X Money platform. The core thesis is that Tesla's technology has reached a level of polish where individual features — particularly Smart Summon and agentic Grok — could capture widespread public attention.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD v14 Light Coming to Hardware 3 Vehicles and International Markets:** Tesla announced that following a future rollout of FSD version 14 light for Hardware 3 vehicles in the US, they plan to expand version 14 light to additional international markets. The announcement was notable because Tesla stated it proactively to reassure Hardware 3 owners, with Elon Musk reposting the message. The hosts note that Hardware 3 vehicles will not support unsupervised FSD but will continue receiving meaningful software updates. They estimate that under 400,000 Hardware 3 owners actually purchased FSD outright (in the US), and speculate that roughly half of those may have already upgraded to Hardware 4 vehicles following upgrade incentives — suggesting approximately 200,000 Hardware 3 FSD purchasers remain. The motivation for Tesla is clear: a compelling version 14 light could drive new paid FSD subscriptions, which the hosts note have already climbed sharply into the hundreds of millions of dollars in recurring revenue.

2. **FSD v14.3.2 Rolling Out Wide — Community Reports Major Improvement:** FSD version 14.3.2 is described as now truly rolling out widely, with the Tesla owner community reporting it is a "magnitude change" over v14.3.1 — notably cleaner parking behavior and faster reactions. The hosts also note a new disengagement popup in v14.3.2 that includes a navigation suggestion, indicating Tesla is still actively collecting disengagement reason data from FSD users to resolve edge cases. A future version is mentioned as being expected to handle pothole avoidance more intelligently, with the car already beginning to swerve around dark spots on pavement.

3. **Smart Summon Unified with FSD and Robotaxi Model:** A significant architectural change in v14.3.2 is that Tesla has unified Smart Summon, FSD, and the robotaxi stack into a single model for more capable and reliable behavior. Previously, Smart Summon operated on a separate code path; now it uses the same neural network and end-to-end architecture as FSD and the robotaxi system. The result is that weight updates propagate across all driving modes simultaneously. Community users report Smart Summon is now dramatically faster, smoother, and more confident — with one video comparison shown on screen demonstrating the improvement. The hosts suggest this feature, especially the "Banish" variant (where the car drops you off and self-parks), could be the feature most likely to go viral when observed by unsuspecting bystanders in parking lots.

4. **Grok Wake Word Integration and Agentic Aspirations:** Tesla has added a "Hey Grok" wake word to its in-car Grok integration, which the host views as a meaningful usability improvement over requiring a button press. CNBC is highlighted as a mainstream outlet now showcasing real-world Grok usage inside a Tesla, with a reporter visibly surprised by the experience. The hosts acknowledge that at present, Grok in the car is primarily a large language model for conversation, route requests, and navigation suggestions — not yet for direct vehicle control. However, the host describes two personal anecdotal instances where Grok appeared to respond to voice commands by physically controlling the car (changing lanes off an HOV lane, and finding an alternate parking spot), though he is uncertain whether these were tests or coincidences. The hosts express that true agentic control of the vehicle — telling Grok where to park, what route to take, or to autonomously complete errands — is the feature that will be transformative, and speculate it may require Hardware AI 5 for sufficient inference capability, though they are uncertain.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Hardware 3 FSD Purchaser Numbers Smaller Than Perceived:** The host clarifies that while Hardware 3 vehicles number in the multi-millions, the count of Hardware 3 owners who actually purchased FSD outright is under 400,000 in the US. With upgrade incentives offered by Tesla and apparent uptake, the hosts estimate the remaining addressable Hardware 3 FSD purchaser base may be around 200,000 vehicles. Elon Musk himself is quoted as having noted that relatively few people outright purchased FSD, though some paid as much as $15,000 or $12,000 for it.

## Market & Competition

1. **Supercharger Virtual Queue Feature:** Tesla has launched a virtual queue feature for Superchargers, allowing drivers to join a digital waitlist when a station is at capacity rather than physically queuing with their vehicle. Drivers near a full Supercharger can join the queue; Tesla manages the order and notifies drivers via phone when it is their turn. The system is currently honor-based — Tesla sends a message asking if the driver wants to start charging immediately, but does not physically block a stall. The hosts contextualize this as infrastructure groundwork for robotaxi fleet orchestration, where multiple Cybercabs arriving at a Supercharger simultaneously will need automated queue management. They also note existing Supercharger management tools: rerouting to less busy stations, and idle fees starting at 80% state of charge at high-utilization stations.

2. **X Money Launch Imminent with 6% Interest and 3% Cash Back:** Elon Musk's X app is described as preparing to launch X Money, a banking and payments platform offering 6% interest on savings, 3% cash back on eligible purchases, and free peer-to-peer transfers. The feature is set to launch this month (April/May 2026). X has received money transmission licenses in 44 states but not all 50 — Washington State is cited as one state where it is not yet available. Brian White cautions that high introductory rates are common among startup banking products to attract depositors and may not be permanent, citing T-Mobile Money as a prior example. The hosts note X Money will also involve some form of crypto integration.
