## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab testing footprint has expanded to 24 states and 88 cities, up two states and 32 cities in 20 days, per community tracker Py. Jo Bhakdi estimates 1,000–2,000 Cybercabs are currently deployed nationally, with over half carrying steering wheels for ongoing validation and possibly not converting to driverless service; he projects a fleet run-rate near 2,000 by September and over 10,000 by December if scaling holds.
- Tesla holds a Cybercab launch event September 3rd in Austin, with a golden-ticket sweepstakes for public riders and Tesla employees expected to ride ahead of the public opening. Bhakdi expects the initial rollout to exceed a token number of vehicles.
- Nevada approved Tesla, Uber, and Waymo to operate robotaxis in Las Vegas; Tesla starts with 10 vehicles in a geofenced area, with statewide allotments up to 5,000 for the market, per futureaza's reporting of Sawyer Merritt coverage.
- Brandenburg, Germany's transport minister confirmed talks with Tesla on deploying driverless shuttles for rural areas with sparse bus service; Bhakdi frames it as politically significant but preliminary, with a rough TAM estimate of ~2 million robotaxis to serve rural US and Europe.
- An EU vote on approving supervised FSD is expected around early October (possibly Oct. 8), which Bhakdi believes gives Tesla incentive to keep any pre-vote Cybercab rollout small and flawless.
- Cybercabs have reportedly been spotted parked in Baltimore and Detroit, taken as a signal of possible future city expansion; Tesla has not applied for a California AV testing permit.

## Humanoid Bots
- Giga Texas Optimus factory construction has progressed from bare dirt in March to a steel-and-roof structure in five months, per footage shared by Joe Tegtmeyer, with an estimated 6–8 months of construction remaining; the facility's stated target is 10 million Optimus units per year.
- Jason Calacanis, after an Optimus factory visit, claimed Optimus hardware is "one and a half generations" ahead of Chinese competitors like Unitree and its "brain" roughly five generations ahead, predicting one billion Optimus units in the world within about ten years — his own forecast, not company guidance.
- Pioneer Lands' base-case model projects roughly half a billion Optimus robots by 2040, with a bull case near two billion, per Jo Bhakdi.
- A separate host claims Tesla's Q4 January earnings call announced discontinuation of Model S and Model X with Fremont lines converted to Optimus manufacturing targeting millions of units per year — framed as evidence Tesla sees robotics as bigger than its car business.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla raised Cybertruck prices $5,000 on two of three trims via an August 24 configurator update: Dual Motor AWD to $74,990, Premium AWD to $84,990; Cyberbeast unchanged at $99,990. No spec changes accompanied the increase.
- Gigafactory Texas's Cybertruck line, built for up to 250,000 units/year, is reportedly selling under 20,000 units/year — under 10% of capacity — with roughly 3,000 trucks in US inventory, per Electrek. The outlet notes SpaceX and Musk-controlled entities accounted for a meaningful share of Q4 2025 US registrations, and calculates non-Musk registrations would have fallen sharply year-over-year excluding those purchases.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX announced a new launch site, informally floated as "Starbase Louisiana," in Vermillion Parish near Pecan Island, designed for high-cadence Starship operations after a roughly seven-year site search. Executives project "north of 10,000" jobs (SpaceX's formal commitment is at least 3,000 permanent jobs) and committed to coastal wetland restoration in an area losing shoreline at 3–23 feet per year.
- Musk said the Falcon program is approaching its 700th flight and reiterated Starship reusability progress, with SpaceX now landing and reflying boosters routinely.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said Starlink has grown to almost 11,000 satellites — more than double all other operators combined — with plans to scale toward 100,000 satellites under the V3 generation, and said he believes Starlink could eventually carry over 90% of global internet traffic.
- Grok Voice is reportedly selling roughly 3,000 Starlink kits per week and resolving over 15,000 support calls per day, per poster-relayed figures.
- SpaceX owns 65 MHz of nationwide spectrum purchased from EchoStar for roughly $19.6 billion. Musk denied a separate rumored $6 billion purchase of additional spectrum from Grain Management, though commentators note ambiguity remains.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX and Nvidia unveiled a space-optimized version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack, intended to launch to orbit in Q4 2026 as the basis for the first Starmind AI satellite, scaling further in 2028; Musk described it as simpler, denser, and lighter than a terrestrial rack.
- Nvidia separately announced SpaceXAI will deploy Vera CPUs to accelerate agentic AI applications tied to Grok and orbital datacenter plans.
- Colossus and Colossus 2 in the Memphis area collectively provide about 1 gigawatt of compute, with plans to scale roughly tenfold across campuses next year, per one source's account of xAI/SpaceX statements.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- JP Morgan reiterated a SpaceX overweight rating with a $240 price target, citing Grok/Cursor momentum; Cursor is cited at $4 billion ARR, 75% from businesses.
- Commentators continue to debate Tesla-SpaceX merger timing, with one host guessing "next year or maybe the year after" and others framing chip design, data centers, Starship, and Starlink integration as building a capital-efficiency case for combination — all speculative, not company guidance.
