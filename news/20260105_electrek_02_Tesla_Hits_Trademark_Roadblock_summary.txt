Electrek reports that Tesla's USPTO trademark application for "Cybercab" has been officially suspended, largely because Tesla failed to file paperwork before publicly unveiling the name, allowing another company to file first.

## Robotaxi
1. **USPTO suspends Cybercab trademark:** A USPTO suspension notice (dated November 14, 2025) for Tesla's application (Serial No. 98806788) to trademark "Cybercab" cites two issues: a likelihood of confusion with existing Registration No. 5963829, and a prior-filed application (Serial No. 79412082) with an earlier filing date, to which the USPTO must give priority.

2. **Timeline shows Tesla filed late:** Elon Musk unveiled "Cybercab" on October 10, 2024 at the robotaxi event. French beverage company Unibev filed a trademark application for "Cybercab" in the vehicle category on October 28, 2024, before Tesla filed its own application in November 2024. The article frames this as Unibev likely trademark-squatting after watching the event.

3. **Unibev's possible motive:** Unibev holds three existing US trademarks for "TESLAQUILA," a name Tesla previously tried to use for its branded tequila, suggesting a possible prior grievance against Tesla.

4. **Negotiations underway, no deal yet:** The examining attorney (Meghan Reinhart) rejected Tesla's arguments against the refusal. A source familiar with the matter told Electrek that Tesla and Unibev are in negotiations, but no agreement has been reached. Tesla's options are to pay Unibev to abandon its claim, argue in court that Unibev has no genuine intent to build cars, or rebrand again — this follows a prior USPTO rejection of Tesla's "Robotaxi" trademark application for being too generic.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues this episode shows Tesla was "unprepared and rushed" in its autonomous-driving marketing, having announced a major product name on stage without first securing the trademark; he says the situation, while rewarding squatting, is "entirely Tesla's fault." A top reader comment (quoted by the article) similarly attributes it to rushed announcements serving stock-price hype.
