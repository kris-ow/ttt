Electrek, citing a Handelsblatt report, describes a December 2 "Giga-Event" Tesla held for Gigafactory Berlin employees featuring rapper Kool Savas, held ahead of an early-2026 works council election against IG Metall, the German metalworkers' union.

## Market & Competition
1. **Awkward anti-union concert:** According to Handelsblatt's report, Tesla organized the event to boost morale and campaign against IG Metall. Rapper Kool Savas performed from a Cybertruck, tried unsuccessfully to start an "Elon, Elon" chant, and after a lukewarm crowd response reportedly asked employees if they had a "stick up their ass" (Stock im Arsch); witnesses and video described the scene as awkward.

2. **Works council election context:** In the 2024 works council vote, IG Metall won the largest faction but not a majority. A new election is scheduled for early 2026, and Tesla management is reportedly concerned the union could gain majority control amid falling European sales and Elon Musk's declining popularity.

3. **Wage increase and competing claims:** Gigafactory Berlin manager André Thierig announced a 4% wage increase at the event and claimed employees would have gotten only 2% under IG Metall collective bargaining — a claim Electrek calls false. IG Metall welcomed the raise but said Tesla needs a further 30% increase to match German auto industry pay.

4. **Expansion tied to election outcome:** Per Handelsblatt's report, Thierig suggested Tesla's expansion plans at the factory, including recently announced battery production, depend on the outcome of the works council election. IG Metall chief Otto, quoted in the report, characterized this as a recurring pattern of linking factory expansion to employees avoiding the union.

5. **European sales and job security:** Tesla reiterated that jobs for the factory's 11,000 employees are secure, but the article notes Tesla's European sales have fallen over 30% in 2025, following a 10% year-over-year decline in 2024. Giga Berlin has been operational for almost four years and still produces only the Model Y.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the union is having a positive effect on Tesla workers (evidenced by the pre-vote wage increase) and criticizes Tesla's anti-union rhetoric as unnecessary, saying management and the union should simply make their respective cases. He also expresses concern about job security at the German factory, suggesting the Tesla Semi program may be the plant's best hope for growth, with the factory otherwise more likely to shrink than expand.
