## Brief

- NHTSA drafts performance-based AV safety standard, not design-based
- NHTSA sends Tesla 21-question audit, due Sept 30
- Starship Flight 14 slips to Sept 28, carries 26 Starlink V3 sats
- SpaceX discloses new ~$13B ARR hosting deal, unnamed customer
- Tesla US EV share hits 52% as market shrinks 30%
- Musk won't rule out eventual Tesla-SpaceX merger

## Autonomous Driving

- NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison said the agency is building a new federal AV standard focused on driving performance rather than physical controls like steering wheels, calling it "a scalpel" not "a hatchet." Five FMVSS amendments are already proposed and a new SAE-partnered consortium, Ascend, was launched to accelerate national AV standards — a structural tailwind for pedal- and wheel-free Cybercab.
- NHTSA issued Tesla a 21-question special order under oath tied to the Cybercab audit, due September 30 with penalties up to ~$28,000/day; questions probe certification basis, temporary human controls, and why Tesla didn't pursue the 2,500-unit exemption route Waymo used. Tesla is self-certifying rather than seeking that exemption, per Lars Moravy's April statement.
- Tesla reported FSD Supervised vehicles in Australia/New Zealand had 40% fewer collisions than manually driven ones — one collision per 1.1 million miles vs. 665,000 miles manually.
- Zurich Insurance began offering an FSD Supervised discount (estimated ~5%, unconfirmed) in Australia, the first insurer there and second globally after Lemonade (~50% discount in the US), citing Tesla's "seven times fewer collisions" safety-report figure.
- Ashok Elluswamy said FSD V15 will bring earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction time, and better collision avoidance; the next V15 tech merge, expected "next month or so," is meant to enable 24/7 robotaxi operation.

## Robotaxi

- Texas robotaxi registrations reached 439 vehicles (390 Model Y, 49 Cybercab) as of Monday, up from ~186 a month earlier, but growth has stalled — only 7 added in the most recent week after a single-day jump around the September 3 Cybercab launch. Tracker operator Ethan McKenna estimates roughly 300 of the 439 are driving driverless; unsupervised miles remain at the 1 million figure Elluswamy announced September 3, with Tesla citing >10% weekly growth against a host estimate closer to 20%/week.
- Tesla is training first responders on Cybercab and Model Y Robotaxi operations, including the on-screen joystick used to move Cybercab in place of a steering wheel.
- Commentary (Cern Basher/Brilliant Advice) argues Tesla's dynamic and network-level pricing — repositioning vehicles ahead of demand, pricing by fleet-wide value rather than per-mile, eventually folding in owner-operated and third-party Cybercabs — could differentiate robotaxi economics from Uber's driver-optimized model; this remains speculative framing, not a Tesla-confirmed strategy.

## Humanoid Bots

- Chinese media reported a Tesla team began a mass-production supplier audit in Ningbo this week; unit-count figures across reports are inconsistent and Tesla has given no 2026 production number. Optimus V3 has not been shown and Musk did not discuss Optimus at the All-In Summit.

## Electric Vehicles

- Tesla's US EV market share rose to 52% year-to-date through August, up from 43% a year earlier (WSJ/Motor Intelligence), but Tesla's US sales are down 16% while the overall EV market fell ~30% — share gains reflect faster pullbacks from Ford, GM, Honda, and VW, not Tesla growth. Model Y alone is about a third of all US EV sales.
- Tesla reopened Roadster reservations at unchanged terms ($5,000 deposit plus $45,000 wire due within 10 days); Roadster reveal dated October 1.
- Tokyo is reportedly offering Model Y under $23,000 due to a weak yen and new subsidies, among the cheapest Model Y pricing globally.
- Korean media reported Samsung's Taylor, Texas fab began trial 2nm production of Tesla's AI5 chip ahead of a planned November start, with volume supply in 2027 — unconfirmed by Samsung or Tesla but consistent with Musk's statement that AI5 will sample this year at both TSMC Arizona and Samsung Texas.

## Energy

- Tesla began construction on a $650 million Megapack facility in Belgium using next-gen Megablock technology and Megapack V3, sized at 2.8 GWh to power 385,000 homes.
- Tesla's Nevada Semi factory (design capacity 1,000 trucks/week) opens September 24; fleet uptime is at 98% this year versus 95% two years prior. European Semi deliveries start 2027, with up to 550,000 km range at 40 tons and Megachargers recovering 60% range in 30 minutes.

## Financials

- Morgan Stanley's Andrew Percoco modeled an autonomous electric Semi as an FSD revenue product, pricing Tesla's software take at 85 cents–$1/mile ($12,000–$18,000/month per truck); the host flags this as hypothetical pending actual Semi autonomy.

## SpaceX — Starship

- Starship Flight 14 slipped from September 22 to "as early as" September 28, pending regulatory approval; the flight will fly ~6 orbits over ~10 hours with no catch attempt on ship or booster.
- Flight 14 will carry 26 Starlink V3 satellites (1 terabit/sec each, ~26 terabits total), which SpaceX's CFO called the company's first "revenue generating flight."
- Musk and Cathie Wood floated a speculative $10 trillion/year Starship revenue scenario by 2030, built on stacked, unconfirmed assumptions (future constellation bandwidth, full-load launch economics, and a flight cadence far beyond Starship's 13 total flights to date); this conflicts with Musk's separately stated ~$1 trillion 2030 SpaceX revenue figure.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

- SpaceX CFO Brett Johnson disclosed a new, unnamed hosting deal worth roughly $13 billion in ARR starting December 1, and said SpaceX has "even more conviction" in a $100 billion ARR target by year-end; the customer is unconfirmed (existing named deals are Anthropic and Google).
- Musk said he is "highly confident" SpaceX will run Nvidia GB200-class computers in space next year, moved up from the "as early as 2028" timeline in SpaceX's May IPO filing — the third time this date has moved earlier.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- Musk declined to rule out an eventual Tesla-SpaceX merger at the All-In Summit, giving a sarcastic non-answer; Shotwell said SpaceX and xAI are "integrating faster than they thought" but not fully integrated. No terms, timeline, or filing exist.
- SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 weighting more than doubled at close, estimated to trigger $15.5B–$22B in programmatic index-fund buying — one of the largest single-day passive flows of the quarter; only 4-5% of SPCX shares were held by index funds when it joined the index July 7.

## SpaceX — Launch Business

- SpaceX won a new $946 million NASA contract covering launch services for three missions, including ground, launch, in-orbit, and return/recovery operations plus a lifeboat capability.

## SpaceX — Starlink

- United Airlines reported over 31 million passengers flown on Starlink-powered aircraft across 464,000+ flights, powering 14.2 million connected devices; passengers can now watch NFL games on Starlink-enabled seatback screens.
