## Brief

- Tesla robotaxi models project $14B–$80B revenue run-rate by end-2027
- FSD v15 already running in robotaxi fleet, per Elluswamy
- Cybercab has no fleet-size cap, exec confirms
- TSLA down ~38% in five days on earnings-call reaction
- SpaceX–Tesla merger speculation intensifies after M&A lawyer on earnings call
- Tesla ships first domestic LFP Megapack cells from Sparks, NV

## Robotaxi
- Scenario modeling anchored to Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 comment on "double-digit," "high compounding" weekly growth in unsupervised miles projects, in the base case, ~900–1,000 vehicles and an ~$120M annualized revenue run rate by end-2026, scaling to ~200,000–230,000 vehicles and a ~$40B run rate by end-2027 (brighterwithherbert/Cern Basher analysis); an optimistic case reaches ~$80B by end-2027 but would need Cybercab capacity near 2 million units/year.
- A Tesla executive ("Lars," per futureaza) confirmed there is no fleet-size cap on Cybercab, quashing speculation of a ~2,500-unit limit; self-certification under FMVSS is described as settled, contrasted favorably with Zoox's waiver-based path.
- Cybercab traffic reported as frequent and near-continuous in Palo Alto and at Giga Texas test tracks, with deployment to Tesla sales/service hubs across the US and Canada for regional testing.
- Some investors accuse Musk of deliberately slow-walking robotaxi to smooth a SpaceX merger; a jobhakdi host dismisses this as unfounded, arguing Musk's attention has simply shifted to Optimus while the robotaxi team executes without him.
- One host (futureaza) argues the market misread cautious earnings-call commentary (Musk's ~10% figure, safety-first framing) as ruling out mass scale this year, and still sees mass deployment as plausible before year-end.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD version 15 is already running in the robotaxi fleet, per Ashok Elluswamy; consumer unsupervised operation is not yet active. Attendees at a Model Y L event were reportedly testing an unreleased, newer FSD build (futureaza).
- FSD attach rate on new vehicle sales is now ~55%; paid FSD sales reportedly rose ~180,000 in Q1 and ~150,000 in Q2 (host-cited figures).
- Tesla's EMEA account reported FSD supervised is over 5.2x safer than manual driving across ~65 million km in five EU countries over four months — lower than a ~7.4–7.6x figure cited for the US, which a host attributes to smaller sample size and less European road training.
- Elluswamy has described an upcoming FSD version as "ridiculously safe," which analysts frame as a potential step-change ("stair-step") in robotaxi growth rather than smooth continuation — though unconfirmed until released (brighterwithherbert).

## Financials
- Tesla Q2 (reported July 22): revenue $28.2B, +26% YoY, beat estimates; EPS missed at $0.33 vs. $0.49 expected; capex $5.79B, +142% YoY, attributed to Optimus/FSD infrastructure spend.
- TSLA has fallen sharply since earnings — cited at roughly 38% over five days and 26% over one month by one host — with commentary noting the stock is more oversold than any point in over a year, a historically bullish setup though not a guarantee.
- One analyst (Herbert, futureaza) argues Tesla's elevated P/E (~200) won't normalize on a 10-year horizon given continuous new business launches, but could compress toward the mid-30s once robotaxi revenue materializes.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Merger speculation escalated after Musk reportedly brought an M&A lawyer onto the earnings call; neither Musk nor Gwynne Shotwell denied merger speculation when asked (futureaza). A paraphrased, unconfirmed characterization has Shotwell saying a combination "would sure make Elon's life better."
- Debate remains split: bulls see SpaceX's Starlink/AI-compute revenue as comparable in scale to Tesla's robotaxi/Optimus opportunity; skeptics warn a merger could reset investor expectations to another multi-year wait for profit, and one host says he'd reject a deal at current prices but would support one near $600–$800/share.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Jensen Huang called xAI's Colossus buildout (100,000 Nvidia H100 chips deployed in 122 days, 19 days from hardware arrival to training start) "superhuman," saying, "As far as I know, there's only one person in the world who could do that."
- Speculation (self-described as unconfirmed by the source) that SpaceX may soon announce large AI data-center rental deals, potentially including Microsoft, plus a mention of "Macrohard" as a longer-shot revenue concept tied to Grok.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla passed 10 million total vehicles produced (ex-Fremont), up from 1 million six years ago — a milestone hosts link to the "tent" assembly line that enabled earlier scaling.
- Multiple sources describe Optimus as Musk's current management focus, following a reported overhaul of the program after he called it "a mess" (unconfirmed characterization, jobhakdi); one host frames Optimus spend as a "zero billion dollar market" bet akin to Nvidia's early CUDA investment.

## Energy
- Tesla shipped its first Megapacks using domestically made LFP cells, produced at Sparks, NV and integrated at the California Megapack factory — framed as supporting 45X-style manufacturing credits and reducing supply-chain risk; facility capacity cited at roughly 10 GWh/year.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla resolved the pre-2026 Cybertruck brake control system (PCS) defect via 2026 model-year design changes and will reimburse owners who paid out of pocket; Foundation Series warranty extended to 8 years/150,000 miles.
- A third-party range test found Model Y Performance beat its EPA rating (by ~4.1 miles) at highway speed, while Rivian R2 fell further short of its EPA rating in the same test.
- Volvo is dropping LiDAR for a vision-only approach, which hosts frame as validating Tesla's camera-only strategy.
- Tesla quietly raised APR financing rates on select Model 3/Y trims (e.g., Model 3 Performance 0.99%→1.99%; base Model Y RWD 0%→0.99%).

## SpaceX — Starship
- Latest Starship test flight executed a clean descent and landing; the recovered stage reportedly floated intact in the ocean for several days afterward. Only one of two stages is currently targeted for catch-tower recovery due to limited tower infrastructure.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- The FAA is proposing streamlined commercial launch licensing to support higher cadence, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Market & Competition
- Costco has not broadly adopted Tesla Superchargers despite favorable dwell times, with at least one location using higher-priced Electrify America instead.
- California excludes Tesla from a $3,750 state EV credit due to lack of an in-state HQ, while Lucid and Rivian qualify; hosts say the sales impact is minimal given Tesla's volume.
