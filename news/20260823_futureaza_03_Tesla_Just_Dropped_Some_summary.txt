## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck event and Semi order:** Tesla is holding a Cybertruck-related event on September 3 in Austin, which some community members reportedly felt excluded from after not being invited. Separately, the host notes Tesla "landed a major win" as Einride agreed to buy 500 Tesla Semis, with rollout scheduled to begin in September and proceed over 24 months across select corridors.
2. **New Megacharger site:** A new Tesla Megacharger site in Vernon, California has six stalls; the host notes at roughly 30 minutes per truck, six stalls could serve about 12 trucks per hour if run continuously.
3. **Tesla solar roof reportedly discontinued:** The host states the Tesla Solar Roof has been removed from Tesla's website and characterizes the product as dead, attributing this to persistent cost and manufacturing problems — the company reportedly aimed for over 1,000 installations per week but never hit that scale, and per-unit costs rose rather than fell over time. The host also describes a technical issue where heat build-up beneath the panels (due to inadequate airflow) damaged underlayment and components, limiting output.
4. **Rivian R2 vs. Tesla Model Y comparison challenged:** The host disputes an article's claim that the Rivian R2 and Tesla Model Y are both priced around $45,000, stating the R2 is actually priced at $61,000. The host argues the two vehicles aren't truly comparable, praises the Model Y's superior range and value, but credits the R2 with a "cool factor" and vehicle-to-load capability (though not yet vehicle-to-house, which still requires an additional $4,000–$6,000 gateway purchase and remains unavailable despite being promised for a while). The host also pushes back on Rivian's stated goal of point-to-point autonomy by the end of the year, calling it dangerous if meant literally and saying the company has shown no credible evidence of a path to get there.

## Robotaxi
1. **Nevada approves robotaxi permits for Tesla, Uber, and Waymo:** The Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved permits allowing Tesla, Uber, and Waymo to each operate robotaxis in the state, pushing back on earlier claims that Tesla was capped at a small number of units. The host notes it's unclear whether additional restrictions apply within specific zones such as the Las Vegas Strip or airport, and that Uber currently has a permit but no clear operational solution in place.
2. **Waymo expands next-gen robotaxi access:** Waymo has opened its next-generation robotaxi service to all riders in Los Angeles, Phoenix, and San Francisco, which the host frames as a sign the company considers the vehicles sufficiently tested.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nio sells battery-swap division to state-owned enterprise:** Nio has transferred its battery-swap assets to a state-owned enterprise with government support, which the host interprets as the government taking over infrastructure that was important for apartment dwellers lacking home charging, while Nio shifts to being paid to operate swap stations rather than bearing the costs.
2. **Chinese automakers target 2027 for solid-state batteries:** BYD, CATL, and Geely are reportedly targeting 2027 for solid-state battery trial production; the host characterizes these as incrementally improved ("drier") batteries rather than breakthrough technology, contrasting this with unspecified competitors he says have produced press releases rather than real batteries.
3. **New US solar panel manufacturer launches:** A company called Translucent Energy has started producing (non-transparent, despite the name) solar panels in South Carolina; the host is cautiously optimistic but notes it's unclear whether the company can scale to cost-effective production.
4. **Hyundai Ioniq China launch:** Hyundai has opened pre-sales in China for a vehicle the host identifies as a previously-spotted prototype, with pricing starting around $18,000 and rising to about $21,000 for higher trims; the host is skeptical it would sell in sufficient volume in the US market.
