Tesla has reintroduced one year of free Supercharging for new Model 3 Premium and Performance orders in North America, while simultaneously claiming non-Tesla EV owners pay a "~40% premium" on the Supercharger network — a figure the article disputes.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Free Supercharging returns for Model 3 Premium/Performance:** Tesla announced the incentive via X, applying it to custom orders (not just inventory) of the Model 3 Premium and Performance trims only — excluding the base Model 3. The author notes this mirrors previous free Supercharging promotions Tesla has run over the years and suggests the offer is targeted at trims where Tesla is experiencing softer demand relative to the Model Y.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's "~40% premium" claim disputed:** Tesla claimed non-Tesla EVs pay a "~40% premium" at Superchargers, but the article's analysis puts the actual non-Tesla premium at 30–35%. In high-cost markets like Los Angeles at peak hours, non-members pay roughly $0.60/kWh versus $0.45/kWh for Tesla owners (~33% premium); during off-peak hours the gap narrows to as low as 23%. Non-Tesla owners can eliminate the premium entirely with a $12.99/month Supercharging Membership.

2. **Supercharger pricing context:** US Supercharger rates currently range from $0.30–$0.45/kWh at most stations, reaching $0.50–$0.60+ at peak in high-demand areas. Tesla cut rates roughly 7% in late 2024. Dynamic live pricing, piloted at ~10 California stations in May 2025, has since expanded to 550+ stations.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues free Supercharging is a modest perk worth roughly $120–260/year for typical home-charging owners, but potentially $600–1,500+ for those without home charging access. On the pricing claim, the author says Tesla is "stretching the numbers" but acknowledges the built-in Supercharger discount is a real and increasingly relevant competitive advantage as more automakers adopt NACS.
