This Tesla Beat podcast episode covers a packed week: the Cybertruck production extension, Tesla's Robotaxi fleet surpassing Waymo in vehicle count, a new San Antonio robotaxi city launch, 24/7 robotaxi operations, Optimus rental-vs-sale plans, European Tesla registration growth, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation, and the upcoming Starship "double catch" test flight.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production window extended through summer:** Elon Musk extended Cybertruck production from roughly June through what the hosts believe is now the end of August, adding a shift at the factory. Hosts noted that only the Cyber Beast variant remains available to order, with everything else reportedly sold out, though some pending orders could still be cancelled if not fulfilled by the cutoff.
2. **Unplugged Performance reportedly made an acquisition offer for the Cybertruck line:** Per one host (Joe), Ben at Unplugged Performance made an offer to take over Cybertruck production, claiming backing from former Tesla engineers, investor support, upfit sales commitments through 2027, and scouted real estate. Hosts were skeptical it could happen, citing Tesla's unique casting-machine capacity and battery-cell constraints (4680 cells shared across Cybertruck, Optimus, Semi, and Model Y production) as likely insurmountable obstacles; one host speculated the real reason for ending Cybertruck production is to redirect battery supply toward Cybercab production.
3. **German and European Tesla registrations surge:** Germany registrations are reportedly up 291% year-over-year, with one host citing a year-to-date figure of roughly 47,000 units in Germany (flagged by the host himself as possibly high). A host noted this growth is notable given Germany's dozen-plus domestic automakers, and compared it to similar Tesla success against homegrown brands in Japan and Korea. Giga Berlin is reportedly still ramping shifts to meet order backlogs and reduce wait times.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla robotaxi fleet count surpasses Waymo, per hosts' figures:** Hosts cited Tesla at roughly 1,300 robotaxi vehicles (729 Model Ys and 573 Cybercabs) versus Waymo's roughly 1,297 (874 Jaguars and 423 Zeekrs) — these specific figures came from the hosts/show rather than RobotaxiTracker.com and differ materially from that tracker's stale figures, so they are attributed to the show. Hosts argued Tesla's manufacturing scale (Model Y and Cybercab both rolling off existing lines) gives it a structural logistics advantage over Waymo, which must order, ship, and upfit vehicles from third-party manufacturers.
2. **San Antonio becomes the eighth robotaxi city:** San Antonio launched Monday at 5:00 a.m. local time, with hosts describing the service zone as roughly 275 square miles, covering five Texas cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth, San Antonio) and three in Florida, not yet including airports due to separate port-authority rules. One host speculated rides between San Antonio and Austin could be possible within a few months given freeway connectivity.
3. **Tesla robotaxi service goes 24/7 in all active cities:** Tesla expanded robotaxi operations to run 24 hours, seven days a week (160 hours/week) across all eight current cities. Hosts debated the rationale, citing low overnight demand but citing brand-presence value (likened to Taco Bell's 24-hour strategy), potential overnight delivery use cases, and reduced collision risk from tired human drivers at night.
4. **California robotaxi launch still pending, reason unclear:** Hosts noted Tesla has not yet filed paperwork for a California robotaxi launch and speculated this may relate to regulatory, testing, or unspecified "geopolitical" factors, without resolving the ambiguity.
5. **European regulatory approvals continue expanding:** Finland became the 11th EU/European country to approve robotaxi-related rules per one host, who listed Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, Finland, Estonia, Slovenia, Czech Republic, Croatia, Slovakia, and Latvia as approved; the host estimated Tesla needs 15 of 27 countries approved to reach roughly 65% of the EU population.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Elon Musk signals Optimus will likely be rented, not sold, at least initially:** At a Barron's conference appearance, Musk was asked whether Tesla would sell or rent Optimus and said he thinks it will "probably" be rented rather than sold, at least in major metros initially — paralleling the planned rental/per-use models for Cybercab and Semi. Hosts framed this as consistent with their prior prediction and discussed the business logic (reduced customer burden for maintenance/consumables, easier fleet servicing) versus consumer preference to own the robots outright.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship "double catch" test flight scheduled:** The episode covers an upcoming Starship launch, with the Super Heavy booster catch expected within minutes of liftoff but the Starship upper stage not returning for about 22 hours; hosts noted FAA final sign-off was still pending as of the recording. A successful full double catch would mark the first time Starship achieves full reusability, which hosts called a major milestone for the SpaceX Starship/Mars thesis. One host noted Starship can reportedly carry roughly 10x the satellite payload of a Falcon 9.

## Financials
1. **Bloomberg reports SpaceX may announce a Tesla acquisition offer in December:** One host cited a Bloomberg report (secondhand, unconfirmed by the hosts) that SpaceX plans to announce an acquisition offer for Tesla in early December, which they suggested could move Tesla's stock if confirmed.
2. **Weekly stock moves:** Over the prior five days, Tesla stock was up roughly 10% but down about 1% on the day of recording, trading near $392; SpaceX (SPCX) was up about 5% over five days and roughly flat on the day.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns amid personal conduct allegations:** Hosts discussed Huang's resignation after apparently multiple incidents involving relationships with employees becoming public. They speculated Huang could start a new venture given his track record, noted a viral Elon Musk tweet suggesting it would be an "honor" to have Huang involved with Tesla's AI chip effort (Terafab), and debated Nvidia's succession planning, with the company's CFO serving as interim CEO. Hosts argued Nvidia's near-term sales are insulated from the loss but its longer-term innovation trajectory is less certain without Huang.
