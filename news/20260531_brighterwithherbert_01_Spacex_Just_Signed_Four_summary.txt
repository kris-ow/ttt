This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and venture capital investor, discussing four major deals recently signed by SpaceX ahead of its June 12th IPO. The conversation covers a US Space Force contract, NASA's lunar base plans, a satellite data center partnership, an airline Wi-Fi expansion, and a brief European spectrum regulatory update — all framed around SpaceX's dominant position in the space and satellite industry. The Tesla Robotaxi connection is referenced only briefly at the end, with Larry noting that both Tesla and SpaceX are on the "very brink" of hitting their stride in the coming three to five years.

## Market & Competition

1. **US Space Force awards SpaceX $2.29 billion Starshield contract:** The US Space Force announced a $2.29 billion contract awarded to SpaceX to build a next-generation military satellite communications network backbone in low Earth orbit, branded as Starshield. This is explicitly a separate network from Starlink, operated through SpaceX's government-focused division. The network is intended to provide low-latency global communications, optical inter-satellite links, high-capacity tactical data transport, a resilient mesh satellite constellation, and worldwide military broadband connectivity — with completion targeted by the end of 2027. Larry characterized the $2.29 billion figure as just the beginning, noting that operating, expanding, and hardening the network against attack will require significant additional investment, including potential data center and AI inference capacity in orbit.

2. **NASA's $20 billion lunar base plan and SpaceX's central role:** NASA, under new administrator Jared Isaacman, announced plans for a permanent US base on the moon with an estimated $20 billion budget. The plan is structured in three phases: Phase 1 (now to 2029) involves a rapid series of robotic missions to scout the lunar south pole, up to 25 missions including 21 landings, crude and autonomous rovers, four drones, and early demonstrations of power and communications systems. Phase 2 (2029–2032) transitions to assembling semi-permanent infrastructure, deploying expanded solar power systems, upgraded rovers, and delivering up to 60 tons of cargo through as many as 24 landings. Phase 3 (2032 and beyond) targets a sustained human presence with rotating crews, analogous to the International Space Station model. Isaacman was quoted describing SpaceX as "probably our greatest commercial space company, hands down," and confirmed that NASA has modified an award to provide up to six additional missions to SpaceX for International Space Station crew transport. Larry noted that by 2032 there may be sharing between SpaceX and Blue Origin, with China also expected to have a competing presence on the moon by that time.

3. **StarCloud–SpaceX partnership for space-based AI data centers:** StarCloud, an aerospace and AI infrastructure startup described as the first company to put a small AI data satellite in orbit, announced a contract with SpaceX to integrate 50 Starlink mini laser terminals across 25 satellites — two terminals per StarCloud satellite. The first hardware is expected in orbit within one year. Larry explained that the laser terminals are critical for enabling low-latency, high-bandwidth communication between satellites for AI inference workloads. He noted that StarCloud's satellite design has converged closely with SpaceX's flat-form-factor, PEZ-dispenser-style deployment approach, and that StarCloud plans to launch via SpaceX's Starship. Larry speculated there is a fair probability that StarCloud could be acquired by a major AI frontier company such as Anthropic or OpenAI, characterizing it as a "poker game" between those players.

4. **American Airlines joins Starlink; Delta holds out with Amazon LEO:** American Airlines announced it will install Starlink Wi-Fi on more than 500 airplanes starting in 2027 (resolved from "next year" relative to publish date of 2026-05-31). This brings the total to 38 airlines partnered with SpaceX Starlink, covering more than 6,300 commercial aircraft already installed or under contract. Larry estimated this represents between 25% and 40% of the addressable commercial aircraft market, after excluding Chinese and Russian carriers. Delta remains the notable holdout, having chosen Amazon's LEO satellite internet service instead. Elon Musk stated publicly that SpaceX requires Starlink Wi-Fi to work effortlessly without a branded portal, claiming Delta wanted to make the experience "painful, difficult, and expensive." Delta's CEO disputed this, saying incorporating Delta Sync with Starlink would have been permitted, and attributed the Amazon LEO choice to it being the "fastest, most cost-effective technology." Larry and Herbert speculated that Amazon Prime movie access may have been a factor in the deal. Larry expressed doubt that Amazon LEO's airplane Wi-Fi — currently with only 300 satellites in orbit — would be ready by 2027, saying "I just don't see it."

5. **EU moves to restrict US operators' share of mobile satellite spectrum:** The European Union announced plans to reserve the bulk of valuable mobile satellite spectrum for European companies to reduce reliance on US tech giants and respond to concerns about China's technological rise. Under the proposed framework, one-third of the spectrum will be reserved for state use (security and military), and the remaining two-thirds will be split equally between EU and non-EU operators. SpaceX and Amazon's LEO can still bid for the non-EU portion. Larry was highly critical of Europe's regulatory direction, characterizing it as economic self-harm given Europe's already stalled growth trajectory.

6. **SpaceX IPO on June 12th and deal-signing pace:** The SpaceX IPO is scheduled for June 12th (approximately two weeks from the video's publish date of 2026-05-31). Herbert questioned whether the recent burst of deal announcements was strategically timed ahead of the IPO. Larry argued that SpaceX has consistently been signing government agreements and bandwidth deals year after year, and that the apparent acceleration is largely a function of increased public awareness rather than a change in pace. He drew a parallel between Tesla being "on the very brink of robotaxi" and SpaceX being on the very brink of Starship achieving full operational scale, predicting both companies will "hit their stride" over the coming three to five years.
