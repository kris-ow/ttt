## Robotaxi
- Tesla has set September 3rd as the Cybercab commercial launch date in Austin, per Jo Bhakdi and corroborated by Sawyer Merritt's report of launch-event invites; Tesla's own confirmation gives no further detail on venue or format.
- Nevada approved Tesla to deploy up to 5,000 Cybercabs/robotaxis versus only 1,000 approved for Waymo — Bhakdi calls this evidence Waymo "is dead in the water" as a scaling competitor, not a sign of a regulatory ceiling.
- Waymo has reportedly cut its autonomy hardware cost from $120,000 to $20,000 (per Bhakdi), still implying a total per-vehicle cost near $55,000–$60,000 versus his roughly $1,000 estimate for Tesla's camera-based hardware; he expects Cybercab COGS below $20,000 by late 2027/mid-2028.
- Bhakdi estimates 1,000–2,000 Cybercabs are already built and staged for deployment, and sets launch-day fleet expectations at ~15 (bear), ~50 (base), 100+ (bull) — saying 50 alone "is not going to save the stock."
- A blind rider ("Mona," Federation of the Blind) testified the Cybercab would be "very beneficial" to the blind community, citing Braille signage she says no competitor offers.
- Bhakdi flags 1,000+ unsupervised Cybercabs (possibly by mid-October, not guaranteed) as the trigger for a major stock repricing, assigning better-than-50% odds it happens sometime in 2026.

## Autonomous Driving
- WSJ's Dan Neil gave a positive personal review of FSD (Supervised): "By the end of the second hour—I was bewitched. FSD is amazing," while noting he is "a sample group of one."
- A viral post showed a 76-year-old owner demonstrating FSD (Supervised) to elderly friends (ages 95, 91, 74) in a 2026 Model Y, cited as favorable anecdotal reception.

## Energy
- Tesla filed a tax incentive application for "Project Crystal Sun," a $10.1 billion, ~3,500-acre vertically integrated solar manufacturing plant in Fort Bend County, Texas, targeting ~9,700 jobs, construction in 2026, and commercial operations by Q1 2029 (per brighterwithherbert).
- SpaceX's separate Bastrop, Texas solar cell plant, under construction since March, will produce 5 GW of aerospace-grade solar cells per floor across two floors for satellites and orbital data centers.
- SpaceX AI has spent $329 million on Megapacks so far this year, per futureaza.
- Memphis compute site power draw is scaling from 1.5–2 GW currently toward a cited 5–10 GW figure from a recent earnings call — a level that would exceed Memphis's own record peak demand of 3.5 GW and rival New York City's draw, to be met via natural gas generation.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Tesla and SpaceX have committed $16.8 billion to build "Terafab," a chip fab in Grimes County, Texas on a retired coal plant site, targeting up to 100 million square feet, 1 million wafer starts/month, and 100–200 billion chips/year — combined with Crystal Sun, roughly $27 billion in committed capital before either project produces output.
- xAI has reportedly overbuilt Colossus compute capacity and now leases surplus GPU capacity to major tech companies (including Google, per the hosts) at highly profitable rates; futureaza's Jordan Giesler estimated ~$72B/year potential revenue tied to ~440,000 GPUs across Colossus 1 and 2, with data centers paying for themselves in roughly a year under his cost model.
- Colossus 2's actual satellite-imagery layout differs from prior predictions, with a third building apparently under construction for cooling/energy/battery infrastructure; Giesler is unsure whether the site will reach Musk's previously cited ~1 million GPU figure.
- Analysts cited by Giesler expect the compute business to remain cash-flow negative until roughly 2030–2035, though he personally expects negative cash flow mainly for the next year or two; a new major compute site beyond Colossus 1/2 may need to be announced soon as land tightens.
- SpaceX's Bastrop campus is undergoing a major expansion — separate from its existing Starlink terminal/circuit-board factory — to build "Star Mind" AI satellites using Nvidia GPUs for sun-synchronous orbit; Joe Tegtmeyer says the site's total production footprint will exceed Giga Texas combined.

## SpaceX — Starship
- New Bastrop-built satellites are reportedly too large for Falcon 9 and require Starship launch, tying the facility's output directly to Starship's flight cadence.
- The satellites share a "pancake" V3 Starlink chassis form factor with terrestrial AI data center racks, per Tegtmeyer, enabling common manufacturing and dispensing systems across space and ground-based compute products.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
