## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Cybercab Launch Event is confirmed for September 3rd in Austin, per Tesla invites cited by Sawyer Merritt and Jo Bhakdi (who says the date only became concrete this week, after his earlier May prediction proved wrong).
- Nevada approved Tesla for up to 5,000 Cybercabs versus only 1,000 for Waymo; Bhakdi calls this a scale gap rather than a regulatory ceiling and says Waymo is "dead in the water" as a competitor (his characterization).
- Bhakdi estimates Waymo has cut per-vehicle hardware cost from $120,000 to $20,000 (plus ~$35,000 for the car itself, putting total cost near $55,000–$60,000), versus his estimate of roughly $1,000 for Tesla's camera-based hardware; he expects Cybercab COGS below $20,000 by end of 2027 or mid-2028.
- Bhakdi estimates roughly 1,000–2,000 Cybercabs already built and staged for deployment, and sets a launch-day fleet range of ~15 (bear), ~50 (base), 100+ (bull) — saying 50 alone "is not going to save the stock."
- Bhakdi flags 1,000 unsupervised Cybercabs as the key repricing trigger, floating mid-October as plausible timing (not guaranteed) and assigning better-than-50% odds it happens in 2026.
- A Federation of the Blind member, "Mona," testified after riding the Cybercab this week that it would be "very beneficial" to blind riders, citing its Braille feature.
- A Dallas rider reported a robotaxi fare roughly 50% cheaper than Uber, per a commenter cited by Bhakdi.

## Autonomous Driving
- WSJ's Dan Neil gave a positive personal review of FSD (Supervised): "By the end of the second hour—I was bewitched. FSD is amazing," while noting he is "a sample group of one."
- A widely-shared post showed a 76-year-old demonstrating FSD (Supervised) to elderly friends (ages 95, 91, 74) in a 2026 Model Y, with riders impressed by its speed/slowdown handling — anecdotal, not data.

## Energy
- Tesla filed a tax incentive application for a $10.1 billion, ~3,500-acre solar manufacturing plant ("Project Crystal Sun") in Fort Bend County, TX, targeting ~9,700 permanent jobs, full vertical integration from ingots to modules, construction in 2026 and commercial operations targeted Q1 2029.
- SpaceX's separate Bastrop, TX solar cell plant (construction started March) targets two floors producing 5 GW each of aerospace-grade cells for satellites and orbital data centers — distinct from Tesla's terrestrial-focused Houston-area project.
- SpaceX AI has spent $329 million on Megapacks so far this year, per futureaza.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- xAI/SpaceX is reportedly leasing surplus Colossus compute to major tech companies (including, per the hosts, Google) at highly profitable rates; Jordan Giesler (The Limiting Factor) estimates Colossus capacity can be paid off in roughly a year, based on ~$50B/GW build cost against ~$50B/GW/year revenue — though he cautions this doesn't mean the buildout is self-funding given a 3–6 month capital-to-revenue lag.
- A ~$72B/year revenue figure was discussed tied to ~440,000 GPUs across Colossus 1 and 2; Giesler says analysts expect the compute business to be cash-flow negative until roughly 2030–2035, though he personally expects negative cash flow mainly for the next year or two.
- Satellite imagery shows Colossus 2's layout differs from Giesler's earlier prediction, with a third building apparently under construction for cooling/energy/battery infrastructure; unclear if the site will reach the ~1 million GPU figure Musk previously cited.
- Memphis compute site currently draws 1.5–2 GW; a recent earnings call cited a 5–10 GW ambition — for comparison, Memphis's own record peak demand is 3.5 GW, meaning the site could out-draw the city itself.
- Tesla and SpaceX committed $16.8 billion (announced March 21) to a "Terafab" chip facility in Grimes County, TX, targeting up to 100 million sq ft, 1 million wafer starts/month, and 100–200 billion chips/year — combined with Project Crystal Sun, roughly $27 billion in committed capital across the two projects before either is operational, per brighterwithherbert.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX's Bastrop, TX campus is undergoing major new construction that drone photographer Joe Tegtmeyer says will give the site more total production capacity than Giga Texas combined; the existing plant already makes Starlink dishes/modems and is described as one of the largest circuit-board manufacturing operations in the country.
- New construction is aimed at next-generation "Star Mind" AI satellites using Nvidia GPUs in sun-synchronous polar orbit for continuous solar power, sharing a "pancake" V3 chassis form factor with terrestrial AI data center racks to gain manufacturing/margin efficiencies.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Tegtmeyer says the new Bastrop-built satellites are too large for Falcon 9 and must launch via Starship, directly tying Starship's success to this satellite/compute strategy.

## Financials
- Bhakdi's updated model puts Tesla fair value above $700 for 2026 and frames the current move from ~$300 to $362 as a "gap-close" rather than a new rally; his plan is to stay leveraged into $380, trim risk near $420 (a likely sell-the-news zone into the Cybercab event), and go "normal long" if shares drift toward $380–390 beforehand.
- Bhakdi flags 10-year Treasury yields near 4.7% and sharply rising Japanese and German bond yields as a longer-term systemic risk, alongside Wednesday's PCE/GDP data and Nvidia earnings as near-term catalysts.
- Cursor was acquired by xAI/SpaceX AI for $60 billion in an all-stock deal announced June 16 and closed August 14, framed by brighterwithherbert as a purchase of developer base/data rather than technology.

## Market & Competition
- Bhakdi argues legacy automakers face existential risk from robotaxi deployment and predicts self-driving becomes broadly commoditized around 2031–2032, warning Tesla "will be in trouble" if it stays under a million deployed Cybercabs in the US/Europe by 2035.
- Bhakdi assigns low probability to a Tesla-SpaceX merger in 2026, expecting 2027–2028 instead given SpaceX's ongoing price discovery post-IPO.
