## Brief
- Cybercab opens to Austin public Friday 5pm (moved up to 2pm CST)
- Tesla crosses 1 million unsupervised robotaxi miles
- Cybercab fares ~20-54% cheaper than Uber/Model Y rides
- NHTSA reportedly auditing Cybercab self-certification, host claims
- FSD version 15 now running fleet-wide on Model Y and Cybercab
- Tesla brand loyalty back to #1 at 69%

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab launched to the general public in Austin, opening early at 2pm CST (originally planned 5pm) due to demand; wait times jumped from 5-10 minutes to over an hour within 20 minutes of going live, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Tesla confirmed roughly 40-45 Cybercabs made available to select riders around the launch event, with multiple independent riders (Herbert Ong, Cern Basher, Tesla Beat hosts, Sawyer Merritt) reporting smooth, largely flawless unsupervised rides across the Austin geofence, including downtown driving with heavy pedestrian traffic.
- Fare comparisons: Merritt's 15-minute ride cost $9.65 in Cybercab vs. $16.99 (Model Y) vs. $21 (Uber) — 54% cheaper than Uber. Separately, Cern Basher cited $17.12 (Cybercab, 2-min wait) vs. $22.30 (Model Y, 7-min wait); Tesla Beat hosts said Cybercab rides run roughly 20% cheaper than Model Y on the app.
- Tesla crossed 1 million unsupervised robotaxi rider miles fleet-wide, per Merritt. Separately, Cern Basher's hosts calculated ~360,000 unsupervised miles around July 22 growing to over 1 million about six weeks later — implying ~17% week-over-week compounding growth, a figure they stress is Model Y-only data and an extrapolation, not a forecast.
- New Cybercab manufacturing details disclosed at the launch: "Supermanifold V3" thermal system enabling 80%-automated production, a simplified bar-wound-stator drive unit with sub-10-second automated production cycles (claimed 18% smaller, 25% lighter than competing EV drive units), and brake-by-wire with no brake fluid — a first for Tesla.
- NHTSA has reportedly opened an audit of Tesla's Cybercab self-certification process, per Bhakdi, who frames this — not any single news event — as the real driver of recent stock weakness. The dispute centers on FMVSS 135's human-usable brake pedal requirement, which the Cybercab lacks; Bhakdi says Tesla's argument that the rule's underlying safety purpose doesn't apply without a human driver "does not hold up in an audit."
- Cybercab door design drew scrutiny after footage showed a door contacting a person; Tesla Beat hosts say the doors lack outward sensors but do sense resistance on closing and reopen, citing their own hand/leg/shirt tests.
- Current Cybercab service reportedly ends around 10pm, with 24/7 service "coming soon" per a Tesla event speaker; Ashok Elluswamy separately addressed timing for a 24/7 robotaxi network without giving a specific date, per Merritt.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD version 15 is now running on all Model Y and Cybercab vehicles in the fleet as of early September, per Cern Basher's sourcing; a Tesla executive at the launch confirmed the Cybercab runs version 15 and said it would roll out to the entire public "very soon," per Ashok Elluswamy relayed by Herbert Ong.
- FSD 14.3.9 adds a new active-safety feature letting supervised FSD intervene autonomously on imminent-collision risk or apparent driver distraction/disengagement; unclear if it requires an active FSD subscription.
- Elon Musk met with French officials in what was described as a constructive exchange aimed at supporting France's FSD approval, with a final decision expected at the European level in coming weeks, per Tesla Beat.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla brand loyalty returned to first place at 69%, with FSD attach rate cited at roughly 5.5 of 10 new vehicle sales, per Tesla Beat.
- Tesla raised U.S. financing APR on Model Y RWD, AWD, and Premium trims from 0.99% to 1.49%; Performance trim stays at 3.99%.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink has roughly 12-13 million subscribers and about $5 billion in quarterly revenue, with 50%+ estimated as profit/cash flow, per a HyperChange guest interviewed by futureaza.
- V3 satellites are described as roughly 10x more capable than the current fleet, with the EchoStar spectrum deal adding a further ~10x; 20 V3 satellites were recently launched via Starship, though none were yet operational at time of taping.
- Elon Musk reportedly said Starlink could eventually handle up to 90% of global internet traffic; the guest's own traffic-share modeling of organic demand tops out around 6-16% (~10% midpoint), suggesting the 90% figure implies a different driver — space-based AI compute traffic ("Star Mind"), per the host's thesis.
- Per Gwynne Shotwell (relayed via Aaron Burnett/Mach 33), SpaceX is building new ground infrastructure as part of a plan to become the largest internet/mobile provider.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's compute-leasing business is at roughly a $31 billion run rate as of end of Q2, with one guest projecting it could exceed $100 billion run rate by December; Musk reportedly said this business will eventually exceed all of SpaceX's other businesses combined.
- Google and Anthropic are both current customers of SpaceX's compute business while competing with Musk's Grok, an arrangement one guest flagged as a potential conflict of interest.
- A cutaway Starlink phased-array antenna was displayed at the Cybercab launch event; the hosts expect similar hardware to eventually appear as an option on consumer Tesla vehicles.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX launched two rockets in 38 minutes from two coasts, illustrating a cadence and reusability (20-40 reuses per booster) the host frames as a durable cost advantage.
- Starship may be certified this month, per one host, opening a new class of large payloads (space stations, telescopes) beyond what Falcon Heavy typically serves.
