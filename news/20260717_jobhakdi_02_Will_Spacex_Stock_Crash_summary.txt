The host argues SpaceX is fundamentally strong but tactically overvalued, explaining why a scrubbed Starship launch and a market-wide selloff in high-growth/AI-adjacent stocks are hitting the stock, while laying out a bear case built on valuation, upcoming share unlocks, and a lack of near-term catalysts until Colossus data-center revenue or large-scale AI satellites materialize.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Launch abort explained as a positive safety signal, not a failure:** SpaceX's latest Starship/Super Heavy launch attempt was automatically aborted at startup after two Raptor engines triggered a warning; Elon Musk said the two Raptors would be removed and replaced, with the most probable launch timing early the following week. The host frames the abort positively, contrasting it with a Blue Origin launch failure (shown in the video) where a problem was not caught before launch, causing loss of payload and pad — arguing SpaceX's ability to detect and stop problems before catastrophe is further proof of its execution edge.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Stock drops sharply on delay amid broader market selloff:** SpaceX shares fell roughly 3.92% overnight after already being down about 4% before the launch, as part of a broader selloff hitting AI-adjacent and semiconductor stocks (including Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix); the host calls the launch-delay-driven drop "stupid" but not surprising given the market backdrop, noting the stock is now below its IPO price of $135.
2. **Valuation seen as stretched versus profitability:** The host estimates SpaceX's market cap at roughly $1.7 trillion against a possible ~$10–11 billion profit run rate for the year, implying a price-earnings ratio around 170 (comparable to Tesla's), which he argues is unsustainable in a market currently punishing even highly profitable, fast-growing semiconductor companies down to single-digit forward P/Es.
3. **Massive share unlocks create looming selling pressure:** Only about 5% of SpaceX shares are currently floated/tradeable; per a lockup schedule cited by the host, roughly 50–60% of total shares outstanding will become unlockable by November as pre-IPO holders gain eligibility to sell, which he says represents a roughly 10x increase in tradeable shares and a clear source of downward pressure through the rest of the year.
4. **Revenue-multiple comparison implies steep downside, offset partly by Colossus:** Applying Tesla's revenue multiple to SpaceX would imply a stock price around $35; accounting for stronger near-term cash flow potential from Colossus, the host doubles that to a rough price target near $70, which he still considers the likely downside scenario absent new catalysts.
5. **Colossus 2 and 3 seen as the main near-term catalyst, of uncertain sufficiency:** The host expects large new data centers (Colossus 2 and 3) and major deals to be announced within roughly the next 3–6 months, boosting cash flow, but is uncertain whether this will be enough to offset unlock-driven selling pressure or push the stock back toward a prior level near $180.
6. **Long-term bull case tied to AI satellites at scale:** The host says the real re-rating catalyst is AI compute satellites operating at scale (at least 100 MW), which he believes could arrive around 2028 or second half of 2028, with market anticipation potentially building in the second half of 2027; he states he personally plans to wait until at least December before initiating a position, despite being fundamentally bullish long-term.
