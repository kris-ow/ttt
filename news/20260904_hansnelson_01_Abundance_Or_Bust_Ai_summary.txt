## Market & Competition
1. **Chip/fab bottleneck landscape and Terafab timeline:** Hans says advanced packaging (integrating wafer, memory, and logic) is currently one of the biggest bottlenecks at TSMC and in memory production, and predicts Tesla's Terafab "mini fab" will likely target packaging first, with a first product possible within one to two years, before expanding into full wafer fabrication.
2. **Free electron laser as a potential ASML lithography workaround:** Hans describes free electron lasers — linear particle-accelerator-like devices that tune electron vibration to produce specific light wavelengths — as a technology Elon Musk has confirmed Terafab is exploring to potentially replace the EUV light-source component of ASML machines, envisioning one centralized laser supplying light to multiple lithography machines across a facility, analogous to a power plant. Hans still expects ASML and Zeiss to remain essential (e.g., for mirror technology) and calls himself comfortable holding ASML shares over a three-to-five-year horizon even accounting for Terafab's efforts.
3. **China's lithography timeline questioned:** The host cites a Bloomberg report attributing to Zeiss's CEO a claim that China is roughly 15 years from building cutting-edge lithography competitive with ASML (though able to produce 7nm chips sooner), a claim the host is skeptical of given AI's accelerating pace; Hans agrees replication is generally easier than original invention and would not assume a 15-year timeline holds.
4. **OpenAI/Broadcom's Jalapeno chip and inference economics:** The host discusses OpenAI's partnership with Broadcom producing a rapidly-taped-out custom inference chip called Jalapeno, said to outperform Nvidia GPUs for OpenAI's specific inference workload. Hans argues Jalapeno's edge comes from being narrowly optimized for one workload, unlike Nvidia's general-purpose platform which serves OpenAI, Anthropic, Grok (at SpaceX/xAI), Gemini, and Meta simultaneously; he expects Jalapeno to face capacity/supply-chain constraints and doubts it will meaningfully take share from Nvidia, calling Jensen Huang several steps ahead in securing upstream memory and supply deals. Hans separately notes Nvidia remains highly competitive on cloud-based inference cost even though it was originally designed for training.
5. **Portfolio allocation preference — Hans leans toward SpaceX:** Asked to choose between Tesla and SpaceX with a hypothetical $100k, Hans says he would allocate 75% SpaceX / 25% Tesla today, contrasting with his actual current holdings (roughly 90% Tesla, 10% SpaceX or less). He cites excitement about SpaceX's near- and long-term prospects, including the xAI/Grok component, as the driver, while remaining excited about Cybercab specifically within Tesla but flagging regulatory uncertainty as the main overhang.
6. **Grok/xAI compute expectations:** Hans says he expects "Grokbot" (xAI's compute/AI effort tied to SpaceX) to substantially outperform market expectations in revenue and capability, citing anticipated token usage and productivity gains, while taking the "under" on aggressive Optimus scale-up timelines by comparison.
7. **Cursor acquisition praised:** The host calls SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor (an AI coding tool team) one of the best acquisitions in history, comparing it to YouTube's acquisition; Hans agrees the team is strong.
