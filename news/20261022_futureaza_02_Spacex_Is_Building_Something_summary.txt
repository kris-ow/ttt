This futureaza livestream recap, done jointly with another creator, covers highlights from a recent SpaceX earnings call: the Terafab chip project, construction progress at a Texas gigascale site, Starlink's subscriber and revenue growth, and an upcoming ambitious Starship test aiming to catch both the booster and ship.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Terafab chip timeline and partners:** The hosts discuss Terafab, SpaceX's chip manufacturing effort, noting a prediction of early proof-of-concept production — not at advanced node sizes but likely memory chips — within roughly six months, something one host attributes to a company statement made in a prior quarter. The project has drawn interest from Intel, TSMC, Samsung, and ASML; Intel reportedly is contributing an alternative lithography approach using an accelerator ring to generate light for very fine etching, differing from standard extreme ultraviolet lithography used by TSMC and Samsung. The hosts frame this as speculative but credible given the caliber of partners now engaging, noting that even if the accelerator approach fails, an ultraviolet emitter alternative exists as a fallback.
2. **Terafab site construction pace:** The hosts describe rapid, round-the-clock construction at the Terafab factory/lab site in Grimes County, Texas, located within a roughly 3,000-acre tract (expanded from an original 2,500 acres). They note the site went from bare land to significant vertical steel structure within about a week, and reference drone footage (citing Joe Tegtmeyer) showing cleared land and foundations laid in quick succession, attributing the pace to large numbers of construction crews working in parallel.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink subscriber and revenue growth:** SpaceX reported 13.6 million consumer Starlink users, with commercial users (aviation, maritime/cruise ships, oil rigs) cited as growing particularly fast. The hosts estimate a back-of-envelope monthly revenue figure in the billion-dollar range from consumer subscriptions alone, based on the user count and typical subscription pricing (noting some markets, like Nigeria, have cheaper plans around $25/month due to local transfer-limit laws, while many customers pay over $100/month for higher-tier service).
2. **Starshield and government launch pricing:** The hosts highlight Starshield (the military/government-focused Starlink variant) as a growing contract area, and cite a claim that the government is paying roughly 70% less than competitors for SpaceX launches while getting a more reliable rocket — though no specific contract source is detailed in the transcript.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Upcoming dual-catch Starship test:** The hosts describe an upcoming Starship test flight as one of the most ambitious since very early test flights, involving separate attempts to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage (not simultaneously, but hours apart, unlike the earlier tandem Falcon Heavy booster landings). They frame this as a critical milestone toward full reusability, noting that after catching and reusing the vehicles, in-orbit refueling remains a further milestone still needed.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Large-scale launch pad buildout:** One host relays a claim, attributed to Elon Musk during the earnings discussion, about plans for a large number of launch pads — the host was unsure of the exact figure ("30 or something... something like 60") — at a very large site. A drone pilot described in the discussion reportedly said the site is too large to film in a single flight, requiring parking midway to cover each half, and that driving its full length at 30 mph would take about 24 minutes — used as an illustration of the site's scale, though specific pad count and site dimensions remain unconfirmed in the transcript.
