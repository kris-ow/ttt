Tesla China upgraded every current Model 3 trim with a 16-inch touchscreen, a new light gray interior option, and up to 2.2 kW of AC power export (vehicle-to-load), while the US Model 3 retains its 15.4-inch screen and remains excluded from V2L entirely.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **China Model 3 hardware and feature upgrades:** Tesla announced on October 1 via official Chinese marketing posters that every current Model 3 trim in China now has a 16-inch touchscreen (up from 15.4 inches), claimed to have higher resolution and refresh rate, though no specific numbers were given. A new light gray "premium interior" option with a black fabric headliner is also available, mirroring a change Tesla made to the US Model Y Premium in January. Every Model 3 and Model Y currently sold in China can now export up to 2,200 W of AC power via a purchasable adapter (not required on the Model Y L).

2. **New China incentives:** Model 3 orders placed by October 31 get 5,000 yuan (~$740) off the final payment, stacked with 5-year 0% financing, an 8,000 yuan insurance subsidy on RWD/Long Range RWD/Long Range AWD trims, an 8,000 yuan paint credit, and a choice of three charging perks. Tesla advertises the Model 3 from 222,500 yuan (~$33,000), but the base RWD list price remains unchanged at 235,500 yuan — the advertised figure only works after combining the final-payment discount and insurance subsidy. Even discounted, this is above BYD's new Formula S, which starts at 189,900 yuan for the RWD sedan.

3. **US Model 3 left behind:** The US Model 3 starts at $36,990 and still has only a 15.4-inch screen per Tesla's spec page. When Tesla introduced V2L in the US in August, it limited the feature to the Model Y Premium, Model Y Performance, and Cybertruck (2.4 kW via an $80 adapter), excluding the entire US Model 3 lineup — unlike China, where every Model 3 gets V2L.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the discrepancy as evidence that competition in China is fierce and that Tesla is prioritizing launching new features and incentives there first, leaving US buyers behind.
