## Autonomous Driving

- **Tesla Vision airbag pre-deployment rolling out fleet-wide:** Tesla cameras detect unavoidable collisions and trigger airbags up to 70 milliseconds before impact — earlier than impact-sensor-only systems. Elon confirmed this arrives "for free on all new cars" via software update, applicable across Tesla's ~8–9 million vehicle fleet.

- **FSD 14.3.2 coast-to-coast: zero interventions, 2,800 miles, under 50 hours:** Zach Black, Dan Berkeland, and Aaron completed a New York–Los Angeles drive on FSD 14.3.2 with no human interventions, beating the prior record by 8.5 hours. Tesla officially reposted the achievement. Per Jeff Lutz, no competitor vehicle can offer anything comparable today.

- **FSD 14.3 architecture: RL breadth, vision encoder upgrade, self-recovery focus:** Per Cern Basher's panel, FSD 14.3's improvements — pothole avoidance, animal avoidance, school zone handling — are primarily reinforcement learning applications. An upgraded vision encoder maximizes the data pipeline to hardware limits, a prerequisite before any model-size scaling is meaningful. Critically for robotaxi, the system can now resolve more edge cases without disengagement — reducing the operations-center intervention load that would otherwise make large-scale deployment uneconomical.

- **Neural net photon reconstruction gives Tesla cameras superhuman low-light vision:** Elon shared images demonstrating Tesla AI's photon-count reconstruction versus human-perceived glare — the AI rendering dramatically clearer. Jeff Lutz framed this as the core architectural advantage: not simply more cameras, but AI-augmented perception exceeding human capability at dawn, dusk, and night.

- **2026 Model Y first vehicle to pass all four new NHTSA/NCAP ADAS tests:** NHTSA confirmed the 2026 Model Y (manufactured on or after November 2025) is the first vehicle to meet criteria for pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention — all four newly integrated advanced safety metrics. No other automaker had passed more than three as of publication.

- **Ireland formally engaging Tesla on FSD supervised; Belgium accelerating analysis:** Ireland's Department of Transport confirmed active discussions with Tesla and the NSAI regarding FSD (Supervised) approval, with compliance required at both Irish and EU levels — no timeline given. In Belgium, local director Anek Ditter announced she is pushing to fast-track FSD analysis, though authorities want limited-scale testing first given road-system differences from the Netherlands. Denmark separately clarified it has expressed neither support nor skepticism about approval, pushing back on a Reuters characterization.

- **China FSD employee beta rumored on v14.3.2 with ~1,000 vehicles and NDAs:** An unverified social media report (attributed to "Ry") claims Tesla China began pushing FSD 14.x internally to approximately 1,000 employee vehicles in late April/early May, with NDAs required. Jeff Lutz cautioned against tying any product milestone to Elon Musk's rescheduled China trip. Tesla China is also actively hiring autopilot test engineers.

- **Chinese auto industry insider acknowledges Tesla FSD gap:** Wang Yao, deputy chief engineer of the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers, stated that "we should also honestly acknowledge some of the gaps" relative to Tesla FSD — notable given Chinese social media claims that domestic brands have surpassed Tesla on autonomy.

- **Tesla robotaxi job posting names miles per intervention as primary KPI:** A new Tesla posting for robotaxi software engineers focused on "code hardening" states: "Miles per intervention is a key metric we look at to measure this and our primary focus is keeping the number as high as possible while robotaxi expands globally." Jeff Lutz noted the secondary metric is likely a bug identification and closure rate, and flagged that much front-end FSD architecture work is now migrating to Optimus.

- **HW3 predicted to never run unsupervised robotaxi:** One Cern Basher panelist stated a personal (non-Tesla-official) prediction that Hardware 3 vehicles will not operate unsupervised in the robotaxi network or in customer unsupervised mode — framing driverless capability as exclusive to HW4 and Cybercab platform hardware.

## Robotaxi

- **Robotaxi-first sequencing: revenue generation before customer unsupervised FSD:** The Cern Basher panel reached consensus that Tesla's internal priority is robotaxi scaling over enabling personal-vehicle unsupervised FSD. The business logic: generate robotaxi cash flow first to buffer the inevitable litigation costs from unavoidable accidents — a risk Elon himself acknowledged publicly, stating AVs will "probably end up avoiding 90% of accidents" but the remaining 10% are unavoidable and Tesla will be sued for them.

- **Austin supervised-to-unsupervised transition ongoing:** Herbert (brighterwithherbert) observed that Tesla appears to be systematically replacing supervised Austin robotaxi rides with driverless ones as technology is validated — the observable path of fleet maturation in real time.

- **Bhakdi: safety largely solved, convenience is the current frontier:** Bhakdi characterized the current robotaxi challenge as reducing non-dangerous but frustrating ride failures — bringing error rates from perhaps 5–10 inconvenient events per 100 rides toward 0.1 — rather than a life-safety problem. He described the current fleet size as exciting for believers but insufficient to move institutional sentiment, with Waymo's ~3,000-vehicle fleet as the institutional benchmark frame of reference.

- **Larry Goldberg: no "magic fleet number" triggers re-rating; scaling not complete in 2026:** Goldberg pushed back on the idea that hitting a specific unsupervised fleet milestone — whether 500 vehicles or surpassing Waymo — will trigger a stock re-rating. He does not expect robotaxi to be "scaled" by end of 2026, and estimates the unsupervised fleet may reach 100–200 vehicles by the time of the SpaceX IPO in June.

- **Competitor AV under NHTSA investigation — 16 accidents in Dallas and Austin:** An unnamed Tesla rival (operating in Dallas and Austin, developed since 2017, available on Uber) is under NHTSA investigation following 16 accidents involving its automated driving system, resulting in one minor injury and property damage. Alleged failures included lane changes into other vehicles and failure to respond to merging traffic.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus production timeline: Fremont late July/August 2026 (likely slipping); Giga Texas target 10M units/year:** Per Farzad, Optimus production at the converted Fremont Model S/X line is targeted for late July or August 2026, though Farzad explicitly expects delays, with 1M units/year run rate more likely 2027–28. The Giga Texas Optimus factory targets 10 million units per year. End-goal unit price: $20,000–$30,000.

- **Optimus unit economics: ~$1.50–$2.00/hour fully loaded vs. $25–$35/hour for U.S. warehouse labor:** At a $25,000 unit cost, 3-year useful life, and ~7,000 productive hours/year, hardware cost alone is ~$1.20/hour; adding energy (~$0.10–$0.15) and maintenance (~$0.20–$0.30) yields ~$1.50–$2.00/hour fully loaded — a 90%+ labor cost reduction versus U.S. warehouse workers.

- **Amazon flagged as potential anchor Optimus customer; competitors already deployed:** Amazon operates 1 million+ robots across 300+ fulfillment centers (up 250,000 in under one year) and is identified as the largest concentrated humanoid market on Earth. However, Tesla has the most catching up to do on actual deployment: Boston Dynamics Atlas is committed to Hyundai's Meta Plant through 2026; Apptronik (Apollo) raised $520M with GXO Logistics as customer; Figure is in BMW Spartanburg; Agility Robotics has subscription humanoid models already operating in warehouses.

- **Piper Sandler: Optimus is "free" at $400/share — not yet in their model:** Piper Sandler published an update stating their valuation assigns zero value to Optimus at $400/share, and they will begin incorporating it once revenue arrives.

- **Larry Goldberg expects Optimus to "really roll out" by Q3–Q4 2026:** Goldberg described Optimus demonstrating genuine capability by Q3–Q4 2026 as one of several simultaneous catalysts — alongside robotaxi progress and the SpaceX IPO — that could drive Tesla stock to a new all-time high this year.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model S and Model X production ends after 14 and 11 years respectively:** Tesla has built its final Model S and Model X, with approximately 750,000 combined deliveries over their lifespans. The final vehicles were signed by factory workers; a planned final delivery event was delayed with 72 hours' notice, with leaked footage suggesting production may not have been fully complete at announcement. Factory capacity is now freed for Model Y, Cybertruck, Cybercab, and next-generation vehicles.

- **China April retail sales: 25,956 units, down 10% YoY — "36% surge" was wholesale output:** Per CPCA final data (via Electrek), Tesla's actual China retail sales fell 9.66% year-over-year in April, with the widely reported 36% surge reflecting Giga Shanghai's 79,478-unit wholesale production figure — of which 53,522 were exports. January–April 2026 cumulative domestic retail: 138,754 units, down 15% YoY. Tesla's NEV market share hit 3.06%, its lowest since November 2025.

- **Giga Shanghai April exports: 53,000 units — highest since October 2022, +80% YoY:** Export volumes to South Korea, Europe, Australia, and Canada drove the wholesale surge. Jeff Lutz noted export markets carry higher margins than China's intensely competitive domestic pricing environment. Canada recently resumed accepting Chinese-made Teslas.

- **Tesla Semi battery capacity confirmed: 822 kWh long range, 548 kWh standard range:** California regulators published official figures. The Semi is now appearing on Tesla's European websites (UK listed, standard range only so far). The Sparks, Nevada factory is designed for 50,000 trucks/year; Tesla received 965 of 1,067 California Class 8 clean truck voucher applications, with Daimler, Paccar, and Volvo combined receiving under 100.

- **Strong European and select market sales cited:** April figures referenced: Denmark +102%, Sweden +111%, France +112% in Tesla vehicle sales volume. These are cited in the context of FSD rollout beginning to drive demand awareness in Europe.

## Market & Competition

- **Amazon Supply Chain Services (ASCS) launched — Farzad frames Tesla as the physical-AI picks-and-shovels play:** Amazon announced ASCS, an end-to-end logistics platform (ocean/air/ground/rail freight, fulfillment, parcel delivery, AI inventory forecasting) open to any business, entering a $9.37 trillion global logistics market projected to reach $13 trillion by 2035. Immediate market reaction: FedEx worst single day in over a year, UPS -10%, GXO -13%, CH Robinson -9%. Farzad's thesis: Tesla Semi (autonomous trucks), Cybercab/Robo Van (autonomous delivery), and Optimus (warehouse labor) comprise a unified FSD-powered stack that is uniquely positioned to supply every layer of ASCS's physical infrastructure needs.

- **SpaceX IPO expected June 2026; Larry Goldberg forecasts 30–40% Tesla stock move:** Goldberg identifies a successful SpaceX IPO as Tesla's single largest 2026 catalyst — arguing it reframes Elon Musk's public credibility, validates his ability to deliver on audacious claims, and helps investors understand the Tesla-SpaceX relationship. He estimates a target IPO valuation of ~$1.75 trillion, with success defined as reaching ~$2 trillion by end of day. A Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement, which Goldberg describes as "extraordinarily positive," would not occur before the post-IPO lockup period ends — likely after end of 2026.

- **Bhakdi: $490 is the near-term technical floor, everything above is "complete randomness" until robotaxi scales:** Bhakdi frames Tesla's return to its December all-time high of $490 as mechanically driven — the broader market is at prior highs, Tesla is fundamentally stronger, therefore $490 is a minimum near-term target achievable within one to two weeks. He explicitly cautions against extrapolating to $500–$600 absent evidence of robotaxi scaling to hundreds of vehicles.

- **Kodiak Robotics: 20 autonomous trucks deployed in Permian Basin, seventh-generation SensorPod:** CEO Don Burnette detailed Kodiak's universal SensorPod design (same pod across OEM makes, breakaway shear-bolt impact protection, seventh-generation hardware), with 20 trucks hauling sand for Atlas Energy at 275,000 lbs gross freight weight operating 24/7 as of end of 2025. Farzad separately named Kodiak's parent-ecosystem competitor Nvidia's Alpamayo as having demonstrated nothing approaching Tesla's coast-to-coast FSD capability, with competitors limited to short-segment YouTube clips.

- **Elon joining Trump's China visit alongside Cook, Fink, Solomon:** Elon Musk is reportedly part of a tech/finance CEO delegation accompanying President Trump to China. Jeff Lutz dismissed concerns about Chinese automakers gaining meaningful U.S. manufacturing foothold, citing U.S. labor rates, supply chain constraints, and structural cost challenges that would make domestic Chinese-brand EV production unprofitable.
