## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.3.2 coast-to-coast record set.** Zach Black, Dan Berkeland, and Aaron drove a Model 3 from New York City to Los Angeles — 2,800 miles, zero interventions, under 50 hours — beating the prior record by 8.5 hours. Tesla officially reposted the achievement. Per Jeff Lutz, no competing vehicle can offer anything comparable.
- **Tesla Vision airbag pre-deployment now live on new cars.** Tesla Vision cameras detect unavoidable collisions and can trigger airbag deployment up to 70 milliseconds earlier than impact-sensor-only systems. Elon Musk confirmed it arrives "for free on all new cars" via software update, applicable across a fleet of approximately 8–9 million vehicles.
- **Neural net photon reconstruction gives Tesla cameras superhuman low-light vision.** Elon shared before/after images demonstrating that Tesla AI's photon count reconstruction resolves scenes with extreme glare that are illegible to the human eye — running in real time on in-vehicle AI supercomputers.
- **FSD 14.3 advances centered on reinforcement learning and operational robustness.** Per Cern Basher's panel, the majority of 14.3 improvements — pothole avoidance, animal avoidance, improved school zone handling — are RL use cases. A upgraded vision encoder (enabling higher data ingestion rates) and improved self-recovery from edge cases without driver intervention are flagged as the most consequential infrastructure upgrades for unsupervised robotaxi scale.
- **Hardware 3 predicted to never run unsupervised robotaxi.** One Cern Basher panelist stated — as a personal prediction, not a Tesla commitment — that HW3 vehicles will not run unsupervised in the robotaxi network or in customer unsupervised mode, implying unsupervised capability is limited to HW4 and Cybercab.
- **Tesla hiring robotaxi software engineers with miles per intervention as primary KPI.** A Tesla job posting explicitly names miles per intervention as the key metric, with the stated goal to keep it "as high as possible while robotaxi expands globally." Jeff Lutz noted that much of the front-end FSD architecture work is now migrating toward Optimus development.
- **2026 Model Y is first vehicle to pass four new NHTSA/NCAP ADAS tests.** The 2026 Model Y (manufactured on or after November 2025) passed pedestrian AEB, lane-keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention — the first vehicle to clear all four. No other automaker had passed more than three as of this reporting.
- **Ireland FSD regulatory engagement confirmed; Belgium and Denmark also in motion.** Ireland's Department of Transport confirmed active talks with Tesla and NSAI on FSD (Supervised) approval, with compliance required at both Irish and EU levels. In Belgium, a local director is pushing to fast-forward FSD analysis, though authorities want limited-scale testing first. Denmark's road authority pushed back on a Reuters report, clarifying it expressed no criticism of Tesla FSD.
- **China FSD employee beta rumored on version 14.3.2.** An unverified social media report (attributed to "Ry") claims Tesla China began pushing FSD 14.3.2 to approximately 1,000 employee vehicles in late April/early May, with NDAs signed. Herbert Ong speculated the timing may align with a potential Elon Musk visit to China; Jeff Lutz cautioned that product milestones would not be built around a rescheduled diplomatic trip.
- **Chinese auto industry official acknowledges gap vs. Tesla FSD.** Wang Yao, deputy chief engineer of the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers, stated that despite domestic social media claims, "we should also honestly acknowledge some of the gaps" relative to Tesla FSD — framed by commentators as a signal of why Chinese regulators have moved cautiously.
- **Unusual volume of NHTSA complaints against Tesla flagged as potentially organized.** A secondhand account relayed by FutureAzA's Brian describes a contact at NHTSA noting Tesla was receiving more complaints than units of the relevant vehicle sold, suggesting coordinated bad-faith filing rather than legitimate consumer grievances.

## Robotaxi
- **Robotaxi-first sequencing locked in; customer unsupervised FSD is priority two.** The Cern Basher panel reached consensus that Tesla's internal focus is overwhelmingly on scaling robotaxi revenue before enabling personal-vehicle unsupervised FSD. The business logic: generate robotaxi cash flow first to buffer the litigation costs Elon Musk himself acknowledged are unavoidable, then expand to customer unsupervised use.
- **Operations center scalability framed as the binding design constraint.** Panelists emphasized that if the current small fleet requires frequent intervention, scaling to tens or hundreds of thousands of vehicles would make the operations center unmanageable. FSD 14.3's self-recovery improvements are directly aimed at reducing the interventions-per-vehicle-mile ratio as a prerequisite for economically viable large-scale deployment.
- **Austin supervised-to-unsupervised transition described as the observable maturation path.** Herbert Ong noted Tesla appears to be systematically replacing supervised Austin robotaxi rides with driverless ones as the technology is validated city by city, which is visible in real time through RobotaxiTracker data.
- **Fleet growth pace defended statistically.** Jeff Lutz pushed back on skeptics, arguing that going from approximately 22 to 33 to 35 unsupervised vehicles within a 7–10 day window represents a ~50% fleet increase — statistically meaningful — though he acknowledged that what Elon means by "volume deployment" remains unknown, with hundreds of thousands as one possible threshold.
- **Cybercab step-change deployment expected in 2026, not April or May.** FutureAzA's Brian stated no large Cybercab deployment step is expected in April or May 2026 but characterized it as "definitely" coming this year, adding: "No roadblocks have appeared. Quite the opposite."
- **Competitor AV under NHTSA investigation for 16 accidents in Dallas and Austin.** A non-Tesla autonomous vehicle company (operating in Dallas and Austin since 2017, available on Uber) is under NHTSA investigation following 16 ADS-related accidents including lane-change conflicts and striking stationary objects, resulting in one minor injury. This is not a Tesla vehicle.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus unit economics: ~$1.50–$2.00/hour fully loaded vs. $25–$35/hour for a U.S. warehouse worker.** Farzad's analysis uses a $25,000 unit cost, 3-year useful life, and ~7,000 productive hours/year to arrive at ~$1.20/hour hardware cost, with energy and maintenance bringing the total to ~$1.50–$2.00/hour — a 90%+ labor cost reduction at scale.
- **Amazon flagged as potential anchor Optimus customer.** Amazon already operates over 1 million robots across 300+ fulfillment centers (up from 750,000 the prior year). Farzad argues Amazon's warehouses are the largest concentrated humanoid market on Earth and that at $20,000–$30,000/unit, Amazon could absorb hundreds of thousands of Optimus units without straining capex. A pilot deployment announcement is characterized as a "generational signal" event.
- **Tesla not currently leading on real-world humanoid deployments.** Farzad explicitly acknowledges competitors are ahead on actual deployment: Boston Dynamics' Atlas is live at Hyundai's Meta Plant in Georgia (all 2026 capacity committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind); Apptronik (Apollo) raised $520M with GXO Logistics as a customer; Figure is at BMW Spartanburg; Agility Robotics has subscription leasing models already operating in warehouses.
- **Optimus production targets: Fremont ~1M units/year run rate by late 2026, Giga Texas targeting 10M units/year.** Farzad explicitly expects the Fremont timeline to slip into 2027–2028. Production is targeted to begin on the converted Model S/X line in late July or August 2026.
- **Piper Sandler: Optimus is free at $400/share.** Piper Sandler's published update argues its valuation model assigns zero value to Optimus at the current share price (~$440 at time of reporting), and will begin incorporating Optimus once revenue arrives.
- **Larry Goldberg expects Optimus to "really roll out" in Q3–Q4 2026.** He frames it as one of several simultaneous catalysts — alongside robotaxi progress and the SpaceX IPO — that could drive Tesla's stock higher this year.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Model S and Model X production ends.** Tesla has built the final units of both models — 14 years of Model S history, 11 years of Model X — with approximately 750,000 combined deliveries worldwide. Factory workers signed the final vehicles. The capacity freed is expected to be directed toward Model Y, Cybertruck, Cybercab, and next-generation vehicles. A planned final delivery event was delayed by 72 hours with little notice.
- **China April retail sales fell 10% YoY; the "36% surge" was a wholesale/export figure.** Giga Shanghai produced 79,478 vehicles in April (+36% YoY), but 53,522 were exports — the second-highest monthly export total in Giga Shanghai's history and up 80% YoY. Domestic retail was 25,956 units, down 9.66% from 28,731 in April 2025. For January–April 2026, domestic retail totals 138,754 units, down 15% YoY. Tesla's NEV market share in China hit 3.06% — its lowest since November 2025.
- **Tesla canceled seven-year low-interest loan option in China.** Tesla retained only a zero-interest plan of up to five years. Promotional financing has not reversed the domestic demand decline so far.
- **Giga Shanghai exports surging.** April exports of 53,522 units were up 80% YoY and 81% vs. March. For January–April, exports totaled 154,122 units, up 127% YoY. Jeff Lutz noted export markets (EU, South Korea, Japan, Australia) carry higher margins than domestic China pricing.
- **Tesla Semi battery capacity confirmed by California regulators: 822 kWh long range, 548 kWh standard range.** The Semi is now appearing on Tesla's European websites (UK), currently listing only the standard range variant. In California's clean truck voucher program, Tesla Semi received 965 of 1,067 Class 8 applications; Daimler, Paccar, and Volvo combined received under 100.
- **Tesla remains the best-selling automotive brand in California** — a distinction no American automaker had held since the 1990s, per a Forbes piece cited by Sawyer Merritt.
- **Tesla issued a recall of 218,000 U.S. vehicles** for a backup camera software glitch, resolved over the air. Characterized as a non-event by multiple sources.

## Market & Competition
- **Amazon launches ASCS — framed as the AWS of the physical world.** Amazon Supply Chain Services bundles ocean, air, ground, and rail freight with fulfillment, parcel shipping, and AI inventory forecasting into a single platform available to any business. Launch customers include P&G, 3M, Lands' End, American Eagle, and Aerie. Infrastructure includes ~1,200 facilities, 350+ fulfillment centers, 40,000+ semi-trucks, 100+ aircraft, and 30,000 Rivian electric vans (committed to 100,000 by 2030). The global logistics market is $9.37 trillion today, projected to grow to $13 trillion by 2035. FedEx, UPS (-10%), GXO (-13%), and CH Robinson (-9%) all fell sharply on the announcement.
- **SpaceX IPO expected June 2026; Larry Goldberg projects 30–40% Tesla stock move on success.** Larry identifies the IPO — targeting ~$1.75 trillion with success defined as reaching ~$2 trillion by end of day — as the single biggest Tesla catalyst of 2026, ahead of any robotaxi fleet milestone. He argues a successful IPO rehabilitates Musk's public image, educates investors on the Tesla-SpaceX relationship, and validates Elon's credibility on autonomous vehicle promises. Tesla rose ~$10 on the SpaceX-Anthropic deal announcement despite no direct Tesla connection.
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger discussed but not expected before lockup ends.** Larry believes any merger announcement would not occur during or around the IPO lockup period (estimated at up to six months), placing any such announcement after end of 2026 at the earliest.
- **Elon Musk joining Trump's China visit alongside Tim Cook, Goldman, BlackRock, and Visa/Mastercard CEOs.** Tesla China SVP Grace Tao posted optimistically about US-China mutual prosperity ahead of the trip. Jeff Lutz dismissed concerns about Chinese automakers building $10,000 EVs in the U.S. as unrealistic given domestic labor rates, supply chain constraints, and labor availability.
- **Stellantis considering selling an underutilized Spanish factory to Leap Motors joint venture.** European factories are operating at roughly half capacity; Brian characterized the sale as guaranteeing — not merely risking — further Chinese automaker strengthening, as steep import tariffs are being bypassed by local manufacturing.
- **Tesla-Intel semiconductor partnership reported.** Elon Musk toured Intel's Hillsboro fab ahead of a reported Tesla and SpaceX partnership with Intel. Wedbush called it a "compelling opportunity" while flagging significant execution risk between announced plans and actual production.
