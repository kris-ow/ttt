Electrek reports that Tesla has quietly begun shipping new Fremont-built Model Y vehicles (delivered late December and January) with a new Full Self-Driving computer, internally labeled "AP4.5"/"AP45" or "AI4.5," identified via a part number previously spotted in Tesla's Electronic Parts Catalog. Tesla made no official announcement.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **New AI4.5 computer discovered by owners:** Model Y owner @Eric5un and others on X found vehicles with a new front camera housing and an Autopilot computer labeled "AP45," confirmed via part number by pulling back the carpet liner near the glovebox. Several affected vehicles also have Tesla's updated 16-inch center display and revised windshield camera housing, part of a minor "2026 model year" update.

2. **Possible three-chip design:** Based on firmware analysis by Tesla hacker @greentheonly, the new HW4.5 computer may use a three-SoC (System-on-Chip) architecture, a change from the dual-SoC design used since HW4 leaked in February 2023. The article speculates this could mean more compute power, enhanced redundancy, or a bridge to AI5, but states the memory configuration of AI4.5 is unclear.

3. **AI5 delays creating a gap:** Musk previously said AI5 would come to autos in the second half of 2025 with "10x" the power of current hardware; Electrek reported in November that AI5 has slipped to mid-2027, with only "a small number of units" arriving in late 2026. Musk recently said AI5's design is "almost done," six months after saying it was "finished" — Electrek notes typical chip design cycles don't run 9 months and urges skepticism. The article argues AI4.5 appears to be Tesla's stopgap to cover the resulting roughly 18-month compute gap before AI5 arrives in volume.

4. **Hardware upgrade pattern and owner risk:** The article recounts Tesla's history of hardware transitions (HW2→HW2.5 with retrofits, HW2.5→HW3 with free retrofits, HW3→HW4 with no retrofits) and notes Musk admitted in January 2025 that HW3 computers would need replacement, a promise Electrek says still has no concrete plan. It raises the question of whether HW4 vehicles could face similar limitations as AI4.5/AI5 roll out, and flags Tesla's ongoing liability exposure tied to HW3 vehicles sold with self-driving promises.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla's quiet hardware rollouts reflect a pattern of avoiding impact on sales of older-hardware vehicles, and contends AI4.5's existence shows both that FSD software is outpacing hardware and that Tesla likely knew AI5 delays were coming. He advises prospective buyers to verify which computer version a new vehicle has before purchase, and recommends buying a Tesla for its current capabilities only, not for future self-driving promises.
