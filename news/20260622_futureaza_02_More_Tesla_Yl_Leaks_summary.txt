This is FutureAzA's Monday news roundup for June 22, 2026, hosted by Brian, covering a wide range of Tesla and EV industry stories. The episode touches on Tesla Model Y L leaks, Slate EV pricing speculation, autonomous trucking developments, robotaxi competitive dynamics, energy storage alternatives, and various EV industry moves from Ford, Honda, GM, Rivian, and others. Brian's tone is editorially opinionated throughout, mixing confirmed news with personal analysis and viewer Q&A.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Model YL Coming to the US This Summer:** Brian reports that according to information from what he describes as a credible source, the Tesla Model Y Long (YL) — a larger, third-row variant of the Model Y — will begin production in Texas in August or September 2026. Brian says his own source separately confirmed August production. Visually, the YL is identical to the standard Model Y from the waist up, but notably larger from the hips down; the key visual tell in spy shots is a significantly larger third rear window. One published article speculates a price of $539.90, but Brian believes the price will be $549.90, estimating a roughly $4,000 premium over the standard Model Y — consistent with the pricing differential seen in China.

2. **Slate EV Pricing Speculation:** A code examination of Slate's website revealed a price of $24,950 still embedded in the site's deprecated code. Brian is skeptical this reflects the actual launch price, calling it likely outdated and noting that inflation alone would have driven the price up. He argues that if Slate launches under $30,000 it could be a viable product, but expresses serious doubt about profitability at the $24,950 figure unless the company is willing to absorb losses at low volume and offset them through stock offerings.

3. **Cybertruck Pricing and Demand:** A viewer raises the possibility that a dual-motor all-wheel-drive Cybertruck at $60,000 could generate overwhelming demand. Brian agrees the truck would sell well at that price, noting that $60,000 in 2026 dollars is equivalent to roughly $48,000 in 2019 terms — "a whole lot of truck" for that inflation-adjusted price. He expresses hope Tesla can make that version profitable enough to reintroduce it.

4. **Ford's LFP Battery Push and EV Pivot Confusion:** Ford revealed plans for a new LFP battery aimed at mass-market EV pricing, with hiring underway at Blue Oval Battery Park in Michigan — over 500 employees hired so far, with plans to reach 800 by end of 2026. Brian is skeptical of Ford's direction, noting the company has repeatedly reversed its EV ambitions. He also raises the issue that China has placed export restrictions on the equipment used to manufacture the latest-generation LFP batteries, meaning any U.S. or Western factory would at best be producing the prior generation — adequate for stationary energy storage but competitively disadvantaged against Chinese EVs. Brian notes Tesla's Nevada facility faces the same constraint. He also mentions (without a sourced article) that Ford may be discontinuing the Mustang Mach-E.

5. **BYD Shark Hybrid Truck and Brazil Battery Production:** Brian covers the BYD Shark, a hybrid (not fully electric) pickup truck available in Hawaii, noting it sidesteps EV range challenges by using a hybrid drivetrain. He highlights that BYD is ramping battery production in Brazil, which he views as strategically significant — localizing production removes Chinese sourcing concerns and, given U.S.-Brazil trade relations, could eventually open a path for those batteries to enter the U.S. market.

6. **Honda + QuantumScape Solid-State Partnership:** Honda has announced a partnership with QuantumScape for solid-state EV batteries. Brian is dismissive of Honda's current EV urgency, quipping they're "in neutral and coasting," and jokes the timing may actually work out since QuantumScape hasn't reached scale production either.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Semi Spotted with Autonomous Validation Equipment:** An enthusiast spotted a Tesla Semi in Sunnyvale equipped with what Brian describes as LIDAR gear on the roof and ground-truthing equipment on the cabin exterior — indicating Tesla is actively preparing the Semi for FSD capability. Brian treats this as unsurprising and largely expected, consistent with Tesla's broader autonomy roadmap.

2. **Tesla Megapod Trademark Filing — AI Data Center Speculation:** Tesla quietly filed a trademark for "Megapod," which Brian connects to the previously discussed pre-assembled Megacharger patent for the Tesla Semi. He speculates the concept could extend to a standalone product — potentially a prefabricated AI compute node co-located with charging infrastructure. He acknowledges the idea runs into latency challenges but finds it worth watching.

3. **Tesla Pre-Assembled Megacharger Patent for Semi:** A patent has surfaced showing Tesla's plan to build pre-assembled, prefabricated Megacharger units for the Tesla Semi. Brian views this as straightforwardly positive — moving permitting and contractor complexity back to the factory improves speed and quality control.

4. **Aurora + Volvo Autonomous Trucks on I-35:** Aurora and Volvo have partnered to run driverless semi trucks on a roughly 200-mile route between Dallas and Oklahoma City, operating up to five days per week. Brian praises the development, noting Aurora has had considerable success testing driverless semis in Texas and that non-Tesla companies blazing the autonomy trail is broadly beneficial for the industry.

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Competitive Scorecard — China Leading:** A company called OTM AI has developed a generative AI benchmarking platform that evaluates all robotaxi operators every 12 hours using publicly available and Creative Commons data. As of the Friday before air date, the top-ranked robotaxi operator was Baidu's Apollo Go program, narrowly edging out Waymo in second place, followed by Pony AI and WeRide, with Tesla in fifth. The system evaluates safety records, deployment numbers, and other publicly available metrics.

2. **Texas AV Registrations — Waymo and Tesla Growth:** Per the OTM AI scorecard data cited in the transcript: as of the video date, Waymo had 620 autonomous vehicles registered in Texas — up from 577 in May, a roughly 7.5% increase. Tesla had 69 registered autonomous vehicles in Texas, up 64% from 42 in May. Zoox had 35 registered vehicles but lacks the correct permit to charge for rides.

3. **Uber Premium Robotaxi Coming to Houston in 2027:** Uber is bringing a premium robotaxi service to Houston in 2027, using a Lucid Gravity vehicle powered by Nuro's self-driving software (rather than Lucid's proprietary system). Brian questions the unit economics, noting the Lucid Gravity costs roughly three times what competing vehicles cost, and that revenue must be split among Uber, Lucid, and Nuro.

4. **Mobileye Plans U.S. Robotaxi Launch in 2027:** Mobileye (Intel subsidiary) announced plans to launch a robotaxi service in the U.S. in 2027, positioning itself as both a hardware/technology supplier and a finished-service operator. Mobileye has cited a target of 17,000 vehicles over the following five years. Brian is skeptical, comparing it to competitors still "lacing up their shoes" 10–15 years into the autonomous vehicle race.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **SoftBank Sells Boston Dynamics Stake for $325 Million:** SoftBank walked away from its investment in Boston Dynamics, selling its roughly 10% stake to Hyundai for $325 million — implying a total company valuation of just over $3 billion. Brian uses this to push back against viewers who cite Boston Dynamics as a credible Optimus competitor, arguing the company lacks the compute infrastructure, data centers, and manufacturing scale to compete — and that the valuation reflects this. A viewer separately mentions having toured the Optimus factory and calling it "mind-blowing," which Brian notes with interest.

2. **GM vs. UAW Over Factory Robots:** Brian briefly covers a GM and UAW dispute in which the union is pushing back against the use of collaborative robots (cobots) on the factory floor. Brian is openly critical of the union's position.

## Energy

1. **Gravity Battery Tower — Skepticism on Concrete Block Energy Storage:** A company is building a 40-story windowless concrete tower that stores energy by lifting 35-ton concrete blocks when power is available (e.g., from wind) and recovering energy as the blocks are lowered. Brian references Wired's math: one 55-gallon drum of concrete stores enough energy to charge roughly four to eight cell phones, and it would take more than 50 such drums to equal the storage of a single Tesla Powerwall. He concludes the economics are unlikely to pencil out at scale due to weight, wear and tear, and construction costs — but supports the attempt as a necessary proof-of-concept exercise.

2. **GM Vehicle-to-Grid Development:** GM is working on a vehicle-to-grid / vehicle-to-load system that could enable time-of-use energy shifting for consumers. Brian notes there is no specific product announcement, only that development is ongoing, and that the fragmented U.S. utility landscape (roughly 4,000 utilities) complicates widespread deployment.

## Market & Competition

1. **Rivian Class Action Lawsuit Over Gen 1 Autonomy Claims:** Rivian is reportedly facing a class action lawsuit alleging fraud, negligent misrepresentation, and unjust enrichment over claims made about the autonomous capabilities of its Gen 1 vehicles — specifically that no software update could deliver the advertised autonomy. Brian notes the lawsuit echoes similar challenges faced by Tesla over early FSD marketing. He doubts the lawsuit will go anywhere because any reasonable settlement would be existentially threatening to Rivian given its low sales volume.

2. **Global EV Adoption Trends:** EV adoption data shows China growing very rapidly (steep upward trend), Europe showing a flattened but recovering trajectory, and the U.S. hovering at a low adoption rate by comparison. Brian frames this as a signal that investors not in EV-exposed equities may be missing the longer-term growth story.

3. **SpaceX Beach Access Ruling:** The Texas Supreme Court unanimously denied an environmental group's request to require beach access during SpaceX launches at Boca Chica. Brian supports the ruling on safety grounds. (Note: this is a SpaceX story, included as general context.)
