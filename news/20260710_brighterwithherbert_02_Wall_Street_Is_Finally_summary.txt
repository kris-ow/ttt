This episode of the show (guest-hosted by Cern Basher with Brian White) surveys a wave of Wall Street price targets on newly-public SpaceX, then pivots to Tesla-specific news including a new RBC price target tied to SpaceX-merger speculation, Megapack order momentum, AI competitive positioning charts, and driverless Cybercab sightings in Austin.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX price target flurry following NASDAQ 100 inclusion:** After SpaceX joined the NASDAQ 100 less than a month after going public, multiple banks initiated or raised coverage: a firm referred to as "NEM" set a $200 target (buy rating, citing category-leading orbital infrastructure and Starlink scale advantage); Goldman Sachs set $225, framing space, connectivity, and AI as multi-trillion-dollar opportunities over a 5+ year horizon; JP Morgan set $225 and projected a 91% five-year revenue CAGR (2025–2030) off a claimed $28 trillion total addressable market; Raymond James set an outlier $800 target implying roughly a $10 trillion valuation, well above peers clustered near $200–300 (with one target as low as $130). SpaceX was trading around $150 (market cap near $1.6 trillion) as of the Wednesday prior to recording.
2. **Bank of America commentary on SpaceX's expanding footprint:** A Bank of America speaker (quoted via clip) argued investors shouldn't lose sight of SpaceX's full scope — launch dominance, Starlink, Starshield (national security), and an emerging orbital compute business — arguing SpaceX in ten years will look very different from today as "doors open more doors," on an estimated $8 billion of total outside investment in the space business to date.
3. **Morgan Stanley "chipflation" memory-market caution:** A Morgan Stanley note (relayed via a third party, Ricky Hoe) argued the AI memory trade is moving from an explosive first phase into a more normalized cycle phase, with memory pricing growth slowing and inventories normalizing; the note warns that if hyperscalers begin reselling excess compute capacity (citing SpaceX/xAI selling compute to other companies as a possible early sign), GPU/memory pricing power could moderate — but frames this as a buying opportunity in memory names rather than a reason to exit the sector. Brian White countered that even if a pricing "bubble" corrects, overall chip/memory unit sales should keep growing as underlying demand — including from Tesla's FSD/autonomy compute needs across an estimated 80 million vehicles a year and future humanoid robots — remains in early innings.
4. **AI competitive positioning tables (per The Information, via Alexandra Merz):** Charts comparing Google, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI, Meta, Nvidia, Apple, Anthropic, and a combined Tesla/xAI ("SpaceX AI") entity showed the combined Tesla/xAI group newly active in AI server chips, AI training clusters, cloud AI server rentals, developer APIs, and enterprise/consumer AI apps — but still lacking a wearable AI device or humanoid robot category on the chart (despite Optimus's existence), which the hosts noted as an inconsistency in the chart's methodology.
5. **South Korea/chip-index tangent:** A brief aside compared two emerging-market ETF indexes (one including South Korea, one not) showing a roughly 20% vs. 40% one-year return gap, attributed to the South Korean chipmaker boom (Samsung reported a large quarterly profit surge before its stock fell on the news) — tangential to Tesla but framed as context for the broader AI/chip cycle discussion.

## Energy
1. **Surge in new Tesla Megapack orders:** Over the prior six weeks, more than $9 billion in new Tesla Megapack projects were announced, totaling more than 43 gigawatt-hours of battery storage — comparable to roughly a full year's worth of Tesla's energy storage deployments (49 GWh delivered in the trailing 12 months per the host). Basher noted Giga Shanghai's Megapack output is still ramping and that China-made units likely face longer overseas shipping/permitting delays before showing up in delivery counts. For scale, the $9 billion in six weeks of bookings was contrasted with Lucid's cumulative all-time revenue of $1.6 billion.

## Robotaxi
1. **More driverless Cybercabs spotted in Austin:** Footage showed a driverless Cybercab navigating what appeared to be a parking lot or apartment complex entrance, plus test-track footage described as vehicles performing self-diagnostic NVH (noise/vibration/harshness) runs — driving over test surfaces, using cabin mics to detect squeaks/rattles, then automatically reporting defects back to the assembly line for correction, described by the hosts as a new level of manufacturing quality automation.

## Financials
1. **RBC raises Tesla price target on SpaceX-merger scenario:** RBC Capital raised its Tesla price target to $500 (from $475), explicitly incorporating a 25–30% valuation premium to reflect a potential SpaceX-Tesla acquisition/merger scenario. Both hosts expressed skepticism about the rationale, with Basher questioning how a merger alone would justify a 25–30% premium and suggesting a combined trillion-dollar-plus entity could instead trade sideways for years due to valuation extremes.

## Market & Competition (SpaceX infrastructure)
1. **SpaceX FCC filing for Gen 3 (Starlink V3) satellite constellation:** SpaceX filed with the FCC to launch and operate a Gen 3 satellite system of 100,000 satellites across two closely-stacked orbital shells at altitudes between roughly 320–473 km — a separate constellation from a previously filed application for up to a million AI satellites. Musk responded to the filing by saying "we're going to need a bigger rocket."
2. **Starlink manufacturing scale and pricing changes:** SpaceX is reportedly producing over 170,000 Starlink kits per week (roughly 8.5 million per year) at its Texas factory. Separately, SpaceX announced a 100% price increase on its business aviation Wi-Fi plans (e.g., global unlimited moving to $20,000/month from $10,000; regional unlimited to $12,500/month; regional 25 Gbps to $4,000/month).
3. **Grok 4.5 public launch teased:** Quoting an unconfirmed statement attributed to Elon Musk, xAI planned to make "Grok 4.5" — described as an Opus-class model that is faster, more token-efficient, and lower cost — available to the public the day after recording, pending official confirmation from Musk or xAI.
