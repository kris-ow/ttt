This video covers Tesla Robotaxi's rapid Texas fleet expansion and new Fort Worth launch, comparing registered vehicle counts and October safety incident logs against Waymo, with commentary from manufacturing executive Jeff Le. It also briefly touches a Bloomberg profit-growth ranking that put SpaceX at the top and prompted a reply from Elon Musk about Tesla.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fort Worth launch and DFW footprint:** Tesla launched unsupervised Robotaxi service in Fort Worth, Texas at 6 a.m. the morning of the video, covering a 143 square mile geofence — larger than its 81 square mile Dallas zone and the third-largest Tesla service area behind Austin (272 sq mi) and Houston (254 sq mi). Combined with Dallas, Tesla now covers 224 square miles across the DFW region; the host and Jeff Le speculate the two zones may eventually be connected given hiring for robotaxi attendants in Arlington and Irving (cities not yet in the service area) and the recent addition of highway driving to the service.
2. **Texas fleet nearly passes Waymo:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com-aligned figures cited in the video, Tesla had 1,244 registered robotaxis in Texas (671 Model Y, 573 Cybercab) versus Waymo's 1,259 registered vehicles — a gap of 15 — with Tesla adding 164 new vehicles in Texas over the prior four days (up from a fleet of 59 in June). The host projects Tesla could pass Waymo's registered count within the week; Jeff Le cautions this isn't really a close "race" given Tesla's cost-structure advantages but notes fleet size alone doesn't confirm operational scale.
3. **Waymo's fleet mix and Toyota partnership:** Waymo's Texas fleet is dominated by 863 discontinued Jaguar I-PACE vehicles, supplemented by Chinese-made Zeekr vans (imported under tariff payment and remanufactured domestically to add Waymo's driver hardware, per Jeff Le) and Hyundai Ioniq 5s expected to be built at a Georgia factory. Waymo also just announced a new partnership with Toyota to add BZ4X EVs to its fleet, but testing won't start in Japan until 2027, with UK and other market deployment planned for later years.
4. **Expansion pipeline and hiring signals:** Tesla's next-market list (from its earnings call) includes Las Vegas, Phoenix, San Antonio, and Jacksonville, with additional markets reportedly including Corpus Christi, Tucson, Chicago, Atlanta, and Nashville per a third-party map (Tesla Zone). Tesla is hiring self-driving data collection supervisors across 37 cities in 20 states (up from 26 cities/12 states a week earlier), including new listings in Louisville, Cincinnati, Columbus, and parts of Oklahoma, Kentucky, and Virginia, plus a robotaxi operations manager role in Scottsdale, Arizona. Jeff Le says these supervisor/data-collection roles likely map problem intersections and edge cases for a process he expects Tesla to eventually automate.
5. **Service improvements:** Robotaxi operating hours were extended to 2 a.m. (up from a prior midnight cutoff), and new features include trip-sharing with friends, a rear-view camera feed, a "calm mode" with dimmed lighting, and Grok now available in Model Y robotaxis.
6. **Out-of-zone Cybercab sightings suggest Austin expansion:** A third-party tracker cited in the video reported a jump from one Cybercab sighting outside Austin's southwest service boundary the week of September 28 to 33 sightings in the following week, which the host interprets as a sign of imminent service-area expansion in Austin.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **October Austin incident log comparison:** NHTSA-reported autonomous vehicle incident logs for Austin in October showed 20 total incident reports — 8 for Tesla (its highest monthly total, up from 5 in September) and 12 for Waymo — with zero collisions for either company. About six of the 20 were logged by Austin police during a music festival weekend. Tesla's two most serious ("red") incidents involved a line of robotaxis not yielding to an Austin Fire Department truck running lights and sirens, and a robotaxi stopping past a crosswalk stop line and attempting to back up while pedestrians crossed, prompting police to block it. Waymo's red incidents included missing an officer's hand signals and stalling mid-intersection, failing to slow for a pedestrian at a midblock crosswalk, and pulling into an opposing lane around stopped cars while an officer held traffic for children. Jeff Le notes the comparison isn't perfectly clean given unknown relative fleet sizes and says Tesla will need to pull logs/video to determine root cause on its incidents.

## Market & Competition
1. **Bloomberg profit-growth ranking and Musk reply:** A Bloomberg analyst ranking of expected net income growth over the next four years reportedly placed SpaceX first at roughly 8,000% growth, ahead of companies including Marvell, AMD, Arm, Palantir, Broadcom, TSMC, Microsoft, Nvidia, Meta, and Amazon; the list did not include Tesla. Elon Musk replied to the post that Tesla's growth "will be very high also." Jeff Le cautions that strong earnings growth doesn't guarantee market multiple expansion, citing Micron and SK Hynix as examples of companies with strong forward earnings that haven't received commensurate valuation credit, while suggesting Tesla and SpaceX's product differentiation may fare better than commodity-like competitive markets.
