## Brief

- Tesla launches "Tesla Home," AI energy optimization built on Opticaster
- Optimus line rising at Fremont, ~40 sublines built in Germany first
- Tesla 2025 Impact Report: Cybercab hits 6.1 mi/kWh, new RIM paint process
- Tesla leases 682,000 sq ft Austin building, use undisclosed
- Megapack momentum: ~$9B in new orders over six weeks, plus $3B Esyasoft deal
- RBC lifts TSLA target to $500 citing SpaceX merger optionality

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's 2025 Impact Report confirms Cybercab will be the "primary vehicle" in the Robotaxi fleet, ditching steering wheel/pedals to cut weight and cost; Tesla claims nearly half the GHG emissions per mile versus Model 3/Y.
- Lars Moravy said the Cybercab line is designed for ~90% full automation and that volume could scale "far beyond what current market estimates say," per brighterwithherbert's automation-focused episode.
- Finished vehicles now undergo a "full self-hearing" quality check: FSD drives them over bumps at the factory while microphones and a trained system listen for defects.
- Tesla's new 682,000 sq ft Austin lease has no confirmed use, but Electrek notes it could support robotaxi-related logistics, parts, or service operations given Tesla's expanding Austin footprint.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus's first real production line is being stood up at Fremont on the former Model S/X floor, per Lars Moravy; Elon Musk visited to walk the floor this week, with initial line footage released.
- Optimus has ~10,000 unique parts, including precision actuators and complex hands, cheaper overall than a car's bill of materials but with no external supplier able to sell a humanoid production line — Tesla must build the capability in-house.
- Tesla Midwest Automation (Brooklyn Park, MN, ex-Perbix) job postings reference work on "Tesla's latest and most advanced products," interpreted as Optimus-related fleet software and virtual commissioning work.
- Munro Live's teardown found a heat pipe cooling system in a high-duty-cycle joint (knee), using phase-change refrigerant to move motor heat efficiently — the same principle as laptop cooling.
- A Tesla VP reportedly said (secondhand) Optimus will reach scaled mass production by end of 2026; a guest on brighterwithherbert projects a production launch around August–September and thousands of units by December.
- Optimus factory steel is now visible near Giga Texas's main plant; construction trackers suggest the North Campus facility could stretch nearly the full length of the existing building.

## Energy
- Tesla launched "Tesla Home," consolidating solar, Powerwall, Wall Connector, and smart-breaker control into one AI-driven app experience — built on the existing Opticaster engine, requiring no new hardware. Electrek frames it as a meaningful rebrand/UI cleanup rather than a new product.
- Opticaster has logged over 100 million operational hours and delivered "tens of millions of dollars" in customer value; Tesla's VPP paid Powerwall owners nearly $10 million in 2024.
- Tesla Energy has announced over $9 billion in new Megapack orders in six weeks (43+ GWh), including a new $3 billion Esyasoft order for 15+ GWh across the UK, Western Europe, GCC, and India.

## Financials
- Tesla leased a 682,000 sq ft speculative industrial building at Austin Hills Commerce Center (completion January 2027), part of Musk's growing Central Texas footprint — now 2.2M sq ft leased plus 10M+ sq ft owned/built across his companies.
- RBC raised its TSLA price target to $500 (from $475), maintaining Outperform, explicitly citing a 25-30% premium tied to potential SpaceX acquisition scenario.
- Grohmann Engineering ($135M, closed Jan 2017) and Perbix (2017) acquisitions are highlighted as the foundation of Tesla's in-house automation capability now powering Optimus and Cybercab lines.

## Electric Vehicles

- Giga Berlin crossed 1 million cumulative drive units in April and posted a record ~61,000 Model Ys in Q1; two consecutive 20% capacity increases (each with ~1,000 new hires) target ~7,500 vehicles/week starting October.
