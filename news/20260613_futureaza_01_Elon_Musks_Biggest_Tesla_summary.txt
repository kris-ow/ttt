This FutureAzA episode, hosted by Brian and Herbert, covers a broad range of Tesla news centered on the slow but steady robotaxi rollout, Cybertruck FSD feature updates, Tesla's strong China sales in May 2026, and a tangential discussion of SpaceX's orbital AI data center announcement. The core thesis is that Tesla's cautious, data-driven approach to scaling its robotaxi and Cybercab programs is deliberate and justified by safety data — and that near-term acceleration is likely once sufficient supervised miles are accumulated.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas DMV registration count vs. RobotaxiTracker:** Only 69 Cybercabs are registered with the Texas DMV as of the time of recording — up from 42 previously. The hosts acknowledge this is the verified official figure, while noting that RobotaxiTracker.com has been a useful but imperfect community tool for monitoring the broader fleet. Both hosts agree that this number is far below what most observers expected a year after launch.

2. **NHTSA safety data since June 2025 launch:** The hosts walk through NHTSA-reported incident data covering the period from December through the recording date. There have been 17 reported incidents since the Austin robotaxi launch in June 2025. Only one involved a speed of 17 mph; the rest were described as fender benders. Of those, only seven were attributed to Tesla's fault, and two of those seven involved human remote operators — not the autonomous system itself. The hosts argue that the NHTSA "crash" classification is overly broad, as it includes events like a tire touching a curb with no damage.

3. **Cybercab lots spotted in Dallas and Houston:** The hosts note recent reports of lots containing 20–30 Cybercabs sitting idle in Dallas and Houston. Their interpretation is that these vehicles are ready to deploy but are awaiting the green light, likely pending additional supervised FSD miles and regulatory confidence specifically for the Cybercab form factor.

4. **Theory: SpaceX IPO timing influenced rollout pacing:** One host speculates that Tesla/Elon Musk deliberately avoided a large-scale Cybercab rollout to prevent any high-profile crash from dominating news cycles and potentially undermining the SpaceX IPO. Now that the IPO has occurred, the host expects more robotaxi news to emerge, though he believes true mass-scale deployment won't happen until a potential Tesla-SpaceX acquisition announcement, which he speculates could come in late July or August 2026.

5. **Cybercab form factor requires its own data set:** The hosts argue — using the Cybertruck Smart Summon delay as a direct analogy — that each new Tesla vehicle form factor requires its own dataset before FSD can be confidently deployed at scale. The Cybercab's unique design means Tesla needs to accumulate sufficient supervised miles specific to that vehicle before mass unsupervised deployment, regardless of how well FSD performs on existing Model 3/Y platforms.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Cybertruck Smart Summon launching soon:** Tesla's official Cybertruck account announced that Smart Summon is coming soon to the Cybertruck — described as overdue after years of silence on this FSD feature for the vehicle. Engineers reportedly cited the Cybertruck's rear-wheel steering and variable front steering as the technical challenges that extended development time.

2. **Ashok Elluswamy presentation on camera perception:** One host references a recent presentation by Ashok Elluswamy (Tesla VP AI/Autopilot) in which he demonstrated that Tesla's cameras can perceive scenes even under blinding sunlight conditions — seeing individual photons and cleaning up the image in ways the human eye cannot. The host uses this to raise the question of whether matrix headlights provide meaningful benefit to FSD's perception systems at night.

3. **Tesla patents smaller, cheaper matrix headlights:** Tesla has filed a patent for a more compact and less expensive method of producing matrix headlights. The significance noted is that Cybertruck's existing small front headlights are too compact to house a traditional projector — this new approach could enable matrix headlights on the Cybertruck. One host describes matrix headlights as a meaningful improvement for nighttime driving, citing over a thousand miles of personal nighttime FSD experience.

4. **Waymo criticized for traffic-blocking incidents:** The hosts argue that NHTSA's crash-reporting framework fails to capture dangerous-but-non-collision events, using Waymo as the primary example. They contend that Waymo vehicles frequently block traffic — creating hazardous conditions for other drivers — without these events appearing in crash statistics, making the safety comparison between Tesla and Waymo misleading.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla China May 2026 sales surge:** Tesla's China sales for May 2026 reached 85,982 vehicles (Model 3 and Model Y combined), up 39% year-over-year and up 8% from April 2026. Herbert notes that April is typically a weak month in China because it is the first month of the quarter and production is directed toward export deliveries; May reflects domestic sales more accurately. Annualized, this pace approaches roughly one million units per year from Giga Shanghai. Tesla has also rolled out purchase incentives and charging benefits in China.

2. **Tesla holding premium position despite macro headwinds:** Both hosts express surprise that Tesla continues to sell well at a premium price point in China despite macroeconomic weakness in that market. Herbert also notes that Chinese local governments have reintroduced EV subsidies following a period of intense price wars that left many Chinese EV makers losing money — a dynamic that indirectly supports Tesla's sales environment.

3. **Chinese EV import threat assessed skeptically:** The hosts push back on the narrative that Chinese automakers would easily displace Tesla in the US market even if tariffs were reduced. Herbert argues that Chinese automakers' cost advantages are deeply tied to local supply chains, low-cost domestic labor, and high sales volume — none of which would transfer easily to US manufacturing. Additionally, current tariffs would raise Chinese EV prices significantly in the US market. The hosts also point out that Tesla already competes successfully against Chinese brands in China itself, suggesting the competitive threat is overstated.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX orbital AI data center — context for Tesla investors:** The hosts briefly cover SpaceX's unveiling of its first-generation orbital data center satellites, dubbed "AI1," with a peak compute capacity of 150 kilowatts and an average of 120 kilowatts, at an efficiency of 70 kilowatts per ton. Elon Musk has stated a target of one gigawatt of compute capacity in space by 2027. The manufacturing facility (referred to as "Gigasat") will expand an existing facility already used for V3 Starlink satellites. The hosts frame this as relevant context for Tesla investors given the interconnected Musk ecosystem, though they note the primary gating factor is whether sufficient Starship launches can be achieved by 2027.
