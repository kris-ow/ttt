This video discusses two Tesla-related stories: Chinese automotive influencers testing FSD 14 in the US and concluding Tesla's autonomous driving remains the global benchmark, and updates on Giga Berlin's production/battery capacity expansion alongside slow-moving German regulatory review of FSD approval. A guest commentator (identified in the transcript as speaking about Ashok's team) argues the autonomy gap between Tesla and competitors is not closing and may be widening relative to the broader industry, while also arguing Tesla's European sales "spell" may be breaking with a production trough behind it.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Chinese influencers say FSD is the benchmark:** Two Chinese automotive influencers who tested Tesla's FSD 14 in Los Angeles reportedly said that before their trip they believed Chinese systems (Huawei's ADS, Xiaopeng's VLA, Li Auto's ADAS) were the world's best, but changed their minds after extensive testing, concluding FSD is now the benchmark for autonomous systems in China and advising domestic firms to "recognize the gap" and try to catch up.
2. **Guest argues the autonomy gap is widening, not closing:** A guest on the show argued that compared to the broader auto industry attempting autonomy, Tesla's lead is actually growing rather than shrinking, with possible exceptions being XPeng, Huawei, and potentially Nvidia's work with an unnamed partner on a project referred to as Alpamayo — and even those he characterized as "keeping up" rather than closing the gap, still a couple of years behind.
3. **Psychological "best release" effect explains Chinese perception shift:** The guest suggested Chinese engineers testing successive releases of domestic systems (Huawei, Li Auto) experience each new version as "the best ever," similar to how US FSD testers reacted to earlier FSD versions, and that exposure to Tesla's current FSD "breaks the spell" once they have a reference point for what full performance should look like.
4. **Germany conducting independent FSD evaluation before approval:** Per a source (Ming), Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority and Ministry of Transport are reviewing safety documents forwarded by Dutch authorities but are also receiving a dedicated Tesla vehicle in July 2026 to conduct independent on-road FSD evaluations rather than relying solely on foreign paperwork.
5. **EU-wide FSD approval vote expected around October, could slip to 2027:** Germany is reportedly holding out for a coordinated Europe-wide regulatory consensus, requiring a technical committee vote expected around October of this year; however, ongoing debates among member states over specific features (e.g., speed offset) could push the final decision into late this year or into 2027, per industry trackers cited in the segment.
6. **Guest: regulatory delay may cost lives, cites Tesla safety data:** The guest argued that regulatory delays in approving FSD in Europe are costing lives because human drivers are involved in accidents where, based on available data, a computer driver is significantly safer — citing a figure of up to nine times better, though the specific data source was not detailed.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Berlin battery capacity raised to 18 GWh, ~$1.4B total investment:** According to Giga Berlin's management report (translated from German), the company plans further investment in battery cell manufacturing capacity, originally targeting up to 8 GWh per year but since raised to 18 GWh per year, with additional investment in the "three-digit million" range bringing total cell manufacturing investment to nearly $1.4 billion.
2. **Giga Berlin production capacity raised to 7,500 vehicles/week by October:** Giga Berlin's production capacity has been increased by 20% twice, reaching a planned 7,500 vehicles per week starting October 2026, building on a previously announced 20% increase from a few months earlier, and accompanied by 3,500 new jobs; the plant currently supplies more than 30 markets with plans to add more.

## Market & Competition
1. **Guest sees a Tesla volume trough turning upward, driven by Model Y/3 and software gains:** The guest suggested Tesla vehicle volumes may have bottomed in the past year or two and are now on an upward trajectory, pointing to rapid software and hardware improvements to the Model Y and Model 3 over the past year as a driver, and argued Giga Berlin was likely underperforming in its first couple of years due to insufficient demand rather than capacity constraints.
2. **Guest: Chinese EV makers gaining share but not expanding factories in Europe:** The guest argued that while Chinese automakers will keep growing sales and gaining market share, they are not building or commissioning new factories in Europe, instead relying on vehicle imports via large shipments, whereas Tesla's added European battery capacity and geographic co-location improve its tariff-related economics.
