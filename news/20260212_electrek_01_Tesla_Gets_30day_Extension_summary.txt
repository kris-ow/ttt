Tesla has received a 30-day USPTO extension, pushing to March 14, 2026, to decide whether to formally oppose a "Cybercab" trademark application held by UNIBEV, a French beverage company, as Tesla nears its planned April 2026 start of Cybercab mass production without legal rights to the name.

## Robotaxi
1. **Extension granted amid naming dispute:** Tesla filed for the extension on February 11, 2026, and the USPTO granted it the same day, per TTAB records, moving Tesla's deadline to oppose UNIBEV's trademark to March 14, 2026.

2. **Origin of the dispute:** Tesla unveiled the Cybercab at its "We, Robot" event on October 10, 2024, without having filed a trademark application. UNIBEV filed for "Cybercab" in the vehicle category on October 28, 2024; Tesla's own application came in November 2024 and was formally suspended as of November 14, 2025 because UNIBEV filed first. UNIBEV also holds trademarks for "TESLAQUILA" and "Cybertaxi."

3. **Experts' theories on the extension:** The article reports trademark experts see three possible explanations: ongoing settlement talks with UNIBEV, a need to gather more evidence before an opposition filing, or a deliberate delay tactic to pressure UNIBEV into a cheaper settlement. A source familiar with the matter previously told Electrek that Tesla-UNIBEV negotiations are underway but unresolved.

4. **Backup names and their problems:** During Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings call on January 29, Musk floated "Cybercar" and "Cybervehicle" as alternatives; Tesla's legal team filed trademark applications for both within 37 seconds of each other after the call. Musk framed this as driven by regulatory concerns over "Taxi"/"Cab" terms in certain states, but the article says experts believe the UNIBEV dispute is the real driver. The article notes "Cybercar" could conflict with UNIBEV's existing mark, "Cybervehicle" is seen as weak branding, and the USPTO already rejected Tesla's "Robotaxi" trademark application in May 2025 as too generic.

5. **Production timeline:** Tesla plans to begin Cybercab mass production at Gigafactory Texas as early as April 2026, meaning the company is approaching production without settled naming rights.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the situation one of the most embarrassing branding failures he's seen, arguing Tesla is weeks from production without a usable name, and predicts the most likely outcome is Tesla paying UNIBEV for the trademark since all alternative names carry worse risks. He also highlights a top reader comment skeptical that Tesla will reach true mass production, suggesting a small hand-built batch may be presented as "ramping up" volume production.
