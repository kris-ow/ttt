This video from brighterwithherbert catalogs a cluster of Tesla and SpaceX catalysts landing in the weeks before the New Year — Robotaxi going 24/7, Starship's first full catch, Cybercab delivery milestones, FSD regulatory progress in Europe and Asia, Optimus demo timing, and a reported Tesla-SpaceX merger timeline — arguing that items under each company's direct control are landing on or ahead of schedule, while items requiring external approval (regulators, shareholders) are moving more slowly but still progressing.

## Robotaxi
1. **24/7 operations begin:** Tesla's Robotaxi account announced Thursday that the service now runs 24 hours a day, seven days a week in all eight cities where it operates (Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) — about seven weeks ahead of Tesla's stated year-end goal. The host notes this is roughly a third more operating hours per car than a month prior (Austin had moved from an 18-hour day to 2 a.m. service before going fully 24/7), which the host argues improves fleet economics since car costs are fixed regardless of hours run.
2. **Speed/weather/highway progress:** Ashok Elluswamy said on Tesla's October 21 earnings call that Robotaxi would soon expand to 2 a.m. then 24/7, add higher-speed roads, highways, and more challenging weather conditions, "hopefully" by year-end. The host says Robotaxi radar data shows cars now driving at 50 mph, indicating progress on the speed dimension, while highway and weather operation remain in planning stages.
3. **Cybercab rollout in Dallas and Las Vegas:** Ashok said at the October conference that Cybercab robotaxi rides in Dallas were targeted for November; by Thursday test runs were spotted in Dallas's northern suburbs, and by Saturday a local observer (Scott's Rule 8) counted over 50 branded Tesla vehicles in Dallas, some reportedly with integrated Starlink equipment. Separately, in Las Vegas, where Tesla received approval in August for up to 5,000 vehicles in Clark County, a local tracker (Sin City Tesla) counted 53 test vehicles as of Monday; a third-party interpretation (Robotaxi Radar) suggests Nevada rules could require commercial service to begin by either November 24 or December 18 depending on permit interpretation — Tesla has not confirmed either date.
4. **Texas fleet scoreboard:** As of Saturday, Tesla had 1,303 registered Robotaxi vehicles in Texas, 18 fewer than Waymo's count, per the host's tracking.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **First customer Cybercab deliveries targeted for December:** Tesla began sending purchase invitations Friday for the Cybercab at $29,990 with expected December delivery, by invitation only and limited to Texas initially — consistent with Lars Moravy's October statement that Tesla aimed to deliver Cybercabs to customers by year-end starting with small volumes. An NHTSA review of the Cybercab was still ongoing as of the October earnings call, which the host flags as a potential risk to December delivery timing even if vehicles are otherwise ready.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD approved in Finland, EU-wide vote possible in December:** Tesla announced FSD (under supervision) is now approved in Finland, the 11th country in Europe. Finland's transport authority (Traficom) said the accelerated approval followed Tesla changes resetting speed-limit-exceeding settings to default at every car start — directly addressing the speeding concern previously raised by Germany's transport ministry, which had confirmed an EU-wide committee vote was scheduled for December. The Dutch regulator had already approved FSD in April and requested EU-wide validity. The host says despite no official agenda and an earlier postponement from October, Finland's approval raises the odds of a December EU vote extending approval from 11 to all 27 countries, rating it "above 50/50" (his own assessment, not a stated fact).
2. **Japan and Argentina FSD timing:** Per a Nikkei report, Japan is reportedly implementing driverless-car regulations starting in December, with Tesla targeting FSD approval there by year-end. Tesla also plans to launch FSD in Argentina on November 20.
3. **China FSD approval still pending:** Musk is reportedly joining Trump on a China trip (per a Walter Bloomberg report citing a US official) for a summit November 18–19 in Shenzhen. The host says Tesla's main ask would be full FSD approval in China, where a limited supervised rollout began in May and full approval — originally targeted for Q3 — has not materialized. The host notes a similar May trip to Beijing did not yield FSD approval and says he would not expect an announcement from this trip either.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus "unscripted" demo video targeted for Nov–Dec window:** At the October 21 conference, Elon said (per a Sawyer Merritt post) Tesla might release a video in "a month or two" showing Optimus performing an action that wasn't pre-programmed, to counter criticism that prior demos were remote-controlled or scripted. Nothing has been published yet; Elon's only subsequent related comment was a brief reference to "Optimus Academy" earlier in November. The host notes Optimus timelines have slipped repeatedly this year and flags Tesla's December 3 annual shareholder meeting as a plausible (but unconfirmed) venue for such a demo.
2. **Production and pricing details:** Production-quality Optimus units are reportedly coming off the Fremont line and being sent to "Optimus Academy" for training rather than factory deployment. The next chip (AI5) is expected in Optimus around April, with deliveries to other companies planned for the end of next year. Per a Nick Cruz post citing Elon at the Baron conference, Optimus will cost $20,000–$30,000 and Tesla plans to lease rather than sell units.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15: first-ever same-flight double catch attempt, partial success:** SpaceX received FAA approval Friday afternoon and launched Flight 15 that evening, attempting to catch both the booster and ship in a single flight for the first time. The Super Heavy booster failed to return to the tower and instead aborted its catch and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico; Elon said Saturday the booster had unfinished engine work and returned with low fuel, but that losing the booster "does not change" SpaceX's schedule for frequent reusable Starship flights. He said the next booster is almost ready to fly and that risking the tower could have cost two to three months of work.
2. **Ship successfully caught after orbit — first in history:** The ship reached orbit, deployed 26 new Starlink V3 satellites, spent over 21 hours in orbit (~14 orbits), then was caught by the tower's mechanical arm on return — the first time a Starship upper stage has been caught after returning from orbit. Google CEO Sundar Pichai publicly congratulated SpaceX on the achievement. Elon and Gwynne Shotwell's stated goal was to return both stages to the tower by the end of 2026; the host notes SpaceX has now caught each type of stage separately (most recently a booster catch in March of the prior year) but not yet both on the same flight.
3. **Reusability tied to launch cadence economics:** SpaceX VP of launch Kiko Dontchev said Falcon 9 requires a new upper stage for each of its roughly 100 annual launches; scaling to thousands of launches per year requires catching and reusing both Starship stages. Elon said Saturday he expects eventually more than 100,000 SpaceX communications satellites (mostly V4/V5), potentially carrying about half of world internet traffic — a goal the host says is infeasible without reliable stage reuse.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **26 new V3 satellites deployed:** Flight 15's ship delivered 26 new Starlink V3 satellites to orbit, adding to the constellation's capacity.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **$100B annualized revenue run-rate targeted for December:** At the November 6 Baron conference, Elon said SpaceX is on track to exceed $100 billion in annualized revenue in December (barring natural disasters) — implying roughly $8+ billion in December revenue alone, versus $14.6 billion for all of Q3 (under $5 billion/month average), a roughly 70% jump from the prior quarterly monthly average. The host says this will be confirmed in Q4 results.
2. **Memphis data center buildout:** Elon told the host (in a reply to a Halloween post) that a data center called "Gun Range" should be operational by end of November and another called "Spam" (named for the building's former Hormel use) in December — both large compute clusters in Memphis. Citing Semi Analysis data published Saturday, one building was reportedly built in about 5 months and Gun Range is planned for completion in four, versus an industry norm of 8–12 months; the Memphis complex overall is said to be growing from 0.4 gigawatts in January to 2.4 gigawatts by year-end, a sixfold increase in one year.
3. **Arizona campus battery order:** SpaceX said its planned Arizona campus will include 24 gigawatt-hours of batteries; per Sawyer Merritt, this implies over $5 billion in Megapack orders from Tesla.
4. **Nvidia leadership transition risk flagged by Musk:** Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday, its first report since Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO Thursday. Elon posted Saturday that Huang was effective at managing Nvidia's supply chain partly through verbal agreements rather than written contracts, and that without Huang some of those deals could be at risk — a concern relevant to SpaceX given its compute infrastructure runs on Nvidia chips. Gene Munster separately warned Nvidia investors to be prepared for the company to withdraw guidance Tuesday.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Bloomberg reports merger talks targeting early December announcement:** Bloomberg's Ed Ludlow reported Thursday that Tesla-SpaceX merger talks appear to be advancing, with a deal announcement possibly coming in early December, followed by board and advisor work on terms and management. Cited hurdles include Tesla's China operations, SpaceX's US defense ties, and required shareholder/regulatory approval on both sides. The host notes this is well ahead of betting-market expectations — Polymarket odds of a December 31 announcement had fallen from 20% (October 30) to 8.5% as of a November 9 post (three days before the Bloomberg report) — and cautions the announcement would only begin a longer approval process, not constitute a completed deal.
2. **Tesla annual meeting December 3:** Tesla's annual shareholder meeting is scheduled for 3:00 p.m. Central time at Giga Texas; shareholders have until December 2 to vote. No merger question is on the ballot, but the host expects Elon to face live questions on any merger news given it is the only day of the year he answers shareholders directly.
3. **SpaceX lockup expiration December 8:** The 180-day post-IPO lockup on SpaceX shares issued before the IPO expires December 8, making additional pre-IPO shares eligible for sale; the host says estimates of the exact share count vary widely. Elon's own shares remain locked up until around June. The Fed also meets December 8–9, with roughly 60% odds (as of around the election period) of a rate move, per the host.
4. **Track record caveat:** The host notes Elon's history includes both hits and misses on stated timelines — Starship launch dates and Optimus milestones have often slipped — while observing that two Tesla-controlled dates (24/7 Robotaxi, Cybercab invites) and the SpaceX ship catch all landed on or ahead of schedule this particular week, contrasting items under direct company control versus those requiring external approval.
