This episode of brighterwithherbert, featuring guest Jeff Lus (a multi-decade supply chain executive and manufacturing consultant), covers Tesla's December 3rd shareholder meeting proxy (notably lacking a merger vote), Elon Musk's pledge to gift SpaceX shares to American children, Musk's comments on where Tesla's long-term value lies, Robotaxi fleet and regulatory updates, a new Tesla patent tied to Starlink, and global EV sales trends.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **No merger item on Tesla's December 3rd proxy:** Tesla's shareholder meeting is set for December 3rd, with only three agenda items: re-election of board members (including Elon Musk, and two others identified by the host as "Robin" and "JB"), executive compensation, and ratification of PricewaterhouseCoopers as auditor. Jeff Lus notes a merger announcement would likely trigger a separate special meeting, so its absence from this proxy doesn't rule it out. He also flags that the SEC filing specifically calls out board overlaps between SpaceX and Tesla and some change-in-control terms, which he says could intensify merger speculation, though he hadn't fully reviewed the details.
2. **Kalshi merger-odds jump:** Per Jeff Lus, prediction-market odds on Kalshi for a Tesla–SpaceX merger occurring or being announced before February 2027 have risen to 67%, up from around 30% a couple of weeks earlier.
3. **Elon pledges SpaceX shares to children:** Musk said that next year he will give one SpaceX share to every child in America, calling it "the first of many," which the host estimates at roughly 72 million children and about $10.5 billion at the current share price. The idea appears to have stemmed from Musk's reaction to an Anthropic proposal to commit a growing share of company equity (starting at 5%, rising toward roughly 20% over time) to a public benefit fund; Musk said he'd rather give shares directly to the public, and confirmed (in response to a commenter) that this would likely run through Trump/Invest America accounts. Jeff Lus believes this would be an in-kind share transfer rather than a stock sale.
4. **SpaceX and Tesla earnings scheduling:** Tesla and SpaceX earnings are now roughly a day apart, with the host noting a livestream planned for SpaceX the day before Tesla's Wednesday earnings call — a shift the host and guest found surprising, as they'd expected a wider gap.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas registered fleet crosses 800:** Tesla registered 34 additional autonomous vehicles with the Texas DMV — all Model Ys — bringing the state registry to 803, up from 769. This is the first increase in Model Y registrations since the count stood at 420 the prior September; per the host, the fleet now totals 454 Model Ys and 349 Cybercabs. Jeff Lus is skeptical this modest addition is meaningful, suggesting it may reflect unit swaps rather than fleet growth, and says he wants to see additions in the hundreds and Dallas service "turned on" (expanded) before reading real momentum.
2. **Midnight service hours confirmed:** Robotaxi service now reportedly runs until midnight in Austin, Dallas, and Tampa (Tampa now operating 6 a.m.–midnight), which the host called out as a change Tesla didn't formally announce; the host says they personally confirmed Dallas's midnight cutoff by calling a ride at 11:10 p.m.
3. **Safety track record cited as a factor in regulatory approval:** Jeff Lus notes Tesla has operated largely error-free with Robotaxi/Cybercab this calendar year (since the program's start late in the prior year), which he argues is critical both for continued fleet expansion and for winning international regulatory approvals.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Slovakia becomes ninth country to approve FSD (supervised):** Slovakia has granted preliminary approval for Tesla's Full Self-Driving (supervised), per Musk. A chart cited by the host shows total population coverage of approved countries at 13.87%, short of a 65% target; adding Germany (if it approves) would bring coverage to 32% of that target.
2. **V15 update anticipated:** The host and Jeff Lus flag an upcoming FSD V15 update, which Tesla has said will "unlock new features and higher levels of safety" compared to V14; it appears portions of V15 are already running in the Robotaxi fleet, with an update expected around Tesla's upcoming earnings call.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Elon on Tesla's long-term value drivers:** Responding to a commenter's suggestion that the new Roadster hints at Tesla–SpaceX synergy, Musk said Optimus and Cybercab will be "the vast majority" of Tesla's value, stating the only things that scale to incredible economic size are super intelligence, robots, solar power, and "massive space" — adding that the Roadster is "super cool" but being built anyway. Jeff Lus interprets this as Musk setting expectations that the companies' revenue mix will look markedly different in the next one to two years, with SpaceX driving super intelligence/space and Tesla driving robots/physical AI, and both sharing solar.
2. **Possible phase-out of manually driven models:** During the Roadster presentation, Musk said the Roadster "will actually be long-term the only manually driven car that Tesla makes," which the host flags as potentially implying Tesla eventually stops selling non-autonomous versions of Model 3, Model Y, and Cybertruck (noting Tesla already dropped Model S and Model X). Jeff Lus favors a different reading — that other models will still be sold but increasingly robot-enabled/autonomous-capable, with a long "gradient" before public adoption of driverless-only vehicles catches up to the technology's readiness.
3. **Global EV sales data and US lag:** Citing chart data, the host notes Norway's battery-electric vehicle share is near 100%, China's battery-electric sales have for the first time surpassed combustion engine sales with electric (battery plus plug-in combined) now over 50% — specifically 58% of new car registrations in China so far this year — while in the US, EV sales have fallen slightly and show no growth, which the host attributes partly to the absence of incentives. Musk responded to criticism that the US is "way behind" by saying "we will catch up." Jeff Lus attributes the US lag to removed incentives combined with tariffs making GM and Ford's EVs (often built outside the US) uneconomical, and predicts Tesla will address US electrification less through personal vehicle sales and more through Cybercab-driven autonomous miles.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Tesla patent links vehicle location to Starlink:** Tesla has filed a patent on locating vehicles using Starlink satellites (not a SpaceX filing), with Musk confirming Starlink enables precise navigation without needing GPS satellites. Jeff Lus frames this as part of continued vertical integration across Musk's companies, noting Cybercabs are being built with Starlink connectivity to supplement existing 4G LTE modems, particularly for fleet management communications in areas with cellular coverage gaps.
