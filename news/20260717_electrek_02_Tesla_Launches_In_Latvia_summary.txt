Tesla launched in two new countries within a day of each other — Latvia and Uruguay — rounding out its Baltic presence and extending its South American footprint.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Latvia launch completes Baltic rollout:** Tesla will open its first Latvian location, a pop-up store at the Spice shopping center in Riga, on August 21, following earlier launches in Lithuania (2024) and Estonia (April 2026). Model 3 already leads Latvia's EV registrations with 1,212 units despite no prior official Tesla presence beyond two Superchargers. Pricing (excluding 21% VAT): Model 3 from €30,990 (~$35,400) to €48,990 for Performance; Model Y from €34,490 (~$39,500) to €53,490. Deliveries for new orders are estimated September–November. Latvia's EV market is small but growing — BEVs held about 7% share through the first eleven months of 2025, up 27% year-over-year, with charging points up 77% to over 2,000.

2. **Uruguay becomes third South American market:** Tesla confirmed its Uruguay launch with an official presentation and vehicles on display; Elon Musk posted "Tesla now in Uruguay." This follows Chile (2024 Supercharger entry) and Colombia (direct sales since November 2025); Tesla named Joaquín Lizarralde country manager for both Argentina and Uruguay in late June. Vehicles ship from Gigafactory Shanghai — Model 3 priced $32,990 (534 km standard) to $49,990 (Performance), with a 750-km Long Range at $37,990; Model Y priced $36,490–$41,490 with 466 km range. Uruguay's new-vehicle market is under 50,000 units/year, but EV adoption rose from roughly 20% of the market in 2025 to 29% by April 2026, and the country generated 99% of its electricity from renewables in 2024 — the highest renewables share of any Tesla market.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues neither market will meaningfully move Tesla's global volume alone, but sees the expansion pattern as important given stalled sales in the US, Europe, and China. He cites Colombia as a model of upside — Model Y became Colombia's best-selling vehicle overall after registrations surged 304% in April — and suggests replicating this in smaller markets could help Tesla reach the 2-million-vehicle annual goal Musk once expected to hit two years ago.
