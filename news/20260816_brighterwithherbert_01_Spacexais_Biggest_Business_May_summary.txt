This video (with guest Larry Goldberg, a venture capital investor) covers three SpaceX AI developments: the release of Grok 4.6 and its cost-efficiency positioning versus rivals, the launch of the consumer AI agent product Grokbot, and Elon Musk's companywide SpaceX presentation revealing a new data center and a claim that AI revenue could soon exceed all other SpaceX revenue. The host and guest also discuss a theory that SpaceX/Tesla and Nvidia are forming a deep, informal "virtual merger" around chip design and AI infrastructure.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok 4.6 released, positioned as frontier-level and best cost-efficiency:** Grok 4.6 launched and, per benchmark charts shown, is now near the top intelligence tier alongside Claude Opus/Fable 5 and GPT-5.6, while being cited as having the highest "intelligence per dollar" of the group. Musk described it as "smart, fast, and amazing bang for the buck." The guest pushed back on one specific comparison (Grok vs. the Chinese open-source "Kimi" model), saying his own hands-on experience found Kimi notably better in quality than the chart implied, though the two were cost-competitive.
2. **Grok 4.7 teased for engineering dominance:** Musk said Grok 4.7 should ship in three to four weeks, with initial training already complete and additional training now being added from SpaceX's proprietary engineering/company data. He said he expects it to "exceed all current models" specifically in real-world engineering tasks, while acknowledging competitors like Anthropic will keep releasing improved models too — this is Musk's own forward-looking claim, not yet demonstrated.
3. **Grokbot launched as a consumer AI agent product:** SpaceX released Grokbot, a teammate-style AI agent (compared to "OpenClaw" and Claude's agent tools) that can log into a user's tools/software and complete tasks autonomously — e.g., managing calendars, negotiating with vendors by voice, running CRM updates, building slide decks during meetings. It runs on separate cloud instances rather than the user's local computer. It is currently in beta, limited to subscribers of "Super Grok Heavy" (cited at $300/month) or certain Cursor subscription tiers; Musk said the beta would widen after fixing early issues. A SpaceX-affiliated commentator noted Grokbot is distinct from the planned "Digital Optimus" project — Musk clarified Digital Optimus involves real-time video/human emulation and sits at a higher level of capability than Grokbot.
4. **Nvidia partnership framed as a "virtual merger":** The guest argued that SpaceX/Tesla and Nvidia, without any corporate merger, are functionally merging at the product level — citing Grok 4.6 reportedly being trained/run on Nvidia GB300 chips (which Nvidia publicly congratulated SpaceX on) and speculating that Nvidia's previously announced roughly $500 billion financing commitment could partly flow toward SpaceX through a shared chip fab arrangement (referred to as "Terafab"). This is presented as the guest's own analysis/theory, not a confirmed deal.
5. **New "Macrohard"-related data center detailed:** In a SpaceX companywide presentation, Musk revealed a new, smaller but denser data center facility (referred to in the discussion as tied to the "Macrohard" buildout) reportedly containing 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips.
