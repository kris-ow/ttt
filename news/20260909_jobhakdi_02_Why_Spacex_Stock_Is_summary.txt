## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Share unlock cited as the cause of today's drop:** The host says SpaceX shares fell today because 319 million previously locked-up shares became tradeable, adding roughly 17% to the float; he estimates that historically about 20–25% of newly unlocked shares get sold quickly for diversification reasons, implying roughly 80 million shares of prospective selling pressure. He notes this pattern echoes an earlier unlock of about 911–912 million shares (a ~143% float increase) that did not depress the stock, which he attributes to the price being below the $135 IPO price at the time.
2. **$135 IPO price as a psychological ceiling:** The host says that, per his conversations with SpaceX shareholders, holders are reluctant to sell below the $135 IPO price but become willing sellers once the stock trades above it — which he says is exactly what happened today, creating a selling ceiling.
3. **Trading strategy — selling short puts, avoiding naked short calls:** He describes having sold short puts (citing strike prices around $340–341 and, earlier, around $140) to profit from the sideways/up price action he expected around unlock dates, while explicitly avoiding naked short calls because a surprise positive announcement (e.g., a new compute deal) could spike the stock and cause heavy losses. He says he exited these SpaceX positions before today's drop and made "much more" profit than expected over the past few weeks, and plans to reassess getting back in by Friday if the stock stabilizes and stops declining, with an "ideal" target near $140 that he calls speculative.
4. **Estimate of remaining selling pressure:** Based on today's elevated trading volume (above the roughly 100 million average) he estimates 40–60 million of the ~80 million shares expected to be sold have already been sold, suggesting the unlock-driven selling could be largely worked through within another one to two trading days.
