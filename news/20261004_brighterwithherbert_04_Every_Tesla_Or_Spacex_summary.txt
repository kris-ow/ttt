This video features a conversation between the host and guest Larry Goldberg (a venture capitalist) about the prospects, timing, and mechanics of a rumored Tesla-SpaceX merger, prompted by comments from Cathie Wood (ARK Invest) on a podcast. The discussion covers China exposure as a potential obstacle, comparative valuation of Tesla vs. SpaceX, SpaceX's rapidly growing compute and launch business, and Elon Musk's new Pentagon advisory role and its potential fallout for Tesla.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Cathie Wood predicts a Tesla-SpaceX merger this year, citing China as the key obstacle:** The host relays that Cathie Wood, speaking on a podcast, believes a Tesla-SpaceX merger could be announced this year and sees China exposure — Tesla's Shanghai factory producing over half its cars alongside SpaceX's defense-contractor status — as the main complicating factor requiring Musk to navigate both Trump and Xi relationships. Goldberg disagrees that China poses a serious obstacle, citing Boeing's precedent of maintaining both Chinese commercial ties and major US defense contracts simultaneously, and says he sees no insurmountable difficulty combining Tesla's China manufacturing with SpaceX's US defense work.
2. **A Wall Street Journal report claimed Tesla may need to spin off its China business; Musk denied it:** The host notes a Journal report suggesting a merger could force Tesla to spin off China operations, which Musk called "absurdly fake news," while ARK reportedly characterizes China as a minor issue.
3. **Goldberg expects a "merger of equals" via an independent valuation committee, not an imminent announcement:** Goldberg argues the merger, if it happens, would be structured as a merger of equal companies requiring independent third-party valuation committees (sometimes two, plus an arbitrator) rather than a board-decided premium buyout. He expects that at most, an upcoming Tesla shareholder meeting (required within 90 days' notice, by year-end) might see a "study the issue" announcement or shareholder question, with Musk likely giving an ambiguous answer, and the actual merger mechanism/negotiations playing out over the following year — not a near-term specific exchange ratio or premium.
4. **Goldberg holds roughly equal stakes in both companies, leaning slightly toward SpaceX:** He cites Tesla's market cap in the $1.2–1.3 trillion range versus SpaceX just over $2 trillion, and says he sees "100% upside potential" in both stocks but has reduced new SpaceX purchases as its price (bought below $160) approaches his target range, while seeing more buying opportunity in Tesla at current levels. He frames the situation as a "merger of equal companies" where current market-set valuations are fair given the state of affairs, rather than one company being clearly cheaper.
5. **Elon Musk appointed co-director of Pentagon's "Project Meridian" alongside Palmer Luckey and Newt Gingrich:** Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth appointed Musk, Palmer Luckey, and Newt Gingrich as co-leaders of a 120-day Pentagon initiative studying the future of warfare and related technologies (drones, AI, swarms). Goldberg warns this carries reputational risk mainly for Tesla (a consumer-facing company), recalling backlash during Musk's earlier government/DOGE involvement, though he believes SpaceX/Starlink is less exposed since it has no real competitor. Goldberg says he is uncertain whether this government return will affect the merger timeline or Tesla brand sentiment, and flags uncertainty about how China might react to Musk's expanded military role.
