This "electrified" channel video reacts to a Tesla Roadster demonstration event, moving past the stunt spectacle to walk through the car's trim lineup, engineering details, and pricing structure. The host argues that understanding the four distinct trims (base, performance, thrust, rocket) resolves most criticism about "unrealistic" or "illegal" capabilities, since only the top trims carry the SpaceX-derived thruster hardware and those are explicitly off-road/track-only.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Four trims planned, production not until 2028:** Tesla plans a base, performance, thrust, and rocket trim for the new Roadster; the host notes the reservation contract states no production is expected before 2028 and that details (especially the base trim) remain unfinalized and subject to change. Tesla is currently using four or five prototypes for development, with a more production-ready version expected in early 2027 per the host's read of the timeline (the transcript says "sometime early next year" relative to the event).
2. **Shared powertrain and battery architecture across trims:** All trims are said to be rear-wheel drive with two motors producing over one megawatt of power — more torque/power than the three-motor Model S Plaid. The car uses a new aluminum-can version of the 4680 cell (lithium nickel-based chemistry) for better heat dissipation, targeting over 300 miles of range, an 800-volt architecture, and 10–85% charging in about 10 minutes; Tesla's Lars Moravy said it can pull over 500 kW at V4 Superchargers.
3. **Lightweight, aerodynamically active body:** Prototypes use a largely carbon-fiber body with adjustable panels for airflow/downforce, including pop-down headlights, an active hood vent, a chin spoiler with internal airflow redirection, and "barge board" side panels that also help cool carbon-ceramic brakes. Tesla is targeting at least one Roadster variant under 1,700 kg (~3,747 lbs), potentially lighter than some Model 3 variants; final numbers are still being determined.
4. **Steer-by-wire, brake-by-wire cabin with a yoke:** The car will use brake-by-wire and steer-by-wire systems with a yoke instead of a steering wheel, reclining seat backs with fixed seat bottoms, forward/back-adjustable pedals, heated low-mounted seats, and tires sized 295/30 front (20-inch wheels) and 335/30 rear (21-inch wheels).
5. **Performance trim — suction/ground-effect system and pricing:** The performance trim (two seats, road-ready) requires a $5,000 deposit plus $45,000 due within 10 days; the host estimates a final price around $300,000. It features a 0–60 time under 1.6 seconds, an active aero package with a deployable rear wing (using Optimus actuators, with road/high-downforce/low-drag modes), and four ~20kW electric fans (based on Tesla's heat pump compressor motor family) creating underbody suction with deployable/retractable skirts for low- and high-speed downforce, enabling cornering over 2g under 50 mph. Tesla claims this gives the best passive downforce of any road car, and Elon Musk stated the Roadster would beat Formula 1 track times even without thrusters, since the suction and active aero are F1-banned technologies.
6. **Thrust trim — cold-gas thrusters and pricing:** The thrust trim adds acceleration via cold gas thrusters in a limited production run, priced at $5,000 due immediately plus $245,000 within 10 days — the host estimates a total cost potentially over $1 million. It uses a 200-liter carbon overwrap pressure vessel (COPV) holding up to 10,000 PSI, currently taking 6–7 hours to refill (Tesla is working to cut this to 30 minutes), providing roughly 5 seconds of usable thrust (one 0–60 launch) per charge; 0–60 time is under 0.9 seconds. The host raises doubts about whether thrust-package cold-gas use would be street-legal due to force and debris kickup.
7. **Rocket trim — invite-only, highest performance:** The rocket trim is invite-only (the host dismisses a circulating $500,000 deposit screenshot as likely AI-generated/unverified) and is confirmed usable off-road only, not on public streets. It drops to a one-seat configuration to fit an additional 400L COPV (600L total) powering six additional cold-gas thrusters (one per corner plus two front for yaw control), using nitrogen gas. Stated 0–60 time is under 0.59 seconds; Tesla posted a current top speed over 230 mph, while Lars Moravy mentioned a new internal goal of over 330 mph. The host speculates pricing could exceed $2 million, versus a possible ~$150,000 base and ~$300,000 performance trim.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **SpaceX-derived thruster technology:** The cold gas thruster (CGT) system powering the thrust and rocket trims uses the same composite overwrapped pressure vessel (COPV) technology SpaceX uses, reflecting direct technology transfer referenced throughout as "the SpaceX package."
