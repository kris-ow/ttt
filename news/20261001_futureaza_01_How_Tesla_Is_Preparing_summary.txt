This futureaza video, hosted by Brian with guest Herbert Ong, debates whether the "time has come" for Cybercab, examining Texas registration/production data, Tesla's state-by-state robotaxi expansion plans, and when robotaxi revenue might become financially material. The core thesis: Tesla is deliberately slow-rolling deployment now (building fleet, collecting data, resolving FSD v15 and European regulatory issues) in order to enable a rapid, S-curve-style scale-up later.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas registration and fleet data:** Herbert Ong cites 126 Cybercabs registered at the Texas DMV (with a weekend registration spike) alongside 420 Model Ys understood to be in the supervised robotaxi fleet; he notes registered vehicles aren't necessarily all active on roads, but third-party tracking (he cites a tracker he calls Ethan McKinnon) reportedly shows hundreds operating, not just the "eight" some skeptics claim. Herbert projects Tesla will aim for roughly 80–90% of its ride-hailing fleet to be Cybercab versus Model Y, since most trips carry one or two passengers, and expects the current ~126 Cybercabs in Austin to grow into the hundreds over the next few months.
2. **Total fleet and production rate (unconfirmed):** Herbert says Tesla is believed to have "a few thousand" Cybercabs already built, with roughly a thousand currently used for supervised FSD testing across various regions; he cites an unverified last report of Cybercab production around 300 per week, explicitly flagging this as unconfirmed ("we don't know how true this is, but let's assume it is").
3. **State-by-state driverless expansion timeline:** Herbert relays that Elon Musk, in a recent interview with Chinese state media, said driverless robotaxis would come "soon" to Nevada and Florida, and that California expansion is targeted for the middle of next year. Herbert views Nevada and Florida as logical next steps given existing regulatory frameworks and (for Florida) an already-small existing robotaxi presence; Nevada has no robotaxis yet but is seen as supportive and has reportedly already approved the framework. He frames the California timeline as "Elon time" and notes Tesla hasn't yet applied for the required state permit, attributing the delay to Tesla's reluctance to accept associated reporting requirements rather than regulatory obstruction.
4. **Rationale for building ahead of deployment:** Herbert argues Tesla is registering and stockpiling vehicles now (seen in parking lots with "hundreds" of cars accumulating) because vehicle mileage life is finite, so starting the usage clock later preserves more useful life; he frames this as Tesla building production/operational infrastructure now so it can scale rapidly once ready, including an expected Houston launch "soon."
5. **Slow rollout attributed to two factors:** Herbert attributes the currently slow pace of city/fleet expansion to (1) FSD version 15 needing fixes before Tesla makes "bigger leaps," and (2) Tesla awaiting a European regulatory decision now expected in December rather than October, forcing a choice between deploying more cars now at some risk of accidents that could cause further delay, or waiting — he believes Tesla's plan is to proceed now regardless.
6. **Timing of material robotaxi revenue:** Herbert does not expect robotaxi to deliver significant revenue by the end of 2027, given repeated delays versus Elon Musk's prior promises, pushing meaningful financial impact beyond 2027; he suggests TSLA shares could still rise well before revenue is material, once the fleet reaches scales like 1,000–5,000 robotaxis across multiple states, as this would validate the scaling model even without near-term profit impact.
7. **City-launch speed as the key metric:** Herbert cites Ashok Elluswamy's statement that the time required to launch a new robotaxi city should approach "almost zero" over time (timeframe unspecified, possibly "in 2 years" per Herbert's own guess), arguing the pace of new city launches — not current car counts or near-term revenue — is the more important signal of scaling, and notes current progress ("meager numbers," Austin-focused) is behind where observers expected (previously expecting around seven cities and 1,000 cars that have not materialized).

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscriber growth and revenue:** Herbert notes autonomy/FSD adoption has grown quarter over quarter, citing roughly 70,000–90,000 new FSD subscribers in a recent quarter (he is unsure of the exact figure) and expects Tesla's upcoming quarterly report to show over 100,000 new subscriptions, which he estimates adds about $10 million per month in near-zero-marginal-cost revenue.

## Market & Competition
1. **Pushback on Tesla Semi criticism:** Herbert responds to a viewer comment claiming Mercedes and Volvo trucks (e.g., the eCascadia) are better and better-charged in Europe, countering that those electric trucks are barely produced/used, and that comparable Volvo/Freightliner (Daimler) trucks available in the US are, in his characterization, far more expensive and less effective than Tesla's offering.
