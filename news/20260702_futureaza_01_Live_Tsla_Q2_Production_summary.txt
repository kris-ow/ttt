This is a live stream hosted by FutureAzA (Brian) and co-host John Twig, recorded on July 2, 2026, awaiting and then reacting to Tesla's Q2 2026 production and delivery results. The video captures the pre-announcement anticipation, the reveal of a significant delivery beat, and a guest appearance by analyst James Stephenson, who came closest among community forecasters to the actual number. The core thesis is that Tesla's vehicle delivery beat is secondary to the broader autonomous driving, energy, and AI story unfolding around the company.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Q2 2026 Deliveries: Major Beat:** Tesla reported Q2 2026 deliveries of 480,126 vehicles on production of 451,758 — Tesla's fourth-best vehicle delivery quarter ever, per Sawyer Merritt, and up 25% year-over-year. The result significantly beat the Wall Street analyst consensus of approximately 406,000, a gap of roughly 20%. Among community forecasters, James Stephenson came closest at 483,000 (off by ~6%), followed by Troy Teslike at 466,000, and Brian at 431,000. Gary Black was at 420,000. Grok produced inconsistent estimates ranging from 410,000 to 450,000 depending on how the question was phrased.

2. **"Other Models" Category — Cybertruck and Model S/X Winding Down:** The "other models" category showed approximately 9,000 units produced and 12,000 delivered, reflecting the wind-down of the Model S/X line and modest Cybertruck volumes. The hosts noted that Cybertruck was not a large seller and that the price could not be reduced fast enough to drive meaningful volume. As Model S/X exits this bucket, Semi and Cybercab are expected to eventually occupy the "other models" category.

3. **Inventory Drawdown:** Tesla delivered roughly 30,000 more vehicles than it produced in Q2, meaningfully clearing inventory. Brian pushed back on prior media framing that characterized a build-up of unsold inventory as problematic, arguing that restocking to a healthy days-of-inventory level is necessary to enable impulse purchases.

4. **Model Y Long (7-Seat) Coming to Giga Texas:** Brian stated he has had inside information for approximately five to six months — from someone working on the commissioning of the production line — that the Model Y Long (7-seat) is being commissioned at Giga Texas. He said this was not speculation or wishful thinking. Lars Moravy's post reportedly teased that July 7 would bring something exciting, accompanied by imagery of a long dog with a Model Y-styled emblem on its collar.

5. **Giga Berlin Ramp to 7,500/Week:** Giga Berlin is ramping production from 5,000 units per week to 7,500 units per week, extended through the rest of 2026. James Stephenson cited this ramp as a key input in his forecast model. At that run rate, the ramp could add approximately 100,000 additional annual units.

6. **FSD Version 14 for Hardware 3:** Brian expressed satisfaction that Tesla is releasing a version of FSD (described as "version 14 light") compatible with Hardware 3 vehicles, calling it significant monetization potential — noting that charging $99/month across millions of legacy hardware 3 cars would be materially measurable revenue.

7. **China Deliveries Up 24% Year-Over-Year in June:** A community member shared in the live chat that China delivery numbers for June came in up 24% year-over-year. The hosts reacted positively to this figure.

## Energy

1. **Q2 Energy Storage Deployments: 13.5 GWh, Slightly Below Projection:** Tesla reported approximately 13.5 gigawatt hours of energy storage deployed in Q2 2026, just below the projected figure of approximately 13.8 GWh. The hosts noted this was near the highest quarter ever. Brian attributed the modest shortfall partly to large projects that cannot be revenue-recognized until they are switched on, meaning deployments can be lumpy quarter-to-quarter.

2. **NAT Power Deal: UK and Italy, Half a Year's Factory Production:** A recently announced Megapack deal with NAT Power covering the UK and Italy was described as representing roughly half a year's worth of production from one of Tesla's Megapack factories. The hosts discussed that a handful of similar deals across major European markets could require Tesla to build two or three additional Megapack factories globally.

3. **New Texas Megapack "Mega Block" Factory Coming:** A new, larger Megapack factory in Texas is described as coming soon, with an estimated annual capacity of approximately 50 GWh, compared to approximately 40 GWh at existing facilities. The hosts speculated this could eventually produce a single-quarter deployment in excess of 50 GWh.

4. **Megapack Cell Chemistry Confirmed LFP:** During a factory tour, Brian confirmed that Megapack uses LFP (lithium iron phosphate) chemistry — Tesla confirmed this directly but declined to identify battery suppliers. LFP was described as preferable for stationary storage due to superior cycle life, adequate energy density, and easier thermal management. Brian noted that most LFP cells are currently sourced from China.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Regulatory Progress in Europe — Germany Stalling:** Germany's official automotive regulatory body formally responded (shared on X approximately one hour before the broadcast) that it will not make an independent decision on FSD approval and is waiting for the EU to decide as a whole — potentially months away. Lithuania and other smaller European countries have already moved forward. Brian and John suggested Germany may be deliberately slow-walking approval to protect domestic automakers, noting that if Tesla were a German company, approval would likely have come already. France and other major markets remain pending.

2. **Volkswagen's Autonomous Driving Struggles:** Volkswagen reportedly switched autonomous driving technology partners (switching to or from Bosch, per the hosts — exact direction unclear) while simultaneously claiming it would have autonomy solved by end of next year. Brian argued that any company new to the autonomous driving challenge that sets a one-year timeline has fundamentally underestimated the scope of the problem.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Testing Without Steering Wheel on Austin Roads:** Tesla posted official footage (day or two before the broadcast) of Cybercab testing on Austin public roads with no steering wheel and no human driver present — described by the hosts as a pivotal moment.

2. **Cybercab Staging Depots Spotted:** Community member JC Christopher reported spotting a group of Cybercabs at a depot in Houston being staged and readied for deployment. Separately, a staging location in Coral Gables, Florida was also reported the prior day.

3. **Cybercab Reporting as Deliveries — Tesla Robotaxi LLC:** James Stephenson noted that Cybercabs deployed to Tesla's robotaxi network will likely be reported as deliveries to Tesla Robotaxi LLC, a wholly owned Tesla subsidiary and separate legal entity that owns the vehicles operated in the robotaxi network. He argued auditors would require recognition of an internal delivery when vehicles are transferred to this unit, and that Tesla cannot simply accumulate produced-but-undelivered Cybercabs indefinitely without reporting them.

4. **Cybercab Efficiency and Design Priorities:** James Stephenson detailed several Cybercab design choices prioritizing efficiency over consumer vehicle norms: the rear track width is narrower than the front (teardrop aerodynamics), front and rear wheels are different heights, and noise/vibration/harshness was deliberately minimized so passengers can work in transit. He stated Cybercabs are more efficient than his own 2018 Model 3 (which achieved a lifetime best of 187 Wh/mile). Relative to Waymo, the Cybercab was described as costing approximately one-third of Waymo's per-unit cost — with the Waymo sensor suite alone costing more than an entire Cybercab and containing as many components as the Cybercab itself.

5. **Wireless Charging Infrastructure Being Installed:** The hosts mentioned reports of Tesla installing wireless charging pads at locations where Cybercabs are being operated, which they said would reveal where Tesla is setting up Cybercab service hubs.

## Financials

1. **Stock Reaction Muted Despite Large Beat:** Despite the ~20% delivery beat versus Wall Street consensus, Tesla's share price in pre-market trading effectively returned to roughly where it started before the number was released (~$430), and subsequently dipped to around $420 — below the pre-announcement price. The hosts attributed this to the stock having already rallied more than 4.20% two days prior and again the day before, suggesting the market had partially priced in stronger numbers.
