Digital insurer Lemonade launched a pay-per-mile "Autonomous Car Insurance" product for Tesla owners that cuts rates by roughly 50% for miles driven with FSD engaged, built on a technical collaboration giving Lemonade direct access to Tesla vehicle telemetry data.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **How Lemonade's discount works:** The discount applies only to miles driven with FSD actually engaged, distinguishing FSD miles from human-driven miles via a direct connection to Tesla's onboard computer. Co-founder Shai Wininger said Lemonade sees driving data "in extremely high resolution, where we see every minute, every second that you drive your car." Pricing also factors in the specific FSD software version and the vehicle's sensor precision, and Lemonade committed that "the safer FSD software becomes, the more our prices will drop," with an eventual goal of insuring FSD vehicles for "almost free."

2. **Lemonade's discount is far larger than Tesla's own:** Tesla Insurance offers a maximum 10% discount (applied only to certain coverages, not the total premium) for drivers who use FSD at least 50% of the time, versus Lemonade's roughly 50% discount on the per-mile rate — described in the article as roughly five times larger.

3. **Tesla's Safety Score system:** Tesla Insurance separately prices premiums using a Safety Score based on hard braking, aggressive turning, unsafe following distance, and late-night driving, measured as a 30-day rolling average weighted by miles driven. Tesla states "average" drivers should save 20–40% versus competitors and the safest drivers 30–60%. The article notes an irony: because the Safety Score is weighted by miles driven with Autopilot/FSD disengaged, heavy FSD users may see less benefit from it.

4. **Safety data remains disputed:** Wininger acknowledged FSD is "not fully autonomous yet" and said the 50% discount reflects the improvement in the driver's performance while using the technology, not autonomous driving on its own. The article notes Tesla's own FSD safety report (released late last year) claiming fewer crashes has not been peer-reviewed and has been called potentially misleading by unnamed autonomous-vehicle experts; NHTSA has investigated multiple Tesla FSD-related crashes and is examining claims of traffic violations by FSD-using vehicles.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert calls Lemonade's move a genuinely interesting industry development and finds it telling that Lemonade's discount exceeds Tesla's own, questioning why Tesla wouldn't offer a bigger discount if it truly believes FSD is "9-10 times safer than human drivers." He reiterates that it is inaccurate to say "FSD is safer than human drivers" — the more accurate framing is that FSD under human supervision is safer than human driving alone.
