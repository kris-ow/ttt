This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel covers the anticipated SpaceX IPO scheduled for Friday, June 12th (2026), framing it as potentially the largest and most historic IPO ever. Host Herbert is joined by guest Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and venture capital investor, to discuss four key reasons the SpaceX IPO could be historic: its unprecedented scale, extraordinary investor demand, capital deployment into growth rather than survival, and expanding business engines including Starlink Mobile and Terafab.

## Market & Competition
1. **Largest IPO in History by Scale:** SpaceX officially launched its roadshow targeting approximately $75 billion in proceeds at a fixed price of $135 per share, implying a market capitalization of approximately $1.75 trillion. The offering consists of 555 million Class A shares. Underwriters also have the option to purchase additional shares, which would bring the total raised to $85.7 billion if exercised. For context, some earlier expectations had placed the valuation closer to $2 trillion. Prediction market Polymarket is reportedly projecting a closing valuation of $2.3 trillion on IPO day.

2. **Unprecedented Retail Investor Access:** Unlike typical large IPOs, SpaceX publicly announced that retail investors will be able to participate at the same $135 per share price as institutional investors. The ticker symbol will be SPCX. The host noted that SpaceX broke convention by setting a fixed price rather than offering a range, reflecting the company's leverage over the process. The IPO is also unusual in being scheduled for a Friday, which Larry Goldberg noted is rare — perhaps only 1 in 10 to 1 in 20 IPOs historically have launched on a Friday — due to risks around end-of-day price tapering heading into a weekend.

3. **Extraordinary Institutional Demand and Unprecedented ETF Interest:** Major banks including Goldman Sachs (lead underwriter), Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citigroup, JP Morgan, and Barclays are all participating. JP Morgan CEO Jamie Dimond is personally involved, hosting a live Q&A with SpaceX President Gwyn Shotwell and CFO Brett Johnson on the Thursday before the IPO. This is notable given that JP Morgan and Tesla (and by extension Elon Musk) had a long legal dispute — JP Morgan sued Tesla in 2021 over a stock options dispute, Tesla countersued, and the two sides only dropped their lawsuits in November 2024. Dimond later publicly joked that he and Musk had "hugged and made up." Additionally, an unprecedented number of ETFs have already announced SpaceX inclusion prior to the IPO.

4. **Record-Low Underwriting Fees Reflect SpaceX's Leverage:** SpaceX is reportedly negotiating to pay less than 0.75% on its approximately $75 billion IPO, far below the typical IPO underwriting fee range of 4% to 7%. Banks could still collect roughly $500 million in fees collectively, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley reportedly taking the largest shares. Goldberg noted that while unusually low, sub-1% fees are not entirely without precedent — GM achieved a similarly low rate on a $15 billion raise in 2010.

5. **Capital Deployed Into Infrastructure, Not Survival:** The filing shows IPO proceeds will be used to expand AI compute infrastructure, launch capacity, satellite constellations, and future growth initiatives — signaling that SpaceX is scaling existing businesses rather than searching for product-market fit.

6. **Starlink Mobile as a Proven Revenue Engine:** SpaceX published data showing its Starlink Mobile constellation consists of 650 satellites in low Earth orbit across six continents, accessible to more than 1.7 billion people. Australian telecom Telstra (described as a major early partner) reported over 200,000 of its customers connect to Starlink Mobile per day, with over 2.7 million customers having connected at least once in the past year. Next-generation V2 satellites are scheduled to begin launching in 2027, promising 5G speeds from space, 100 times the data density of current V1 satellites, up to 150 Mbps speeds, thousands of spatial beams, and 20 times the throughput capability compared to the first generation. Goldberg noted a notable connection: Tesla's chairperson Robyn Denholm was previously the CFO of Telstra, which he suggested explains the close relationship.

7. **Terafab: A $55 Billion Chip Manufacturing Project Linking SpaceX, xAI, and Tesla:** Grimes County, Texas commissioners voted 4-to-1 to approve major tax incentives for SpaceX's Terafab chip manufacturing project, located east of College Station. The tax abatement grants SpaceX a full exemption on property taxes tied to the project's machinery and equipment. Elon Musk noted that Terafab will increase annual tax revenue for Grimes County by 25% even with the abatement — and significantly more when employee and contractor taxes are included. The project is described as a "generational change" by county officials. Musk explained the rationale: property taxes on expensive chip-manufacturing equipment would create a serious competitive disadvantage globally. Larry Goldberg added geopolitical context, noting the US-China competition in AI and semiconductor manufacturing, and flagging TSMC's Taiwan operations as a systemic vulnerability if China were to act against Taiwan.

## Financials
1. **Employee Wealth Creation:** The host outlined projections for SpaceX employee wealth outcomes at the $1.75 trillion valuation. An estimated 4,000 employees are expected to become millionaires, and approximately 400 are expected to become "100 millionaires" (i.e., worth over $100 million). Employees who joined before SpaceX's first successful launch in 2008 and held their equity — estimated at 150 to 250 people still holding — could see returns of $100 million each. Gwyn Shotwell and CFO Brett Johnson are each reported to have cleared over $1 billion. Elon Musk also reportedly carved out 5% of the offering for friends, family, and executives, with no lock-up period. Elon Musk himself is expected to become a trillionaire as a result of the IPO, though the host and Goldberg emphasized these are stock options, not cash.

2. **Elon Musk's Economic Contribution Framed as Historic:** Goldberg stated that Musk has generated an estimated 0.4% of US GDP — a figure Goldberg described as thousands of times more than the average individual's contribution — and argued Musk has made the single biggest economic contribution of any living person in the United States. Both host and guest expressed the view that Musk's wealth is deserved given the personal sacrifices and near-bankruptcy experiences in SpaceX's early years, when the first three rocket launches failed.
