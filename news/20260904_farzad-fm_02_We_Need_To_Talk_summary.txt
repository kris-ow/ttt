The host, a Tesla owner in Austin, documents a night ride in Tesla's newly launched driverless Cybercab following an invite-only launch event, describing the vehicle's interior features and ride quality while offering his read on Tesla's fleet-scaling strategy and rollout pace.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab publicly launches in Austin:** The host reports that starting the evening after the launch event, any resident can hail a Cybercab (no steering wheel or pedals) across the full Austin geofence via the robotaxi app, expanding beyond the Model Y-only service; he calls the launch event itself "underwhelming" with little new detail shared beyond the go-live announcement.
2. **Tesla appears to be building a third-party fleet-management model:** The host notes Tesla posted a signup page (dated 2026-09-03) inviting people to become fleet owners or providers handling cleaning/repairs for Cybercabs. He infers (his own speculation, not confirmed by Tesla) that Tesla intends to manufacture Cybercabs at scale — citing the company's stated goal of producing millions per year — while offloading maintenance, cleaning, and ownership costs to third parties, keeping the rides exclusively on Tesla's own robotaxi app rather than opening it to platforms like Uber.
3. **Full geofence launch signals a mature FSD stack:** Unlike the initial Model Y robotaxi rollout, which started in a small downtown Austin area, Cybercab launched directly across the entire Austin geofence. The host takes this as a sign Tesla successfully transferred its FSD/robotaxi software stack to the new vehicle and speculates Cybercab's rollout could scale faster than Model Y's did.
4. **Expected cautious, safety-first rollout pace:** The host expects Tesla to expand the Cybercab fleet gradually rather than rapidly, prioritizing safety and avoiding negative press over speed; he also expects Tesla to employ demand-based pricing, raising rates during high demand and adding vehicles to the fleet over time, with Tesla describing pricing as "competitive."
5. **Ride experience and vehicle impressions:** The host describes Cybercab as feeling similar to riding in a personal Tesla on FSD, with a smooth, plush ride (less floaty than Cybertruck), a large trunk, bench seating, in-cabin ambient lighting customizable via the app, Netflix/YouTube access, and voice interaction with Grok; he says the car behaved more cautiously than his own Model Y on FSD, including a safe right-turn-on-red.
