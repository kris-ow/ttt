The SEC approved Tesla's request to let retail shareholders opt into a standing voting instruction that automatically backs the board's recommendation at every future meeting until canceled, a move Electrek frames as suspicious given Tesla's unscheduled 2026 annual meeting and Musk's ongoing hints at a SpaceX-Tesla merger.

## Financials / Corporate Governance
1. **SEC approves "Issuer Voluntary Retail Voting Program":** Tesla general counsel Brandon Ehrhart and Sullivan & Cromwell submitted a no-action request dated September 29, which the SEC approved the same day, following what a footnote called "months-long" prior discussions. Retail investors can opt in to have their shares automatically vote per the board's recommendation on every matter, or on every matter except contested director elections and M&A/divestiture votes; there is no option to vote against the board or follow another policy. Votes are cast as soon as Tesla files its definitive proxy, before shareholders receive the proxy statement itself; participants can override or opt out for free and get an annual reminder.
2. **Not unique to Tesla:** ExxonMobil received similar SEC relief in September 2025, with more than 100,000 Exxon shareholders enrolled by March per Tesla's letter; Goldman Sachs received its own approval the day before Tesla's. Tesla wrote its request as a "framework" applicable to any company running the same type of program. Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev publicly praised the approval, saying Robinhood worked with Tesla on it and describing broker-run "Hubs" for enrolling across multiple companies' programs.
3. **Retail voting patterns cited by Tesla:** Tesla's letter cites Broadridge data showing retail investors voted only 28% of their shares in the 2025 proxy season versus 76.6% for institutions, and says it spent over $2 million on proxy solicitors across its last two annual meetings. The article notes retail votes were decisive last year when proxy advisers ISS and Glass Lewis recommended against Musk's pay package, which passed anyway on retail support; conversely, a shareholder proposal for Tesla to invest in xAI failed (1.06B for, 916M against, 473M+ abstaining, with abstentions counted as "against") despite no board recommendation against it — Tesla invested $2 billion in xAI months later anyway, and that stake later converted to SpaceX stock after SpaceX took over xAI.
4. **Annual meeting delay:** Tesla's last annual meeting was November 6, 2025; an amended 10-K filed April 30 stated the board had not yet set a 2026 meeting date, and as of the article's publication no proxy has appeared on EDGAR. The 2025 meeting was similarly delayed to November after shareholder pressure. Under Texas law (Tesla's incorporation state since 2024), a shareholder can petition a court to order a meeting after 13 months without one — a threshold Tesla would cross in early December, per the article.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation and pay package mechanics:** Musk has hinted at a Tesla-SpaceX merger for months, telling Tesla's Q2 earnings call in July that the companies have "more and more overlap" but that combination talk isn't appropriate on an earnings call; the WSJ reportedly reported Tesla was weighing selling its China business to facilitate a deal. SpaceX, public since June, is described as worth about $2 trillion; Tesla was worth about $1.47 trillion "last week" per the article. Musk owns about 20% of Tesla but controls over 80% of SpaceX's voting power. The article explains that under Musk's 2025 CEO Performance Award, a change-in-control (e.g., a SpaceX acquisition of Tesla) would drop operational milestones (like robotaxi or Optimus targets) and vest tranches based solely on market cap — meaning a deal valuing Tesla at $2 trillion (~$506/share given ~3.95 billion shares outstanding, a roughly 36% premium to last week's price, per Electrek's earlier reporting) could trigger Musk's first tranche without any operational goals being met.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the retail-voting problem Tesla cites is real, but calls the fix one-sided since it only lets shareholders pre-commit to backing the board, not against it or toward an independent policy. He contends Tesla's board is not meaningfully independent, citing its push of the pay package over proxy advisers' objections and its xAI investment after shareholders rejected it, and states the confluence of the new voting program, the delayed annual meeting, and merger speculation raises questions Tesla should answer before any SpaceX deal vote — while acknowledging he "can't say for sure" these elements are connected.
