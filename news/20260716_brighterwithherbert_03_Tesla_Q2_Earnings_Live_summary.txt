This is a pre-show, live earnings-call watch, and post-show analysis of Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings, featuring a panel of retail analysts (James/Invest Answers, Alexandra Merz, Nick Gibbs, Jeff Lutz, Cern Basher) reacting to weak EPS/margins offset by strong revenue, record FSD attach rates, and cautious but detailed robotaxi commentary from Ashok Elluswamy, alongside recurring speculation about a Tesla-SpaceX merger.

## Financials
1. **Q2 results: revenue beat, EPS and margins missed.** Tesla posted trailing-12-month revenue over $100 billion for the first time, with Q2 revenue around $28 billion (up ~26% YoY), but GAAP diluted EPS came in around 32 cents, below expectations, driven by weak automotive gross margins. Free cash flow was negative (roughly -$1 billion per CFO Vaibhav Taneja, though panelists initially debated the exact figure), driven by capex more than doubling sequentially to about $5.7–5.8 billion.
2. **Automotive and energy margins compressed.** Automotive gross margin excluding credits fell from 19.2% to 16.3% quarter-over-quarter; management attributed most of the sequential decline to one-time Q1 benefits (warranty true-ups and tariff relief, ~$230 million) not repeating, calling the underlying margin roughly flat controlling for that. Energy gross margin dropped sharply from 39.5% to 20.4%, due to a ~$240 million warranty true-up on legacy vendor cell issues, non-repeating tariff benefits, and declining industrial storage ASPs; management said energy margins should normalize in the "mid to low 20%" range long-term.
3. **SpaceX equity stake boosted net income via mark-to-market gain.** Tesla recorded roughly a $1 billion mark-to-market gain on its SpaceX equity investment, which panelists calculated was the primary factor keeping EPS from being far lower (one panelist estimated EPS would have been about $0.10 instead of $0.32 without it); this was partly offset by FX losses (~$300 million) and Bitcoin losses (~$100 million).
4. **Capex guidance reaffirmed at "more than $25 billion" for the year, with a new $30 billion debt facility.** CFO Taneja said capex will keep growing for the next two to three years across robotaxi fleet expansion, Optimus capacity, a semiconductor fab, solar manufacturing, and AI compute; Tesla has also secured debt facilities allowing borrowing of up to $30 billion to help fund this. Panelists (notably Alexandra Merz) had pre-call speculated capex could reach $35–40 billion on a rolling 12-month basis, and debated whether the $25 billion guide was calendar-year or trailing.
5. **Regulatory credit revenue fell sharply.** Regulatory credits came in around $146 million, down from roughly $380 million in the prior quarter, reflecting the broader industry pullback in EV subsidy-driven credit revenue.
6. **Services and other business hit record margins.** This segment posted quarterly revenue over $4.5 billion with record gross margin of 14.1% (up from 9.2%), driven by supercharging, used cars, insurance, and service centers, plus early infrastructure investment for robotaxi.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscription attach rate hit a new record.** Over 55% of new North American deliveries included an FSD subscription at time of delivery, per CFO Taneja; total paid FSD customers reached nearly 1.5 million (up 56% YoY), split roughly 55% upfront purchases and 45% subscriptions, with the purchase option now removed in most markets (recently including the UK).
2. **Ashok Elluswamy detailed robotaxi safety and scaling metrics.** Elluswamy stated Tesla has driven more than 380,000 miles of unsupervised robotaxi operation across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents attributable to the vehicle. He said unsupervised miles driven per week have grown at a double-digit compounding rate since the start of the year, that this is expected to continue for the rest of the year, and that early builds of a "V15" FSD software (with roughly 40% of seven planned major improvement tracks merged) are already running in the robotaxi fleet.
3. **Panel skepticism on the "10% per week" growth claim.** Analysts debated how to interpret Elon Musk's and Ashok's statement of "more than 10% weekly" growth in unsupervised miles; Jeff Lutz calculated this implies roughly doubling every seven weeks (~142x annualized), while Nick Gibbs was skeptical of taking the precise figure at face value given Tesla's history of unmet guidance.
4. **Cybercab requires its own driving-data accumulation.** Elon Musk and Ashok explained that because Cybercab is a new chassis, Tesla must independently accumulate calibration driving data using retrofitted test vehicles (steering wheels/pedals) before scaling deployment, distinct from the data Tesla already has from Model 3/Y fleets.
5. **NHTSA relationship described as constructive.** Lars Moravy said Tesla has a good relationship with NHTSA leadership on federal autonomous vehicle rules, though state-level regulation (e.g., New Jersey) was called "disheartening"; the company said its approach is to let vehicle performance drive regulatory and public acceptance.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab and robotaxi fleet expansion into new markets.** Tesla confirmed robotaxi service has expanded to seven US markets, with FSD/robotaxi rollout also extending to two new Florida cities (Tampa, Orlando) mentioned by panelists prior to the call. Engineering test drives of production Cybercabs on public roads began in Q2, and Tesla began offering employee rides in Cybercabs at Giga Texas this month — both described by the company as precursors to robotaxi fleet deployment.
2. **Active Robotaxi fleet: 773 (as of 2026-07-22) per RobotaxiTracker.com** (Bay Area 634, Austin 105, Dallas 18, Houston 16); unsupervised fleet last reported at 39 (as of 2026-05-09, stale).
3. **Management defends multi-city rollout strategy over concentrating in one city.** Elluswamy, Vaibhav Taneja, and Lars Moravy each said expanding across multiple cities (rather than scaling Austin alone) is intentional — to prove the software stack generalizes with minimal per-city tuning, to work through operational/regulatory kinks in a controlled way, and because transportation regulation varies city-by-city and state-by-state in the absence of a federal framework.
4. **Vertical integration confirmed; reliability ("march of nines") cited as the binding constraint.** In response to an analyst question about third-party ride-share partnerships, Musk said Tesla expects to remain fully vertically integrated with robotaxi and that demand will outstrip supply; he said the main constraint on scaling is achieving sufficiently high reliability ("nines" of reliability), not demand.
5. **Panel critique: vague quantitative guidance despite detailed qualitative color.** Multiple panelists (Jeff Lutz, James, Nick Gibbs) felt Tesla gave more operational detail than prior quarters (via Ashok) but still failed to give clear numeric guidance—e.g., no vehicle count targets, no explicit timeline for public Cybercab availability, and no benchmarked "nines" reliability target—leaving significant ambiguity about pace of scaling.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production ramp confirmed, "flat and long" initial S-curve expected.** Musk said Optimus construction at the Fremont factory (former Model S/X line) is underway and stunning to see, but warned the early phase of the manufacturing ramp will be slow due to the complete lack of existing supply chain for the robot's parts, requiring heavy in-house/insourced production.
2. **Optimus 4 (Austin) targeted for much higher volume and vertical integration.** Musk said Optimus 4, to be built in Austin, will be far more vertically integrated (in-house PCB work) than Optimus 3, with an aspirational target of 10 million units/year for Optimus 4 versus roughly 1 million/year for Optimus 3 — with heavy caveats about the difficulty of scaling.
3. **Data collection strategy leverages factory workers and "Optimus Academy."** Ashok Elluswamy described training Optimus using data from Tesla factory workers performing tasks, a dedicated data-collection team, broad internet data, and a planned "Optimus Academy" reinforcement-learning flywheel where bots learn from their own successes/failures, using the same end-to-end AI architecture as FSD (now on V12–V15).
4. **Micron committed significant memory allocation for Optimus.** Musk thanked Micron for allocating substantial memory supply at favorable pricing, which analyst Cern Basher noted is significant since Optimus reportedly requires roughly 4x the memory of a car.

## Energy
1. **Q2 energy storage deployment grew but margins fell sharply.** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of storage in Q2 (up 53% sequentially, second-largest quarter), but gross margin fell from 39.5% to 20.4% due to warranty costs, non-repeating tariff benefits, and declining ASPs amid competition.
2. **Musk: batteries could roughly double US energy output.** Musk stated the US has about 1.2–1.3 terawatts of power generation capacity against only ~0.5 terawatts average usage, arguing batteries could unlock much higher effective output by smoothing supply, and said Megapack demand — including from SpaceX for AI data center power smoothing during training runs — is expected to be very high going forward.

## Market & Competition
1. **Alphabet/Google raised capex guidance sharply.** Panelists noted Google increased capex guidance from roughly $180 billion to $190–205 billion, cited as evidence hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending is accelerating industry-wide, contextualizing Tesla's own capex increase as comparatively modest relative to peers (Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle).
2. **Panel frames FSD/EV demand as robust despite fading subsidies.** Jeff Lutz noted EV growth continues globally even as the US, China, and EU pull back subsidies, framing this as a sign of organic demand strength as regulatory credit revenue fades.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger question directly addressed on the call — notable shift.** An analyst from Bank of America asked Musk directly whether Tesla-SpaceX synergies could lead to combining the companies; Musk cited growing overlap (especially around Terafab) but said he couldn't discuss combining companies "on [the] call" without proper process, then handed the question to General Counsel Brandon, who said Tesla has "deepened" its SpaceX relationship via an investment and framework agreement, without further detail. Panelist Alexandra Merz called this a significant signal since Tesla had never previously routed a merger question to legal, and maintains her (self-described speculative, low-confidence) theory that a merger announcement could come around SpaceX's August 4 earnings call, though she noted SpaceX's stock decline (to around $115–120, down from a scenario she'd modeled near $200) complicates the economics and timing.
2. **Terafab (AI chip fab) to get its own dedicated announcement.** Musk said Tesla will announce a Terafab location "soon" and is deliberately reserving fuller details for a standalone launch event rather than the earnings call, calling it crucial to avoid being AI-chip-supply-constrained for scaling Optimus production; equipment orders have already been placed for a development fab in Austin combining lithography, mask production, logic, memory, and packaging under one roof.
3. **SpaceX collaboration deepening across Starlink, Grok, and digital Optimus.** Musk described Starlink connectivity being integrated into Cybercab (for both passenger entertainment and to avoid cellular dead zones), Grok powering "Digital Optimus" (computer-use AI) in partnership with SpaceX, and a "mega pod" compute design combining Tesla AI4 chips with x86 processors, deployable at Supercharger sites (Tesla cited roughly seven gigawatts of Supercharger power capacity).
