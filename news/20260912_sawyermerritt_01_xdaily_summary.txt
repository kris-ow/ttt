## Key Posts / Themes

1. **European Tesla Semi unveiled:** Tesla officially unveiled the European version of the Tesla Semi, with a dedicated European website now live. Specs cited: ~550 km (~342 miles) range, no traditional side mirrors (unlike the U.S. version), a headlight design similar to the Model Y Standard, energy consumption of 1.0 kWh/km (1.61 kWh/mile), and fast-charging capability (specific charging figure was cut off in the source text). A separate post compares the U.S. and European Tesla Semi Standard Range variants, though specific comparative figures weren't detailed in the digest.

2. **Morgan Stanley on Semi autonomous trucking economics:** A new Morgan Stanley note estimates Tesla could generate roughly $12,000–$18,000 per month per truck from autonomous trucking software, based on a $0.85–$1.00/mile subscription fee applied to about 18,000 miles driven per month. The note contrasts this with FSD generating roughly $100/month on consumer vehicles today. The note reportedly extends this to a fleet-wide scenario (Tesla "reaching" a stated Semi fleet figure), but the full scaled dollar projection was truncated in the source post, so no fleet-wide revenue total should be treated as confirmed.

3. **Megapack order in Italy:** Tesla secured an approximately $800 million Megapack order from Zhero to supply 3 GWh of battery energy storage capacity in Italy — a sizable Energy segment win.

4. **China Model Y Performance launch:** Tesla officially released the Model Y Performance in China, priced starting at RMB 369,000 (~$55,000 USD), with deliveries beginning in two weeks from the Sept 11 announcement.

5. **Cybercab Japan tour:** The Cybercab is now on public display in Japan as part of a tour running the rest of September, with stops in Tokyo (Omasake Aoyama Garden by GMO, Sept 11–14), Osaka (Tesla Grand Front Osaka, Sept 17–20), and Nagoya (Tesla Nagoya, Sept 22–23) — an international marketing/demo push rather than a deployment or regulatory milestone.

6. **Starlink Mobile competitive threat downplayed by T-Mobile CFO:** In a new interview, T-Mobile's CFO said Starlink Mobile poses no competitive threat "from a wireless perspective," arguing satellite-to-phone signal is too weak by the time it reaches a handset (illustrated with a comment about not being able to get signal through a Tesla windshield). This is a competitor's characterization and should be read as T-Mobile's stated position, not an independent assessment of Starlink Mobile's capability.
