## Brief
- San Antonio becomes robotaxi city #8, ~275 sq mi geofence

## Robotaxi
- Tesla launched driverless Robotaxi service in San Antonio at 5am Sunday, six days after Fort Worth (city #7). Robotaxi Tracker measures the advertised geofence at ~274 square miles — over four times Waymo's ~59-square-mile San Antonio zone — covering ~1.0 million residents, but as of the article no Tesla vehicle had yet been logged operating in the city.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert reports independent testing (Koby Pierce) shows Tesla's advertised geofences in six other cities only actually accept rides across 59%–78% of the mapped area (Austin 59%, Dallas 78%), suggesting San Antonio's real usable area is closer to 160–210 square miles.
- Austin's service zone expanded 62% to roughly 442 square miles, reclaiming its status as Tesla's largest robotaxi zone, per the show; Electrek separately puts it at 443 square miles, up 178 square miles since mid-September.
- Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla's registered Texas robotaxi fleet reached 1,282 vehicles (573 Cybercabs) after adding 38 Model Ys on Nov 8 — just three vehicles behind Waymo's 1,285 in the state; Electrek separately cites Waymo running 4,000+ driverless vehicles nationally versus Tesla's 666.
- A commentator reported possible unannounced overnight testing of unsupervised Cybercabs in Austin outside posted service hours, with highway driving capability reportedly added.
- Saudi Arabia's transport ministry has agreed a Cybercab testing plan for Q3 2027, with data export approval underway and supervised FSD as an on-ramp.
- Robotaxi service hours extended to 21 hours/day (5 AM–2 AM), up from 6 AM–2 AM, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Electric Vehicles
- Musk confirmed Cybertruck production at Giga Texas will wind down around mid-2027 to free capacity for Cybercab, adding an extra shift to clear backlog and returning deposits where unable to fulfill orders; he said this reflects opportunity cost, not unprofitability — a claim Jeff Lutz was skeptical of.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert counters with Tesla's own data: the Cybertruck line has run at roughly a quarter of installed capacity, Q3 "Other Models" deliveries imply a ~30,000-unit annual pace (down 48% YoY), and Cox Automotive estimates show US sales falling from 14,416 (Q3 2024) to 3,744 (Q2 2026); the same day the wind-down was announced, Tesla raised Cybertruck prices up to 10% and paused orders for the cheapest trim.
- Musk separately told X the real constraint is a shortage of "smart, dedicated people" to run multiple vehicle programs simultaneously — a third rationale in four days, per Lambert, who notes 7 of 14 executives shown at Tesla's 2023 Investor Day as "bench strength" have since departed, along with four Cybercab program leaders since February.
- Musk announced coming Cybercab efficiency gains via a smaller 40 kWh pack (down ~16% from today's pack) and a new "liquid silver" color option; Morgan Stanley read the Cybertruck pullback as a bullish signal of confidence in robotaxi's long-term potential.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's FSD safety data now cited as nine to ten times safer than the average driver, up from a prior seven-times figure, per cernbasher's hosts; a social-media extrapolation (not an independent study) claimed full FSD adoption could save ~6% of US GDP, which Musk endorsed as "that's true."
- EU approval progress: 10 of 27 member states (13% of population) have approved Tesla's driving tech; 15 states and 65% population are needed. Italy's transport minister Salvini voiced support for rapid EU approval; Germany reportedly signaled support two weeks earlier. FSDtracker.eu's best case for an EU vote is December–February, more realistic February–April next year.
- Tesla's onboard computer naming has shifted from "AI4/AI5" to "SI3/SI4/SI5" ("superintelligence"), per the hosts.
- Musk reportedly told Chinese media he expects California robotaxi approval possibly by mid-next year; California has approved only Mercedes-Benz, Nuro, and Waymo for driverless operation, and Tesla has not yet applied.

## Energy
- Tesla broke ground on a $10 billion vertically integrated solar manufacturing facility near Houston ("Project Crystal Sun"), with construction finishing ~2028 and commercial operations targeted for Q1 2029.

## Humanoid Bots
- Per Tesla VP Lars Moravy, Optimus robots are planned to charge Cybercabs at service centers and handle broader service-area tasks, potentially starting next year — seen as a highly visible real-world test program for Optimus.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX shares fell sharply (to around $128, per one account) amid a broad AI-stock selloff; hosts framed it as market-wide contagion rather than a SpaceX-specific issue, and a business as usual period for the company.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas nearly doubled his 2027 SpaceX revenue forecast to $175 billion; some forecasters reportedly see $300 billion in revenue possible as early as 2027.
- Ark Invest bought roughly 350,000 SpaceX shares during the drop.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX selected Qualcomm to supply Dragonwing chips for a nationwide 5G Starlink Mobile network, with deployment across tens of thousands of US cell sites beginning in 2027.
- Starlink subscribers have roughly doubled year-over-year to over 13.5 million; Starlink satellites now make up about 66% of all active satellites in orbit (~11,000 of ~16,800) as of October 31.
- The FCC voted 3-0 to advance satellite-to-phone communication rules, opposed by AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile; still at proposal stage.
- Ookla data shows the US road-test no-signal rate fell from 2% to 0.5%; Starlink adoption is expanding among airlines (United targeting 100% fleet coverage by end of next year, Emirates at 65 aircraft).

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX has reportedly stopped selling new commercial Falcon 9 launches after 2028 and is not bidding on three new Space Force contracts, planning to wind Falcon down once Starship flies multiple times per week.
- Gigabay construction near Kennedy Space Center is nearly complete, with one large compartment door remaining.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

- Banks have reportedly been syndicating portions of a previously secured $40 billion Nvidia chip financing package for SpaceX's data centers to institutional investors — described as routine, not distress.
