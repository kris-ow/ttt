Electrek reports that Terafab, the $16.8 billion Tesla-SpaceX chip megafactory in Grimes County, Texas, will be powered by newly built natural gas plants and large battery arrays rather than Tesla solar, despite Tesla being a co-owner of the project and a maker of solar and storage products.

## Energy
1. **Terafab's power plan skips Tesla solar:** SpaceX confirmed Terafab will run on its own natural gas power plants and "very large battery arrays," built on the site of a former coal-fired power plant. The article notes there is no mention of Tesla solar in the power plan despite Tesla co-owning the project and selling solar panels, Powerwalls, and grid-scale batteries.
2. **Terafab project scale:** Terafab is described by the companies as the largest chip manufacturing facility in the world, with a first-phase cost of $16.8 billion, down from a $25 billion figure previously floated in March. It is intended to produce over a terawatt of compute per year for SpaceX and xAI data centers, plus future chips for Tesla's Optimus and Cybercab.
3. **Broader gas pattern across Musk companies:** The article frames Terafab's gas plan as consistent with xAI's Colossus data center near Memphis, which has run on gas turbines since 2024, many reportedly unpermitted. It cites a lawsuit by the NAACP, Southern Environmental Law Center, and Earthjustice over 27 unpermitted turbines in Southaven, Mississippi, alleged to emit over 1,700 tons of nitrogen oxides and 19 tons of formaldehyde annually, and quotes NAACP's Abre' Conner: "A data center should not be a potential death sentence for a community's health." The article states Trump's DOJ intervened to keep the turbines running on national-security grounds when regulators moved in.
4. **Musk's gas turbine investments:** The article states Musk personally bought gas turbine company APR Energy for about $1 billion in July, adding more than a gigawatt of turbine capacity, and that SpaceX has separately committed more than $2.8 billion to gas turbines for its data centers.
5. **Tesla's unused clean-energy alternative:** The article notes xAI bought $269 million of Tesla Megapacks earlier this year, and that Tesla and Sunrun launched a 16 GW virtual power plant in June aimed at data centers — positioned by the article as evidence Tesla is marketing solar-plus-storage for AI power demand even as Terafab bypasses it.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that while firm, 24/7 power is genuinely hard to deliver with solar alone, Musk's choice to go gas-first with zero Tesla solar at Terafab is driven by speed and cost rather than technical necessity, and by incentives — noting Musk owns about 20% of Tesla but 100% of APR Energy. The author contrasts this with Tesla's founding mission around sustainable energy, calling it ironic that its founder is now behind a major gas buildout.
