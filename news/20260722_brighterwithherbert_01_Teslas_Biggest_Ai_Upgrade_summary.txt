This episode of the Tesla-focused show (hosted by Cern Basher with guest Joe Tegtmeyer, filling in while the regular host is away) covers the Starlink-Cybercab integration announcement, robotaxi unit-economics modeling, SpaceX's upcoming earnings catalyst tied to Colossus, Tesla's growing FSD-centric retail strategy, and a broader macro riff on AI disruption, plus quick hits on Grok's vehicle rollout and Model Y L cabin space.

## Robotaxi
1. **Starlink V5 to integrate directly into Cybercab:** Tesla and SpaceX confirmed Starlink V5 will be built into the Cybercab, enabling high-speed in-vehicle connectivity; Elon Musk described watching 4K streaming while the Cybercab drives, and a Tesla AI team member (Yun Tasai) confirmed the SpaceX-Tesla integration. A Tesla-watcher (Chuck Cook) noted connectivity isn't required for FSD safety today but could become useful for fleetwide dynamic rerouting (traffic, potholes, closures) and possibly a future hardware requirement; a Tesla exec responded that it's more about fleet-management usefulness than operational necessity. The host argued this exemplifies unmatched cross-company synergy (rockets, satellites, and cars) that competitors cannot replicate.
2. **Robotaxi revenue-per-unit modeling and TAM:** The host's base-case model assumes roughly $45,000 in earnings (Abbott/EBIT terminology used loosely) per robotaxi per year at scale across millions of units, versus a wider range some estimate at $20,000–$50,000; at 1 million robotaxis and roughly $50,000 each, that implies about $50 billion in earnings — roughly 10x Tesla's current total earnings — with a market the host sizes at potentially 500 million+ vehicles globally (versus ~1.4 billion cars on the road today). The host called an inflection point likely within the next two to six months, while acknowledging frustration among investors over repeated delays to Robotaxi scaling timelines.
3. **Fleet counts referenced (canonical):** Discussion referenced current limited robotaxi deployment ("only two robotaxis" quipped early on) contrasted with RobotaxiTracker.com's canonical active fleet figures (Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, Houston) as evidence of gradual real-world scaling versus the eventual millions-of-vehicles vision.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD wiper behavior and Tesla's response:** A user complained about FSD triggering dry wipes; a Tesla AI team member (Yun Tasai) said the team reviews customer clips and that the system prioritizes wiper activation for visibility (dust, moisture) rather than purely visual windshield state, and is actively refining wet/dry surface detection.
2. **Tesla's new Santana Row store is FSD-first:** The showroom's design (wall displays, camera-visibility coffee table exhibit, "evolution of autonomy" timeline) is described as almost entirely focused on selling FSD rather than vehicles, which the hosts read as a signal of high internal confidence in the product; one host noted the FSD miles-driven counter shown (12 billion miles) likely understates current fleet totals.
3. **Cloud profile / loaner car experience:** A Tesla owner described a loaner vehicle automatically loading all personal settings (saved addresses, audiobooks, Spotify, even garage door opener) via cloud profile — the first time in ten years of ownership he'd needed this — which Tesla's North America account confirmed works for any Tesla with cloud profiles enabled.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX earnings call date and Colossus catalyst:** SpaceX's first earnings call is set for August 4th, earlier than the host expected (he'd anticipated mid-to-late August). The host believes Colossus-related deals could be announced as early as Q3 (possibly August/September) or by Q4, potentially doubling SpaceX's company-wide profit (framed as going from roughly $11 billion to $20 billion in earnings, per the host's own analysis) — a claim the host presents as his own estimate, not confirmed guidance.
2. **Valuation trajectory view:** The host expects SpaceX shares to still see further downside near-term due to ongoing share unlocks (pressure continuing into around November), but argues the stock is transitioning from "extremely overvalued" toward being "potentially extremely undervalued" if earnings roughly double as he anticipates, citing forward P/E figures around 50 and 25 in his hypothetical scenarios.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok engineering training data from SpaceX:** Elon Musk stated that SpaceX's engineering data corpus (excluding ITAR-restricted material) will be used in supplemental training of a large Grok model run, intended to significantly improve Grok's engineering capabilities.

## Market & Competition
1. **Grok's consumer UX gap:** The host praised Grok's underlying frontier model and enterprise coding tools but criticized its lack of a simple consumer-friendly app/download experience compared to competitors, calling this a "management deficit" and a missed opportunity to convert tens of millions of potential users.
2. **Grok expanding into vehicles across Asian markets:** A promotional video showed Grok integration coming to Tesla vehicles in India, Thailand, Singapore, Philippines, and Malaysia.
3. **Ford's smaller EV pickup spotted at a Tesla Supercharger:** The truck reportedly features a native NACS charging port and an LFP battery, with a rumored starting price near $30,000 and a planned launch next year; the host suggested Ford would likely lose money at that price point.
