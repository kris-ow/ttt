<summary>
This video covers SpaceX's first-ever quarterly earnings report as a public company and the subsequent trading action around a large post-IPO share unlock, with the host arguing that terrestrial AI compute buildout — not Starship or launch — is what will drive SPCX stock in the near term. The host also gives a detailed trade narrative on how the stock behaved around the unlock and where he expects resistance.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute capacity beat expectations:** SpaceX ended Q2 with 1.4 gigawatts of compute readiness capacity, ahead of the host's own model, which had projected 1 to 1.1 GW.
2. **Forward guidance raised, host skeptical of high end:** Management guided to 2 GW of compute built out by end of 2026 and over 5 GW by end of 2027; Elon Musk reportedly said capacity would be closer to 10 GW than 5 GW by then. The host calls the higher figures likely exaggerated given permitting constraints, though he raised his own bull case to 4 GW based on these statements, while keeping his base case lower.
3. **Power/cooling targets called very exaggerated:** SpaceX reportedly targets 20 GW of power and cooling by end of 2027, with Musk expecting 15 GW after delays; the host considers this vastly exaggerated given the difficulty of building out that much power, and notes these figures cover power/cooling infrastructure, not installed GPU compute.
4. **Compute lease economics extrapolated:** SpaceX has roughly a $26 billion annualized run rate on about 500 megawatts of leased compute (including capacity reserved for Grok/internal use, with the host estimating roughly half is leased externally). Extrapolating this rate, the host calculates that scaling to 2 GW by year-end would imply roughly a $100 billion revenue run rate, and 4 GW by end of next year roughly $200 billion — explicitly framed as rough, back-of-envelope math rather than the firm's detailed model.
5. **New cloud contracts and payback claims:** New cloud agreements contributed $1.6 billion of incremental Q2 infrastructure revenue, and SpaceX signed an additional $6.7 billion cloud services contract after quarter-end (ramping starting in October). SpaceX also stated new compute deployments pay back in under a year — a claim the host says cannot be independently verified from public data.
6. **Nvidia exclusivity and supplier relationships:** SpaceX affirmed Nvidia as its exclusive GPU provider, which boosted Nvidia's stock; the host frames this as a mutual arrangement where Nvidia prioritizes SpaceX supply. He also notes Micron was thanked on the Tesla earnings call for guaranteeing high-bandwidth memory supply.
7. **Grok iteration pace:** The latest Grok release is version 4.5, with version 4.6 expected within about a week and 4.7 within roughly the following month, reflecting rapid iteration enabled by SpaceX's compute buildout.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and network metrics:** SpaceX reported 12 million Starlink subscribers, 10,200 satellites in orbit, and coverage in 167 countries; the host says none of these figures were particularly surprising.

## Financials
1. **Revenue growth reported:** Overall revenue was up 92% year-over-year, with AI revenue up 247% year-over-year, which the host also characterizes as not a major surprise given known cloud agreements.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Post-earnings volatility and share unlock:** SPCX rose about 10% then dropped roughly 10-11% after earnings, which the host called an irrational move with no real signal. Two days after earnings, a roughly 900-million-share unlock occurred; the stock rose about 6% that day and then ran up sharply the next trading day, which the host says confirmed his view that the stock had bottomed once the unlock didn't trigger a major selloff. He disclosed taking a position around this point.
2. **Anticipated resistance near IPO price:** The host expects selling pressure and resistance as the stock approaches the roughly $135 IPO price level, reasoning that early employees/investors who held through the unlock (because they didn't like the prior price) will look to sell once the stock nears their cost basis; he says it's uncertain how long this resistance will last or whether the stock can break through.
3. **Investment thesis prioritization:** The host argues that for the next 12–24 months, stock-moving value comes from terrestrial AI compute buildout, not Starship or the launch business; Starship's importance shifts to delivering AI compute satellites starting around 2028, which he frames as a "second phase" that could remove the ceiling on the stock if terrestrial compute growth slows due to permitting constraints in 2028-2029. Grok/enterprise AI is described as a "joker" — a potential wildcard amid a competitive, messy market with free models and Chinese open-weight competitors expected next year.
