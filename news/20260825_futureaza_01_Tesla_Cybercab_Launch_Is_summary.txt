This futureaza video (hosted by Brian, with guest Randy Kirk) pushes back on social-media claims that Tesla's robotaxi program is "dead," using recent Nevada permitting news and the upcoming Cybercab launch event as evidence the rollout is progressing. The discussion mixes regulatory updates, event-invite gossip, and anecdotal FSD performance reports to argue the thesis remains intact despite vocal skepticism online.

## Robotaxi
1. **Nevada permitting clarified as expansion, not rejection:** Contrary to comments framing a "10-vehicle" permit as a setback, the hosts describe reporting (including coverage by Sawyer Merritt) that Tesla, Uber, and Waymo all received approval to operate robotaxis in Las Vegas — Tesla starting with 10 vehicles in a geofenced area with room to scale, while the Nevada DOT separately authorized up to 5,000 statewide; Waymo and another operator reportedly received similar statewide allotments.
2. **Cybercab launch event set for September 3rd in Austin:** Tesla is holding a Cybercab launch event on September 3rd, with a "golden ticket" sweepstakes for public attendees (entries via prior robotaxi rides or mail-in requests, with Randy Kirk saying he believes five winners will be drawn). Per the hosts, Tesla is barring invited guests from bringing a content creator as their plus-one, a rule several known Tesla-community figures said excluded them; Tesla employees are expected to ride Cybercabs ahead of the event, with public rides theoretically starting the day of.
3. **Cybercabs already staged in multiple cities:** Randy Kirk notes Cybercabs have reportedly been spotted parked in Baltimore and Detroit, among other locations, which the hosts take as a signal that Tesla may be preparing to open robotaxi service in additional cities.
4. **California AV permit status unresolved:** The hosts note Tesla has not applied for a California AV testing permit and has technically done no formal autonomous testing there despite operating supervised vehicles in the state; Randy suggests approval "has to be around the next corner" but the exact reason for the delay is unclear to both speakers.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Long-distance FSD runs without disengagement:** Randy Kirk references an unnamed driver's widely followed long-distance FSD trips, saying the first run was around 11,000–12,000 miles and the current run has exceeded 25,000 miles as of his last check — cited as evidence against claims that FSD "doesn't work."
2. **Influencer and first-ride videos highlighted as evidence:** The hosts cite recent videos — one showing elderly passengers (associated with Sawyer Merritt) enjoying an FSD ride, and another showing a blind passenger's first ride in an FSD-equipped or robotaxi vehicle — as anecdotal support for FSD's real-world reliability, while acknowledging pickup/drop-off execution is still inconsistent and lacks the volume of training data that highway/city driving has.
