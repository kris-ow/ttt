This is a live-reaction stream covering Tesla's Q3 production and delivery report as it dropped, comparing various analyst and community estimates against the actual figure, alongside discussion of energy storage deployments and a couple of SpaceX launch notes. The core thesis from the hosts is that delivery numbers matter less each quarter as FSD/software recurring revenue becomes a bigger value driver, and that this quarter's beat came despite minimal incentives and the loss of the US EV credit comparison.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Q3 deliveries beat estimates:** Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3, above the analyst consensus estimate cited in the stream of 461,947–461,974 (figure given slightly differently at different points) and above nearly every community forecaster's guess except Sawyer Merritt, who posted 486,532 (matching the actual figure) and was independently cited as the closest. The host's own estimate of 482,191 was about 4,000 off, which he claimed was the closest personal guess discussed on the call.
2. **Deliveries outpaced production:** Tesla delivered roughly 24,000 more units than it produced in the quarter, which the hosts said confirms Tesla drew down existing inventory; one host noted US showrooms were reported largely emptied of demo and floor stock by quarter end.
3. **Minimal incentives used:** The hosts said Tesla offered nearly zero incentives this quarter, noting a $1,000 discount in China late in the quarter and 0.99% financing on Model Y; UK incentives, previously near zero, were said to have risen slightly.
4. **10 million cumulative vehicle milestone:** The hosts noted Tesla has now passed 10 million cumulative vehicles produced/delivered.
5. **Berlin production ramp:** One host estimated Giga Berlin is now producing around 7.5 thousand units per week (up roughly 2,500/week), implying roughly 30,000+ incremental units if sustained across the quarter.
6. **Texas capacity still underutilized:** The hosts said Giga Texas's Model Y lines are currently running a single shift rather than the plant's full potential capacity, with hope that Model Y L demand could push a higher run rate; Texas was also described as having idled briefly a couple of times during the quarter.
7. **Cybercab beginning to be counted in reported figures:** The hosts said Cybercab units are starting to show up somewhere in Tesla's production/delivery accounting (likely production rather than deliveries), though still a small number relative to total volume.
8. **Secondhand Tesla values and FSD:** The hosts discussed that used Teslas, including those with FSD/Autopilot hardware, have held value better than expected, and mentioned a company called Boltwise that lets buyers scan a car's screen to check build date, processor, hardware version, and software entitlements.

## Financials
1. **Beat vs. analyst consensus:** The 486,532 delivery figure beat the stated analyst consensus (cited as approximately 461,947–461,974 in the stream), which the hosts characterized as a significant beat; premarket shares were up roughly 2% and the stock moved from the $360s into the mid-$360s shortly after market open, which the hosts attributed to the delivery beat.
2. **Shift toward recurring revenue thesis:** Hosts argued delivery/production numbers are becoming less important to the investment thesis than recurring software revenue (e.g., FSD), framing a vehicle sale as generating both one-time margin and ongoing software revenue over roughly the following two years.

## Energy
1. **Energy storage deployment figures:** Tesla reported energy storage deployments described in the stream as around 13.7 GWh for the quarter; hosts said this was only slightly up year-over-year on a trailing-12-month basis, less than they expected given the ramp of a new Megapack factory (referred to as "Megapack factory 3").
2. **Total storage capacity ramp:** Hosts estimated total storage manufacturing capacity ramping to roughly 30+ GWh, split roughly 10 GWh in Lathrop ("Lyre"), 10 GWh in Shanghai, and about 12.5 GWh in Texas, cautioning that Texas capacity may not be fully ramped and that deployment timing is lumpy quarter to quarter.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9/Heavy launch cadence:** Hosts noted a recent stretch of SpaceX activity described as two Falcon 9 launches plus one Falcon Heavy within about 13 hours, with five total booster landings across those missions; they noted Falcon 9 is being phased out in favor of Starship despite still flying at very high cadence in the near term.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **New Starlink satellite inter-satellite capacity:** Hosts mentioned that newer Starlink satellites have substantial capacity for satellite-to-satellite communication, which they said could support more internet traffic without routing back to ground stations and could potentially support a future "Star Mind" initiative, though they said there is no concrete news yet on that program.
