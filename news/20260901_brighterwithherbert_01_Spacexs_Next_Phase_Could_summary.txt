This episode of a Tesla/SpaceX news roundup, hosted by Herbert with guest Jeff Watts (a supply chain executive and manufacturing consultant), covers five SpaceX-centric stories: a Morgan Stanley note on Starship's valuation potential, SpaceX's move into in-house gas turbine blade manufacturing and its acquisition of APR Energy, expanded military Starshield contracts, a new Starlink license in the UAE, and Elon Musk's long-term musings on Terafab and space-based climate engineering. The throughline is that SpaceX and Tesla are converging on infrastructure-scale projects — data center power, rockets, and chips — by building capabilities in-house rather than relying on external supply chains.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Morgan Stanley sees Starship as underappreciated:** Analyst Adam Jonas's team called SpaceX attractively valued, modeling revenue reaching $3.5 trillion by 2040 based on 5,800 annual Starship launches by then, assuming two launches per pad daily across 15 planned launchpads (13 operational by end of 2027) tied to the newly announced $100 billion Starbase Louisiana project. A commentator (Aaron Burnett) noted this figure is roughly half of SpaceX's own stated revenue target and ten years later than the company's internal timeline, implying an internal goal near $7 trillion by roughly 2030. Elon Musk responded that his own best guess for hitting $3.5 trillion in revenue is 2033, not 2040.
2. **Starbase Louisiana sized against historic infrastructure projects:** A commentator (Brett Winton of Ark Invest) placed the $100 billion Starbase Louisiana project alongside history's largest infrastructure builds — citing the ~$7 billion U.S. Interstate Highway System, the ~$203 billion International Space Station, and comparing it to projects like the Three Gorges Dam and Hong Kong Airport — to illustrate its scale.
3. **Musk on Terafab's long-term scale:** Musk said Terafab could be worth over a trillion dollars long-term if it reaches a terawatt per year of silicon production, still representing a strong ROI at that scale.
4. **APR Energy acquisition clarified as a SpaceX purchase, not personal:** Musk bought turbine company APR Energy (reported as a roughly $1 billion deal) through SpaceX, not personally; he said that because he is SpaceX's controlling shareholder, media coverage incorrectly characterized the purchase as a personal acquisition.

## Energy
1. **SpaceX building its own gas turbine blade casting to unblock data center power:** Musk said gas turbines are sold out through 2030 and that the "vanes and blades" component is the true bottleneck, with only three casting companies globally, all massively backlogged; SpaceX and Tesla intend to do in-house blade/vane casting, which Musk said could pull turbine delivery timelines forward by up to 18 months. He also stated SpaceX and Tesla are each building 100 gigawatts per year of solar production capacity, with natural gas still needed to supplement and bootstrap solar for several years. Watts noted this leverages casting and materials expertise SpaceX developed solving turbine blade cracking issues on its Merlin rocket engine, and a shared materials/structures engineering team between Tesla and SpaceX.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Starshield expanded to worldwide military aircraft:** The Space Force expanded Starshield connectivity to military aircraft globally, from cargo planes to fighter jets, with a filing reportedly stating SpaceX is currently the only provider capable of meeting the requirement. Total contract value and the number of aircraft covered remain redacted.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **UAE-wide 10-year Starlink license:** Starlink won a 10-year license covering the entire UAE, spanning individuals, businesses, governments, aviation, and shipping.

## Market & Competition
1. **Institutional accumulation and Tesla's valuation debate:** Watts pointed to visible institutional accumulation of Tesla shares (citing analysis from a commentator named Alexander) and Ark Invest's continued buying (reportedly holding over $1 billion combined in Tesla and SpaceX shares) as evidence markets are pricing in future scaled businesses like robotaxi and reusable launch, rather than just current products. He noted Tesla's high P/E is partly justified by the breadth of its product/business portfolio (including the Semi's potential to shift freight from rail), but cautioned that Tesla's dominant EV share applies within a still-small total EV market, so overall market growth matters as much as share.
