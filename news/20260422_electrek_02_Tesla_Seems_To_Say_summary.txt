Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings release was accompanied by a quiet but significant change to its Robotaxi rollout graphic, suggesting the previously committed "1H 2026" timeline for five announced US cities has been pushed back to the indefinite "preparations underway" status.

## Robotaxi
1. **Timeline slip for five cities:** Tesla's investor letter graphic dropped the specific "1H 2026" launch commitment for Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas, replacing it with "preparations underway." With only two months remaining in 1H 2026, the author interprets this as a de facto delay. Dallas and Houston were recently added but are characterized as limited, not genuine consumer availability.

2. **Graphic language change:** Tesla also changed the section header from "Planned Robotaxi Coverage" to "Announced Near-Term Planned Robotaxi Coverage," signaling intent to expand beyond the current list without announcing specific new cities.

3. **San Francisco clarified as human-driven:** The updated graphic includes a footnote citing California TCP permit #TCP0046782-A — a standard taxi company permit — confirming Tesla's Bay Area operations are human-driven, not autonomous. Tesla has not filed for an autonomous taxi permit in California.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Austin operations remain limited:** Tesla launched supervised (Level 2) Robotaxis in Austin in June 2025. The article states that rides claimed to be "unsupervised" are often supervised remotely, and the total number of such rides has been "vanishingly few."

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo comparison:** Waymo currently operates in 11 cities exclusively as Level 4 driverless robotaxis, with 20 more announced including two outside the US — significantly ahead of Tesla's footprint and autonomy level.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames Tesla's Robotaxi program as a pattern of repeated, unmet promises dating to 2016, and treats the graphic change as confirmation that Tesla is again kicking the can on announced timelines. The author also distinguishes editorially between "robotaxi" (genuinely autonomous) and Tesla's "Robotaxi" (may be human-supervised), a distinction the author says reflects the operational reality.
