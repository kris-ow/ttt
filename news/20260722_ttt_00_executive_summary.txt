## Brief
- Tesla Q2 revenue record $28.24B, EPS misses badly at $0.33
- Free cash flow negative $1.1B, first cash burn since early 2024
- Robotaxi expands to Tampa, Orlando; paid miles growth flatlines
- France delays FSD approval, wants EU-wide sign-off first
- May 2026 Autopilot/FSD crashes hit record 207, per NHTSA data
- FSD V14 Lite completes 4,200-mile HW3 road trip, zero interventions

## Financials
- Q2 revenue $28.24B (+26% YoY), beat consensus ~$26.4B; first real revenue growth in over a year and first time TTM revenue topped $100B. Non-GAAP EPS $0.33, down 18% YoY, well below $0.53 expected.
- Operating income fell 57% to $398M; operating margin sank to 1.4% from 4.1%, as opex jumped 47% to $4.35B on AI, Optimus, robotaxi and stock-comp spending.
- Capex more than doubled to $5.8B, driving free cash flow to -$1.1B — Tesla's first cash-burning quarter since early 2024. GAAP net income $1.11B (-5%), cushioned by a $590M gain including bitcoin mark-to-market.
- Regulatory credit revenue collapsed to $146M (-67% YoY, down from $380M in Q1), reflecting the expired $7,500 federal EV credit and a law change eliminating fuel-economy penalties — a revenue source not expected to return.
- Ahead of results, commentators had flagged expected negative cash flow and a ~$10B full-year FCF drag from AI compute buildout, plus EPS estimates ranging from the high-40s to low-70s cents — the actual $0.33 print landed below nearly all of them.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla launched unsupervised Model Y robotaxi rides in Tampa and Orlando, bringing the footprint to 7 metro areas; SFO airport rides also launched for the Bay Area service. Vehicle counts in the new markets were not disclosed.
- Electrek calculates Tesla's own cumulative paid-mile chart (past 2.4M miles) obscures a stalled quarter: paid miles added were ~900,000 in both Q1 and Q2 2026, breaking the prior acceleration trend from ~150,000 (Q3 2025) to ~450,000 (Q4 2025).
- Electrek reports its own tracking shows the active unsupervised fleet near 21 vehicles across Austin, Dallas, Tampa and Orlando, down from an Austin peak of ~25 in late April to ~17 now — a shrinkage, not growth, versus RobotaxiTracker.com's stale May 9 count. California rides reportedly still carry a Tesla employee safety monitor.
- Cantor Fitzgerald reiterated an overweight rating with a $510 price target ahead of earnings, citing Robotaxi and an upcoming software platform; separately, analyst Dillon Loomis (per jobhakdi) noted Dallas/Houston saw only 5–10 active vehicles roughly 94 days after launch.
- Starlink V5 will be built directly into Cybercab for high-speed in-vehicle connectivity, confirmed by Musk and a Tesla AI team member; one Tesla-watcher suggested it could eventually become a fleet-management tool or hardware requirement rather than a safety necessity.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla logged a record 207 Autopilot/FSD crashes in May 2026 per NHTSA SGO data, more than all of 2021 combined; H1 2026 crashes (826) are already on pace to roughly double 2025's full-year total (1,043), a 73% YoY jump. Tesla redacts 99.9% of crash narratives, versus near-zero redaction by GM, Ford, Honda and Toyota.
- France's Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot declined to approve FSD Supervised as-is, citing speeding behavior and inadequate driver attention monitoring in urban settings; France wants EU-wide harmonized approval rather than following the Netherlands' April approval or Denmark's June approval. Sweden separately urged the EU in June to reject FSD unless the speeding behavior is removed.
- FSD V14 Lite is widening rollout to HW3 vehicles; testers report smoother highway and city driving, though self-parking remains weak. Two drivers completed a 4,200-mile Key West-to-Washington trip with zero manual inputs in an HW3 car.
- FSD V14.3.6 began rolling out; Tesla's new Santana Row store is designed almost entirely around selling FSD rather than vehicles.

- Tesla raised U.S. Model 3 lease pricing up to 15.2% across trims, tightening consumer financing incentives.
- Tesla opened its first public Megacharger station for Semi in Bloomington, CA (6 stalls, 1MW+ peak), replacing an earlier lower-power site.
