This Tesla Beat Podcast episode rounds up a busy week of Tesla and SpaceX news: a teased Cybercab public launch event, Nevada's approval of Tesla's full autonomous vehicle network permit for up to 5,000 vehicles, a large Tesla Semi order from Amazon via Einride, wireless charging patent news, a large China recall, the apparent discontinuation of the Solar Roof, and a shift toward institutional ownership in SpaceX shares. The hosts frame most developments optimistically as signs of Tesla's autonomy and energy/semi businesses maturing, while flagging skepticism toward Rivian's point-to-point driver-assist claims.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab public launch teased via ride-referral contest:** Tesla ran a promotion where riders could accumulate referral entries (some flooding the system with rapid rides) for a chance to be one of five winners of an exclusive Cybercab launch event; entries reportedly had to be handwritten rather than typed. Hosts speculate the event could occur within about two weeks and that winners might be picked up by a robotaxi, with wider employee and then public rollout to follow, though this is their speculation, not confirmed detail.
2. **Nevada approves 5,000-vehicle autonomous network permit:** Tesla received full approval for its autonomous vehicle network permit in Nevada covering up to 5,000 vehicles, up from an initial smaller approved batch; hosts expect a ramp rather than immediate full deployment and highlighted Las Vegas' existing Boring Company Loop (supervised FSD Teslas) as a complementary underground network to future unsupervised Cybercabs above ground.
3. **Model Y used as robotaxi in Vegas Loop draws attendee praise:** A host relayed secondhand comments from a trade-show planners' conference session where attendees cited Vegas Loop rides and Amazon Zoox robotaxis as highlights of a recent large trade show, illustrating rider enthusiasm for autonomous transport experiences.
4. **Wireless charging patents point toward robotaxi depot use:** Recently published patents from Tesla's WiTricity/wireless-charging acquisition include hot-object detection (e.g., to protect pets on charging mats) and pad designs compatible with both 400V and 800V vehicles. An Austin robotaxi depot application reportedly includes around 80 wireless charging pads, which the host argues would let fleets park vehicles far more densely than cable charging allows.
5. **Analyst claim: Tesla holding back Model Y robotaxi scaling in favor of Cybercab:** A host cited an unnamed analyst's claim that Elon Musk said Tesla could scale up Model Ys for robotaxi use but has deliberately not done so due to confidence in Cybercab; this is relayed secondhand and not independently verified in the episode.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD avoids rear-end collision by moving into shoulder:** A viral video shows a Tesla running FSD detect that a trailing BMW would not stop in time, move into the shoulder, and avoid being hit while the BMW plowed into the vehicle ahead; hosts cite this as evidence of safety benefits humans can't replicate due to limited rear awareness.
2. **Cybertruck reportedly avoids pedestrian jumping in front of vehicle:** A host described a separate video (from a friend) showing a Cybertruck moving out of the way of a man who appeared to jump in front of the car at night, before the driver noticed, though this account is secondhand and unverified.
3. **Rivian's point-to-point driver-assist claims met with skepticism:** Rivian published a blog post claiming point-to-point assisted driving later this year with wider 2027 rollout; hosts (particularly one host, citing salesperson conversations at a Rivian showroom) are skeptical, arguing Rivian lacks the compute, training miles, and data Tesla accumulated over years to reach comparable capability, and calling the near-term claim "shenanigans."
4. **Nearly 3 million vehicle recall in China over emergency door releases:** Chinese regulators ordered Tesla to fix nearly 3 million vehicles (reportedly affecting other automakers too) over emergency door release design concerns, described as Tesla's largest such action to date; hosts note this is likely a hardware/design fix rather than a software update and drew a comparison to an early Fisker vehicle with a similarly confusing manual door release.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi gets dedicated webpage touting economics:** Tesla published an updated Semi webpage citing lower cost than diesel, up to 500 miles of range fully loaded (at over 80,000 lbs gross weight), 60% range recovery in 30 minutes via a 1.2 MW Megacharger, and fleet software for trip planning and remote support.
2. **Amazon orders 500 Tesla Semis via Einride:** Amazon is a customer of Einride (described by a host as a Swedish company) for North American delivery routes, and reportedly ordered 500 Tesla Semis — characterized by hosts as the largest Semi deal to date; one host speculated Tesla could eventually become its own fleet operator charging per mile rather than selling to third-party fleet operators.
3. **Porsche to end Taycan production:** Porsche plans to discontinue its Taycan sedan after declining sales, shifting focus to the Macan and Cayenne EVs; hosts attribute this partly to SUV preference over sedans and note Porsche/VW EVs cost roughly double comparable Teslas, limiting volume.
4. **Tesla dominates "Made in America" auto index:** Seven of the top 10 vehicles in a 2026 Made in America auto index are Teslas, which hosts frame as notable given Tesla's small model lineup.
5. **Vehicle-to-load rolled out for Model Y Premium:** Tesla launched vehicle-to-load capability for all Model Y Premium trims in the US via an $80 outlet adapter (120V, 20A, ~2.4kW max), enabling powering of devices like space heaters and power tools.
6. **Solar Roof appears discontinued:** The Solar Roof product listing has disappeared from Tesla's website; unconfirmed by Tesla but hosts note the company recently announced in-house solar panel manufacturing/assembly in the US, suggesting a product transition rather than exit from solar.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Shift toward institutional SpaceX ownership discussed:** Hosts discussed a claim (originating from other commentators, not independently verified) that institutional investors are increasing their share of SpaceX ownership at the expense of retail investors; one host was unsure how a cited 16% figure was derived and speculated it might depend on how Elon Musk's shares are categorized.
2. **SpaceX and Tesla stock performance context:** Hosts noted TSLA was up modestly and SPCX down roughly 6% over the past week, hovering near its IPO price, which one host viewed as having "aged well" now that employees can sell shares.

## Financials
1. **Retail investor patience with Tesla stock questioned:** A host noted Tesla stock has underperformed the S&P 500 and other major tech companies over the past five years, suggesting some long-term retail holders nearing retirement may be cashing out, contributing to institutional investors picking up shares on dips.
