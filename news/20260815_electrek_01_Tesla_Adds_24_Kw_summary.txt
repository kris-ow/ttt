Tesla has enabled 2.4 kW vehicle-to-load (V2L) capability for the Model Y Premium in the US and Puerto Rico, requiring an $80 adapter accessory rather than a built-in outlet.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **How the V2L adapter works:** The $80 adapter attaches to the end of the Gen 3 Mobile Connector (the charging cable included with new Model Ys, sold separately for $300), plugging into the charge port to deliver 120V/20A household outlets at up to 2.4 kW. It is the same part Tesla launched for Cybertruck in April 2025 at the same price and power ceiling.

2. **Limited vehicle compatibility:** Tesla's shop now lists three compatible vehicles: Model Y Premium, Model Y Performance, and Cybertruck. The cheaper Model Y Standard and the entire Model 3 lineup do not support it. The article notes Electrek reported in October 2025 that the Model Y Performance shipped with bidirectional charging hardware already installed, suggesting the feature was withheld for trim strategy rather than engineering reasons.

3. **Competitive position on V2L power:** Tesla's 2.4 kW offering trails competitors: Hyundai has offered 3.6 kW V2L on the Ioniq 5 since 2021 (with an interior outlet and adapter included), Kia offers the same on EV6/EV9, Ford's F-150 Lightning starts at 2.4 kW and reaches 9.6 kW on extended-range trims via built-in outlets, and Chevy's Silverado EV offers up to 10.2 kW.

## Energy
4. **No home backup for Model Y:** Tesla's Powershare page still states Powershare is not available for Model S, Model 3, Model X, or Model Y. Home backup (11.5 kW output for three-plus days) remains exclusive to Cybertruck, which Tesla has gotten approved as a vehicle-to-grid asset with PG&E in California.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert welcomes the move as a step in the right direction and calls the $80 adapter a better deal than portable power stations, but criticizes Tesla for lagging the industry on bidirectional charging power levels and for charging extra for a feature Hyundai included free five years ago at lower power. He also dismisses the idea that Tesla withholds V2L/V2H capability to protect its Powerwall business.
