Tesla has enabled Powershare Home Backup for the Model 3 and Model Y, letting them supply power to a home during outages — a capability previously limited to the Cybertruck. The feature requires a Powerwall 3 and is only guaranteed for Model 3/Y vehicles ordered on or after October 1, 2026, with older units having inconsistent hardware support.

## Energy
1. **How Powershare works and its limits:** Owners plug the car into a Wall Connector or Universal Wall Connector at a home with a Powerwall 3; during a grid outage, the car's battery extends backup power. Tesla says a Model 3 or Model Y provides "over two days" of backup (standard trims "up to two days") for a home using 30 kWh/day from a 90% charge, versus "more than three" days for Cybertruck; maximum continuous output is listed at 11.52 kW. Owners with an existing Powerwall 3 and Wall Connector need no extra hardware but must contact Tesla support to activate it. Powerwall 2 and Powerwall+ support is "coming soon"; there is no Powershare Gateway option for Model 3/Y (unlike Cybertruck), no grid-selling ("Grid Support") capability, no Model S/X support, and availability is limited to the US and Puerto Rico (Canada's site still lists Powershare as Cybertruck-only).
2. **Hardware determines eligibility for existing cars:** Bidirectional power on Model 3/Y runs through an inverter Tesla calls PCS2 Lite; Tesla hasn't disclosed when this part entered production for each trim, so owners must check their touchscreen (Software > Additional Vehicle Information) for a "Powershare Support" flag to know if their car has the capability.

## Market & Competition
3. **Tesla lags Ford and GM on vehicle-to-home:** Ford launched home backup on the F-150 Lightning in 2022 with 9.6 kW output. GM committed in 2023 to bring vehicle-to-home to all Ultium-based EVs by model year 2026 and has delivered it across models including the Equinox EV, Blazer EV, Silverado EV, 2027 Bolt, Sierra EV, Hummer EV, and five Cadillac models; Kia offers it on the EV9 via a Wallbox charger. At Tesla's March 2023 Investor Day, then-SVP Drew Baglino said bidirectional charging could arrive "within the next two years" (implying 2025), a deadline Tesla missed; Elon Musk said at the same event he didn't think many people would want bidirectional charging unless they had a Powerwall — which the article notes is how Tesla ultimately shipped the feature. The article also notes that Sigenergy's home battery system reportedly achieved vehicle-to-home with Tesla vehicles over DC last year, without Tesla's involvement.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla is playing catch-up to Ford and GM, and suggests tying the feature to Powerwall 3 reveals Tesla avoided vehicle-to-home earlier to protect Powerwall sales. He calls the rollout "a step in the right direction" given it reaches Tesla's volume models with no extra hardware needed for existing Powerwall 3/Wall Connector owners, but criticizes Tesla for excluding most existing vehicles, calling it a missed opportunity for "gigawatts of load that could be saved."
