This episode centers on U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's call for a single federal AV standard to replace the current state-by-state patchwork, alongside Tesla's release of European FSD safety data ahead of an October 6th EU approval vote, plus updates on FSD mileage milestones and competitor robotaxi expansion (Waymo, Zoox). The hosts (with guest Brian White of "Future of Autonomous") frame federal standardization and the EU data release as the two biggest near-term catalysts for the Tesla autonomy thesis.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Duffy pushes for one federal AV standard:** In a televised interview, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the state-by-state regulatory patchwork is the "biggest issue" for autonomous vehicles and called for Congress to set a single federal standard rather than 50 individual state rules. He described his approach as "Goldilocks" — moving too slowly kills innovation and drives companies overseas, moving too fast risks safety — and said he is actively questioning whether autonomous vehicles need steering wheels, pedals, or mirrors at all. One host pushed back, noting federal rules are typically minimums that states can still exceed, so a federal standard wouldn't necessarily preempt stricter state rules.
2. **Tesla open-sources European FSD safety data ahead of Oct. 6 vote:** Ahead of an EU-wide approval vote for FSD Supervised on October 6th, Tesla released safety data used to support its Netherlands (RDW) approval, shared with all EU member states in April. Key figures: FSD is approved in five European countries (Netherlands, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania), used by over 70,000 customers driving over 1 million km/day, and shows 4x fewer crashes than manual driving across 100 million km of EU public road data. Tesla also disclosed North America data showing FSD Supervised cuts major crashes vs. manual driving by 45% (highway), 69% (arterial), 50% (city), 40% (local) across 3 billion km in 2025, plus up to 84% fewer AEB activations, up to 81% less harsh braking, ~99% fewer minor collisions, and a scenario-test success rate of 98% across 230,000 tests in cities including Amsterdam, Munich, and Barcelona. Data also showed FSD drives at or below surrounding traffic speed 98% of the time and reduces driver fatigue events by roughly two-thirds versus manual driving.
3. **Sweden's infrastructure minister rides FSD, floats robotaxi pilot:** Sweden's infrastructure minister Andreas Carlson rode along during an FSD demonstration for elder care, with the trained safety driver not touching the controls; he called the experience "very interesting, great fun" and said it "feels like riding with a person." He said he now wants the town to become a pilot municipality for future robotaxis, and the hosts noted this appears unaffected by the ongoing IF Metall labor strike in Sweden that some had worried would block approval.
4. **FSD crosses 14 billion cumulative miles:** Tesla's FSD Supervised fleet passed 14 billion cumulative miles, with about 5.4 billion in city driving; Tesla's safety hub reports FSD has seven times fewer major collisions than human drivers. The first billion miles took 3.5 years; the most recent billion took just 28 days, following a progression of roughly 202, 80, 120, 90, 70, 70, and 53 days for prior billion-mile milestones. The fleet hit 10 billion miles in early May and 13 billion on August 3rd.

## Robotaxi / Market & Competition
1. **Waymo opens paid rides in three new cities, expands footprint:** Waymo began offering paid robotaxi rides to the general public in Denver, San Diego, and Tampa, bringing its total to 14 U.S. cities (13 full-access plus invite-only San Antonio, with Las Vegas on deck). Waymo's fleet is described as still under 4,000 vehicles. One host noted Waymo has substantially more paid, unsupervised robotaxi miles than Tesla, which still needs to close that gap, while Tesla's current data is largely supervised.
2. **Zoox expands to Houston and San Diego:** Zoox is expanding its robotaxi service to Houston and San Diego. One host speculated (without confirmation) that Zoox's proprietary purpose-built vehicle may face more reliability issues than an off-the-shelf vehicle platform, given it's a newly manufactured product, and suggested proprietary vehicles could cost meaningfully more per unit to deploy than adapting existing cars.
