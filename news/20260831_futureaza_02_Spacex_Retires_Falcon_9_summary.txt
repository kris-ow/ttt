This is a weekly news roundup from futureaza covering SpaceX launch news, EV competitor updates (Waymo, BYD, Lucid, Honda, BMW, Kia, a Mexican low-cost EV), battery/energy storage developments, and Starlink international expansion — with no Tesla-specific or SpaceX-Tesla-thesis content beyond general SpaceX launch operations.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9 exits Florida Starlink launches:** The host reports Florida will no longer launch Starlink missions on Falcon 9, with that vehicle continuing only from Vandenberg for polar orbits rather than the typical ~53–58° inclination missions. He notes Falcon 9 first flew in 2010, has flown nearly 700 times, and was the first orbital rocket to land and refly a first-stage booster; one unnamed source cited by the host claims a specific booster has flown almost as many times as Space Shuttle Discovery did over its 27-year career.
2. **Falcon Heavy launches Nancy Grace Roman telescope:** SpaceX's Falcon Heavy launched the telescope toward the Sun-Earth L2 point; the host describes it as functionally similar to Hubble but with a much wider field of view, and says that once calibrated it is expected to gather roughly as much data in a few months as Hubble has produced to date.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink expands via telecom partnership in Uganda:** MTN Uganda and Starlink expanded their partnership to bring broadband access to Uganda; the host frames this as a "leapfrog" infrastructure play, noting Starlink's price is much lower than the roughly $100/month U.S. price in countries that can't afford that rate.
2. **UAE grants Starlink a 10-year satellite internet license:** The host notes this adds another country to Starlink's coverage map, and separately mentions that domestic-ownership rules in some countries (India, Nepal among the ones he flew over) still block Starlink connectivity mid-flight.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo to launch a robotaxi service in Munich, Germany:** Waymo said it plans to launch in Munich after establishing a German entity, still needing to clear mapping/testing and regulatory hurdles; the host frames this as robotaxis "going international" and argues competition will reward whoever has the better product.
2. **BYD's flash-charging network scales up:** BYD has deployed 10,000 flash chargers in six months, covering 325 cities, with 1.83 million users (33% not even BYD customers) and 210 million kilowatt-hours delivered.
3. **Lucid recalls ~27,185 Air sedans over a software lighting issue:** A software defect controlling exterior lighting circuits could allow overheating; the host notes the fix has already been deployed via software.
4. **Honda reportedly reviving its EV program for next decade:** The host is skeptical of the long timeline, contrasting it with BMW's cumulative 2 million EVs sold and EVs representing seven in ten European deliveries last quarter, even as BMW's overall deliveries fell about 7.4% and Chinese sales dropped over 20%.
5. **Kia EV3 priced at $31,385 for the U.S.:** Positioned as "a lot of car" at roughly Bolt/Leaf-level pricing rather than being the cheapest EV on the market.
6. **Ultra-fast EV chargers (800kW–1.5MW class) arriving in the U.S. ahead of compatible vehicles:** The host warns against under-speccing new charging infrastructure, citing a conversation with a Nepal power-company engineer about stranded lower-power charging assets deployed too early.
7. **$9,000 compact EV reportedly made in Mexico:** A low-range, low-speed city car the host tried unsuccessfully to see in person in Mexico City; he calls the price point compelling if it can be sustained at scale.

## Energy
1. **Sungrow begins construction of a 10 GWh battery storage factory in Egypt:** A $50 million facility in the Suez Canal economic zone; the host questions whether that budget is sufficient for the stated 10 GWh capacity and says reliability remains to be seen.
2. **Non-flammable sodium battery reportedly passes extreme safety testing:** The host notes sodium battery volumetric energy density still lags lithium for automotive use — estimating that even using a full Tesla battery pack's volume might yield only around 52–54 kWh of usable energy versus 75 kWh today — though safety validation is an important milestone.
3. **Global co-located solar-plus-storage investment reaches $327 billion in H1 2026 per Bloomberg (per the host):** Of that, $25 billion was specifically co-located solar-plus-storage investment, per Bloomberg as cited by the host.
