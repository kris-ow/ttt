This is a casual, informal year-end roundtable ("Tesla Boyz Xmas") among several Tesla-focused YouTube hosts reflecting on 2025 progress and offering personal predictions for 2026, covering FSD maturity, robotaxi expansion, Cybercab production signals, Optimus manufacturing, and TSLA stock price targets. Much of the transcript is holiday banter and community reflection with comparatively little hard data, but several concrete claims and forecasts are made throughout.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD called "done" by hosts and cited analyst view:** Multiple hosts say recent FSD versions (described as version ~14.2 and later) perform at a level where hosts describe it as essentially solved in the cities they've tested, including one host's claim of a multi-state, multi-day road trip with a reported success rate "well over 99%," with manual interventions limited to parking-lot navigation and personal choice points (e.g., stopping to view Christmas lights). One host says Morgan Stanley has stated FSD is "done" and robotaxi service is here (attributed to Morgan Stanley per the host).
2. **Hardware 3 performance called flawless in extended testing:** Two hosts independently describe extensive Hardware 3 (older FSD computer) testing — one in the LA area over roughly four hours round-trip, another over four to five days in Tampa — with no or almost no disengagements reported.
3. **International FSD expansion cited as rebutting "never" claims:** Hosts say FSD has a limited release in China with demonstrated performance on a one-lane dirt mountain road, plus rollouts/demos in Australia, New Zealand, parts of Europe (trial with a Tesla employee in the seat), and South Korea, which they cite against commenters' claims that autonomy will "never" work outside the US.
4. **Lidar cited as unable to see through fog/snow:** One host reiterates a point attributed to a colleague ("Herbert," on a separate show) that lidar systems cannot see through fog or snow, contrasting with FSD's camera-based approach.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi deployment expanded to multiple cities in 2025:** Hosts highlight that Tesla's robotaxi service launched beyond Austin into the Bay Area/Palo Alto region during 2025, calling this the most significant milestone of the year, alongside the debut of Tesla's own ride-hailing app (avoiding the previously assumed need to partner with Waymo-style platforms).
2. **Cybercab described as on track for an April production launch:** One host says "every little sign" — including a Cybercab being driven through an In-N-Out drive-thru (described as Tesla's traditional signal that a design is production-ready), public road testing, crash testing, and job postings for mass-production roles (e.g., quality engineer) — points to Tesla still being on track to launch Cybercab around April.
3. **Hidden factory section at Giga Texas speculated to be Cybercab-related:** A host describes touring the Fremont factory and separately raises a blacked-off, security-fenced section of a factory (the discussion conflates this with speculation about Optimus production; see Humanoid Bots) — used as evidence Tesla is scaling up a new production line.
4. **2026 robotaxi fleet size predictions vary widely between hosts:** One host, citing conversations with "serious people," says some expect as many as 50,000 robotaxis by the end of 2026 but personally lands on roughly 20,000 as a more comfortable estimate, citing Cybertruck's roughly 50,000-unit first-year production pace as a reference point for a new vehicle line. A second host independently estimates 20,000–50,000 robotaxis by end of 2026, citing underestimated infrastructure/"command station" buildout needs across new cities as a key constraint. Both emphasize these are personal estimates, not confirmed guidance.
5. **Current robotaxi fleet size (RobotaxiTracker.com, last known):** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active Robotaxi fleet stood at 839 vehicles (Bay Area 657, Austin 135, Dallas 22, Houston 25) as of early August 2026, with an unsupervised (driverless) subset of 48 vehicles as of late July 2026 — both figures flagged as stale/unchanged for several weeks as of this recording.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production equipment reportedly being installed at Giga Texas:** A host recounts a recent Fremont factory tour where a factory employee accompanying him suggested a blocked-off, security-fenced section too small for car manufacturing is being tooled up for Optimus production — the host's inference, not a confirmed company statement.
2. **Optimus predicted to enter industrial use in 2026, not homes:** The same host expresses confidence Optimus will be deployed in 2026 in industrial/factory settings (citing labor shortages at Giga Nevada as a driver) rather than consumer homes, and floats Panasonic (which operates battery production alongside Tesla at Giga Nevada) as a plausible early customer, along with other closely tied suppliers — explicitly speculative.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Tesla models introduced in 2025:** A host cites the Model Y L launch in China (with European homologation now underway) plus the Model Y Standard and Model 3 Standard as the year's new model introductions, noting the Standard trims face weaker US demand (attributed to loss of EV tax credit) but better prospects internationally.
2. **Sentiment vs. product quality debate:** Hosts discuss the swing in public/political sentiment toward Tesla and Elon Musk over 2025 — from very negative (vandalism, dealership attacks) earlier in the year — arguing that Tesla's product value ultimately outweighs sentiment, citing (unconfirmed, hearsay) that a prominent Tesla critic personally bought a new Model Y using expiring referral credits.

## Energy
1. **Megapack manufacturing capacity expanded:** A host notes Giga Shanghai's Megapack line is up and ramped, with a new Megapack facility under construction in Texas, calling energy storage a large and growing part of Tesla's business.
2. **Ford entering stationary storage business:** Hosts mention (skeptically) that Ford is entering the stationary battery storage market, drawing comparison to GM's stationary storage announcement roughly three and a half years ago that the hosts say never materialized into visible products.

## Financials
1. **TSLA stock predictions for end of 2026:** One host predicts Tesla stock will reach the $500 level (timing uncertain, possibly late 2025 or early 2026) before pulling back, and forecasts an end-of-2026 close around $600–650 as his central case, explicitly distancing from other predictions circulating in the community of $700–2,000+. A second host declines to give a specific price target, predicting only that TSLA will continue to outperform the broader market, and says he would not sell any Tesla shares to invest in a SpaceX IPO.
2. **Earnings growth timing debated:** Hosts discuss a view attributed to "Warren Regul" (as cited by one host) that sustained earnings-upon-earnings growth for Tesla may not materialize until 2027 rather than the second half of 2026, and separately note Elon Musk has said robotaxi revenue won't meaningfully move Tesla's financials until the second half of 2026 (with hosts noting that timeline could itself slip to 2027).

## Market & Competition
1. **No credible "number two" in autonomous driving named:** Hosts recount a conversation with an acquaintance asking who would be the second-best option for a self-driving car after Tesla, and conclude no company has a comparable offering — noting Waymo isn't purchasable, Comma.ai only converts existing cars, and Ford's driver-assist system ("Blue Cruise") and GM's Super Cruise are limited to highway use.
