This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers a wide range of Tesla-related developments with a particular focus on the regulatory landscape for Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi operations — both in California and Texas. The central thesis is that California's evolving AV rulemaking process means Tesla's true unsupervised paid robotaxi service there is unlikely before 2027, while Texas operations are gradually expanding. Additional topics include FSD software rollout, Cybercab production observations, the Model Y L sighting in the US, Figure's humanoid robot progress, Tesla Semi charging infrastructure, and new California AV regulations that will affect all AV operators.

## Robotaxi

1. **California Regulatory Status — No AV Permit Applied For:** Dillon Loomis contacted the CPUC (referred to as the "CPU") in California and received an official response confirming Tesla has not applied for any autonomous vehicle permits. Tesla currently operates under a TCP (Transportation Charter Party permit), which classifies the service as a chauffeur/limousine-type operation rather than a true autonomous vehicle service in California's eyes. Loomis believes the reason Tesla has not yet applied is that new rulemaking is underway at the CPUC specifically covering autonomous vehicle passenger transportation services, and Tesla has been confirmed to be making public commentary on those proposed rules — suggesting Tesla is trying to shape the regulatory framework before formally entering it.

2. **California Unsupervised Timeline Pushed to 2027:** Based on the regulatory situation, Loomis estimates it will be at least 6 to 9 months — and possibly longer — before Tesla sees an actual unsupervised paid customer ride service in California. He notes that other companies like Waymo have historically taken 6 to 9 months to obtain a true AV permit. He does allow for the possibility that Tesla could hit the 50,000 miles of AV testing California currently requires sometime later in 2026, but concludes that a commercial unsupervised paid service in California is unlikely until sometime in 2027.

3. **Austin Expands to Evening Unsupervised Hours:** Tesla's robotaxis in Austin began operating unsupervised in the evenings for the first time as of the prior Sunday, a meaningful operational expansion. Previously, unsupervised operation in Austin ended in the mid-afternoon. Loomis notes that a majority of Tesla's incidents reported to NHTSA under the SGO standing order did occur at night, making evening expansion a notable safety and operational milestone to watch.

4. **Fleet Count and New City Ramp — Houston and Dallas:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised robotaxi fleet stands at 35 total: 24 in Austin, 6 in Houston, and 5 in Dallas. Tesla's official robotaxi website lists service hours of 6:00 a.m. to 2:00 a.m. Central time, but Loomis notes anecdotal reports suggest Houston robotaxis are not starting until 9:00 a.m., and that the new cities are likely shutting down well before 2:00 a.m. He expects Tesla to reach 10 to 15 unsupervised vehicles in both Houston and Dallas within the next two to three weeks, describing growth as slow but in line with expectations.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Crosses 10 Billion Supervised FSD Miles:** Tesla officially crossed the 10 billion miles driven mark on supervised Full Self-Driving. Loomis contextualizes this as equivalent to driving coast to coast in the US roughly 3.5 million times. However, he cautions this milestone does not appear to be a "light switch" moment for scaling unsupervised operations, walking back an earlier period of optimism on that front.

2. **FSD 14.3.2 Rolling Out, Not Yet Wide Release:** FSD version 14.3.2 is being rolled out to additional AI4 customers in North America, with the fleet estimated at roughly 55–60% having received the update. Early user feedback is mixed — most early recipients report no major issues, but some users report degraded performance compared to the 14.2 branch, including jittery behavior, start-stop issues, and at least one instance of the system requesting an immediate takeover. The intervene popup was also modified again. Loomis concludes the software is approaching but has not yet reached a true wide release.

3. **FSD Subscription Expands in Netherlands; Outright Purchase Ending:** After launching FSD subscriptions in the Netherlands last month, Tesla announced it will end the one-time FSD outright purchase option as of May 15th, 2026, leaving the monthly subscription as the only option. Tesla also reported that Netherlands owners drove 10 million km on supervised Full Self-Driving in under one month — approximately 6.2 million miles, or just over 200,000 miles per day.

4. **New California AV Regulations Effective July 1, 2026:** The California DMV announced that law enforcement will be allowed to issue tickets directly to autonomous vehicle manufacturers for traffic violations starting July 1st. New rules include: a required 50,000-mile safety test before a commercial permit is issued; remote operations personnel must meet state licensing and training requirements; vehicles must have an onboard communication system enabling first responders to reach a company representative within 30 seconds; and local officials can enact temporary electronic geofencing zones to exclude AVs during emergencies. Loomis notes these rules would have put Waymo in trouble had they been in place for the past two years, referencing past incidents of Waymo vehicles stalling in San Francisco streets and blocking first responders, and the revelation that some Waymo safety operators were based in the Philippines.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Figure 3 Robot — Capabilities and Production Rate:** Figure released extensive factory access and promotional content for its Figure 3 humanoid robot. Key specs: approximately 130 lbs, 5'6" tall, with a foam/clothing outer layer, fifth-generation hands with palm-mounted cameras and improved grip, able to lift 40 lb boxes, 4–5 hours battery life per charge, and about 1 hour to recharge via wireless inductive charging through foot pads at 2 kW total. All movement is controlled by their Helix 2.0 neural network. The current production facility has a stated capacity of 40,000–50,000 units per year at full capacity, with a long-term goal of one million units per year. Brett Adcock stated they produced 55 humanoid robots in one week, suggesting a rate of roughly 1 to 200 robots per week, up from one per day just 120 days prior. New customer announcements are expected within the next 90 days.

2. **Tesla Model X Line Shutting Down for Optimus Production:** Tesla shared a photo of the last Model X ever produced, consistent with Elon Musk's statement on the earnings call that the S/X production lines would be shut down in May to prepare for Optimus production. This signals a concrete step in factory conversion for Optimus manufacturing.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybercab Production Rate — Early Stage:** Joe Tegtmeyer reported approximately 25 to 30 Cybercabs located around the Giga Texas site early Monday, implying a current production rate of roughly 5 to 15 units per day at this early stage.

2. **Model Y L Spotted in the United States:** Multiple reports have emerged of Tesla testing the Model Y L in the US, specifically in Palo Alto. Loomis notes that months ago a source told him the Model Y L was indeed coming to the US despite Elon Musk's skeptical comments. He views these sightings as a strong indication of an imminent US launch and notes the timing would be well-suited to fill the gap left by the shutting down of the Model X line.

3. **Model 3 Pricing in Canada Drops Significantly:** Following Canada signing a deal with China reducing import tariffs on Chinese EVs from 100% down to 6%, the Model 3 from Giga Shanghai is now available in Canada at approximately CAD equivalent of $29,000 USD for the premium rear-wheel drive variant — described as the lowest pricing seen in some time in North America. The performance variant also dropped over 15%. The 6% tariff rate is capped at approximately 49,000 vehicles per year on a first-come, first-served basis, growing to about 70,000 by 2030. The Model 3 from China does not qualify for Canada's $5,000 EV incentive.

4. **Tesla Semi — Major Washington State Rebate and Charging Infrastructure:** Washington State announced a $175,000 rebate (via the WEZI program) for Class 8 electric trucks including the long-range Tesla Semi. If the long-range Tesla Semi costs approximately $290,000, this rebate would bring the effective price down to roughly $115,000 — below the average cost of a new diesel Class 8 truck (~$175,000). Additionally, Tesla's semi-charging suite now includes the 1.2 MW Megacharger for quick stops and a new 125 kW base charger for longer dwell/overnight charging. The base charger has no separate cabinet, uses V4 post and electronics components, features a 6-meter cable (vs. 3 meters on the Megacharger), and up to three units can be daisy-chained on a single breaker. One base charger costs approximately $20,000; one Megacharger approximately $94,000. The base charger can add up to 60% range in 4 hours. Deliveries of the base charger are estimated to begin early 2027. For revenue-generating charging sites, Tesla charges an all-inclusive fee of 8 cents per kWh (not the retail price end customers pay). Big F Transport ordered 40 Tesla Semi units through Forum Mobility, to operate out of a depot in Rancho Dominguez expected to go online in Q1 2027.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla European Registrations Rebounding:** Tesla's European registrations reportedly jumped 111% in Sweden, 102% in Denmark, 112% in France, and 23% in the Netherlands in April. Loomis cautions this is single-month data and more noise than signal, preferring to wait for Q2 year-to-date figures. He notes Europe's overall BEV share of new car registrations reached 20.5% in Q1 2026, up from 13.2% in Q1 2025, trending toward 25%.
