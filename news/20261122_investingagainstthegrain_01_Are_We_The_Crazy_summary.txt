The host argues that the sharp market sell-off, driven by Nvidia's leadership change, Fed rate-hike talk, AMD/China fears, outage anxiety and anti-data-center politics, is an overreaction. He points to historical stats showing oversold conditions tend to precede rebounds, and he uses Tesla's FSD v15 rollout and his own Cybercab rides as evidence that AI delivers real-world value.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia sell-off is narrative-driven, not fundamental:** The host says Nvidia fell 14%, ending its longest weekly losing streak since 2008, after Jensen Huang's departure and an SEC probe. He notes that acting CEO and CFO Colette Kress raised year-over-year growth expectations from 70% to 75% (excluding China) on the earnings call, so he sees no change in demand, margins or backorders. He says Elon Musk is the rare exception to the idea that no individual is bigger than the company, and he names Arm's Rene Haas as an obvious CEO candidate.
2. **Macro and sentiment pressures:** He lists Fed talk of a 50 basis point hike (which he calls a likely mistake, citing past cycles where the Fed broke the market), a short report on AMD's China exposure, AI moratorium politics, and a string of outages (Microsoft 365, FAA ground stop, airlines, Apple). He regards the outages as more routine than sinister.
3. **Historical oversold stats:** Citing Odd Stats and the Kobeissi Letter, he says the S&P 500 Infotech forward P/E of 16.4 is its lowest since March 2020. He also cites a QQQ Thursday/Friday drop pattern seen only twice before, and a 7-day S&P losing streak with a total return of -9.31%, after which 1-week and 1-year forward returns were nearly all positive. He expects a green week but cautions that history is no guarantee, and he describes defending his portfolio with collars and covered calls.
4. **Data-center and AI backlash is overblown:** He argues data-center water and electricity complaints are overstated, since many sites use closed-loop cooling, build behind the meter and feed excess power back to the grid. He says CEOs at the TomorrowX Summit (AMD, Dell and others) described no slowdown in buildout.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v15 rollout:** Tesla began rolling out FSD v15 over the weekend, upgrading every part of the end-to-end network, with Tesla claiming 25 times better handling of safety-critical cases. New features include a "reality view" live visualization of the drive's plan, navigation that learns from fleet route preferences (with later v15 releases expanding this to the whole route), better detection of signs, animals, debris, potholes and standing water, more natural speed handling in school zones and bad weather, slowing for speed cameras, and a bike rack mode. The host asks whether Hardware 4 vehicles without a front bumper camera will get the visualization.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab rides in Austin:** The host says he rode Cybercabs non-stop all week in Austin while attending the TomorrowX Summit, at an average cost of about five or six dollars per ride. He argues that cheap, safe autonomous transport would most help lower-income households by removing car payments, insurance and fuel costs, and that displaced Uber drivers could become fleet managers.

## Energy
