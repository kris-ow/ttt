Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla is ramping Cybercab production at Giga Texas despite having neither regulatory/consumer sales approval nor unsupervised autonomy to actually operate the steering-wheel-less vehicle, questioning the rationale for building it now.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab production ramping, but fleet has no path to use:** More than 100 steering-wheel-less Cybercabs have reportedly been spotted in Giga Texas outbound lots; the first unit rolled off the line in February 2026, with continuous production confirmed on Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call in April. Musk described the ramp as a "stretched out S-curve" that will go "kind of exponential towards the end of the year." Tesla designed the Cybercab to self-certify against Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, avoiding NHTSA's 2,500-unit annual cap on exemption vehicles.
2. **Austin robotaxi fleet remains small and reportedly shrinking:** City officials peg Tesla's Austin fleet at roughly 50 vehicles; per Electrek's own tracking, the unsupervised (driverless) portion has fallen from a peak of about 25 cumulative cars to roughly 14 active ones. Tesla expanded its Austin geofence to cover the metro area but runs only about 20 cars there; similar small geofences exist in Dallas and Houston, with a new sliver mapped in Miami — Tesla's first market outside Texas.
3. **Musk says safety validation, not mapping, is the bottleneck:** On the Q1 2026 earnings call, Musk said safety validation is limiting Robotaxi expansion and that Tesla is waiting for a rewritten FSD v15, now targeted for late 2026 or early 2027. He also said Robotaxi "likely will not see material revenue until at least 2027."

## Autonomous Driving
4. **Supervised FSD crash rate cited:** The article states Tesla's supervised fleet has been crashing at roughly four times the rate of an average human driver, and Tesla has reported a string of crashes to NHTSA in Austin.
5. **Camera-only sensor bet questioned:** Tesla's Robotaxi program relies solely on cameras (no radar or lidar); the author says he isn't convinced this technically precludes Level 4 autonomy, but notes companies running driverless cars at scale currently use radar/lidar, and Tesla's slow deployment a year in isn't a strong endorsement of the vision-only approach.

## Editorial Stance
The author's opinion: the Cybercab is a manufacturing achievement built ahead of a software promise (FSD v15) that keeps slipping, comparable to Tesla's earlier removal-then-reinstatement of the turn signal stalk on the Model 3 (which VP Lars Moravy admitted was "deleted too much"). The author argues Cybercabs won't be sold to consumers or aid Robotaxi improvement in the foreseeable future, and expresses doubt that FSD v15 will be sufficient for Tesla to let owners use FSD unsupervised, despite acknowledged improvements in v14.
