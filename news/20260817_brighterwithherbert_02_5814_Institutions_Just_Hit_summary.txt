This episode of brighterwithherbert covers a shift in Tesla's shareholder base toward institutional ownership, an update on Tesla's Robotaxi rollout across nine cities (including a restrictive new Nevada permit and a ballard-strike incident in Austin), evidence that FSD is becoming a demand driver for used Teslas, a "most American-made car" ranking favoring Tesla, and reports of an imminent Roadster unveiling potentially involving SpaceX technology. Host Herbert is joined by manufacturing/supply-chain executive Jeff Loomis, who frames Tesla's Robotaxi delays as software-driven edge cases rather than hardware limitations.

## Financials
1. **Retail Tesla ownership hits record low as institutions hit record high:** Per data attributed to Alexandra, retail investors now hold about 15.8% of Tesla shares (down from roughly 50% previously), while 5,814 institutions hold a record 2.2 billion shares out of 3.95 billion shares outstanding; Elon Musk holds 699 million voting shares (423 million with mirror voting rights), and retail holds about 625 million shares. Invesco added $7.4 billion in net inflows last quarter, expanding its position by over 17 million shares. Loomis attributes the retail selloff partly to negative reaction to Tesla's July earnings call (missed EPS, unclear guidance) and to a broader "AI trade" de-grossing among momentum/AI stocks in July, with institutions buying the dip.
2. **Barclays resumes accumulating Tesla:** After three straight quarters of selling, Barclays bought roughly 1 million Tesla shares in Q2 (a swing of $427 million in positive money flow for the quarter), bringing its total holding to about 11 million shares valued around $4.8 billion; Barclays maintains a hold rating with a $370 price target.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi scorecard: seven of nine cities on or near plan:** Austin, Dallas, and Houston are running fully unsupervised and on plan; San Francisco Bay Area remains stuck with a safety driver with no movement toward unsupervised status, which Herbert calls disappointing; Phoenix is behind plan; Las Vegas and Miami/Orlando are small-scale. Loomis argues Tesla is behind its own stated timeline but frames this as a software/edge-case problem rather than a hardware limitation, noting Tesla keeps deploying hardware at scale, which he reads as a signal of internal confidence that software will catch up; he estimates Tesla's driven-miles base may be doubling roughly every 5–8 weeks.
2. **Nevada grants Tesla a restrictive Robotaxi permit:** Tesla received an interim Nevada operating permit covering only 10 fully autonomous vehicles — far short of the 5,000 vehicles it had requested — restricted to the approved operational design domain (described as essentially the Las Vegas Strip), with a ban on roads posted above 45 mph and a requirement that vehicles be clearly marked as Robotaxi. Loomis says this incremental approach mirrors how Waymo scaled and reflects deliberate risk management amid intense regulatory and media scrutiny of Tesla.
3. **Austin wait times reportedly improving:** A user (Arian Bhutala) reported now getting consistent single-digit-minute wait times for Robotaxi rides in Austin at random times and locations, a contrast to a month earlier when high demand left him unable to get a ride home; Loomis frames this as evidence Tesla is building a real, usable service rather than just accumulating a vehicle count.
4. **Unsupervised Robotaxi struck plastic ballards in Austin:** A user (Abanu Yadav) filmed an unsupervised Robotaxi making a right turn and driving through a set of plastic ballards; no damage to the car or injuries were reported, but it is described as possibly the first instance of a Robotaxi hitting a physical object rather than a bollard/post at a parking spot. Loomis calls this a solvable software edge case but notes that scaling fleet size 10x without fixing such issues would multiply the number of incidents proportionally.
5. **Waymo receives CPUC approval to expand in California:** Waymo announced approval to expand its autonomous ride-hailing service across the San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles, and to bring service to Sacramento and San Diego, with a gradual rollout. Loomis views Waymo's expansion as broadly positive for the autonomous ride-hailing industry and, by extension, not necessarily negative for Tesla, arguing the real competitive question is business model and pricing rather than raw vehicle counts.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD credited with multiple safety saves:** The video shows viewer-submitted clips of Tesla FSD (supervised) avoiding collisions, including a Cybertruck stopping in a red-light-runner scenario in about a quarter of a second, an Ontario, Canada incident where FSD stopped for a car running a red light despite sun glare, and Loomis's own account of his Cybertruck avoiding a multi-car spinout on a highway. Loomis emphasizes that FSD "doesn't get nervous" and avoids the overcorrection risk that can cause secondary accidents, while noting the system remains supervised.
2. **FSD as a demand driver — used Tesla prices rising:** The video cites examples of used Tesla Model 3s (one with about 28,000 miles, described in the transcript as "2.8,000 miles") priced roughly 14% above new-vehicle pricing, which Herbert and Loomis interpret as evidence that FSD capability is now supporting resale values; Loomis adds that Tesla has mostly raised prices and reduced incentives through the year (rather than cutting them), which has drawn less media attention than prior price cuts.
3. **Tesla's rationale for not advertising FSD:** Herbert and Loomis reference a prior interview in which Lars Moravy explained Tesla's view that advertising can't convince skeptics that a car can drive itself — people need to experience it directly — so Tesla's strategy is to let Robotaxi deployment build organic trust rather than market FSD directly.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla tops "most American-made car" rankings:** Per the 2026 Kogod Made in America Auto Index, seven Tesla models placed in the top 10, with Tesla's top model at 84% domestic parts content versus 63% for the next-closest manufacturer; Tesla Cybertruck is cited at 65% US/Canada parts content versus 45% for the Ford F-150. Herbert and Loomis criticize a Wall Street Journal article framing Ford and GM as competing for the "most American" title without mentioning Tesla, which led Tesla executives (referenced in the transcript as Katie Miller and a Cybertruck program lead) and Elon Musk to publicly push back.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster unveiling reportedly imminent:** According to a report from The Information citing three sources, Tesla's long-delayed Roadster could be unveiled this month with a demonstration involving cold-gas thrusters (described as SpaceX-powered) enabling the car to hover, plus a stunt involving the car driving upside down on a ramp. Tesla's website reportedly states a range figure of 620 miles for the Roadster. Both hosts speculate the demo timing could be tied to messaging around Tesla-SpaceX collaboration, though this is explicitly framed as speculation.
