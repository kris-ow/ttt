## Robotaxi
- NHTSA's Special Order (signed Sept 10) demands Tesla justify under oath how the pedal/mirror-free Cybercab complies with FMVSS 135, with a 21-question list due Sept 30; penalties reach $139M for incomplete/untruthful answers and up to 15 years imprisonment for falsification, plus a daily fine up to ~$28,000. The order explicitly probes why Tesla self-certified rather than pursuing Waymo's Part 555 exemption route (capped at 2,500 units/year).
- Texas robotaxi registrations grew from 186 to 439 over a month but growth has now stalled: only 7 added in the most recent week, with Cybercabs at 49-58 depending on the day (Sawyer Merritt). Estimated ~300 of 439 vehicles are driving driverless, per tracker operator Ethan McKenna.
- A Cybercab ride in Austin took 70 minutes instead of ~10 after the vehicle avoided highways and at-grade rail crossings, per Electrek — fleet-wide surface-street-only routing, not a Cybercab-specific issue.
- Ashok Elluswamy said the next V15 milestone, expected "next month or so," would enable 24/7 robotaxi operation, up from the current ~6am-10pm window; unsupervised miles remain at the 1 million figure disclosed Sept 3, growing roughly 10-20%/week depending on source.
- Tesla brought Cybercab to China for a static showcase (Beijing, Shanghai) explicitly excluding sales or driverless ride-hailing — described by Electrek as a showroom-traffic tactic given regulatory barriers there.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison said the agency is drafting a performance-based AV safety standard rather than a design-based one requiring physical controls, calling it "a scalpel" not "a hatchet" — a structural tailwind for the wheel/pedal-free Cybercab even as the Special Order proceeds. Five FMVSS amendments are proposed alongside a new SAE-partnered consortium, Ascend.
- Electrek reported Tesla filed a record 236 driver-assist crash reports to NHTSA in July (up from 209 in June), including four fatal crashes across Maryland, California (x2), and Minnesota, all marked "Verified Engaged" with narrative, software-version, and operating-area fields redacted — and Tesla's format logs only one fatality per multi-fatality crash, undercounting the true toll.
- Zurich Insurance became Australia's first insurer offering FSD discounts, citing Tesla data of 132M+ km with 7x fewer collisions than average; Tesla separately reported FSD Supervised vehicles there had 40% fewer collisions than manual driving (one per 1.1M miles vs. 665,000).
- FSD 14.3.10 rolled out with release notes unchanged from the prior build, a minor/incremental update; V15 — Tesla's "large model" (~10B parameters vs. v14's ~1B) — remains the key catalyst, targeted for "hopefully by the end of this year."

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 14 has slipped repeatedly (Sept 22 → Sept 28), targeting SpaceX's first full orbital insertion and first payload deployment: 26 Starlink V3 satellites (~1 Tbps each, ~26 Tbps total), plus reuse of two recovered heat-shield tiles for the first time. No catch attempt planned; Musk puts tower-catch odds at 50-60% for Flight 15.
- A separately recovered ship ("Ship 40") survived re-entry and ocean splashdown intact in the Indian Ocean and was towed ~600 miles, yielding landing-guidance data that validates tower-catch precision.
- Musk and ARK's Cathie Wood floated a speculative $10 trillion/year Starship revenue scenario by 2030 (10,000 flights/year at ~$1B/launch); Musk called it "not impossible" though this conflicts with his separately stated ~$1 trillion 2030 SpaceX revenue figure — an unreconciled discrepancy across his own statements.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX CFO Brett Johnson disclosed a new AI compute/hosting deal worth $1.1B/month (~$13B ARR) starting Dec 1, customer unnamed, reaffirming a ~$100B ARR target by year-end (total compute run rate estimated near $41B/year by December).
- Grok 4.7 (2.1T parameters) graded by Musk as merely on par with Anthropic's Opus 5.0 — a step down from his August claim it would "exceed all current models" — and shipped late against his own 10-day target due to RL reward-shaping issues. Grok 4.8 is finishing base training this week; Grok 5 (6-10T parameters) is positioned as Tesla's AGI attempt.
- SpaceX confirmed acquiring turbine maker APR Energy to bring blade/vane casting in-house, which ARK's Daniel Maguire says could pull the AI compute buildout forward ~18 months toward ~10GW by end of next year; Musk said he's "highly confident" SpaceX will launch Nvidia Vera Rubin-based AI computers in space next year.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk declined to rule out a Tesla-SpaceX merger at the All-In Summit, telling Freedberg "who can imagine what action one might take" given the firms' collaboration — a shift from his more guarded Q2 answer, though no terms, timeline, or filing exist. Analyst estimates of announcement odds this year range around 20-30%.
- SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 weighting more than doubled at close, estimated to trigger $15.5B-$22B in programmatic index-fund buying — one of the largest single-day passive flows of the quarter.
- Barron's writer Al Root published a reversal on SpaceX, writing "we were wrong about SpaceX" as revenue "accelerated faster than anyone expected."
- SPCX fell on a mid-week share unlock (~328M shares, ~15% of float), the second such unlock in two weeks, with another expected roughly 10 days out.

## Financials
- Morgan Stanley raised its Tesla bull-case target to $840 (from $820, base case unchanged at $400), citing autonomous Semi software economics estimated at $12,000-$18,000/truck/month versus ~$100/month for consumer FSD today; one commentator called the per-truck estimate too high and the 82,000-unit 2040 deployment assumption too low.
- Tesla's US EV market share rose to 52% YTD through August (from 43% a year earlier), but Tesla's US sales are down 16% while the overall EV market fell ~30% — share gains reflect faster pullbacks from Ford, GM, Honda and VW, not Tesla growth.
- SpaceX won a $946 million NASA contract covering launch, in-orbit, and return/recovery operations for three missions, including a lifeboat capability.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla reopened Roadster reservations at $50,000 ($5,000 upfront, $45,000 wire due within 10 days) ahead of the October 1 Waco unveil — the second time Tesla has collected this deposit for a car unshipped after nearly nine years, per Electrek. Musk has teased a SpaceX cold-gas-thruster hover demo; Polymarket puts under 5% odds of 2026 delivery.
- Tesla's Nevada Semi factory (design capacity 1,000 trucks/week) opens Sept 24, with current output estimated at only 10-20 units/week; IMC Logistics ordered 50 Semis for California drayage, and Tesla will operate public Megachargers at three Forum Mobility depots (30 MW added).
- Tesla officially unveiled its European Semi variant: 342 miles (550 km) range, camera-based mirrors, ~1.6 kWh/mile efficiency versus 1.8 for the US version — over a 10% gain a Morgan Stanley analyst says likely reflects new underlying architecture.

## Bear Case of the Week
- NHTSA's Special Order demands Tesla justify Cybercab's FMVSS compliance under oath by Sept 30, with penalties up to $139M and criminal exposure up to 15 years for falsification — the most consequential regulatory escalation yet against the robotaxi program.
- Electrek documented a record 236 Tesla driver-assist crash reports to NHTSA in July, including four fatal crashes, all "Verified Engaged" with narrative and software-version fields redacted; Tesla's reporting format also undercounts multi-fatality crashes (5 deaths logged as 2-3).
- Texas Cybercab registration growth has essentially stalled — 49 to 58 vehicles over the week, after a single-day jump around launch — far short of bull-case scaling scenarios.
- Grok 4.7 graded by Musk as merely on par with a competing model, a downgrade from his own August claim, and shipped late — undercutting the AI-compute bull narrative underpinning SpaceX's valuation story.
- Starship Flight 14 has slipped twice in one week (Sept 22 → Sept 28), the latest in a recurring pattern of schedule slippage on SpaceX's next major orbital milestone.
