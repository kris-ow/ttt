This is a weekend livestream/Q&A from investor-commentator Jo Bhakdi previewing a volatile earnings week for SpaceX, Meta, and Tesla, set against a broader momentum-stock selloff driven by rising bond yields and hedge fund AI-position unwinding. Much of the video is off-topic political and personal chat with viewers; the substantive Tesla/SpaceX content covers earnings expectations, a major SpaceX share unlock, and renewed merger speculation.

## Financials
1. **Market-wide AI selloff tied to bond yields, not company fundamentals:** Bhakdi attributes the prior week's selloff — the second-worst for momentum stocks in recent years, after a worse one in July — to a CPI-driven spike in 10-year Treasury yields, which pressured debt-financed AI buildouts (coreweave, hyperscalers) and triggered a hedge-fund unwind; he cites Goldman Sachs prime-brokerage data showing hedge funds' net AI exposure has turned net negative over the past two years. He says this dynamic has nothing fundamentally to do with Tesla or SpaceX's business.
2. **Tesla showed relative strength versus the market during the selloff:** Over the five-day period, the NASDAQ fell 3.71% while Tesla fell only 4.6% (vs. Bhakdi's calculated beta-implied decline of roughly 7.5%), which he characterizes as Tesla holding up notably better than its typical volatility would suggest; SpaceX, by contrast, fell about 9%, which he says sold off harder than it should have.
3. **Earnings calendar shuffled, fueling merger speculation:** SpaceX earnings were moved up to Tuesday (from a date roughly two weeks later), landing one day before Tesla and Meta report Wednesday; Bhakdi says this unusual scheduling has triggered speculation about a possible Tesla–SpaceX merger announcement, and he says he would not bet against merger news being announced at any point through year-end, though he notes (relaying a comment attributed to Elon Musk) that an earnings call itself is reportedly not considered the venue for such an announcement.
4. **Options positioning:** Bhakdi says he is avoiding selling calls on either Tesla or SpaceX and avoiding selling puts on Tesla through earnings given the elevated two-sided risk; he describes holding a modest, inexpensive position in Tesla November call options purchased ahead of earnings, which he plans to sell before Tesla reports to avoid a volatility hit.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Major share unlock imminent:** Bhakdi says SpaceX faces a large unlock event — roughly 1.3 billion shares, representing about 40% of the float, newly timed to follow Q3 earnings by two days (around Thursday), plus a second tranche of about 328 million shares shortly after, for a combined total he puts at roughly 1.6 billion shares (about half the total float) unlocking within the week. He expects this to be a strongly negative mechanical catalyst causing a selloff even if earnings themselves are well received, predicting a rally on earnings day followed by a selloff into the unlock.
2. **Bullish on SpaceX earnings based on data center buildout economics:** Bhakdi expects SpaceX to report "crazy" revenue growth tied to its data-center buildout (referencing Macrohard/related gigawatt-scale projects), reasoning that if roughly 450 megawatts of capacity produced around $26 billion in additional annualized revenue, scaling toward 2 gigawatts could in theory produce a much larger run-rate; he explicitly frames this as a rough, speculative extrapolation ("in theory... might be talking about") rather than guidance, and separately speculates SpaceX could report an earnings run rate as high as $50 billion, which he says would still value the company around a 40x multiple given current market cap levels.
3. **Tesla–SpaceX merger scenario discussed as a real upside risk to Tesla's stock:** Bhakdi argues that if a merger occurs, SpaceX would need to acquire Tesla at a premium of 30%+, creating asymmetric upside risk (he floats Tesla potentially jumping to $400–450) that he believes is not currently priced into Tesla shares; he frames this as his own conviction/speculation, not confirmed news.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab deployment seen as Tesla's key near-term stock catalyst:** Bhakdi says Tesla earnings themselves likely hold few surprises, and that Tesla's stock trajectory depends heavily on Cybercab fleet scaling rather than the earnings print — citing scenarios where deploying around 500 Cybercabs by December could support a move toward $400–420, while scaling to around 2,000 could support a move toward $500; failure to scale leaves the stock range-bound. He does not expect Tesla to make specific numerical deployment announcements during the earnings call itself.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Secondhand claim about Optimus progress:** A viewer (identified by a handle) relayed secondhand that a tech-entrepreneur contact who has seen Optimus in person described upcoming progress as "unbelievable" and expects it to "blow your minds"; Bhakdi says he has heard similar secondhand accounts and believes them, expressing confidence Tesla will become the leading humanoid robot company, though he says the timing of when humanoids become commercially real remains uncertain, guessing sometime next year.
