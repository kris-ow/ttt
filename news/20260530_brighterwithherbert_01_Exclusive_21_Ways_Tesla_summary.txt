This video features host Herbert interviewing Cern Basher, a chartered financial analyst and founder of Brilliant Advice investment advisory, who presents a structured analysis titled "21 Ways SpaceX Drives Value for Tesla." The central thesis is that regardless of whether a Tesla-SpaceX merger occurs, SpaceX is already functioning as a significant growth driver for Tesla across energy sales, chip manufacturing, connectivity infrastructure, talent sharing, AI development, and entirely new business categories. The discussion frames Tesla and SpaceX as converging around common mega-themes — energy, compute, autonomy, robotics, manufacturing, connectivity, and AI — and argues that SpaceX's success expands the total addressable market in which Tesla can compete.

## Energy

1. **SpaceX as a Major Megapack Customer:** SpaceX has already become a substantial buyer of Tesla's energy products. Basher cites approximately $500 million in Megapack purchases by SpaceX in 2025, up from just under $200 million in 2024. This demand is driven by SpaceX's need for stable power at AI data centers, where power fluctuations are a critical problem that Megapacks can moderate. As XAI continues scaling compute on Earth and SpaceX expands to additional launch sites (with Louisiana mentioned as a rumored location), Megapack demand is expected to grow further. Basher also notes Meta recently purchased Megapacks, suggesting broader data center adoption of Tesla's energy storage products.

2. **Resilient Infrastructure Bundle:** Basher describes a potential "resilient infrastructure bundle" combining Starlink for communications, Megapack for energy storage, solar for generation, Tesla vehicles for transport, Tesla Semi for logistics, Optimus for labor, Grok for operational intelligence, and SpaceX orbital data centers for AI compute. He argues no other company on the planet can offer even half of this bundle, making it uniquely valuable for governments, military bases, hospitals, utilities, and disaster response scenarios.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **SpaceX as Cybertruck Customer:** SpaceX purchased approximately $131 million worth of Cybertrucks in 2025, and a large fleet is visibly deployed at Starbase. Basher dismisses the narrative that this was SpaceX "bailing out" Cybertruck sales, arguing the Cybertruck is simply the right vehicle for SpaceX's needs, and that Cybertruck demand is recovering independently as prices have been lowered and production is ramping. He acknowledges that $131 million is not a financially material number for Tesla but notes the relationship could grow as SpaceX expands to new launch sites.

2. **Tesla Semi as Future SpaceX Logistics Vehicle:** Basher identifies Tesla Semi as a future high-volume product for SpaceX, which has extensive logistics needs moving rocket components, engines, satellites, and manufacturing materials between facilities in Texas, Seattle, Florida, and potentially new sites. He suggests SpaceX could eventually become a larger Semi customer than Pepsi as SpaceX scales its manufacturing and satellite deployment operations.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Grok as the Reasoning Layer Above FSD:** Basher distinguishes between Tesla's FSD stack — which handles physical-world navigation and decision-making — and the reasoning layer that sits above it. He argues Grok (developed by XAI, now backed by SpaceX) can serve as the general reasoning and conversational interface for Tesla vehicles, enabling passengers to interact with the car about its behavior, route decisions, maintenance, charging, and FSD actions in natural language. He notes FSD is currently not built for this type of conversation, and Grok fills that gap.

2. **Grok Accelerating FSD and Cybercab Development:** Basher references a Tesla AI VP (identified as Yasai in the transcript) stating that Grok Build is actively being used to accelerate development of both FSD and Cybercab. He emphasizes that Tesla AI employees have effectively unlimited token usage because XAI's compute infrastructure is vertically integrated within the Elon Musk company ecosystem, giving Tesla engineers a productivity advantage over teams at other companies who must pay per-token to Anthropic, OpenAI, or other providers.

## Robotaxi

1. **Starlink as Communications Backbone for the Robotaxi Fleet:** Basher outlines Starlink's potential role as the communications layer for Tesla's robotaxi network, covering dispatch, routing, payments, fleet monitoring, remote assistance, real-time map updates, and safety alerts. He notes that Cybercabs appearing in Austin have been observed with Starlink terminals mounted on their roofs, suggesting Tesla is already moving in this direction. He references the risk of communications failures — citing Waymo experiencing shutdowns during power outages and BYD-related incidents in China where hundreds of vehicles stopped simultaneously — as evidence that redundant, satellite-based connectivity is critical for safe fleet operations.

2. **Grok as Fleet Orchestrator:** Basher and Herbert discuss Elon Musk's stated vision of Grok serving as the orchestration layer for the entire robotaxi and Optimus fleet — managing swarm behavior across thousands of vehicles and robots simultaneously, analogous to how ants or coordinated stadium crowds operate in unison. They reference Figure AI's demo where one robot seamlessly subbed out for another as an example of the type of inter-bot communication layer needed.

3. **Starlink Enabling Global Robotaxi Expansion:** Starlink currently operates in 164 countries while Tesla has Superchargers in approximately 55 countries. Basher argues that Starlink's existing international footprint — including regulatory relationships, permitting contacts, and subscriber bases — gives Tesla a ready-made infrastructure foundation and customer base to leverage when expanding robotaxi services to new markets. He draws a parallel to WeChat's integration of ride-hailing in China and suggests X could serve the same bundling function for Tesla's robotaxi service globally.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **SpaceX as the Ultimate Long-Term Optimus Customer:** Basher presents SpaceX as potentially Tesla's largest long-term customer for Optimus robots. Elon Musk has stated that off-Earth use of Optimus could eventually exceed on-Earth use, even though the on-Earth opportunity already involves an estimated 8 to 24 billion robots needed. Basher argues that Moon bases and Mars colonies are environments where humans cannot easily survive, making humanoid robots not just useful but essential — and uniquely, no competing robot company has the same privileged access to SpaceX's off-world demand. He frames this as a structural competitive moat for Tesla's Optimus business.

2. **Optimus for SpaceX's Starbase, Starlink Factories, and Off-World Operations:** Beyond the long-term space opportunity, Basher notes that SpaceX's near-term facilities — including Starbase, Starlink terminal factories, and rocket manufacturing sites — are natural early deployment environments for Optimus. He notes Tesla is already training Optimus in its own factories (including facilities in California and Nevada) and the logical next step is deployment at sister companies and then suppliers.

3. **Macrohard / Digital Optimus as a Near-Term Scalable Opportunity:** Basher identifies what he calls the single most underrated and near-term opportunity in the entire analysis: a joint Tesla-SpaceX project called Macrohard (also referred to as Digital Optimus), described as an agentic AI system that replicates what humans do on computers without requiring a human in the loop. He describes it as a blend of Tesla's FSD visual reasoning technology with Grok, capable of operating any software interface via vision rather than APIs — meaning it could theoretically learn to use legacy enterprise software the way a human employee would, without requiring custom API integrations. Basher quotes the SpaceX S-1 prospectus: "We believe Macrohard has the potential to fundamentally transform how companies across all industries are structured and operate, thereby allowing dramatic increases in human productivity and prosperity." He notes Elon Musk has indicated a product could be available by end of the current year (implying end of 2026). He draws a comparison to Anthropic, which he describes as having gone from near-zero valuation to close to a trillion dollars in four to five years, and argues Tesla and SpaceX have an equal or larger opportunity in this space.

## Market & Competition

1. **Terafab: Securing Chip Supply Independence:** Basher identifies Terafab — a joint chip fabrication initiative between Tesla and SpaceX, with Intel already announced as a partner and ASML rumored to be interested — as strategically critical for both companies. He frames it not as Tesla and SpaceX against the semiconductor industry, but as the two companies leading the industry toward faster, cheaper, more efficient chip production. He notes SpaceX's upcoming IPO is expected to raise $75 billion or more, which Elon Musk has said will fund the initial phase of Terafab. Basher speculates Nvidia could also become a capital partner given its strong cash generation. The strategic rationale is reducing dependency on external chip suppliers for the massive scaling of robotaxi and Optimus — Basher notes SpaceX is already the largest printed circuit board manufacturer in the United States through its Starlink terminal factory, demonstrating the companies' track record of vertical integration.

2. **SpaceX's Government Relationships as a Partial Shield:** Basher argues that SpaceX's critical importance to the U.S. government and military — particularly through Star Shield — creates some political protection for the broader Elon Musk company ecosystem, including Tesla. He draws a parallel to how defense contractors like Boeing or Northrop Grumman are rarely subject to existential political attacks. He acknowledges the counterargument: that the perception of a "mega-corp" combining Tesla and SpaceX could generate more political enemies and could risk Tesla's position in China, though he expresses skepticism about the China risk materializing.

3. **X and Starlink Ecosystem as Customer Acquisition Infrastructure:** Basher and Herbert discuss how the X platform (with approximately 350 million users, cited as having more visitors than TikTok) and Starlink's 164-country subscriber base represent ready-made customer pipelines for Tesla's robotaxi network — eliminating the need to acquire customers from scratch or purchase a platform like Uber. They envision bundled subscription packages combining Starlink, X, X Money payments, and robotaxi access, analogous to Amazon Prime, and note that X Money's expansion into payments and identity could become the default payment layer for robotaxi rides.

## Financials

1. **Tesla's SpaceX Equity Stake:** Tesla holds just under 19 million shares of SpaceX, representing less than 1% of the company. Basher characterizes this as a minor financial benefit — the value appreciates as SpaceX's valuation rises, but it is not strategically significant. He notes it is disclosed in SpaceX's prospectus.
