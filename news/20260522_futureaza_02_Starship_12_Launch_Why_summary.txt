## Market & Competition
1. **Starship Flight 12 Launch Recap:** The SpaceX Starship Flight 12 successfully launched at 5:30 p.m. Central time from the new Pad 2 at Starbase, Texas. This was the debut flight of the fully redesigned Starship Version 3, featuring new Raptor 3 engines, a redesigned booster with three enlarged grid fins (down from four), an integrated hot stage ring, and a new electromechanical chopstick actuator system replacing hydraulics. The booster initially lit all 33 Raptor 3 engines but experienced issues during boost-back, ending its mission in the Gulf of Mexico rather than completing a full boost-back burn. The ship lost one RVAC (Raptor vacuum) engine on ascent but continued on five of six engines, extending its burn to approximately 9 minutes.

2. **Previous Day's Abort Explained:** The prior day's scrub was caused by three compounding issues: a hydraulic locking pin on the chopstick arms that holds Starship during stacking and fueling failed to retract, triggering an automatic abort; a pressure issue with the quick disconnect arm; and a tripped sensor on the launchpad's water deluge system. The SpaceX engineering team made overnight hardware and software fixes, including welding in hardware to reduce arm motion during pin release, and updated software logic to eliminate overly conservative hold triggers.

3. **Payload Deployment and Mission Objectives:** The ship successfully deployed all 22 payloads — 20 Starlink mass simulators and two specially modified "Dodger Dog" satellites (modified V2 Starlink satellites outfitted with V3 components, solar arrays, and cameras). The Dodger Dogs successfully activated their onboard flashlight-equipped cameras and transmitted imagery of Starship's heat shield from space via the Starlink constellation — described as a key technology demonstration for future heat shield inspection. The planned in-space Raptor relight was skipped due to the engine anomaly, but the ship completed re-entry, a "flap slap" structural stress test at Mach 7, and a soft splashdown in the Indian Ocean using two (rather than the planned three) sea-level Raptor engines for the landing burn.

4. **Starship Version 3 Hardware Highlights:** Key upgrades on V3 include: Starship capable of carrying up to 100 metric tons to Earth orbit; the PEZ dispenser (payload deployment system) was "supercharged" to deploy satellites faster, with capacity for up to 60 Starlink V3 satellites; docking ports were added (two below the payload bay door, two near the bottom) to enable orbital propellant transfer between Starships; Raptor 3 engines were redesigned with an updated ignition system, increased thrust, and internal integration of sensors and feed lines, dramatically reducing weight; the booster's fuel transfer tube was redesigned for faster and more reliable engine ignition. Starship V3 stands just over 407 feet tall fully stacked.

5. **Florida Expansion and Future Launch Infrastructure:** SpaceX is constructing a second Starship launch tower at Launch Complex 39A in Florida (which has flown 126 Falcon missions since 2017), a new Starship launchpad at Slick 37, and "Gigabay" — a massive assembly and maintenance facility designed to hold 24 work cells in over 800,000 square feet — to support future Florida Starship launches.

6. **Starlink V3 and Lunar Connectivity Plans:** Starlink V3 satellites are designed to deliver 60 terabits per second of downlink capacity per satellite, described as 20 times more than each Falcon 9 delivers today. SpaceX is also exploring deploying a Starlink constellation around the moon to enable gigabit connectivity on the lunar surface using laser inter-satellite links, potentially providing hundreds of terabytes of capacity for rovers, habitats, and astronauts.

7. **Artemis Program Update:** A NASA administrator appeared on the SpaceX webcast and noted that Artemis 2 successfully sent humans around the moon for the first time since the Apollo era. NASA is already stacking hardware for Artemis 3 in the Vehicle Assembly Building and hopes to roll it to the pad before the end of 2026 for a wet dress rehearsal.
