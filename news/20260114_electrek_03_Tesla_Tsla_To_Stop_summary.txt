Elon Musk announced via X that Tesla will stop selling Full Self-Driving (FSD) as a one-time purchase after February 14, making it available only as a monthly subscription going forward — a reversal of Tesla's longstanding "appreciating asset" pitch for the software.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD purchase option ends Feb 14:** Musk posted that Tesla "will stop selling FSD after Feb 14" and that it "will only be available as a monthly subscription thereafter," ending FSD as a product purchasable with the vehicle.
2. **Pricing history contradicts past promises:** FSD's upfront price rose from $5,000 to a high of $15,000 in late 2022, with Musk claiming in 2020 that the price would keep rising as the software neared full capability and calling FSD-equipped cars "appreciating assets." Tesla reversed course starting in 2023, cutting the upfront price from $12,000 to $8,000 in April 2024 and halving the subscription price from $199 to $99/month around the same time — a combination the article says made buying upfront take nearly 7 years to break even versus subscribing.
3. **Low take rate cited as context:** The article references Electrek's prior reporting suggesting FSD's take rate (percentage of buyers opting for the software) is in the "low double digits," and speculates the subscription-only move could push long-term owners to subscribe now, aiding Tesla's cash position this quarter — a quarter the article says is expected to be weak due to lost subsidies.
4. **Competitive pressure from rivals:** Rivian recently launched its own driver-assist competitor, Autonomy+, priced at $50/month or a $2,500 one-time fee; NVIDIA launched an open-source platform for automakers (starting with Mercedes-Benz) to build FSD competitors; and several Chinese automakers reportedly offer similar systems for less, or bundled into vehicle price.

## Financials
1. **Liability framing:** The article argues that under a subscription-only model, customers pay only for that month's Level 2 driver-assistance functionality and cannot claim they were promised unsupervised self-driving, which the article says limits Tesla's ongoing liability exposure (while not affecting liability already incurred from past FSD sales) — this liability framing is the author's analysis, not a company statement.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the move "the final nail in the coffin" for Tesla's "appreciating asset" narrative, arguing it effectively amounts to Tesla acknowledging FSD is a service rather than an asset tied to the car, and that Tesla is detaching itself from a promise it has "consistently failed to deliver." A reader comment (not independently verified by the author) speculates the change is also motivated by Musk's compensation package target tied to reaching FSD subscription milestones, and predicts further price cuts.

Notable quotes and data:
- Musk via X: "Tesla will stop selling FSD after Feb 14. FSD will only be available as a monthly subscription thereafter."
- Musk, 2020: "The FSD price will continue to rise as the software gets closer to full self-driving capability with regulatory approval."
- FSD upfront pricing: $5,000 (early) → $10,000 → $12,000 → $15,000 (late 2022) → cut to $8,000 (April 2024).
- FSD subscription pricing: cut from $199/month to $99/month (April 2024).
- Rivian's Autonomy+: $50/month or $2,500 one-time.
