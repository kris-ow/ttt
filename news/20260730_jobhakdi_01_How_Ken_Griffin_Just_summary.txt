## Market & Competition
1. **Alleged Citadel-engineered squeeze on a leveraged fund:** Per Bhakdi's own theory (explicitly framed as his own conclusion/thesis, not confirmed fact), Citadel's Ken Griffin made bearish Fed-rate comments before a Fed meeting that triggered a selloff, forcing a leveraged hedge fund (~4x leverage on a reported $20 billion) to liquidate its largest holdings — Nebius, Micron, SanDisk, Bloom Energy, and Korea-linked positions — creating what he calls a "technical glitch" sell-off unrelated to fundamentals; he estimates Griffin could have made roughly $1–2.5 billion buying the liquidated positions at depressed prices, though he stresses the exact transaction size is unknown.
2. **Bhakdi's own trading response:** He held a short put position in Micron that he had to roll over (from an 850 strike down to 780) due to the volatility, expects to break roughly even for the week, and has since sold aggressive puts on Micron (strikes around 850–900) and added Nebius exposure, betting the sell-off was technical rather than fundamental and that these stocks won't retest their lows; he states an 80–90% confidence level that no major renewed drop occurs in the near term.
3. **AMD viewed as a distant #2 to Nvidia:** Responding to a question about AMD's new AI rack, Bhakdi argued AMD is a serious but still-behind competitor — strong on GPUs and racks but lacking Nvidia's scale advantages across networking systems and full AI-factory integration.
