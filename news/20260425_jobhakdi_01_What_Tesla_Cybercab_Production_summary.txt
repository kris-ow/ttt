This video by Jo Bhakdi reacts to Tesla's announcement — made during or around the Q1 earnings call — that mass manufacturing of the Cybercab has officially begun at Giga Texas in Austin. The core thesis is that the start of production-level Cybercab manufacturing (visually distinguishable by a glossy finish versus earlier matte prototypes) represents a genuine inflection point in Tesla's robotaxi scaling trajectory, and that the bears and skeptics are missing what is hiding in plain sight. Jo Bhakdi argues that the combination of Cybercab rolling off the production line without steering wheels, FSD already being deemed safe enough by Elon Musk, and a plausible deployment math toward tens of thousands of units by year-end makes Tesla extremely bullish heading into Q2 2026.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab Mass Production Has Officially Begun:** Jo Bhakdi highlights that as of Wednesday (around April 23, 2026), Tesla officially started mass manufacturing of the Cybercab at Giga Texas in Austin — transitioning from production pilots to real, mass-manufacturable units. The most visible signal of this transition is the finish: earlier prototype versions were matte, while the new production units are glossy. Drone footage captured by Joe Tegtmeyer at 1:00 p.m. on April 23rd in the Giga Texas outbound lot shows multiple production-spec Cybercabs in the parking lot with people gathered around them.

2. **No Steering Wheel Signals Robotaxi-Only Intent:** Jo Bhakdi systematically addresses three camps of interpretation for what Tesla will do with these vehicles. He dismisses the idea that units with steering wheels will be sold to consumers as driveable cars (calling it "slightly delusional"), and equally dismisses the idea of selling no-steering-wheel units directly to consumers. His firm conclusion is that units rolling off the line without functional steering wheels are intended exclusively for autonomous robotaxi deployment — and that the evidence is simply what is in front of everyone's eyes.

3. **Deployment Math: Tens of Thousands by Year-End:** Starting from an estimated production rate of 10 Cybercabs per day (approximately 70 per week, or roughly 210 per month in the first month), Jo Bhakdi projects that even at a conservative 50% monthly production increase, Tesla would reach over 1,000 units produced within four months, 1,000 units per month shortly after, 2,000 per month, and 3,000 per month by December — totaling over 10,000 deployed units by end of 2026. He characterizes this as "painfully slow" scaling, which is precisely the language Elon Musk used, and notes that "painfully slow" for Tesla likely means roughly half or a third the pace of the Cybertruck ramp, which itself was described as painfully slow.

4. **Planned Deployment Cities Cited:** Jo Bhakdi states that Cybercabs being produced are expected to be deployed across multiple cities, including Austin, Dallas, Houston, the Bay Area, Los Angeles, Florida, Georgia, Boston, and Chicago. His narrative suggests units will be physically positioned in these cities in May and June, then switched on for service as deployment readiness is confirmed.

5. **FSD Version Already Deemed Safe Enough:** Jo Bhakdi references Elon Musk's comments that the current version of FSD (referred to as version 14.3) has already cleared the safety bar — safety is no longer the obstacle. The remaining issues are described as convenience problems: vehicles being overly cautious, occasionally getting stuck in loops, or taking significantly longer than expected on certain rides. He characterizes these as solvable in a matter of months and notes they are improving incrementally week over week, making the path to commercial deployment a question of gradual software refinement rather than fundamental safety work.
