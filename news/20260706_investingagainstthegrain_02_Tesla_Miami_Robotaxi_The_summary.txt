This vlog-style video follows the host and his wife on a spontaneous trip to Miami where they hunt down and ride Tesla's newly launched driverless Robotaxi service, comparing it against their personal car's FSD on the same route. Most of the runtime is personal vlogging (dinner, drinks, sponsor plugs) but the core substance is a real-world assessment of Robotaxi availability, ride quality, and rollout pace in Miami.

## Robotaxi
1. **Miami Robotaxi rides without a safety driver:** The host confirms Tesla is operating Robotaxi in Miami with no safety supervisor in the vehicle, which he contrasts with Elon Musk's guidance on a prior earnings call (described as "two earnings ago") that supervised rides would run for three to four months before removing the driver — a timeline that has clearly moved faster than stated, especially since Dallas and Houston reportedly launched unsupervised as well.
2. **Extremely limited fleet availability:** The host's app booking was cancelled once with no explanation, then re-booked after a "high demand" wait loop; from his experience and an evasive answer from a Tesla remote support agent, he estimates there may be only one to a few Robotaxi vehicles currently active in this part of Miami — not enough, in his view, to constitute a real service yet, though he notes similarly slow scaling in Austin.
3. **Ride quality praised, routing questioned:** Both the host and his wife found the driverless ride smooth and comfortable, with the wife specifically praising the ride comfort and suspension of the newer Model Y hardware used as the Robotaxi versus their personal Model Y performance. However, the host noted the Robotaxi took a notably inefficient, slower route compared to his own car's FSD navigating the same destination, arriving roughly 10–11 minutes later; he speculates the routing may reflect preset "comfortable" roads or deliberate route avoidance.
4. **Age restriction and pickup/drop-off friction:** The host notes Robotaxi rules currently prevent riders under age eight (even in a car seat) from being in the vehicle, which excluded his wife and son from riding along. He also flags awkward pickup logistics — being picked up in front of a random person's house rather than at a shopping plaza — as a customer-experience issue Tesla will need to address.
5. **NHTSA steering wheel/pedal rule change cited as evidence of readiness:** The host references a recent NHTSA decision no longer requiring steering wheels or pedals in these vehicles, arguing this shows regulators and Tesla's own software now consider the vehicle capable enough that such hardware is unnecessary — he speculates this regulatory shift may be timed to support the Cybercab rollout, alongside continued use of driver-hardware Model Ys for families needing multi-passenger rides.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Referenced "death loop" bug from prior earnings call:** The host recalls Ashok Elluswamy and Elon Musk discussing on a prior earnings call a known issue where the vehicle repeatedly attempts a route it doesn't "remember" is closed (e.g., due to a parade or road closure), looping ineffectively — characterized as a memory/mapping limitation rather than a fundamental driving capability problem.
2. **Anticipated near-term updates:** The host expects more clarity on Robotaxi scaling and city rollout plans from an unspecified Tuesday event referenced as being led by Lars Moravy, as well as from Tesla's upcoming earnings call, and speculates an additional city launch could occur before then.
