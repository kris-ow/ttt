EVgo, a public fast-charging provider, is scaling up installation of Tesla's NACS charging connectors across its network, citing rapid industry-wide adoption of the standard.

## Market & Competition
1. **EVgo's NACS rollout plan:** After a 2025 pilot installing nearly 100 NACS connectors across 22 major metro areas, EVgo plans to have more than 500 NACS connectors installed by the end of 2026 at existing and new sites, typically in groups of two to four per location with room to expand based on demand.
2. **NACS becoming the North American default:** EVgo states that more than 80% of new EVs sold in North America are expected to be NACS-compatible by 2030, and more than 35 NACS-equipped models are expected to be on US roads by the end of 2026. EVgo plans to expand NACS stalls in markets including Austin, Houston, Las Vegas, Orlando, Phoenix, Chicago, Dallas, Detroit, and San Francisco, aiming to offer NACS charging in more than 25 states by the end of 2026.
3. **Driver experience features:** Tesla drivers and other eligible NACS vehicles can use EVgo's Autocharge+ feature to start charging automatically without an adapter; most CCS drivers can already use the feature. EVgo says its NACS hardware, tested at its Innovation Lab, uses liquid-cooled cables for high-power charging, and that it is already seeing higher NACS throughput as more compatible vehicles join its network.
4. **Company scale:** EVgo currently operates more than 1,100 DC fast-charging stations across 47 states.

## Editorial Stance
The author (electrek's Michelle Lewis) cautions that connector counts don't always translate into real-world charging availability, noting that what matters most to drivers is whether connectors reliably deliver power when needed, and flags that balancing headline stall counts against actual output and uptime will be an essential test as Tesla and non-Tesla drivers increasingly share the same charging sites.
