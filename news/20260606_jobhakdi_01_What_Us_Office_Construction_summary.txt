This video by Jo Bhakdi presents a bullish investment thesis on SpaceX as a business, independent of the Tesla-SpaceX merger question. The core argument is that the explosive, measurable growth in U.S. data center construction spending — now eclipsing total U.S. office construction — represents a massive and quantifiable demand pressure that SpaceX's Starship orbital AI data center program is uniquely positioned to capture. Bhakdi constructs a rough revenue projection model for SpaceX's orbital lift business based on extrapolating current data center construction growth rates.

## Market & Competition

1. **U.S. Data Center Construction Spending Reaches Record Levels:** U.S. data center construction spending jumped 34% year-over-year in March 2026 to a record $50 billion — and this figure covers only construction and real estate costs, not chips or hardware. For context, spending on data centers is up 437% since the beginning of 2021, when the annualized rate stood at $9 billion, and up 688% since the start of 2018. Bhakdi highlights that growth accelerated sharply after AI emerged in 2023 and has been on a near-straight-line upward trajectory since.

2. **Data Center Spending Now Eclipses All U.S. Office Construction:** Meanwhile, office building construction spending fell 9% in March 2026 to $46 billion — the lowest level since 2015. This means total U.S. data center construction spending has now surpassed the entirety of U.S. commercial office construction spending, a crossover Bhakdi argues would have seemed completely implausible in 2018. He presents this as a visual chart comparison of the two trend lines.

3. **Terrestrial AI Buildout Will Hit Hard Constraints:** Bhakdi argues that the 35% annual growth rate in data center construction cannot continue on Earth due to hard physical limits — scarce land, insufficient energy supply, lack of gas turbines, and public opposition. He frames these constraints not as a reduction in demand, but as demand pressure that cannot be satisfied on the ground, creating an opening for SpaceX's orbital data center solution.

4. **SpaceX's Orbital AI Data Center Thesis as the "White Knight":** Bhakdi's central thesis is that SpaceX and Starship represent the escape valve for capital that cannot be deployed into terrestrial data center construction. He argues that space offers effectively unlimited real estate and energy for AI compute infrastructure, and that companies like Google, Amazon, and Oracle will ultimately need to direct data center construction budgets toward SpaceX's orbital platforms and satellite launches.

5. **ARK Invest's SpaceX Revenue Projection Framed as Conservative:** Bhakdi references ARK Invest's projection that SpaceX will generate $300 billion in revenue by 2035 — which he attributes specifically to Starlink revenue. He argues this figure will be dwarfed by data center construction budgets alone flowing to SpaceX, projecting those construction-related revenues could reach $600 billion by 2035, or roughly double ARK's Starlink estimate.

6. **Starship Commercial Readiness Expected in 2027:** Bhakdi expresses confidence that Starship will be commercially ready in 2027, noting SpaceX is expected to raise $75 billion in 2026 and direct the majority into Starship mass manufacturing. He anticipates hundreds of launches per day and tens of thousands of Starships by the early 2030s, describing it as "the biggest operation ever." He states that by 2028, the competitive dynamic will be essentially decided ("game over").

## Financials

1. **Projected SpaceX Orbital Lift Revenue Model:** Bhakdi presents a rough, self-described "raw" revenue projection for SpaceX's orbital lift business derived from the data center construction spending trajectory. His model assumes SpaceX captures an additional 10% of the total U.S. data center construction market per year, eventually reaching up to 80% market share. The resulting projections are: approximately $700 million in 2027, ~$10 billion in 2028, ~$24 billion in 2029, ~$50 billion in 2030, ~$90 billion, ~$152 billion, and ~$246 billion in subsequent years. He notes the $50 billion figure for 2030 would already represent roughly half of Tesla's total revenue at that time.

2. **Revenue Attribution and Methodology Caveats:** Bhakdi clarifies that his construction cost figures include satellite construction elements such as radiators and solar panels but explicitly exclude GPUs, compute racks, and chips. He assumes SpaceX captures roughly half of the construction-related revenue (with the other half going to satellite construction partners), though he acknowledges orbital lift's share may actually be larger than half since GPU/rack costs are excluded from the construction statistics entirely. He presents the model as illustrative of magnitude rather than a precise forecast.

3. **Philosophical Framing — Intelligence as Infinitely Scalable Economic Value:** A significant portion of the video is devoted to Bhakdi's argument that AI-generated intelligence is the first product in human history with effectively infinite economic demand, because intelligence is the means by which any desired future state can be engineered. He argues this means capital formation around AI compute can sustain a 35% annual growth rate indefinitely in terms of desire to spend, even if physical constraints limit actual deployment on Earth — and that this demand pressure will redirect capital to SpaceX's orbital platform.
