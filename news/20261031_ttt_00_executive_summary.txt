## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Lars Moravy (Munro Live) says Cybercab competition "comes down to cost per mile": 25 assembly stations vs. 5-10x more for Model Y, 78% automation, no paint shop, and a goal of building thousands of units per week; he says the line is currently running ahead of robotaxi rollout.
- Cybercab designed for at least 500,000 miles of service life; battery lead Robbie Schumpf reportedly graded the Cybercab pack an "A-minus" for buildability versus a "D" for Model Y's and "B-minus" for Cybertruck's.
- Texas robotaxi registrations rose from 858 last Thursday to 1,080 by Tuesday, with all new additions being Cybercabs; Robotaxi Radar says Tesla added over 470 units in October. Waymo still leads Texas with 1,259 registered vehicles (Oct 29) versus Tesla's 1,080.
- Houston service area expanded roughly 10x this week, though one analysis found the real geofence about 26% smaller than mapped; riders report mixed experiences, including smooth trips and wrong-way navigation cancellations. Cybercabs are also testing in Dallas, Atlanta, and Pittsburgh, with Jacksonville planned.
- Houston price comparison: Tesla Model Y robotaxi ride cost ~$18 versus ~$32 for Waymo on the same rush-hour route (~43% cheaper); Tesla also beat UberX (~$3/mile vs. ~$5/mile). But in Austin, UberX beat Tesla on price during both rush hours, and Waymo reportedly won recent comparisons in Tampa and Dallas.
- Robotaxi service hours extended to 6am–2am (20 hrs/day); AI lead Ashok Elluswamy tweeted about the change. New app features include trip-sharing, a rear-view camera feed, and a "Calm Mode" display dimmer.
- A blind rider described his first robotaxi trip as fully accessible, with the car honking on arrival and Grok announcing travel time via voice.
- Hosts on one show claim weekly fleet growth has accelerated to roughly 20% week-over-week, with the fleet surpassing 1,000 vehicles and projected to hit ~2,000 by year-end — described as speculative, not confirmed.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's Robotaxi safety page reportedly passed 4 million driverless miles, up from 1 million on September 3; Tesla's own NHTSA-based data shows zero injuries, airbag deployments, or pedestrian/cyclist collisions since late January, versus an estimated nine fatal accidents the company says comparable human driving would have produced — a Tesla-sourced comparison.
- Tesla's board letter disclosed 1.74 million people currently pay for FSD.
- Tesla launched its first official FSD (Supervised) referral program: successful referrals earn one month free or a $99 Tesla Credit; Sawyer Merritt reported hitting his 12-referral annual cap within an hour of launch.
- Nico Rosberg (2016 F1 champion) called his first FSD (Supervised) experience "life-changing"; Turo also promoted FSD availability on rental Teslas.

## Market & Competition
- Alphabet's "Other Bets" (includes Waymo) posted $372M Q3 revenue (+8% YoY) and a ~$2B operating loss, versus $1.4B a year earlier; year-to-date losses total ~$6B. Waymo raised $16B privately at a $126B valuation plus a new $5B loan this month, totaling ~$21B in funding this year — all pre-IPO Waymo figures.
- Waymo has covered 300 million autonomous miles across 15 cities and over 500,000 paid weekly trips as of spring; it added ~250 square miles of coverage this week and partnered with Toyota to build a bZ4X-based robotaxi fleet, with Japan testing starting 2027 ahead of US deployment. Waymo reportedly aims to double its fleet to 8,000 vehicles by year-end.
- Tesla's NHTSA special-inquiry response deadline (21 questions on pedal-free vehicle safety) passed October 30; responses not yet public. NHTSA also opened a preliminary probe October 9 into Tesla robotaxi school-zone speeding (four incidents, no injuries), decision expected around January 21. NHTSA updated lighting rules to add blue lights for driverless operation.
- Tesla is urging Australia to move autonomous vehicle trial approvals to 2027 from a planned 2029 start, arguing (per Tesla's own figures) it could save hundreds of lives and unlock over $10 billion in economic value.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX has third-party confirmation (per SemiAnalysis, cited by Brian Wang) of 10 gigawatts of data center capacity, with 20 gigawatts aspirational; Musk reportedly said power infrastructure for 20GW is feasible but chip supply remains the constraint, now calling it "not likely" rather than impossible.
- Wang says Google is leasing SpaceX compute at roughly $1 billion/month ramping in Q4, and Anthropic's spend (per its S-1) is rising from roughly $1.2B to $3.1B/month — speculative framing of hyperscalers renting finished SpaceX capacity for their own stranded chips.
- Power sources cited for the buildout include Doosan reactors, APR Energy turbines, Megapacks, and a reported 10-gigawatt Memphis site; satellite imagery reviewed in the discussion shows active construction at Colossus 2 (Memphis/Mississippi) and a new site near Starbase/Brownsville on 444 disannexed acres plus 893 adjacent acres undeveloped.
- Wang's own model projects a scenario of 18 gigawatts (10 owned, ~5 leased) generating a ~$600B run rate — explicitly his speculative estimate, not a confirmed figure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
