Electrek covered two Tesla stories: another delay to the EU vote on FSD approval, and a newly registered Tesla company at the headquarters of German machine-tool maker EMAG that may be linked to Optimus manufacturing.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **EU FSD vote pushed to December 15:** Electrek reports the EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles will not meet on December 1 as planned; a European Commission spokesperson confirmed the new date, December 15, to Reuters. Nobody has given a reason. The committee has discussed Tesla's file at two meetings without a vote. Approval needs at least 15 of 27 states representing at least 65% of the EU population. Twelve countries, including Italy last week, have recognized the Dutch approval, which Electrek counts at roughly 29% of the population; with Germany, about 47%. Per Reuters, an EU-level rejection would invalidate existing national approvals. Fred Lambert calls the slip minor but says the delays add up, and argues that safety data, not a rebrand, would help Tesla's case. Musk said on Tesla's Q3 call that he'd be surprised if EU-wide approval took longer than Q1 2027. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/25/eu-delays-tesla-fsd-vote-december-15-tcmv/)

## Humanoid Bots
2. **Tesla registers company at EMAG's headquarters:** Electrek reports Tesla has registered Tesla Machining Technologies Management SE, with €120,000 in share capital, at EMAG's address in Salach, Germany. It began as a shell company entered on August 26 and was renamed this month. Per Business Insider Germany, three people run it, including the head of HR at Tesla's Grünheide factory. Neither company confirmed a deal. The Stuttgarter Nachrichten reports, per Electrek, that around 220 EMAG employees will move to Tesla and that the deal concerns humanoid robots; neither company has confirmed this. EMAG, which cut 455 German jobs a year ago, markets machines for humanoid robot actuator parts. Lambert says the paper trail lines up with Tesla's push to mass-produce Optimus. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/25/tesla-emag-germany-machining-company-optimus/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is skeptical of Tesla's European timelines, noting a long line of missed dates and arguing that data, not rebranding, is what regulators want. On Optimus, he treats the EMAG filing as a strong circumstantial signal while noting that nothing is confirmed.
