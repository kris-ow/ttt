## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD fleet crosses 10 billion miles**, the exact threshold Musk designated in January 2026 as necessary for "safe unsupervised" driving — itself a moved goalpost, as he had previously cited 6 billion miles as sufficient. Per Electrek/Lambert, the milestone is a marketing marker rather than a functional unlock; consumer unsupervised FSD is deferred to Q4 2026 at the earliest.
- **Data collection rate has nearly doubled** since the start of 2026, reaching ~29 million miles/day by late April versus ~14 million miles/day in January.
- **Safety figures carry asterisks.** Tesla reports one major collision per 5.3 million FSD miles versus one per 660,000 miles for average U.S. drivers, but critics note incompatible crash-counting methodologies. Separately, the Austin robotaxi fleet logged 14 NHTSA-reported crashes across ~800,000 miles through February 2026 — approximately four times the human crash rate in comparable urban conditions.
- **FSD V14.3.2 is in active rollout.** Tesla's official V14.3 release notes confirm improved small animal handling via reinforcement learning on harder training examples and added rewards for proactive safety. Per Sawyer Merritt, a firsthand observation confirmed the system slowing for a small animal in the road.
- **FSD subscriptions: 1.28 million active as of Q1 2026**, up 51% quarter-over-quarter. The full-vesting milestone on the Tesla pay plan requires 10 million subscriptions — current penetration is ~12.8% of that target.

## Robotaxi
- **Robotaxi miles doubled quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026**, per the Herbert Ong analysis of Tesla's pay plan disclosures. The full-vesting milestone requires 1 million robotaxis in commercial operation — the current fleet of 663 (per RobotaxiTracker.com) represents a fraction of that target.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Figure has scaled manufacturing 24x in 120 days**, moving from one robot per day to one robot per hour (~55 robots/week). Robotics expert Scott Walter, who visited Figure's campus in January 2026, projects Figure reaches a run rate approaching 400 units/month by end of 2026, with full-year output of 3,000–4,000 units and a possible 5,000 run-rate. Brett Adcock has targeted "thousands" of bots in 2026.
- **Figure's Vulcan AI controller enables fault-tolerant operation**: robots losing up to three lower-body actuators can now maintain balance and walk to a repair station autonomously rather than falling. Fleet-level coordination allows healthy nearby robots to automatically swap in when a unit signals a fault.
- **1X opened a 58,000 sq ft NeoFactory in Hayward, California** — built in three months, 200 employees, stated capacity of 10,000 Neo home robots in 2026 and 100,000 by end of 2027. The factory received 10,000+ pre-orders in its first five days. Neo weighs ~30 kg versus 50–70 kg for competitors and uses tendon-driven actuation designed for safe human contact. 1X has already produced 17,000 Rev 2 motors at the facility.
- **Tesla Optimus Gen 3 remains unrevealed.** The original reveal was expected end of 2024, slipped to end of 2025, then Q1 2026; Scott Walter now expects fall 2026 at the earliest and possibly later. He caps 2026 Optimus output at "a couple thousand," pointing to precision component supply constraints (planetary roller screws) as the real bottleneck. Musk said on the most recent earnings call it is "literally impossible to predict" the ramp. The Tesla pay plan requires delivery of 1 million Optimus units for full vesting — currently described as being "handbuilt in a workshop."
- **Tesla's Fremont Optimus line and North Campus permit:** The converted Model S/X lines at Fremont are characterized by Scott Walter as a ~10,000-unit learning line. A new building permit for a "North Campus Manufacturing" facility is described as targeting 10 million units/year — the future scale facility once manufacturing processes are proven.
- **Competitive framing:** Scott Walter places Figure and 1X as the current US leaders in humanoid manufacturing, with Tesla, Boston Dynamics, and Apptronik in a "fight for third place" for next-gen reveals. China's Unitree produced ~5,000 robots in 2025 alone, primarily purchased for R&D readiness.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Canada launched an aggressive Model 3 pricing refresh.** A new Model 3 Premium RWD starts at $39,490 CAD (~$29,000 USD) — approximately 50% cheaper than the prior Canada entry-level Model 3. The Model 3 Performance was cut 17% from $89,990 to $74,990 CAD. Per Sawyer Merritt, the base 2026 Toyota Camry Hybrid costs only ~$1,000 CAD less, positioning Tesla as directly price-competitive with mainstream hybrid sedans for the first time in Canada.

## Financials
- **Tesla's pay plan milestone structure** (approved November 2025 with 75% shareholder support) requires: $2 trillion market cap before any vesting begins; full vesting at $8.5 trillion with EBITDA growing from $13 billion to $400 billion (~30x), 20 million vehicle deliveries, 1 million robotaxis, 1 million Optimus units, and 10 million FSD subscriptions. The plan covers ~424 million restricted shares (~12% of the company) across 12 tranches over 10 years.
- **SpaceX's January 2026 compensation plan** targets a $7.5 trillion market cap (and 1 million humans permanently on Mars for first tranche). The $1 trillion gap between SpaceX's $7.5T target and Tesla's $8.5T target is characterized by Herbert Ong as structurally deliberate, not coincidental.
- **Tesla generated ~$30 billion in cash last year.** The host argues a combined Tesla-SpaceX balance sheet, augmented by Starlink subscription cash flows, would be unmatched globally for simultaneously funding Mars, Optimus, and robotaxi programs.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla-SpaceX merger thesis is gaining institutional traction.** Dan Ives of Wedbush places merger probability at 80–90% by early 2027; prediction markets price it at 22–26% before mid-2027. SpaceX's summer 2026 IPO will feature a dual-class structure giving Elon ~79% voting control on ~42% equity, making SpaceX the structurally logical acquiring entity in any combination — Tesla shareholders would roll into SpaceX shares, making the conversion ratio the key number to watch.
- **Waymo contrast sharpens the liability argument.** Waymo operates Level 4 across 10 cities, reports 90% fewer serious injury crashes and 82% fewer airbag deployments versus humans across 127 million miles, and accepts legal responsibility for driving. Electrek's Lambert argues Tesla's unwillingness to accept liability — not data volume — is the real barrier to unsupervised deployment.
- **Robots-as-a-Service (RaaS) is emerging as the near-term commercial model** for humanoid robots. Per Scott Walter, customers contract for throughput and efficiency rather than purchasing units outright; many are currently deploying under R&D budgets even below ROI thresholds, treating it as competitive positioning for when humanoids become economically compelling in two to three years.
- **China's humanoid production scale is already substantial.** Scott Walter notes multiple Chinese companies are producing at scale — Unitree alone made ~5,000 units in 2025 — while US manufacturers are only now reaching hundreds of units per month.
