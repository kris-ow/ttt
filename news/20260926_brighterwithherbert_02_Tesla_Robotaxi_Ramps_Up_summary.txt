This episode of "brighterwithherbert" rounds up a week of Tesla and SpaceX news, centered on accelerating Cybercab registrations in Texas, the opening of Tesla's Semi factory in Nevada, a leaked Optimus Gen 3 render found in the Tesla app, mixed European FSD regulatory news, and SpaceX's upcoming Starship flight and 100,000-satellite Starlink filing. The host's overall take: everything within Tesla's direct control is progressing on or ahead of schedule, while regulatory outcomes (Europe, NHTSA, FAA) remain the open swing factors over the next 10 days.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas Cybercab registrations accelerating:** The Texas DMV registry grew from 448 vehicles on Friday the 18th to about 489 by the following weekend, including a batch of 28 units (19 Model Y, 9 Cybertruck used in robotaxi service) added Monday evening, plus further additions Tuesday and Wednesday. The fleet mix is now roughly 420 Model Y and 69 Cybercabs (used as robotaxis), up from 314 total registrations at the end of August. Per RobotaxiTracker.com-style tracking cited by the host, only a few hundred of the registered vehicles are believed to actually be carrying passengers.
2. **Cybercab powertrain production rate disclosed:** Tesla's recruitment account posted that the plant is now producing one powertrain (motor and gearbox) every 10 seconds — the first concrete production-rate figure shared for the Cybercab line, though the host stresses this refers to the power unit, not full vehicle assembly.
3. **First Cybercab using domestic cathode material:** Tesla announced the first Cybercab built with cathode material from its own Gigafactory Texas cathode plant; Elon Musk confirmed it was made from nickel cathode produced locally. The host notes cathode is the most expensive battery component and ties this to Tesla's stated target of a 20–25 cent per mile operating cost for the Cybercab.
4. **Vegas rollout groundwork laid, Belgium vote effectively delayed:** Cybercabs were reportedly staged on a parking garage roof and at venues ahead of a Las Vegas expansion; Tesla has reportedly had Nevada paperwork ready since August 20 but approval has taken longer than the expected ~30 days.
5. **JP Morgan robotaxi revenue model:** Analyst Rajat Gupta (JP Morgan, rating: neutral) projects roughly $320 billion in Tesla robotaxi revenue by 2035, with about $314 billion from Tesla-owned/operated fleet vehicles and only about $5 billion from third-party network cars — a model the host says is nearly the opposite of the "Airbnb"-style network concept many expected.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi factory opens in Nevada:** Tesla held a livestream Thursday night opening its Semi factory next to Giga Nevada, with Elon Musk appearing via recorded video (he was attending a White House state dinner) while Lars Moravy and Dan Priestley hosted. The plant spans 1.8 million square feet with a designed capacity of 50,000 trucks/year (about 1,000/week); Tesla did not disclose the current actual production rate. Deliveries began on stage to customers including PepsiCo, DHL, and US Foods.
2. **Semi range and charging network specs:** Lars Moravy said the Semi achieves 500 miles of range fully loaded (82,000 lbs) and over 600 miles half-loaded. Tesla plans over 30 megacharging stations with over 200 charging points operational by year-end, and cited uptime this year above 98%. The truck uses a new "megacollector" heat pump/thermal management system shared with the Cybertruck.
3. **Major fleet order: 2,500 Semis via Z Scale:** Tesla was named primary manufacturer for an order of 2,500 Semi tractors from Z Scale, a shipper/carrier group including PepsiCo and Microsoft; Tesla Semi's own account said this order alone will double the entire US Class 8 electric truck fleet.
4. **Morgan Stanley economics on autonomous Semi:** The host cites a Morgan Stanley note estimating a diesel truck earns its owner about $37,000/year versus about $202,000/year for an autonomous electric truck, due to roughly double the annual miles and 20% lower per-mile cost — framing Semi customers as economically motivated FSD adopters.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Gen 3 renders found inside Tesla app:** Tesla enthusiasts discovered signed 3D render files inside the Tesla Android app in late August, labeled with names like "robot close-up Gen 3" and "robot home full body Gen 3." Visible design changes versus Gen 2 include fully enclosed joints (no exposed actuators/wiring), a solid torso, thicker forearms with thinner wrists (suggesting finger actuators moved into the forearm), and matte black knees/shins replacing the prior gold paint. Tesla and Elon Musk have made no official comment on these files.
2. **Conflicting reports on Chinese Optimus supplier audits:** Chinese media reported Tesla's humanoid team began auditing suppliers Tuolu, Sanhua, and Joyson, who allegedly received Optimus component orders; two of the three suppliers (Tuolu, Sanhua) denied receiving any such notices, and Joyson declined to comment — leaving the claims unverified.
3. **Oppenheimer's Optimus timeline skepticism:** Analyst Colin Rusch of Oppenheimer visited the Austin facility and, per his report, is positive on Cybercab cost trajectory but expects limited Optimus revenue until 2029, versus Tesla's internally stated target of first commercial Optimus revenue by end of 2027 — Rusch reportedly doubts Tesla can hit the market's implied growth rate.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Czech Republic becomes 7th European FSD market:** The Czech Ministry of Transport approved supervised FSD after what it described as months of evaluation on road-sign/speed-limit response and driver attention monitoring, reversing earlier reservations. Tesla says deployment will begin soon. This brings the total to 15 approved markets worldwide (including Puerto Rico and limited China rollout) per Tesla's own count.
2. **EU-wide FSD vote likely pushed to December:** A pan-European meeting remains scheduled for October 6 per Reuters, but the host says reporting suggests this will be a discussion, not a formal vote — with the actual vote potentially not occurring until December 1st. Passage requires 15 countries covering 65% of EU population; the seven approving countries so far represent only about 12% of the population.
3. **Belgium raising speed-regulation concerns:** The host flags an upcoming Belgian report (based on one city, one FSD version from July) raising concerns about Tesla FSD's handling of speed regulation — characterized as fair criticism still pending Tesla's public response.
4. **NHTSA response due:** NHTSA's answers to 21 questions posed to Tesla are expected around Wednesday (~October 1).

## Market & Competition
1. **Morgan Stanley frames Tesla/SpaceX as a unified entity:** Analyst Adam Jonas described Tesla and SpaceX as effectively sharing technology, talent, and infrastructure toward a joint "energy into intelligence" mission, citing 71 mentions of Tesla in SpaceX's IPO documents and noting SpaceX/xAI purchased about $500 million of Megapacks and $130 million of Cybertrucks from Tesla last year for the Colossus project. Jonas also referenced Terafab, reportedly expected to cost over $119 billion to build (a construction cost estimate, not a valuation). The host reiterates there is no confirmed merger, timeline, or exchange ratio, and that Jonas's piece argues the merger rationale without formally calling it one.

## Financials
1. **SpaceX weighting jump in Nasdaq 100 rebalancing:** A Monday Nasdaq 100 rebalancing increased SpaceX's index weight from 1.3% to 2.82% due to lock-up expirations and increased free float, forcing an estimated $15–22 billion in index-fund buying, with further lockup expirations potentially pushing this higher.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 targeting Monday, September 28:** SpaceX conducted full-stack testing and a full refueling test ahead of a planned Monday launch (pending FAA license, not yet issued as of Thursday), with a 7:15 a.m. Central window. The flight will carry 26 Starlink V3 satellites, complete about six orbits over roughly 10 hours, land the ship in the Pacific west of Chile, and attempt a booster water landing without catch. This would mark the first orbital delivery of production-grade V3 satellites.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **FCC accepts filing for up to 100,000 third-gen Starlink satellites:** SpaceX's application for satellites in two very low orbits (~325 km and ~475 km) was accepted for review (not approved) by the FCC. Elon Musk stated in response to a question that per-satellite bandwidth would be about 10 terabits bidirectionally (versus the filing's stated 1 terabit target), with a path to over 100 terabits, satellite power of 250 kilowatts, and confirmed — when asked directly — that this involves a full Nvidia Vera Rubin/VL72 rack per satellite, tied to the "Starmind" compute concept; the host notes these figures come from Musk's answer, not the filing itself, and their accuracy is unconfirmed.
2. **SpaceX CFO on demand rationale:** CFO Bret Johnson said at a Goldman conference that communication with humans, robots, autonomous cars, or aircraft can't be served by ground-based solutions alone — cited by the host as underpinning demand from Optimus, Cybercab, and a future Roadster.
3. **Starlink V5 terminal and mobile V2 announced:** Elon Musk said the Starlink V5 terminal is in production, weighing 2.4 lbs (versus 6.4 lbs for V4), using half the power, providing up to 375 megabits, and positioned as a cheaper, slightly slower mass-market unit. A new Starlink Mobile V2 offers 100x the data density of V1; KDDI in Japan signed on as the first Asian mobile partner, with service expected around 2027.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok 4.7 released, Musk claims SpaceX AI ranking:** Musk said Grok 4.7 puts SpaceX's AI in third place behind Anthropic and OpenAI in agent/decoding performance per lab measurements (fourth place per a separate AI-model analysis he referenced). He said Grok 4.8 would finish training and begin reinforcement training that week, dismissed an October release date as speculation, and separately claimed that if their "second derivative" of progress stays high, SpaceX could take the AI lead within about six months — a prediction the host treats with skepticism given a prior miss on Grok 4.7 expectations.
