The article reports that US EV average transaction prices (ATP) rose year-over-year in July for the first time since December, as automakers pulled back on incentives, according to Kelley Blue Book/Cox Automotive data.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EV prices rise as incentives shrink:** The average new EV sold for $56,126 in July, up 1.2% from June and 1.6% from July 2025. EV incentives averaged $6,626, down 9.1% month-over-month and 24.3% year-over-year, falling to 11.8% of ATP from 15.8% a year earlier. Industrywide incentives averaged 6.4% of transaction price, and the overall new-vehicle ATP was $49,855, $6,271 below the EV average.

2. **Tesla prices and incentives follow the same trend:** The average price paid for a new Tesla rose to $53,891, up 1.5% from June and 1.6% year-over-year. Tesla incentives fell nearly 34% year-over-year to $5,599, representing 10.4% of Tesla's ATP, down from 16% in July 2025.

## Market & Competition
1. **Analyst commentary on pricing pressure:** Cox Automotive executive analyst Erin Keating said incentive spending eased in July but that rising sticker prices are also driven by 2027 model-year vehicles arriving with new content and features, adding upward pressure on ATPs and MSRPs even as consumers gravitate toward more affordable segments.
