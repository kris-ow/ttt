This is a live webcast transcript of SpaceX's Starship Flight 13, the second test flight of Starship Version 3, covering booster launch, hot-staging separation, booster splashdown, deployment of 20 new Starlink V3 satellites, an in-space Raptor relight demonstration, atmospheric re-entry, and a soft ship splashdown in the Indian Ocean. The broadcast frames the flight as a major milestone-heavy success, with several "firsts" achieved across booster recovery, payload deployment, and ship survivability through splashdown.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Booster performs full ascent and controlled ocean splashdown:** All 33 Raptor engines on the Super Heavy booster ignited at liftoff; the vehicle passed through max aerodynamic pressure and executed hot-staging (shutting down all but five engines) before the ship's six engines ignited for separation. The booster then completed its boost-back burn — a objective it had missed on the prior flight — and executed a landing burn using its center engines, though hosts noted not all 13 planned engines appeared to light, resulting in a higher-than-targeted landing velocity before splashing down in the Gulf within a pre-cleared zone.
2. **Ship reaches orbit-like trajectory and later achieves softest-ever splashdown:** The ship's six Raptor engines burned for roughly eight minutes to reach a "nominal orbit insertion" call-out on a suborbital trajectory toward the Indian Ocean. After re-entry — during which hosts called out passing max heating and max dynamic pressure — the ship executed a landing burn (three engines down to two, then one) and achieved what hosts described as the softest splashdown yet recorded for Starship, remaining intact and afloat, which they called a "dream scenario" for data collection.
3. **In-space Raptor relight demonstrated with longer burn duration:** SpaceX successfully relit a single sea-level Raptor engine on the ship in space for about 14 seconds, longer than a roughly 3-second relight achieved on a previous flight. Hosts described this as a critical capability for future orbital missions requiring de-orbit burns.
4. **Heat shield imaging test conducted via satellite cameras:** Six of the deployed Starlink V3 satellites were fitted with engineering cameras and flashlights intended to image Starship's heat shield after ascent, a capability SpaceX says it needs to build confidence for eventually attempting to catch (recover) the ship itself, something not yet achieved (only the booster has been caught to date).

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **First real Starlink V3 satellites deployed from Starship:** Starship deployed 20 Starlink V3 satellites — described as fully functional units rather than demonstration mass simulators used on a prior flight — via a "PEZ dispenser" mechanism, with SpaceX confirming contact and laser communication links established with all 20 satellites within minutes of deployment. Each V3 satellite is said to offer 1 terabit per second of downlink capacity, and hosts stated a Starship V3 launch of these satellites delivers roughly 20 times the constellation capacity of a single Falcon 9 launch carrying V2 satellites; a fully loaded Starship V3 flight is designed to carry up to 60 such satellites.
2. **Starlink powers real-time flight telemetry and video:** Hosts noted the existing Starlink constellation provides approximately 240 megabits per second of data rate from Starship in flight, enabling the extensive onboard camera views and real-time telemetry used throughout the broadcast and for rapid engineering iteration.
