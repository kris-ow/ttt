## Brief

- Roadster reservations reopen at $50,000 ahead of Oct 1 unveil

## Robotaxi
- Texas DMV shows Tesla Robotaxi LLC at 439 vehicles (390 Model Ys, 49 Cybercabs), up 258 in 30 days but flat over the latest 7 days — a separate Sawyer Merritt post puts Cybercabs at 58, up 9 day-over-day, indicating continued incremental registration activity.
- Ashok Elluswamy said on X (Sept 4) that the next V15 milestone, expected "next month or so," would enable 24/7 robotaxi operation, up from the current ~6am–10pm window.
- At the Sept 3 Cybercab event, Ashok said the unsupervised robotaxi fleet passed 1 million miles, up from 380,000 miles disclosed on the Q2 call — implied roughly 17% compounded weekly mile growth over six weeks, versus Tesla's own "more than 10% a week" language.
- Investors should watch Q3 earnings (late October) for whether more than 40% of V15's seven improvement tracks have merged, and whether Florida cities (Miami, Orlando, Tampa) scale beyond single-digit car counts.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD 14.3.10 began rolling out with release notes unchanged from the prior version, suggesting a minor/incremental update.
- V15, described as Tesla's "large model" (~10B parameters vs. v14's ~1B, AI4-only), remains the key near-term catalyst; Musk has said it should arrive "hopefully by the end of this year but certainly by early next year" and take safety "to another level."
- Musk said destination/parking memory — cars automatically learning preferred drop-off and parking spots — addresses what he called the single biggest cause of FSD interventions today, with critical safety interventions "extremely rare."

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla reopened $50,000 Roadster reservations ($5,000 upfront, $45,000 wire due within 10 days) ahead of an October 1 unveil near Waco, Texas — the second time Tesla has collected this deposit for a car that still hasn't shipped after nearly nine years, per Electrek.
- Musk has teased a demo using SpaceX-designed cold gas thrusters letting the car "fly" or hover, though Electrek describes the stunt car as expected to be remote-operated and not street legal.
- Polymarket pricing (thinly traded): under 5% chance of Roadster delivery by end of 2026, ~61% for 2027 delivery.

## Humanoid Bots
- A robotics-focused guest on the futureaza podcast flagged actuators as Optimus's top hardware risk, since humanoid actuators have only been custom-built in-house since roughly 2022–2023 with no long-term reliability data, versus ~20,000-hour industrial actuator norms.
- Whole-body control — coordinating dozens of actuators as a coupled system — was named the harder unsolved software problem versus balance, which the guest called largely solved.
- Data scarcity for humanoid movement training was described as a likely Tesla moat, given its decade of FSD camera-data collection has no equivalent in robotics yet; Tesla's training center is running with compute "still ramping."
- Optimus production has started at Fremont with a second factory in progress; Tesla's Optimus-related job postings (~342 cited) were framed as evidence of active scaling.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 14 targets Tuesday, September 22 — Starship's first attempt to reach orbit, roughly six orbits over a ~10-hour flight near 270 km altitude, with Pacific splashdown west of Chile and a booster boost-back/landing burn in the Gulf of America.
- SpaceX is reportedly targeting about 13 launch towers within two to two-and-a-half years, per Larry Goldberg on the brighterwithherbert podcast.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 14 will carry the first 26 Starlink V3 satellites (~1 TB/s each, 26 TB/s total) — about 10x a single Falcon 9 V2 Mini launch's capacity.
- Musk said the full V3 constellation will ultimately deliver more than 100x the bandwidth of the current ~11,000-satellite constellation; Goldberg noted SpaceX plans to launch around 100,000 such satellites.
- Gwynne Shotwell signed a new Starlink direct-to-cell deal with Japan's KDDI for broadband and voice calls on regular phones in dead zones, targeted by end of 2027.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Shotwell and Musk discussed rebranding orbital data centers as "supercompute," arguing space-based sites avoid Earth real estate, permitting, and cooling costs; Goldberg said data-center satellite launches are expected to begin next year.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- ARK Invest's Cathie Wood projected SpaceX could generate up to $10 trillion in annual revenue if Musk's goal of 10,000 Starship flights/year by 2030 is realized, based on ARK's calculation of roughly $1 billion revenue per launch; Wood called this a scenario that would make SpaceX's reported $1.75 trillion IPO valuation look like a "deep value opportunity" in hindsight. Musk called the outcome "not impossible"; Goldberg said he doubts the 2030 timeline specifically but sees the cadence achievable within about five years.
- Shotwell said Boeing has been paid more than SpaceX for its human capsule program and that SpaceX isn't retiring Dragon from the market yet.
