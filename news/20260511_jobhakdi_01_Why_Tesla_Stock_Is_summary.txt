Jo Bhakdi delivers a live stream covering his near-term Tesla price thesis, options trading strategy, and outlook on the robotaxi ramp. The core argument is that Tesla's stock returning to its all-time high of ~$490 is a technically and fundamentally driven inevitability — not a repricing event — given that both the broader market and Tesla's fundamental position are stronger now than they were when the stock last hit that level in December. He also reviews Pioneer Alpha's recent performance and introduces a new bond product structure for qualified investors.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla's technical path back to all-time highs:** Bhakdi argues that Tesla hit an all-time high of $490 on December 16th, then slid through Q1, and is now in what he describes as its strongest 33% up-run in a very long time, having bounced from a May 6th low of $385. His reasoning is purely mechanical: the broader market is at or above its prior all-time highs, and Tesla is objectively stronger as a company than it was in December, therefore the stock has a technical forcing function back to $490. He explicitly frames $490 as the near-term minimum target, not a ceiling, and says it could be reached as early as the current week or the following week.

2. **Caution above $490 until robotaxi scales:** Bhakdi strongly cautions against extrapolating the move to $500, $550, or $600. He argues that everything above $490 is, in his words, "complete randomness and arbitrary" until robotaxi proves itself at scale. He references the RobotaxiTracker.com data, noting that the unsupervised fleet stands at 39 vehicles, and contrasts this with Waymo's approximately 3,000 vehicles, arguing that cold-blooded institutional investors will dismiss the current robotaxi numbers as insignificant. He says the forcing function for a true repricing — one that would justify much higher prices — would require robotaxi scaling to hundreds of vehicles, not dozens.

3. **Waymo comparison as institutional benchmark:** Bhakdi explicitly invokes Waymo's ~3,000-vehicle fleet as the frame of reference that institutional investors will use when evaluating Tesla's robotaxi progress. He suggests that until Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi count becomes meaningfully large by that standard, the market will not re-rate Tesla above its prior all-time high on robotaxi fundamentals alone.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised fleet size and growth trajectory:** Bhakdi references the RobotaxiTracker.com data showing 39 unsupervised robotaxi vehicles currently operational. He acknowledges the exponential growth trend visible on the tracker chart but emphasizes that the absolute number remains too small to move institutional sentiment. He frames this as exciting for believers but insufficient for a fundamental repricing.

2. **Cybercab as the key catalyst:** Bhakdi states that "Cybercab is live" and that Tesla is now beginning to scale unsupervised operations, albeit delayed from earlier expectations. He characterizes the current phase as fine-tuning for convenience rather than safety — suggesting that safety is largely solved, and the remaining work is reducing the share of rides that are annoying or inconvenient to customers (e.g., getting stuck at intersections), bringing that error rate from perhaps 5–10 per 100 rides down toward 0.1 per 100.

3. **Slow growth in unsupervised numbers:** In response to a viewer question about slow growth in unsupervised FSD numbers over the prior few days, Bhakdi says he is not concerned, characterizes it as normal early-stage growth on a very low base, and notes that Pioneer Alpha is building out more sophisticated robotaxi margin and growth models that he hopes to share soon, possibly as early as the following week.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Safety vs. convenience as the current frontier:** Bhakdi expresses confidence that Tesla has effectively solved the safety dimension of Full Self-Driving, citing visible data as his basis for that belief (echoing Elon Musk's own statements). He frames the current challenge as a convenience and reliability problem — reducing the frequency of non-dangerous but frustrating ride failures — rather than a life-safety issue.

He explains that this strategy profits if Tesla goes up, sideways, or even down modestly (to roughly $435 in his example), and only loses if Tesla falls significantly — which he describes as the rare scenario requiring aggressive risk management and immediate position reduction regardless of conviction level.
