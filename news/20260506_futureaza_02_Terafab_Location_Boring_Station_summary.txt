## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Fast-Tracked in Belgium:** FSD has been fast-tracked for regulatory approval in Belgium through a process described as "rapid homologation." The host notes this may not cover all of Belgium — potentially only portions — but welcomes any expansion of FSD's geographic reach as an opportunity to demonstrate the technology to more people.

2. **FSD Quality Perception at a Plateau:** The host notes anecdotal feedback from the community that FSD has become so capable that users can no longer easily identify what has improved between versions. The host personally recounts a round-trip drive to Costco where nothing notable happened — no edge cases, no interventions — describing this as a positive sign, not a negative one.

3. **China Pauses New Robotaxi License Issuance:** On March 31st, over 100 of BYD's Apollo Go robotaxis froze simultaneously due to a server connectivity failure. In response, Chinese regulators paused the issuance of new robotaxi expansion licenses and new applications, though operators already approved are allowed to continue. The host views this as a measured regulatory response.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla Robotaxi App Launches on Android:** Tesla's robotaxi app has officially launched on Android, bringing feature parity with the existing iOS version. The host notes Android users missed the introductory pricing window (humorously referenced as $4.20) that was available at the iOS launch.

2. **Nuro/Lucid/Uber Partnership — Skepticism Warranted:** Nuro has received a modified DMV driverless testing permit in California to test Lucid Gravity SUVs as part of a three-way partnership with Lucid and Uber. Lucid has delivered 75 engineering vehicles to Nuro for testing. The vehicles use Nvidia's Drive AGX Thor compute platform. However, the host is sharply skeptical: Nuro has held a driverless permit for 6 years but only ever operated low-speed vehicles, scrapped that program, pivoted to licensing technology, and is not yet ready to launch a real service. The host questions what proprietary technology Nuro actually brings to the table. In California, the host notes there are four distinct permitting steps required to operate a real commercial service, and Nuro is far from that.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi Receives 370-Unit, $100 Million Order:** At the ACT Expo, Tesla Semi received an order for 370 units totaling approximately $100 million from three separate buyers. Updated pricing was confirmed: $260,000 for the short-range variant and $290,000 for the long-range variant. The host, citing industry analysts and professionals in electric trucking, says $300,000 is the price threshold below which electric trucking makes economic sense — putting both Semi variants in favorable territory.

2. **First Model Y Long (Model Y L) Mule Spotted in North America:** A camouflaged Model Y Long mule was photographed on I-280 northbound in what the host describes as expected timing for Q2 2026. The host had been hoping for a formal announcement by this point but acknowledges Tesla is likely holding back to avoid an "Osborne effect" on current Model Y sales. The host notes the vehicle is visually similar to a standard Model Y and the camouflage actually draws more attention than the car would otherwise.

3. **Tesla Battery Certification Feature Rolled Out:** Tesla has quietly added an official battery repair certification feature in its app. When Tesla performs a major high voltage battery repair or full replacement, a permanent, verifiable digital certificate now appears in the vehicle's service history tab, downloadable as a PDF. The host frames this as solving a major used EV market pain point — service records often disappear when vehicles change hands, making battery health a negotiation liability for sellers.

## Energy
1. **$225 Million Megapack BESS Project in South Australia:** A new $225 million Megapack battery energy storage system is under construction in South Australia, with the Kulonga battery project scheduled for completion by September 27th. Uno Ekis is the developer, owner, and operator. The host clarifies this is a deployment project, not a new Megapack factory.

2. **Solar and Wind Reach Price Parity:** Solar and wind energy now costs $54–$82 per megawatt hour, down from approximately $100 just a few years ago. New coal capacity in China costs $70–$85 per megawatt hour, and new gas capacity globally exceeds $100 per megawatt hour — putting renewables at or below parity with fossil fuel alternatives. The host uses this to rebut common arguments that renewables are too expensive or require subsidies, noting fossil fuels also receive substantial subsidies.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX Proposes $55–$119 Billion Chip Fab in Grimes, Texas:** SpaceX has filed to build a next-generation, vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing and advanced computing fabrication facility in Grimes County, Texas, approximately 2 hours from Austin. The proposed investment is listed at $55 billion with a possible ceiling of $119 billion. A public hearing is scheduled for June 3rd. Elon Musk noted this is one of several locations under consideration for what he described as the largest and most advanced chip fab in the world. The host cheekily insists the chip fab already planned near Giga Texas will be more advanced.

2. **Boring Company Announces Nashville Residential Loop Station:** The Boring Company announced its first residential loop station agreement in Nashville, serving residents of the Prime, Al Cove, and Paramount Towers. Residents will take an elevator from their building directly to a loop station, with travel times of 9 minutes to the airport and 2 minutes to lower Broadway. The host is enthusiastic about the time savings and dismisses aesthetic objections to underground infrastructure.
