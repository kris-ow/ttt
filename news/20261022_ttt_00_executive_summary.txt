## Brief

- Tesla Q3: $28.05B revenue, FSD attach rate hits 75% in North America
- Cybercab fleet up 10x in Austin in one month
- Dallas robotaxi launch next month, more cities by year-end
- Tesla quietly cuts 2026 capex guidance to $20B+ from $25B+

- SpaceX Q3 revenue $14.58B, up 161% YoY; swings to profit
- Tesla skipped top shareholder question on HW3 upgrade path

## Robotaxi
- Austin Cybercab fleet grew roughly 10x since launch a month prior, Musk said on the Q3 call; cumulative autonomous mileage cited at 3.1–3.3 million miles (~2.1M Austin, ~608,000 Dallas) per the earnings discussion, with zero injuries, airbag deployments, or pedestrian/cyclist collisions reported.
- Dallas launches next month; Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio cited as upcoming cities, with Tesla targeting 24/7 and highway operation by year-end.
- Lars Moravy said Tesla is trying to get Cybercabs to some outside customers by end of 2026, though production currently feeds the internal fleet exclusively and the commercial rationale for limited sales remains unclear.
- CFO Vaibhav Taneja's 30-cents-per-mile target is an operating-cost figure under favorable conditions, not a passenger fare, per clarification from analyst Brian (FutureAzA).
- Unsupervised Robotaxi fleet averaging ~80,600 miles/day and rising, per Tesla's new safety report site with a live mile counter.

## Autonomous Driving
- Customer-owned vehicles expected to join the robotaxi network around Q2 2027 via FSD v15, Ashok Elluswamy said — applies to HW4 only, not HW3.
- Tesla is building its own in-house AI-based mapping system to replace reliance on external maps, per Elluswamy ("we shouldn't be depending on external people's maps") — a reversal from Musk's 2019 stance calling map-dependence "a huge mistake." Electrek's Fred Lambert ties this shift to reported FSD speeding issues (55% of tested Brussels 30 km/h zones) and an NHTSA review of Robotaxi school-zone speeding.
- FSD now operates in nine EU countries; Musk said he'd be surprised if EU-wide approval took longer than Q1 2027, with a relevant vote expected around December 6.
- Tesla's earnings call skipped the top-ranked shareholder question (557 votes) on whether HW3 owners will get a free compute upgrade, per Electrek — notable given Musk's Q1 2026 statement that "Hardware 3 simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD" and a Dutch collective claim now covering nearly 7,000 HW3 owners.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus external deliveries now targeted for end of 2027, Musk said, citing unresolved hand, durability, and AI issues; early production has begun on a high-volume Fremont line designed for ~1 million units/year (~20,000/week), though reaching that rate will take a year or more.
- Tesla plans 10,000–20,000 robots for an "Optimus Academy" to generate training data, and aims to integrate its AI5 chip into Optimus around April 2027.
- A demo video showing Optimus performing non-pre-programmed tasks is expected within one to two months.
- Electrek's Lambert disputes Musk's claim that "no one is close" to Tesla, citing XPeng's IRON line (mass production targeted end of 2026) and Boston Dynamics' Atlas already deployed at Hyundai's Metaplant.

## Financials
- Tesla Q3 non-GAAP EPS $0.48 (vs. $0.45 est.), revenue $28.05B, adjusted EBITDA $3.90B, GAAP net income $787M, free cash flow $1.53B; largest passenger-vehicle order backlog in four years, with battery availability the limiting factor on volume.
- Electrek's Lambert flags that Tesla's 2026 capex guidance was quietly cut in the 10-Q to "in excess of $20 billion" from "more than $25 billion," and that without a $2.59B working-capital boost (including $588M billed to SpaceX for undelivered Megapacks, unpaid at quarter-end), free cash flow would have been roughly -$1.06B. Tesla also signed $30B in new credit facilities.
- SpaceX Q3 revenue $14.58B, up 161% YoY and 87% QoQ, adjusted EBITDA $8.81B (up 306% YoY); SpaceX guided Q4 capex to $35–40B.
- JPMorgan raised its SpaceX price target to $500 (from $240), implying a $6.6T market cap, and projects 2027 SpaceX revenue of $254B.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Elon Musk and SpaceX CFO Brett/Bret Johnson reportedly guided to $500 billion–$1 trillion in SpaceX revenue next year; Musk's own ceiling reaches as high as $1 trillion in annual recurring revenue by end of 2027 — roughly 10x the 2026 pace cited by Johnson (~$100B ARR by year-end).
- A sharp SpaceX selloff followed earnings; Jo Bhakdi attributes it to a mechanical share unlock (~1.3 billion shares, ~45% of float) rather than fundamentals, expecting pressure to clear within days.
- On the earnings call, a SpaceX executive confirmed Tesla and SpaceX have each formed special committees of independent directors to evaluate a potential merger, said he's recused due to conflict of interest, and cautioned a merger "is not inevitable" — Tesla would need approval from 50% of all outstanding shares, not just votes cast. Brad Gerstner separately estimated a potential merger share price range of $420–$550.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX reported 1.7 GW of Colossus compute capacity as of end-September 2026, targeting 13–20 GW by end of 2027 (figures vary by source); Musk says power, cooling, and infrastructure — not chip supply — are the binding constraint.
- SpaceX is developing in-house casting of gas turbine blades to accelerate power delivery, claiming a 12-18 month speed advantage over competitors; independent analysts (Jefferies, per WSJ) estimate building a single-crystal casting line from scratch takes four-plus years, casting doubt on the timeline.
- Terafab, SpaceX's chip manufacturing effort, is reportedly targeting proof-of-concept production (likely memory chips) within about six months, with interest from Intel, TSMC, Samsung, and ASML; construction at the ~3,000-acre Grimes County, Texas site is proceeding around the clock.
- Google's Project SunCatcher prototype launched on SpaceX's Transporter 18 mission October 1, testing AI chip resilience in orbit; a two-satellite follow-up targeting coordinated operation is planned for 2027.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX reported 13.6 million consumer Starlink users, with commercial segments (aviation, maritime, oil rigs) growing particularly fast.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX's next Starship flight, expected next month, aims to recover both booster and ship for the first time (on separate attempts); longer-term cadence targets are weekly launches in H1 next year, scaling to twice-weekly and eventually daily.
- SpaceX has reportedly written about building a lunar mass driver to launch AI-equipped satellites assembled on the Moon, framed as energetically practical given the Moon's lack of atmosphere and lower gravity; lunar oxygen extraction is described as the larger long-term prize for propellant production.
