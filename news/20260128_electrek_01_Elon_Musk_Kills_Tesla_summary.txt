Tesla will end production of the Model S and Model X by the end of Q2 2026, CEO Elon Musk announced on the company's Q4 2025 earnings call, citing "autonomy" as the reason; the article reports the Fremont factory line will be converted to Optimus humanoid robot production.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model S/X discontinuation timeline:** Musk said Tesla is winding down Model S/X production and will end it entirely by the end of Q2 2026. The Model S launched in 2012 and the Model X in 2015; Tesla stopped reporting their sales separately starting in 2023, folding them into an "other models" category with Cybertruck and Tesla Semi.

2. **Sales decline:** The article cites its own prior reporting estimating Model S/X deliveries fell over 30% year-over-year, with sales below 50,000 units globally by late 2024, worsening further after that (per the article's own tracking); it estimates actual 2025 Model S/X volume was likely around 30,000 units once Cybertruck and Semi are excluded from the combined "other models" figures. Tesla has 100,000 units of annual Model S/X production capacity at Fremont but has reportedly been running at a fraction of that for years.

3. **June 2025 refresh criticized:** Tesla updated the Model S and Model X with a new paint color, front bumper camera, slightly improved range, and ambient lighting (features already on Model 3/Y) — alongside a $5,000 price increase, bringing the Model S to $84,990 and Model X to $89,990. The article's earlier coverage quoted VP Lars Moravy saying the refresh would show the lineup "some love," which the author calls an afterthought rather than a substantive update.

4. **Competitive position:** The article states Lucid Air has surpassed the Model S and the Rivian R1S outcompetes the Model X, framing the discontinuation as reflecting a decade-old platform that competitors have caught up to.

## Humanoid Bots
5. **Fremont line to become Optimus production:** Musk said the Model S/X production line at Fremont will be replaced by a production line for Optimus, Tesla's humanoid robot program.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues the discontinuation was inevitable but still significant, criticizing Tesla for having "stopped caring" about these vehicles for years, calling the June refresh "embarrassing" for its price hike with minimal substantive changes (no steer-by-wire or 48-volt architecture), and speculating whether Cybertruck could be next. A quoted top reader comment states Musk has been "disinterested in Tesla as an automobile company for a while now," noting it has been nearly seven years since the last successful new product announcement.
