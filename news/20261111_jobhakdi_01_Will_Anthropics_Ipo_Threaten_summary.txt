This livestream from Jo Bhakdi covers Anthropic's imminent IPO (expected to price around November 18th and begin trading November 19th) and argues the deal's strength is broadly bullish for the AI trade, including SpaceX, while also addressing SpaceX-specific stock weakness ahead of a Starship test flight and fielding viewer portfolio questions on Tesla, SpaceX, Micron, Nvidia, and Bitcoin.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Anthropic IPO terms and scale:** The host describes Anthropic's IPO as set to raise roughly $57–64 billion in the base deal (825 million Class A shares from the company, 81 million secondary shares, plus a green-shoe option for up to 123 million additional shares), with a concurrent $26 billion private placement from seven strategic buyers including Google and Nvidia ($10 billion each) plus AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC. He says the IPO is multiple times oversubscribed and that bankers are reportedly considering raising the valuation from an initial ~$1 trillion/$1.2 trillion range toward $1.5 trillion or higher, up from a prior private-round valuation the host states was $965 billion. He notes this would make it the second-largest IPO ever after SpaceX's own ~$75 billion raise, and speculates Anthropic could ultimately surpass SpaceX's IPO total.
2. **Anthropic revenue and growth claims:** The host cites Anthropic's Q3 revenue at roughly $17 billion and a current run rate near $80–81 billion as of October 31, with some unspecified estimates projecting revenue in the hundreds of billions by 2030 (he cites a range he is unsure of, "$600, $800, $900 billion").
3. **Anthropic's compute contract with SpaceX disputed by host:** The host references reports/rumors (attributed to sales-side communications, unverified and secondhand per the transcript) that Anthropic told analysts its compute contract with SpaceX "will not be extended beyond the middle of next year." He personally dismisses this as sales talk aimed at reassuring investors that Anthropic will stop overpaying SpaceX's spot pricing, predicting the contract will in fact be extended when the time comes. He states a separate claim that SpaceX will receive $125 billion from Anthropic per Anthropic's S-1 over the next several years, and says a dedicated video on this is coming within days.
4. **SpaceX valuation and price targets (host's own view):** The host says SpaceX is "currently" around a $1.9 trillion valuation (his figure) and expects it to reach roughly $250 billion in earnings by the end of next year, implying what he calls an "eight or nine" forward P/E — a view he presents as his own bullish thesis, not a sourced fact. He sets a short-term target near $170–180 driven by SpaceX's January earnings call, which he calls a likely "bonanza," and separately cites a slightly longer 2027–2028 horizon tied to orbital AI compute coming online in 2028–2029.
5. **Possible reasons for SpaceX stock weakness:** The host offers several of his own hypotheses for SpaceX's share weakness on the day: investors rotating cash out of SpaceX into the Anthropic IPO ahead of its pricing; the unverified contract-extension rumor; test-flight jitters ahead of Friday's Starship launch; and a viewer's point that SpaceX is not in the S&P 500 so index-tracking funds must sell it to fund other allocations. He states he does not know the actual cause.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Upcoming test flight:** A Starship test flight is scheduled for Friday, November 13th, with an attempt to catch the booster (and possibly both stages). The host says he will not hold a leveraged SpaceX position through the launch but will keep his core position, estimating roughly 90% odds of a smooth outcome. He argues a successful double-catch would be historic and could boost sentiment but says he doesn't expect it to drive a major stock repricing since success is already assumed in the thesis, whereas a failure would represent downside tail risk rather than a thesis-breaking event.

## Market & Competition
1. **AI/semiconductor stocks called dramatically mispriced:** The host argues NASDAQ 100 forward P/E ratios have fallen to levels comparable to the depths of a prior market panic, and cites sharp rebounds in Micron (up double digits) and SK Hynix (up double digits) after what he calls irrational sell-offs given their cash generation and dominant position in high-bandwidth memory. He argues the market is mispricing memory-chip cyclicality risk, asserting there is no current evidence of a supply glut materializing by 2028–2030, and that Chinese competitors cannot deliver competitive HBM by 2030.
2. **Host's broader portfolio framing:** In response to viewer questions, the host says he holds core positions skewed heavily toward SpaceX and Tesla, describes buying SpaceX call options (strike $170, expiring February) as a high-conviction bet, and ranks his preference order among SpaceX, Micron, Marvell, and Bitcoin, placing SpaceX first. He states growing caution on Bitcoin, citing hacking risk and preferring SpaceX/Tesla upside potential over Bitcoin's.
