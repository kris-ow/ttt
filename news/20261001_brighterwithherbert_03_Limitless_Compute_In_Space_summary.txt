This short segment features Jo Bhakdi speculating on SpaceX's long-term plan to deploy AI compute infrastructure in space, arguing that Starship's launch cadence and satellite manufacturing scale could let SpaceX bypass the permitting and power-grid constraints that limit land-based data centers. He frames this as a multi-year scaling story running into 2028-29.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Orbital compute as an end-run around land-based constraints:** Bhakdi argues that terrestrial data centers face permitting delays, power plant construction, and turbine procurement bottlenecks, whereas satellite-based compute avoids these "permissions" entirely — he describes this as removing the main scaling constraint on AI infrastructure.
2. **Projected scaling trajectory to 2028:** He speculates SpaceX could scale space-based computing power from gigawatts to tens and eventually hundreds of gigawatts (and speaks of "terawatts" as a further extrapolation), driven by mass production of satellites via a dedicated "terrafactory" (and a corresponding solar panel terrafactory) loaded onto Starship flights. He calls 2028-29 a potential inflection point ("the end of the game") for this scaling.
3. **Dismissal of "AI bubble" concerns:** Bhakdi pushes back on the idea that current AI infrastructure investment is a bubble, asserting that "intelligence is not a bubble" as a justification for continued aggressive buildout.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship cadence as the enabler:** He states that SpaceX testing of Starlink-related satellites is already underway and envisions a future state of producing thousands of Starships and launching up to 10 times per day to deploy an "unlimited number" of satellites — though no timeline, contract, or official SpaceX source is cited for this cadence figure.
