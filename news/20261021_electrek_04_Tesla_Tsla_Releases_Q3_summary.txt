Tesla reported Q3 2026 results showing revenue flat year-over-year at $28.05 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $0.48, beating consensus but down 4% from a year ago, as gross margins improved but operating income and net income continued to decline.

## Financials
1. **Revenue and EPS beat consensus but profit still falls:** Tesla posted $28.05 billion in revenue (flat YoY, in line with the $28.06 billion consensus) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.48 (beating the $0.45 estimate but down 4% from $0.50 a year ago). GAAP net income dropped 43% YoY to $787 million, or $0.22 per share, including a $288 million unrealized gain on bitcoin holdings and a $142 million unrealized loss on Tesla's SpaceX equity investment.
2. **Margins recovered but operating income fell sharply:** Total gross margin was 19.6%, up from 18.0% a year ago and 16.8% in Q2. Operating income fell 56% YoY to $718 million (operating margin 2.6%, vs. 5.8% a year ago and 1.4% in Q2), as operating expenses rose 39% YoY to $4.78 billion. R&D spending jumped 69% to $2.76 billion, attributed to spending on "Super Intelligence" (SI), Optimus, and Cybercab; stock-based compensation (including the 2025 CEO pay package) rose to $1.17 billion from $663 million a year ago.
3. **Capex pace lags full-year guidance:** Capital spending was $4.73 billion in Q3, more than double the $2.25 billion a year ago, but down sequentially from $5.79 billion in Q2. Year-to-date capex totals $13.01 billion against management's "more than $25 billion" full-year guidance — implying roughly $12 billion would be needed in Q4 alone to hit the target, which the article says did not get updated in the letter. Free cash flow was $1.53 billion, versus $3.99 billion a year ago and negative $1.09 billion in Q2; Tesla ended the quarter with $45.4 billion in cash and investments.
4. **Auto margin ex-credits improves; regulatory credits shrink further:** Automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits was 18.1%, up from 16.3% in Q2 and 15.4% a year ago, though below Q1's 19.2% (which had one-time warranty/tariff benefits). Tesla attributes the improvement to regional/product mix and FSD subscription revenue, partly offset by lower average selling prices. Regulatory credit revenue fell 73% YoY to $113 million.

## Energy
5. **Energy revenue fell despite higher deployments:** Energy generation and storage revenue fell 16% YoY to $2.88 billion, which Tesla partly attributes to tariff-related customer refunds, even as deployments rose 10% YoY to 13.7 GWh. Segment gross margin rose to 33% from 20% in Q2, but energy gross profit still slipped to $954 million from $1.07 billion a year ago.

## Electric Vehicles
6. **Tesla cites battery supply, not demand, for flat sales:** Tesla says battery availability was "the limiting factor" on auto volumes in Q3 and that it ended the quarter with its largest passenger-vehicle backlog in four years. Deliveries fell 2% YoY; "other models" (Cybertruck, Semi, Model S and X) fell 48%. Tesla delivered 22,141 more vehicles than it produced, cutting inventory to 11 days of supply from 15 in Q2. Average selling prices fell again in the quarter.
7. **Services revenue hits record profit:** Services and other revenue grew 33% YoY to $4.61 billion, with Tesla reporting record gross profit of $734 million from charging, insurance, and parts sales.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
8. **FSD subscriptions and Robotaxi mileage grow:** Tesla says active FSD subscriptions reached 1.74 million, up 67%, though the article notes this count includes upfront purchases as defined under Musk's 2025 pay package. Tesla's Robotaxi service reached nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles by quarter-end, a month after Cybercab joined the Austin fleet.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) frames the quarter as "better... but the trend hasn't changed," noting margin recovery alongside continued profit decline, and flags inconsistencies: falling average selling prices despite a claimed battery-driven supply constraint, and a capex pace far behind the full-year guidance, suggesting either a huge Q4 spending surge or quiet slippage of the 2026 capex plan into 2027. A top reader comment (quoted, not the author's own view) sarcastically criticizes Tesla for citing battery bottlenecks after years of promises to build its own batteries.
