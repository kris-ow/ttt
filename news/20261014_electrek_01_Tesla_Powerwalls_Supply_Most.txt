Title:       Tesla Powerwalls supply most of record 745 MW California grid dispatch
Author:      Fred Lambert
URL:         https://electrek.co/2026/10/14/tesla-powerwall-sunrun-record-745-mw-california-grid-dispatch/
Published:   2026-10-14 14:46 UTC
Fetched:     2026-10-14 19:01 UTC

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Tesla Powerwalls did most of the heavy lifting as California’s home batteries set a new grid record last week. On October 6, more than 85,000 Powerwall owners sent over 600 MW to the grid, the bulk of a combined 745 MW dispatch that Sunrun announced today.

That’s 28% more than the 580 MW record the two companies set less than a month ago. And it’s bigger than most gas peaker plants in the state.

## Tesla posted its record first

Tesla Energy put out its own numbers on October 8, two days after the event. The company said 85,000+ Powerwall owners across California dispatched more than 600 MW to help meet peak demand, calling it “a new record for the largest Powerwall VPP event.”

Sunrun followed this morning with a release that has Sunrun “leading” the dispatch. It says “the companies” delivered 669 MW on October 5 and 745 MW on October 6, but it doesn’t break out how much came from Sunrun customers versus Tesla customers.

 
 
 

Put Tesla’s figure against Sunrun’s total and Powerwalls account for roughly 80% of the record. In September, the share was even higher: 517 MW of the 580 MW came from about 110,000 Powerwalls, and Sunrun owns or operates 55% of those.

So both companies are taking a victory lap for largely the same batteries. Sunrun is the biggest Powerwall channel in the country, which is why the two keep showing up in each other’s press releases.

There’s also an interesting detail in Tesla’s math. Just over 600 MW from about 85,000 homes works out to roughly 7 kW per home, well above the 5 kW continuous output of a Powerwall 2. That points to a fleet increasingly made of Powerwall 3 units, rated at 11.5 kW, and homes with more than one battery.

## How the dispatch worked

The events came during an October heat wave, at the request of the California Energy Commission and the state’s utilities. Batteries discharged through multi-hour evening peaks to cut demand and push down real-time power prices.

Sunrun says October 5 was the first event this year where the state’s Demand Side Grid Support (DSGS) and Emergency Load Reduction Program (ELRP) both called dispatches at the same time across every participating investor-owned utility territory. DSGS kicks in once CAISO’s day-ahead price passes $200/MWh.

According to Sunrun, 745 MW is enough to cover every household in San Francisco and San Jose combined during peak hours. The company now has more than 120,000 home batteries enrolled in California grid programs.

The pace is what stands out. The previous residential record, 539 MW, stood from July 29, 2025, until September 9, 2026. It grew 8% in over a year, then 28% in four weeks.

Customers get paid for this, too. Sunrun CEO Mary Powell said “customers are being compensated for helping strengthen grid reliability.”

## Next up: San Jose

Sunrun also says a Google-funded distributed power plant with PG&E in the San Jose area plans to enroll nearly 21,000 devices with Tesla and Everyday Electric, the company formerly known as Renew Home. Initial dispatches are expected this month.

It’s the first concrete piece of the 16.8 GW virtual power plant plan for data centers that Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced in June. But it’s early: Sunrun notes the Tesla and Everyday Electric part of the project is still under a non-binding letter of intent.

## Electrek’s Take

Tesla’s real advantage shows up here: a huge installed base and software that gets 85,000+ homes to respond to the same grid signal at once. Nobody else in residential storage is close.
