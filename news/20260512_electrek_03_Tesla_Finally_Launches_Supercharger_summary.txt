Tesla has launched a virtual waitlist pilot at five Supercharger locations, allowing drivers to join a queue via the Tesla app rather than physically waiting — arriving over a year behind the originally announced Q2 2025 timeline.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Virtual queue pilot details:** Drivers at capacity Supercharger stations can join a virtual queue through the Tesla app, which displays their position and wait time via a Live Activity notification. Tesla uses both vehicle and phone location to determine eligibility. The system does not technically enforce queue order — it displays a prompt asking drivers if they want to skip the queue, relying on the honor system.

2. **Five pilot locations:** Los Gatos CA, Mountain View CA, San Francisco CA (Lombard St.), San Jose CA, and Bronx NY. Four of the five are in the Bay Area, Tesla's highest-density market. The Bronx location is notable given past congestion from ride-hailing drivers at NYC Superchargers.

3. **Background and timeline:** Tesla first promised the feature following a viral fight video at a crowded Supercharger, then announced a Q2 2025 pilot launch in February 2025. The pilot is launching in Q2 2026 — one year late. A separate congestion measure, a $1/minute fee for charging past 90% at busy stations, was introduced in 2023 to address dwell time rather than wait-queue management.

## Market & Competition
1. **Non-Tesla EV access:** The virtual queue feature supports non-Tesla EVs through the Tesla app. Approximately 70% of Tesla's 80,000+ Supercharging stalls are now open to other automakers, contributing to higher station utilization and making congestion management increasingly relevant.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert welcomes the launch but criticizes the year-long delay for what is a software-only feature, notes that 1% congestion across 53 million Q1 2026 charging sessions implies hundreds of thousands of affected visits annually, and flags that the honor-system enforcement model may prove insufficient as network utilization grows.
