## Brief

- Q2 deliveries hit 480,126, best Q2 ever
- Stock fell ~7% despite beat, no clear cause identified
- First production Cybercab tests Austin roads, no controls
- Robotaxi launches unsupervised service in Miami
- Katy, TX driver charged with manslaughter in FSD crash
- Tesla caps employee AI spend at $200/week, exempts Grok

## Robotaxi
- First production Cybercab began engineering testing on Austin public roads with no steering wheel, pedals, or occupant touching manual controls.
- Robotaxi launched unsupervised (no safety driver) service in Miami, including Miami Beach and airport-area coverage; Tesla's published Miami geofence is narrow, covering only West Miami and a strip toward Doral/Sweetwater, excluding downtown, Miami Beach's core, the airport, and most of Coral Gables — a discrepancy between the launch claim and the mapped service area.
- Active Robotaxi fleet: 750 as of 2026-07-02 per RobotaxiTracker.com (Bay Area 618, Austin 105, Dallas 16, Houston 11). Texas DMV-authorized robotaxi registrations reportedly climbed past 100, up roughly 10 in a single day.
- City officials estimate Tesla's actual Austin operating fleet at only ~50 vehicles a year post-launch, with wait times routinely exceeding 15 minutes and no cars available in over a quarter of checks (per Electrek); this contrasts with the DMV registration figures cited elsewhere.
- Lars Moravy teased a "cool news" scaling announcement tied to Giga Texas expected around July 7, and reportedly put Cybercab automation readiness at 90%, saying he was surprised there weren't more problems.

## Autonomous Driving
- Harris County prosecutors charged Tesla driver Michael David Butler, 44, with manslaughter over a June 19 Katy crash that killed Martha Avila; the complaint says he had been Googling that FSD was "too timid" weeks before overriding FSD's speed and reaching ~73 mph before crashing into her home.
- Musk has said on Tesla's Q1 2026 call that safety validation, not mapping, is the bottleneck holding back Robotaxi scaling until FSD v15 arrives; independent analysis cited by Electrek pegs Austin's crash rate at ~4x the average human driver.
- A German parliamentary state secretary's response on easier autonomous-transport approval rules has fueled speculation of a surprise FSD approval in Germany within weeks, per Jo Bhakdi. Separately, Swedish authorities have reportedly recommended the EU block FSD rollout over speed-setting concerns.

## Electric Vehicles
- Q2 2026 deliveries: 480,126 vehicles, +25% YoY, beating Wall Street's ~406,000 consensus by roughly 74,000 units — Tesla's best-ever Q2 and 4th-best quarter overall; production was 451,758.
- Tesla is on track to deliver its 10 millionth cumulative vehicle in Q3 2026.
- Model Y L launched in the US as a $61,990 Launch Series AWD trim with an 83 kWh battery, 0.216 drag coefficient, PowerShare capability, and US deliveries expected September; also launched in the UAE with November deliveries.
- Giga Berlin capacity reportedly expanding another ~20% amid reports Volkswagen may cut up to 100,000 jobs.

## Financials
- Tesla stock fell roughly 7% despite the delivery beat, which multiple hosts called unexplainable by fundamentals; one described it as a possible tactical or coordinated move rather than a news reaction.
- Tesla imposed a $200/week employee AI spending cap effective July 6 after engineers were burning "thousands of dollars' worth of tokens each week"; the cap exempts xAI beta products despite Grok reportedly being unpopular among staff, who prefer Anthropic's Claude.
- Tesla Energy deployed 13.5 GWh in Q2 2026, its second-best quarter ever.

## Market & Competition
- Ford's US EV sales fell 40.7% YoY to 9,746 in Q2; Rivian delivered 12,194 (+14.4% YoY) and raised FY2026 guidance to 65,000–70,000; Lucid delivered 3,953 (+19% YoY).
- SpaceX is set to acquire Cursor parent Anysphere for $60 billion in an all-stock deal expected to close this quarter.

## Humanoid Bots
- Jeff Lutz assessed first production Optimus units and start of production could be "a month or two" away based on Moravy's comments on automation readiness and field acceptance testing at Fremont, though scaled production (10,000–100,000 units) is not yet confirmed.
- Musk reaffirmed Optimus production "will be extremely slow at first."
