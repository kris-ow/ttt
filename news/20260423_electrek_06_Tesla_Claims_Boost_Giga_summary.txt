Tesla's Gigafactory Berlin plant manager announced a 20% production increase starting in July, 1,000 new hires, and 500 temp-to-permanent conversions — but the article argues the underlying numbers reveal persistent underutilization rather than genuine operational strength.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Berlin production vs. stated capacity:** Tesla's Q1 2026 filings list Giga Berlin's installed annual capacity at over 375,000 Model Y units (~93,750/quarter). The plant's "record" Q1 2026 output of 61,000 units represents approximately 65% utilization — up from ~40% earlier in the year, but well short of capacity. Even after the announced 20% boost, output would reach roughly 73,200 units/quarter (~78% of stated capacity).

2. **European demand recovery:** The refreshed Model Y posted 33,723 European registrations in March 2026, a 117% year-over-year increase, aided by higher gas prices. Germany alone saw registrations quadruple to 9,252 units. For Q1 2026, the Model Y finished second in Europe with 51,468 units, behind only the Renault Clio. The article notes Q1 2025 was a catastrophic baseline — registrations had fallen 37% — making the year-over-year comparisons appear more dramatic than underlying trends may justify.

3. **Workforce and production timeline:** André Thierig, Tesla's senior director of manufacturing at Gigafactory Berlin, announced approximately 1,000 new hires beginning in May — a ~9% expansion from the current 11,500 employees — plus additional planned hires for battery cell production. The production increase takes effect in July. The plant has produced over 700,000 Model Y units since opening in 2022, and Thierig noted the factory's 8 millionth Tesla was recently built at the Grünheide facility.

## Financials
1. **Global deliveries and inventory buildup:** Tesla delivered 358,023 vehicles globally in Q1 2026, missing Wall Street expectations by roughly 7,600 units. The company produced approximately 50,000 more vehicles than it delivered, indicating an inventory buildup. The article flags the tension between ramping Giga Berlin output and carrying excess global inventory.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues that celebrating a "record" quarter at 65% capacity, followed by a production ramp while carrying surplus global inventory, deserves scrutiny rather than applause — and that one strong quarter of European registrations rebounding from a historic low does not validate the strategic decision to build a 375,000-unit factory in Germany. The author will be watching Q2 and Q3 European registration data to determine whether increased production is absorbed by real demand or adds to the inventory pile.
