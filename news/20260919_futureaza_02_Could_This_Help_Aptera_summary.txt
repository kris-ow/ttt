This video is an interview with Russell, founder of a small carbon-fiber manufacturing company called Carbon District, about a novel high-compaction carbon fiber production process he believes could dramatically cut costs for Aptera's body panels (currently manufactured abroad, reportedly in Italy). Tesla and SpaceX are not substantively discussed; Tesla comes up only briefly as an aspirational customer and via a comparison of the guest's material to Tesla's giga casting process.

## Market & Competition
1. **Guest pitches cost savings for Aptera via US-based carbon fiber, not Tesla:** Russell argues Aptera's sheet-molded-compound body panels — currently made abroad and shipped in low-density, high-volume containers (which he says makes shipping economically "insane") — could be produced far more cheaply domestically using his company's high-compaction (32,000+ PSI), low-polymer carbon fiber process, which he says achieves roughly 78–80% carbon content versus a typical infill of 60% or lower in conventional composites. He states his material is cost-competitive with aluminum and outperforms it, and costs somewhat more than steel but not double, while being lighter and structurally comparable to what a designer wants.
2. **Passing comparison to Tesla's giga casting, and interest in reaching Tesla:** Russell describes his composite process as "very similar to giga castings" in enabling complex single-piece parts, and says he would like to talk to Tesla about applying the technology, though he states he has no specific numbers or plans for scaling it himself — he'd prefer to license or partner rather than manufacture at scale.

Notable quotes and data:
- "My product is carbon with a little bit of plastic rather than plastic with a little bit of carbon" — Russell, describing his ~78–80% carbon infill vs. a typical ≤60%.
- Russell says his mold compaction force exceeds 32,000 PSI, which he believes is why others struggle to replicate his void-free material.
- Host recounts being told the BMW i3's carbon-fiber body was roughly 30% carbon by an unnamed source at a body inspection.
- Russell: the material is "competitive with aluminum" and "vastly outperforms" it in cost, while costing more than steel but "not double."
- Russell on his outreach goal: "I'd be cool to talk to Tesla... I don't want to make a bajillion dollars off it... I would rather apply the technology to something."
