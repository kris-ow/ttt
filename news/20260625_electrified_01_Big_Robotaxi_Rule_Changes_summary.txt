This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers a wide range of Tesla and broader tech news including a significant NHTSA regulatory update removing the manual brake pedal requirement for autonomous vehicles, Giga Berlin's production expansion announcements, FSD developments in China, Tesla Semi production scale hints, and several adjacent stories involving SpaceX, OpenAI, and Polestar. The core thesis is that regulatory tailwinds for autonomous vehicles are gradually building, Tesla's manufacturing base is finally approaching full utilization, and the longer-term robotaxi and Optimus narratives remain the dominant drivers of Tesla's valuation.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **NHTSA drops manual brake pedal mandate for AV-only vehicles:** NHTSA announced it is updating federal safety standards to remove the requirement for hand or foot-operated brake controls in vehicles designed exclusively for automated operation. The NHTSA administrator framed this as necessary for America to lead the "greatest technological revolution in vehicle tech since the Model T." Braking performance requirements will be preserved, but the method of achieving them is now open. The public has 30 days to comment before the Department of Transportation decides whether to approve the changes, so this is not yet finalized. For Tesla, this matters because Tesla has already self-certified the Cybercab as meeting all FMVSS regulations without needing an exemption through Part 555; removing the brake requirement reduces the chances NHTSA challenges that self-certification later. For companies like Zoox still going through the Part 555 exemption process (which carries a 2,500-unit annual cap), this also simplifies their path.

2. **UN adopts first global autonomous vehicle framework (not yet binding law):** The UNECE adopted what it describes as the first-ever global rules allowing fully autonomous vehicles, backed by the US, China, the EU, Japan, Canada, and the UK. The framework mandates safety management systems, credible testing, safety case validation, and continuous in-service monitoring. Importantly for Tesla, the framework is hardware-agnostic — there is no requirement for lidar, radar, or any specific hardware, only a requirement to demonstrate safety. However, one notable clause states the requirements are written with the expectation that ADS software does not include online in-vehicle learning that self-modifies system behavior. Dillon Loomis notes Tesla's current over-the-air update model (training done offboard, static software pushed to vehicles) should be compliant today, but flags this could become a point of contention in the 2030s as on-the-fly AI learning matures. The framework is entered into the UN registry and is "in force" internationally, but is not binding on any manufacturer until individual countries write it into national law — the US has supported and helped draft the framework but has not yet taken adoption steps. The most likely path is that parts of the framework are eventually incorporated into FMVSS standards.

3. **FSD in China: test fleet at 200 vehicles, targeting 600:** A Chinese source reported that Tesla's FSD test fleet in China has reached 200 vehicles with a target of 600. Each vehicle averages over 280 km (~173 miles) per day, and the 200-car fleet uploads approximately 1.7 terabytes of data daily. Underlying compute and storage is estimated at 6 petabytes or more, housed in Tesla's Phase 2 factory in Shanghai to keep data within China. OTA approval timelines in China have stretched from 2 weeks to 60 days, constraining Tesla's normal update cadence. Tesla has partnered with BYD for mapping in China due to data security requirements. FSD 14 will only be available on AI4 hardware. The host notes that the technology is reportedly ready, but the bottlenecks are policy approvals and localization, with a broader approval hoped for in Q3 2026.

4. **FSD 14.3.4 safety incidents flagged:** Two FSD incidents were highlighted by the community. In one, FSD 14.3.4 failed to detect a gas can that had fallen off a trailer and drove over it; Tesla's Yun Tasai acknowledged the clip and said it was forwarded to the team. In another, FSD was prepared to drive through a lowered railroad crossing arm despite red warning squares affixed to it. The host notes the inconsistency — FSD has been observed braking hard for small animals but failed to react to these more prominent obstacles.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab conversion capability confirmed:** Per Bradford Ferguson, Cybercabs can be converted from having a steering wheel and pedals to a pedal/wheel-free configuration anywhere — including on-site at a lot — and reportedly in a matter of hours. A conversion was observed performed in a small tent. This suggests Tesla could rapidly convert existing staged inventory should deployment decisions warrant it.

2. **Cybercabs accumulating at lots; Tesla hiring robotaxi logistics senior analyst:** Cybercabs continue to accumulate at staging lots, including one in Dallas. Tesla posted a job listing for a Senior Analyst for Robotaxi Logistics in Austin, whose responsibilities include owning final-mile logistics for robotaxi deployments across North America, optimizing routes, selecting carriers, managing rail partners, and improving logistics hub and distribution workflows. The host interprets this as Tesla actively preparing for broad North American robotaxi distribution.

3. **4680 cell expansion tied to Cybercab and Semi demand:** Joe Tegtmeyer noted (the host characterizes it as somewhat tongue-in-cheek) the expansion of 4680 production at Giga Texas, hinting at significantly higher production volumes ahead. The 4680 cell is used in the Cybercab, Tesla Semi, and Cybertruck. The host also notes Tesla may still be shipping some 4680s from Austin to Giga Berlin for use in Model Y there, at least until Giga Berlin's own 4680 production scales up.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Micron says Optimus could become a bigger memory customer than Tesla's cars:** During Micron's recent earnings call, the CEO outlined a long-term vision in which humanoid robots become a significant new memory market, stating that humanoid robots carry 10 times the memory of an average Level 2+ vehicle. The CEO said Micron expects "a sustained, substantial multi-decade memory demand cycle to begin in the latter part of this decade," driven by advances in simulation, foundation models, and integrated hardware/software for physical AI. The host also notes Tesla's Terafab is expected to address memory needs as well.

## Energy

1. **Tesla signs LOI for Green Turtle battery storage project in Belgium:** Tesla and Giga Storage signed a letter of intent for the Green Turtle project in the Belgian province of Limburg (rendered as "Lindberg" in captions). The project will have a storage capacity of 2,800 MWh (2.8 GWh), making it one of the largest battery storage projects in Europe. Tesla will be responsible for full EPC (engineering, procurement, and construction), battery supply, commissioning, and long-term maintenance. Tesla will deploy its latest-generation Megablock technology. Final contracts are expected to be signed in summer 2026, after which project financing will be finalized. The host notes this is the first Megablock announcement at gigawatt-hour scale; a prior Megablock deal with Neoen was under 1 GWh.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Giga Berlin production ramping to 7,500 units/week — approaching full capacity:** Giga Berlin plant manager André Tütig previously announced a production increase from 5,000 to 6,200 units per week starting in July, adding 1,000 new jobs, plus substantial new investment in the cell factory to enable 18 GWh of 4680 manufacturing capacity starting in 2027, adding 1,500 further jobs. A new announcement followed: production will be further increased to 7,500 units per week, beginning to ramp in October 2026, with another 1,000 employees hired. At 7,500 units/week × 50 weeks, that equals 375,000 units per year — matching the Model Y annual capacity figure shown in Tesla's most recent slide deck, meaning Tesla is finally planning to utilize nearly full capacity at Giga Berlin roughly five years after opening. Across recent weeks, these announcements total 3,500 new jobs at Giga Berlin, representing over a 30% headcount increase from a base of ~11,000 employees. The host calculates the incremental 125,000 annual units would add roughly $6.2 billion in revenue; applying a 20% gross margin yields over $1.2 billion in incremental gross profit.

2. **Polestar banned from US market:** The US Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security refused Polestar the clearance needed to continue selling new cars in the US, citing the connected vehicle rule that bars vehicles with Chinese or Russian software and hardware in connectivity systems on national security grounds. Polestar's majority owner is Geely, a Chinese company. Existing Polestar 3 and 4 inventory will remain on sale until stock runs out; 2027 model year vehicles will not be authorized. Volvo, also Geely-owned, received a waiver in May 2026. The software restrictions take effect for 2027 model year vehicles; hardware restrictions follow in 2030. In Q1 2026, 94% of Polestar's retail sales were outside the US, so the US was not central to its strategy, though the fate of its US manufacturing facility is now in question.

## Financials

1. **Tesla Semi production: high confidence in doubling total North American Class 7/8 EV registrations in year one:** In a recently aired interview, Dan Priestley stated there are currently 2,829 Class 7 and 8 electric trucks registered in aggregate in the US and Canada. When asked if Tesla could double that number in its first year of Semi production, he said he had "very high confidence" in that outcome — implying Tesla could manufacture somewhere in the neighborhood of over 3,000 Tesla Semis in that timeframe. The Tesla careers page currently lists dozens of Semi-related positions across the country, including service adviser roles nationally.

## Market & Competition

1. **OpenAI builds first in-house AI chip "Jalapeño":** OpenAI announced its first custom AI chip, Jalapeño, designed from the ground up by OpenAI and brought to production with Broadcom. It is purpose-built for LLM inference workloads (not training), powering GPT, the API, and future agentic products. Per analyst Patrick Collison (the host's attribution), the chip is likely the first virtually entirely developed by Codex and GPT. It is expected to be manufactured by TSMC and is the first in a planned series of chips between OpenAI and Broadcom. The host's framing: custom ASICs are now "table stakes" for any frontier AI company.
