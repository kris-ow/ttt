## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Tesla won a full Nevada autonomous-network permit replacing its 10-vehicle interim order, raising the cap to 5,000 over 12 months; Tesla told regulators it would be "extremely happy" to hit ~2,500 in Nevada within a year and expects commercial operations to start within about 30 days, implying possible September Las Vegas service (per Sawyer Merritt).
- Waymo received a matching Nevada permit the same day, capped at 1,000 vehicles — the number it requested, versus Tesla's requested 5,000.
- Tesla told Nevada regulators its service will launch first with Model Ys, with Cybercab coming later — sitting in tension with a same-day JP Morgan note saying Tesla is intentionally holding Model Ys back from the robotaxi fleet to focus scaling on Cybercab; the discrepancy is unresolved.
- Multiple signals point to an imminent Austin Cybercab public launch: Tesla told Nevada regulators public rides are "imminent," a Tesla Robotaxi contest requires rides by August 23 with a "launch event" announcement August 25, and a third-party report describes a staged rollout (employees first, then public riders) by end of August — no official date confirmed.
- Permit filings show 48 V3 charging stalls plus 80 wireless vehicle chargers near Tesla's Austin St. Elmo service center, the wireless chargers reportedly built for Cybercab expansion since driverless cars can't plug in manually.
- Waymo says its sixth-gen system cuts per-vehicle hardware/sensor/compute cost from ~$115,000 to ~$20,000 via vertical integration — narrowing but not eliminating Tesla's advantage, since Tesla's sub-$30,000 Cybercab figure covers the whole vehicle.
- A rider reported being stuck roughly 8 minutes at a red light in a Tesla robotaxi while other cars honked — framed as a customer-experience issue rather than a safety one.
- Modeling by Cern Basher suggests Cybercab gross profit to owners could run $14,000–$41,000/year depending on utilization (30% to higher tiers), with Tesla taking a modeled 35% cut; he sees Ark Invest's $0.25/mile long-run cost as achievable only at large fleet scale and high utilization.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA incident data from mid-June to mid-July shows zero crashes caused by Tesla's Model Y robotaxi fleet; the only two logged incidents involved other drivers striking stationary robotaxis with no rider aboard.
- JP Morgan's note (post Fremont visit and Tesla IR meeting) frames FSD v15 as a step change on par with v13-to-v14, built on seven core technologies (~40% already tested in the robotaxi fleet); current AI4 hardware can already run v15, with AI4.5 offering ~10% more compute as future-proofing, not a requirement.
- Tesla FSD has surpassed 13.6 billion cumulative supervised miles globally; South Korea alone has logged over 50 million km (31 million miles).
- A CFA's liability framework argues a Robotaxi fleet meaningfully safer than human drivers will still produce more absolute deaths at scale, and that Tesla's real edge may lie in superhuman avoidance reactions rather than raw driving skill; unsupervised/paid autonomous miles stood at 380,000 as of Tesla's late-July earnings call.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla Semi's per-mile energy cost is estimated at roughly 20–30 cents versus 80–90 cents for diesel, putting Semi fuel costs at about a quarter to a third of diesel's over the truck's life, per one analyst's breakdown citing PepsiCo fleet validation.
- Tesla appears to have discontinued its Solar Roof product, removed from its website with no official statement; it was originally unveiled in 2016.

## Market & Competition
- JP Morgan reports Tesla is intentionally holding back Model Y units from the Austin robotaxi fleet, citing confidence in near-term Cybercab scaling as the reason the fleet size has been flat.
- Genesis, Scout Motors, and a Chinese EV mining-truck maker all announced competing products today, illustrating broadening EV competition with no direct Tesla financial impact.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- One commentator predicts SpaceX's terrestrial AI compute buildout will hit an energy-access "wall," estimating SpaceX won't reach 10 gigawatts by end of next year and may see growth stall the year after; he expects space-based AI satellites to eventually resume growth but flags a gap period where the market "will get punished" in between.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
