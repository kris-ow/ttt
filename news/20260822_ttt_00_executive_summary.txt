## Brief

- Nevada approves Tesla for up to 5,000 robotaxis
- Waymo gets matching Nevada permit capped at 1,000
- Tesla signals imminent public Cybercab launch in Austin
- NHTSA data: zero robotaxi-caused crashes, mid-June to mid-July
- JP Morgan calls FSD v15 a "step change," holds $475 target
- Tesla appears to discontinue Solar Roof product

## Robotaxi
- Nevada Transportation Authority replaced Tesla's 10-vehicle interim order with a full network permit allowing up to 5,000 robotaxis over 12 months, covering Clark County with statewide expansion via notification only. Tesla told regulators it expects commercial operations within about 30 days pending inspections, insurance, and rate filings — implying paid Las Vegas service as soon as September (per Sawyer Merritt).
- Tesla said it would be "extremely happy" to reach roughly 2,500 robotaxis in Nevada within a year, treating the 5,000 cap as headroom, and plans to launch the state first with Model Ys, with Cybercab coming later.
- Waymo won the same permit type same-day, capped at 1,000 vehicles (its own requested number), and an executive said its sixth-gen system cuts hardware/sensor/compute cost from ~$115,000 to ~$20,000/vehicle — a figure that narrows but doesn't erase Tesla's whole-vehicle Cybercab cost edge.
- Multiple signals point to an imminent Austin Cybercab public launch: Tesla told Nevada regulators rides are "imminent," a Tesla Robotaxi contest requires rides by August 23 with an August 25 "launch event" announcement, and a third-party report says Tesla is prepping a public no-wheel, no-pedal launch by end of August in two phases (employees, then public). No date is officially confirmed.
- Permit filings show 48 V3 charging stalls near Tesla's Austin St. Elmo service center plus 80 wireless vehicle chargers, read as Cybercab-expansion prep given the lack of anyone aboard to plug in a driverless car.
- JP Morgan reports Tesla is intentionally holding back Model Y units from the Austin robotaxi fleet, citing confidence in near-term Cybercab scaling — a stance in tension with Tesla telling Nevada it will launch there with Model Ys first.
- A widely repeated claim that Tesla is capped at 2,500 Cybercabs misapplies NHTSA's exemption-pathway limit (used by Waymo); Tesla is pursuing self-certification instead, which carries no unit cap, per Lars Moravy (relayed by Sawyer Merritt).
- Rider Scott Busi reported being stuck roughly 8 minutes at a red light in a Tesla robotaxi while other cars honked and swerved — a usability, not safety, complaint.
- Cern Basher's unit-economics model (via futureaza) pegs a $30,000 Cybercab, 600,000-mile life, and 165 Wh/mile energy use; at $1 base + $1/mile pricing and 30% utilization, owner gross profit is ~$14,000/year, rising to ~$41,000 at higher utilization, with a long-run $0.25/mile fare seen as achievable only at high fleet scale.
- Separately, Basher argues liability costs at scale (3–7 cents/mile) remain a manageable operating line — $3–7 billion annually at 100 billion miles — rather than an existential risk, comparable to how airlines and insurers reserve for predictable losses.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA incident data for mid-June to mid-July shows zero crashes caused by Tesla's Model Y robotaxis; the only two logged incidents were other vehicles striking stationary robotaxis (Dallas, Austin), with no rider aboard either time.
- JP Morgan, following a Fremont visit and Tesla IR meeting, says Tesla frames FSD v15 as a step change on par with v13-to-v14, built on seven core technologies (~40% already tested in the robotaxi fleet); current AI4 hardware can already run v15, with AI4.5 offering ~10% more compute and ~2x memory as future-proofing, not a requirement.
- Tesla FSD cumulative miles surpassed 13.6 billion globally; South Korea owners alone have driven over 50 million km (31 million miles) on FSD Supervised.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla appears to have discontinued its Solar Roof tile product, removed from its website with no official statement; it was originally unveiled in 2016.
- Tesla Semi's energy cost is estimated at ~20–30 cents/mile versus ~80–90 cents/mile for diesel, based on ~6–7 mpg diesel trucks and ~$5.60/gallon diesel pricing — a structural fuel-cost advantage cited alongside PepsiCo fleet experience as validation (details of PepsiCo's specific comments unavailable).
- Tesla opened its second public Megacharger site, in Vernon, CA, with 6 stalls up to 1.2MW each, relevant to Semi charging infrastructure.
- Tesla released its first video of the Giga Texas cathode factory in operation, showing in-house battery material production.

## Market & Competition

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

This is speaker opinion, not confirmed SpaceX guidance.
