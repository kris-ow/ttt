2. **Bond Yields as Primary Concern:** Bhakdi flags 30-year US bond yields approaching the 5% threshold and the 10-year at what he describes as a very concerning level. He observes that yields moved from 4.46% to 4.6% over just one week — a ~14 basis point jump that he considers alarming. He notes the same trend across German bunds, UK gilts, French bonds, and especially Japanese government bonds, warning that a reversal of the yen carry trade could structurally pull capital out of US equities.

3. **Strait of Hormuz Oil Disruption:** Bhakdi identifies the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz as the root cause of rising inflation fears. He notes that over 20% of global oil passes through the strait and that Iran's blockade, now joined by Trump's posture, is creating real supply stress. He cites UBS projecting OECD commercial oil inventories could approach record lows of 7.6 billion barrels by end of May if trends continue, and JP Morgan flagging early June as when commercial inventories approach operational stress levels. He notes that 250 million barrels were drawn from reserves in March and April alone, at a rate of 4 million barrels per day. The EIA (as of mid-May) assumes the closure continues through late May, with losses peaking at 10.8 million barrels per day in May.

4. **Tactical Derisking:** Bhakdi states he is currently over-leveraged by his own assessment — roughly 20% more leveraged than he should be — having been caught off guard by the macro deterioration while focused on Tesla-specific analysis. He warns that if Monday opens lower, he will be forced to deresk and take losses. He acknowledges Friday likely resulted in Pioneer Alpha's worst week of the year, estimating approximately -3% alpha versus Tesla, after two of the best weeks of the year.

## Financials

1. **Tesla Price Targets and Portfolio Exposure:** Bhakdi reiterates his Tesla price target of $490 as the near-term all-time high target, $700 as achievable in November 2026 under bullish scenarios, and $4,000 by 2030 as his standalone Tesla (ex-SpaceX) long-term target. He projects that in a merged entity scenario, Tesla shareholders would gain ~38% in 2027 (vs. 50% standalone), but ~80% in 2028 (vs. 66% standalone) and ~88% in the following year (vs. 60% standalone) — making the merger a net negative in year one but a significant long-term winner.

3. **Starship V3 Launch on May 20, 2026:** Bhakdi flags the Starship V3 first flight, scheduled for Wednesday May 20, 2026 at 6:30 p.m. Eastern / 5:30 p.m. Central, as a pivotal data point for the SpaceX IPO valuation and the orbital AI thesis. He explains that the central question is not whether Starship works (it already reaches orbit) but the cost curve — specifically dollars per kilogram to orbit. He estimates current cost at approximately $2,500/kg, and identifies ~$200/kg as the key inflection point at which space-based data centers become approximately 60% cheaper to operate over 10 years than terrestrial equivalents. He notes the current Starship version already reduces costs roughly 3–4x versus Falcon Heavy even without Starship reusability, due to scale and booster reuse alone. He emphasizes that reliability (failure rate) is even more important than reusability because insurance/self-insurance costs from payload loss can dwarf launch costs — using the example that a 1-in-5 failure rate on a launch carrying $600 million in cargo adds $120 million in hard costs on top of a $90 million launch price.
