Brian and drone pilot Joe Tegtmeyer walk through aerial footage of the Optimus factory under construction at Giga Texas, covering its scale, build speed, equipment signals and remaining gating items. The core argument is that Tesla is building at extreme speed, but a completed steel structure does not mean production is imminent. Power supply, equipment installation and finishing work still stand between the structure and output.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Factory scale and design:** Based on permits and what he has heard at the facility, Joe estimates a little over 7 million square feet of production capacity. About three-quarters of the width has four floors and the full length is covered. The east side, roughly four columns wide, looks like a tall atrium. The corners are rounded rather than angular, but the building should otherwise resemble the main Giga Texas factory. Its address is 1 Harold Green Road. The reason for the tall atrium is unclear: it may serve receiving doors and access points or future mezzanines, and Joe says he asked but got no answer.
2. **Construction speed and timeline:** Groundbreaking was in March and steel construction began around late May or early June. The steel structure is still targeted for completion by the end of March or perhaps April, about one year from the start of construction. Crews appear to work nearly around the clock, and Joe saw them working at midnight on a Thursday. The hosts contrast this with Giga Texas, where parts were made while the building was still going up. Here the whole structure is built first and then filled with equipment. Joe cautions that structural completion does not mean production readiness, since walls, materials and equipment installation remain, and fall and winter weather could slow progress.
3. **Casting machine foundations:** Four large foundations are being poured, which both hosts assume are for casting machines. Joe notes that if they are not for large-scale gigacasting, they would suit robot limbs, with dies that could produce 10 to 15 parts per shot. The hosts say the machines would be long and narrow, like the presses in the main building but much smaller than its 5,000–9,000-ton presses. Brian speculates that Tesla may use thixo-casting, a semi-liquid magnesium process that could yield strong, light parts quickly. The hosts expect the next few months to reveal much about Optimus production.
4. **Roof waterproofing:** Grout is being applied over the steel decking, followed by insulation panels and a white waterproof membrane. The goal seems to be waterproofing most of the roof, which Joe reads as a lesson learned from Giga Texas, where production ran with the roof still open and leaking badly. The final waterproofing will wait until the parapet wall is installed.
5. **Power as the key indicator:** Joe calls utilities, especially electricity, the most important item beyond design and equipment installation. The facility has no industrial power yet, and he sees nothing significant leading to the plant. Work is under way on an electrical distribution unit south and west of the building, and he says this is the main indicator he is monitoring. The hosts say they will cover it in a follow-up video.
6. **Site infrastructure:** A parallel road is being added that connects to Tesla Road and north to a rebuilt Harold Green Road. River Road starts near the Colorado River, passes under State Highway 130 and runs by the east-side advanced technology chip factory, the boring tunnel and the battery cathode plant before reaching Lone Tree Field. Together these will give much better access for materials and construction. Joe says this "North Campus" is being graded and may host ancillary buildings such as warehouses or a yard, with possible room for future expansion. He also mentions underground water management along the perimeter.
7. **Fremont and Texas scale-up:** Brian says Optimus is believed to be already under construction in Fremont, but that factory is not big enough for the target of a million per year, which is why Texas is being built.
