Tesla has filed an S-8 registration statement with the SEC to formally register 303,960,630 shares of common stock for CEO Elon Musk under his 2018 compensation award, effectively closing the multi-year legal battle over the largest executive pay package in corporate history. At Tesla's current share price of ~$376, the shares are worth over $114 billion.

## Financials
1. **2018 Pay Package Now Executed:** Tesla's board signed an "Implementation Agreement" on April 21, 2026 to execute the 2018 award, and CFO Vaibhav Taneja filed the S-8 with the SEC on April 24. The original package granted Musk options to purchase ~304 million split-adjusted shares at $23.34 per share, contingent on 12 escalating market cap and operational milestones — all of which Musk met by December 2021. The Delaware Supreme Court reversed the lower court's voiding of the package in December 2025, ruling full rescission would be "inequitable."

2. **Governance Guardrails and Financial Impact:** The board simultaneously revoked a $29 billion interim pay package created as a hedge while the original deal was in legal limbo. New conditions require Musk to remain CEO or a product development executive through at least 2028, and to hold the shares for five years. Tesla disclosed $9.97 billion in unrecognized stock-based compensation expense tied to the award, which will be charged against earnings over coming quarters.

3. **Additional Compensation Package Approved:** In November 2025, shareholders approved a separate, larger compensation plan that could grant Musk an additional 424 million shares worth up to $1 trillion if Tesla hits 12 new performance milestones over the next decade.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitive Position Under Pressure:** The editorial section notes that BYD outsold Tesla in global all-electric vehicles in 2025 by over 600,000 units, Tesla registrations in Europe declined while the broader BEV market grew, and Tesla confirmed its first year of sales decline in China.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Liability Exposure:** The article references Tesla facing up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits tied to Autopilot and FSD crashes, false advertising claims, and Musk's acknowledgment that millions of Hardware 3 vehicles cannot achieve the "Full Self-Driving" capability that was marketed to buyers.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert acknowledges Musk's legal and corporate victory but argues the underlying governance failures identified by Judge McCormick remain unresolved — and that shareholders are bearing the costs of dilution, stock underperformance, and mounting legal liability from FSD-related claims, all under a board structure the author characterizes as lacking independence from Musk.
