## Executive Summary
Today's posts centered on two notable Tesla developments: the official end of production for the Model S and Model X after a combined ~750,000 units delivered, and active regulatory engagement between Tesla and Irish authorities over FSD (Supervised) approval. The overall signal is mixed — the S/X discontinuation marks a meaningful product-line transition, while the Ireland FSD talks represent incremental but real international regulatory progress.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Model S and Model X Production Officially Ends:** Tesla has ceased production of both the Model S and Model X, with the final units of each model now confirmed built. The two vehicles, which entered production in 2012 and 2015 respectively, collectively reached approximately 750,000 deliveries worldwide over their lifespans. Sawyer frames their legacy as transformative for the broader auto industry — crediting them with catalyzing EV adoption at a time when no other American automaker was competing at that level. For investors, this confirms a product portfolio consolidation that has been anticipated, freeing up factory capacity and resources potentially directed toward Model Y, Cybertruck, Cybercab, and next-generation vehicles.

2. **Tesla Engages Irish Authorities on FSD (Supervised) Approval:** Tesla is actively in discussions with the Irish government, including the National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI) and the Department of Transport, regarding regulatory approval for FSD (Supervised). The Department of Transport confirmed the engagement, noting that approval would require compliance with Irish and broader EU regulatory frameworks. This is a meaningful incremental data point for Tesla's international FSD rollout strategy — Ireland would represent a foothold for FSD deployment within the EU regulatory environment, which has historically been a more complex approval landscape than the U.S.

3. **Tesla's California Market Dominance — Context from Forbes:** A Forbes piece by Brooke Crothers highlighted that Tesla remains the best-selling automotive brand in California, a distinction no American automaker had held since the 1990s. While qualitative, this reinforces Tesla's brand strength in its most important domestic market at a time when broader EV demand trends and competition are under scrutiny.
