This video argues that the SpaceX IPO S1 filing reveals Elon Musk's overarching strategy across all his companies: build expensive physical infrastructure, then rent access to it as recurring service revenue — a playbook identical to Amazon's transformation from retailer to cloud giant via AWS. The host draws a direct line from the Anthropic compute deal to Tesla's own ambitions in robotaxi, FSD subscriptions, energy, and Optimus, framing Tesla not as a car company but as a physical AI platform in the making.

## Market & Competition

1. **The Anthropic Deal — "Elon Web Services":** In May 2026, SpaceX signed a cloud services agreement with Anthropic for $1.25 billion per month through May 2029, with capacity ramping up in May and June at a reduced fee. Either side can cancel on 90 days' notice, and Anthropic retains ownership of its content, AI models, and data. Investor Brad Gerstner, appearing on the All-In podcast, dubbed this "EWS — Elon Web Services," drawing a direct analogy to AWS. Elon Musk himself called the strategy "AI compute as a service." Gerstner estimated the Anthropic deal would generate an incremental $4–5 billion in revenue for SpaceX in 2026 (not the full annualized $15 billion run rate, because capacity is still ramping through mid-2026).

2. **The Amazon Playbook Applied to SpaceX:** The S1 filing states verbatim that the structure "allows us to monetize unused compute capacity in our infrastructure while still permitting a reallocation of the capacity for our own internal initiatives if needed in the future." The host explains this mirrors Amazon's early 2000s move: Amazon built compute for its own store, found spare capacity sitting idle, and rented it to others — that became AWS, which generated most of Amazon's profit for years. SpaceX built the Colossus data center cluster (near Memphis, described as a mix of hardware including H100s, better suited for inference than top-tier model training) to train Grok, then rented the underutilized capacity to Anthropic. A rival AI company (Anthropic competes with Grok) is effectively funding the data centers that train Elon's own AI — the same dynamic as Netflix running on Amazon servers while competing with Amazon Video.

3. **SpaceX's Stated TAM and Orbital Data Centers:** The S1 filing claims SpaceX has "identified the largest actionable total addressable market in human history" at $28.5 trillion — broken down as approximately $370 billion in space, approximately $1.6–1.26 trillion in connectivity (Starlink broadband and mobile), and approximately $26.12 trillion in AI. The filing explicitly notes China and Russia were excluded from that estimate. SpaceX's longer-term vision includes orbital data centers — the company has requested permission to launch up to one million satellites for this purpose and says deployment could begin as early as 2028, leveraging unlimited solar power and the cold of space for cooling, made economically viable by reusable Starship rockets.

4. **Starlink as the Cash Engine:** According to the filing, Starlink generated approximately $11.4 billion in revenue in 2025, with operating income of $4.4 billion, growing nearly 50% in a single year. Subscribers grew from 5 million to 10.3 million across 164 countries in 12 months. The host frames Starlink as the retail business that funds the compute buildout — analogous to Amazon's retail arm funding early AWS. SpaceX as a combined entity (including the AI buildout) is still posting a net loss of a few billion dollars per quarter; the Anthropic deal is described as moving toward break-even, not immediate profitability.

5. **Competitive Risks and Honest Caveats:** The host flags three key risks: (1) The 90-day cancellation clause means the $15 billion annual run rate is not locked in — Anthropic could build its own data centers and walk away quickly. (2) The combined SpaceX/AI business is still losing billions per quarter; the Anthropic revenue offsets costs but does not yet produce profit. (3) SpaceX's valuation of approximately $1.75–2 trillion on under $20 billion in revenue implies an enormous multiple based largely on belief in Starship, orbital compute, and Grok — none of which are yet generating serious profit.

Cumulative supervised miles are stated at approximately 9–10 billion. FSD is presented as the first layer of Tesla's recurring revenue stack, analogous to Starlink subscriptions funding SpaceX's infrastructure.

## Robotaxi

However, he explicitly notes Austin is the only city where this is operating and flags it as a key metric to watch — specifically whether the city list expands, with the next milestone being additional cities by 2027 as a scenario for the thesis to "half work."

## Humanoid Bots

He also notes Tesla has not given a 2026 production number for Optimus. This is offered as a candid counterpoint to the bull case — the recurring revenue story from robotics remains a thesis, not yet reflected in financial results.

## Financials

1. **Tesla's AI Capex Surge:** On Tesla's Q1 earnings call, the company disclosed it is more than doubling its AI compute in approximately 6 months. Tesla raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to over $25 billion, described as roughly three times what it spent the prior year. The host frames this as Tesla pouring money into the same category of physical AI infrastructure as SpaceX, and says the thesis is "working" only if that spending eventually converts into service revenue.

2. **The Platform Valuation Thesis:** The host argues that if even a portion of Tesla's installed base (cars already on the road), FSD subscriptions, robotaxi network, charging network, insurance, and energy business (Megapacks and Powerwalls) converts to recurring service revenue, valuing Tesla like Ford or GM "stops making any sense." The SpaceX filing is presented as the strongest real-world evidence yet that the "physical AI platform" playbook can work — but the host is careful to note the recurring revenue has not yet shown up meaningfully on Tesla's income statement.
