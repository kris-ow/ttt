## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.2: driving excellent, navigation regressing.** FutureAzA's real-world review of 14.3.2 found vehicle control — lane selection, merging, pedestrian handling — "exceptional" and edge cases increasingly rare. However, the system looped the block multiple times before successfully entering a bank parking lot, behavior the reviewer's passenger confirmed is a regression: "It didn't used to loop the block three times." The reviewer's verdict: "The navigation has absolutely gotten worse."

- **Two new 14.3.2 UI features observed.** A new on-screen indicator shows FSD's intent to park before executing the maneuver. A new in-app button lets drivers log intervention reasons without a written note — described as a meaningful quality-of-life improvement for data collection.

- **FSD 14.3 adds slippery weather detection.** Per Sawyer Merritt, FSD V14.3 now automatically caps speed at 70 mph and limits to Standard mode when it detects potential hydroplaning surfaces — addressing a gap critics have historically flagged.

- **Danish authorities publicly contradicted a Reuters report** claiming they were skeptical of FSD, stating they had released no official position for or against the technology.

- **FSD 14.x cited as the threshold that enabled unsupervised Texas launches.** The panel on brighterwithherbert noted that even minor point-version differences in FSD produce measurable safety outcome differences, and that HW3 was explicitly characterized as incapable of unsupervised operation. Elon Musk on the most recent earnings call suggested FSD version 15 may be needed before truly scaled unsupervised deployment, with consumer unsupervised FSD targeted for Q4 2026 or Q1 2027.

## Robotaxi

- **Brian Wang's 60-day 10x thesis.** With the unsupervised Texas fleet at 39 vehicles per RobotaxiTracker.com, Brian Wang argues it could grow to 300+ unsupervised vehicles by end of June 2026. His logic: Dallas and Houston proved 100% unsupervised operations viable at launch, Austin proves it at larger geographic scale, and reportedly 80+ parked robotaxi-badged vehicles sit ready to activate in each city.

- **Austin full conversion to unsupervised expected within ~10 days.** Wang predicts Tesla will convert the remaining ~24 supervised Austin vehicles to unsupervised shortly, bringing Austin's unsupervised count from 28 to 50+. The panel found no clear rationale for keeping them supervised, with one caveat: Tesla may be running a deliberate A/B test to measure whether safety monitors are doing anything meaningful before removing them entirely.

- **Dallas and Houston launched directly unsupervised — no supervised ride-hail phase.** The panel theorized this was enabled by FSD 14.x reaching a sufficient safety threshold, combined with months of quiet pre-launch supervised (non-ride-hail) testing. The strategy was seen as deliberate: avoid bad PR by never announcing a supervised phase, then launch clean and unsupervised.

- **Bay Area fleet remains puzzling.** The Bay Area fleet — approximately 1,000 vehicles operating under a ride-hail limo permit with human FSD drivers across ~1,000 square miles — has not been converted to autonomous despite Tesla reportedly having accumulated the 50,000 miles required for a California AV permit. The panel offered theories (political caution, waiting for federal AV legislation) but described it as genuinely unexplained.

- **Herbert's year-end estimate: 10,000–15,000 robotaxis across ~15–20 cities.** He frames the bulk of the scaled fleet as Cybercab, with Model Y retained for the ~10% of rides requiring more than two passengers. Bhakdi separately reiterates his thesis that ~1,800 unsupervised vehicles constitutes the mainstream investor inflection point, acknowledging he predicted April–May and may be "a little early."

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus Giga Texas factory: 10M units/year targeted capacity.** The Fremont line — repurposed from Model S and Model X production — targets 1M units/year. V3 production is set to begin summer 2026. 2027–2028 production targets are 100,000–300,000 units across both facilities, with a cost target of $20,000–$25,000 per unit at scale.

- **Model S and Model X discontinued to free Fremont for Optimus.** Announced on the Q1 2026 earnings call, the discontinuation is framed not as a demand failure but as a deliberate reallocation of production capacity — a formal signal that Tesla has transitioned its strategic identity from car company to physical AI company.

- **Optimus supply chain does not yet exist.** Elon on the Q3 earnings call: "With cars, you have an existing supply chain. With humanoid robots, there is no supply chain." Tesla is building actuators, sensors, hands, and forearms itself. Elon described the hand and forearm as "more difficult than the entire rest of the robot."

- **Optimus reveal expected within 90 days per Bhakdi.** He describes it as a "huge, huge soft catalyst" for the stock and cites a news item that "Tesla pauses Optimus production line to retool for faster assembly" as a signal of active development momentum rather than a setback.

- **Morgan Stanley estimates humanoid robot market at $5 trillion by 2050.** Some analysts model Optimus as representing 80% of Tesla's total value by that date. Elon reportedly believes the market is larger than Morgan Stanley's estimate.

## Energy

- **Tesla loses ~$400M/year 45X credit in 2026.** IRS Notice 2026-15, issued February 2026, introduced Prohibited Foreign Entity rules. Because battery cells account for ~80% of pack cost, Tesla's use of imported CATL cells at Lathrop immediately and substantially exceeds the PFE allowance, eliminating the $10/kWh module assembly credit — worth roughly $400M annually at 40 GWh throughput — unless Tesla pre-built a qualifying inventory stockpile before year-end 2025.

- **Tesla's Q1 2026 energy gross margin of ~39% included non-recurring tariff benefits.** Management flagged the record margin included "tariff recognitions." Benchmark's Iola Hughes suggests the most likely explanation is an overpayment Tesla made during turbulent tariff changes — not a recurring benefit. Tesla's 10-Q confirmed it was unrelated to the February Supreme Court ruling.

- **Nevada LFP factory expected to restore credit eligibility — but cathode sourcing is the obstacle.** Tesla's planned Nevada LFP cell manufacturing line, using CATL-licensed equipment, is expected to qualify for both the $10/kWh module assembly credit and potentially the $35/kWh cell manufacturing credit. However, ~99% of LFP cathode supply is currently China-based, and cathode accounts for ~40% of cell cost, creating a substantial compliance hurdle.

- **Tesla Megapack priced at ~$190–$200/kWh at volume — competitive with Western peers, not with China.** That positions Tesla $30–$40/kWh cheaper than other Western suppliers, but Chinese competitors deploy systems in China at under $100/kWh. In unprotected markets (Europe, Australia), Chinese products are materially cheaper. Tesla's 2025 global energy storage market share was ~15% (46.7 GWh of ~315 GWh global); Benchmark forecasts share declining but staying above 10%.

- **Giga-scale project pipeline accelerating sharply.** Q1 2026 saw 11 projects over 1 GWh come online; the full-year 2026 pipeline stands at 122, versus 50 in all of 2025 and 17 in 2024. Ex-China markets grew ~112% year-over-year in Q1; Australia became the second-largest market globally with over 8 GWh installed in Q1 alone.

- **Tesla on innovation: responding to the market, not leading it.** Hughes: "It's not necessarily them taking a leap ahead of everyone, but it's more a kind of response to put them in line with where the market's already going." Chinese manufacturers have progressed from 500 Ah to 700 Ah cells and are working on 1,000+ Ah cells, with container energy densities now roughly 5x Megapack 1 levels.

- **UK Renewable Energy Bundle launched.** Tesla introduced a Solar + Powerwall 3 subscription for £199/month (including installation, 0% interest), with a combined Model 3 + energy package available at £494/month. The offer targets existing Tesla owners and represents a maturing cross-product bundling strategy in a key European market.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab production underway at ~20 units/day per Herbert.** A Tesla Semi was filmed delivering Cybercabs, with Joe Tegtmeyer reporting ~40–50 on a lot and ~20 arriving daily. Herbert estimates ~500–600/month and ~2,000 within three months. The panel consensus: vehicles are currently being distributed nationally for road validation, not commercial service. Cybercab production confirmed to have begun in April 2026.

- **Cybercab must launch commercially as fully unsupervised.** The two-seat layout makes a safety-driver configuration socially and practically unworkable — a safety monitor sitting beside a passenger is described as impractical — raising the validation bar and explaining the extended pre-launch testing period.

- **Cybercab hardware likely superior to robotaxi-badged Model Y.** Brian Wang argues Cybercab contains not just cost-optimized hardware but better camera placement and sensor hardware, drawing on Cybertruck lessons. He and Cern Basher both emphasized that any high-profile Cybercab incident would damage the brand in a way a Model Y incident would not: "The Cybercab has to be perfect… it is the brand for Tesla's robotaxi."

- **Model S/X Signature Edition delivery event postponed.** The previously scheduled May 12 event has been delayed with no new date announced.

- **Frost Blue Model 3 Performance rolling out.** Car haulers have been spotted transporting large numbers of the new color variant, with showroom deliveries described as imminent.

## Financials

- **Tesla guided to negative free cash flow for the remainder of 2026.** Capital commitments include Terafab ($55B upfront, $119B total across all phases), xAI's Colossus 2 supercomputer ($18B in chips), and the Optimus supply chain buildout.

- **Terafab: $119B vertically integrated chip factory in Austin.** Jointly owned by Tesla, xAI, SpaceX, and Intel, targeting over 1 terawatt of AI compute per year. Elon: "All the current chip fabs on Earth combined produce only about 2% of what Tesla and SpaceX will need across all their projects."

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla stock down ~4.75% YTD vs. Google up ~26%.** Bhakdi maintains a $490 price target (prior all-time high was $490 on December 16), arguing Tesla is fundamentally stronger than at that peak. He characterizes the post-earnings setup as a "Goldilocks moment" with limited downside absent a macro shock.

- **BYD took the top spot for global energy storage system shipments in 2025.** Chinese players — Sungrow, CATL, Hithium, Cornex, EVE Energy — are aggressively pursuing share in previously Tesla-dominated markets like Australia with significantly lower prices. CATL's global energy storage cell share fell from ~30% to 22% as competition intensified.

- **Powin declared bankruptcy.** The previously third-largest US energy storage integrator failed after taking on excessive debt during rapid growth — Hughes draws a parallel to Northvolt in Europe and expects further industry consolidation.

- **SpaceX–xAI merger valued combined entity at ~$1.25 trillion** (February 2026, described by Reuters as the largest corporate merger ever recorded). Days later, SpaceX filed an FCC application to launch up to 1 million satellites functioning as orbital data centers.
