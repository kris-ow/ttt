## Robotaxi

- **Brian Wang's 10x-in-60-days thesis:** With the unsupervised Texas fleet at 39 vehicles, Wang argues Tesla could reach 300+ unsupervised robotaxis across Austin, Dallas, and Houston by end of June 2026. His logic: Dallas and Houston proved 100% unsupervised operations viable at launch, Austin demonstrates it at larger scale, and reportedly 80+ parked robotaxi-badged vehicles sit ready in each city.
- **Austin full conversion imminent:** Wang predicts all remaining supervised Austin vehicles convert to unsupervised within approximately 10 days, lifting Austin's unsupervised count from 28 to 50+. The panel saw no logical reason to keep the remaining ~24 supervised given that over half already run driverless; the lone caveat was a possible A/B safety-monitor test.
- **Dallas and Houston skipped the supervised phase entirely:** Tesla launched both cities directly as unsupervised operations, apparently enabled by FSD 14.x reaching a sufficient safety threshold plus months of unannounced pre-launch supervised (non-ride-hail) testing. The strategy avoids bad PR and allows clean data collection before a public driverless launch.
- **Bay Area remains an open question:** The ~551-vehicle Bay Area fleet operates under a ride-hail limo-service permit with human drivers. Tesla has not applied for a California AV permit despite reportedly accumulating sufficient miles. Theories include political caution, a preference for federal AV legislation, and headline risk aversion — but the panel called it "genuinely puzzling."
- **Herbert's year-end estimate:** 10,000–15,000 robotaxis across ~15–20 cities in ~12 states by end of 2026, roughly 500–1,000 vehicles per city, with the bulk eventually being Cybercabs rather than Model Y.
- **Bhakdi's 1,800-vehicle inflection thesis:** Bhakdi reiterates that ~1,800 unsupervised robotaxis is the threshold at which mainstream investors accept the thesis as proven. He had predicted April–May 2026 and acknowledges being slightly early but not materially so.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.2 driving performance strong, navigation regresses:** FutureAzA's real-world review of 14.3.2 rates vehicle control — lane selection, merging, pedestrian handling — as "exceptional," with genuine edge cases becoming rare. However, FSD looped the block three times before entering a bank parking lot, a behavior the passenger confirmed as a regression. Verdict: "The navigation has absolutely gotten worse."
- **FSD 14.3.2 new features:** A new on-screen indicator shows parking intent before the maneuver; a new in-app button lets drivers log intervention reasons without written notes. Both are quality-of-life additions in this version.
- **FSD V14.3 slippery weather detection:** Per Sawyer Merritt, FSD V14.3 now detects potential hydroplaning surfaces and automatically limits the vehicle to Standard mode, capping speed at 70 mph — addressing a gap critics have flagged in adverse-weather handling.
- **Danish authorities contradict Reuters skepticism report:** Danish regulators publicly stated they had not released any official position for or against FSD, directly contradicting a Reuters article claiming regulatory skepticism. A data point for investors tracking European regulatory posture.
- **FSD 14.x as the unsupervised launch threshold:** Brian Wang cited FSD 14.3/14.x as the version that gave Tesla sufficient confidence to launch Dallas and Houston without safety drivers. HW3 was explicitly described as incapable of unsupervised operation.
- **Elon on consumer unsupervised FSD:** On the most recent earnings call, Musk targeted unsupervised FSD for consumer-owned vehicles by Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, while suggesting FSD version 15 may be needed before truly scaled unsupervised deployment.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus factory scale:** Tesla broke ground on a dedicated Optimus factory at Giga Texas targeting 10 million units/year capacity. The Fremont line — repurposed from Model S and Model X — targets 1 million units/year. V3 production is set to begin summer 2026. Combined 2027–2028 production target is 100,000–300,000 units at a cost target of $20,000–$25,000 per unit.
- **Model S and Model X discontinued for Optimus:** Tesla announced on the Q1 2026 earnings call it is discontinuing Model S and Model X to repurpose the Fremont line for Optimus — framed not as weak demand but as a formal strategic pivot from car company to physical AI company.
- **Optimus reveal within 90 days:** Bhakdi states an Optimus reveal is coming in less than 90 days, describing it as a "huge soft catalyst" for the stock. He also cites a report that Tesla paused the Optimus production line to retool for faster assembly — characterizing it as a development positive, not a setback.
- **Supply chain being built from scratch:** Elon has stated the humanoid robot supply chain does not exist and Tesla is building actuators, sensors, hands, and forearms itself. He described the hand and forearm as "more difficult than the entire rest of the robot."

## Energy

- **Tesla loses ~$400M/year 45X credit:** IRS Notice 2026-15 (February 2026) introduced Prohibited Foreign Entity rules. Because battery cells account for ~80% of pack cost, Tesla's use of CATL cells at Lathrop immediately exceeds the PFE allowance threshold, eliminating the $10/kWh module assembly credit — worth approximately $400M/year at 40 GWh throughput. Tesla is not earning this credit in 2026 unless it pre-built a qualifying inventory stockpile before year-end 2025.
- **Nevada LFP line may qualify for both credits:** Tesla's planned LFP cell manufacturing line in Nevada, using CATL-licensed equipment, is expected to qualify for the $10/kWh module assembly credit and potentially the $35/kWh cell manufacturing credit. The CATL licensing agreement predates the relevant legislation, safe-harboring the equipment/IP position — but cathode sourcing will be decisive, as ~99% of LFP cathode supply is currently China-based.
- **Megapack pricing and market share:** At ~20% volume discounts, Megapack prices at ~$190–$200/kWh — roughly $30–$40/kWh cheaper than Western competitors but significantly above Chinese players deploying under $100/kWh domestically. Tesla held ~15% global energy storage share in 2025 (46.7 GWh of ~315 GWh global); Benchmark forecasts share declining but staying above 10%.
- **Giga-scale pipeline accelerating sharply:** 11 projects over 1 GWh came online in Q1 2026 alone; the full-year 2026 pipeline stands at 122, up from 50 in all of 2025 and 17 in 2024. Ex-China markets grew ~112% year-over-year in Q1 2026; Australia became the second-largest market with over 8 GWh installed in Q1.
- **Tesla innovation gap flagged:** Benchmark's Iola Hughes describes Tesla as potentially lagging Chinese players on cell energy density innovation — Chinese manufacturers have progressed to 700 amp-hour cells and are developing 1,000+ amp-hour cells. Tesla's next-gen Megapack 3 and Mega Block are characterized as responses bringing Tesla in line with where the market already is, not leaps ahead. Tesla retains a reliability and after-sales service advantage.
- **UK Renewable Energy Bundle launched:** Tesla introduced a Solar + Powerwall 3 bundle in the UK for £199/month (including installation, 0% interest), with a combined Model 3 option at £494/month — a cross-product subscription structure targeting existing Tesla owners in a key European market.
- **Battery tariff trajectory:** Current US battery tariffs total ~38.4% (25% Section 301 + 10% Section 122 + 3.4% baseline). Hughes estimates ~50% is a reasonable forward planning assumption. The Section 122 tariff's 150-day expiry window creates near-term uncertainty.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab production at ~20 units/day:** Herbert estimates current Cybercab production at approximately 20 units/day (~500–600/month), with ~40–50 units reportedly on lots and ~20 more arriving daily per Joe Tegtmeyer. Consensus: these are being distributed nationally for road validation, not yet commercial service. The panel estimates Tesla needs ~1,000 Cybercabs driving in a given area for several months before launching unsupervised commercial service under the Cybercab brand.
- **Cybercab must launch commercially as fully unsupervised:** The two-seat design makes it socially and practically untenable to seat a safety monitor beside a passenger, meaning Cybercab cannot run a supervised ride-hail phase — raising the validation bar and explaining the extended pre-launch testing period.
- **Cybercab brand integrity as the key constraint:** Cern Basher: "The Cybercab has to be perfect… it is the brand for Tesla's robotaxi." Brian Wang: "Cybercab is waiting for robotaxi, not the other way around." Any high-profile Cybercab incident would damage the brand in a way a Model Y incident would not, per the panel.
- **Model S/X Signature Edition delivery event postponed:** Tesla postponed the May 12th delivery event with no new date announced, per Sawyer Merritt. Worth monitoring for rescheduling signals.
- **Frost Blue Model 3 Performance rollout underway:** Car haulers spotted transporting large numbers of Frost Blue Model 3 Performance vehicles, suggesting showroom deliveries are imminent.

## Financials

- **Tesla guided to negative free cash flow for the rest of 2026:** Major capital commitments include Terafab ($55B upfront, $119B total), xAI's Colossus 2 supercomputer ($18B in chips), and the Optimus supply chain buildout. The host of Source 2 acknowledges this as a legitimate bear case concern for short-horizon investors.
- **Q1 2026 energy gross margin record includes one-time tariff benefit:** Tesla reported a ~39% energy gross margin in Q1 2026 — a record — but management flagged one-time "tariff recognition" benefits. Hughes' most likely explanation is a tariff overpayment related to timing of goods entry, not a recurring benefit. Tesla's 10-Q clarified it was not related to the February Supreme Court tariff refund ruling.
- **Terafab: $119B vertically integrated chip factory:** Announced March 21st, jointly owned by Tesla, xAI, SpaceX, and Intel in Austin. Target: over 1 terawatt of AI compute per year. Elon stated all current chip fabs on Earth combined produce only ~2% of what Tesla and SpaceX will need across their projects.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla stock lagging despite strengthening fundamentals, per Bhakdi:** Tesla is down ~4.75% year-to-date while Google is up ~26% and the broader Magnificent 7 returned to all-time highs. Bhakdi's price target remains $490 (prior ATH: December 16), arguing the company is fundamentally stronger now with Cybercab in production and robotaxi live in three cities.
- **BYD took top spot for energy storage shipments in 2025:** CATL's global energy storage cell share fell from ~30% to ~22% as competition intensified. Chinese players including Sungrow, CATL, Hithium, Cornex, and EVE Energy are aggressively pursuing Australian and global share at prices Tesla cannot match outside tariff-protected US markets.
- **Powin bankruptcy signals industry consolidation:** The third-largest US energy storage integrator declared bankruptcy, attributed to excessive debt amid hyper-competitive growth — the same pattern as Northvolt in Europe. More consolidation events are expected.
- **SpaceX–xAI merger creates $1.25T combined entity:** The February 2026 merger, described by Reuters as the largest corporate merger ever recorded, combines launch capability with AI compute infrastructure — flagged by Source 2 as relevant context for Tesla's AI training and chip supply ecosystem.
