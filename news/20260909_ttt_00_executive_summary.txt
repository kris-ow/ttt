## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Cybercab count in Texas reached 49 registered vehicles (up from 45), per RobotaxiTracker.com-aligned tracking; the fleet remains legally capped at that level pending the NHTSA review. Wells Fargo called last week's Austin launch event "disappointing," and TSLA fell ~6% the following Friday.
- NHTSA opened an audit query into how Tesla self-certified the steering-wheel-and-pedal-free Cybercab; commentators stress this is fact-finding, not a violation, but note it could delay further scaling.
- Fare data from Austin showed a Cybercab ride priced at $7.77 versus $12.10 for a Model Y on the same route, though Tesla confirmed pricing is dynamic and snapshots aren't representative.
- Multiple creators (Bhakdi, Farzad-FM, Electrified) converged on cost-per-mile as the central unresolved metric: estimates for Cybercab operating cost range from ~20 cents (Musk's original 2024 target) to 80 cents (Farzad-FM's conservative current estimate), with "empty miles" (deadhead/occupancy rate) flagged as a hidden variable that could swing true per-passenger-mile cost by 10+ cents.
- Independent door-safety testing found mixed pinch-detection results — passenger doors passed but the trunk failed a banana test; Tesla reportedly told a tester a software fix is coming.
- A Tesla fleet-purchase interest form drew ~73,000 clicks per Sawyer Merritt before a Tesla engineer's post confirming "tens of thousands" of submissions was deleted — read by one host as a sign third-party fleet ownership is still distant.
- Cybercabs are now rolling off the production line with Starlink hardware built into the hatch tooling, addressing spotty Austin cell coverage seen in early rides; the vehicle does not support AC home charging, relying solely on Superchargers.
- Production line targets one Cybercab every 10 seconds currently, with a longer-term goal of ~5 seconds; specs shared include 47.6 kWh battery, 6.1+ mi/kWh efficiency, 3,113 lb curb weight, 43.4" legroom, and Tesla's largest-ever in-car screen.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek confirmed via Tesla's NHTSA filing that a December 2025 Alabama crash killing driver Kayleigh Page (her infant daughter survived) had automation "Verified Engaged" at 55 mph; Tesla redacted the software version and narrative, so it's unclear whether basic Autopilot or FSD was active. State troopers' original report made no mention of driver-assist involvement.
- FSD V14.3.9 began rolling out with a new Automatic Collision Evasion feature that proactively activates Supervised FSD if it detects an imminent crash or inattentive driver; separately, FSD 14.2 Light began rolling out to Hardware 3 vehicles for the first time.
- Unsupervised miles surpassed 1 million, up from 380,000 at the Q2 call — roughly 17% week-over-week growth, ahead of the 10% guidance Tesla gave; Waymo's unsupervised total is reported at over 220 million miles by comparison.

## SpaceX — Starship
