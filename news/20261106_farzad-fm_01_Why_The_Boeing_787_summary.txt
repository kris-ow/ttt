Notable quotes and data:
- 787 airframe structural mass: ~50% composites, 20% aluminum, 15% titanium, 10% steel, 5% other (per 2013 Boeing chart), versus 70% aluminum on the 777.
- Toray T800S carbon fiber: tensile strength 5,880 MPa (853,000 psi); density 1.80 g/cm³, about one-third lighter than aluminum.
- Composite design gave an average 20% weight savings versus traditional aluminum designs, per a 2006 Boeing report.
- Single-piece composite fuselage sections eliminated 1,500 aluminum sheets and 40,000-50,000 fasteners per section; overall fewer than 10,000 holes drilled on the 787 versus about 1 million on the 747.
- 787 cabin altitude maintained at 6,000 feet versus ~8,000 feet on conventional aluminum jets.
- Boeing's April 2004 787 launch promised 20% lower fuel burn; VP John Feren (2005) attributed ~40% of the gain to new engines and 3-4 points each to materials, aerodynamics, and electrical systems; ANA confirmed 21% savings per flight in actual operation (Nov 2012).
- 787 fleet had carried 1 billion passengers by April 30, 2025, and opened more than 550 new non-stop routes by September 2026.
- June 2009 ground test found wing stringer delamination at ~18 points per wing; first flight delayed to December 15, 2009.
- Boeing halted 787 deliveries from late 2020 to August 2022 (with a brief resumption) over fuselage gap tolerance issues, delivering only 14 aircraft in that window; rework costs estimated at $6.3 billion.
- Deferred 787 production costs peaked at $32.42 billion (March 2016) and stood at about $14.31 billion plus $1.35 billion in unamortized equipment as of March 31, 2026.
- Air India Flight 171 crash (June 12, 2025): 241 of 242 aboard and 19 on the ground killed; preliminary report cited both fuel cutoff switches flipping off within one second of reaching 180 knots.
- Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg said (Oct. 27, 2026 earnings call) 787 production had stabilized at eight aircraft per month; CFO Jesus Malave forecast 90-100 deliveries for 2026.
