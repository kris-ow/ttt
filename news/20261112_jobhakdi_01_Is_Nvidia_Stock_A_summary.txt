This video analyzes Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation amid a scandal involving an alleged affair with a Singaporean executive with purported ties to Chinese intelligence, and debates whether the resulting ~20-27% stock selloff makes Nvidia a buy. The core thesis is "short-term buy, long-term sell": the host expects a recovery into upcoming earnings but believes Nvidia's long-term innovation edge leaves with Huang, drawing a parallel to Apple after Steve Jobs. Tesla and SpaceX appear only tangentially, via Musk's reaction to the news and a mention of a Tesla/SpaceX chip manufacturing ambition and an Nvidia-SpaceX orbital compute project.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia selloff and Huang's resignation:** Nvidia stock fell roughly 20-27% over the prior month, accelerating after the affair allegations broke, with the host noting the board forced Huang out before an internal investigation into the matter had concluded. The host calls this a mistake by what he terms a "mediocre board," drawing a comparison to Sam Altman's brief 2023 OpenAI ouster and reinstatement, and assigns "less than 50%" but "significant" odds that Huang could similarly return if the board is replaced.
2. **Competitor reaction:** AMD and Broadcom shares reportedly each rose meaningfully (the host cites roughly 19-22% gains) in the days following the scandal, which the host attributes to markets pricing in reduced competitive pressure from a destabilized Nvidia.
3. **Investment thesis — short-term buy, long-term sell:** The host argues Nvidia's near-term fundamentals (upcoming earnings, existing contracts, Vera Rubin technology pipeline) are unaffected by Huang's departure for several years, making the stock a short-term buy, but says he personally plans to exit once shares reach around $200, viewing Nvidia's long-term innovation capacity as diminished without Huang — likening the company post-Huang to Apple post-Jobs or to Micron.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Musk's reaction and Terafab ambitions:** The host notes Musk publicly responded positively (calling it "an honor") to a suggestion that he hire Huang, and ties this to Tesla's and SpaceX's planned Terafab chip manufacturing site, described as potentially the largest chip manufacturing facility in the world, expected to go live in roughly four to five years and still in need of leadership.
2. **Nvidia-SpaceX orbital compute project:** The host states Nvidia and SpaceX are developing an orbital AI compute system (referred to as involving an "NV72" GPU rack and Vera Rubin technology) targeted for roughly Q4 2027, as part of Huang's broader legacy of data-center-level innovation.
