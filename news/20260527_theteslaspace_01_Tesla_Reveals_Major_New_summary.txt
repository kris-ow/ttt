This episode of The Tesla Space covers three main stories: the long-awaited Tesla Roadster's production plans finally crystallizing around Giga Texas, the Boring Company's shift toward centralized remote tunneling operations, and Tesla's accelerating Full Self-Driving rollout across Europe. The episode's core thesis is that Tesla's most ambitious and delayed projects — the Roadster, FSD in Europe — are moving from speculation into concrete execution, while the Boring Company is quietly transforming itself from a construction contractor into an automation company.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Roadster confirmed for Giga Texas production:** Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Morvi and chief designer Franz von Holleshausen confirmed on the Ride the Lightning podcast that the next-generation Roadster will be built at Tesla headquarters in Giga Texas. Morvi stated: "We can say it's going to be built in Texas. We've made first plans on that and you start to see a lot of things start to unfold in the next months." The video frames this as a meaningful milestone — allocating production space implies tooling, engineering validation, and factory planning are converging around a real vehicle program, not just a concept.

2. **Roadster's production location history and Tesla's strategic shift to Texas:** When the Roadster was first unveiled, Tesla planned to build it in Fremont. During the Q2 2020 earnings call, Elon Musk said the Roadster would make sense as a California-built product. That has since changed, with Tesla increasingly concentrating its newest and most ambitious programs — Cybercab, Optimus, Terafab, and now the Roadster — around Giga Texas. The video notes the symbolic fit: SpaceX's Starship is built in Texas, and the Roadster is described as a Tesla/SpaceX collaboration confirmed by Elon Musk in 2024.

3. **Original Roadster specs and the delay problem:** The Roadster was first unveiled in November 2017 at Tesla's semi-event, with specs that seemed near-impossible at the time: 0–60 mph in 1.9 seconds, 0–100 in 4.2 seconds, a quarter-mile in 8.8 seconds, over 620 miles of range, and a top speed exceeding 250 mph. Originally targeting 2020 deliveries, the car never launched. The video argues that the industry has since caught up — Tesla's own Model S Plaid (launched 2021) matched the 1.9-second 0–60 claim, and competitors like Lucid, Porsche, Rimac, and Xiaomi's SU7 Ultra (claimed 1.98 seconds at a fraction of the Roadster's expected price) have reached similar or better figures. Simply matching the original specs would no longer be revolutionary in 2026.

4. **Roadster's evolved identity — the last great driver's car:** Elon Musk has repeatedly hinted at a far more extreme version of the Roadster than originally planned. In 2018 he mentioned an optional SpaceX package using small cold gas thrusters (similar to those used on the Falcon 9 for stabilization during re-entry) to enhance acceleration, braking, and cornering. He has also claimed the car could hit 0–60 in under 1 second, and stated: "There will never be another car like this, if you could even call it a car." Most significantly, during Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call, Musk said: "Over time, it's going to make sense for our whole lineup to be autonomous vehicles of different sizes. Long-term, the only manually driven car will be the new Tesla Roadster." This reframes the Roadster as not just the fastest car in the world, but potentially the last great manually driven car before full autonomy arrives.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised approved in Lithuania — second EU country:** Tesla received regulatory approval to deploy FSD Supervised in Lithuania, making it the second European country to activate the system after the Netherlands. Lithuania used the EU's mutual recognition framework to leverage the Dutch approval rather than starting from scratch, completing its own process in under 40 days. By contrast, the Dutch Vehicle Authority had spent more than 18 months evaluating the system using 1.12 million miles of European driving data. Tesla had been quietly preparing behind the scenes — opening FSD ride-along experiences at its Vilnius showroom in late April and hiring FSD vehicle operators in Lithuania and eight other European countries around the same time.

2. **Greece poised to be the third EU country:** The Greek Secretary General of Transport has publicly stated that Greece intends to adopt FSD Supervised using the same Dutch mutual recognition framework as part of an upcoming transportation bill. Greece had hoped to be the second European country to approve it but was narrowly beaten by Lithuania. The video identifies a clear pattern: Tesla is building country-by-country momentum using mutual recognition rules already embedded in the European regulatory system, rather than waiting for a slow continent-wide process.

3. **European FSD mileage milestones and safety data:** Tesla Europe announced that drivers in the Netherlands and Lithuania have collectively logged more than 12.5 million miles (20 million kilometers) with FSD Supervised active. That milestone was reached less than three days after the same markets crossed 9.3 million miles (15 million kilometers) — meaning roughly 3 million miles were added in under 72 hours. Dutch Tesla owners alone surpassed 6.2 million miles in under a month after launch. Tesla also reported that when FSD Supervised is active, the probability of a collision per kilometer driven is up to seven times lower compared to manual driving.
