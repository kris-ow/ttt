## Market & Competition

1. **Anthropic's "Fable" (Mythos) model benchmark performance:** Jo Bhakdi reviews the newly released Claude Fable (Mythos 5) benchmarks, noting it significantly outperforms all prior models across multiple categories. On agentic coding (SWE-bench Pro), Fable scores 80.3% versus Claude Opus 4.8 at 69% — an 11-point jump. Agentic coding capability reportedly rose 29% from 13% for the prior Claude model. Cybersecurity benchmarks jumped from 40% to 78%, and health benchmarks improved from 56% to 66%. Bhakdi notes the model is available free until June 22, 2026, after which it switches to usage credits, which he interprets as a signal of severe compute constraints at Anthropic.

2. **AI compute demand thesis — the "unlimited intelligence" argument:** Bhakdi argues that with models like Opus 4.8 and now Fable, AI has crossed a threshold analogous to a "130 IQ" human worker — sufficient to replace median and even top-quartile white-collar workers. He contends that unlike humans, AI workers can be replicated billions of times if compute is available, meaning demand for intelligence is now functionally unlimited. He cites Jevons Paradox — that lowering the price of a resource increases absolute demand — as the economic mechanism driving this conclusion. He believes this threshold was already crossed before Fable's release.

3. **SpaceX orbital AI satellite and the compute supply thesis:** Bhakdi discusses SpaceX's AI satellite (AI1), describing it as featuring a centralized compute rack of roughly 70–78 Nvidia GPUs (Blackwells or Vera Rubins), deployable liquid radiators, and solar arrays in sun-synchronous orbit providing continuous power. He argues this satellite solves the three constraints on compute expansion — permits (none in space), physical space (abundant), and energy (effectively unlimited, up to a trillion times current Earth consumption from the sun). SpaceX's prospectus cites a $28 trillion labor market TAM, which Bhakdi treats as directly tied to whether AI intelligence demand is unlimited. He states Starlink is merely a "startup booster" for near-term cash flow; the orbital compute business is the real prize.

4. **NASDAQ selloff and Tesla stock commentary:** Bhakdi briefly addresses the day's market selloff, noting the NASDAQ ran up approximately 20% in roughly two and a half months (April–May 2026) and is now in a normal pullback. Tesla was trading around $369–$396 during the broadcast.
