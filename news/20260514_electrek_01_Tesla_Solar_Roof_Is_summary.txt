Tesla's Solar Roof — unveiled in 2016 with a target of 1,000 installations per week by end of 2019 — has installed roughly 3,000 total systems in the US and is being quietly replaced by conventional solar panels as Tesla's residential energy strategy.

## Energy

1. **Solar Roof deployment vs. promises:** Tesla missed its 1,000-per-week target by 97.7% — peak deployment reached only ~23 roofs per week (~2.5 MW/quarter) in Q2 2022. Wood Mackenzie estimated ~3,000 total US Solar Roof installs through early 2023; Tesla disputed the figure but never provided its own number. Tesla stopped reporting solar deployment figures entirely in Q1 2024, removing the line item from quarterly reports. The company's last dedicated Solar Roof post on its official X account was June 23, 2023.

2. **Customer experience and product problems:** Tesla has largely exited direct Solar Roof installation, redirecting customers to a small network of third-party certified installers. In Florida, solar projects have been canceled entirely. Tesla Energy holds a 2.6/5 rating on SolarReviews. The Solar Roof's string inverter architecture — unlike micro-inverters or power optimizers used by competitors — causes partial shading to shut down entire strings, with some owners reporting underperformance of 20% or more vs. contracted estimates. A 2023 class-action settlement cost Tesla $6 million over bait-and-switch pricing allegations. An average Solar Roof costs ~$106,000 before incentives vs. ~$60,000 for a traditional roof plus conventional panels, with a payback period of 15–25 years compared to 7–12 years for standard panels.

3. **Pivot to conventional panels:** Tesla launched the TSP-420 panel (assembled at Gigafactory New York in Buffalo) in early 2026, featuring an 18-zone power optimization system that addresses the shading limitation of Solar Roof. In January 2026, Musk announced at Davos a target of 100 GW/year of US solar manufacturing capacity before end of 2028 — a ~300x increase from Tesla's current ~300 MW annual capacity in Buffalo. Tesla is reportedly in talks to purchase $2.9 billion in Chinese solar equipment, primarily from Suzhou Maxwell Technologies. The company also announced its first solar team expansion in five years and launched a new solar lease product.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Tesla made specific, binding promises to Solar Roof customers — on production levels, energy independence, and durability — and has quietly abandoned those commitments without public acknowledgment, while the pivot to conventional panels is likely the correct business decision going forward.
