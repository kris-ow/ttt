## Brief

- Tesla tells JPMorgan FSD v15 is a "step-change"
- Optimus Gen 3 external sales targeted as early as H2 2027
- Cybercab targets ~$0.30/mile, HW4 claimed sufficient
- SpaceX AI revenue may exceed all else combined in September
- Starlink surpasses 11,000 satellites, eyes 100,000-satellite gen-3
- Roadster unveiling rumored within weeks, SpaceX thruster package teased

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla briefed JPMorgan that FSD v15 rests on seven "core technologies," ~40% already running in the Austin robotaxi fleet, with early feedback "encouraging" — the article notes this is at least the third consecutive version Tesla has pitched as the breakthrough (per Electrek).
- Tesla claims its current HW4 (AI4) computer can run v15 and unsupervised FSD while simultaneously rolling out AI4.5 (~10% more compute, ~2x memory); Electrek notes Tesla made the same "sufficient hardware" claim about HW3 for years before reversing it, with HW3 now downgraded to a reduced FSD v14 "Lite." AI5 has slipped to mid-2027, and Cybercab is now slated to launch on AI4/AI4.5.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla told JPMorgan it's confident in near-term Cybercab scaling via an "unboxed" manufacturing process, is deliberately limiting further Model Y robotaxi conversions, and targets ~$0.30/mile long-run cost, with fleet expansion accelerating from late 2026 into early 2027 contingent on safety. Electrek contrasts this with Tesla's disclosed 380,000 unsupervised miles since launch versus Waymo's 200M+.
- Tesla launched a Cybercab Launch Event promotion in Austin: robotaxi riders between Aug 17–23 are automatically entered for a chance to attend, with each ride earning an extra entry (per Sawyer Merritt); no date or production details disclosed.
- Waymo introduced its 6th-gen "Ojai" vehicle (13 cameras, 6 radars, 4 LiDARs) to LA, Phoenix, and San Francisco fleets, now open to all public riders — underscoring Waymo's sensor-heavy approach versus Tesla's camera-only stack.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla told JPMorgan Optimus Gen 3's design is finalized, its supply chain "essentially locked in," with a production line being installed in Fremont's former Model S/X facility; external sales could start "as early as" H2 2027, with long-term capacity ambitions of ~1M units at Fremont and ~10M in Texas. Electrek notes repeated past delays to the Gen 3 reveal and Musk's prior unmet claim of 5,000 Optimus units last year, versus only a few robots currently in testing.

## Financials
- JPMorgan's Rajat Gupta (replacing longtime bear Ryan Brinkman) upgraded Tesla to Neutral with a ~$445 price target; Electrek characterizes the note as largely relaying Tesla's Fremont guidance without significant pushback, and argues its value lies in what Tesla tells investors privately rather than the rating itself.

## Electric Vehicles
- Roadster unveiling rumored "as soon as this month" per The Information (three unnamed sources), potentially featuring a SpaceX-developed cold-gas thruster package enabling a brief hover; Tesla has made no official announcement. Franz von Holzhausen separately told Jay Leno's Garage a reveal is coming "very soon." Hosts on FutureAzA expect no stock impact and note early "free Roadster" reservation holders got only the base "launch edition," not the thruster package.
- Tesla enabled V2L (vehicle-to-load) on all US Model Y Premium trims with a newer power conversion system, via an $80 outlet adapter (120V/20A, 2.4kW); Tesla also launched a Certified Pre-Owned program (150+ point inspection, remainder of 8-year battery warranty, one free month of FSD Supervised).
- Used Tesla prices reportedly rising, in some cases above new MSRP — e.g., a Portland dealer's 2025 Model 3 (~9,000 miles) priced near $40,000 — attributed largely to expiration of the federal EV incentive (per Brian White, unconfirmed accuracy).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Elon Musk told employees at an August 11 all-hands that AI revenue would probably exceed all other SpaceX revenue combined in September and significantly exceed it in Q4; SpaceX stock reportedly rose 11% the following day. Q2 revenue was cited as AI $2.56B, connectivity $4.29B, launch $962M (combined non-AI $5.25B) — meaning Musk's claim implies AI revenue roughly doubling quarter-over-quarter into September.
- Compute capacity forecasts diverge: one host projects SpaceX compute "significantly north of 2 gigawatts" by year-end, both skeptical hosts across sources peg ~5 gigawatts as more plausible for next year versus Musk's reported ~10GW target, citing available energy supply as the binding constraint.
- xAI's Colossus supercomputer was described as exceeding 200,000 Nvidia GPUs with plans to reach 1.5 million, at 2 gigawatts of power (~1.5 million homes' worth).
- Grok 4.6 was shown by investor Gavin Baker as "Pareto dominant" on quality-vs-cost versus Anthropic's Claude on Cursor Bench; commentators attribute xAI's rapid catch-up to the Cursor acquisition and a SpaceX-personnel management reshuffle roughly six months ago. Grokbot is framed as xAI's enterprise entry point (priced not usable below $200/month), distinct from Digital Optimus per Musk, though one investor views them as targeting the same market; current enterprise AI revenue tied to Grok/Cursor was estimated around $4 billion.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX has surpassed 11,000 Starlink satellites in orbit, adding roughly 1,300 this year, and requested FCC approval last month for a third-generation constellation of up to 100,000 satellites.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- Chinese company LandSpace landed the first stage of its ZQ-3 rocket for the first time, a milestone in international reusable-launch competition relevant to SpaceX's market position.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Cognition denied a Bloomberg report claiming SpaceX sought to acquire the company; no further detail on the original claim was provided.
