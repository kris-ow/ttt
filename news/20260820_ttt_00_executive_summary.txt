## Brief

- Tesla tells JPMorgan FSD v15 is a "step-change"
- Cybercab targeting ~$0.30/mile long-run cost
- Optimus Gen 3 external sales "as early as" H2 2027
- SpaceX stock reportedly rose 11% on Musk's AI-revenue claim
- Starlink surpasses 11,000 satellites, eyes 100,000-satellite gen-3
- Roadster unveiling rumored within weeks, per The Information

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla briefed JPMorgan that FSD v15 is built on seven "core technologies," ~40% already running in the Austin robotaxi fleet, with "encouraging" early feedback, per Electrek. Tesla claims current HW4/AI4 hardware is sufficient for unsupervised driving while simultaneously rolling out AI4.5 (~10% more compute, ~2x memory); Electrek notes Tesla made the same "sufficient hardware" claim about HW3 for years before reversing it, and AI5 has slipped to mid-2027.
- Electrek's editorial stance: the outlet says it will treat unsupervised FSD on HW4 as real only when a customer car does it with Tesla accepting liability, framing v15 as at least the third straight version pitched as the breakthrough.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla told JPMorgan it's confident it can scale Cybercab near-term, is deliberately limiting further Model Y robotaxi conversions, and is using an "unboxed" manufacturing process, targeting ~$0.30/mile long-run cost with fleet expansion accelerating from late 2026 into early 2027. Electrek contrasts this with Tesla's disclosed 380,000 unsupervised miles since launch versus Waymo's 200M+.
- Tesla announced a Cybercab Launch Event in Austin; robotaxi rides taken August 17–23 automatically enter riders for a chance to attend, per Sawyer Merritt — a ridership-driving marketing push with no date or production figures disclosed.
- Waymo launched its 6th-gen "Ojai" vehicle (13 cameras, 6 radars, 4 LiDARs) to public riders in LA, Phoenix, and San Francisco, underscoring its sensor-heavy approach versus Tesla's camera-only strategy.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla told JPMorgan Optimus Gen 3's design is finalized, supply chain "essentially locked in," with a production line being installed in Fremont's former Model S/X facility; external sales could start "as early as" H2 2027, with long-term capacity ambitions of ~1 million units at Fremont and ~10 million in Texas. Electrek notes repeated past delays to the Gen 3 reveal and Musk's prior (unmet) claim of 5,000 Optimus units last year.
- Digital Optimus remains distinct from Grokbot and has not yet been publicly demonstrated, per commentary on a Gavin Baker/Jo Bhakdi conversation.

## Electric Vehicles
- Roadster unveiling rumored within roughly two weeks per a report from The Information (three unnamed sources), potentially featuring a SpaceX-developed cold-gas thruster package; Tesla has made no official announcement. Chief designer Franz von Holzhausen separately told Jay Leno's Garage a reveal is coming "very soon."
- Tesla enabled V2L on US Model Y Premium trims with newer power conversion hardware, via an $80 outlet adapter (120V/20A, 2.4kW); a Powerwall gateway is still required for grid-selling.
- Tesla's Certified Pre-Owned program went live in the US/Puerto Rico with a 150+ point inspection, remaining 8-year battery warranty, and one free month of FSD Supervised plus premium connectivity.
- Used Tesla prices reportedly rising, with some listings above new MSRP (e.g., a Carvana Model 3 RWD listing ~14% over sticker, per an unconfirmed source); attributed partly to expiration of the federal EV incentive.

## Financials
- JPMorgan's Rajat Gupta (replacing longtime bear Ryan Brinkman) upgraded Tesla to Neutral with a ~$445 price target; Electrek characterizes the note as largely relaying Tesla's Fremont guidance without significant pushback.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Elon Musk told employees at an August 11 all-hands that AI revenue could exceed all other SpaceX revenue combined in September and significantly exceed it in Q4; SpaceX stock reportedly rose 11% the following day, per one source. Cited Q2 revenue breakdown: AI $2.56B, connectivity $4.29B, launch $962M.
- Compute capacity forecasts diverge: one source expects SpaceX to exceed 2 GW by year-end with 5 GW more plausible than Musk's reported ~10 GW target for next year; a third-party "semi analysis" reportedly called 10 GW "within reach," while Jo Bhakdi models a 5 GW bull case worth over $300B in potential data rental revenue versus ~$500B at 10 GW.
- Gavin Baker (via Jo Bhakdi commentary) says Grok 4.6 now beats Anthropic's Claude on a quality-vs-cost basis, attributing xAI's catch-up to the Cursor acquisition and a SpaceX personnel reshuffle into xAI management; enterprise AI revenue tied to Grok/Cursor currently estimated around $4 billion, with Bhakdi cautioning proof of much larger revenue growth is still needed.
- xAI's Colossus supercomputer cited at 2 GW (enough to power 1.5 million homes) with over 200,000 Nvidia GPUs currently, expanding toward a planned 1.5 million.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink has surpassed 11,000 satellites in orbit, adding roughly 1,300 this year; SpaceX has also requested FCC approval for a third-generation constellation of up to 100,000 satellites.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- Chinese company LandSpace successfully landed its ZQ-3 rocket first stage for the first time, a milestone in growing international reusable-launch competition.
