Covenant Logistics, one of the largest US trucking companies (2,600+ tractors, $1.16B in 2025 revenue), completed a two-week Tesla Semi evaluation in California, including a loaded run over the Grapevine pass on I-5. The company's VP of Sustainability and Innovation reported the driver "was amazed at the performance of the Tesla Semi and felt a level of confidence that was hard to match in a diesel truck."

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Grapevine evaluation:** Covenant tested the 500-mile long-range Tesla Semi with a major customer in California. The evaluation included a loaded run over Tejon Pass (4,160 ft elevation, 6% grade for 5 miles), one of the most demanding freight corridors in the US. McLelland highlighted regenerative braking as a structural advantage on the steep southbound descent — recovering energy rather than risking brake fade. A full evaluation summary is described as "coming soon."

2. **Growing pattern of fleet evaluations:** Covenant joins a list of operators conducting structured Tesla Semi trials since Tesla began volume production at its Nevada Gigafactory on April 29. ArcBest logged 4,494 miles over three weeks, averaging 1.55 kWh/mile. DHL Supply Chain completed a 3,000-mile trial averaging 1.72 kWh/mile at full load and has since taken delivery of its first Semi. CEVA Logistics reported avoiding 4.38 metric tons of CO₂ on a West Coast trial. MDB Transportation and AiLO Logistics launched three-week port drayage pilots in late April. Tesla has also landed a 60-truck order from port drayage fleets.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that driver preference for the Tesla Semi on demanding routes like the Grapevine removes a key friction point in fleet adoption, and that with positive results now spanning long-haul, regional, and port drayage operators, the bottleneck may be shifting from product validation to whether Tesla can scale production fast enough.
