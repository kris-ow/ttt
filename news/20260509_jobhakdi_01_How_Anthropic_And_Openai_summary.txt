Jo Bhakdi argues that two newly announced joint ventures — one by Anthropic and one by OpenAI, each partnering with major private equity and asset management firms — represent a structural turning point in the deployment of AI as a mass labor replacement technology. His core thesis is that the primary barrier to widespread job displacement is no longer capability (which he claims is already sufficient to replace the majority of white-collar workers) but distribution, and that these deals solve the distribution problem by routing AI deployment through private equity firms that collectively own or control vast numbers of mid-sized companies. He predicts a tsunami of job destruction arriving by 2027, with early signals potentially appearing by January of that year.

## Market & Competition

1. **Anthropic's joint venture with private equity:** On Monday (relative to the video's publish date), Anthropic announced a joint venture focused on deploying enterprise AI services. Founding partners include Blackstone, Helman and Friedman, and Goldman Sachs. Additional backers include Apollo Global Management, General Atlantic, GIC, Leonard Green, and Sequoia Capital. The Wall Street Journal reported the venture is valued at $1.5 billion, with $300 million committed each from Anthropic, Blackstone, and Helman and Friedman.

2. **OpenAI's parallel "Development Company" venture:** Hours after the Anthropic announcement, Bloomberg reported that OpenAI is raising funds for a new venture called "the Development Company," targeting $4 billion from 19 investors against a $10 billion valuation. Named investors include TPG, Brookfield Asset Management, and Bain Capital. Bhakdi notes there is no apparent overlap between OpenAI's and Anthropic's respective investor groups, suggesting the two ventures together cover a broad swath of the private equity market.

3. **The distribution logic: private equity as the deployment channel:** Bhakdi's central structural argument is that the critical missing link between AI capability and mass enterprise deployment is mid-sized companies, which represent the majority of the economy in his view. Large enterprises (Fortune 500) are already being served directly by AI labs and consulting firms like Accenture, McKinsey, and BCG. Small businesses are too fragmented to matter at scale. Mid-sized companies, however, are heavily owned or controlled by private equity — making private equity the ideal distribution partner. These ventures give Anthropic and OpenAI preferred sales access to the portfolio companies of their PE partners, while the PE firms gain early access to AI tools they can use to cut labor costs and drive margins.

4. **"Forward deployed engineers" as the implementation model:** Bhakdi notes that Anthropic's announcement explicitly references the "forward deployed engineer" model popularized by Palantir, in which AI consultants embed with a company's existing teams (clinicians, IT staff, etc.) to build tools that fit into existing workflows. He interprets this as a sanitized description of a process that ultimately results in large-scale workforce reduction.

5. **AI funding at scale:** Bhakdi cites OpenAI announcing $122 billion in new funding at the end of March 2026 against a valuation of $852 billion. He also cites a TechCrunch report that Anthropic is in the final stages of a funding round seeking $50 billion in new funding against a $900 billion valuation. He additionally references Anthropic doing compute deals with SpaceX (Colossus One), Google (TPU access), and Amazon.

6. **Bhakdi's labor displacement thesis:** Bhakdi claims that as of the video's publish date, AI agents are already capable of replacing over 50% of white-collar and administrative jobs, and that within 8–12 weeks this figure will rise to approximately 80%. He defines the affected category broadly: any job involving sitting at a screen, typing, and emailing — spanning healthcare administration, law firms, accounting firms, insurance companies, hospital management, and government administration. He frames the technology barrier as fully solved and the remaining challenge as purely one of deployment and distribution.

7. **2027 job destruction prediction:** Bhakdi states he made this prediction roughly 16 months prior to the video and reaffirms it: 2027 will be the year a "tsunami" of job destruction becomes visible to the public, with the inflection potentially arriving as early as January 2027 or as late as July–August 2027. He explicitly discounts official U.S. unemployment statistics as structurally incapable of capturing this displacement, arguing that the survey methodology (which counts any paid work, including informal gigs) means measured unemployment cannot rise meaningfully even in a scenario of mass job loss.

8. **Economic decoupling thesis:** Bhakdi predicts that mass job destruction will not trigger a traditional recession. Instead, he argues the economy will increasingly decouple — with companies and shareholders capturing enormous value from AI productivity while a large share of the population loses income. He frames this as a theoretical possibility within AGI-era economics where aggregate output growth does not require broad consumer purchasing power.
