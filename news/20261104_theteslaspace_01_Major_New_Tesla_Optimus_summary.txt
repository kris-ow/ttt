## Humanoid Bots
1. **Leaked details on Tesla's Optimus data-collection program:** Tech commentator Robert Scoble posted on X (an unconfirmed, single-source claim per the host) that Tesla workers at its Palo Alto research facility have been running what he calls "Robot University" for many months, contradicting press reports that the program had just started. Scoble says workers wear suits weighing about 24 lb loaded with up to 40 cameras and 3D sensor grids, working in multiple squads across three 24-hour shifts, with similar efforts reportedly spotted at Giga Texas. Scoble said he could not reveal his source for this information, and Tesla has not confirmed these specific numbers.
2. **Prior reporting corroborates a real but evolving program:** Business Insider reported in November 2025 that it observed a glass-walled Tesla lab where workers wore helmets with five cameras and backpacks weighing 30–40 lb during 8-hour shifts doing repetitive tasks like wiping tables. If Scoble's newer account is accurate, the hardware has become lighter (24 lb vs. 30–40 lb) while capturing far more camera angles (up to 40 vs. 5) in under a year.
3. **Tasks split between "home data" and factory data:** According to Scoble, some teams capture everyday tasks like making a sandwich ("home data"), while others record factory work such as installing HVAC systems in cars; workers reportedly don't know the origin of their assigned tasks.
4. **Why Optimus needs so much more data than Tesla's cars:** Elon Musk said on Tesla's Q3 earnings call that, unlike cars, Optimus lacks a large existing customer fleet to source training data from. Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's VP of AI software, compared this to the difference between watching soccer and playing it — observation alone doesn't build skill, so the robot must practice directly.
5. **"Optimus Academy" and self-play at scale:** Musk said Tesla needs on the order of 10,000–20,000 Optimus units to run self-play, where robots attempt tasks and improve iteratively without human guidance, comparing it to a toddler learning to walk through practice. Elluswamy said combining human-taught instruction with simulation and real-world self-practice is "pretty close to having very good results" (his characterization, not a specific metric).
6. **Data collection program extends beyond California:** German outlet Handelsblatt reported in July that selected Giga Berlin workers would wear backpack cameras during normal assembly work, citing an internal Tesla memo. The host also notes roughly 45 Optimus data-collection job openings across 15 US cities on Tesla's careers page, most in cities without a Tesla factory.
7. **Workers nickname robots "clumpers":** Per Scoble, factory workers have started calling the robots "clumpers" because they are too heavy and walk loudly rather than softly; Scoble expects this to be fixed over time but says Optimus still has unresolved refinements.
8. **Next Optimus demo video and AI5 chip timing:** Musk said on the Q3 earnings call that a new Optimus video is roughly one to two months away and will show the robot performing non-pre-programmed tasks. Musk also said Tesla plans to put its next-generation AI5 chip into Optimus around April of next year, which he said offers roughly an order of magnitude more compute than the current AI4 chip.
