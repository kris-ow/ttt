This video from the "electrified" channel covers a wide range of Tesla news, with particular focus on the Tesla Semi's growing order book and the Watt EV "truck-as-a-service" business model, updates on FSD regulatory progress in the EU and China, competition in autonomous driving, and a grab-bag of smaller Tesla news items including brand loyalty data, new product updates, and the Boring Company's Nashville residential loop announcement.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi Orders and Watt EV Deployment:** Watt EV announced an order for 370 Tesla Semis to be deployed across California. Deliveries are expected to begin with 50 trucks in 2026, with the full fleet operational by the end of 2027. More than 300 of those trucks will be deployed under a joint program with the Port of Oakland. The deployment will be supported by megawatt charging depots at the Port of Oakland and Fresno, with additional planned sites in Stockton and Sacramento. Watt EV also has 15 additional charging sites under development as part of a broader statewide and eventual nationwide network.

2. **Watt EV's "Truck-as-a-Service" Model:** The host argues that a widely overlooked aspect of the Tesla Semi story is Watt EV's business model. Rather than simply operating its own freight fleet, Watt EV offers a turnkey electrification solution — bundling the truck, access to charging depots, maintenance, telematics, and operational support into a single monthly subscription. This eliminates the major upfront capital cost for smaller fleet operators looking to electrify. Fleets that already own electric trucks can also subscribe separately to Watt EV's charging depot network. The host contends this model — mirrored by companies like Alio and Volvo on Demand — will be a critical accelerant for electric semi adoption, particularly for smaller operators, while large players like Amazon, Walmart, and FedEx are expected to continue buying direct.

3. **Tesla Semi Dashboard Tracker:** The host announced they are building a crowdsourced dashboard (vibe-coded with Perplexity Computer) to track Tesla Semi orders and the status of megawatt charging depots nationwide. The tracker will be made publicly available within the next week or two, and currently shows confirmed orders from Watt EV, NICA, and Big F Transport, along with depot locations filterable by operational, under construction, and planned status.

4. **Cybertruck Lease Price Increase:** Tesla raised the price of the Cybertruck Premium all-wheel drive lease in the US by 10%. The host declined to read deeply into this but expressed eagerness to see Cybertruck sales for Q2 and Q3 2026, hoping for a directional improvement rather than a dramatic jump. Anecdotal evidence and lower-priced trim deliveries are cited as reasons for cautious optimism.

5. **Model Y L Five-Star Safety Rating and Imminent US Launch:** Tesla's Model Y L received a five-star safety rating from ANCAP. A mystery car spotted under a cover at Giga Texas is speculated by the host to be the Model Y L, with the release described as feeling "imminent" for the US market.

6. **Tesla Semi ACT Expo UI and Mega Charger Showcase:** David Moss captured footage of the Tesla Semi at the ACT Expo in Las Vegas, showing the truck's UI — including deck lights, side storage, hood vitals, charge port controls, and per-tire PSI and temperature readouts. The Tesla Semi's Mega Charger handle and stall scale were also showcased, described as significantly larger than standard charging hardware.

7. **Next-Generation Roadster Trademark:** Sawyer found a new trademark application for what appears to be the next-generation Tesla Roadster logo or badge. The filing describes lines depicting speed, propulsion, heat, or wind. One observer noted the shape closely resembles SpaceX's Starship V3 quick-disconnect receptacle panel, reinforcing speculation about a SpaceX technology collaboration for the vehicle.

8. **Battery Health Certificate in Tesla App:** A Tesla app decompile revealed Tesla is adding an official, exportable digital certificate confirming high-voltage battery repairs or replacements directly in the app. The host highlights this as a meaningful improvement for the used EV market, where verifying battery health has historically been a limiting factor for buyers.

9. **Giga Texas Test Track Under Construction:** Joe Tegtmeyer shared footage of asphalt being laid at Giga Texas for a new on-site test track, which will allow Tesla to conduct proving ground-type testing — including high-speed handling, braking, acceleration, and durability testing — without shipping vehicles to a remote location.

10. **Tesla Brand Loyalty Leads All US Brands:** S&P Global Mobility data for February 2026 shows Tesla at 61.1% brand loyalty, ranking number one in the US ahead of Subaru, Toyota, and Ferrari. The host notes this sharply contrasts with headlines from August 2025 claiming Tesla's brand loyalty had collapsed following Elon Musk's political activities.

11. **FSD One-Time Purchase Ending in Europe:** Tesla is ending one-time FSD purchases across Europe on May 21st, 2026, affecting the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark, and others — consistent with Tesla's strategy in North America to shift customers toward subscriptions.

12. **Tesla Supercharger Lounge in France:** Tesla is opening a Supercharger lounge in partnership with BK World in Lyon, France, featuring 28 Superchargers. This is the fifth joint location in France.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **EU FSD Regulatory Update — No Vote at May 5th Meeting:** The May 5th TCMV meeting passed without a vote on supervised FSD approval across the EU. Regulators sat through approximately one hour of FSD demonstrations covering European traffic and mixed weather conditions, but raised concerns about the system occasionally exceeding speed limits, wildlife hazards (specifically moose in Nordic countries), icy roads, and the name "Full Self-Driving" itself. There was also reported pushback related to political pressure from Tesla supporters contacting officials.

2. **Next EU Vote Window and Individual Country Paths:** The next opportunity for an EU-wide vote is June 30th, 2026. If no vote occurs then, the next gathering is in October. Roughly 15 of 27 member countries would need to vote in favor for the measure to pass. The host describes a broad EU FSD rollout this summer as "pretty optimistic" and suggests Q4 2026 may be a more realistic timeline. In the interim, individual countries can choose to recognize the Netherlands' existing approval independently.

3. **Belgium, Denmark, Sweden, and Switzerland Express Interest:** Belgium's Minister of Mobility asked Tesla to transfer supervised FSD files to his administration, instructing it to provide clarity on a possible "rapid homologation" by end of week. A regulator from Denmark stated Tesla's vehicles "performed very well in the complex traffic of rush hour in Copenhagen." Sweden's transport agency said the country is "generally positive on automated driving tech as long as it follows regulations." Switzerland's regulator (Astra) said it has no problem with level two plus systems as long as Tesla clearly communicates that the driver remains fully responsible.

4. **FSD in China — Internal Employee Trial and Q3 Approval Target:** An unverified account reported that Tesla made a version of FSD (cited as version 14.3.2) with full functionality available to employees in China. Separately, analyst Ming noted Tesla recently added a confidentiality clause to its employee FSD trial policy in China, with violations carrying a penalty of approximately $732,000 — suggesting Tesla employees are unlikely to be leaking information. On Tesla's Q1 earnings call, the company stated it expects broader FSD approval in China in Q3 2026. The host characterizes FSD's status in China as "a black box" and describes the current approval as limited, with Tesla working to gather data and accelerate the process given strong local competition.

5. **Momenta — Key Chinese Autonomous Driving Competitor to Watch:** The host introduces Momenta, a Chinese autonomous driving software company that has been operating for roughly a decade. Momenta supplies its AI software to OEMs rather than building vehicles itself, and uses an end-to-end neural network approach handling perception, planning, and control — similar to Tesla's FSD architecture. The company uses lidar but claims its software is system- and chip-agnostic. Investors include GM, Mercedes, and Tencent. More than 50 production vehicle models have been delivered with Momenta's software, representing hundreds of thousands of vehicles. Momenta has two products: M Pilot (level two plus, driver-assisted) and MSD (targeting full autonomy at levels three and four). It also has a strategic partnership with Uber to launch in Europe. The host frames Momenta as a significant competitive threat to Tesla FSD in China and a name to watch in Europe.

6. **Huawei NCA Incident in China:** The host briefly noted a reported incident in which Huawei's NCA level two autonomous driving technology struck a child. The individual who shared video of the incident on social media was reportedly reported to police and forced to sign a pledge not to spread negative news about Huawei. Chinese authorities are alleged to be suppressing further discussion.

## Market & Competition

1. **Nuro Receives California DMV Permit for Driverless Lucid Gravity Testing:** The California DMV modified Nuro's existing driverless AV permit to include the Lucid Gravity SUV, allowing Nuro to test its autonomous system without a safety driver on public roads. Nuro has held a driverless permit in California for six years, previously covering only low-speed autonomous delivery vehicles. However, Nuro still requires two additional approvals — a driverless ride-hailing permit from the CPUC and a separate commercial deployment permit from the DMV — before it can offer paid rides. The host notes that, like Tesla, Nuro has not yet applied for the CPUC permit needed for a true AV passenger service, putting both companies at a similar stage for commercialization despite Nuro being slightly ahead on the DMV testing permit.

2. **Tesla Regulatory Credits in Australia:** Tesla earned $3 million in regulatory credits sold to legacy automakers in Australia under the country's new vehicle emission standards, which came into force in 2025 — the first year such income was generated. The host notes that emission targets tighten annually, meaning demand for Tesla's credits is likely to grow. Also highlighted: battery storage has grown from 15% of Tesla's Australian revenue in 2023 to 55% in 2025, surpassing EVs as the primary revenue driver in that market.

## Energy

1. **Tesla Battery Storage Dominates Australia Revenue:** In Australia, battery storage now accounts for 55% of Tesla's revenue, up from 15% in 2023, despite a substantial fall in battery prices over that period. The host frames this as evidence of the surging success of Tesla's storage business internationally and notes that regulatory vehicle credits do not cover the storage segment.
