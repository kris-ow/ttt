## Robotaxi

- **Austin unsupervised zone now covers the entire metro.** On June 3, Tesla expanded its driverless operating boundary to include suburbs such as Pflugerville and Manor, I-35 corridors, Giga Texas, and Austin-Bergstrom International Airport. The prior boundary was ~245 square miles; the new coverage more than doubled that. This marks roughly the fifth boundary expansion since limited driverless rides began in January 2026.

- **Expansion pace materially faster than Waymo.** Tesla went from a ~20-square-mile sliver a year ago to the entire Austin metro in under twelve months. The host of *brighterwithherbert* attributes this to Tesla expanding one general driving system rather than hand-mapping each neighborhood — each new zone gets easier to add, not harder.

- **Six additional metro launches targeted before end of June 2026.** Community analysts and some commentators expect Tesla to copy the Austin playbook into six more cities this month. Speed of replication is framed as the key signal to watch.

- **36 Robotaxi job postings reveal a fully commercial operational buildout.** Roles span 24/7 fleet operations (night-shift supervisors in Palo Alto), field incident response specialists in Austin, Dallas, and Houston (including on-site tire swaps), a Robotaxi Business Manager tasked with global commercial expansion, a dynamic pricing engineer building surge and demand-based algorithms, and a Senior Real Estate Manager securing support, parking, and storage locations nationally.

- **Logistics role confirms North America-wide deployment scope** — explicitly including Canada and Mexico, not just the U.S. The ideal candidate has experience in auto transport, rail, or high-value freight.

- **Grand Prairie, Texas facility confirmed as Robotaxi depot template.** The 51,000 sq ft facility near Dallas-Fort Worth, originally permitted as a sales/service/delivery center, was revised in city documents to also charge, clean, and maintain autonomous vehicles. Both *brighterwithherbert* and Herbert Ong's *Brilliant Advice* segment treat this physical real estate commitment as a more credible long-term signal than software announcements alone.

- **Customer-owned Cybercab network integration still more than a year away**, per CFA Cern Basher (Brilliant Advice). None of the 36 Robotaxi job listings reference interfacing with customer-owned vehicles. Tesla is expected to establish its company-owned fleet at scale first, partly for quality control (e.g., verifying tires meet safety standards before a vehicle enters the network).

- **Code hardening role makes miles per intervention the primary commercial metric.** A Senior Software Engineer listing states: *"The goal of Full Self-Driving isn't just to get it working — it's to make it more dependable than any human driver could ever be."* Herbert and Cern interpret this as confirmation that the software is functional and focus has shifted to reliability at scale.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Estonia approved FSD supervised on May 29**, becoming the third EU country to greenlight it after Lithuania (earlier in May 2026) and the Netherlands. Tesla's official Europe account confirmed a rollout starting soon.

- **~1.3 million vehicles worldwide now have FSD access**, per Ashok Elluswami at the CVPR conference in Denver on June 3. Elluswami also showed a slide of countries where approval remains pending — including Germany, France, the UK, Italy, Spain, India, Japan, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Brazil. Each pending approval represents near-zero marginal cost revenue upside once granted.

- **FSD 14 Light for Hardware 3 still not shipped.** Hardware 4 cars receive FSD the day a new country approves it; HW3 vehicles are awaiting a lighter FSD v14 build, which Tesla is targeting for later summer 2026. Landing this version would significantly widen the pool of eligible vehicles across newly approved markets overnight.

- **Belgium FSD homologation clears the 5,000 km public-road testing requirement.** Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder confirmed the test file has moved to her administration for formal homologation advice. Approval has not yet been granted, but this is a material procedural step.

- **Coast-to-coast Canada FSD drive completed with zero claimed disengagements.** Three owners drove Vancouver to Halifax — 3,760 miles in just under five days — on FSD v14.3.3, including autonomous parking at Supercharger stops. Ashok Elluswami publicly congratulated them. The host of *brighterwithherbert* notes this was a supervised enthusiast drive and the zero-disengagement claim is self-reported, not audited.

## Humanoid Bots

- **~208 Optimus job openings across 10 cities signal a full-scale robotics company buildout.** Top locations: Palo Alto (111), Fremont (44), Austin (17), Langley BC (10, actuators), Athens Greece (6). Additional postings in Reno, Houston, Dallas, Sparks NV, Sunnyvale, San Diego, and others.

- **65 manufacturing and industrialization roles are the single largest Optimus hiring category** — covering production line maintenance, process engineering, precision machining, general assembly, molding, power electronics, and supplier quality. Cern Basher frames this as the definitive signal: "You do not staff a 65-person manufacturing organization for a research project."

- **Digital Optimus is emerging as a standalone AI agent software product.** Six of 17 AI/ML Optimus listings contain "Digital Optimus" in the title — including Reinforcement Learning Engineer and AI Engineer for Generalizability. Cern and Herbert describe it as a digital counterpart that can reason, learn, and operate computers, competing directly with Anthropic Claude and OpenAI's agentic AI products. They identify 2026–2027 as the key market window.

- **"Engineering Technician Optimus Hand Repair" role confirms hands are deployed and wearing out in real-world use.** Cern treats the existence of a dedicated hand-repair technician role as evidence the Optimus hand has moved beyond development into active operational deployment.

- **21 data collection listings include night-shift operators walking 7+ hours with 30 lbs of motion capture equipment.** Human operators in Austin, Houston, Dallas, Fremont, and other cities are generating physical training data for Optimus — a model analogous to what Chinese robotics companies are already doing at scale.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y L caught testing under camouflage at Fremont**, captured by drone. The stretched, three-row, six-seat captain's chair variant is on sale in China at ~$47,000. Fremont testing is the strongest indication yet of a North American launch; analyst estimates put US production start around September 2026. Pricing and range for the US market are unconfirmed.

- **US Model Y RWD ($39,990) and AWD ($41,990) now include all-black headliner and 16" center screen as standard** — a meaningful value add with no price increase, per Sawyer Merritt.

- **Tesla introduced a "Coastal Blue" exterior exclusively for the base Model Y in Europe at €1,300.** This brings the Europe Model Y to four distinct blue variants. The exclusivity suggests targeted demand stimulus in a key market.

- **Model 3 US incentives modestly tightened.** Free Supercharging on Model 3 Premium orders ends June 15; base Model 3 RWD lease price rose from $299 to $329/month; Model Y AWD financing rate moved from 0% to 0.99% APR. Sawyer Merritt reads this as demand or margin management heading into Q2.

- **Cybercab casting design shifts to a skeletal structural frame**, eliminating large flat areas that add weight without structural contribution. Giga casting expert Luca Greco (FutureAzA) notes this approach is feasible specifically because the Cybercab is not a conventional consumer vehicle, and could not be directly applied to the Model Y.

## Market & Competition

- **Waymo's "Waymo One High" is a new vehicle, not a new city.** The *brighterwithherbert* host corrects a widespread misconception: it is a Zeekr-built minivan rebranded for US riders, now available to select San Francisco, LA, and Phoenix riders as of May 28. The vehicle carries 13 cameras, four lidar units, and six radar sensors — a fundamentally different (and more expensive) cost structure than Tesla's camera-only stack.

- **BYD became the first automaker globally to accept full crash liability for Level 2 city driving assist in China.** The host reads this as the same directional signal as Lemonade's FSD insurance pricing: parties financially exposed to crashes are growing more comfortable with assisted driving systems.

- **Lemonade autonomous car insurance now operating in three states** (Arizona, Oregon, Indiana), charging ~50% less per mile driven on FSD. Co-founder Shai Wininger's stated rationale: FSD miles are approximately twice as safe as human-driven miles based on their pricing models.

- **Anthropic raised $3 billion at a $965 billion valuation** (May 28) and filed confidentially to go public (June 1). Elon Musk is reportedly renting his Colossus 1 data center in Memphis to Anthropic because xAI's Grok hasn't captured comparable market traction — meaning a competitor is paying for compute Tesla's network helped build.

- **Terafab — a ~$55 billion AI compute facility in Grimes County, Texas — cleared a key local vote.** Grimes County commissioners voted 4-1 on June 3 to grant a 35-year property tax exemption worth over $2 billion to SpaceX. Partners include SpaceX, Tesla, xAI, and Intel; the stated goal is 1 terawatt of AI compute per year. Tesla's role is as a customer and co-partner with a slice of compute earmarked for FSD chips and Optimus — not as owner or operator. Elon flagged that Grimes County is the front runner but not locked.

- **Ferrari's CEO ruled out full autonomy**, explicitly stating driver engagement is core to the brand. The *brighterwithherbert* host frames legacy luxury brands ceding the autonomy space as a structural long-term tailwind for Tesla.
