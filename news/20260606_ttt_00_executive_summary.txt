## Autonomous Driving

- **Estonia approves FSD supervised, third EU country greenlit.** May 29 approval follows Lithuania (earlier May 2026) and the Netherlands. Ashok Elluswami confirmed at CVPR (June 3) that approximately 1.3 million vehicles worldwide now have FSD access; a slide shown at CVPR listed Germany, France, the UK, Italy, Spain, India, Japan, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Brazil among markets still pending — each representing a near-zero-marginal-cost revenue unlock when approved.

- **FSD 14 Light for Hardware 3 still pending.** HW4 vehicles receive FSD the day a country approves; HW3 owners must wait for a lighter variant Tesla is targeting "later in the summer of 2026." Once it ships, it materially widens the addressable installed base across newly approved markets overnight.

- **Belgium clears 5,000 km public-road testing threshold.** Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder confirmed the test file has moved to her administration for formal homologation advice — a procedural step forward, though formal approval has not yet been granted.

- **Vancouver-to-Halifax FSD drive: 3,760 miles, zero disengagements claimed.** Three owners completed the coast-to-coast Canada run in under five days on FSD 14.3.3, including autonomous Supercharger parking. Ashok publicly congratulated them. The drive was supervised with a driver present, and the zero-disengagement figure is owner-reported, not audited by Tesla.

## Robotaxi

- **Austin unsupervised zone expands to the entire metro effective June 3.** The new boundary encompasses Pflugerville, Manor, I-35, Giga Texas, and Austin-Bergstrom International Airport. Tesla's prior covered area was approximately 245 square miles; coverage more than doubled. This is roughly the fifth boundary expansion since driverless rides began in January 2026. Tesla did not publish exact new square mileage; third-party figures are estimates.

- **Pace of expansion contrasts sharply with Waymo.** Tesla went from a ~20-square-mile zone last June to the entire Austin metro in under a year. Waymo expanded San Francisco and Phoenix block by block over years. The architectural distinction: Tesla is scaling one general driving system, not hand-mapping each neighborhood.

- **51,000 sq ft Grand Prairie, Texas facility permitted for robotaxi operations.** City documents confirm Tesla revised plans for the Dallas-area site so it can charge, clean, and maintain autonomous vehicles — functioning as a robotaxi depot alongside its sales, service, and delivery role. Herbert and Cern Basher (Brilliant Advice) frame this as the physical infrastructure template for every city Tesla enters.

- **Tesla's 36 open robotaxi job listings signal commercial-scale buildout.** Roles span 24/7 fleet operations (Palo Alto night-shift supervisor, fleet support specialists), field incident response specialists in Austin, Dallas, and Houston with physical duties including on-site wheel swaps, a Robotaxi Business Manager tasked with "global commercial expansion strategy," a Senior Real Estate Manager securing parking and storage sites (including a listing in Pennsylvania suggesting East Coast expansion), a Senior TPM for charging automation with a mandate to influence future hardware design, and full-stack engineers building dynamic and surge pricing algorithms with variables including base costs, taxes, tolls, and real-time market demand. A logistics role explicitly covers Canada and Mexico — not just the U.S.

- **Customer-owned Cybercab network integration is more than a year away, per Cern Basher.** None of the 36 robotaxi listings reference interfacing with customer-owned vehicles, which Basher reads as a signal Tesla will establish its company-owned fleet at scale before onboarding personal Cybercabs — likely beyond the end of 2026 at the earliest.

## Humanoid Bots

- **~208 open Optimus job listings across 10 cities.** Breakdown: Palo Alto 111, Fremont 44, Austin 17, Langley BC 10 (actuators), Athens Greece 6. Additional postings in Reno, Houston, Dallas, Sparks NV, Sunnyvale, San Diego, Littleton CO, Draper UT, Tempe AZ, and Henderson NV.

- **65 manufacturing and industrialization roles are the largest single job category.** Postings cover production line maintenance, process engineering, precision machining, general assembly testing, molding, power electronics, and supplier quality. Basher's read: you do not staff a 65-person manufacturing organization for a research project — this is "the machine that makes the machine."

- **"Engineering Technician Optimus Hand Repair" posting confirms hands are in active field use.** The role focuses on hands-on repair of the Optimus humanoid robot hand, which Basher interprets as evidence of real-world wear and tear beyond the development phase.

- **Digital Optimus emerges as a distinct software-AI-agent product.** 17 AI/ML/Digital Optimus listings include Reinforcement Learning Engineer, AI Engineer for Generalizability, and AI Infrastructure Engineer roles. Herbert and Basher position Digital Optimus as a software product that can scale faster than hardware, competing directly with Anthropic Claude and OpenAI's agentic AI — with 2026–2027 identified as the key market window.

- **21 data collection listings include a night-shift operator role requiring 7+ hours of walking with 30 lbs of motion capture equipment.** This confirms Tesla is generating Optimus training data via human operators wearing physical capture gear — a model already deployed at scale by Chinese robotics companies.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y L spotted testing under camouflage at Fremont.** The stretched three-row variant — roughly six additional inches of wheelbase, six-seat captain's chair layout, on sale in China at ~$47,000 — has now been captured by drone on the Fremont test track, the strongest indication yet of a North American launch. Tesla has made no official US announcement; analysts estimate US production could begin around September 2026.

- **Model Y US base spec upgraded at no price increase.** The $39,990 RWD and $41,990 AWD now include the all-black headliner and 16-inch center screen as standard equipment.

- **Tesla modestly tightening US Model 3 incentives.** The 1-year free Supercharging promotion on Model 3 Premium ends June 15; Model 3 RWD lease rose from $299 to $329/month; Model Y AWD financing moved from 0% to 0.99% APR — suggesting improved demand or margin management heading into Q2 2026.

- **New "Coastal Blue" color launched exclusively for base Model Y in Europe at €1,300.** Brings total blue variants to four on the European Model Y.

- **Tesla's Cybercab casting design has shifted to a skeletal structural frame.** Luca Greco (independent giga casting expert, FutureAzA) notes the latest Cybercab rear casting eliminates large flat areas that add weight without structural contribution — a design approach feasible specifically because the Cybercab is not a conventional consumer vehicle and is not applicable to the Model Y.

## Market & Competition

- **Waymo's new vehicle is a cost-reduction move, not a new city.** "Waymo One High" is a rebadged Zeekr minivan from China — cheaper to build, more durable for fleet use — now available to select riders in SF, LA, and Phoenix as of May 28. The vehicle carries 13 cameras, four lidar units, and six radar sensors on top of an imported chassis. Tesla's camera-only, in-house-chip stack on an existing production line is structurally cheaper if the vision-only approach continues to hold.

- **BYD takes on crash liability for Level 2 city driving assist in China** — the first automaker in the world to promise full damage coverage for a customer crash while using its assisted driving mode. Framed as a demand-side confidence signal for the broader industry shift toward autonomous liability acceptance.

- **China giga casting adoption is overwhelming, with significant machine oversupply.** Virtually all Chinese OEMs are implementing giga casting; suppliers built excess capacity now sitting unused for at least five years. Greco notes Western OEMs could potentially acquire brand-new, unused Chinese machines at a discount. Dongfang is close to launching a full battery tray cast on a 16,000-ton machine in Wuhan — the largest known giga casting machine in existence.

- **Terafab: ~$55B compute facility in Grimes County, Texas, approved 4-1 on June 3.** Partners include SpaceX, Tesla, xAI, and Intel. Target: 1 terawatt of AI compute per year. Grimes County granted a 35-year property tax exemption worth over $2 billion to SpaceX. Tesla's role is as a customer and co-partner with a compute allocation earmarked for FSD chips and Optimus — not as project owner. Elon flagged Grimes County as a front-runner but not locked.

- **SpaceX compute revenue run rate: $2.17B/month ($26B annualized).** A $920M/month Cloud Service Agreement with Google was announced, combining with a prior Anthropic deal. SpaceX is upsizing its Japan IPO by 25% to $2.5B. Morgan Stanley projects SpaceX revenue at $3.4 trillion by 2040; both Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs project ~$160B in SpaceX revenue by 2028. Not directly Tesla-relevant but material to Tesla investors tracking Elon's capital allocation and the broader AI compute buildout.

- **US data center construction spending hit $50B annualized in March 2026** — up 34% year-over-year, up 437% since 2021, and now exceeding total US office construction ($46B, lowest since 2015). Bhakdi (jobhakdi) argues terrestrial physical constraints — land, energy, turbine scarcity — will redirect AI compute capital toward SpaceX's orbital platform, with Starship commercial readiness projected "more likely than not" by 2027.
