## Autonomous Driving

- **Austin safety milestone — 1.8x safer than humans:** Analyst Reigns, using NHTSA's human baseline of one accident per 249,000 miles and fleet-size data from RobotaxiTracker.com to estimate monthly mileage, found that Tesla's Austin robotaxi crossed the human safety threshold on a three-month rolling basis for the first time in April 2026. The calculation carries caveats: 17 incidents is a small sample, every incident to date had a human safety monitor present, and Tesla does not disclose clean unsupervised mileage figures.

- **17 unredacted NHTSA crash narratives released:** Tesla retroactively unredacted all crash report narratives back to the Austin launch on June 22, 2025. RobotaxiTracker.com's fault attribution found roughly 53% attributable to other parties and ~41% to Tesla; EV Wire calculated an average incident speed of ~5.9 mph with no major injuries across all 17 events.

- **Only 3 of 7 "Tesla at-fault" incidents involved the autonomous system:** Two were caused by remote teleoperators taking manual control — one drove the car over a curb into a metal fence, another ran it into a construction barricade at ~9 mph. Two more were low-speed cosmetic curb scrapes. The three genuine ADS errors all involved low-speed precision around unusual stationary objects: a chain strung across a parking lot entrance, a gooseneck trailer hitch protruding into a street, and a tow truck bed on a tight road.

- **Autonomous tire response captured in incident data:** In December 2025, after driving over uneven pavement that punctured a rear tire, the vehicle independently detected pressure loss, slowed to ~18 mph, and pulled over to a minimal risk condition without human input. The tire clipping the curb during that maneuver is why the event appears in the crash database at all.

- **Tesla rebrands FSD as "Tesla Assisted Driving" in mainland China:** The new Chinese name — 特斯拉辅助驾驶 — drops both "Full Self-Driving" and "Intelligent," reflecting stricter Chinese regulatory standards. Tesla still uses "Full Self-Driving" on its Hong Kong English-language site, which operates under separate traffic regulations. A Chinese media real-world ADAS test ranked Tesla first on both highway and city segments, but by a narrow margin over domestic rivals, several of which offer comparable systems at no cost.

- **FSD Europe shifts to subscription-only:** Tesla has eliminated one-time FSD purchase options across Europe, moving to £99/month in the UK and €99/month across most other European countries. The change converts a capital purchase into recurring revenue and lowers the adoption barrier, though lifetime value will depend on retention.

- **Tesla FSD fleet as AI data engine:** Per farzad-fm's AI competitive analysis, Tesla has over 1.2 million vehicles actively running FSD, generating labeled real-world perception data continuously — a dataset the source argues Waymo's ~few million accumulated miles over 15 years cannot match in volume or refresh rate.

## Robotaxi

- **Regulatory thesis: evidence and political cover arrived together in May 2026:** Per brighterwithherbert, the shift from redacted incident counts to fully readable crash narratives (evidence) coincided with a New York Times guest essay by neurosurgeon Dr. Jonathan Slotkin — co-signed by other doctors and nurses — calling faster AV regulatory approval a "public health imperative" (political cover). The video argues both are prerequisites before regulators can permit monitor-free scaling.

- **Waymo peer-reviewed safety data cited by medical community:** The NYT essay cited a Waymo study across 56 million+ driverless miles showing 91% fewer serious-injury-or-worse crashes and 80% fewer any-injury crashes versus humans on the same roads. A separate projection cited in the essay: 30% AV adoption could prevent 40% of crashes.

- **Waymo vs. Tesla federal incident counts are not a clean comparison:** Monthly NHTSA counts for 2026 (January: Waymo 100, Tesla 5; February: Waymo 73, Tesla 0; March: Waymo 50, Tesla 2) reflect Waymo operating across ~10 cities versus Tesla's Austin-only ADS classification. Waymo's all-time total per Mayhof tracker exceeds 1,800 incidents; Tesla's is 17 — a reflection of scale and geography, per the source, not necessarily a safety differential.

This is a significant named hyperscaler win and reinforces Megapack's positioning as critical infrastructure for the AI buildout.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX prospectus TAM: $28.5 trillion; analyst argues Tesla is central to ~80% of it:** Per Cern Basher (CFA, Brilliant Advice) via brighterwithherbert, SpaceX's IPO prospectus states a $28.5 trillion TAM. The largest segment — ~$22.7 trillion (~80%) — is AI-powered enterprise applications ("Digital Optimus" on Tesla's side), which Basher argues uses the same perception-planning-control architecture as FSD applied to computer desktop video feeds. The second-largest segment (~8%) is AI infrastructure including Terafab (described as a Tesla-SpaceX JV) and Dojo/AI6. Basher's conclusion: Tesla investors have not yet modeled Digital Optimus as a standalone revenue opportunity.

- **SpaceX IPO expected ~June 2026; Tesla market cap referenced at ~$1.4 trillion:** Basher presents a five-scenario merger valuation framework, noting the key variable is ownership ratio at time of merger rather than absolute price. In his base scenario with both companies at ~$2 trillion at merger, Tesla shareholders hold 50% of a $4 trillion combined entity. If SpaceX rises to $2.5 trillion pre-merger while Tesla stays at $2 trillion, Tesla shareholders hold only 44.4% — requiring ~$6.25 trillion in combined synergies to make them whole at a $25 trillion standalone Tesla future value. Elon Musk's current SpaceX voting control is ~85% via Class B super-voting shares; a merger would reduce this to ~54%.

- **Colossus build speed cited in SpaceX prospectus as competitive moat:** Colossus 1 built in 122 days, Colossus 2 in 91 days, versus ~2 years for comparable competitor builds, per the prospectus. As of February 2026, the Memphis complex houses ~555,000 Nvidia GPUs representing ~$18 billion in silicon, approaching 2 gigawatts — described by farzad-fm as ~4x the next largest dedicated AI training site.

- **Mercedes plans urban autonomous driving launch in Germany by end of 2026:** Mercedes announced a point-to-point automated urban driving system for selected German cities by year-end, per Sawyer Merritt. The system is expected to be ODD-constrained and city-limited, materially different in scope from Tesla's FSD approach.

- **AI competitive landscape — structural dynamics:** Per farzad-fm's analysis, training costs have grown ~2.4x per year since 2016 (GPT-3: $4–12M in 2020; GPT-4: $40–100M in 2023), with a single frontier training run potentially exceeding $1 billion by 2027. OpenAI posted a net loss of $13.5 billion in H1 2025 and projects $14 billion in losses against ~$20 billion in revenue for 2026. Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to $125–145 billion (vs. $72 billion in all of 2025). The host identifies Google and the Musk integrated stack (xAI + SpaceX + Tesla) as the two likely AI winners by 2030.
