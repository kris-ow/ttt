This episode of "brighterwithherbert" covers three major developments the host argues have shifted Tesla's trajectory: the SpaceX S1 IPO filing revealing a $28.5 trillion total addressable market heavily weighted toward AI enterprise applications, new Tesla robotaxi infrastructure discoveries in the Dallas–Fort Worth area, and updated NHTSA safety data showing Tesla's robotaxi is safer than human drivers. Host Herbert is joined by panelists Jeff Lutz, Cern Basher, and Larry Goldberg for analysis across all three topics, plus discussion of FSD approval in Lithuania, the status of FSD in China, Elon Musk's OpenAI lawsuit loss, and the FSD v14.3 rollout.

## Robotaxi

1. **New Robotaxi Depot Discovered in Dallas–Fort Worth:** A permit filing revealed a Tesla robotaxi service facility in Irving, Texas (Dallas–Fort Worth area), featuring 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building spaces, 17 EV charging stations, and 24/7 operations including cleaning and tire replacement. The panelists note this facility mirrors a previously reported Las Vegas facility of similar size and scope, and Jeff Lutz emphasizes that the Cybercab's interior was designed from day one for automated servicing — with seat layouts and vehicle access designed to minimize human intervention in maintenance workflows.

2. **Unsupervised Fleet Count Flat After Recent Spike:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised (driverless) robotaxi fleet stands at 39 vehicles — 28 in Austin, 5 in Dallas, 6 in Houston — after having spiked from roughly 2 unsupervised vehicles to nearly 40 in a short period. The panel attributes the recent plateau to Tesla sizing the fleet to a quality threshold: they believe Tesla staggers fleet expansion in discrete jumps tied to software release quality targets, rather than a continuous ramp, to avoid concentrating vehicles with unresolved convenience issues in a single market.

3. **NHTSA Safety Data: Tesla Robotaxi 1.81x Safer Than Humans:** Updated NHTSA incident data covering events since the Austin robotaxi launch in June 2025 shows 17 total reported incidents. AI-assisted analysis of the now-unredacted narratives classified 53% of incidents as other-party fault and 41% as Tesla's fault — equating to approximately 7 incidents where Tesla was at fault. Of those 7, categories included curb strikes, backing collisions, sideswipes, road hazards, and a collision with a stationary object, with all incidents occurring at very low speeds (approximately 4 mph or less). An analyst named "Reigns" (as referenced in transcript) calculated Tesla's robotaxi is 1.81 times safer than human drivers as of April 2026, even including curb rashes. Jeff Lutz flagged that curb rash events are reportable for robotaxis but not for human drivers, suggesting the safety advantage is understated.

4. **Fleet Expansion Model: Staggered Jumps, Not a Smooth Curve:** Jeff Lutz described Tesla's fleet expansion methodology as mathematically driven — Tesla's internal model determines how many additional unsupervised vehicles can be added while keeping the total fleet failure rate within acceptable bounds. He expects fleet growth to appear as discrete step-ups following software improvements rather than a continuous 45-degree ramp.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD v14.3 Rollout — Dramatically Reduced Nag:** FSD version 14.3, which Elon Musk described as a "banger," began rolling out and all three panelists confirmed receipt. The headline feature is a significantly reduced attention nag. Larry Goldberg reported inadvertently driving extended distances in Texas while reading emails before realizing he had not been nagged. Jeff Lutz noted the Model X showed far fewer nags than the Cybertruck on the same build, and flagged one new bug: the system was frequently routing vehicles to park in rear alleyways of shopping centers in more than 50% of his visits. The panel's overall assessment is that v14.3 represents a meaningful step toward unsupervised operation.

2. **FSD Approved in Lithuania — Second European Country:** Following the Netherlands' approval of FSD supervised on April 11, 2026, Lithuania became the second European country to approve FSD supervised. Greece stated it intends to be next (likely June 2026), Belgium's two regions both expressed support for approval, and Sweden was characterized as still deliberating basic highway scenarios. The full EU member-state vote is expected in either June or September 2026.

3. **FSD in China — Job Postings Signal Imminent Progress:** Tesla China posted approximately six urgent job listings related to AI, which the panel interprets as preparation for a data-capture effort required by Chinese regulators before granting FSD approval. The transcript references a Tesla CFO statement from the most recent earnings call that FSD approval in China is expected in Q3 2026. Jeff Lutz noted that the job postings are a reliable forward-looking signal that approval is more imminent than distant, though panelists acknowledged China's regulatory timeline is inherently unpredictable.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX S1 Filing: $28.5 Trillion TAM, Primarily AI Enterprise:** SpaceX's S1 IPO filing revealed a total addressable market of $28.5 trillion broken down into launch (~$370 billion), Starlink broadband and mobile connectivity (~$1.6 trillion), and AI enterprise applications (~$22.7 trillion). The panel argues the launch business is the structural enabler of the entire stack — without Starship's cost-per-launch economics, competitors cannot build equivalent AI infrastructure at scale. Larry Goldberg estimated SpaceX is 10–20 years ahead of competitors in launch capability.

2. **Anthropic Deal: $1.25 Billion Per Month Through May 2029:** SpaceX's S1 revealed the Anthropic AI compute contract is valued at $1.25 billion per month through May 2029, covering both Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 (GB200s), with a 90-day termination clause. Elon Musk publicly stated additional partners beyond Anthropic are in place. The panel noted analyst Brian Wang had previously estimated roughly $16 billion, which appears consistent with the revealed figures. Cern Basher framed the deal as evidence that Anthropic needs the compute more than SpaceX needs the revenue — cancellation would threaten Anthropic's ability to operate, not just scale.

3. **Elon Musk Loses OpenAI Lawsuit on Statute of Limitations:** A court dismissed Elon Musk's lawsuit against Sam Altman and Greg Brockman on a statute of limitations technicality (three-year limit), without ruling on the merits. Musk announced he will appeal to the Ninth Circuit, citing concern that allowing the behavior to go unpunished would set a precedent for looting charitable organizations. Larry Goldberg assessed the Ninth Circuit as an unfavorable venue and suggested the more viable path, if pursued, would be a further appeal to the Supreme Court. Sam Altman announced OpenAI's IPO plans within 48 hours of the ruling.

4. **Potential Tesla–SpaceX Merger:** All three panelists said "yes" when asked if a Tesla–SpaceX merger would eventually occur, though all qualified the answer as a long-term possibility and stated that no actual deal terms are known. The panel noted the S1 filing contains what they characterize as breadcrumbs hinting at a potential combination, with a full picture potentially taking 6 months to a year to emerge.
