This is a live session from Jo Bhakdi covering the Iran ceasefire deal and its market impact, SpaceX's IPO dynamics, and Tesla's near-term stock outlook. The core thesis is that the Iran deal removes a major macro overhang, setting up a broader market rally that Jo Bhakdi believes will disproportionately benefit Tesla — driven by an imminent Cybercab/robotaxi scaling catalyst — while SpaceX's post-IPO mechanics will eventually cause a sharp correction. The session blends macro geopolitical analysis with tactical trading commentary on Tesla, SpaceX, Bitcoin, and Ethereum.

## Market & Competition

1. **Iran Ceasefire as Market Catalyst:** Jo Bhakdi argues the Iran ceasefire was predictable because the Strait of Hormuz closure represented a hard economic forcing function — he claims he predicted the war's end five weeks prior to this session. He states that if the strait had remained closed past end of June 2026, a global economic catastrophe would have been unavoidable, and that the U.S. administration's deep ties to oil and economics circles made ending the conflict before that deadline a near-certainty. With the deal announced, NASDAQ rallied approximately 2.89%, the S&P 500 rose 1.61%, and the Dow Jones gained approximately 1%.

2. **Bitcoin and Ethereum as Leading Indicators:** Jo Bhakdi notes Bitcoin bounced off a key floor near $60,000, recovering from roughly $63,694 to $66,589 in the five days around the ceasefire announcement. Ethereum rose approximately 5.74% in a single day, recovering from the $1,500 range. He treats both as leading indicators for broader market sentiment and believes they have built up significant upside potential.

3. **SpaceX IPO Mechanics — Buy Now, Crash Later:** Jo Bhakdi describes SpaceX's post-IPO rally (up approximately 43% since IPO, reaching near $194) as the result of three converging forces: (1) maximum global hype, (2) forced buying by index funds due to Russell inclusion on June 26, 2026 and NASDAQ 100 inclusion on July 6, 2026, and (3) lockup clauses preventing insiders and retail IPO buyers from selling for 15–180 days. He argues these forces guarantee continued near-term upside but predicts a sharp crash once buying pressure fades and unlock windows open — potentially driving SpaceX stock to $80–$100 — creating a long-term buying opportunity. He explicitly states he does not currently own SpaceX and considers it tactically overvalued.

4. **SpaceX Valuation Concerns — Revenue Model Insufficient:** Jo Bhakdi argues that SpaceX's current compute deals (citing Anthropic and Google as examples) cannot sustain the valuation of what he describes as a ~$2.2 trillion company, estimating those deals generate roughly $20 billion per year — insufficient to justify the price. He believes the AI satellite business (Starlink AI constellation) is the eventual justification but that it cannot be accelerated to the first half of 2027, leaving a gap that will drive the crash.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Deployment Scaling Across Texas:** Jo Bhakdi highlights a community-sourced image showing 55 Cybercabs in what appears to be a Dallas-area deployment, describing this as evidence of rapid physical scaling across Texas cities including Austin, Dallas, and Houston. He frames this as consistent with Tesla's earnings call guidance (approximately four to four and a half months prior to this session) that Tesla planned to add five more cities by the end of the first half of 2026.

2. **Two-Week Probability Window for a Major Robotaxi Announcement:** Jo Bhakdi expresses high conviction that the next two weeks represent a "local probability maximum" for a significant robotaxi announcement or expansion event, citing the end of H1 2026 as a catalyst deadline. He states Tesla is unlikely to have a demand problem — citing a community report of high-volume hail demand for Tesla robotaxis in the Bay Area the prior weekend — and that supply scaling is the binding constraint. He warns that if Tesla stock rises to 420–440 without a Cybercab catalyst, he will reduce leverage.

3. **FSD 14.3.4 Viewed as a Major Leap:** Jo Bhakdi describes FSD version 14.3.4 as a significant upgrade relative to prior versions, noting it has received strongly positive reviews even from previously critical observers. He characterizes this as a sign Tesla is approaching readiness for broader unsupervised deployment, suggesting the gap to full-scale robotaxi operation is now measured in "days or weeks."

4. **Cybercab Platform Camera Architecture Not a Concern:** Responding to a viewer question about whether Cybercab's different form factor requires extensive FSD retraining, Jo Bhakdi argues Tesla would not build a Cybercab platform with camera placements materially different from the Model Y, as that would require full retraining of the FSD system — something he considers an obvious mistake Tesla would not make.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3.4 Deployment and Summer Scaling Timeline:** Jo Bhakdi discusses FSD version 14.3.4 as the current version driving Cybercab deployment confidence, contrasting it with 14.3.3 which he says still had "some issues here and there." He believes the rapid deployment of Cybercabs across Texas and Nevada reflects Tesla's internal confidence that the software is near production-ready. He dismisses concerns about winter weather limiting robotaxi scaling, stating he is not certain that is a constraint.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Core Business Rebounding as a Secondary Tailwind:** Jo Bhakdi notes that independent of robotaxi, Tesla's core vehicle business is recovering, with energy (including solar) rebounding and Tesla Semi expected to become a meaningful contributor in H2 2026. He predicts Semi will evolve into a "robot trucking" platform that becomes a significant earnings driver by 2027, providing tailwinds to Tesla's financials even before robotaxi revenue scales materially.
