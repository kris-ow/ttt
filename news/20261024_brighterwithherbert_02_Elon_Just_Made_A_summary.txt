This episode recaps Tesla's Q3 earnings report and call, focusing on new dates and targets set across Robotaxi/Cybercab, FSD, Optimus, Terafab, and the SpaceX merger process, while noting the stock fell about 3% the next day despite the news.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet and mileage growth:** Tesla's Robotaxi safety report (posted by Ashok Elluswamy) stated the fleet has driven 3.3 million autonomous miles total with no injuries or airbag deployments; the presentation showed unmanaged/unsupervised miles grew eightfold in the quarter to nearly 2 million by quarter-end, with double-digit weekly growth. Elon said the Cybercab fleet in Austin grew tenfold since its early-September launch.
2. **Current footprint and new cities:** Ashok said Tesla "currently operates in six cities" (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa per Sawyer Merritt's compiled list), with Cybercab service coming to Dallas next month. Tesla's official robotaxi account separately announced four more target cities — Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio — with no dates yet; a Cybertruck was spotted testing in San Antonio this week.
3. **Austin service expansion:** Following the earnings call, Tesla extended Austin robotaxi hours from midnight to 1:00 a.m. and expanded the service area (~8 additional square miles, ~272 total per the robotaxi tracker) to cover the area around the Formula 1 track ahead of a 400,000-person event; Ashok said 24/7 operation, highway driving, and operation in harder weather are targeted by year-end. Cybercabs also began using a stretch of I-35 in Austin previously limited to Model Ys.
4. **Fleet registrations:** Community trackers showed the Texas robotaxi registry rising from 769 to 858 cars week-over-week (+89), almost all Model Ys, with the daily registration pace rising from roughly 3/day in July to about 13/day in October; including Florida's roughly 168 active vehicles, total registrations reportedly crossed 1,000 for the first time. Lars Moravy said Cybertruck production isn't the bottleneck for robotaxi supply, but it's unclear why Model Y additions are outpacing Cybertruck additions.
5. **Cybercab customer sales and economics:** Lars Moravy said Tesla is "trying" to get Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, starting small while Tesla keeps most units for its own network; Elon said the vast majority of Cybercabs will be owned by Tesla as a transportation service. Elon said robotaxi financial impact will be minimal this quarter but should become noticeable by Q2 of next year and a "big deal" by Q3/Q4 of next year; Tesla has not yet hit its 30-cents-per-mile cost target but sees a path to it, per CFO Vaibhav Taneja.
6. **Elon's long-term prediction:** Elon stated, "Unless governments stop us, Tesla will eventually do most of the passenger transportation on Earth in any country where it is allowed to operate freely," explicitly asking people to hold him to the forecast.
7. **NHTSA inquiry deadline:** NHTSA asked Tesla in September how it certified a vehicle without a steering wheel or pedals; Tesla's response is due October 30. No problems have been found so far, but the topic was not raised during the earnings call.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate and revenue:** Vaibhav Taneja said over 75% of new North American deliveries took paid FSD at delivery in Q3, up from 55% the prior quarter; paying FSD customers reached 1.74 million (up 67% year-over-year), with roughly 260,000 added in the quarter (~2,800/day), half now on monthly subscription. Analyst Pierre Ferragu asked if FSD revenue is approaching roughly $1 billion/year, which Vaibhav confirmed; Tesla plans to begin disclosing FSD revenue and growth rates going forward.
2. **Version 15 and robotaxi fleet access timeline:** Ashok Elluswamy said FSD version 15 uses the same software stack as Cybercab/Model Y robotaxis and will reach customer cars soon; Tesla expects to connect the first customer-owned vehicles to the robotaxi fleet around Q2 of next year, starting with a small subset within existing Tesla service areas under Tesla's fleet management system. Shortly after, the Tesla_App_iOS account found code referencing a VIN-based registration process for adding personal vehicles to the robotaxi fleet with status tracking (pending/active/dispatched/deleted).
3. **International regulatory progress:** FSD is live in nine EU countries with over 100,000 users; Elon hopes for EU-wide approval within a few months and said he'd be surprised if it takes longer than Q1 of next year, though timing depends on regulators. South Korea's transport minister confirmed Tesla has applied for FSD approval there; currently only US-assembled Teslas (not Korea's China-assembled units) can use FSD, and Vaibhav said the Model Y was likely South Korea's best-selling passenger car last quarter.
4. **AI mapping:** Ashok said Tesla is building its own AI-based maps, which will begin appearing with version 15.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production start:** Elon said Optimus production has begun on a high-performance line in Fremont, with the first units now coming off the line — a plant that was still being installed as of the July call.
2. **Timeline and ramp expectations:** Elon said it will take at least a year, possibly longer, to reach a production pace of 20,000 units per week, citing roughly 10,000 things that need to go right, ongoing design changes, durability requirements, and AI refinement needed for less-structured tasks; he expects these issues resolved around the end of 2027, when Tesla plans to begin supplying Optimus to industrial customers first (dangerous/monotonous, round-the-clock work), with consumer versions much later.
3. **Upcoming milestones:** Elon said a video may come in a month or two showing Optimus performing unprogrammed actions; around April, Optimus is expected to get the AI5 chip with roughly ten times the compute of the current chip. Vaibhav said construction has begun on a second Optimus plant in Austin with a capacity far exceeding Fremont's — a line targeted at 10 million units per year.

## Financials
1. **Q3 results:** Tesla reported $0.48 EPS versus $0.45 expected, revenue of about $28 billion roughly in line with expectations, and an operating margin of just 2.6% as R&D, AI compute, and new-product spending rose about 40% year-over-year; Vaibhav said this elevated spending will continue until new products are commercialized.
2. **Capex guidance cut:** Tesla lowered its 2026 capex forecast to about $20 billion from over $25 billion, which Elon attributed to spending coming in lower than expected, with capex rising again next year and reaching "tens of billions" by 2028. Tesla holds a reserved $30 billion credit line it does not plan to use this year; cash on hand hit a record $45 billion, and free cash flow was about $1.5 billion.
3. **Stock reaction and analyst targets:** Shares fell about 3% the day after earnings amid broader Nasdaq weakness tied to AI-spending concerns, but most sell-side analysts raised price targets: Piper Sandler to $475, Mizuho to $460, JP Morgan to $430, UBS to $425, Morgan Stanley to $410; CLSA upgraded its rating, and JP Morgan cited higher perceived odds of a Tesla–SpaceX merger as a factor.
4. **Delivery volumes and auto business:** Tesla delivered about 486,000 vehicles in Q3 — the second straight quarter above 480,000 — with Vaibhav citing battery supply as a production bottleneck; the order book is the largest in four years. Tesla raised financing rates on most Model Y trims in the US from 1.99% to 2.49%.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger process status:** Elon declined to elaborate on the Tesla–SpaceX merger during Tesla's call, referring back to comments made at SpaceX's earnings call the prior day, where he said both companies formed special committees (since he's an interested party and can't participate in merger discussions), that a merger isn't inevitable, and that while he personally favors it, shareholders must ultimately decide. Tesla's 10-Q confirmed its board formed a special committee to study deepening the SpaceX relationship, including possible business combinations; SpaceX's filing confirmed its own committee, with no decisions made and no guarantee of a merger.
2. **Approval structure:** Tesla's report frames the matter as requiring shareholder approval, while SpaceX's report frames it as requiring approval by its controlling shareholder (Elon) — implying Tesla's public shareholders would be the key vote if terms are ever agreed. Per Bloomberg, Elon reportedly wants to close a merger before the end of the current US presidential term (unconfirmed); per Reuters, some insiders are reportedly concerned his public comments could delay the process by several weeks (unconfirmed). No merger item is on the December 3 annual meeting ballot.

## Energy
1. **Megapack sold out and margin dynamics:** Elon said Tesla has "effectively sold out almost all of the Megapacks by 2027" and is ramping production as fast as possible, driven partly by AI data center energy demand, with gross margin expected to improve significantly. Energy segment margin reached 33% this quarter, but this was largely attributed to one-time tariff rebates and battery-supplier cost reimbursements; energy revenue actually fell 16% year-over-year. The Megapack plant in Texas began production this quarter.
2. **Solar expansion:** Fort Bend County near Houston approved tax breaks for Tesla's proposed $10 billion "Crystal Sun" solar plant, projected to create about 9,700 jobs, with solar panel production targeted for early 2029; Tesla has not finalized the location and is still evaluating other states.

## Market & Competition / Electric Vehicles
1. **Terafab chip timeline:** Elon gave the first concrete Terafab dates: equipment installation at the Austin facility begins in the first half of next year, with a first test chip targeted for around May or June (contingent on things going well) and mass production targeted for late 2028. Cost is estimated in the tens of billions, with Tesla and SpaceX still determining cost-sharing; a Korean newspaper reported (unconfirmed by Tesla) that Tesla is prepaying about $800 million for a dedicated Samsung line for circuit boards and chips.
2. **Semi and Roadster:** Lars Moravy confirmed the Semi is in production in Nevada, with the top trim rated for 500 miles of range; no autonomous-driving timeline for the Semi was given this quarter, despite Elon mentioning a self-driving Semi target for this year back in July. The Roadster demo remains listed as "design development" with no production date.
3. **Cybertruck setbacks:** Tesla told Australia/New Zealand pre-order holders the Cybertruck will not be sold in those markets, offering a free year of FSD with a new Model 3 or Model Y purchase instead — a move described as unpopular with affected customers.
4. **Tracking July promises:** Several July commitments were delivered on schedule or ahead — Cybercab rides began September 3, Optimus lines in Fremont are now producing robots, the Megapack Texas plant is in production, and robotaxi weekly mileage growth (eightfold in the quarter) beat the roughly 10%/week target. Capex guidance came down (seen as reasonable), but the previously stated goal of a fully autonomous Semi by year-end was not mentioned on this call, leaving its timeline unclear.
