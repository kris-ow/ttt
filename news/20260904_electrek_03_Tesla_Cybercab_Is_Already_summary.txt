NHTSA opened a federal audit into Tesla's Cybercab on the same day the company began putting paying passengers in it in Austin, examining how Tesla certified a vehicle with no steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors as road-legal.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
1. **NHTSA audit details:** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened "Open Audit Query" AQ26002 on September 3, prompted by public information, covering an estimated 1,000 Cybercab vehicles. The agency is examining "the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab and related issues," including "the extent to which Tesla's certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable to the Cybercab."

2. **Self-certification issue:** US automakers self-certify compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS) rather than receiving pre-approval; many FMVSS assume a human driver with a steering wheel, pedals, and mirrors, none of which the Cybercab has. Tesla told NHTSA it certified the Cybercab as compliant with all applicable FMVSS and plans to gradually expand deployment to more vehicles and locations.

3. **Broader regulatory context:** The article notes NHTSA already has an open probe into "Full Self-Driving" covering 3.2 million vehicles tied to visibility-related crashes, and previously collected crash data on a Tesla remote operator crashing a Robotaxi earlier this year; the Cybercab runs software similar to FSD.

4. **Launch details:** Tesla commercially launched Cybercab in Austin with no livestreamed reveal, stage presentation, or public remarks from Elon Musk; a small group of shareholders and influencers rode first, reportedly under NDAs, before rides opened more broadly via the Robotaxi app.

## Editorial Stance
The author expects the NHTSA investigation to likely be another "nothingburger," similar to past probes like the Cybertruck launch investigation. The author also reiterates a prior claim that Tesla plans to deploy Cybercab only within its geo-fenced robotaxi service rather than selling it to the public, and speculates Tesla may try to have fleet owners buy Cybercabs to run their own robotaxi services, shifting deployment burden — calling this "going to be a mess."
