## Brief

- Tesla announces Cybercab launch event, ride lottery through Aug 23
- The Information: internal Tesla target for Austin public Cybercab launch this month
- NHTSA data: zero at-fault Robotaxi crashes mid-June to mid-July
- Driverless Robotaxi rams through bollards in Austin, no injuries
- Einride orders 500 Tesla Semis for Amazon, largest deal yet
- Model Y becomes Korea's best-selling new car in July

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Robotaxi account is running a promotion: complete a ride by 11:59pm Aug 23 for a chance to attend a "Cybercab launch event," with winners announced by 8-9am Aug 25. Multiple hosts read this as a strong signal a public Cybercab rollout is imminent in Austin.
- The Information reported, citing unnamed sources, that Tesla told staff it is targeting a public Cybercab launch in Austin as soon as this month, with a sequence of employee rides on private roads (underway), then employee rides on public roads, then public rollout. Commentators view the timeline as plausible but caution internal targets can slip.
- Drone footage showed the largest CyberCab test fleet yet lined up at Giga Texas with hatches open, plus new Supercharger installations at the test track's operations center — read as fleet-wide launch prep.
- A local TV report described Cybercab as a two-passenger vehicle with about 293 miles of range, still in on-road testing/employee pilots rather than commercial service; at least 15 Cybercabs were reportedly spotted in Franklin, Tennessee.
- Tesla's robotaxi app now supports multi-stop trips, letting riders edit a destination and start a new ride in the same car without booking separate trips.
- Waymo has imported roughly 3,200 China-built Zeekr robotaxi vans into the US despite a tariff described as doubled — a strategy questioned given minivans oversize the market's dominant one/two-passenger ride profile.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA data covering roughly mid-June to mid-July shows zero crashes caused by Tesla's Robotaxi fleet; the two recorded incidents both involved other drivers striking stopped or parked Robotaxis in Houston and Dallas.
- A driverless Model Y Robotaxi in Austin repeatedly stopped, crept, and backed up in front of plastic bollards sealing a curb lane, then drove through them with an audible impact; no injuries. Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this reflects a decision-making failure, not a sensing gap, contrasting it with Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 earnings-call claim of 380,000+ unsupervised miles with "zero notable incidents." Electrek notes Waymo has logged 220M+ rider-only miles versus Tesla's 380,000, and cites a prior report that only 14 Tesla vehicles were operating unsupervised as of June, down from a peak of 25.
- A Polish Tesla owner (Michal Gapinski) says he DIY-retrofitted his 2022 Model 3 from HW3 to HW4, calling it "not as bad as it seemed"; the project remains incomplete with no driving video shared. Electrek notes Musk proposed HW3-to-HW4 "microfactories" in April with no update since, and that pending class actions over undelivered self-driving capability could reportedly cost Tesla "tens of billions of dollars."
- Waymo received CPUC approval to expand autonomous ride-hailing into Sacramento and San Diego. Rivian reiterated plans for point-to-point assisted driving later this year with wider rollout in 2027; commentators are skeptical given Rivian's smaller fleet and unproven urban-driving data relative to FSD.

## Electric Vehicles
- Einride will deploy 500 Tesla Semis for Amazon across California, Texas, New Jersey, Illinois, and Georgia — the largest disclosed Tesla Semi order to date, taking Einride's fleet from ~250 to ~750 trucks over 24 months. Estimated hardware cost is $130–145 million against Einride's ~$77 million cash position; the gap is attributed to undisclosed third-party financing. Einride's cited "$800 million" figure is potential long-term ARR pipeline, not confirmed backlog, per Electrek.
- Model Y overtook the Hyundai Grandeur to become Korea's best-selling new car in July, taking 33% of the imported-car market in a historically Hyundai/Kia-loyal market.
- China Model Y revenue share fell ~8% year-over-year in July, but commentators tie this to export reallocation rather than weak demand: China exports hit 295,000 units in the first seven months (already 30% above all of last year), with July exports at 66,000 units, up 143% year-over-year.
- Tesla settled a California Supercharger idle-fee class action covering pre-December-2016 buyers; payouts are $50 or $350 depending on outage length, characterized as immaterial in size.
- Hyundai cut the Ioniq 5 price by $6,300 to $59,900, still roughly $5,000 above Model Y; Tesla has made no price cuts or promotions this quarter.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- New SEC filings reportedly show Elon Musk holding 48.7% of SpaceX (6.42B shares), though Musk noted much of this depends on unvested stock options tied to strong outcomes. Other disclosed holders include Google (~$77B), Nvidia (~$17–20B), AMD ($463M), Fidelity, Ron Baron, Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, and Vanguard.
- Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund disclosed 154 million SpaceX shares (~$25B), its largest Q2 buy; a cited claim that this represents 65% of the fund is disputed, with a correction putting it closer to 2.7% of the full fund or ~69% of its US equities portfolio.
- SpaceX closed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor on August 14, folding the team into SpaceX's AI group working on Grok, Grok Build, Grokbot, and the Grok API.
- A separate report claims Bloomberg said SpaceX is considering merging with Tesla, xAI, or both ahead of a potential IPO, with Nevada legal entities allegedly formed in January and major banks lined up as advisors — described by the host as confirmed, though no direct Bloomberg quote was given and the cited valuations ($800B SpaceX, $1.4T Tesla) are internally inconsistent.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Grok 4.6 is positioned as near-frontier intelligence at a fraction of rival cost per token, per a guest analyst; Musk said Grok 4.6 is optimized for the "Grok Build" harness. A Tesla AI executive said he uses Grokbot for managing multiple AI agents, though it can't yet make calls or send texts.
- A Keystone/Mach33 model projects $3.2 trillion/year in AI compute infrastructure revenue by 2040 on ~500 gigawatts; Musk said actual demand will be higher and reiterated that orbital compute will likely become necessary to scale AI by around 2029 due to land power and permitting constraints.
