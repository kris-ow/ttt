## Robotaxi
- Tesla's official Robotaxi account is running a promotion: complete a ride by 11:59pm August 23 for a chance to attend a "Cybercab launch event," with winners announced by 9:00am August 25. Multiple hosts read this as a strong signal of an imminent public Cybercab rollout in Austin, though estimates on timing diverge — some expect launch within days of the winner announcement, others think full public rollout is unlikely within August given an expected employee public-road testing phase of two-plus weeks.
- The Information reported, citing unnamed sources, that Tesla told staff it's preparing a public CyberCab rollout in Austin as soon as this month, progressing from employee rides on private roads (underway) to employee public-road rides to public launch.
- Drone footage showed the largest CyberCab test fleet yet lined up at Giga Texas with hatches open, plus new Supercharger installations at the test track — read as mileage accumulation ahead of launch.
- Tesla's robotaxi app now supports multi-stop trips, letting riders edit a destination and start a new ride in the same car without booking separately.
- A driverless Model Y Robotaxi in Austin repeatedly stopped in front of curb-lane bollards, then drove through them; no injuries. Electrek argues the failure was decision-making, not sensing, since the car clearly detected the posts before hitting them. This comes weeks after Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy told investors Tesla's 380,000+ unsupervised miles had "zero notable incidents," calling the record "impeccable."

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA data covering roughly mid-June to mid-July shows zero crashes caused by Tesla's Robotaxi fleet; the two recorded incidents both involved human drivers striking stopped or parked Robotaxis.
- Electrek reports Tesla has filed 22 automated driving system incident reports with NHTSA to date, and notes the unsupervised fleet was down to 14 vehicles across all markets as of June, from a peak of 25.
- A Polish Tesla owner (Michal Gapinski) says he DIY-retrofitted his 2022 Model 3 from HW3 to HW4 with "light ECU hacking," calling it "not as bad as it seemed" and saying Tesla "could have done it ages ago" — though the project remains incomplete and no driving video has been shared. Musk's April "microfactory" proposal for city-scale HW3→HW4 swaps has seen no further update.
- Waymo received CPUC approval to expand autonomous ride-hailing into Sacramento and San Diego.
- Rivian reiterated plans for limited point-to-point assisted driving later this year with wider rollout in 2027; a guest on one show argued Rivian's system remains highway-only-caliber and questioned whether it has the data or compute to hit that timeline.

## Electric Vehicles
- Einride will deploy 500 Tesla Semis for Amazon across California, Texas, New Jersey, Illinois, and Georgia — the largest publicly disclosed Semi commitment yet, taking Einride's fleet from ~250 to ~750 trucks over 24 months. Estimated hardware cost ($130–145M) exceeds Einride's ~$77M cash on hand; the gap is attributed to undisclosed third-party financing. Einride's cited "$800 million" figure is pipeline ARR, not confirmed backlog.
- Model Y became South Korea's best-selling new car in July, taking 33% of the imported-car market in a historically domestic-brand-loyal country.
- Tesla settled a California Supercharger idle-fee class action (pre-Dec-2016 buyers): $50 payout for 30-day access loss, $350 for longer — a small, immaterial pool per commentary.
- Hyundai cut the Ioniq 5 price by $6,300 to $59,900, still roughly $5,000 above Model Y; commentary framed this as Hyundai facing pricing pressure, not Tesla.
- China Model Y revenue share fell ~8% year-over-year in July, but China exports hit 295,000 units in seven months (already +30% vs. all of last year), with July exports up 143% YoY — framed as supply reallocation to export markets, not weak demand.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- New SEC filings reportedly show Elon Musk holding 48.7% of SpaceX (6.42B shares), though Musk noted his fully-vested percentage is lower since much depends on options tied to strong outcomes. Google, Nvidia (~$17–20B), and AMD ($463M) are also disclosed holders.
- Saudi Arabia's PIF disclosed a 154-million-share SpaceX stake (~$25B), described as its largest Q2 buy; a cited correction says this represents about 2.7% of PIF's total fund rather than 65%.
- SpaceX completed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor (effective August 14), folding the team into SpaceX's AI group working on Grok, Grok Build, Grokbot, and the Grok API.
- A separate video cited a Bloomberg report that SpaceX is considering merging with Tesla, xAI, or both ahead of a potential IPO, alongside claims of Nevada legal entities and advisory banks already lined up — details presented without direct sourcing and containing an internal valuation inconsistency ($800B SpaceX vs. $1.4T Tesla was called "most valuable").

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Grok 4.6 is positioned as near-frontier intelligence at a fraction of rival cost per token, per a guest analyst; a Tesla AI executive said he uses Grokbot for managing multiple AI agents, though it can't yet make calls or send texts.
- Musk said actual AI compute demand will exceed a modeled $3.2 trillion/year infrastructure projection (500 GW by 2040), and reiterated orbital compute may become the only way to scale AI by around 2029 due to land-based power and permitting constraints.
