## Autonomous Driving

- **European FSD approvals compounding rapidly.** The Netherlands approved supervised FSD after ~150,000 km of testing; Lithuania followed shortly after. Greece, Belgium (Flemish region planning 5,000 km of testing first), and Ireland are all in motion. Per FutureAzA, a broader EU member-state vote involving ~28 countries is expected June or September 2026 — and early approvers effectively create a "copy-paste" template for the rest.

- **FSD now live in ~10 regions globally.** Paid subscriber count grew 170,000 last quarter (per FutureAzA/Brian), with 1.28 million active subscribers total. Cumulative supervised miles stand at approximately 9–10 billion.

- **European fleet data accumulating fast.** Sawyer Merritt reported that Tesla owners in the Netherlands and Lithuania collectively passed 15 million kilometers, with the subsequent 48-hour run rate implying roughly 2.5 million km/day from that cohort alone — a strong signal for fleet training data momentum in early European markets.

- **FSD V14 behavioral improvements demonstrated.** Sawyer Merritt observed FSD V14.3.3 pulling well right to yield for an emergency vehicle, and a separate V14 trip where the system detected a car's reverse lights at an intersection and proactively backed up to create room — qualitative indicators of improving real-time reactive logic.

## Robotaxi

- **Bhakdi's $490 thesis explicitly excludes robotaxi scaling** — he views $490 as Tesla's prior all-time high that the stock "belongs at" based on fundamentals alone. However, he flags that if the stock reaches $490 and robotaxi still isn't scaling, the position becomes vulnerable. Conversely, he states: "If we have 15,000 robotaxis end of the year, we will not be at $490 — we will be at a completely different number."

- **Cybercab production as the forcing function.** Bhakdi argues 10,000–15,000 Cybercabs will be produced by end of 2026, with a higher scenario of 40,000–50,000 cited but not used as his base case. His logic: Tesla would not build that hardware volume to leave it idle. Cybercab parking infrastructure has been spotted under construction in Dallas and other cities.

- **Austin unsupervised mix may be rising.** A viewer noted that Austin robotaxi counts appear to be declining; Bhakdi offered the interpretation that Tesla may be shifting the mix toward a higher proportion of unsupervised rides rather than maximizing total supervised volume — a quality-over-quantity transition, though he provided no fleet figures.

- **brighterwithherbert context:** The "physical AI platform" framing from SpaceX's S1 is applied directly to Tesla's robotaxi network — described as a ride-hailing cut-of-every-ride recurring revenue model analogous to AWS, with Austin driverless operations confirmed but flagged as limited to one city currently.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus reveal ~4–6 weeks out.** Bhakdi places a Tesla Optimus reveal in late June to early July 2026 and describes it as potentially "very big." ## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX S1's "physical AI platform" playbook is the dominant narrative today.** The $1.25B/month Anthropic compute deal (through May 2029, ~$15B annualized run rate) anchors two separate brighterwithherbert pieces arguing that Elon Musk's cross-company strategy — build expensive physical infrastructure, rent it as recurring service revenue — is the template for Tesla's own autonomous driving, energy, and robotics businesses. The SpaceX filing is presented as the strongest real-world evidence yet that this playbook can work.

- **Iran/Strait of Hormuz resolution as the key macro unlock for Tesla stock.** Bhakdi frames the Iran situation as the primary overhang that pulled Tesla back from $450 to $395. Weekend reports (Trump/CBS News; Iran's semi-official FARC agency) indicate negotiators are finalizing an agreement to reopen the Strait. Bhakdi's base case: a face-saving, low-enforcement nuclear deal plus Strait reopening removes the largest near-term macro headwind.

- **Nvidia earnings cited as hard validation of AGI investment cycle.** Bhakdi notes PE ratios are declining even as profits expand across the market — the opposite of bubble conditions — and views Nvidia's results as confirming the AGI capex cycle is structural, not speculative.

- **SpaceX IPO (~$75B, ~70% institutional) not a liquidity drain for Tesla.** Bhakdi dismisses rotation fears; argues SpaceX success correlates positively with Tesla via "merger fantasies," though he has reduced his 2026 merger probability and expects any combination more likely in 2027 or later.
