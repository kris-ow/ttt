This video (published 2026-10-26) covers Tesla's stock jump following a stretch of positive news: rapid Robotaxi fleet growth in Texas, a Houston service-area expansion, favorable NHTSA rulemaking proposals, Wall Street upgrades from Barclays and Canaccord Genuity, and renewed debate over whether Tesla's Q3 earnings call was "boring" or "absurdly grandiose." Host Herbert and guest Jeff Watts (a supply-chain executive and manufacturing consultant) frame the week as evidence Tesla's robotaxi operation is shifting from a pilot into a scaling network, while flagging the company's continued lack of forward guidance as a point of caution.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas fleet surges past 900, nearing Waymo's local scale:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla's active Texas Robotaxi fleet reached 970 as of October 26, up 167 vehicles (to 970 from roughly 803 a week earlier) in the past week; Cybercabs account for a large share of that growth (reported as 463 Cybercabs as of the weekend, rising further through the week). The host and guest note Tesla is closing in on Waymo's Austin fleet size after about 17 months of driverless operations there, versus Waymo's multi-year head start.
2. **Record Cybercab turnout at Austin F1 race:** Over the F1 race weekend in Austin (attendance cited as around 120,000), Tesla logged a reported 1,250 Cybercab sightings in a single day, with the large majority described as driverless rides; Tesla's AI lead, Ashok Elluswamy, was quoted saying Robotaxis were visible "as far as the eye can see."
3. **Houston service area expands dramatically:** Tesla significantly enlarged its Houston geofence from a small initial zone to a much larger service area, announced via the official account's "Houston, we have liftoff" post; Dallas Robotaxi rides are planned to begin in November per the Q3 earnings call.
4. **Utilization climbing toward Waymo's benchmark:** A third-party analysis (Genma JP), derived by combining Texas DMV registrations with Florida vehicle counts and dividing by fleet size, estimated unsupervised miles per vehicle per day have risen from the low 40s in July to roughly 100 currently — still below Waymo's typical ~200 miles/day/vehicle, suggesting further headroom as the network matures.
5. **Newly built Cybercabs drive themselves off the factory line:** The host noted that Cybercabs reportedly leave Giga Texas with no steering wheel or pedals and drive autonomously directly into service/staging areas in Austin, which Jeff Watts characterized as Tesla automating a costly distribution step, similar to how Model Y and Cybertruck units already exit the factory.
6. **Growth pace and limiting factors:** Jeff Watts estimated Tesla would need to add vehicles faster than recent daily increments (cited examples of 32–69 added at a time) to hit a target of a couple thousand Cybercabs by year-end, but suggested Tesla is deliberately balancing fleet growth against regulatory risk and operational readiness rather than maximizing unit additions. Analysts reportedly estimate fleet week-over-week growth exceeding 15%, accelerating to 20% in some recent weeks.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **NHTSA proposes rules easing autonomous vehicle requirements:** NHTSA proposed eliminating mandatory manually-operated turn signal controls, allowing autonomous systems to control turn signals/hazard lights/high-beams without traditional switches, removing certain dashboard indicator requirements, permitting optional exterior lights signaling autonomous operation, and easing headlight design restrictions — aimed at removing regulatory barriers for vehicles like Tesla's Cybercab. Tesla's response to NHTSA's outstanding questions on the Cybercab was due by month-end, with the outcome not yet known.
2. **Tesla assisted driving (formerly FSD Supervised) approved in a 10th EU country:** Latvia became the 10th European country to approve Tesla's assisted driving system; a broader EU vote (described as around December 3rd or 6th) will determine wider approval, with Elon Musk reportedly expecting a decision by Q1.

## Financials
1. **Barclays and Canaccord Genuity upgrade Tesla:** Barclays raised its rating to overweight with a $450 price target (up from $370), citing accelerating robotaxi miles and FSD attach rates above 75%, and flagged a potential SpaceX merger as a bullish catalyst. Canaccord Genuity's George Gianarikas raised his target to $442, calling the Q3 call "one of the most unapologetically bullish Tesla calls in many a moon" and pushing back on characterizations of it as subdued.
2. **Earnings call "grandiose statements" debated:** Tesla executives made a series of bold claims on the Q3 call — Tesla will "take over transportation of any kind on Earth," Optimus will be "the biggest product on Earth," Terafab will be "the most valuable building in the world," and Megapacks are sold out through 2027 — which Musk himself described as "absurdly grandiose." Jeff Watts argued the market is now less interested in grandiose rhetoric and more interested in concrete guidance, noting SpaceX's call included specific revenue guidance while Tesla's did not, which he views as a shortcoming given Tesla's history of specific robotaxi guidance that was not met by mid-year.
3. **Tesla reportedly supply-constrained with low inventory:** Jeff Watts stated Tesla has described itself as supply-constrained for a couple of quarters, with vehicle inventory levels lower than he's seen in years of tracking; he expects Tesla, as a strong manufacturing operation, to resolve supply disconnects within roughly six months and predicted meaningful production increases and improved inventory in the current quarter.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla outpacing Waymo's historical growth curve:** Commentary noted that comparing Tesla's robotaxi ramp to Waymo on a simple calendar basis is misleading since Waymo began driverless operations years earlier (around December of a prior year per the host) versus Tesla's service start around mid-2025; adjusted for this, Tesla's rate of fleet and utilization growth is characterized as faster than Waymo's over a comparable early period.
