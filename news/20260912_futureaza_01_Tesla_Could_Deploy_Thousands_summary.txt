This is a conversational interview between the host (Brian) and a guest referred to as Herbert, who recently attended a Tesla event and spoke with engineers and executives about the Cybercab rollout. The core thesis: Tesla is deliberately ramping Cybercab deployment slowly for brand-protection reasons, but Herbert projects the fleet could scale into the thousands by year-end depending on regulatory clearance and FSD progress.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas Cybercab registrations rising fast:** Herbert says Texas DMV Cybercab registrations went from 45 to 49 in a matter of days, which he links to the Austin robotaxi service going live with a choice between Cybercab and Model Y, and demand reportedly high enough that riders are waiting roughly an hour to try a Cybercab.
2. **Herbert's VIN-based fleet estimate:** Herbert estimates, based on checking VINs, that Tesla has built somewhere over 2,000 and under 3,000 Cybercabs total nationwide — but cautions this total does not equal the number that will enter robotaxi service, since he guesses (his own rough assumption, not a stated fact) that around 1,000 will remain permanently dedicated to FSD validation and expansion into new markets/states rather than commercial service.
3. **NHTSA probe and the ~1,000-vehicle authority:** Herbert notes a Wall Street Journal article citing NHTSA language referencing "a thousand" Cybercabs, which he interprets as the number Tesla currently has authority to operate while the standard-issue audit query (probe) that follows a new vehicle launch proceeds; he characterizes this probe as a routine post-launch audit, not a sign of a defect.
4. **Rollout pacing and brand-risk rationale:** Herbert argues Tesla is intentionally rolling out slowly (his phrasing: "the first month I would go slow and then I would start adding hundreds") because most of the public doesn't distinguish the Cybercab vehicle from the robotaxi service brand, so any early incident would be disproportionately damaging; he does not believe the pacing is due to reliance on remote/teleoperated support, and cites one anecdote (a Cybercab carrying an unnamed rider that stalled on a street and needed support intervention) as an example of occasional but not limiting support needs.
5. **Comparison to Waymo's support burden:** Herbert cites Waymo's roughly 4,000-vehicle fleet as needing "tremendous" operational support, implying Tesla's caution partly reflects lessons about scaling support infrastructure; the host separately voiced skepticism about Waymo's approach and pricing (comparing Waymo cost to "Uber Black"), calling this a barrier to affordability-driven safety gains.
6. **Growth-rate-based projection for year-end fleet size:** Herbert projects, based on a stated 17% week-over-week growth rate in paid unsupervised driving miles (tied to Tesla surpassing 1 million such miles), that the robotaxi fleet could reach roughly 2,000–3,000 vehicles by year-end even before a newer FSD version is completed, and suggests this aligns conveniently with an operating limit of 2,500 vehicles he references. If that FSD version is completed in the coming months, he speculates the growth rate could accelerate further, potentially pushing the fleet toward 3,000–4,000 vehicles — explicitly caveated as speculative ("who knows?").
7. **Cybercab ride experience described as materially improved:** Herbert, based on riding in the vehicles at the event, describes the current software as noticeably smoother and more confident than prior versions, with accurate pickup/drop-off pin placement; he says Tesla engineers and executives told him to "wait till [FSD] 15," implying the current build is not yet that version and further improvement is expected.
8. **Regulatory comment-period timing cited as reason for delayed launch:** Herbert says the Cybercab launch was delayed (despite production starting earlier in the year) because Tesla needed to wait for a regulatory comment period to close before it could self-certify the vehicle, which he offers as the explanation for shareholder frustration over the gap between production start and public launch.
9. **State-level testing footprint:** Cybercabs have reportedly been spotted in 36 states, which Herbert attributes mainly to FSD validation testing (racking up miles, testing conditions) rather than imminent service launches in those states.
