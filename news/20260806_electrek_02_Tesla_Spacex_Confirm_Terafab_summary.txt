Tesla and SpaceX confirmed the location and initial financial scope of Terafab, a jointly planned semiconductor megafactory, while the article highlights inconsistencies between the companies' public statements and prior disclosures about how firm the project actually is.

## Financials
1. **Initial phase priced at $16.8 billion, down from earlier $25 billion figure:** SpaceX and Tesla put the first phase of Terafab at roughly $16.8 billion, with "future expansion phases bringing total investment much higher," per the companies' statements. This is lower than the $25 billion headline figure Musk gave when the project was first announced in March, and the article notes the remainder of the total cost is left vague.
2. **SpaceX's own IPO filing described the project as non-binding:** The article reports that SpaceX's S-1 filing from May described Terafab as only a "general framework" with no binding commitments, no finalized IP split between the two companies, and no obligation for either side to continue participating — which the article says undercuts the confident tone of the site announcement.

## Market & Competition
1. **Site and scale details:** Terafab will be built in Grimes County, Texas (about an hour northwest of Houston) on the Gibbons Creek Reservoir, previously used to cool a now-shut coal-fired power plant. SpaceX says the site will draw water from the reservoir rather than local groundwater, addressing community concerns about water use. The finished facility is planned to span more than 100 million square feet — a figure the article notes would be more than ten times the floor space of TSMC's largest gigafabs — and employ at least 3,000 people, mostly local hires. SpaceX describes it as combining logic, memory, packaging, and testing in one facility, intended to be the largest chip manufacturing site in the world.
2. **Purpose and existing chip supply:** Tesla stated that "Both Tesla & SpaceX will need far more chips than current & future global production can supply," citing a goal of producing over 1 terawatt of compute per year; Terafab chips are intended for Optimus, Cybercab, and SpaceX's planned space-based data centers. The article notes Tesla's near-term chip supply is already contracted separately: Samsung's Taylor, Texas fab is producing Tesla's AI5 chip under a previously reported $16.5 billion deal for the next-generation AI6 chip, with TSMC covering additional supply. The article also references an earlier Electrek report that Tesla itself may not build the fab, with Intel reportedly lined up to handle manufacturing.
3. **Scale claims questioned:** The article states that TSMC is spending roughly $165 billion to build six fabs in Arizona, which are described as a fraction of Terafab's stated footprint, and argues that no existing fab is designed to output a "terawatt of compute" per year, since that figure derives from Musk's space/AI ambitions (tied to SpaceX's February absorption of xAI) rather than standard chip-industry metrics.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues Terafab is more tangible now (site, permits, community outreach) than when announced in March, but says a real contradiction exists between the companies' confident public claims and SpaceX's own IPO filing calling it a non-binding "general framework." He questions how much of the $16.8 billion-plus cost will land on Tesla's balance sheet given Tesla's car business has stopped growing and it already committed $16.5 billion to Samsung for existing chip needs, framing Terafab as evidence Tesla is becoming a compute/robotics company rather than primarily an automaker.
