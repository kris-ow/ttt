FutureAzA hosts a live end-of-Q2 stream covering his proprietary Q2 2026 production and delivery estimates for Tesla, alongside a rapid-fire news roundup spanning Cybercab engineering progress, battery theft at Giga Nevada, lithium price trends, competing EV startups, and miscellaneous industry headlines. The host's core thesis is that Tesla's 2025 weakness was primarily supply/production-constrained rather than demand-driven, and that year-over-year growth in Q2 2026 should be achievable given the low comparison base set by last year's retooling period.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Q2 2026 Delivery and Production Estimates:** The host projects Q2 2026 production of 420,420 vehicles and deliveries of 431,210 — the higher delivery figure reflects an expectation that inventory built up in Q1 will be drawn down. He notes that in Q1 2026, Tesla produced approximately 408,386 vehicles but delivered only 358,023, leaving a gap of roughly 50,000 units either in transit or awaiting purchase. He expects production to come in below deliveries this quarter as a result.

2. **Wall Street Consensus and the Number to Beat:** Tesla has published a delivery consensus figure of 406,024 for Q2 2026 — a practice the host notes is relatively new for the company. Analyst estimates range as high as 420,000. The host frames year-over-year growth as the most important metric, since Q2 2025 was depressed by factory retooling across multiple sites.

3. **US EV Market Weakness:** The host expresses disappointment in US EV demand, noting that the top-selling EV in the country — Tesla Model Y — moved an estimated 78,000 units in the period tracked, with Model 3 at approximately 31,000. He characterizes the US as having "been kicked down to the minor leagues" in EV adoption and says he is not expecting extremely high domestic numbers this quarter.

4. **Europe Turning Positive; Giga Berlin Expansion:** Europe has reportedly shifted from a weak spot to a source of growth for Tesla. The host cites strong sales in Nordic countries and Spain specifically. Tesla is reportedly adding more labor to Giga Berlin now, with a further production jump expected later in 2026.

5. **Cybertruck "Smuggler's Bay" on Base Model:** A community post from the Cybertruck Owners Club highlights that the base $60,000 Cybertruck does not include the smuggler's bay storage feature. The host notes this as a fun consumer-interest item.

6. **Cybercab Delivery Timeline Uncertainty:** The host references a bet by Marques Brownlee that the Cybercab will not be shipped to a paying customer for under $30,000 this year, suggesting it may have merit. He notes that Cybercab units will count toward production figures but not as deliveries in the traditional sense, since internal/fleet deployments are not customer deliveries. Engineering tests of the first production Cybercab have begun in Austin; EPA certification and first-responder documentation are reportedly in place.

7. **Tesla Semi Drifting Tests:** The host describes footage of Tesla Semi stability and drift-control testing, noting the truck maintained control even with a trailer pushed deliberately out of line. He observes the test vehicle lacks the top fairing (as expected for low-center-of-gravity testing) but is loaded with concrete blocks and heavy steel to simulate real weight.

8. **Competing EV Startups — Slate and Telo Truck:** The host notes that Slate may be on track for production by end of 2026, with a possible slip into Q1 2027 (which he would still consider "on time" for a new company). Telo Truck (referred to as "Tot truck" in captions, corrected contextually) is also reportedly on track to deliver by end of year. He raises the fundamental scaling question for both: can they survive long enough to achieve economies of scale?

9. **New Tesla Markets — Uruguay:** Model 3 and Model Y are reportedly getting closer to availability in Uruguay. The host frames this positively, noting Uruguay's compact geography makes service center coverage more feasible.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Engineering Tests Underway in Austin:** Engineering tests of the first production Cybercab unit have begun in Austin. The host confirms EPA certification and first-responder paperwork are in place, characterizing the program as on track after years of skepticism.

2. **Cybercab Production Reporting:** The host is uncertain whether Tesla will ever break out Cybercab production as a separate line item in its reports. He expects Cybercab units to appear in the "all other models" production category but not in delivery counts until vehicles are actually deployed to customers or a recognized fleet entity.

## Energy

1. **Q2 Energy Deployment Consensus Seems Low:** The host flags that Wall Street's consensus for Q2 2026 energy deployment is 13.8 GWh, which he considers low — particularly given that Q1 2026 came in at only 8.8 GWh, and he notes that the Leipzig Megapack factory alone can produce approximately 10 GWh. He attributes some of the constraint to logistics: large Megapack units must be shipped and installed before they count as deployed.

2. **Battery Theft Epidemic at Giga Nevada:** According to sheriff records obtained by *Wired*, nine major suspected cargo thefts occurred at Giga Nevada in January 2026 alone, with at least 11 incidents since December 2025. Trailers containing millions of dollars' worth of Tesla car and home batteries were allegedly stolen directly from loading docks. One alleged operation also targeted battery recycler Redwood Materials. The host cites an estimate that US shipping theft doubled from 2022 to 2024, costing approximately $18 million per day nationwide, and notes these figures may be undercounts due to corporate reluctance to disclose theft.

3. **Iron-Air Battery Milestone in Europe:** Europe's largest 1 GWh iron-air battery installation was announced, capable of storing clean power for up to 100 hours. The host frames this as part of an "all of the above" approach to energy storage, particularly relevant as lithium prices fluctuate.

4. **Lithium Price Drop — Context Required:** China's lithium carbonate futures fell nearly 10% over 10 sessions after reports that a major producer (the host refers to "CL") may reinstate production at a mine, with prices dropping to approximately 151,750 yuan per ton — described as a three-month low. The host cautions viewers that on a five-year chart, this drop is barely visible after the massive run-up and subsequent decline in lithium prices, urging perspective.

## Market & Competition

1. **BYD in the US — Tariff Math:** Viewers raise the idea of BYD selling premium models in the US. The host explains that doubling the price to cover tariffs still doesn't account for shipping, service center buildout, floor-plan financing costs (estimated at 1–2% per month), and the overhead of establishing a US dealer/service network across a geographically large country. He concludes BYD would not be motivated to do this for modest US sales volumes.

2. **Sodium Battery Hype Check:** A report claims 10,000 EVs could use sodium-ion batteries by end of 2026. The host urges caution, noting that two years ago sodium batteries were limited to short-range (~50–80 mile) applications in China and would not have met Tesla's requirements. He acknowledges the chemistry has improved but notes that larger sodium ions constrain volumetric energy density, and that the technology is still in early scaling stages.

3. **Sweden's Record HVDC Transformer Shipped to China:** Sweden shipped what is described as the most powerful transformer of its kind ever built — 446 tons, transported on a 350-foot trailer with over 300 wheels — destined for China to power 10 million homes via a high-voltage DC transmission line. The host frames this as evidence that Western manufacturing retains technology advantages China cannot yet replicate domestically.

4. **AI Compute Scarcity and SpaceX Server Rental:** Google reportedly capped Gemini usage due to AI compute demand straining capacity. The host connects this to SpaceX's server rental business, suggesting rising compute scarcity could benefit SpaceX's infrastructure revenue. SpaceX's private market price is noted at $169.80 (per share, implied context).
