## Executive Summary
The dominant story of the day is the formal, simultaneous confirmation from both Tesla and SpaceX that their respective boards have each formed special committees to evaluate a potential business combination — a significant, concrete step for the long-rumored merger thesis. Alongside that, Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call produced several bullish data points (FSD subscriber growth, Megapack sell-out, 5M vehicle/year ambition), while SpaceX advanced its Starlink Mobile buildout with new FCC spectrum approval.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla and SpaceX both confirm special committees to evaluate a combination:** In coordinated disclosures, Tesla's board "formed a special committee to explore deepening Tesla's relationship with SpaceX, which could be through certain arrangements including without limitation a business combination," per Tesla's official statement. SpaceX separately confirmed its own board "has formed a special committee to evaluate a potential combination between SpaceX and Tesla," while cautioning that "no determination by our special committee has been made regarding any potential combination, and any potential [transaction remains unconfirmed]." This is the most substantive, officially sourced merger-related development to date and drew heavy engagement (3.1K and 1.5K likes respectively) — a notable escalation from speculation to formal corporate process on both sides.

2. **Tesla Q3 2026 earnings call highlights:** Per a recap post, Tesla said its 2027 Megapack production capacity is "nearly sold out," the company is targeting production of 5 million vehicles annually by 2030, and showroom traffic is increasingly driven by customers coming specifically to experience/learn about Tesla technology (post was cut off before further detail). A separate post flagged that Elon Musk spoke on the call, without further detail captured in this digest.

3. **FSD subscriber growth hits record pace:** Tesla is now adding an average of 2,800 FSD subscribers per day, described as a record high, and is generating $92 million per month ($1.1 billion annualized run-rate) from FSD subscriptions, per the post's figures.

4. **Starlink Mobile spectrum approval and "disruption" framing:** SpaceX received FCC approval to acquire three additional spectrum licenses from Tristar (15 MHz in the 1695–1710 MHz band) to expand its upcoming next-gen Starlink Mobile network; the FCC also granted related waivers (detail cut off in source post). Separate posts frame Starlink Mobile as poised to be "the first network operator to deploy both satellite and terrestrial spectrum" and a disruptive potential "fourth major carrier in the U.S." — these are the poster's own characterizations/opinions rather than sourced facts from SpaceX or the FCC.

5. **Tesla manufacturing expansion — "Project Crystal Sun":** Fort Bend County, Texas commissioners were set to consider tax incentive and economic development agreements that could help bring a $10.1 billion Tesla manufacturing facility ("Project Crystal Sun") to the county; the post notes the project "could eventually" reach that scale, indicating this is a proposed/incentive-stage project, not a confirmed built facility.

6. **Minor product/market items:** Tesla will not sell the Cybertruck in Australia or New Zealand; affected Cybertruck reservation holders in those markets are being offered one free year of FSD (Supervised) if they order a new Model 3 or Model Y by the end of 2026. Tesla also added new shop merchandise items (backpacks, umbrella) — low investment signal.
