A host and guest Larry Goldberg discuss SpaceX's sharp stock decline amid a broader AI-stock selloff, arguing the drop was market-wide contagion rather than a change in SpaceX's underlying business, which they say kept delivering positive news during the same period — including a major Starlink Mobile chip partnership, surging subscriber growth, a bullish analyst revenue revision, and continued progress on Starship-related infrastructure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink Mobile partners with Qualcomm for nationwide 5G:** SpaceX selected Qualcomm to supply chips (Dragonwing Q3 QRU100/QDU100 platforms, including radios, baseband units, and tier-one software) for a nationwide 5G Starlink Mobile network integrating satellite and terrestrial coverage; deployment across tens of thousands of US cell sites is set to begin in 2027. The host and guest frame this as SpaceX effectively becoming a mobile carrier; Elon Musk reportedly reacted that at least one network will have good signal everywhere.
2. **Subscriber growth and satellite dominance:** Starlink subscribers have roughly doubled over the past year to over 13.5 million. As of October 31, roughly 11,000 of about 16,800 active satellites in orbit are Starlink satellites — about 66% of all active satellites in orbit, per the host.
3. **FCC advances satellite-to-phone rules:** The FCC voted 3-0 to advance rules enabling direct satellite-to-phone communication, despite lobbying against it from AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile; this is a proposal stage, with public comment and a final vote still to come. The guest frames it as pro-competition rather than Musk-specific, benefiting any satellite operator (e.g., also Jeff Bezos's efforts).
4. **Dead-zone improvements and airline adoption:** Per Ookla (a third-party connectivity measurement firm), the no-signal test rate in US road tests dropped from 2% to 0.5%, which the host attributes partly to Starlink/T-Mobile integration. Starlink adoption is also growing among airlines: Japan reportedly saw passenger growth of 12.3% year-over-year tied to Starlink; United Airlines operates it on 50% of daily flights with a goal of 100% of its fleet by the end of next year; Emirates has equipped 65 aircraft; Norway's government and Canada are also cited as adopting or moving toward Starlink access.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Falcon 9 being wound down to focus on Starship:** SpaceX has reportedly stopped selling new commercial Falcon 9 launches after 2028, is completing Crew Dragon commitments for NASA by 2030, and is not bidding on three new Space Force contracts, according to the discussion. Elon Musk reportedly said verbally that Falcon will wind down once Starship is flying multiple times per week, with the guest comparing this to SpaceX's earlier cancellation of the Falcon 1 program shortly after its first successful launch.
2. **Gigabay construction progress:** Construction continues at the large Gigabay facility near Kennedy Space Center, with only one large compartment door remaining to be installed, per footage referenced in the show.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Orbital data centers seen as dominant value driver:** The guest reiterates a view from a prior show that roughly 80–90% of SpaceX's valuation depends on the future potential of orbital data centers, which he argues are largely insulated from near-term U.S. political risk (e.g., state-level opposition to terrestrial data centers) because Starship development is a federal matter.
2. **Political risk to terrestrial data center buildout:** The guest flags real — though not certain — risk that newly elected state governments (citing Texas specifically) could slow terrestrial AI data center buildout, while cautioning this is different from any threat to Starship or orbital data center plans. He notes Texas officials have said they won't affect existing projects, but uncertainty remains for new ones like the Starbase project on newly acquired Brownsville land.
3. **$40B Nvidia chip financing package:** Banks (Goldman Sachs and Apollo, per the host) have reportedly been selling portions of a previously secured $40 billion financing package tied to Nvidia AI chips for SpaceX's data centers to institutional investors. The guest characterizes this as routine loan syndication for an already-secured deal, not a sign of financial distress, and says any political friction around it should pass.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Stock drop attributed to broad AI-stock selloff:** SpaceX shares fell sharply (the guest mentions a drop to around $128, filmed Thursday) alongside a wider selloff in AI-related stocks; both host and guest frame this as a buying opportunity rather than a fundamental business problem, with the guest expressing regret at not acting on options that day.
2. **Analyst revenue forecast raised sharply:** Morgan Stanley analyst Adam Jonas nearly doubled his 2027 SpaceX revenue forecast to $175 billion; the guest notes other forecasters (including a figure referred to as Brian Wang) have also had to revise estimates upward, and some believe SpaceX could reach $300 billion in revenue as early as 2027 rather than 2028.
3. **Ark Invest buying the dip:** Cathie Wood's Ark reportedly bought about 350,000 SpaceX shares on one of the days of the price drop.
4. **Nationalization calls dismissed:** The guest characterizes calls from the far left for nationalizing SpaceX as politically unrealistic and self-defeating, given the company's scale.
