## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Per the Financial Times via Electrek, all-stock merger talks are "moving towards a potential year-end deadline," though people familiar say an agreement isn't close. The combined group would be worth about $3.5 trillion. Tesla approval requires more than 50% of all outstanding votes. Electrek notes Musk holds about 20% of Tesla's voting power and about 84% of SpaceX's. Electrek's own view is that the deal is a Musk self-deal and the Tesla committee is not independent of him.
- Tesla's special committee is Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung (adviser: Evercore). SpaceX is represented by Roelof Botha and Randy Glein (advisers: Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs). Per the FT, Texas governance rules keep Musk out of active negotiations. Tesla's annual meeting is December 3, where Denholm is up for re-election.
- Timing is contested. Per the host of one video, Bloomberg reported a push to announce in the first half of 2027, which neither company confirmed. An earlier Bloomberg report had pointed to a possible December 3 announcement.
- At DealBook, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would probably be better together, that a Tesla vote may or may not succeed, and that he should not be compensated for doing a merger.
- About 800M SPCX shares (roughly 6% of the company, about $100B at Friday's close) unlock December 8, the last of nine unlocks. Close to 4B shares are already sellable. Per the host, the figure exceeds some trackers' roughly 330M because a prospectus early-release trigger around $175 never hit. Musk's shares stay locked until about June 12 next year.
- The December unlock is the smallest by float impact, adding roughly 18% to the tradable float. Even if all 800M sold, that would be under seven days at Friday's volume of about 120M shares. December 8 is also the first day of the Fed meeting.
- Founders Fund's stake fell from about 427M to about 314M shares between end of June and end of September. The filing does not say whether the shares were sold or distributed. Nvidia added about 7.7M shares in Q3, and ARK bought 378,515 shares on November 16.
- SPCX closed Friday around $128, down almost 8% on a day the Nasdaq fell over 5%. It closed at $133.35 on the day covered by Electrified, up 4.35%. Jo Bhakdi put SPCX down about 5% in his session and attributed it to the merger and neocloud correlation. The sources' price moves are not directly comparable and cover different sessions.
- At about $128, SPCX trades at roughly 17x the December run rate, per the host. CFO Bret Johnsen reiterated the goal of $100B annualized revenue by year-end and guided Q1 2027 revenue to $33B–$37B. Q3 revenue was $14.6B (up 161% year on year) and backlog rose from about $47.5B to $63B in a quarter.
- Bear case per the host: SpaceX lost about $3.4B and burned close to $40B of free cash flow over 12 months. Q3's $4.4B profit included about $1.6B of interest, investment gains and a tax benefit. SpaceX sold $32B of AI chip-backed bonds, including a $25.2B tranche at 8.259%.
- Per Anthropic's IPO filing as the host reads it, its monthly SpaceX bill is expected to reach about $37B a year once capacity is delivered in December. After the first commitment periods end in January and March, either side can cancel on 90 days' notice.
- Citi raised its SPCX target to $280 from $240. The average analyst target is around $260.
- Bhakdi estimates a roughly 30% premium, around $500 a share, for Tesla holders. He holds SPCX calls at a $170 strike. These are his views.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V15 rolled out November 21, per Bhakdi, and appears limited to Model Y vehicles with the front bumper camera. Tesla Scope expects no further wave before Thanksgiving and targets over 70% of eligible vehicles before Christmas.
- Early testers report stronger pothole avoidance, a more cautious pedestrian response and few regressions versus the V14 launch. Complaints include stop-and-go creeping, phantom braking and weak parking. FSD cannot pick a specific spot while engaged, and Banish is not yet available.
- Chuck Cook's chain test showed V15 performing the same as V14 despite release notes promising improvement.
- The reality view is generated by the same neural network that drives the car, per the host, using about 8% of AI4's neural accelerator capacity. Musk said interleaving universal vision encoder features along the diffusion transformer layers should cut roughly 35% of the latency. The view currently lags reality by about half a second.
- JP Morgan highlighted V15's redesigned AI architecture and a 25% safety improvement per Tesla. It said compute efficiency could reduce near-term AI5 needs.
- Italy became the 12th country to approve FSD and the first major EU country. EU population coverage is 28.7% against the 65% needed. One host relays a rumor that the early-December EU TCMV vote has been delayed with no new date.

## Robotaxi
- Bhakdi's own tracker, which consolidates several sources, showed 582–583 Cybercabs, with 776 registered. He projects about 2,000 registered by year-end and says Tesla could then scale quickly. These are his projections.
- Robin took 18 Cybercab rides in Austin totaling 484 minutes for $265, about $33 per hour. The host calculates about $72,000 gross per vehicle per year at 6 hours a day. He says Tesla sometimes charges $3–$4 per mile at peak demand and questions sustainability once the novelty fades.
- Sawyer Merritt relays that Robotaxi riders will "soon" be able to schedule rides. Cybercabs in Dallas already carry Starlink hardware, though riders cannot connect to it yet.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk said AI5 is going to Optimus, an order-of-magnitude hardware improvement, while cars stay on AI4. He called Optimus Tesla's finest product ever.
- Jason Oppenheim cited a Fremont plant for a million robots a year and a planned Texas plant for 10 million a year. Guest Jeff Lutz said Oppenheim is directionally correct that software is the differentiator.

## Financials
- Tesla closed at $385.46, up 11.46%, on a day the NDX rose 6.86%. The host attributes the rally to Nvidia reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO.
- HSBC upgraded Tesla from reduce to hold and raised its target to $359 from $157. The target blends a SpaceX-buys-Tesla-at-a-33%-premium case with a standalone value of about $212. UBS initiated at hold with a $425 target. JP Morgan stayed neutral and called FSD v15 an illustration of Tesla's head start in consumer autonomy.
- Musk said AI4 now costs twice as much due to memory price hikes, versus about $650 in March. The host estimates it could now exceed $1,200 per chip, a margin headwind.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla raised U.S. financing APRs, per Sawyer Merritt. Base Model Y RWD and Model Y Premium RWD and AWD moved to 2.99% from 2.49%. Model Y Performance is unchanged at 3.99%. Merritt reads it as a pullback on incentives.
- Lutz found one new Model Y in inventory within 200 miles of Chicago and an order quote of February to April. He says Tesla exited last quarter with about 11 or 12 days of inventory.
- Tesla released a new photo of its next-gen Roadster with no timing or specs.
- Per Musk's AI4.5 roadmap, a RAM upgrade to LPDDR5X is expected around mid-2027, with RAM cut from 32 GB to 24 GB.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage for the first time on November 14. Musk wrote that "Catching the ship was the last major piece of the puzzle."
- Morgan Stanley calculates one Starship launch of Starlink V3 satellites pays back its cost in about 13 weeks. Adam Jonas said the catch materially derisked long-term TAMs across all three segments.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk wrote that "The cash shortage is also going to be nuts next year." He said delays of a few months between paying for compute and earning revenue would mean hundreds of billions in incremental working capital. Lutz reads it as a warning on cash conversion and possibly a jab at strained neoclouds such as CoreWeave and Nebius.
- At DealBook, Satya Nadella said "If he has SpaceX capacity that I can lease, I would lease it."
- Per reporting relayed by the Electrified host, Musk told SpaceX employees the company is falling behind in AI and now requires engineers to justify GPU use. The host dismisses it as nothing new.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said native Starlink in all Tesla models should hopefully arrive toward the end of 2027, excluding China. The host notes it could slip into early 2028.
- SpaceX launched a Starlink page for merchant vessels, plus a Global Business Merchant plan from $4,650 per month in 2027.

## Market & Competition
