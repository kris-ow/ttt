## Brief

- Starship Flight 13 scrubbed, engine issue; retarget July 20

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14 Light begins rolling out to Hardware 3 vehicles in Korea, with wider rollout to follow — framed as backporting a modern stack onto ~7-year-old hardware.
- FSD Supervised has surpassed roughly 31 million miles across the Netherlands, Estonia, Belgium, Lithuania, and Denmark, just three months after first EU regulatory approval.
- Germany's approval timeline slipped: a state secretary said Tesla's FSD type-approval decision awaits a Europe-wide TCMV vote, earliest October; KBA is expected to receive a test vehicle this month.
- NTSB found a home-crash incident resulted from the driver manually pressing the accelerator to 100% (speed over 70 mph), overriding FSD — not attributable to FSD behavior.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's officially registered Texas Robotaxi fleet reached 175 vehicles (all Model Y), with 73 added in a single week, the largest weekly addition to date; actual active-operation count remains unknown, per Electrified.
- Tesla employees are now riding in Cybercab, described by a Tesla executive as near-production final testing ahead of customer rides, per brighterwithherbert.
- A shareholder "Say" question representing ~900,000 shares accuses Tesla of missing robotaxi guidance three quarters running; panelists expect a dismissive or non-answer at earnings.
- Goldberg (futureaza) predicts robotaxi will be Tesla's largest profit driver within three years, with Optimus overtaking it in five.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus Gen 3 production line is underway at Fremont in former Model S/X space; Musk said production will start slow, per farzad-fm.
- Gen 3's hand has 22 degrees of freedom versus 11 on Gen 2.
- Panelists (brighterwithherbert) expect a first-off Gen 3 production unit around late summer to late September, arguing generalization — not manufacturing — is the real bottleneck.
- Optimus is framed by multiple sources as the next major driver of AI inference compute demand, potentially dwarfing current agentic-AI workloads.

## Financials
- Tesla Q2 2026 deliveries exceeded 480,000 units, its best Q2 ever, per futureaza.
- Ahead of Wednesday's earnings call, panelists model $27–28B revenue and EPS near/above $0.60, but flag possible negative free cash flow this quarter given heavy capex.
- Cybercab fleet may be capitalized as fixed assets (~$20,000/unit), implying $20B in capex per million units produced annually.
- SpaceX's first public-company earnings call expected one to two weeks after Tesla's, with August 9 as the outside 10-Q deadline.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla launched in Latvia (pop-up store opening August 21) and Uruguay within a day of each other, its Baltic rollout complete and its third South American market.
- Japan H1 2026 registrations hit ~12,200, up ~166% YoY, already exceeding full-year 2025; Tesla added its 150th Japan Supercharger site via a new 7-Eleven partnership.
- Fred Lambert (Electrek) argues neither Latvia nor Uruguay moves global volume alone, but cites Colombia's 304% April registration surge as a model for reaching Tesla's stalled 2-million-vehicle goal.

## Energy
- Elon Musk personally acquired mobile power-generation company APR Energy for over $1 billion, structured outside Tesla/SpaceX/xAI — widely read as addressing grid-connection delays for xAI's Colossus supercluster.
- Tesla's lithium refining facility in Texas is now complete and fully ramped, shipping material to Giga Texas for 4680 cell production, per a third-party lithium-day attendee.
- Goldberg projects Tesla energy deployment reaching a 20 GWh quarter by year-end or Q1 next year, doubling to ~25 GWh/quarter by end of next year (his stated "hope," not certainty).

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 13 aborted at startup after two Raptor engines failed to trigger properly; Musk said the engines will be replaced, with next attempt targeted July 20, 6:45pm ET.
- The flight was set to carry the first 20 V3 Starlink satellites on a suborbital deployment test, with six units carrying cameras to scan the heat shield during re-entry.
- jobhakdi frames the abort as a positive safety signal (contrasted with a Blue Origin failure), while brighterwithherbert notes SpaceX hit its 600th Falcon 9 booster reflight.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX detailed V3 satellite specs: ~1 Tbps downlink (~10x V2) and 160 Gbps uplink (~22x V2), with 2,048 beams each direction; a host estimates ~7.5 Starship launches of 60 V3 satellites each could double Starlink's current ~450 Tbps cumulative capacity.
- A new Starlink V5 terminal launched: lighter (2.4 lb vs 6.4 lb), higher wind rating (165 mph vs 110 mph), but slightly lower peak speed (~375+ Mbps vs 475 Mbps) — framed as a margin play via lower cost/weight rather than a speed upgrade.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk raised AI1 orbital data-center satellite specs: peak power up 67% to 250 kW (battery-assisted), average power up 33% to 160 kW, sufficient for an Nvidia Rubin NVL72 rack (~72 GPUs); first launches slated for late 2027, per farzad-fm.
- Colossus (Memphis) is described as running ~550,000 GPUs as of early 2026, targeting ~1 million GPUs and ~2 GW of power.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX shares fell to $123.99 (Electrified), a further ~3.92% overnight drop (jobhakdi), now below its $135 IPO price amid a broader AI/semiconductor selloff.
- jobhakdi's bear case: ~170x P/E on a possible $10–11B profit run rate, with only 5% of shares currently floated and 50–60% becoming unlockable by November — a major looming supply overhang; Tesla-multiple comparisons imply downside toward $35–70 absent new catalysts, with Colossus 2/3 and AI-satellite scale (2028) seen as the eventual re-rating triggers.
