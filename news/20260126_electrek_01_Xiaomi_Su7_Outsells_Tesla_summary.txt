Xiaomi's SU7 sedan outsold the Tesla Model 3 in China for full-year 2025, marking the first time since the Model 3's 2019 China launch that another automaker has beaten it in the premium EV sedan segment, according to China Passenger Car Association (CPCA) data.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Xiaomi SU7 beats Model 3 in 2025 China sales:** Xiaomi delivered 258,164 SU7 units in 2025 versus Tesla's 200,361 Model 3 deliveries, a gap of roughly 50,000 units, per CPCA data. The SU7 launched in March 2024.
2. **Pricing, range, and tech comparison:** The base SU7 starts at RMB 215,900 (~$31,000) versus the Model 3 at RMB 235,500 (~$33,800), about 9% cheaper; the SU7 offers 700 km CLTC range versus 634 km for the Model 3 RWD, about 10% more. The SU7 includes free driver-assistance features, standard LiDAR on the updated model, and integration with Xiaomi's HyperOS ecosystem.
3. **Tesla's overall China decline:** Tesla's full-year 2025 China deliveries fell 4.78% year-over-year to 625,698 vehicles, with Model Y deliveries down 11.45%, though Model Y remains China's bestselling SUV overall. The article references a prior report that Tesla confirmed its first year of domestic sales decline in China.
4. **Tesla's countermeasures:** Tesla introduced an RMB 8,000 (~$1,150) insurance subsidy for Model 3 purchases through February, added to a 7-year low-interest financing plan announced earlier in the month. The article reports that Xiaomi, Li Auto, Xpeng, and other Chinese brands matched Tesla's financing offer within days.

## Market & Competition
1. **Xiaomi's expansion plans:** Xiaomi already sells the YU7 SUV, which the article says is competing with the Model Y, and plans to launch additional models in 2026.
2. **Third-party reactions cited:** The article reports that Ferrari was "reportedly" impressed enough by the SU7 to buy one for study, and that Ford CEO Jim Farley has praised the SU7, prompting Ford to form a team focused on competing with Chinese EVs.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the Model 3's five-year China benchmark status has fallen, and that Xiaomi has closed the technology/build-quality gap in under two years by leveraging its smartphone-ecosystem advantage on price, software, and integration. He characterizes Tesla's subsidies, financing deals, and vehicle variants (e.g., Model YL) as a "defensive crutch" rather than a growth strategy, concluding the Model 3 is a good car but no longer good enough in China's market. A top reader comment (25 likes) adds skepticism toward analysts like Dan Ives predicting a 2026 Tesla sales rebound, calling two years of declining data the opposite signal.
