Two hosts (Brian and Randy Kirk) from the channel debate whether Tesla or SpaceX would be the better standalone investment over the next 5–10 years, concluding the question is fundamentally unknowable given rapidly emerging new opportunities (space-based data centers, Macrohard, Optimus scaling) that didn't exist as considerations even months earlier. They also discuss the mechanics and investor implications of a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger without taking a firm position on its likelihood or terms.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus TAM debated as still expanding:** The hosts note that even the view from roughly a year ago that Optimus represented the largest total addressable market in the world may now be outdated, as Elon Musk-linked ventures have since introduced additional TAMs (e.g., space-based data centers, Macrohard) that could rival or exceed Optimus's market size. One host argues mass Optimus deployment (potentially into the hundreds of millions or billions of units) would likely be decisive for Tesla's outcome, while noting skepticism exists among some viewers about whether this happens at all versus within a decade.
2. **Mars as an underappreciated Optimus market:** Beyond Earth, the hosts argue Optimus (or descendant robots) would likely dominate labor on Mars, framing this as an overlooked component of the long-term TAM.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX data-center rental business framed as a new, unpredicted catalyst:** The hosts highlight that SpaceX's plan to rent out space-based data center compute — now reportedly involving multiple clients — was not predicted by analysts and only became feasible due to lower launch costs enabled by Starship; they stress this remains speculative upside dependent on unresolved cost variables (compute costs, panel costs, next-gen chip efficiency).
2. **Compute leasing and chip demand as a shared opportunity:** Both companies are described as benefiting from AI/compute-related opportunities — Tesla via FSD chips, neural networks, and autonomy algorithms, and SpaceX via a large low-earth-orbit satellite constellation subscriber base (cited around 12 million subscribers) — with the hosts arguing this reduces risk in aggressive AI-related cash spending broadly attributed to Musk-linked ventures.
3. **Starlink real-world performance anecdote:** One host recounts inconsistent in-flight Starlink performance on a recent narrow-body flight (paid for but intermittent), contrasted with a strong experience roughly a year earlier, used to illustrate variability in rival/current satellite internet service depending on aircraft type and passenger load.

## Financials
1. **Tesla valuation framed as a matter of perspective, not certainty:** In response to ongoing "sandbagging" speculation (which one host says he sees no evidence for, practically, ethically, or legally), the discussion pivots to Tesla's high price-to-earnings ratio, arguing that believing a stock is undervalued at a hypothetical fair value doesn't mean current price weakness isn't itself a legitimate buying opportunity; one host references personally buying Tesla in 2019 near $178 based on this reasoning.
2. **Merger uncertainty and investor outcome framing:** The hosts describe a scenario where an investor holding shares in both companies could see a combined stake grow substantially (illustrated hypothetically as ~$40,000 in Tesla shares potentially becoming ~$800,000 with sufficient combined appreciation), arguing that even an unfavorable merger split would likely still leave shareholders well off given expected growth in both businesses. One host says he continues to hold Tesla shares and is waiting for SpaceX's share price to fall below its IPO price (noted as trading around $155 at time of filming) before considering a purchase.
