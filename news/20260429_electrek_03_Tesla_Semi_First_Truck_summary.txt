Tesla has produced the first Semi truck off its new high-volume production line at Gigafactory Nevada, marking a significant milestone for the long-delayed electric truck program after nearly nine years since its 2017 unveiling.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **High-volume production begins at dedicated Nevada factory:** The first Tesla Semi has rolled off a new high-volume production line at a dedicated 1.7-million-square-foot factory adjacent to Gigafactory Nevada in Sparks. The facility is designed for 50,000 trucks annually, though Tesla will ramp gradually. Analysts project 5,000–15,000 unit deliveries in 2026, though the author considers that too optimistic. Two trims are available: a Standard Range (325 miles at full 82,000-lb GCW) priced at ~$260,000, and a Long Range (500 miles) at $290,000 — both positioning the Semi as the lowest-priced Class 8 battery electric tractor on the market.

2. **Specs and charging infrastructure:** Both trims feature an 800-kW tri-motor drivetrain producing 1,072 hp and support 1.2-MW Megacharger speeds, restoring 60% range in ~30 minutes. Tesla has opened its first Megacharger station in Ontario, California, and has mapped 66 Megacharger locations across 15 states. The 4680 battery cells are manufactured on the same Nevada campus, resolving the prior supply chain bottleneck that had deprioritized the Semi.

3. **Ecosystem expansion:** Alyath plans to unveil a "Tesla Semi as a Service" model at ACT Expo on May 4, 2026, offering a bundled monthly payment covering the vehicle, charging infrastructure, and energy supply — eliminating fleet capex barriers. Drayage operator MDB has also launched a three-week freight pilot at Southern California ports.

## Market & Competition
1. **Strong demand signal and competitive lead:** In California's Clean Truck & Bus Voucher program (January 2025–February 2026), the Tesla Semi accounted for 965 of 1,067 applications, versus fewer than 100 combined for Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo. Competing Class 8 BEVs — Freightliner eCascadia and Volvo's trucks — ship in limited numbers at higher prices and shorter range. Nikola, a former competitor, has gone bankrupt. Volvo is noted as the global electric truck leader by units delivered.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert calls the high-volume production milestone the one that "matters," arguing the economic case — $290,000 for 500-mile range, plus a claimed 3% TCO advantage over diesel — is becoming difficult for fleets to ignore. He frames the key remaining challenge as execution speed: ramping production and building out the Megacharger network fast enough to match demand, comparing the Semi's potential market impact to the Model 3 in midsize sedans.
