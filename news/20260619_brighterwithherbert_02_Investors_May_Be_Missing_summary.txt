This video features host "brighterwithherbert" interviewing Brian Wang, futurist and blogger at nextbigfuture.com, who argues that investors are dramatically underestimating SpaceX's valuation potential. Wang's core thesis is that SpaceX is transitioning from a launch/satellite company into a major AI infrastructure and data center rental business — driven by its Colossus supercomputer, Cursor acquisition, Grok AI, and upcoming Rubin chip deployments — and that this transformation could drive revenue and stock appreciation far faster than the widely-cited Micron analogy suggests. The discussion is entirely focused on SpaceX and XAI, with Tesla mentioned only briefly in passing comparisons.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX as AI Infrastructure Giant — The Core Thesis:** Wang argues that most investors still view SpaceX primarily as a launch and Starlink company, but the company is rapidly becoming an AI infrastructure and data center rental business. He draws a direct analogy to Micron, which grew from roughly $6 billion per quarter in revenue to nearly $25 billion per quarter over two years as AI demand exploded, with its stock going from around $100 to over $1,000. Wang believes SpaceX could be growing even faster than Micron did.

2. **Revenue Growth Trajectory — Q1 2026 to Q4 2026:** Wang presents a quarter-by-quarter analysis. He states SpaceX was at approximately $4.6 billion per quarter in Q1 2026, and estimates it will reach roughly $15 billion per quarter by Q4 2026 — driven by AI rental revenue (from deals with Anthropic and Google), the Cursor acquisition contributing approximately $1 billion per quarter now and heading toward approximately $0.7 billion per month, plus approximately $6–7 billion from ongoing Starlink and launch revenue. He says this is roughly twice as fast as Micron's comparable growth window.

3. **Full-Year and Annual Run Rate Projections:** Wang states SpaceX had approximately $18.7 billion in revenue in 2025 and projects the company will reach approximately $46 billion in revenue by end of 2026. He agrees with others who project a $60 billion run rate by Q4 2026, and says SpaceX could reach $120–150 billion in revenue and approximately $100 billion in earnings as Rubin chips come online, potentially reaching $200 billion in revenue if Rubin deployments scale.

4. **Colossus Compute — Existing and Planned Capacity:** Wang outlines the current Colossus supercomputer infrastructure. He states Colossus 1 has approximately 230,000 chips (context suggests H100/H200 class). The S1 filing disclosed plans for an additional 220,000 GB300 chips with 400 megawatts of power for Colossus 2, potentially bringing the total to approximately 550,000–700,000 chips. Wang notes that approximately 345,000 chips remain available for rental beyond what is already committed, and that every 100,000–110,000 chips rented at Blackwell rates generates approximately $10–11 billion in rental revenue per year (per Wang's estimate). He notes that Anthropic received approximately 230,000 chips under its deal.

5. **Rubin Chip Allocation and Revenue Multiplier:** Wang cites Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker on the Altimeter BG2 podcast as saying XAI/SpaceX has approximately 20% market share of Nvidia Rubin chip allocations reserved. He states Rubin chips offer approximately 10x the inference compute of Blackwell chips, and that a fully monetized Rubin deployment could generate $100 billion or more per year in rental revenue — potentially $1.5–2 trillion at full scale per figures Wang attributes to comments by "Chamath" on the All-In podcast. Wang believes Rubin rack production is already underway from Dell and others, with meaningful scale likely by September–October 2026, and 100,000 Rubin chips per quarter potentially achievable by Q4 2026 or Q1 2027.

6. **Cursor Acquisition and AI Monetization Upside:** Wang states SpaceX acquired Cursor, which he says is currently at a $4 billion run rate heading toward a $10 billion run rate. He cites the host's figure that Cursor is used by approximately 70% of Fortune 1000 companies. Wang argues that if Grok with SpaceX inference becomes competitive with Anthropic Claude, SpaceX could replace Anthropic as the AI provider within Cursor — effectively internalizing what Anthropic currently earns (which Wang estimates at approximately $50 billion in revenue run rate). He notes Anthropic grew from a $9 billion run rate to a $50 billion run rate in approximately five to six months after releasing Claude Code.

7. **Third Data Center Location — "Macrohard" in Mississippi:** Wang describes a third XAI/SpaceX data center facility, which he refers to as "Macrohard," located in Mississippi. He states it is approximately 810,000 square feet (48 acres), was a former logistics warehouse acquired around December 2025–February 2026, and has been under internal buildout preparation for approximately four to five months. He says it is ready for power and chip installation but notes no recent public updates on it. He suggests it could eventually support 0.5–2 gigawatts of power capacity.

8. **Energy Infrastructure — Gas Turbines and Power Sourcing:** Wang details SpaceX/XAI's power strategy. He states Korean gas turbine maker Doosan makes 388-megawatt gas turbines, and XAI/SpaceX has an order for 12 of them — with 2 expected by end of 2026, approximately 5 more in 2027, and another 7 starting in 2029, totaling approximately 4.66 gigawatts when fully delivered. He also describes a joint venture in which XAI/SpaceX owns 49.9% of Solar Turbines (a Caterpillar subsidiary), securing approximately 67% of their turbine capacity — locking up approximately 1.4 gigawatts of mobile natural gas generation. Wang also mentions the Tennessee Valley Authority providing approximately 150 megawatts of grid power per new data center location. He estimates total current on-the-ground power for Colossus 1 and 2 combined at just under 2 gigawatts.

9. **Space-Based AI — Starship Launch Path to 1 Gigawatt:** Wang explains SpaceX's plan for space-based AI compute. He states current Starship Version 3 can launch approximately 100 tons, with AI satellite systems consuming approximately 70 kilowatts per ton, yielding approximately 7 megawatts of AI compute per launch. At current regulatory approval of approximately 120 launches, that caps space-based AI at approximately 700 megawatts — just under 1 gigawatt. Reaching 1 gigawatt would require approximately 130 dedicated AI launches plus FAA approval to increase launches to 160–200. Wang says SpaceX is building five Starship launch towers (two at Boca Chica, two at Kennedy Space Center LC-39A, and two integrated towers at Cape Canaveral SLC-37). He estimates Starship Version 4, planned by end of 2027, will double per-launch payload to approximately 14 megawatts. Wang values each 7-megawatt launch at approximately $700 million per year in rental revenue at current Blackwell rates, rising to approximately $2.1 billion per launch per year if full Anthropic-level monetization is achieved, and potentially $5–10 billion per launch with Rubin-class chips.

10. **Valuation Floor and Comparable Analysis:** Wang argues that SpaceX's terrestrial data center business alone — excluding Starlink, launch, and space AI — justifies a valuation floor of approximately $2 trillion today, rising to approximately $5 trillion or more by end of 2027. He compares SpaceX's projected Q4 2026 revenue profile to Broadcom (approximately $88 billion annual run rate, approximately 65% margins, valued at approximately $1.9 trillion) and to Nvidia in 2025 (approximately $88 billion revenue run rate at the time of its $4–4.5 trillion valuation). He argues SpaceX at $60 billion revenue with faster growth and 70–80% margins would not trade below those comparable valuations.

11. **IPO Structure and Stock Inclusion Schedule:** Wang describes SpaceX's IPO as having raised approximately $85 billion (including the greenshoe option, up from an initial $75 billion), at approximately 5% liquidity. He states the stock has fluctuated between $2.5–3 trillion market cap since IPO. He outlines a schedule of index inclusion events: Russell inclusion on approximately June 26, 2026 (estimated $12 billion of passive buying); NASDAQ inclusion on approximately July 6, 2026 (estimated additional $12–15 billion); NASDAQ rebalancing September 18, 2026; and Russell/NASDAQ rebalances in December 2026. He notes large shareholder unlocks in August and December 2026, but argues each unlock is followed within three to five weeks by a major index rebalancing buying event. He also mentions a potential S&P 500 inclusion in June 2027 if not accelerated earlier.
