Tesla has raised Model Y prices in the US by up to $1,000 on its Premium and Performance trims — the first price increase on the model in two years, ending a prolonged period of aggressive discounting.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Model Y pricing:** The increases apply to the Premium RWD ($45,990, +$1,000), Premium AWD ($49,990, +$1,000), and Performance AWD ($57,990, +$500). The base RWD ($39,990) and base AWD ($41,990) remain unchanged. Increases are under 3% on affected trims.

2. **Prior pricing history:** Tesla had slashed Model Y prices by up to $2,000 in April 2024, reaching the model's then-lowest price. A broader series of cuts beginning in early 2023 had removed as much as $13,000 from the sticker price.

## Financials
1. **Margin trajectory:** Automotive gross margins dropped from above 25% in early 2023 to below 18% by mid-2025 amid sustained price cuts. Tesla's Q1 2026 deliveries showed a 50,000-vehicle inventory build and missed expectations. Full-year 2025 deliveries totaled 1.636 million, down from 2023's peak.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitive positioning:** The price increase comes as BYD, Hyundai's Ioniq 5, and the Ford Mustang Mach-E compete aggressively in the mid-size EV SUV segment. The Model Y Premium AWD at $49,990 now sits above the Ioniq 5 AWD (starting ~$45,000) and the Mach-E Select AWD ($42,995).

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert views the increase as a selective confidence signal — Tesla is capturing more margin on higher-trim, higher-profitability configurations while protecting the entry-level price point, suggesting strength in a specific segment rather than a broad demand recovery. He notes lower US gas prices are likely contributing to improved demand.
