## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk said he'll give one SpaceX share to every child in America starting 2027 — roughly 72 million kids, about $10.5 billion at the current ~$146 share price — calling it "the first of many." The pledge followed Anthropic's announcement of a public-benefit equity fund; Gwynne Shotwell and her husband separately pledged one share each to over 2 million lower-income kids. Mechanism and timing remain unspecified; one host noted most of Musk's SpaceX stock is restricted from sale until June 2027.
- Tesla's December 3 shareholder proxy carries no merger item — only board re-election (Musk, Robyn Denholm, JB Straubel), a say-on-pay vote, and PwC auditor ratification. Electrek's Fred Lambert argues board overlaps (Ira Ehrenpreis and Kimbal Musk both sit on SpaceX's board), merger-friendly vesting mechanics in Musk's pay packages, and a new retail auto-voting default collectively resemble merger-vote preparation, though no merger is confirmed. Kalshi odds on a merger being announced before February 2027 have risen to 67%, up from ~30% two weeks ago, per one source.
- SpaceX and Tesla earnings now fall roughly a day apart (SpaceX first, Tesla the following day), fueling speculation across multiple sources that proximity could pressure a merger decision — though this remains explicitly speculative in every source that raised it.
- A new ~444-acre SpaceX data center site has been identified outside Brownsville, Texas, with ~400–500 MW of on-site power — described as a third major compute complex after two in Tennessee/Mississippi. Estimates of its revenue potential (from $132B to $183B depending on chip generation) are the reporting host's own projections, not company guidance.
- Reports of a roughly $40 billion debt deal with Apollo tied to SpaceX's data-center buildout remain unconfirmed.

## Autonomous Driving
- Slovakia has granted preliminary approval for FSD (supervised), the ninth country to do so per Musk; approved countries now cover 13.87% of a stated 65% population-coverage target, with Germany's approval (if granted) pushing that to 32%.
- FSD V15 is anticipated around Tesla's upcoming earnings call, with Tesla saying it will "unlock new features and higher levels of safety" versus V14; portions reportedly already run in the Robotaxi fleet.

## Robotaxi
- Texas's registered autonomous-vehicle fleet rose to 803 (from 769), driven by 34 new Model Y registrations — the first such increase since last September. The host's tally puts the state fleet at 454 Model Ys and 349 Cybercabs; one commentator is skeptical the addition reflects real growth rather than unit swaps.
- Robotaxi service now reportedly runs until midnight in Austin, Dallas, and Tampa, an unannounced extension one host says he personally confirmed by phone.
- Firsthand rider accounts from the Austin X Takeover describe a polished Cybercab experience — ambient lighting for car ID, functioning exit-camera indicators, and tipping options including $0.69 and $4.20 presets.

## Electric Vehicles
- Musk reiterated that Optimus and Cybercab "will be the vast majority of Tesla's value," naming super intelligence, robots, solar power, and "massive space" as the only things that scale to enormous economic size; he called the Roadster "super cool" but not a value driver, and said it may become Tesla's only manually driven model long-term.
- Tesla's new Roadster drive unit delivers 33% more power at 50% less weight than the Plaid's tri-motor unit (~2.6x power-to-weight gain); engineers expect this to migrate into Cybertruck, Model Y, and Cybercab powertrains. The car uses Optimus leg actuators in its rear-wing and throttle systems, and weighs roughly 1,700 kg with a ~1 MW (1,341 hp) output.
- Model Y L Launch Series U.S. delivery windows have slipped to January–March 2027 (some to February–April), from a prior January–February 2027 estimate — a possible demand or logistics signal.
- China's battery and plug-in EVs together made up 58% of new car registrations this year; US EV sales have stabilized at 5–6% of new-car sales after the tax credit's expiration, down from an ~11% pulled-forward share in 2025.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's shareholder letter included an image one host believes depicts Optimus V3, though Tesla hasn't confirmed the version; Tesla is reportedly withholding details amid competitive pressure from Chinese humanoid makers.
- Optimus leg actuators were reused in the Roadster's rear-wing and throttle-valve hardware, giving the Optimus team real-world stress-test data on actuator durability.

## Financials
- Musk's reported 2025 compensation hit $158.36 billion on paper (a 2,522,203:1 pay ratio vs. median employee pay), combining the 2025 CEO Performance Award and a now-forfeited interim award; Tesla's own "realized compensation" table shows he received $0 in 2025.
- Tesla's 2025 net income fell 46% to $3.86 billion as revenue declined for a second straight year to $94.8 billion.
- Tesla energy executive Snider is departing after joining in 2014; AJ Booth takes over. Tesla's energy business grew 9.6% in Q3 by energy deployed.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas called SpaceX's orbital distributed-inference compute business the most underappreciated part of the SpaceX story, a framing Musk endorsed as "very big." Planned orbital racks are expected to use Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips in Q1 next year.
- Musk's stated compute target is 10 gigawatts by end of 2027, responding "maybe more than 10" when pressed.
- Grok token consumption is reportedly growing ~16% month-over-month over three months and roughly 8x year-over-year, cited as evidence of compute demand outpacing supply.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- An analyst estimate projecting ~925% Starlink revenue growth over five years drew a simple "of course" from Musk. Jefferies forecasts Starlink reaching ~23% of aircraft by 2028 (from ~9% today), citing SpaceX's 11,200+ satellite fleet versus a few hundred for its nearest rival; United Airlines is at 40% fleet installation now, targeting 100% by end of 2027.
- A newly filed Tesla patent ties vehicle location to Starlink satellites rather than GPS, which Musk confirmed enables precise navigation; Cybercabs are reportedly being built with Starlink connectivity to supplement 4G LTE for fleet-management communications.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX has applied to raise Starbase launch/landing frequency to as many as 456 Starship flights per year, up from a current limit recalled as roughly 25–50 annually with restricted hours.
- Separate explainers detailed Falcon 9's "hoverslam" landing technique (first achieved December 2015, now routine) and the rationale for catching Super Heavy with tower arms rather than legs — reducing vehicle weight and enabling fast turnaround without trucking the booster between flights; the first tower catch succeeded on its first attempt in October 2024.
