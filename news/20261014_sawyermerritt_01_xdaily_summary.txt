## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla Megapack orders keep stacking up:** Tesla announced a new ~$265 million Megapack order from Taiwan's Apollo Power for 1 GWh of storage (260 Megapack 2XL units) for enterprise and grid projects. Separately, a new Tesla Megapack facility — Bocanova 2, built by Nightpeak Energy in Brazoria County, Texas near Houston — came online as the second phase of a storage development (following the 150 MW first phase, Bocanova 1). These reinforce continued strong demand in Tesla's energy storage business.

2. **Model Y dominates Q3 EV sales; EV market shows growth:** Per Cox Automotive, Tesla sold an estimated 99,037 Model Ys in Q3 2026, representing 37.2% of the entire EV market and roughly 9x the volume of the next best-selling non-Tesla EV. EVs made up about 6.5% of all new U.S. car sales in Q3, up 7.7% from Q2 — described as the first significant growth in about a year.

3. **Roadster unveil and other Tesla corporate/market items:** Tesla will unveil its next-generation Roadster on 2026-10-15, livestreamed on X at 8:55 PM ET; Sawyer Merritt will attend in person. Tesla also opened Q3 earnings call question submissions ahead of next week's call. Tesla announced plans to launch vehicle sales in Oman starting 2027, already hiring a first team in Muscat. In Canada, Tesla paused Model 3 Premium orders after exhausting its import quota, with resumption expected once the government issues more quota (expected March 2027).

4. **Cybercab unsupervised rides spotted in Austin:** A post shows a Cybercab operating in Austin giving unsupervised rides, consistent with Tesla's ongoing driverless robotaxi rollout in that market (per RobotaxiTracker.com, the last known unsupervised fleet count for Austin was 32, as of 2026-07-30 — stale, unchanged for ~76 days).

5. **GM reiterates no robotaxi ambitions:** GM CEO Mary Barra said the company is on track for eyes-off, hands-off driving by 2028 but has no plans to enter the robotaxi business, emphasizing that customers value private vehicle ownership and flexibility over ride-hailing — a competitive contrast to Tesla's robotaxi strategy.

6. **Grok Voice 3.0 launches across SpaceX and Tesla:** SpaceX introduced Grok Voice 3.0, described as the "world's most intelligent speech-to-speech model" with sub-one-second latency and lower cost than other frontier models (per SpaceX's own announcement). SpaceX said Starlink call centers use Grok Voice to handle roughly 20,000 customer calls per day, with about 70% resolved with no human in the loop, and roughly one in four sales calls ending in a purchase before the caller hangs up. Grok Voice 3.0 is also stated to handle millions of conversations daily inside Tesla vehicles.

7. **SpaceX seeks major Starbase launch-cadence expansion:** SpaceX filed for FAA approval to conduct up to 456 Starship launches and 912 landings per year from Starbase — a large increase from the current limit of 25 launches and 50 landings annually. The filing reportedly outlines a phased approach, with Phase 1 covering up to 100 annual launches and up to 200 annual landings (100 of which would be Starship-related, per the post, though the full phase breakdown is not fully detailed in the excerpt).

8. **Morgan Stanley flags SpaceX's "Distributed Inference Cloud" as underappreciated:** Analyst Adam Jonas wrote in a new SpaceX note that the company's largest uncontemplated growth driver, in his view, is a "Distributed Inference Cloud" business, which he says investors are largely ignoring, though early signs are reportedly emerging. This is an analyst opinion/thesis rather than a confirmed SpaceX business line.

9. **Starship Ship 40 returns to Starbase:** A brief post ("Ship 40 is home!") with an accompanying image indicates the Starship test vehicle has returned to Starbase, though no further detail on its testing status or next steps was given.
