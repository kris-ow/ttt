This video features FutureAzA host Brian in conversation with Herbert from Brighter, discussing Tesla's Q1 2026 delivery numbers, sales performance across global markets, and the trajectory of Full Self-Driving as a future demand driver. The core thesis is that pessimistic narratives around Tesla's China and Europe sales miss critical context — particularly the delivery wave mechanics and the Model Y refresh disruption from the prior year — and that Tesla's fundamentals are stronger than headlines suggest.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla's delivery wave explained:** The hosts walk through Tesla's well-known "delivery wave" pattern, where cars destined for distant markets (exports via ship to Europe, the US, etc.) are produced early in the quarter to allow for transit time, while domestic deliveries are fulfilled later. This explains why Tesla's April China domestic sales appear weak — Giga Shanghai prioritizes exports at the start of the quarter. The hosts note this pattern is not new and that media outlets covering it as a negative story are being deliberately misleading or ignoring known context.

2. **China April data — exports vs. domestic sales:** Tesla's April numbers out of Giga Shanghai showed strong export figures, with the hosts characterizing it as record exports from China since 2022. Brian references a post from Tesla Owner Silicon Valley showing approximately 79,478 Model 3 and Y units produced. A Yahoo Finance/Benzinga article cited a 36% jump in China sales in April, which included exports. The framing of Tesla "falling out of the top 10" in China's NEV domestic market was criticized as misleading because it excluded the export component.

3. **US sales dominance in Q1 2026:** The hosts highlight that the Model Y and Model 3 ranked first and second in US EV sales in Q1 2026, with the Model Y selling over 70,000 units and the Model 3 over 30,000 units, totaling approximately 110,000 combined. The next closest competitor — identified as a Toyota BZ model — sold around 10,000 units in the quarter, meaning the Model Y outsold the next competitor by roughly seven times.

4. **Europe sales rebound and Giga Berlin expansion:** Tesla broke a 13-month sales losing streak in Europe, selling 13,740 cars across the EU, UK, and EFTA countries in the reported period, up 29% year-over-year. The hosts contextualize the prior year's weakness as a result of the Model Y refresh requiring factory downtime. Germany is highlighted as a key benchmark market that has shown signs of recovery. Giga Berlin announced it has produced 750,000 cumulative vehicles and plans to increase production by 20%, which the hosts interpret as a strong forward-looking demand signal.

5. **South Korea and other markets:** Tesla is noted as the top imported car brand in South Korea, a market where Hyundai and Kia together hold approximately 90% domestic market share — making Tesla's import performance there particularly notable.

6. **EV adoption trends in Europe:** The hosts reference recent data showing gas car sales in Europe fell 17% and diesel fell 13.5%, describing these as accelerating structural trends. They suggest rising gas prices will further push consumers toward EVs, with early signs of this appearing in Australia and New Zealand export demand from China.

7. **Oregon's weight-mile tax proposal:** The video briefly covers a proposed Oregon measure that would replace the gas tax with a biannual weight-mile fee requiring vehicle owners to bring cars to testing stations twice a year to measure weight and miles driven. Both hosts criticize this as impractical and unfair to EV owners. Brian notes Washington State charges an EV premium of roughly $250–$300 on annual vehicle tabs to offset lost gas tax revenue.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD as a future demand driver in Europe:** Herbert argues that the rollout of Full Self-Driving in Europe will become a significant demand driver for Tesla, potentially converting consumers who were previously indifferent to EVs. He notes that the Netherlands has already approved FSD, Ireland is in talks, Sweden and Belgium are also progressing (Belgium conducting a 5,000 km Tesla FSD trial), and that consumer awareness of self-driving capability is already changing purchasing intent anecdotally.

2. **FSD version 14.3.2 user experience:** Brian shares a first-person account of driving with FSD version 14.3.2, describing an impressive real-world scenario where the car detected a stopped vehicle with hazards on ahead, began reversing and signaling to go around, then cancelled the maneuver when the car ahead resumed driving — all without driver input. He contrasts the impressive driving capability with frustration over navigation routing, which he jokes is overtrained on circuitous routing patterns.

3. **FSD market size estimate:** Brian estimates that if Full Self-Driving appeals to even 10% of US car buyers, that translates to roughly 1–2 million additional vehicle sales per year in the US alone, framing it as a "ginormous" incremental demand opportunity.

## Market & Competition

1. **Legacy automaker struggles vs. Tesla:** The hosts contrast Tesla's production expansion at Giga Berlin with GM laying off hundreds to thousands of IT and salaried workers. They acknowledge that many EV startups and legacy players are still losing money overall but note that per-unit profitability is improving across the industry, citing a Honda engineer working in China as a source. The broader narrative is that electrification is not reversing — the competitive threat to Tesla is rising but so is the overall market.

2. **Europe's 2035 ICE ban:** The hosts debate whether the 2035 European ban on new internal combustion engine vehicles will hold. Herbert predicts the deadline will be repeatedly pushed back under lobbying pressure, while Brian argues that feasibility objections are harder to sustain now that Tesla, Chinese automakers, and Volkswagen (ID.4, ID.3, ID Buzz) have demonstrated mass-market EV production is viable.
