This video is a pre-earnings Q3 delivery forecast discussion between the host and guest Randy Kirk, breaking down expected production by factory (Shanghai, Berlin, Fremont, Texas) and comparing their estimates against Wall Street consensus and analyst targets ahead of Tesla's October 2 delivery report.

## Financials
1. **Wall Street consensus and analyst estimates for Q3 deliveries:** The host cites a Wall Street consensus of 454,000 deliveries, with a roughly 45,000-unit spread between the highest and lowest analyst estimates. Individual bank estimates cited: Goldman Sachs 435,000, an estimate around 446,000 (attributed to "Stone 10"), USB roughly 470,000, and Barclays/Cowen around 480,000.
2. **Host and guest forecasts diverge, guest revises down mid-discussion:** The host's own forecast is 482,191 deliveries. Guest Randy Kirk initially states 530,000, built from Shanghai (~260,000–270,000), Giga Berlin (~78,000, based on an assumed ~6,000/week run rate over 13 weeks), Fremont (125,000) and Texas (50,000), but revises down to 425,000 during the conversation, citing a lack of urgency compared to the prior year's record quarter and skepticism that Tesla is building meaningful inventory reserves.
3. **Prior-year record quarter cited as benchmark:** Kirk references a prior record of 497,099 deliveries in the year-ago Q3 period, which he attributes to a tax credit/loan expiration driving demand; he says he doubts Q3 2026 will surpass that record due to reduced urgency in the market this time.
4. **Anecdotal low-inventory signals:** Kirk says he visited a local Tesla store and found only one car in the showroom, and reports hearing from followers on X that some local showrooms have zero inventory or none within a 200-mile radius — cited as a possible sign Tesla is pushing for a strong delivery number, which he speculates could be related to the SpaceX-Tesla merger context, though he offers no direct evidence for that link.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Shanghai (Giga Shanghai) production tracking near capacity:** Kirk estimates Shanghai produced roughly 90,000 units per month in the first two months of the quarter and expects a similar or slightly higher third month, projecting a quarterly total around 260,000–270,000. He notes Shanghai is operating above rated capacity, domestic China sales are weak, and China recently introduced a temporary buyer incentive (described as roughly $1,000) that may pull forward some end-of-quarter sales; strong export demand to South Korea, Japan and other markets is offsetting weak domestic sales.
2. **Giga Berlin output and European registration trends:** Tesla reportedly guided toward increasing Berlin production to around 6,500 cars/week starting in the quarter; Kirk based his estimate on a more conservative 6,000/week, yielding roughly 78,000 units. Norway registration data (cited via Roland Pircher/Bloomberg-style tracking) reportedly showed weak July–August figures followed by a September delivery surge, with the seasonal delivery peak this year shifted later in the quarter versus the prior year. The host argues year-to-date pace doesn't point to a record European quarter, and that a meaningful share of cars registered in Europe (including Norway) are actually built in China, implying Berlin's own output may be weaker than headline registration numbers suggest.
3. **Fremont and Texas (Giga Texas) production split debated:** Kirk projects Fremont at 125,000 units and Texas at 50,000 units for the quarter (total ~250,000 combined with other lines), citing empty showrooms, a production ramp, multi-month wait times, and a same-day leasing rate increase as evidence of strong Fremont output; he also notes Model S/X are expected near zero and Cybertruck volumes are underperforming, with Semi tractor units possibly folded into an "other" category to offset. He mentions Texas plant downtime days and describes the plant as not running as aggressively as in prior periods, and Cybercab units may be in production but not yet counted as sold.
