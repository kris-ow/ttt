The host (Dillon Loomis) argues that the Cybercab hardware unveiling is a distraction from the real bottleneck — Tesla's FSD software, particularly the unreleased V15 — and works through a grab-bag of Cybercab launch details: door safety testing, pricing anomalies, fleet-purchase interest, a NHTSA audit query, and current unsupervised mile totals versus Waymo.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Everything is hinging on V15, not the Cybercab hardware:** The host says Tesla engineers at the Cybercab event reportedly responded to hard questions from an attendee (Chuck Cook) with "wait on V15," reinforcing his argument that FSD software, not the vehicle hardware, is the real gating factor for robotaxi scaling.
2. **Thin horizontal object detection — not unsolved:** Following up on a prior video referencing Dr. Knowitall's concerns about convolutional neural networks struggling to detect thin horizontal objects (e.g., chains, gates), the host shares an anecdote of his Model 3 on FSD 14.3.8 successfully detecting and reversing from a chain-link fence, while cautioning this is one anecdote and does not mean the broader problem is solved. He argues viable technical paths exist (higher-resolution image retention, larger context windows/memory) and expresses confidence Tesla will address this via V15 or a future update, noting Tesla and Elon Musk have both cited memory as a current hardware/software limiting factor.
3. **FSD version rollout status:** Tesla Scope reported FSD 14.2 Light rolling out to Hardware 3 vehicles for the first time in eligible regions, while FSD 14.3.9 is rolling out to some Hardware 4 users.
4. **Unsupervised mile milestone:** Tesla surpassed 1 million unsupervised miles, up from 380,000 at the Q2 earnings call — a roughly 17% week-over-week growth rate, above the 10% guidance given on the Q2 call. The host contrasts this with Waymo's reported 220+ million unsupervised miles, noting Tesla's sample size, while growing quickly, remains very small.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab ride-quality anecdote:** A commentator (Ethan McConna, per the host) reported taking 16 Cybercab rides after the event with no "looping" issues, and suggested pickup/drop-off behavior has improved, though he still wants dynamic pickup wait times (citing Waymo as having this) since Tesla's current 7-minute wait window can leave the Cybercab blocking traffic.
2. **Fleet and hub tracking:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla has 437 total vehicles registered in Texas, including 49 Cybercabs. The host notes Florida city fleet sizes are uncertain but cites a claim from David Moss that each Florida city has seven vehicles with only two active at a time (one at night), implying the tracker's 15-per-city figure is an upper limit; Florida operations have run for roughly 50–68 days as small-scale pilots.
3. **Dynamic pricing confirmed:** Tesla confirmed Robotaxi pricing is dynamic (supply/demand based), meaning pricing comparisons/screenshots are only snapshots; one example cited showed a Cybercab ride priced higher than a Model Y ride for the same trip.
4. **Fleet-purchase interest form, later deleted:** After the Cybercab event, Tesla circulated a form gauging interest in fleet vehicle purchases, mobility hubs, and infrastructure collaboration. Per Sawyer Merritt, about 73,000 people clicked the link; a Tesla Cybercab engineer (Eric) said tens of thousands filled it out but later deleted that post — which the host interprets as a sign third-party fleet ownership is still a long way off, given many unresolved questions around pricing, insurance, liability, geofencing, and operations.
5. **NHTSA audit query on self-certification:** NHTSA is conducting an audit query into Tesla's self-certification of the Cybercab. The host stresses this is a review, not a finding of wrongdoing, and cautions that a "1,000 Cybercab" figure used as a placeholder in the query is being wrongly cited by some as evidence of imminent large-scale deployment — it is not. He notes Tesla is legally capped at 49 Cybercabs on Texas roads currently and likely won't scale further until the query is resolved.
6. **Door safety testing issues:** Independent testers found mixed results on Cybercab pinch-detection: passenger doors passed banana and fist tests, but the trunk failed a banana test (crushing through) and only partially recognized objects in follow-up tests; cardboard boxes were reportedly not detected by doors in another test, knocking over a tester. Tesla reportedly told one tester a Cybercab software update addressing this is coming soon.
7. **Production and infrastructure details:** Cybercabs are now rolling off the production line with a Starlink module built into the hatch tooling (per Joe Tegtmeyer), suggesting a shift from retrofit to production-line integration — though it's unconfirmed whether passengers will get personal-device Starlink hotspot access. Separately, the Cybercab does not currently support AC (home) charging and relies solely on the Supercharger network, per Kyle Field.
8. **Operational hiccups:** Reported incidents include a canceled Cybercab ride with no explanation (Josh Field) and a ride showing an internal engineering interface with a manual joystick control (David Moss), which the host frames as evidence that scaling from dozens to hundreds of vehicles multiplies small operational bugs.
9. **Fire department concerns in Austin:** An Austin fire captain said Tesla has trained the department on Cybercab's touchscreen controls but voiced skepticism about relying on a touchscreen (rather than physical driver controls) if a Cybercab blocks a fire engine, citing risks like gloved hands, cracked screens, or power failure; the same official was also noted as having publicly criticized Waymo.
10. **Possible loaner-vehicle replacement:** Code found in the Tesla app suggests Tesla may eventually offer Robotaxi rides in place of loaner vehicles during service visits, though this is not live and would initially be limited to cities with active Robotaxi operations.
11. **Europe timeline:** Elon Musk said he hopes to bring Cybercab to Europe "soon," but the host is skeptical given ongoing regulatory hurdles for supervised FSD approval in Europe, predicting cybercabs are unlikely there before 2027.
