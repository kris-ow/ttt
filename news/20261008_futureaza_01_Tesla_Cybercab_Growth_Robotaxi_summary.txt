This futureaza episode, featuring guest commentator Herbert, covers delays and progress across several Tesla product lines — the Roadster, Optimus, Cybertruck, Cybercab/robotaxi rollout, and Semi — while closing with speculation about the long-rumored Tesla-SpaceX merger. The core thesis is that most delays stem not from manufacturing capability but from battery supply constraints (Semi, Cybertruck) and FSD software readiness (robotaxi scaling), with hosts arguing Tesla is deliberately prioritizing robotaxi development over other launches.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab fleet growth tracked at 17% weekly, could reach 1,000+ by year-end:** Citing Munro Live's growth-rate analysis, the hosts say that at a sustained 17% weekly growth rate, the Cybercab fleet could exceed 1,000 units by the end of this year and surpass 2,000 by April of next year. They note the fleet went from about 69 to roughly 169 Cybercabs over a recent weekend (40+ added in one day), arguing this proves manufacturing capacity isn't the bottleneck — FSD readiness is.
2. **FSD version 15 seen as the gating factor for scaling:** The hosts say the remaining "edge cases" for Autonomous driving are parking and destination memory, and expect growth to accelerate (potentially to 20-25% weekly by December) once FSD v15 ships, which a Tesla-affiliated figure (referred to as "Yasha") reportedly called extremely safe. They describe this as a "flip the switch" moment where daily additions could jump from dozens to hundreds, though one host cautions reaching 50,000 cars by the end of next year is "hard to say."
3. **Las Vegas robotaxi deadline and projections:** Per the hosts, Las Vegas has approved up to 5,000 autonomous vehicles, and Tesla told a Vegas committee it expects around 3,000 by mid-next-year. Vegas reportedly requires some paid commercial robotaxi service to launch within 90 days of a proposal, implying a launch by the end of this year, starting small and scaling to "hundreds at a time." The hosts project a Tesla robotaxi fleet (combining Houston, Dallas, Vegas, etc.) could exceed 2,000 cars by February or March of next year.
4. **Investor frustration over pace of scaling:** One host notes significant investor frustration that the rollout isn't scaling faster, referencing Elon Musk's ambitious robotaxi targets from a shareholder meeting roughly a year prior as having proven "too ambitious," while arguing the slow crash rate suggests safety isn't the limiting factor.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi truck rollout stalled, batteries suspected cause:** Despite the Semi factory opening and a recent event showcasing trucks with customer liveries, the hosts note that about half of those trucks remained in the yard the following Monday, uncollected by customers. They attribute the delay primarily to 4680 battery supply constraints (also cited by Tesla on a recent earnings call as limiting vehicle supply), calculating that even redirecting all Cybertruck battery allocation to Semi would cover only about 5,000 trucks — short of a stated ~12,500-per-quarter target — leaving them convinced batteries, not component shortages generally, are the bottleneck.
2. **Roadster demonstration delayed again, to October 15:** A planned Roadster demonstration event was postponed from October 1st due to heavy rain/tropical storm conditions in Austin, with the event reportedly rescheduled to October 15th. Hosts express skepticism given the Roadster's roughly decade-long history of repeated delays, and say they do not expect the car to be available for sale this year or next, viewing the event as a demonstration only, with Tesla's full focus currently on robotaxi.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus version 3 delayed, demonstration timeline unclear:** The hosts note Optimus has seen repeated delay and schedule confusion (originally expected around a Christmas timeframe, then pushed), and say the pace still seems roughly in line with expectations from about a year ago. One host relays a third party's (Dr. Scott Walter's) theory, flagged as speculative, that Tesla may try to differentiate Optimus by showing a very large number of units simultaneously (the third party speculated figures in the thousands) rather than individual demos, to make an impact distinct from competitors' single-robot showcases.
2. **Optimus supply chain audits reportedly underway:** One host claims (self-described as something listeners "didn't hear from me") that supply chain audits for Optimus components are ongoing not just in China but in other countries as well, and says they would be surprised if parts weren't already in transit — though this is presented as the host's own unconfirmed claim rather than a confirmed fact.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation continues without confirmation:** Hosts discuss ongoing speculation about a Tesla-SpaceX merger, noting Elon Musk was reportedly asked about timing on the All-In podcast a couple of weeks prior, with no concrete answer given. They mention a commentator (referred to as Nicholas Gibbs) predicted the merger would be announced at the Roadster event, which the hosts view as unlikely, and cite an analysis by Alexandra Merz finding that corporate mergers/acquisitions are almost always announced on a Sunday or Monday — implying an October 15th (Thursday) announcement would be atypical. No merger terms, valuation, or firm timeline were disclosed.
