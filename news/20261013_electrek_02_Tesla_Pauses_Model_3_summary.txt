Electrek reports that Tesla has paused orders for its Shanghai-built Model 3 Premium (RWD and AWD) in Canada, citing exhaustion of its "import quota" for Chinese-made EVs, but Canada's official government tracker shows the national quota is far from exhausted.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model 3 Premium pulled from Canadian configurator:** Tesla removed both Model 3 Premium RWD and AWD trims — the Shanghai-built version priced at $39,490 CAD — from its Canadian order page. The only Model 3 still orderable is the Performance AWD, listed at $936/month financing. Tesla's site message states orders are "paused after high demand exhausted Tesla's import quota," with resumption "expected March 2027," tied to Canada's March-to-February quota year.
2. **Pre-Premium pricing context:** Before the Model 3 Premium launched in Canada in May, the cheapest orderable Model 3 was the Fremont-built Long Range AWD at $79,990 CAD, which was subject to Canada's 25% counter-tariff on US-made vehicles; Tesla shipped its remaining US-built Model 3 inventory out of Canada in March.

## Market & Competition
1. **Canada's EV quota mechanics:** Canada cut its tariff on Chinese EVs from 100% to 6.1% in January and set a national cap of 49,000 vehicles per year, split into two six-month periods, allocated first-come-first-served per Global Affairs Canada, with no stated per-manufacturer limit.
2. **Quota tracker contradicts Tesla's claim:** Per the Export and Import Controls tracker (updated October 9), only 15,931 of 49,000 annual permits have been used since March 1, leaving 33,069 (67%) unused; the current six-month period started with 33,397 permits (24,500 base plus 8,897 carried over).
3. **Import volume collapsed in September/October:** Monthly imports of electric cars valued at or under $35,000 (the customs bracket covering the $39,490 Model 3 RWD) rose from 465 in June to 4,743 in July and 2,597 in August, then dropped to 7 in September and 0 in October as of the latest tracker update — despite two-thirds of the annual quota remaining unused.
4. **Possible explanation — per-OEM quota set-aside:** Global Affairs Canada's notice allows it to reserve "a portion of the available quota for the use of OEMs, including new entrants" for "equitable access"; the article speculates this could mean Ottawa capped Tesla's share to leave room for BYD and Geely, which (per the article) are still building out Canadian dealer networks. No company-specific allocation has been published, so the article notes this explanation is not confirmed. The article also raises a second possibility: Tesla's order backlog may already cover all permits it expects before February.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the $39,490 Model 3 generated genuine high demand in Canada despite lingering anger at Elon Musk, but calls Tesla's "exhausted quota" claim misleading given two-thirds of the national quota is unused. He argues that if Ottawa capped Tesla's allocation to preserve room for other Chinese-EV entrants, that's a defensible policy goal but should be disclosed publicly rather than left opaque. He also contends the 49,000-unit national cap is too small given Canada sells roughly 1.9 million vehicles annually, and questions whether Ottawa will raise the quota (already set to grow to 70,000 by 2030) faster than planned.
