This episode of the Tesla Beat Podcast (episode 148) from FutureAzA features host John alongside regulars Brian and Joe, plus first-time guest Randy Kirk. The discussion covers a wide range of Tesla and SpaceX topics including the Grok AI integration in Tesla vehicles, Cybertruck Smart Summon, Cybercab regulatory progress and deployment readiness, FSD expansion into new international markets, and Q2 2026 delivery and energy expectations. The broader framing is bullish on both Tesla and SpaceX, with considerable time spent on the SpaceX IPO and its terrestrial data center business as a near-term profit driver.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybertruck Smart Summon (V14 Update):** The panel celebrates the rollout of actual Smart Summon for Cybertruck via the V14 update, which they describe as one of the biggest Cybertruck updates since FSD itself was rolled out. Joe, who owns Cybertruck unit #1387, describes using it as a "chariot" at events, noting the visual impact of such a large vehicle navigating a parking lot autonomously. The host mentions a $60,000 Cybertruck variant is rolling out and expresses excitement for more owners to experience the feature.

2. **FSD in Australia:** Joe references a video released showing FSD operating in Australia — driving on the left side of the road — and describes it as performing impressively. The panel jokes that it's doing well "upside down and backwards."

3. **Grok AI Integration Roadmap:** Randy Kirk and Brian discuss the current and near-future state of Grok integration in Tesla vehicles. Brian describes wanting Grok to proactively manage parking preferences, curate playlists intelligently, and become more conversational and context-aware. He notes Tesla is rolling Grok out cautiously, citing an incident in China (in a non-Tesla vehicle) where an AI assistant turned off headlights on a highway when asked to "turn off the lights." The panel expects deeper vehicle-wide Grok integration — including controlling rear entertainment screens — as a significant upcoming catalyst.

4. **EV Tax Credit Removal — Competitive Advantage:** The host argues that the removal of EV tax credits, widely predicted to hurt Tesla, has instead created a "perfect storm" for the company. Competitors relying on those credits now face higher effective prices, while Tesla, having already optimized its cost structure, continues to move vehicles. He cites a friend who started using FSD on shorter trips and found he no longer enjoys manual driving.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Approval Rumor — Spain:** Randy Kirk shares a rumor from what he describes as a very reliable source in Spain that FSD could receive regulatory approval there by the end of the current month (June 2026). He notes the source is excellent but advises taking it with "a grain of salt." He was unable to independently confirm it fully.

2. **Sweden Blocking FSD Over Speed Limit Compliance:** Randy Kirk reports that Sweden has stated it will not approve FSD unless Tesla removes the ability for the vehicle to exceed the speed limit. The panel finds this absurd, noting that human drivers also speed and that locking FSD to the speed limit would make it effectively unusable in most real-world driving conditions, particularly in the US where 10 mph over is described as the norm. Brian notes the EU framework would make approval easier but not mandatory, and individual countries can maintain more restrictive local laws.

3. **EU Regulatory Framework:** Brian explains that EU-level FSD approval would create an easy permitting pathway but would not override local country laws. Countries could still impose additional restrictions. He draws a parallel to the US federal framework discussion, where federal approval would simplify but not automatically legalize deployment across all states.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab EPA Certificate of Conformity:** Joe reports that the Tesla Cybercab has received an EPA Certificate of Conformity, confirming it meets all applicable federal emissions requirements and can legally be introduced into US commerce. Randy clarifies that while it sounds counterintuitive for an electric vehicle with no tailpipe, the certificate is a required regulatory step before commercial deployment or fare collection — and notes that this box is now checked.

2. **Texas Deployment Readiness:** Randy states that Texas already has the permitting in place and is not subject to the federal 2,500-vehicle cap. He argues Tesla could deploy Cybercab commercially in Texas today and believes the remaining holdup is likely a software tweak. He characterizes the situation as "getting real close."

3. **Austin Cybercab Observations:** The host describes visiting Austin the prior Friday and seeing approximately 10 Cybercab units on the road within 2–3 hours, plus roughly 30 more staged behind a black fence at Tesla's robotaxi hub. He notes all current Model Y rides in Austin are unsupervised. He also mentions recent reports of accidents caused by other drivers hitting the autonomous vehicles.

4. **Active Robotaxi Fleet and City Expansion:** The host describes seeing Cybercab units appearing in Las Vegas, Florida, the Boston area, and even reportedly New Zealand, suggesting Tesla is pre-positioning vehicles across multiple cities. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active robotaxi fleet (all modes) stands at 732 as of 2026-06-20, with 605 in the Bay Area and 104 in Austin.

5. **"Four Weeks Away" Estimate:** Randy relays (per "Jeff Le," unconfirmed) that each city's robotaxi operation is like a small factory requiring setup — charging infrastructure, repair capability, fast responders, remote support staff — and that Jeff Le believes these city-level factories are "just about there," putting full commercial launch approximately four weeks away at most.

6. **Cybercab Track Testing at Giga Texas:** The host references footage from Joe Tegtmeyer showing a dedicated test track at Giga Texas that is nearly complete. The track is designed not only for high-speed testing but also includes suspension and braking challenges — bumps, slaloms — mirroring the setup at the Fremont factory.

7. **Waymo Comparison:** The host mentions seeing Waymo vehicles operating unsupervised throughout the Bay Area and expresses hope that Tesla can light up robotaxi service in California cities like Palo Alto. He also references a Waymo recall currently underway (unspecified details).

## Financials

1. **Q2 2026 Delivery Expectations:** Randy Kirk discusses upcoming Q2 2026 delivery numbers expected around July 2, 2026. He consulted Grok, which estimated a maximum of 575,000 vehicles if everything executes perfectly, with a more likely range of 475,000–525,000. Randy expresses personal belief there is a good chance Tesla gets close to or over 500,000 units, which he expects would be a positive surprise for the stock and could generate pre-announcement FOMO.

2. **Energy Deployment Expectations — Q2 2026:** Randy notes Tesla is manufacturing approximately 17 gigawatt-hours of Megapack per quarter. Last quarter came in at 8.8 GWh, implying significant "lumpiness" with roughly 8 GWh of undeployed capacity. He suggests Q2 2026 could see anywhere from 14 to 25 GWh deployed depending on timing, which he believes would be a major positive shock to the market. The range of 17 GWh (normal quarter) to ~25 GWh (with carryover) is cited as the realistic band.

3. **SpaceX Stock Mark-to-Market Impact on Tesla:** Randy raises that Tesla holds SpaceX stock and, now that SpaceX is public, that position must be marked to market each quarter. He estimates this could contribute approximately $2 billion in profit — roughly $0.75 per share — to Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings. He notes he believes he read this figure in a major financial publication (he thinks Fortune) the prior day, though he is not certain of the outlet.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO and Valuation:** The panel discusses the SpaceX IPO extensively. The stock IPO'd at $135, and at the time of recording had reached approximately $185, with an intraday high the panel estimates around $200–$210 — representing roughly a 60% gain from IPO price. Tesla's stock is noted at approximately $400 at time of recording. Randy cites analysis from Brian Wong projecting SpaceX at approximately $40 billion in revenue in the current year (2026), growing to approximately $80 billion in 2027, with roughly $35 billion in profit driven largely by data center rental income (including a reported ~$1 billion/month from Anthropic). He argues this shifts SpaceX's valuation multiple from ~95x sales to ~40x sales with real earnings.

2. **SpaceX Terrestrial Data Centers as Near-Term Driver:** Randy argues that SpaceX's ability to build and light up data centers faster than any competitor — in roughly one year versus the three-to-four years typical for others — is the key near-term value driver. He cites a rumor (attributed to Brian Wong and Cern Basher, per Randy) that the Macrohard building may be converted into a fourth data center, with Cern Basher reportedly identifying the energy supply source. Randy estimates SpaceX could add two new data centers in 2027 and two to three more in 2028.

3. **Space-Based Data Centers — Longer Timeline:** Randy tempers enthusiasm for orbital data centers, personally estimating significant revenue from that segment is unlikely before 2031, even as Elon Musk has suggested 2029. He cites outstanding uncertainties including the lack of an orbital Starship trip and no successful ship landing to date.

4. **Nvidia Chip Allocation Claim:** Randy relays a claim attributed to "Peter the Amandas" via the Moonshots podcast (unconfirmed) that Elon Musk/xAI has placed an order representing 22% of Nvidia's total output capacity. The panel treats this as credible but unverified, noting it aligns with xAI's aggressive GPU cluster buildout strategy and the Terafab chip manufacturing ambitions.

5. **US Government Visit to Tesla Fremont:** Joe reports that US Trade Representative Ambassador Greer toured Tesla's Fremont manufacturing facility, viewing the production line and Optimus. The Texas Department of Transportation's executive director also visited to observe the Cybercab. The panel frames these visits as meaningful government recognition of Tesla as an American manufacturing leader — a shift from the prior administration's posture.
