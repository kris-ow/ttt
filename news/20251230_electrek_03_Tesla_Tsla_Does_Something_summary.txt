Tesla published its internal, company-compiled Q4 2025 delivery consensus publicly via its investor relations website—a departure from its usual practice of sharing this data privately with select analysts—which the author interprets as an attempt to lower expectations ahead of official delivery results expected in early January.

## Financials
1. **Tesla's self-published consensus is lower than Wall Street's:** Tesla's company-compiled consensus pegs median Q4 2025 delivery expectations at 420,399 vehicles (mean 422,850), notably below Bloomberg's broader public consensus of roughly 440,000 units. The author speculates this gap could let a result like 425k be framed as a "beat" relative to Tesla's own anchored figure, even though it would fall short of prior street expectations.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Second consecutive year of delivery declines:** If Tesla hits the ~420,000 median Q4 target, full-year 2025 deliveries would total approximately 1.64 million vehicles, down roughly 8% year-over-year from 2024's 1.79 million (which itself was down about 1% from 2023's peak of 1.81 million).
2. **Sharp sequential drop after tax-credit expiration:** Q4 was expected to be weak following the expiration of the U.S. federal EV tax credit at the end of Q3, which likely pulled demand forward into Q3's record ~497,000 deliveries. The article notes a sequential drop of over 75,000 units from Q3 to the projected Q4 figure is steeper than many bulls anticipated.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the public release of the consensus "a very defensive move" and unusual for Tesla, arguing that even if the company hits its own lowered target, 420,000 deliveries would be poor performance and confirm a clear decline trend—especially set against a global EV market the article says is expected to grow about 25% year-over-year in 2025. He frames back-to-back annual delivery declines as a "massive reality check" for a company once priced for 50% annual growth.
