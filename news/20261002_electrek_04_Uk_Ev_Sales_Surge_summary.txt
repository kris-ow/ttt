UK battery-electric car registrations hit a record 99,199 units in September 2026, up 36.3% year-over-year and 28.3% of the market, as the overall UK car market (350,518 units) grew 12.1% in its best September since 2017, according to SMMT data.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla leads UK EV sales in September:** The Model 3 was the UK's best-selling EV with 9,929 units (also the #2 car overall regardless of powertrain), and the Model Y added 5,946 units for fifth place overall. Together the two models made up 15,875 units, or 16% of all EVs registered. Tesla registered 12,403 cars in the UK in June (its prior end-of-quarter push), and 42% of the Model 3's 23,704 UK registrations this year came in September, reflecting Tesla's pattern of loading deliveries into the final month of each quarter.
2. **Chinese and Korean brands fill out the EV top sellers:** BYD took the next two EV spots with the Sealion 7 (3,191 units) and Seal (3,184 units), ahead of the Kia EV3 (3,026) and Leapmotor's B10 (1,883, making the top 10). The article notes BYD is selling well despite not being eligible for the UK's Electric Car Grant when it launched the prior year. Separately, the Jaecoo 7 (a Chery SUV sold with petrol and plug-in hybrid powertrains) was the UK's best-selling car overall in September with 10,813 units, now second for the year behind the Ford Puma.
3. **Plug-in hybrids and overall plug-in share:** Plug-in hybrids jumped 55.7% year-over-year to a record 59,563 units and 17% share. Combined, battery-electric and plug-in hybrid cars totaled 158,762 units, or 45.3% of the market, while petrol cars fell to 131,861 units and a 37.6% share (down from 45.2% a year earlier). Private buyer registrations rose 13.9%, outpacing fleet growth of 9.6%.

## Market & Competition
1. **Year-to-date EV share and 2026 mandate target:** Through nine months of 2026, the UK registered 454,945 EVs (up 30.2%), a 26.2% share of 1,739,253 total new cars. The UK's ZEV mandate sets a 33% EV target for 2026 (up from 28% in 2025). SMMT said hitting it would require another 265,000 EV registrations in Q4, based on its full-year forecast of 2.183 million cars; the article's own math implies this would require roughly 60% EV share for three straight months, which it says is not realistic. The mandate allows automakers to meet the target via credits for CO2 cuts on non-electric cars, borrowing from future years, and buying credits from other automakers — New AutoMotive puts the "real" effective 2026 target at 24.6% once these flexibilities are counted, a figure the current 26.2% share already exceeds.
2. **SMMT's policy stance:** SMMT chief executive Mike Hawes attributed the record to greater model choice and high fuel prices, while also stating that "the UK still has the world's toughest targets and highest energy costs," and the SMMT is pushing for the mandate to be loosened to better reflect what it characterizes as actual demand.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues the mandate is working as intended — citing 178 electric models now on sale in the UK (more than double the 2023 count, and roughly twice the US's selection) — and is skeptical of the SMMT's push to weaken targets despite record sales, noting the mandate's existing flexibilities already prevent fines over the gap to 33%. The author credits Hawes's point about high UK energy costs as a separate, legitimate issue from the target level itself, and flags plug-in hybrids' faster growth as a trend to watch, warning that increased PHEV credit in a policy review could slow EV growth. The piece also contrasts the UK's competitive, multi-brand top-10 (including Tesla, BYD, Kia, Skoda, Volvo, Mercedes, Leapmotor) and 28% EV share against the US's roughly 8% EV share.
