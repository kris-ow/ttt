## Brief

- Samsung confirms AI5 chip tape-out on 2nm at Taylor fab
- AI5 in vehicles pushed to ~2028, AI4 Plus fills gap mid-2027
- Tesla says wheelchair-accessible AV in development in Texas
- Optimus factory steel rising, ~10M units/year capacity cited
- FSD 14.3.5 early access rolling out, parking still inconsistent
- TSLA closed $394.76, down 3.19% on the day

## Autonomous Driving
- Samsung Foundry engineer James Kim (in a since-deleted LinkedIn post) confirmed Tesla's AI5 chip has taped out for production on Samsung's 2nm process at the Taylor, Texas fab — a node previously assumed reserved for AI6. Samsung expects volume production of Tesla's AI chips at Taylor in H2 2027; Electrek's Fred Lambert cautions tape-out is a milestone, not a finish line, noting AI5 arrives nearly two years after Musk first said it would be in vehicles.
- Per Electrified, Musk's April comments suggest AI5 is really targeted at Optimus and Tesla's compute clusters, with an interim "AI4 Plus" (32GB RAM/SoC, ~10% more compute) slated for production around mid-2027 and AI5 in cars not expected until roughly 2028.
- FSD 14.3.5 hit early access overnight; testers report inconsistent parking (including one handicapped-spot miss) alongside cleaner attempts from other users, a more relaxed but shorter-fused driver-monitoring nag, and manual blinker override still not working for turns.
- A widely shared clip showed FSD 14.2.2.5 at 53 mph braking late on a dog in the road (dog survived); the host argued FSD likely reduced severity versus an average human driver.
- Tesla began rolling out an FSD "light" version for Hardware 3 vehicles to early-access owners in Korea, ahead of a similar US/Canada tier rollout.
- Unverified rumors point to a nearing German FSD approval (dealer chatter, software pre-staged on some cars), though pending KBA sign-off.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla policy advisor India Herdman told DC Council members Tesla is building a purpose-built, wheelchair-accessible autonomous vehicle in Texas, per WIRED — no timeline, name, or specs given; Electrek's Fred Lambert is skeptical this is more than the unlaunched Robovan/Robobus.
- Cybercab sightings expanded: ~100 units reported in Houston, testing observed in Miami with a safety operator, and 8 Cybercabs parked visibly at a Peabody, MA Tesla store, all still unbadged.
- A Tesla-focused analyst (on brighterwithherbert) argues broader unsupervised Cybercab rides could begin within roughly two-to-six weeks, with production scaling from hundreds/month toward thousands/week by end of Q3 and a hoped-for terminal unit cost of $17,500–$19,000+.
- New Jersey and DC regulatory fights continue: a proposed NJ bill would effectively mandate LiDAR-style redundancy (excluding Tesla's vision-only approach), while an Uber lobbyist is separately pushing NJ to require human drivers for 85% of driverless rides.
- Waymo announced expansion to Denver, Las Vegas, San Diego, Santiago, and Tampa; multiple hosts argue Waymo's per-city fleet concentration model can't match Tesla's scalable retail-fleet economics.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's Optimus factory near Giga Texas is under active steel construction, with commentary citing a stated 10-million-unit annual capacity — roughly Giga Texas–sized.
- Rival Neo showed a hand demo (25 degrees of freedom, 8 m/s finger speed, IP68-rated) handling glasses, light bulbs, and Legos, but is targeting only 10,000 hands/year (~5,000 robots); Figure also shared a progress update.
- Peter Diamandis said China ships ~90% of the world's humanoid robots; Musk replied "For now."
- Analyst estimates (via Lars Moravy remarks) suggest low-volume Optimus production could begin roughly a month after end of summer, on a high-throughput "unboxed" Fremont line, with meaningful revenue not expected until 2028.
- Bearish counterpoint: robotics-industry watcher Scott Walter argued no company, including Tesla, has a humanoid prototype robust enough to scale to hundreds of thousands of units, predicting the "million-plus" Optimus version is a later, unrevealed generation. Ashok Elluswamy countered he's confident Optimus "will not disappoint."

## Electric Vehicles
- California's new EV rebate ($3,500 new/$1,750 used) caps eligibility at $50,000 MSRP but exempts California-headquartered Rivian and Lucid — a cap that applies to Tesla since its HQ move to Austin. Governor Newsom also said Musk has "put the brakes on his own innovation" in EVs.
- Tesla Semi gained another prospective fleet: Wisconsin carrier Paper Transport (~800 trucks) is piloting the Long Range Semi for dedicated Chicago routes; Electrek notes charging infrastructure, not demand, is now the bottleneck (66 Megacharger sites across 15 states).
- China's H1 auto market fell 20% y/y overall (BYD -46%, VW -29%, Toyota -17%); Tesla's China retail sales fell a comparatively modest 9.3%, among the best of the top 20 brands.

## Market & Competition
- Samsung's Taylor fab win on AI5 is framed as a needed proof point for its 2nm yield and customer base, with reports Anthropic could also manufacture chips there.
- China EV market attrition: one analyst claims roughly 80 Chinese EV makers launched, 60+ have since failed, versus Tesla's profitability with fewer than five models.
