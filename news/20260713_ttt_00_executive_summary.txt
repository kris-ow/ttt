## Brief

- Samsung confirms AI5 chip tape-out on 2nm at Taylor fab
- Tesla tells DC Council it's building wheelchair-accessible robotaxi in Texas
- Paper Transport starts Tesla Semi Chicago pilot
- California EV rebate caps $50K MSRP, disadvantages Tesla vs Rivian/Lucid
- TSLA closed $394.76, down 3.19% on the day
- FSD 14.3.5 early access rolling out, parking still inconsistent

## Autonomous Driving
- Samsung Foundry engineer James Kim's (since-deleted) LinkedIn post confirmed Tesla's AI5 chip reached tape-out for production on Samsung's 2nm process at the Taylor, Texas fab — a node previously assumed reserved for AI6. Electrek frames this as a manufacturing-readiness milestone, not proof AI5 lands in vehicles soon; Tesla still needs several hundred thousand completed AI5 boards line-side before switching production, a volume Musk has said isn't expected until mid-2027, with an interim "AI4 Plus" upgrade (doubled RAM, ~10% more compute) targeted for production around mid-2027 instead. AI6 mass production has reportedly slipped to Q4 2027 at the earliest.
- FSD 14.3.5 early access feedback is mixed: parking remains inconsistent (one tester saw repeated poor attempts including one handicapped-spot entry; another had five clean attempts), manual blinker override still doesn't work for turns, and driver-monitoring nags feel more relaxed but trigger with a shorter fuse once activated.
- A widely shared clip showed FSD 14.2.2.5 at 53 mph failing to brake in time for a dog in the road (unable to swerve due to oncoming traffic); the dog was hit but survived.
- Tesla began rolling out FSD "light" for Hardware 3 vehicles to early-access owners in Korea, ahead of a planned US/Canada rollout for the same hardware tier.
- Unverified rumors suggest German FSD approval may be near, with some service centers reportedly prepping for customer test drives; flagged as speculative.
- New Jersey bill would require a "redundant safety system" for autonomous vehicles, interpreted as effectively mandating LiDAR and excluding Tesla's vision-only approach; Tesla has launched an outreach page urging residents to contact legislators.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla policy advisor India Herdman told DC Council members Tesla is developing a purpose-built, wheelchair-accessible autonomous vehicle "active" in Texas, per WIRED. Electrek's editorial stance is skeptical, noting no timeline, name, or specs were given, and suspects this may describe the previously unveiled Robovan rather than a new product.
- Cybercab presence continues expanding pre-badging: sightings include roughly 100 in Houston and testing (with a safety operator) in Miami; 8 Cybercabs were also spotted staged visibly at a Tesla store in Peabody, Massachusetts.
- Anecdotal wait times improving: longest Dallas wait was 7 minutes over a weekend, Miami saw a 4-minute wait — attributed mainly to low demand/awareness rather than large fleet size.
- An Uber lobbyist is pushing New Jersey to require driverless ride-hailing platforms to use human drivers for 85% of rides, language that would effectively block standalone robotaxi apps from Waymo, Zoox, and Tesla if adopted.
- Waymo announced testing expansion to Denver, Las Vegas, San Diego, Tampa, and Santiago; one host called Denver the only genuinely new market of note.
- One analyst's view: Tesla is roughly two-to-six weeks from broader unsupervised Cybercab rides, projecting production scaling from hundreds/month currently to thousands/week by end of Q3 and multiples higher by year-end, with a hoped-for terminal unit cost near $17,500–$19,000+.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's Optimus factory near Giga Texas (steel erection underway, capacity reportedly designed for up to 10 million robots/year) continues construction, with hosts framing manufacturing scale as Tesla's key edge over rivals like Neo and Figure.
- Neo demoed a dexterous robot hand (25 degrees of freedom, IP68-rated, up to 8 m/s finger speed) but is targeting only ~10,000 hands/year (~5,000 robots) — far below Tesla's ambitions.
- Bearish counterpoint: robotics-space commentator Scott Walter argued no company, including Tesla, currently has a humanoid prototype robust enough to scale to hundreds of thousands or millions of units, and predicted a million-plus Optimus version is a later generation, not revealed this year.
- Ashok Elluswamy said Optimus has "big shoes to fill" but he's confident it "will not disappoint," referencing Tesla's Fremont line as possibly the largest manufacturing line outside Shanghai.
- One analyst, citing Lars Moravy's remarks, estimated Tesla could reach low-volume Optimus production roughly a month after end of summer, with meaningful revenue not expected until 2028.

## Electric Vehicles
- California Governor Newsom signed a new EV rebate ($3,500 new/$1,750 used) capped at $50,000 MSRP, with an exemption for California-headquartered automakers (Rivian, Lucid) that doesn't apply to Austin-based Tesla. Newsom also said Musk has "put the brakes on his own innovation" in EVs to focus on robotics.
- China's domestic EV market fell 20% year-over-year in H1 (BYD -46%, VW -29%, Toyota -17%, Geely -25%); Tesla's China sales fell 9.3% over the same period — among the best performances of the top 20 brands.

## Market & Competition
- Paper Transport (PTI), a ~800-truck dedicated carrier, is evaluating the Tesla Semi Long Range for Chicago-market routes; a host speculated a future order of 40–60 units if the pilot succeeds. Tesla Semi accounted for 965 of 1,067 California Clean Truck voucher applications from Jan 2025–Feb 2026, versus fewer than 100 combined for Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas argued SpaceX's terrestrial data-center compute business is underappreciated and that SpaceX currently trades cheaper than Tesla, projecting SpaceX EBITDA growing from ~$20B (H2 2026) to a ~$50B run rate within two years.
