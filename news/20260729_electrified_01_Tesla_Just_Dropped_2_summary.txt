This Electrified episode covers two new Tesla power purchase agreements signaling major future electricity demand growth, alongside updates on robotaxi expansion, a Cybertruck supplier lawsuit, EU FSD safety data, and a whistleblower lawsuit tied to Houston FSD testing.

## Energy
1. **Tesla–Zelestra Texas solar PPA:** Tesla agreed to purchase the entire output of Zelestra's 140 MW AC Lumen solar farm in northeast Texas, expected to begin construction in 2027 and become operational in 2029. The host explains this is a financial hedge (via Texas's ERCOT wholesale market) rather than physical electron delivery, structured so Tesla locks in a fixed price and settles the difference with the wholesale rate, while also collecting renewable energy credits. The host ties rising demand to Cortex 1/2, Cybercab production, Optimus production, the Optimus Academy factory, robotaxi hubs/charging, and Tesla's Megapack factory in Brookshire, Texas.
2. **Tesla–ContourGlobal Arizona hybrid PPA:** Tesla signed a long-term PPA covering roughly 1 terawatt-hour per year from ContourGlobal's Sterling Renewable Project, a hybrid solar-plus-storage plant in Arizona with over 1.4 GWh of battery storage and 450 MW AC of solar — described as one of the largest single-plant solar-plus-storage corporate PPAs in US history. Construction started last year, groundbreaking is planned this year, and completion is expected in 2028; the deal covers about 90% of the project's annual output and includes firm transmission rights into California's CAISO market via a "pseudo-tie" arrangement. The host notes no public confirmation on whether Tesla will supply the solar panels or Megapacks for the project, though he considers the latter more likely.
3. **Scale context and future compute needs:** The host estimates the Arizona deal's scale is roughly equivalent to the annual electricity use of 30,000 EVs, and speculates the accumulating power needs point toward a possible future data center announcement (e.g., a "Cortex 3"), noting Elon Musk has said Optimus training could require roughly 10x the compute capacity needed for FSD.

## Robotaxi
1. **Houston FSD testing whistleblower lawsuit:** A former Tesla employee, Javier Madrono, who oversaw FSD testing (not robotaxi remote operation) in Houston prior to launch, is suing Tesla after being let go. He claims Tesla's internal target was a 15:1 ratio of test vehicles to supervisors, but he was responsible for 38 vehicles alone across 24-hour shifts starting around June of a prior year, and alleges sleep deprivation led him to give unsafe guidance during one incident. He is seeking reinstatement, back pay, and damages; a pre-trial conference is scheduled for November 19. The host frames this as evidence Tesla tested extensively (roughly two years) before Houston's robotaxi launch, and cautions that visible testing in a new city doesn't mean an imminent launch.
2. **Fleet and operating hours updates:** Active Robotaxi fleet: 809 (Bay Area 652, Austin 116, Dallas 21, Houston 20) and Unsupervised Robotaxi fleet: 46 (Austin 32, Dallas 6, Houston 6, Tampa 2), per RobotaxiTracker.com. Separately, a rider reported booking a ride after 8:00 p.m. in Houston for the first time (service still ended at 8:30 p.m.), and a third-party tracker using license-plate tracking estimated around 55 unsupervised robotaxis in Austin — which the host flags as an independent estimate he can't verify, noting his own prior estimate of 30 may have been low.
3. **Operational complexity as the real bottleneck:** The host argues vehicle count is the wrong metric; unsupervised miles per week matter more, and Tesla must build extensive infrastructure (charging, cleaning, maintenance, fleet positioning, dead-head-mile reduction, regulatory work per city) before scaling further.
4. **Charlotte and nationwide testing:** Axios Charlotte reported 14 Cybercabs and 14 Model Ys with Texas plates spotted in a Charlotte-area lot; the host speculates Tesla could test there for 6–9 months without necessarily launching until next year, and estimates (his own guess) roughly 1,500 Cybercabs deployed nationally, including winter testing in New Zealand.
5. **Waymo–Uber partnership strain and competitive context:** Per a cited Ark Invest comparison, Waymo took five years to reach five cities after its 2020 Phoenix launch, versus Tesla reaching seven cities roughly one year after its Austin launch — a comparison the host calls not a great standard but useful context. He also relays reports that Waymo is considering ending its Uber partnership over operational/commercial disputes (cleanliness, routing, vehicle availability, financial terms), noting the partnership already ended in Phoenix and remains active for now in Austin and Atlanta; the host questions what this implies for Rivian and Uber's planned 50,000-vehicle robotaxi deployment.
6. **Minor UX update:** Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi vehicles now have silent turn signals and hazard lights, per the robotaxi account.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **EU supervised FSD safety data:** Tesla reported that over the past four months, supervised FSD was 5.2 times safer than manual driving across roughly 65 million km (about 40 million miles) in five EU countries — about 10 million miles per month. Of these, roughly 26 million miles were highway (zero collisions) and 14 million were non-highway (six collisions, with no fault/detail disclosed).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y demand in Canada:** Online configurator delivery dates are showing late 2026 into 2027, driven partly by Model Y shipments from Giga Berlin and partly by Model Y sales in Canada up nearly 80% in the first half of the year (off a low base due to prior-year tariffs).
2. **FSD subscription growth:** Tesla added around 200,000 FSD subscriptions in Q2 alone; the host is uncertain whether this growth rate is sustainable for a full year but views it as encouraging ahead of broader EU FSD approval.
3. **Cybertruck supplier dispute:** Tesla is in a legal battle with Angstrom Automotive Group, which intends to close a Troy, Texas manufacturing site producing Cybertruck and Model Y parts. Angstrom has refused to ship roughly 700 finished parts (diecast machinery, trim dies, fixtures, tooling) that Tesla says no other supplier can currently produce. Tesla is seeking emergency court access to retrieve its tooling; a Tesla executive said on-hand inventory could run out within days, and Angstrom is reportedly demanding $250,000/week beyond existing purchase orders to keep the facility running. The host notes Angstrom recently emerged from a six-year court fight resulting in a $30 million judgment and a subsidiary's Chapter 11 filing, suggesting possible financial distress on Angstrom's side.
