## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- WSJ reported Tesla advisers have discussed spinning off, selling, or winding down Tesla's China business to remove a national-security obstacle to a Tesla-SpaceX merger, per a person familiar with the matter; Tesla and SpaceX did not respond to requests for comment. Musk called the report "absurdly fake news" and said it has never come up in discussions, per Electrek's account of his denial.
- Commentators (brighterwithherbert panel) read the WSJ story as a deliberate pre-merger disclosure rather than a signal of an imminent sale, projecting a possible merger announcement around the week of August 9th tied to SpaceX's first investor lockup window; estimated timeline from announcement to consummation is 9–12 months. This remains speculative and unconfirmed.
- On Tesla's Q2 call, Musk cited "more and more overlap, especially with Terafab" between his companies but said combining them "has got to be done with the appropriate process," deferring to Tesla general counsel Brandon Ehrhart; per Electrek, Tesla avoided shareholder questions on a potential merger.
- SpaceX's first public-company earnings call is set for August 4 at 4:30pm ET, using a custom Grok-built Q&A platform instead of SAY; the first major post-IPO share lockup unlocks August 6, two days later.
- SpaceX's IPO filings disclosed no current general counsel, per Merz — flagged as unusual timing given merger-related legal questions ahead of the earnings call.

## Robotaxi
- Lars Moravy, Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering, told Sawyer Merritt in person that Cybercab and Tesla's robotaxi program are "NOT limited by any type of vehicle cap."
- Community trackers reported driverless Cybercabs — no occupant — operating on public Austin roads beyond the Giga Texas facility, viewed by commentators as more significant than incremental fleet-count growth.
- Tesla's daily active robotaxi fleet in Austin was around 30 per a clarification one host received, distinct from higher total fleet counts reported by trackers; the host argues operational/software readiness, not regulation, is the current bottleneck.
- NHTSA fast-tracked AV deregulation, including a Part 555 exemption letting Amazon's Zoox commercially deploy up to 2,500 steering-wheel-free robotaxis annually for two years — a first for the industry. Tesla's Cybercab is largely unaffected since it's already self-certified to FMVSS standards, so the change benefits competitors more directly.
- Riders reported operational hiccups: a Houston rider was told to exit and cancel a ride after boarding; a Dallas robotaxi got stuck in a turnaround loop for 3–5 minutes on a "poor connectivity" message before support intervened.
- Bhakdi says he's become "tactically less bullish," now expecting his prior 15,000-robotaxi-by-year-end target to slip roughly three months into Q1, though he says it could still land this year with lower confidence.
- Giga Texas has three major facilities under simultaneous construction, including an expanding Cybercab test fleet and test track (now with cobblestone sections) and a multi-bay Tesla Semi megacharger.

## Humanoid Bots
- Drone footage shows steel structure rising for an Optimus factory at Giga Texas sized for roughly 1 million units of annual capacity, only slightly smaller than the main vehicle plant.

## Autonomous Driving
- A proposed New Jersey bill would require a "redundant safety system" for AV motor vehicle control, which one commentator argues would effectively mandate lidar-style redundancy and disqualify Tesla's vision-only system; a state senator questioned whether single-sensor software can match human capability. Tesla added a website tool for users to contact NJ legislators, citing state data of 578 traffic deaths and 94 serious crashes attributed to human error.
- NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation of roughly 1.2 million 2018–2020 Model 3 and 2021–2023 Model Y vehicles after 156 complaints of front lower lateral link separation causing loss of steering control, sometimes without warning; NHTSA said it's not aware of resulting crashes or injuries and that this is distinct from prior related recalls (2,800 Model 3 units in 2021, 422 in 2023).
- A user reported an FSD crash into a pole in a parking garage on the fourth day of ownership.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla announced its 10 millionth vehicle produced — a Diamond Black Model Y built at Fremont — alongside the 10-year anniversary of Giga Nevada.
- New Cybertrucks are shipping with carbon fiber underbody panels, described by a Tesla-affiliated source as lighter, cheaper, more durable than aluminum, and slightly more efficient; retrofit availability for existing trucks is unknown.
- Tesla extended Cybertruck PCS warranty to 7 years/70,000 miles (2026 vehicles) and 8 years/150,000 miles (2024–2025 vehicles) following complaints of charging-limiting PCS failures, will reimburse prior out-of-pocket repairs, and pushed a software update to preserve Supercharging despite PCS failure.
- A dispute with tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group (via acquired Anderton Casting) over custom Cybertruck die-casting tooling is reportedly headed to court; Tesla says replacement tooling would take five to six months while component inventory dwindles.
- New Model Y L hands-on impressions highlight improved rear visibility, new 20" "Uberhelix" wheels, and a redesigned climate UI; the test unit ran unreleased FSD build 14.3.7.

## Energy
- Tesla confirmed three solar PPAs with Concord New Energy totaling 469 MW AC, expected to supply ~1,200 GWh/year to the Supercharger network starting H1 2027, alongside a separately reported Arizona solar-plus-battery PPA (~458 MW).
- Tesla's first Megapacks using US-made LFP cells (Sparks, Nevada) began shipping, qualifying for a $35/kWh domestic manufacturing tax credit estimated at roughly $245 million/year, though likely already priced into Tesla's guided 20–25% energy gross margins.
- Tesla posted a new "Megapod" hardware lead role tied to AI data center hardware.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Existing SpaceX compute lease deals total roughly $28B/year already under contract: Anthropic renting all of Colossus 1 (~$15B/year), Google renting part of Colossus 2 (~$11B/year), and Reflection renting part of Colossus 2 (~$2B/year); Colossus 1 is fully leased, Colossus 2 about a third leased.
- Musk described a new, denser "Mini Hard" data center design using the same 220,000 GB300 chips and 800-gig NICs as "Macro Hard" but in roughly 25% of the floor space.
- A third-party report (relayed via investor Gavin Baker, unconfirmed) claims SpaceX AI has ~8 GW of demand-side compute lined up (~4 GW this year, ~4 GW next), potentially ~$100B in annual revenue; Bhakdi disputes this as unrealistic, estimating closer to ~2 GW deployed this year.
- SpaceX said it will remove unpermitted turbines powering Memphis-area xAI data centers, transitioning to a permanent 1.2 GW natural gas plant by July 2027; it currently runs 69 gas turbines at Colossus.
- SpaceX/xAI's agreed acquisition of Cursor (>$4B ARR, ~2.1M paying seats, reportedly already profitable) has not yet closed; close expected late August or September.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- China achieved its first successful controlled recovery of an orbital-class booster (Long March 10B, caught by a ship-based net system) on July 10, 2026, following two earlier failed attempts by Landspace and Long March 12A. Analysts note the booster hasn't flown a second time and SpaceX's lead — ~600 Falcon landings, 165 orbital missions in 2025 (over half of global launches that year), some boosters flown 30+ times — remains measured in years of scale and refurbishment data, not just one landing.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- A Starlink terminal factory near Bastrop, Texas is producing roughly 170,000 terminals/week (~8.5 million/year) and has doubled in size since a prior visit.
- Futurist Brian Wang outlined a speculative plan (he says referenced by Musk on a recent earnings call) to use Starlink-equipped Cybercabs, superchargers, Powerwalls, and Optimus units as small-cell mesh nodes, potentially 10–20x cheaper than traditional cell tower buildouts — framed as an unconfirmed, forward-looking thesis rather than an announced product.
