## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- WSJ reported Tesla advisers have discussed spinning off, selling, or winding down Tesla's China business to remove a national-security obstacle to a Tesla-SpaceX merger, given SpaceX's status as a major US defense contractor; Tesla and SpaceX did not comment. Musk called the report "absurdly fake news" and said it never came up in discussions, per Electrified; commentators remain split on how seriously to weigh the report versus the denial.
- Giga Shanghai built its 4 millionth China-built car in December and accounts for over half of Tesla's global deliveries, per Electrek, underscoring the stakes of any separation.
- SpaceX will hold its first public-company earnings call August 4 at 4:30pm ET, using a custom Grok-built Q&A platform instead of third-party tools like SAY.
- SpaceX's first post-IPO share lockup tranche unlocks August 6; commentators disagree on how much forced selling this creates, with some noting most early-investor shares remain locked well beyond this tranche and others flagging mechanical selling pressure as the float roughly doubles through December.
- SpaceX's IPO filings disclosed no current general counsel following predecessor David Harris's 2025 retirement, per one commentator.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla VP Lars Moravy told Sawyer Merritt directly that Cybercab and Tesla robotaxi are "NOT limited by any type of vehicle cap."
- Community-tracked sightings show fully driverless Cybercabs (no occupant) operating on Austin public roads beyond Giga Texas, viewed by commentators as more significant than incremental fleet-count updates.
- Tesla's daily active robotaxi fleet in Austin is around 30, according to a clarification one host received — distinct from higher total fleet figures shown by trackers — suggesting operational/software readiness, not regulation, is the current scaling bottleneck.
- Users reported service hiccups: a Houston rider was told to exit and cancel a ride after boarding, and a Dallas robotaxi got stuck in a turnaround loop for 3-5 minutes citing "poor connectivity."
- NHTSA granted Amazon's Zoox a temporary exemption to commercially deploy up to 2,500 steering-wheel-free robotaxis annually for two years — a competitive first that doesn't affect Tesla, whose Cybercab is already self-certified to FMVSS standards.
- A map circulating today (per Grace K) shows driverless deployment in four cities (three in Texas plus Miami), with Tampa/Orlando pending, planned expansion to Phoenix, Las Vegas and Reno, supervised operation in San Francisco, and test-driver hiring elsewhere.

## Autonomous Driving
- A proposed New Jersey bill would require a "redundant safety system" for AV control, which critics argue effectively mandates lidar and would disqualify Tesla's vision-only stack; Tesla has added a website tool for users to contact NJ legislators, citing 578 statewide traffic deaths last year.
- NHTSA's broader deregulatory push (new AV performance-standard work, modernized exemption process) mostly bypasses Tesla since Cybercab is already self-certified; a federal framework for actual robotaxi operations (e.g., the SELF DRIVE Act) remains stalled, leaving state-by-state rules as the real constraint.
- A user reported an FSD crash into a parking-garage pole on the fourth day of ownership.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla announced its 10 millionth vehicle produced — a Diamond Black Model Y built at Fremont — alongside Giga Nevada's 10-year anniversary.
- New Cybertrucks are shipping with carbon fiber underbody panels, described as more durable, lighter, cheaper and slightly more efficient than the prior aluminum design.
- Tesla extended Cybertruck PCS warranty coverage (7yr/70k miles for 2026 models, 8yr/150k for 2024-25 models) after failures limited AC charging, and will reimburse prior out-of-pocket repairs and offer free Supercharging to affected owners.
- A dispute with tooling supplier Angstrom Automotive Group (via acquired Anderton Casting) over withheld die-casting tooling is reportedly headed to court; Tesla says replacement would take five to six months while component inventory dwindles.
- NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation into roughly 1.2 million 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y vehicles after 156 complaints of front lower lateral link (suspension) separation causing loss of steering control; NHTSA says it's aware of no crashes or injuries and that this is distinct from prior related recalls (2,800 Model 3 in 2021, 422 in 2023).
- Hands-on impressions of the new Model Y L highlight improved rear visibility, new 20" "Uberhelix" wheels, and a redesigned climate UI; the test unit ran unreleased FSD build 14.3.7.

## Financials / Market & Competition
- TSLA closed at $311.21 (+0.76%); private SpaceX shares closed at $108.37 (-3.41%).
- Commentators attribute Tesla's July decline (from roughly $420 to ~$308-310) largely to a broad momentum-stock selloff rather than Tesla-specific news, though one panel notes Tesla fell ~25% over the trailing month versus flat Nvidia and Google down ~5%, arguing the market specifically punished Tesla's guidance to spend beyond free cash flow without clear robotaxi-revenue linkage.
- Institutional ownership of Tesla is now at its highest level ever (over 2 billion of ~3.95 billion shares outstanding, excluding Musk's ~1.1 billion), while retail ownership has fallen to roughly 20% from over 50% previously, per data cited by one commentator.
- One investor (Bhakdi) said he rebuilt his Tesla position back to a full core holding near $310 after selling around $366 pre-earnings, betting on a broader AI-trade recovery rather than a Tesla-specific catalyst; he also pushed back his robotaxi deployment expectation (15,000 units) from year-end to roughly Q1, a three-month delay.
- China achieved its first successful controlled recovery of an orbital rocket booster (Long March 10B, caught by ship-based net, July 10) — a milestone commentary frames as years behind SpaceX's scale (~600 Falcon landings, 165 orbital missions in 2025) but a signal of long-term competitive risk given China's manufacturing scale.

## Energy
- Tesla confirmed three solar PPAs with Concord New Energy totaling 469 MW AC, expected to supply ~1,200 GWh/year to the Supercharger network starting H1 2027, alongside a separate ~458 MW Arizona solar-plus-battery PPA.
- Tesla began shipping its first Megapacks using US-made LFP cells (Sparks, Nevada), qualifying for a $35/kWh domestic manufacturing tax credit estimated at roughly $245 million annually, though likely already reflected in guided 20-25% gross energy margins.
- Tesla is hiring for a new "Megapod" hardware lead role tied to AI data-center hardware.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX compute lease deals already total roughly $28B/year under contract (Anthropic ~$15B, Google ~$11B, Reflection ~$2B); combined future sites (Colossus 1&2, Macro Hard, Mini Hard) could reach ~1.2 million GPUs, hypothetically worth ~$100B/year if fully leased — against SpaceX's total 2025 revenue of ~$18B.
- Musk described a new, denser "Mini Hard" data center design using the same chip count as "Macro Hard" in ~25% of the floor space.
- A third-party report (via investor Gavin Baker) claims SpaceX AI has ~8 GW of demand-side compute locked in (~3M GPUs); Bhakdi called this unrealistic, estimating ~2 GW deployed this year as more plausible.
- SpaceX said it will remove unpermitted turbines powering Memphis-area xAI data centers, transitioning to a 1.2 GW natural gas plant by July 2027; it currently runs 69 gas turbines at Colossus.
- Cursor (developing its own LLM, "Vega") is being acquired by SpaceX, with closing expected late August/September; Cursor is cited as profitable at over $4B ARR.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- China's Long March 10B achieved the country's first successful ship-based net recovery of an orbital booster on July 10, though it has not yet reflown; SpaceX's scale advantage (~600 landings, over half of 2025's global orbital launches) remains commentary's key reference point.
