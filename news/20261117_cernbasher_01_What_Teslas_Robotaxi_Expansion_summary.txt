This panel discussion (hosted on the cernbasher channel) covers Tesla's Robotaxi service going 24/7 across all its operating cities this week, rapid geofence expansion in Texas (including a new San Antonio launch and Fort Worth expansion), and why Florida cities (Tampa, Orlando) remain comparatively small and slow to expand. Speakers debate whether the lag is due to validation, operational readiness/staffing, or regulatory caution, and touch on a recent pause in new Cybercab registrations in Texas.

## Robotaxi
1. **24/7 operations launched across all cities:** Tesla announced this week that Robotaxi now operates 24/7 in every city where it's available — Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa. Speakers note this follows a comment from Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy in Q3 (roughly two months prior) that 24/7 operation would arrive "in a month or so" as part of the next technology update under what one speaker called the "V15 plan."
2. **Rapid Texas geofence expansion:** Over the past two weeks, Tesla added a large new San Antonio geofence (described as possibly the second-largest zone), expanded Fort Worth, and expanded Houston and Austin — with Austin still the largest service area. One speaker says Tesla has caught up to and now trails Waymo in Texas coverage by only a few service areas, a gap he expects may flip again imminently given the pace of change.
3. **Florida expansion lags Texas, reason unclear:** Panelists flag that Florida geofences (Tampa, Orlando) remain small despite Florida's flat terrain and relatively simple roads, which they'd expect to make expansion easier than Texas. The leading theory offered is operational readiness/staffing in Florida rather than technical validation — one speaker notes Tesla CFO-adjacent commentary (attributed to "Nurock" on an earnings call roughly four months prior) described a state-by-state rollout approach, Texas first. Panelists suggest tracking Tesla job-opening postings and their closure rates as an unconfirmed proxy for operational readiness in new markets.
4. **Cybercab registration pause in Texas:** One panelist raises a claim, sourced to an unnamed third party, that Tesla has not added new Cybercabs to Texas registrations since October 28, and speculates this could relate to Tesla submitting responses to NHTSA questions (said to be submitted October 20) as part of a certification process. Other panelists are skeptical this is the cause, instead suggesting new city launches are simply using Model Y vehicles rather than Cybercabs for now, and that it's too early (data point is the panel's own, not confirmed) to attribute the pause to a regulatory holdup.
5. **Regulatory caution ahead of Europe approvals:** A panelist notes Tesla may be proceeding cautiously on expansion given anticipated regulatory approvals expected in Europe in the first week of December.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Software improvement framed as precondition for expansion:** Panelists argue Tesla's approach is to improve the underlying driving software/product first, which then enables geofence and fleet expansion — citing Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 earnings comment that time-to-launch in new cities should approach zero, eventually enabling autonomous operation across entire states rather than individual cities.
2. **Geofencing methodology differs from competitors:** One speaker characterizes Tesla's approach to geofencing as identifying areas to avoid rather than exhaustively pre-mapping every street, differentiating it from other autonomous vehicle operators.
