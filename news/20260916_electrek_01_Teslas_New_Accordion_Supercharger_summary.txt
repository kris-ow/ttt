The article reports that Tesla deployed its first pre-assembled "Accordion" Supercharger, a factory-built 16-stall unit intended to speed up a Supercharger buildout that has slowed sharply since Elon Musk fired the charging team in April 2024, while noting BYD is now expanding fast-charging infrastructure faster than Tesla.

## Energy
1. **Accordion Supercharger design:** The new unit folds like an accordion so one truck can carry 16 V4 stalls at once. Tesla says it installs 20% cheaper than a conventional station, commissions instantly without a technician needed to energize it, and fits between parking spaces at sites where trenching behind a curb isn't possible. Tesla's charging account stated the company's goal is to pre-assemble all Superchargers in a factory.

2. **Earlier prefab efforts:** Tesla built a full Supercharger station in 8 days using an early prefab system in March 2022, and by April 2024 cut that to 4 days from delivery to online, with then-charging chief Rebecca Tinucci citing 15% deployment savings at the time.

3. **The 2024 layoff and its aftermath:** In late April 2024, Musk laid off Tesla's entire ~500-person Supercharger team, including Tinucci. Tesla rehired some employees within weeks and Musk pledged $500 million for Superchargers that year, but deployment pace still suffered afterward.

4. **Slowed connector growth:** Tesla added 2,687 connectors in the quarter before the layoffs, then just 2,017 in Q2 2024 (its slowest pace in three years). Quarterly additions were 1,821 in Q1 2025, 3,865 in Q4 2025, then 2,236 and 2,439 in the first two quarters of 2026. Annual connector growth, which ran 35–45% year-over-year in 2020–2021, is down to about 17% as of Q2 2026, with 82,357 connectors across 8,704 stations. The article notes flat Tesla EV deliveries since 2024 and network access opened to non-Tesla EVs as contributing context.

## Market & Competition
5. **BYD's faster charger rollout:** BYD deployed 10,000 of its 1,500 kW "flash charging" stations across 325 Chinese cities in five months (March–August 2026) and is targeting 20,000 by year-end. At two guns per station, that's roughly 40,000 charging points — approaching half of Tesla's global stall count — built in under a year. BYD's chargers can take its Blade 2.0 battery from 10% to 70% in about five minutes, versus Tesla's V4 cabinets topping out at 500 kW. The article notes BYD's figures are largely China-based (with some expansion into Europe and other Asian countries) versus Tesla's global footprint, and that BYD's peak-power claims depend on its own vehicles, so the comparison isn't exact — but states BYD is now deploying more than twice the charging power per month that Tesla is, a gap that didn't exist two years ago.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the Accordion Supercharger genuinely great engineering and the right approach to scaling charging via factory pre-assembly, but argues Tesla only needs it this badly because of an "unforced error" — the 2024 mass layoff of the charging team that built what had been the world's best network growing over 35% annually. He argues Tesla's growth rate is still roughly half what it once was more than two years later, and that while Tesla's network remains better outside China "for now," BYD's faster and more powerful charging rollout is a real competitive threat.
