This episode of Electrified covers Tesla's Robotaxi expansion into Fort Worth, Texas, lingering FSD safety issues with highway lane-closure arms, and a cluster of SpaceX news spanning a possible Boost Mobile acquisition, new supercomputer sites, Starlink's shift to dynamic pricing, and fresh doubts about a Tesla–SpaceX merger following a comparable real-world stock deal's poor reception.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fort Worth launch makes it Tesla's third-largest Texas geofence:** Tesla's robotaxi service launched in Fort Worth at 6:00 a.m. the day of filming, becoming Tesla's third-biggest geofenced city behind only Austin and Houston, and its first new robotaxi city in roughly three months. The geofence covers about 144 square miles, and reporting so far indicates unsupervised operations. Fort Worth is notable as the first Tesla-covered city where Waymo is not active, while Waymo covers San Antonio, which Tesla does not.
2. **Texas fleet composition flips back toward Model Y:** Texas now has 1,244 registered Tesla robotaxi vehicles across seven active cities, with 573 being Cybercabs — meaning Model Y vehicles now outnumber Cybercabs again in the state. Per the host, Tesla trails Waymo by only 15 registered vehicles in Texas as of recording, and he expects Tesla could surpass Waymo in registered vehicle count by the end of the week given the current rollout pace.
3. **Coverage comparison and the Fort Worth–Dallas gap:** The host added a population coverage metric to his dashboard: Waymo covers about 9.7 million people versus Tesla's roughly 3.4 million. By geofence area estimates, Tesla's four Texas cities cover about 751 square miles versus Waymo's roughly 307 (about 2.4x more area). The host flags a 19-mile gap between the Fort Worth and Dallas geofences as the best near-term candidate for the first connection between major robotaxi cities, noting AT&T Stadium and DFW Airport both sit roughly between the two zones.
4. **Officially announced expansion cities not exhaustive:** Tesla has officially announced plans for Phoenix, Las Vegas, Jacksonville, and San Antonio, but the host argues the surprise Fort Worth launch shows these four are not the only cities in play for imminent expansion.
5. **App feature updates roll out to the fleet:** Recent Robotaxi app updates include operating hours extended to 6:00 a.m.–2:00 a.m., a live rear-view camera feed during rides, trip-sharing with friends, live activity widget updates, a new "calm mode" with dimmed screen and relaxed cabin lighting, Grok now available on Model Y vehicles, an improved vehicle-finding arrow with haptic feedback, and music control via the screen reader.
6. **Long Houston ride and Halloween/incident anecdotes:** A rider reported a 3-hour-5-minute, 74-mile robotaxi trip across the Houston geofence and back for $133.25 (about $1.79/mile), which he called flawless. Separately, a minor incident occurred in Austin where a pedal-party vehicle struck a Cybercab's open door; the unofficial account from the Cybercab passenger placed fault on the pedal-party driver, not the door design.
7. **Japan moves toward driverless certification; Tesla's path unclear:** Japan is preparing new safety standards, to be discussed at a meeting on November 4th and expected to take effect in December, certifying Level 4/5 vehicles without steering wheels, pedals, mirrors, or driver monitoring systems; Japan is targeting 10,000 Level 4-capable buses, taxis, and other vehicles by 2030. Tesla displayed the Cybercab in Japan earlier this year but has reportedly done no on-road testing there. Tesla's Japan president said the company is working toward FSD approval in Japan before year-end but that this depends on regulators, and that a Japan robotaxi launch has not been decided, though Tesla is interested in bringing Cybercab there if the opportunity arises.
8. **European Cybercab tour resumes November 5th:** Tesla Europe announced the Cybercab tour restarts November 5th, running through January across cities including Zurich, Bratislava, Dusseldorf, Prague, Hamburg, Warsaw, Berlin, Bucharest, Munich, Budapest, the Brussels Motor Show, and Vienna Drive.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD continues to misjudge closed express-lane crossing arms:** The host cites a video showing FSD failing to recognize a closed-lane crossing-arm barrier, plus a separate report from a Model Y owners' group describing a teen's FSD-engaged vehicle driving into a closed express lane and smashing its windshield on the arm. A state trooper reportedly told the family this was the fourth Model Y that week alone to hit that same express-lane arm, underscoring this as an ongoing, unresolved FSD edge case pending a future software version.
2. **New owner-to-owner FSD referral program launched in the US:** Tesla rolled out its first-ever dedicated referral program for supervised FSD: subscribers generate a referral link in the Tesla app, and the referrer gets one free month of FSD (or $99 in Tesla credit if the referred buyer purchases outright) each time someone uses their link, capped at 12 uses per calendar year. The host interprets Tesla's willingness to finally offer this as a signal of growing internal confidence in FSD's current state ahead of a newer version.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **SpaceX reportedly in talks to buy or access Boost Mobile's network:** SpaceX is reportedly in talks to acquire EchoStar's Boost Mobile wireless service or gain access to its network (currently leased from AT&T and T-Mobile), which had 7.38 million subscribers (Boost Mobile and Gen Mobile combined) at the end of Q2. Per the host, Elon Musk met with EchoStar chairman Charlie Ergen last month to discuss a deal; any transfer of Boost's network leasing agreements would likely require AT&T/T-Mobile consent, which the host views as a likely friction point. This would reportedly be in addition to, not a replacement for, SpaceX's previously reported separate talks to acquire roughly 80,000 U.S. cell towers for its own terrestrial network.
2. **Starlink introducing quarterly dynamic pricing in select US markets:** Starlink announced residential plan prices in select areas (including parts of Kansas and Missouri) will now be reviewed roughly quarterly and can move up or down based on local supply/demand, rather than being fixed. Per the host, website code suggests changes will be capped at a maximum 20% increase or decrease per adjustment.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grokbot usage caps tightened mid-cycle, then partially restored:** Over the weekend, Elon Musk said the cost of serving Grokbot was unsustainably high relative to revenue, after users reported their usage allowances were cut mid-subscription-cycle; the host says some users have since had portions of that usage reinstated. The host frames this as reflecting that consumer AI subscription pricing across the industry (citing reports that OpenAI's $200/month plan could cost it far more in underlying API-equivalent compute if maxed out) is generally subsidized and loss-making on heavy users.
2. **New SpaceX compute sites "Gun Range" and "Spam" are renamed existing projects:** Elon Musk clarified that "Gun Range" (a training-focused site, built on a former police/fire training facility, to run models like Grok 4.8) is tracking to be operational by end of November, and "Spam" (an inference-focused site occupying a former Hormel food distribution warehouse) is tracking for December. The host notes these are not new projects but renamed versions of previously disclosed plans, and both are already counted within SpaceX's stated goal of 2.5–3 GW of compute online by year-end, consistent with Musk's earlier (September) statement about roughly 220,000 GB300s coming online in November and a further ~220,000 by late December.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Growing doubts about a Tesla–SpaceX merger, with a cautionary real-world comp:** A commentator the host cites (Alexandra, "Tesla Boomer Mama") said her confidence in an eventual Tesla–SpaceX merger has fallen from being fully convinced in an earlier month to 85% in October to 50% now, citing the need for a very good deal to pass a Tesla shareholder vote and the loss-of-voting-rights concern for institutional holders. Separately, the host discusses Gary Black's point that Marvell's all-stock acquisition of Lumentum (announced at a 4.5-Marvell-shares-per-Lumentum-share ratio, initially valued around $1,132 per share) has since seen Marvell stock drop 25% since October 28th (shedding $55 billion in value) as arbitrage selling and dilution concerns pushed the implied deal value down to about $849 per share — illustrating, per the host, that a stock-based SpaceX–Tesla merger carries real execution and share-price risk and is not a guaranteed win for Tesla shareholders even if it occurs.
