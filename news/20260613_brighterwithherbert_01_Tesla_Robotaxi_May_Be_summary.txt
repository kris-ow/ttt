## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised approved in Belgium and Denmark, now five European countries total:** Belgium's Ministry of Transport of Flanders signed approval for FSD Supervised covering the entire Belgian territory (both Flemish and Belgian roads), coming just days after Elon Musk and Tesla Europe announced approval in Denmark. This brings the total number of European countries with FSD Supervised approval to five: Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, and Estonia — representing approximately 11% of all European countries. Brian White speculates Spain may be next, given its apparent eagerness, followed potentially by Ireland.

2. **Piper Sandler analyst declares Tesla has achieved Level 4 autonomy in most conditions:** In a note released on Wednesday (approximately June 11, 2026), Alex Potter of Piper Sandler wrote that "Tesla has solved the self-driving puzzle" and that FSD is "ready for dissemination beyond early adopters." Potter cited his personal experience having FSD drive him from Missoula, Montana to Minneapolis, Minnesota in April as evidence, stating "there is no substitute for personal experience." Herbert notes he interviewed Potter at a Cyberbulls event a couple of weeks prior.

3. **FSD Supervised surpasses 11 billion cumulative miles globally:** Tesla's global fleet just crossed 11 billion miles driven on FSD Supervised, up from 10 billion just one month prior — meaning Tesla owners are collectively driving approximately 1 billion FSD miles every 37 days. The hosts note that as more European countries gain approval and Tesla's fleet continues to grow, the average daily FSD miles figure is accelerating. Brian White mentions personally using FSD 98% of the time over roughly 8,000–9,000 miles driven.

4. **FSD data from Denmark shows 3.5x safety improvement over human drivers:** Herbert references data presented a few days prior showing that in Denmark — after four months of FSD Supervised use on European roads — FSD Supervised is approximately 3.5 times safer than human drivers across key metrics including less hard braking and fewer hard turns. The hosts argue this real-world European safety data should compel regulators to accelerate approvals, since every day of delay effectively costs lives.

5. **Hardware 3 vs. Hardware 4 capability gap widening; FSD version 14 "light" expected:** Brian White drove a Hardware 3 Model 3 with 158,000 miles and found it still impressive, but noted the capability gap between Hardware 3 and Hardware 4 continues to widen as Hardware 4 keeps improving. Herbert notes that a "FSD version 14 light" port for Hardware 3 vehicles had been discussed as potentially arriving by end of June 2026, though nothing has been heard. Brian references statements by Ashok Elluswamy as the source of that timeline but cautions he does not follow Elon Musk's timelines closely.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab fleet accumulating rapidly in Texas, ~10 new vehicles registered per day:** As of the filming date (Wednesday, approximately June 11, 2026), approximately 69 Cybercab vehicles are registered with the Texas DMV, up by roughly 10 vehicles in a short period, with registrations increasing at a rate of at least 10 per day. Photos from community members show large clusters of parked Cybercabs in both North Houston and Dallas parking lots. Brian White frames this as a deliberate strategic buildup: "When you see troops amassing at your border... they're doing this because they have a plan."

2. **Munro Live interview flags safety readiness for robotaxi deployment:** Brian White references a recent interview between Munro Live and Will from Tesla Jigsaw, in which Munro Live was characteristically direct in arguing that FSD is already safer and questioning why regulators — particularly in Europe — are holding it back. Munro Live's position is that while zero deaths cannot be guaranteed due to unpredictable variables, the system is already demonstrably safer than human drivers.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **European Tesla sales up ~95,000 units year-over-year in first five months of 2026:** Data from analyst Roland Percher (tracked at "Poli") shows that across the European countries he monitors, Tesla sold approximately 95,000 more vehicles in the first five months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 — close to 100,000 units of surplus. Year-over-year monthly comparisons for May 2026 vs. May 2025 show a difference of approximately 24,000 vehicles in his tracked subset. The hosts note this comparison is somewhat skewed by factory retooling shutdowns in early 2025, and prefer comparison to 2024 data. Brand sentiment negativity has also diminished, aiding sales.

2. **BYD records eighth straight month of sales decline in China, down ~30% year-to-date:** Data highlighted by Michael Dunn (former CEO of GM Indonesia) shows BYD Auto has experienced eight consecutive months of declining sales in China, with approximately a 30% drop since the start of 2026. Geely, Changan, and SAIC are cited as applying competitive pressure. The April top three in China were Geely, Xiaomi SU7, and Model Y — though Model Y was number one in the prior month.

3. **Xiaomi losing approximately $5,000 per vehicle sold:** Brian White notes that Xiaomi is losing roughly $5,000 on every car it sells, which he argues makes a long-term path to profitability difficult even given the brand's broader tech conglomerate revenue streams. He draws a parallel to Tesla's own "production hell" era with the Model 3, suggesting Xiaomi will need to replicate that cost-reduction journey.

4. **2026 Tesla Model 3 RWD named most efficient EV in Edmunds real-world testing:** Edmunds tested the 2026 Model 3 rear-wheel drive and found it traveled 393 miles on a full charge — 30 miles (8.3%) beyond its EPA-rated 363-mile figure. More significantly, it consumed only 21.7 kWh per 100 miles in real-world testing, 13% better than the EPA rating of 25 kWh per 100 miles, making it the most efficient EV currently in production that Edmunds has tested. The review also noted improved cabin finish quality.

5. **Tesla ranks sixth in long-term vehicle durability study, beating Mazda, Subaru, and Porsche:** A study by iSeeCars analyzing 174 million vehicles found Tesla twice as likely to reach 250,000 miles as Subaru. Toyota, Lexus, Honda, and Acura led the rankings, with Tesla ranking higher than Mazda, Subaru, and Porsche. The hosts attribute Tesla's ranking — which they consider understated — to the car's relative newness limiting sample size, and predict Tesla will ultimately rank number one as more vehicles accumulate high mileage. Brian White cites battery degradation of approximately 1% per year as a key data point, meaning roughly 15% capacity loss over 15 years.
