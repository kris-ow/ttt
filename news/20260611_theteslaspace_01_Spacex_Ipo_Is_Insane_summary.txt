This episode of The Tesla Space covers four major stories: SpaceX's unprecedented IPO roadshow presentation outlining an extraordinarily ambitious long-term vision; Tesla's application to bring its robotaxi network to Las Vegas at massive scale; the Tesla Semi's early groundwork being laid in Europe; and a tentative unveil window for the long-delayed new Tesla Roadster. The video's core argument is that each of these developments — from SpaceX's trillion-dollar addressable market framing to Tesla's Nevada permit filing — represents a company preparing infrastructure and relationships well ahead of large-scale execution.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO Roadshow — A Vision Far Beyond Rockets:** SpaceX published what the host describes as the most detailed vision of the future the company has ever put on paper, delivered as a formal roadshow presentation to institutional investors ahead of its stock market debut. Rather than focusing narrowly on margins and near-term financials, CFO Brett Johnson — who has held the role for 15 years — walked investors through SpaceX's history, current businesses, and long-term opportunities spanning lunar bases, asteroid mining, AI satellites, orbital manufacturing, and Mars colonization. The company frames its three current business areas — space, connectivity, and artificial intelligence — as representing nearly $6 trillion in addressable market today, expanding to roughly $28.5 trillion with AI-driven growth.

2. **Starlink's Scale and Revenue:** The roadshow highlighted Starlink as a direct product of SpaceX's launch cost advantage. The constellation now consists of more than 9,600 satellites serving approximately 10.3 million subscribers across 164 countries. The connectivity segment generated more than $11 billion in revenue in 2025, with the subscriber base roughly doubling year-over-year. The presentation framed Starlink as proof that cheap, reliable orbital access can serve as a foundation for entirely new industries.

3. **XAI Colossus and Major Cloud Compute Deals:** SpaceX is positioning itself as an AI compute infrastructure provider through XAI's Colossus supercomputing platform, described in the roadshow as the world's largest coherent supercomputer and the first gigawatt-scale AI training cluster. SpaceX entered a cloud services agreement with Anthropic providing access to Colossus and Colossus 2 at approximately $1.25 billion per month through May 2029. Google separately signed an agreement for access to approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs at approximately $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029. Combined, these two deals represent more than $2.1 billion per month — a remarkable profile for a company primarily known as a rocket manufacturer.

4. **SpaceX's Lunar Economy Roadmap:** The roadshow devoted a dedicated section to the lunar economy, beginning with NASA's Artemis program and progressing through a permanent lunar base, lunar factories manufacturing AI compute satellites, and a mass driver (electromagnetic catapult) on the lunar surface to launch those satellites directly into orbit. The stated end goal is growing computing capacity to terawatt scales. The host notes SpaceX frames this as following the same playbook it already executed with reusable rockets and Starlink — iterative acceleration toward a seemingly implausible goal.

5. **SpaceX's "Accelerate" Philosophy:** The host references Elon Musk's invocation of the SR-71 Blackbird aircraft at an XAI engineering open house in October 2024 — the plane's only defense strategy was speed, never maneuver. This philosophy, the host argues, is visible across all of SpaceX's projects: accelerating rocket reusability, satellite deployment, AI cluster construction, and now orbital compute infrastructure. SpaceX's positioning is that orbit is no longer the destination — it is the starting point.

## Robotaxi

1. **Nevada Permit Application — Up to 5,000 Vehicles in Clark County:** Tesla filed an application for an autonomous vehicle network company permit with the Nevada Transportation Authority covering Clark County, which includes Las Vegas. If approved, Tesla would be authorized to deploy up to 5,000 autonomous vehicles in the region within the first year. The host contrasts this directly with Tesla's current operation of approximately 30 unsupervised robotaxi vehicles in Austin, calling 5,000 vehicles "a completely different scale of operation" and characterizing the filing as preparation for an "all at once" expansion moment that Elon Musk has previously described.

2. **Strategic Value of the Las Vegas Market:** The permit application specifically covers Harry Read International Airport and Henderson Executive Airport. The host argues Las Vegas is among the most naturally suited environments for autonomous vehicles anywhere in the country — high passenger density, predictable road layouts, around-the-clock demand from the Strip, the convention center, and the entertainment corridor, plus highly valuable airport pickup queues in one of the world's busiest tourist destinations.

3. **Tesla's Nevada Infrastructure Already Underway:** Permit filings in Clark County from May 2026 revealed Tesla was already renovating a 36,000 square foot facility on Mohawk Street for Cybercab maintenance, washing, and charging — indicating Tesla had been preparing ground-level infrastructure ahead of the formal network permit application.

4. **Austin Robotaxi Program Expands to Full Metro Area:** Tesla expanded its unsupervised robotaxi service to cover the entire Austin metropolitan area, marking the fifth expansion of the operating zone since the program launched in June 2025. The coverage has grown from an original 20 square mile patch of South Austin to a much larger footprint that now includes suburbs like Pflugerville and Manor, major highways including Interstate 35, and Austin Bergstrom Airport. The active unsupervised fleet operating across this territory remains at approximately 30 vehicles, per the transcript.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi European Groundwork Begins in Germany:** Tesla posted a job listing for a business development manager for the Semi based in Parsdorf, just outside Munich, Germany. The role involves lining up fleet customers, configuring trucks and charging infrastructure, and expanding the Semi customer base across Europe. Elon Musk stated earlier in 2026 at Giga Berlin that the Semi would "hopefully" come to Europe in 2027 — at least seven months away — but Tesla sells directly to fleet operators, meaning the sales cycle is long and relationship-building must begin well in advance. Germany is the largest truck market in Europe and the center of the continent's logistics network.

2. **Tesla Semi US Production Already Ramping:** The host notes that Tesla began high-volume Semi production in late April 2026 at its dedicated factory near Giga Nevada, targeting 50,000 trucks per year. Early fleet customers like PepsiCo and Frito-Lay have been running their trucks for several years, accumulating real-world operational data that European fleet operators will want to review before committing. The Semi's center driving position makes it compatible with both left-hand and right-hand drive markets without requiring a separately engineered vehicle.

3. **New Roadster Unveil Targeting August 2026:** At Tesla Takeover Europe in Austria, chief designer Franz von Holzhausen told attendees that the Roadster unveil is coming "in a few weeks" — described by the host as the most concrete timing signal any Tesla executive has given on the car in years. The Information separately reported the unveil is targeting August 2026, which the host calculates as roughly seven weeks away. The next-generation Roadster was first unveiled in 2017 and was originally slated for 2020 deliveries; that timeline never materialized. Tesla has confirmed the car will be built at Gigafactory Texas and is an official collaboration with SpaceX. Elon Musk has claimed it could hit 0–60 mph in under one second and has said it will long-term be the only manually driven car in Tesla's lineup.
