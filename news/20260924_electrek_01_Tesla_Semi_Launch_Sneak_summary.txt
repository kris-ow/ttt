Drone footage shot by YouTuber Zanegler ahead of Tesla's Semi factory inauguration event shows at least 11 customer-branded Semi trucks parked outside the Nevada plant, signaling Tesla plans to showcase fleet customers at the event.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Customer trucks spotted at Nevada factory:** Drone footage shows trucks branded for PepsiCo, US Foods, IMC Logistics, Einride, LTS, OK Produce, ABF (ArcBest), DHL, and WattEV parked outside Tesla's Semi factory in Sparks, Nevada, with at least two more logos too blurry to identify.

2. **Known vs. new customers:** PepsiCo received the first Semi deliveries in late 2022; DHL took its first Semi delivery last December; ArcBest's ABF Freight bought Semis in June after a pilot reportedly showing 1.55 kWh per mile efficiency; Einride placed a 500-truck order in August; IMC Logistics announced a 50-truck order for California drayage two days before this article. US Foods, OK Produce, LTS, and WattEV are new names the article says haven't previously been reported as firm Semi customers, suggesting Tesla may confirm orders from them at the event.

3. **Launch event details:** Tesla is holding a livestreamed event (9 p.m. ET) inaugurating its dedicated Semi factory next to Gigafactory Nevada, which the company says can build 50,000 trucks a year. The plant has been producing trucks on its high-volume line since April. Tesla offers a Standard Range version (325 miles) and a Long Range version (500 miles), both with an 800 kW three-motor powertrain.

4. **New large order reported:** Tesla was named primary supplier this week for ZET SCALE's 2,500-truck order, backed by Microsoft and PepsiCo; the deal also includes Kenworth, Volvo, and RIDE as suppliers.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the visible fleet of branded customer trucks shows real commercial demand after years of delays since the 2017 unveiling, but cautions this should be kept in perspective: analysts expect Tesla to deliver only 5,000–15,000 Semis this year versus the plant's 50,000-unit capacity, and the author believes 5,000 would be "a great win" with actual deliveries likely below that. He states the event will only be meaningful if Tesla discloses pricing, current production rate, actual delivery numbers, and how many customers have committed to large volumes rather than small trial orders.
