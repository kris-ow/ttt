This futureaza video is a conversation between the host and Dillon Loomis (Electrified) about SpaceX's expanding business lines beyond rockets — payload/launch, terrestrial AI compute leasing, and Starlink (including V3 satellites and mobile/enterprise applications) — with both speakers stressing heavy uncertainty around sizing these markets and repeatedly cautioning against taking Elon Musk's numerical predictions literally.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9's economics come from internal demand, not external market:** Loomis argues launch is "the least interesting part" of SpaceX financially but the critical foundation — Falcon 9 is cheap because SpaceX generates its own demand (Starlink launches) to reuse boosters 30-40+ times, rather than relying on third-party customers.

## SpaceX — Starship
2. **Starship success underpins most of SpaceX's future value:** Both speakers note that a large majority of SpaceX's future potential hinges on Starship succeeding; Loomis cites a comment attributed to Elon Musk that Starship might fly as often as once a day within a year, which Loomis explicitly discounts, saying he's applying skepticism similar to how he's learned to treat Musk's FSD timeline predictions — he expects flight cadence to increase but doubts the specific "once a day" figure materializes on that timeline.
3. **Payload market size called unknowable:** Loomis says it's very difficult to size the future payload/cargo market given competitive uncertainty, preferring to judge SpaceX qualitatively — asking whether the company/team will be bigger and better in 3-5 years (he believes yes) rather than modeling specific TAM numbers.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
4. **Musk reportedly frames AI as SpaceX's dominant long-term value driver:** Loomis says Elon Musk stated this week that in the long term, 99% of SpaceX's value/market cap potential will come from AI — a claim Loomis flags as needing significant skepticism given Musk's history of aggressive predictions (e.g., comparing it to 2018-era FSD forecasts).
5. **Terrestrial compute leasing framed as opportunistic, uncertain durability:** SpaceX is leasing out terrestrial AI compute at high margins with reportedly ~1-year capital payback periods, driven by scarcity of frontier Nvidia chips. Loomis says Musk has stated he doesn't expect the chip supply/demand imbalance to change through 2030, and that SpaceX has deepened its Nvidia partnership, but Loomis stresses nobody can confidently say how durable this leasing business is, and doesn't expect SpaceX to scale it into a broad multi-hundred-customer AWS-style business.
6. **Musk's $500B compute revenue prediction discounted by host, with an internal inconsistency:** Loomis cites Elon Musk projecting $500 billion in revenue from terrestrial compute leasing by the end of 2027, describing this as roughly an order of magnitude more compute online by then; Loomis then does a rough calculation at $30/watt implying "$300 to $500 billion" in revenue — a figure that doesn't derive cleanly from the stated $500B/order-of-magnitude framing, so this specific dollar range should be read as Loomis's own back-of-envelope estimate rather than a verified figure. Loomis says he generally discounts Musk's stated dollar figures by roughly 30-70% and applies a timeline multiplier when interpreting his predictions.
7. **Why Tesla isn't competing in compute leasing:** Loomis argues Tesla won't stand up a competing compute-leasing business because (1) Tesla wouldn't compete with SpaceX, (2) frontier chip access is scarce, and (3) Tesla has no spare compute — its Cortex cluster is fully allocated to training FSD and Optimus, which Loomis calls Tesla's durable advantage; he suggests SpaceX "almost walked into" the leasing business accidentally after overbuilding compute for Grok.
8. **Compute payback math discussed favorably:** The host frames SpaceX's leased compute deals as attractive even in a "worst case" where leased-compute value collapses after about 11 months — he calculates that would still return roughly 88% of the capital, calling that a good outcome, after which the compute itself is essentially free.

## SpaceX — Starlink
9. **HughesNet/EchoStar spectrum deal cited as major capacity unlock:** The host notes SpaceX gained access to HughesNet/EchoStar spectrum said to be 10 times broader than what SpaceX previously used, ahead of the Starlink V3 satellite generation, which both speakers expect to substantially expand capability and reduce competition.
10. **Musk's trillion-dollar Starlink broadband claim also discounted:** Loomis says Musk called Starlink's broadband/Wi-Fi business alone a trillion-dollar opportunity on X, but reiterates his standard discount (30-70%) on such figures; he also notes Musk has talked about a majority of internet traffic eventually running through Starlink, but pins that outcome to the 2030s, contingent on Starship launch cadence for deploying V3 satellites.
11. **Starlink Mobile adoption pace uncertain; enterprise/B2B seen as nearer-term:** Loomis says he personally won't switch from Verizon until Starlink Mobile reliability improves, and that broad consumer adoption depends on how fast SpaceX can build out terrestrial femtocell infrastructure, which hasn't been detailed publicly. The host expects the initial approach to be business-to-business, with SpaceX partnering with existing carriers who handle customer-facing traffic management, and cites Musk's stated view that enterprise use cases (maritime, commercial aviation) should exceed consumer broadband revenue.
12. **Aviation Starlink pricing example:** The host cites a reported aviation Starlink package price of roughly $50,000 per month per plane, arguing this is reasonable once divided across daily flights and passengers, and shares a personal example of paying an extra $20 for Starlink-equipped Wi-Fi on a 15-hour Qatar Airways flight.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
13. **Host stance: no position, valuation unclear, but execution positive:** The host states he is not a SpaceX shareholder and doesn't know whether the current valuation is over- or undervalued, but says SpaceX's most recent earnings report came in far better than he expected, which he calls a comfort to early believers.
