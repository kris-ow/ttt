Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that Elon Musk has systematically abandoned his founding "solar electric economy" thesis — burning millions of tons of fossil fuels to power an underperforming AI chatbot, leasing that compute to a company he publicly vilified, and now promoting space-based solar primarily to justify SpaceX's upcoming IPO.

## Energy
1. **xAI's gas turbine buildout contradicts Musk's solar legacy:** xAI is operating 62 unpermitted methane gas turbines across data centers in Memphis, TN, and Southaven, MS, powering Grok's Colossus supercomputers. Per xAI's own permit applications, the combined facilities could emit more than 6 million tons of greenhouse gases per year and over 1,300 tons of air pollutants. The EPA closed the regulatory loophole xAI was exploiting in January 2026, but thermal drone footage from February showed turbines still running; the NAACP and Earthjustice have sought emergency court action. xAI was also purchasing an additional $2.8 billion in gas turbines.

2. **SpaceX spent on Megapacks but not on solar:** Per the SpaceX S-1 filing, xAI spent $697 million on Tesla Megapacks for its data centers but did not purchase a materially significant amount of solar panels from Tesla. SpaceX separately spent $131 million on 1,279 Cybertrucks.

3. **Space-based solar thesis tied to IPO, not energy needs:** SpaceX's S-1, filed May 20, targets a valuation of $1.75–$2 trillion with a planned $75–$80 billion raise. The filing introduces a space-based solar thesis, claiming orbital arrays generate "more than five-times the energy" of terrestrial ones due to 24/7 illumination, and includes an FCC filing for an "orbital data center" constellation of up to one million satellites in low Earth orbit. Lambert argues this thesis exists to justify Starship launch demand and higher valuation multiples, not to address an actual energy gap — noting the U.S. installed only 37 GW of solar in 2024 against theoretical needs in the thousands of GW.

## Market & Competition
1. **Grok's market collapse:** Grok entered 2026 as the second most-popular AI chatbot globally — boosted by X auto-replies — but fell to fifth place by April, behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek. Monthly active users dropped 12.5% in a single month to 12.2 million, while Claude surged 44% to 23 million users. Downloads fell 60%. In enterprise, only 7% of companies reported using Grok in March 2026, versus 48% for Claude and 40% for Gemini. A survey of 260,000 Americans found just 0.174% paid for Grok in Q2 2026.

2. **xAI leases Colossus to Anthropic despite prior attacks:** Unable to fill Colossus's 220,000 Nvidia GPUs with Grok demand, Musk leased the facility to Anthropic — a company he publicly called "misanthropic and evil" in February 2026, accusing it of racism and data theft. By May, Anthropic was paying xAI $1.25 billion per month, a deal worth over $40 billion through 2029. Lambert notes the deal helps shore up SpaceX's IPO narrative by converting sunk capital expenditure into recurring revenue. The agreement gives Musk a contractual "kill switch" over Anthropic's compute if its AI "engages in actions that harm humanity."

## Editorial Stance
Lambert argues Musk's pivot from terrestrial solar to space solar is financial engineering for the SpaceX IPO, not an energy solution — and that Musk's own decade-long arguments about cheap, abundant ground-level solar have never been invalidated. He frames the entire arc — gas turbines, Grok, the Anthropic deal, and space solar — as a coherent abandonment of Tesla's founding mission for short-term business model construction.
