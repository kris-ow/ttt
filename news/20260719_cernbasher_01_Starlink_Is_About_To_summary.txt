This episode features a co-host (Phil) demonstrating real-world Starlink performance (beach, plane, car) via his own network-testing app, then discussing SpaceX's upcoming Starship 13 launch and its plan to deploy 20 functioning V3 Starlink satellites — a major bandwidth upgrade. The back half is a long interview with drone photographer/videographer Joe Tegtmeyer, live from Starbase, covering Starship 13 launch prep, Giga Texas/Optimus Factory construction progress, and the state of Tesla's Cybercab production and robotaxi rollout readiness.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **V3 satellites represent a ~10x bandwidth leap:** Starship 13 is expected to deploy 20 fully functioning Starlink V3 satellites (versus 22, mostly non-functional/test units, on the prior Starship 12 flight). Because V3 satellites are too large to fit on a Falcon 9 fairing, this Starship launch is the first opportunity to put them in orbit; the host estimates 23 V3 satellites equal the capacity of roughly 260 V2 Mini satellites. V2 Minis run about 96 Gbit/s downlink; V3 satellites are expected to exceed a terabit — roughly 10x the downlink and about 25x the uplink bandwidth versus V2 Mini, per the host.
2. **Launch cadence continues at high volume:** SpaceX launched 258 Starlink V2 Mini satellites between mid-June and mid-July 2026 (roughly 23–24 satellites across about 10–12 missions), which the host frames as now routine rather than extraordinary.
3. **Latency and architecture explained:** Starlink satellites orbit at roughly 300 miles altitude (vs. ~20,000 miles for geostationary competitors like Viasat/Panasonic), enabling latency well below the ~400ms floor that geostationary systems face; this is what makes gaming and video conferencing viable over Starlink, per the host.
4. **V3 platform will also underpin orbital AI data centers and direct-to-cell:** The host believes SpaceX's orbital compute satellites (referred to as "A1s") share manufacturing, solar, and radiative tech with V3 but are physically larger with bigger solar arrays for power, intended to house GPUs (initially Nvidia, later moving to other chips). He also believes the V3 platform will eventually support direct-to-cell mobile connectivity, though he is not certain the specific satellites launching this flight have that capability.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship 13 launch prep and design changes:** Joe Tegtmeyer reported the ship (Ship 40) had a rollout delay from 6pm to 10pm the night before launch. The flight will carry 20 Starlink V3s (14 standard, 6 with cameras/sensors/thrusters) on a suborbital trajectory, with roughly 30 minutes to check satellite systems before reentry. SpaceX is pushing higher booster thrust-to-weight for this flight to reduce gravity losses and maximize payload, resulting in expected higher structural loads — hence more heat-shield tile sensors on the leeward side.
2. **Static fire and cadence acceleration:** About a week prior, SpaceX conducted a full 33-engine, 25-second static fire to validate Raptor 3 engines and a new stage-zero water deluge/flame diverter system, testing ground infrastructure's ability to sustain higher launch tempo. SpaceX aims to move toward roughly monthly cadence after this "prototype" flight, with an eye on 2027 Artemis 3 milestones requiring Starship/Blue Origin lander rendezvous demonstrations in low Earth orbit.
3. **Kennedy Space Center buildout:** SpaceX plans to transport boosters/ships to Kennedy Space Center horizontally via a converted drone ship and a "breakover" pivoting system, then re-erect them using existing ring-stand/SPMT infrastructure — a previously undemonstrated operation. Per Tegtmeyer, Gwynne Shotwell said roughly a month earlier that Flight 15 might launch from Kennedy; a Giga Bay there is expected around the end of 2026, with the Star Factory taking considerably longer.
4. **Heat shield tiles remain a top engineering challenge:** Recovery drones (an estimated half-dozen, per Tegtmeyer's guess) and Starlink-equipped buoys will image the ship's heat shields during reentry, flip, and burn to compare against on-orbit imagery, aiming to solve what Tegtmeyer calls one of the last major engineering hurdles. Drones will also attempt recovery of components like COPVs and tiles.
5. **Orbital refueling called the critical unlock:** Tegtmeyer, a former Air Force tanker pilot, described on-orbit refilling as essential for extending Starship's range beyond low Earth orbit, comparing it to aerial refueling's role in extending military reach — necessary for lunar/Mars missions.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Factory construction outpacing Giga Texas's early build speed:** Tegtmeyer notes the Optimus Factory (address: One Herald Green Road) is going up faster than Giga Texas did at the same stage — floor decking and roof structure are already in with seven cranes (three north, four south) versus six at Giga Texas historically. He estimates the building at roughly 9–10 million square feet across four floors, comparable to Giga Texas's ~11 million square feet.
2. **Rough production/deployment timeline:** Tegtmeyer expects equipment installation to begin possibly next spring, small-scale initial production in late 2027, with 2028 being "the big year" for Optimus ramp. He is uncertain whether a battery cell production line will be located in the Optimus Factory itself, noting Giga Texas's main factory has been expanding 4680 cell production toward a goal of eight lines.
3. **Battery supply questioned for 10 million/year robot target:** In response to the host's reference to a 10-million-robots-per-year goal, Tegtmeyer speculated (using an illustrative unconfirmed assumption of ~20 batteries per robot) that this would require roughly 200 million battery cells — flagged by both speakers as a rough, informal estimate rather than a company figure.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab production estimated at "dozens per day," pre-dating wider fleet rollout:** Tegtmeyer estimates Cybercab production has been running at "easily dozens per day, many dozens," translating to roughly 100–200 per week given a four-day production week, ongoing since around April 2026. He states Tesla is not yet "pushing the gas pedal down" on production, implying capacity exceeds current output because there is not yet a large enough deployed fleet to absorb more units.
2. **Four gating factors for wider Cybercab/robotaxi rollout, per Tegtmeyer:** (1) production capability — deemed largely solved; (2) autonomous driving software readiness against internal safety/KPI goals — still being worked on based on ongoing test-track and around-country testing; (3) fleet management infrastructure (charging, cleaning, repair, customer support) which scales with each new city and which he calls an underappreciated challenge; (4) a fragmented regulatory environment across states (Texas more permissive, California stricter, others unknown).
3. **Employee-driven pre-testing pattern observed before service expansions:** Tegtmeyer says Tesla has historically used employee drivers to pre-test Cybercabs in areas before officially expanding the robotaxi service area, and believes this same pattern is likely occurring now ahead of a broader Cybercab robotaxi launch; he expects inclusion "sooner than later" but could not commit to a specific date, and suggested the public may be surprised by how much groundwork has already occurred.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y L was known internally 6+ months before public confirmation:** Tegtmeyer said he had known about the Model Y L "all year" — roughly six months before it became publicly obvious — based on early signals like body-in-white units arriving in crates for extensive crash testing.

## Market & Competition
