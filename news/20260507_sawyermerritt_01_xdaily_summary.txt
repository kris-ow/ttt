## Executive Summary
Today's posts from @SawyerMerritt were light on hard robotaxi/FSD data but carried several noteworthy Tesla-specific signals: the sunset of one-time FSD purchases in Europe (deadline May 21, 2026), a new Tesla Roadster trademark filing, and ongoing Cybercab visual comparisons. ## Key Posts / Themes

1. **FSD Europe Subscription Transition — Deadline May 21, 2026:** Tesla is ending one-time (perpetual) FSD purchases across Europe on May 21, 2026, affecting the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark, and other markets. After that date, FSD will only be available via subscription. This is a meaningful monetization model shift — transitioning European customers to recurring revenue — and gives prospective buyers roughly two weeks to lock in a one-time purchase. This mirrors a transition Tesla has already made or signaled in other markets, and has direct implications for Tesla's software revenue mix and long-term ARR modeling.

2. **Tesla Roadster Trademark Filing:** Merritt flagged a newly discovered Tesla trademark application for what appears to be a next-generation Roadster logo/badge. The filing describes the design lines as depicting "speed, propulsion, heat or wind." No launch date, specs, or pricing were mentioned. While the Roadster has long been vaporware in public perception, an active trademark filing suggests some level of internal activity around the brand, though this is a weak signal on its own.

3. **Cybercab Visual Comparisons:** Merritt shared side-by-side imagery comparing glossy vs. matte Cybercab finishes, noting that the glossy variant has a "nicer and richer gold color." No new production, pricing, or timeline data was included — this is a visual/aesthetic observation with minimal investment signal beyond confirming that multiple Cybercab finish variants exist in the wild.

4. **FSD Snow Performance — Anecdotal Validation:** Merritt shared a personal account of using FSD extensively during winter conditions, including low-visibility scenarios with obscured lane markings. He pushed back on the claim that FSD doesn't work in snow, calling it "simply not true" while acknowledging the need for increased vigilance. This is anecdotal but directionally positive for FSD's all-weather capability narrative.

5. **Tesla Semi Incentives Summary:** Merritt compiled available incentives for the Tesla Semi, highlighting California's HVIP program offering $120,000–$330,000 per Semi truck, with over $100M in total funding available on a first-come, first-served basis. Additional unspecified programs were referenced. This is relevant for Tesla Semi demand and fleet adoption velocity, as substantial per-unit subsidies materially reduce TCO for commercial buyers.

6. **Energy & Competition Context — IRENA Renewables Cost Data:** A new IRENA report cited in Merritt's posts shows solar and wind with battery storage now costs $54–$82/MWh, down from over $100/MWh in 2020, and is now cost-competitive with new coal in China ($70–$85/MWh) and cheaper than new gas globally (>$100/MWh). Total installed costs have declined significantly since 2010. This is a macro tailwind for Tesla's Energy segment (Megapack), as falling storage costs accelerate grid-scale battery deployments.

7. **Mercedes All-Electric C-Class Pricing:** Mercedes announced final pricing for its new all-electric C-Class at €68,000 (~$79,900 USD), with up to 350 miles of expected EPA range, a 94 kWh battery, 330kW peak charging, 800V architecture, 0–60 mph in 3.9 seconds, and 482 hp. This positions a premium European legacy OEM directly in Tesla Model S/Model 3 Performance territory at a high price point, suggesting legacy competition remains concentrated at the premium end rather than mass market.

8. **SpaceX/xAI Ecosystem Developments — Indirect Tesla Relevance:** SpaceX and xAI signed an agreement giving Anthropic access to the Colossus 1 supercomputer, with Anthropic citing "over 300 megawatts of additional capacity." Separately, xAI is reportedly being dissolved as a standalone entity into SpaceX ("SpaceXAI"). Also, SpaceX filed to build a $55B (up to $119B total) next-generation semiconductor and advanced computing fabrication facility in Grimes County, Texas. These are primarily SpaceX/xAI stories, but the semiconductor fab ambition — if it extends to Tesla's custom AI chip supply chain — could have long-term relevance to FSD and Dojo compute costs. No direct Tesla connection was stated in the posts.
