This episode of FutureAzA features host Brian and Herbert from Brighter Width analyzing why Tesla stock has been volatile during the week of June 11, 2026, examining macro factors including Middle East tensions, CPI inflation data, and the SpaceX IPO. The video also covers a string of FSD regulatory approvals across Europe and briefly touches on Tesla's competitive moat in autonomous driving. The core argument is that Tesla's stock decline is driven by broader macro forces rather than Tesla-specific bad news, and that FSD's international expansion is quietly accelerating.

## Market & Competition

1. **Three Reasons for Tesla's Stock Decline:** Barron's cited three drivers for Tesla's pre-market decline: Middle East tensions contributing to broader market sell-offs, a reported 4.2% year-over-year rise in the Consumer Price Index fueling interest rate hike fears, and anticipated downward pressure from the upcoming SpaceX IPO. Brian and Herbert are skeptical of the framing, noting the language used ("crushed," "landslide") is overblown relative to the actual price action.

2. **Tesla as a "Double Beta" Stock:** Herbert explains that Tesla behaves as a high-beta stock, meaning it falls roughly twice as far as the broader market during sell-offs. As of Wednesday's close, Tesla was down 3.8% on the day, 9% on the week, and 14% on the month — but Brian contextualizes this as a minor blip when zoomed out to a year-long chart.

3. **SpaceX IPO Impact Overstated:** Herbert notes that only 31% of Tesla shares are held by retail investors, limiting the impact of Tesla shareholders selling to fund SpaceX allocations. Additionally, the SpaceX float being raised is only $75 billion — roughly 7–8% of SpaceX's total outstanding shares — making it a small absolute figure. Herbert acknowledges it is likely a contributor to selling pressure but not a dominant driver.

4. **Middle East and Oil Markets:** Herbert references commentary from Jeff Lutz indicating that oil is still flowing through despite the geopolitical conflict, which is why broader markets have largely shrugged off the war. Tech and "story stocks" like Tesla remain more sentiment-sensitive and are disproportionately affected by any shift in risk appetite.

5. **No Real Second Place in U.S. Autonomy:** Brian and Herbert discuss who holds second place in U.S. autonomous driving for consumer vehicles. Their conclusion is that no car available for purchase in the U.S. today can genuinely drive itself — all competitors offer highway assist at best. Herbert names Xpeng as second in the world for self-driving consumer cars, but notes it is only available in China and cannot be imported. The hosts joke that if Tesla disappeared tomorrow, they would buy something cheap just to get by until a real alternative emerged.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Approved in Belgium — Fifth European Country:** Belgium's Minister of Transport signed FSD approval covering both the Flemish and broader Belgian regions, announced just hours before recording. Belgium had initially aimed to be the second European country to approve FSD after the Netherlands but was leapfrogged by Lithuania, Estonia, and then Denmark, making it the fifth. Belgium had conducted its own internal testing of approximately 5,000 kilometers before signing approval.

2. **FSD Rollout Sequence Across Europe:** The order of European FSD approvals discussed is: Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and then Belgium. Herbert notes that Reuters incorrectly described Belgium as the third EU country. The pace of approvals is accelerating — from weeks between approvals to now nearly every two days — and Herbert credits Jo Bhakdi's insight that once enough countries approve, peer pressure causes others to follow quickly.

3. **China FSD Approval Still Pending:** Despite a high-profile U.S. trade delegation including President Trump and roughly 15–17 top tech CEOs traveling to China, no announcement of full Tesla FSD approval in China materialized. Herbert clarifies that China technically has limited FSD approval for a small number of vehicles running an older FSD version, but full market-wide approval has not been granted. Herbert speculates the EU-wide FSD approval may not come until September at the earliest.

4. **FSD V14 Light for Hardware 3 Vehicles:** Ashok Elluswami reportedly stated that FSD V14 Light is targeted for Hardware 3 cars by the end of June 2026. Herbert and Brian estimate there are approximately 3 to 4 million Hardware 3 Tesla vehicles on the road. Even a small FSD take rate on that base would represent hundreds of thousands of additional subscribers, generating meaningful recurring revenue. Brian muses that FSD subscription revenue could eventually make per-vehicle manufacturing margins less critical.

5. **10 Million FSD Subscriptions — Compensation Milestone:** Brian references one of Elon Musk's compensation plan milestones requiring 10 million active FSD subscriptions, which he acknowledges sounds impossible given the current install base. However, he frames the Hardware 3 V14 Light rollout as a meaningful step toward eventually scaling toward that target.
