This video features host Herbert interviewing Cern Basher, a chartered financial analyst and founder of Brilliant Advice wealth management, who argues that Tesla investors are significantly underappreciating the implications of SpaceX's upcoming IPO prospectus. The core thesis is that SpaceX's success is inextricably linked to Tesla's success — not because a merger is guaranteed or even necessarily desirable in the near term, but because the two largest components of SpaceX's stated total addressable market (enterprise AI applications and AI infrastructure) are fundamentally dependent on Tesla's technology and capabilities. The conversation also walks through several hypothetical merger scenarios using a present-value/future-value framework to help Tesla investors think more rigorously about what deal terms would be fair.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX Prospectus TAM and Tesla's Role:** According to Cern Basher's reading of the SpaceX IPO prospectus, SpaceX's total addressable market is stated at $28.5 trillion. The single largest category — at approximately 80% of the TAM, or roughly $22.7 trillion — is AI-powered enterprise applications, branded as "Digital Optimus" on Tesla's side and "Macrohard" on the xAI side. Basher argues this category uses the same perception-planning-control architecture that Tesla developed for Full Self-Driving, but applied to computer desktop video feeds rather than car cameras — essentially autonomous agents operating computers, workflows, and business processes. He stresses that Tesla investors have not yet modeled Digital Optimus as a standalone revenue opportunity and should begin doing so.

2. **AI Infrastructure as the Second-Largest TAM Category:** The second-largest segment of SpaceX's stated TAM is AI infrastructure — orbital compute, Terafab, Dojo chips, and AI6 — estimated at roughly 8% of the $28.5 trillion TAM. Basher notes that Terafab is described as a joint venture between Tesla and SpaceX and that the Dojo 3 chip is associated with AI6. He argues that neither the enterprise applications category nor the AI infrastructure category is achievable without Tesla, which in his view gives Tesla significant leverage in any potential merger negotiation.

3. **Colossus Build Speed as Competitive Moat:** Basher highlights that the SpaceX prospectus itself touts Elon Musk's infrastructure build speed as a key competitive advantage. He cites figures from the prospectus stating Colossus 1 was built in 122 days and Colossus 2 in 91 days, while competitors typically take approximately two years for comparable builds. He draws a parallel to Anthropic, which he says has scaled to $15 billion a year in revenue in part because it had extra compute capacity available — arguing that speed of infrastructure deployment directly enables rapid revenue ramp.

4. **Digital Optimus as Potentially Tesla's First Scalable Business:** Basher makes the provocative claim that Digital Optimus — an AI agent product that can operate computers and automate digital workflows — may actually be the first of Tesla's major new business lines to scale meaningfully, ahead of the robotaxi network and Optimus humanoid robots, because it is a purely digital service with no hardware manufacturing constraint. He acknowledges significant uncertainty, noting that Elon Musk has suggested a beta test could be available within roughly six months (from the time of the conversation), but cautions that xAI is still catching up in AI capabilities relative to competitors.

5. **Competitive Landscape for AI Agents — Trust as the Key Differentiator:** Both Herbert and Basher discuss that the AI agent market (Digital Optimus / Macrohard vs. OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Google) will be highly competitive, and they identify trust as the primary differentiator. They argue that an AI agent capable of acting on a user's behalf — managing emails, filing taxes, and accessing all personal data — requires a level of trust that current incumbents have not established. They suggest Tesla and xAI's "maximally truth-seeking AI" positioning could be an advantage in this regard.

6. **SpaceX's Unique Position in Launch and Orbital Infrastructure:** Basher argues that while the enterprise AI category is highly competitive, SpaceX's launch capability, satellite constellation (already 10,000 satellites deployed), and orbital data center ambitions are effectively unmatched at this time. He notes Blue Origin as a distant potential competitor but argues the market is large enough that competitive dynamics are secondary. He predicts that following a successful Starship launch, SpaceX will receive inbound calls from governments, enterprises, and companies like Google (which he says is already in discussions to reserve launch capacity). He speculates that Microsoft may be the next major partner announced.

7. **Starlink Connectivity for Tesla Fleet and Optimus:** Basher raises the point that Tesla's robotaxi network and Optimus robot fleet would benefit from always-on connectivity via Starlink Mobile, and that Elon Musk would logically prefer to connect his own vehicles and robots to his own network. He suggests this could enable new revenue streams within the robotaxi product, including real-time multiplayer entertainment experiences during rides.

## Financials

1. **Hypothetical Merger Valuation Framework — Five Scenarios:** Basher presents a five-scenario analytical framework for evaluating a hypothetical Tesla-SpaceX merger, emphasizing that the key question is not the current exchange ratio but whether Tesla shareholders' stake in a combined company would be worth at least as much as their expected standalone Tesla value at some future point. He explicitly states these are illustrative numbers, not predictions, and encourages investors to plug in their own assumptions.

2. **Scenario 1 — Merger of Equals at Equal Future Value:** In the first scenario, both companies are assumed to be worth $2 trillion at the time of merger, creating a $4 trillion combined entity with Tesla shareholders holding 50%. If both companies later reach $25 trillion in standalone value (for a $50 trillion combined value), no synergies are required — Tesla shareholders are fully made whole. Basher uses this as a baseline to illustrate that dilution is only a problem when the ratios diverge.

3. **Scenario 2 — SpaceX at $2.5 Trillion, Tesla at $2 Trillion:** If SpaceX rises to $2.5 trillion post-IPO while Tesla is valued at $2 trillion, the combined entity is worth $4.5 trillion and Tesla shareholders receive 44.44% of the combined company. Assuming the same $25 trillion standalone future value for Tesla, this leaves Tesla shareholders $2.78 trillion short of their standalone expectation. Because they only own 44% of the combined entity, the total synergy required to make them whole is approximately $6.25 trillion — meaning the combined enterprise must generate that much additional value above standalone projections.

4. **Scenario 3 — Both Companies Grow Before Merging:** The third scenario considers a case where SpaceX rises to $5 trillion and Tesla rises to $4 trillion before a merger is announced, creating a $9 trillion combined deal. Tesla shareholders still hold only 44% of the combined entity. Basher's point is that waiting for Tesla's stock to rise before a merger does not necessarily improve Tesla shareholders' relative position if SpaceX is also rising — the ownership ratio, not the absolute price, is what determines value capture.

5. **Scenario 4 — SpaceX Future Value Exceeds Tesla:** If SpaceX's standalone future value is $30 trillion versus Tesla's $25 trillion, and both companies merge at equal current valuations (each at $2 trillion), Tesla shareholders actually benefit by hitching their wagon to a faster-growing company. Basher argues this is the scenario where a merger is most clearly accretive for Tesla shareholders on a long-term basis.

6. **Scenario 5 — Tesla Future Value Exceeds SpaceX:** Conversely, if Tesla's future standalone value is $30 trillion and SpaceX's is $25 trillion, a 50/50 merger leaves Tesla shareholders with 50% of $55 trillion ($27.5 trillion) versus a standalone expectation of $30 trillion — a $2.5 trillion shortfall. Because they only own 50% of the combined entity, the total synergy required to bridge this gap is approximately $5 trillion. Basher uses this to illustrate why the merger becomes harder to justify for Tesla shareholders the more Tesla outpaces SpaceX.

7. **Control Structure and Merger Feasibility:** Basher explains that his premise is Elon Musk's primary motivation for any merger would be to preserve his control structure. He notes that Musk currently holds approximately 85% of voting control over SpaceX via Class B super-voting shares (10-to-1). A merger with Tesla would reduce his voting control to approximately 54%, with a secondary protection requiring both the general shareholder vote and Musk's Class B vote to approve major decisions. Basher argues that if Tesla's valuation significantly outpaces SpaceX's, a merger becomes unattractive to Musk because the dilution of his control would be too great — meaning there is a "sweet spot" window for a merger to occur.

8. **Current Tesla Market Cap Referenced:** The video references Tesla's current market capitalization as approximately $1.4 trillion (as of the time of discussion, implying May 2026), with SpaceX's IPO valuation expected to be approximately $2 trillion based on pre-IPO reporting. SpaceX's IPO is described as upcoming, with the actual market valuation to be determined around June 2026.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Scale Trajectory and Infrastructure Dependency:** Basher argues that Optimus humanoid robot production will eventually scale to 1 million and ultimately 10 million units per year, but notes that Tesla will eventually bump into chip supply constraints at scale. He suggests the company may be "okay for a few years" but that the chip supply bottleneck arrives quickly. He also discusses Tesla's Cortex 1 and Cortex 2 training centers as essential infrastructure for Optimus intelligence development, and raises the question of whether access to SpaceX's Colossus data center would accelerate Optimus training.

2. **Beyond Humanoids — All Form Factors:** Herbert speculates that Tesla will expand into robotics beyond humanoid form factors — any device of any size that can be equipped with a camera and a chip becomes an intelligent, autonomous product. He includes potential wearable devices (jewelry, pins, bracelets, rings, glasses) as adjacent opportunities, noting these have not yet been modeled by investors. He argues that the combination of Tesla's chip and AI capabilities with SpaceX's manufacturing of small satellite hardware could make this category emerge "out of nowhere."

3. **Digital Optimus vs. Physical Optimus TAM Framing:** Basher frames the full GDP opportunity as follows: robotaxi (autonomous transportation) represents roughly 5% of GDP; physical Optimus (humanoid labor) represents roughly 15–25% of GDP; and Digital Optimus (non-physical labor automation) represents the remaining approximately 70% of GDP. He uses this breakdown to argue that Digital Optimus is potentially the largest single addressable market Tesla is pursuing — larger than either robotaxi or physical Optimus individually.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi

1. **FSD Architecture as the Foundation for Digital Optimus:** Both speakers emphasize that Tesla's Full Self-Driving perception-planning-control stack is not just relevant to cars but is the foundational architecture for Digital Optimus AI agents. The same visual understanding system that processes camera feeds around a vehicle can be applied to processing a computer desktop video feed, enabling AI agents to understand and act on what they "see" on a screen. This architectural reuse is described as Tesla's core advantage in the enterprise AI agent market.

2. **Robotaxi Network Scale and Starlink Integration:** Basher notes that Tesla will have a "massive robotaxi network" and raises the point that always-on Starlink connectivity for each vehicle in that fleet would be both commercially logical and strategically valuable. He describes entertainment monetization opportunities within robotaxi rides — including real-time multiplayer gaming across the fleet — as one example of incremental revenue streams enabled by reliable connectivity.
