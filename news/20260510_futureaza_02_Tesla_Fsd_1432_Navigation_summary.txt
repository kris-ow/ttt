This video from FutureAzA is a real-world driving review of FSD version 14.3.2, filmed during a series of errands including a bank visit, a trip to the local PUD (utilities department), and a car wash run. The presenter evaluates FSD's driving performance versus its navigation decision-making, with a passenger in the back seat providing additional commentary. The core argument is that FSD's driving capabilities have become impressively reliable and difficult to fault, while navigation and route planning remain a notable weak point.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3.2 driving performance rated highly:** The presenter describes FSD's actual vehicle control — lane selection, pedestrian handling, merging, and responding to other drivers' unexpected behavior — as "exceptional." He notes that finding genuine edge cases where FSD makes a poor driving decision is becoming increasingly rare, calling it a positive sign for the technology's maturity. He comments that the car handled several challenging situations he "wouldn't trust anyone else's FSD with."

2. **Navigation and route planning identified as the weak spot:** Despite strong driving performance, FSD repeatedly looped around the block multiple times before successfully entering a bank parking lot, apparently confused by traffic and parking lot entry points. The presenter and his passenger both confirm this behavior represents a regression from prior versions, with the passenger noting it "didn't used to loop the block three times before realizing we should enter the parking lot." The presenter states plainly: "The navigation has absolutely gotten worse."

3. **Home location repeatedly lost after updates:** The presenter notes that FSD has once again lost the saved home location, requiring him to reteach it after every update — and sometimes between updates. He describes this as "frustrating" but "not unexpected."

4. **New parking UI indicator observed:** During the approach to the bank, the presenter and passenger notice what appears to be a new on-screen indicator showing that FSD intends to park, calling it a new feature in this version.

5. **New intervention reporting feature noted:** After a manual disengagement, the presenter observes a new in-app button allowing drivers to log the reason for intervention without having to leave a written note or use profanity in feedback. He describes this as a welcome quality-of-life improvement.

6. **Grok voice assistant used for navigation:** The presenter demonstrates using Grok (Tesla's AI assistant) via voice command to navigate to the PUD rather than using a long-press input. Grok initially routes to a closed location before correcting to the right destination approximately 1 mile away, arriving in an estimated 5 minutes. The presenter describes this as a convenient alternative input method.

7. **Danish authorities push back on Reuters FSD skepticism report:** The presenter mentions a news item in which Danish authorities publicly contradicted a Reuters article claiming they were skeptical of FSD, stating they had not released any official position for or against it. He frames this as an example of media misrepresentation of regulatory attitudes toward FSD.

8. **Overall verdict — driving vs. navigation gap:** The presenter's final assessment is that FSD drives well enough that making compelling critical review videos is becoming difficult, but navigation planning remains unreliable enough to be a meaningful real-world problem. He suggests that "learning to navigate will be easier for it than learning to drive," implying he expects navigation issues to be resolved faster than driving challenges were.
