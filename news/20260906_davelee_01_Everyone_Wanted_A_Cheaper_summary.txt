This video is a speculative essay by the host arguing that Elon Musk deliberately cancelled a rumored cheaper, next-gen Tesla with a steering wheel in favor of going all-in on the Cybercab/FSD robotaxi paradigm, and that this bet — despite causing short-term revenue stagnation — is intended to lay the groundwork for a new high-volume vehicle lineup built around AI-driven, rider-focused design rather than driver-focused design.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Rumored affordable next-gen vehicle allegedly cancelled:** The host recounts rumors from a couple years ago that Tesla was developing a cheaper next-generation vehicle (speculatively around $30,000) with a steering wheel, and that this program was reportedly axed, despite Tesla and Elon Musk publicly denying a cancellation at the time; Tesla later released the standard Model Y and Model Y L instead, which the host says disappointed people hoping for an all-new cheaper model.
2. **Cybertruck volume shortfall:** The host says Cybertruck has not met its roughly 250,000-unit volume target, attributing this to higher-than-expected cost of goods and weaker-than-expected demand, which he attributes to a combination of price and charging issues rather than the vehicle's looks; he also offers his own unsourced idea that a small onboard-generator range-extender design (as used by some Chinese automakers) could have lowered cost by reducing battery needs.
3. **Model Y demand described as robust despite flat growth:** The host states Model Y demand has stayed robust, including growing uptake in Europe and Asia, arguing Tesla's demand problem is really a lack of a new high-volume, more-affordable model rather than a collapse in demand for existing models.

## Robotaxi
1. **Core thesis — Cybercab chosen over a driver-controlled affordable car:** The host's central, speculative argument is that Tesla management (per his own reasoning, not sourced) likely favored building both a cheaper driver-controlled car and the driverless Cybercab in parallel, but that Elon Musk chose to cancel the driver-controlled program to force company-wide focus on Cybercab and FSD, betting the company's future on succeeding in the new AI-driven paradigm rather than hedging with a conventional cheaper EV.
2. **Six-step roadmap framed by the host:** He lays out a six-step framework he believes describes Tesla's plan: (1) define the end-state paradigm of driver-free transport, (2) build the FSD software and a clean-sheet vehicle (Cybercab) using unboxed assembly and modular, low-part-count manufacturing, (3) prove it out via a geofenced robotaxi network with controlled software rollout, (4) pass regulatory approval, (5) begin consumer sales of Cybercab as a genuinely new, rider-focused vehicle rather than a stripped-down Model Y, and (6) scale the paradigm into a full lineup of small, medium, and large AI-driven vehicles to restore high-volume production goals (he references prior aspirational goals of up to 10–20 million vehicles/year).
3. **Cybercab now live on Austin streets:** The host notes that in just the past few days, people could download the robotaxi app in Austin, Texas and ride in a Cybercab, framing this as evidence the plan is moving from concept to reality.
