SpaceX announced it has acquired xAI, forming a combined entity reportedly valued at $1.25 trillion ahead of a planned IPO; the deal excludes Tesla, which invested $2 billion in xAI last month.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Deal structure and valuations:** SpaceX, last valued at roughly $800 billion in its last secondary offering, is combining with xAI, valued at $230 billion after its recent $20 billion funding round. Musk described the merged company as a vertically-integrated venture spanning AI, rockets, space-based internet, direct-to-mobile communications, and X (the "free speech platform").
2. **Planned IPO:** The combined SpaceX-xAI entity is expected to pursue an IPO later this year that, according to Wall Street rumors cited in the article, could raise as much as $50 billion.
3. **Orbital data center ambitions:** Musk's stated vision includes building "orbital data centers" — a constellation of up to one million AI satellites powered by solar energy in space to run AI compute; SpaceX filed with the FCC last week seeking authorization to launch these satellites. Musk wrote that he estimates space will be the lowest-cost location for AI compute within 2 to 3 years.
4. **Musk's ownership stakes:** The article states Musk owns roughly 18% of Tesla, 42% of SpaceX (with 79% voting control), and a controlling stake in xAI.

## Financials
1. **SpaceX profitability vs. xAI cash burn:** The article states SpaceX reportedly generated between $2 and 5 billion in profit in 2025, while xAI is described as burning cash at a rapid rate competing with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. The author frames the acquisition as using SpaceX's profitable operations to fund xAI, though suggests the more likely goal is giving xAI investors an exit via SpaceX's IPO.
2. **Tesla's $2B investment reframed:** Tesla's recent $2 billion investment in xAI now becomes an indirect stake in the combined SpaceX-xAI entity, per the article.
3. **Existing shareholder lawsuit:** A lawsuit over Tesla's xAI investment alleged breach of fiduciary duty, claiming Musk used Tesla's balance sheet to support his private companies; the article states this acquisition does not resolve those concerns and arguably complicates the web of transactions further.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla excluded from "Musk Inc.":** Despite speculation of a three-way merger, Tesla was not included, which the article attributes to Tesla's public shareholder base and fiduciary complications of merging a public company into what was, until now, a private one. This creates two camps: SpaceX-xAI-X (space, AI, social media) versus Tesla (vehicles, energy, robotics), raising questions about whether Musk's attention will shift toward the larger entity.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert characterizes the acquisition as Musk "bailing out" a cash-burning xAI that lags competitors, comparing it to the 2016 SolarCity bailout. He argues Tesla's CEO now runs a larger, more ambitious rival for AI talent and mindshare, and calls the deal a weak vote of confidence for Tesla investors given the company's second consecutive year of declining vehicle sales. A top reader comment (56 likes) was more pointed, accusing Musk of bundling debt-laden, cash-burning companies with inflated valuations and taking them public to profit personally while leaving others exposed if valuations collapse.
