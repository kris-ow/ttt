## Robotaxi
- Unsupervised robotaxi miles hit 1 million, up from 380,000 at the Q2 call — roughly 17% weekly growth, ~2.6x in six weeks per ARK's Tasha Keeney — but Texas DMV Cybercab registrations crawled from 45 on launch day to only 49 within a week, still well behind Waymo's 200 million cumulative autonomous miles.
- NHTSA opened an audit query into Cybercab's FMVSS self-certification the same day as the September 3 launch, covering an estimated 1,000–10,000 vehicles (sources diverge); TSLA fell ~6% that Friday. Commentators split between framing it as a routine self-certification review (parallel to Zoox's eventual exemption) versus a real brake/pedal/mirror compliance risk pending a new NHTSA rule.
- Cybercab pricing swung wildly within days of launch: $7.77 vs. $12.10 for Model Y on launch day, but by the following Sunday a comparable ride hit $15 with over an hour wait versus $7.95 for UberX — a supply-constrained fleet, not a fixed discount.
- ~100 additional Cybercabs spotted stockpiled near Palo Alto HQ, fueling speculation of a California permit push as soon as Q4; Tesla's fleet-purchase interest form drew "tens of thousands" of clicks, but a confirming Tesla-engineer post was deleted, and no pricing or financing terms have been disclosed.
- Cybercabs now ship with built-in Starlink hardware; production cycle time is down to roughly 10 seconds per unit versus 34 for Model Y, with a 5-second target.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek matched Tesla's own NHTSA filings to at least four additional fatal crashes this week — a NJ stop-sign death, an Alabama fatality with a surviving infant, and an I-35 Missouri crash — all marked "Verified Engaged" with software version and narrative redacted, continuing a pattern the outlet has now documented across multiple weeks.
- Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi gave DOT until September 30 to respond on an NBC investigation finding 43 videos of apparently sleeping Tesla drivers; ties to NHTSA's existing ~3.2M-vehicle FSD probe, which has logged nine incidents (one fatality, one injury) in reduced-visibility conditions.
- Musk amplified a viral video of a 98-year-old FSD user saying he no longer watches the road ("I trust it 100%"); Electrek argues this undercuts Tesla's driver-supervision legal defense amid a $243M Florida jury verdict and up to $14.5B in cited lawsuit exposure.
- Slovenia became the sixth EU country to approve FSD Supervised; the EU-wide vote lands October 6, needing 65% population-weighted approval, with France, Sweden and Germany flagged as uncertain. FSD 14.3.9 rolled out Automatic Collision Evasion (steering, not just braking) on Hardware 4, briefly paused mid-week before resuming.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX signed a new $1.11B/month compute hosting deal starting December 1 (~$13B incremental ARR), reinforcing its ~$100B ARR target by year-end versus under $19B in all of 2025 revenue.
- Terrestrial compute ended the period at just over 2 GW, guided to 5–10 GW next year; orbital compute satellites are targeted to fly next year aiming for tens of gigawatts by 2028. Compute leasing runs $30–50/watt, with SpaceX near the high end.
- Grok 4.7 slipped a few days on reinforcement-learning tuning issues, per Musk.

## Electric Vehicles
- Next-gen Roadster unveil confirmed for October 1 in Waco, TX, with invites sent to reservation holders — following weeks of unconfirmed rumor, now dated and official.
- Tesla's European Semi specs revealed: up to 550 km range at 40 tonnes, 800 kW Megacharger charging, no side mirrors; Electrek flags the curb weight matches the US Standard Range spec, questioning whether 550 km reflects a long-range pack.
- Tesla China August retail sales fell 12.4% YoY to 50,000 units (weakest August since 2022) despite a new cash incentive and a Model Y Performance launch at RMB 369,000; Giga Berlin is running mandatory extra shifts to hit a 7,500/week target by mid-October.
- Model Y L US deliveries expected within two weeks; Model S/X production ended June 30.
