Citing new Kelley Blue Book estimates, the article reports that EV prices fell again in January 2026, led by Tesla price cuts, but overall EV sales declined rather than improved.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EV transaction prices fall:** The average transaction price for a new EV fell to $55,715 in January 2026, down 3.1% from December and 0.6% lower year-over-year, even as average prices across the broader auto market rose.
2. **Tesla's price cuts and market share:** Tesla accounted for roughly 60% of all EV sales in January, with its average transaction price dropping to $52,628 (from $53,678 in December) — 2.2% lower than January 2025, while the rest of the auto industry saw average transaction prices climb 1.9% year-over-year.
3. **Incentives decline but remain elevated:** EV incentives fell to an average of 12.4% of transaction price in January, down from a revised 18.3% in December and below the 2025 average of 13.7%, though still above broader industry norms.
4. **Sales decline despite lower prices:** Kelley Blue Book estimates just over 66,000 EVs were sold in January, down nearly 30% year-over-year and about 20% from December — though this was a smaller decline than the overall auto market's 25.4% month-over-month drop.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Michelle Lewis) concludes that price cuts alone are not pulling EV buyers back in as 2026 begins, despite Tesla continuing to drive most of the industry's pricing moves.
