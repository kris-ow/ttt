This video is a pre-earnings call preview hosted by the futureaza channel, featuring the host and guest Mark from the Tesla Life Weekly show. The two discuss the most upvoted community questions ahead of Tesla's earnings call, offering their best guesses on topics ranging from Optimus Gen 3 timelines to robotaxi expansion, FSD unsupervised milestones, and energy strategy. The tone is candid and speculative, with both hosts openly acknowledging the limits of their knowledge while framing investor expectations.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Gen 3 Reveal Timeline:** Both hosts expect the Optimus Gen 3 reveal to happen sometime in 2026, with their best guess being Q2 or Q3. They do not commit to a specific event or date beyond that general window.

2. **Optimus Production Start and Ramp:** The hosts believe Optimus production will not start before midyear 2026. They note that the Model S/X (SNX) production line is ending imminently, with a final signature series owner event tentatively pegged around May 12th, after which they expect Tesla to begin tooling up the factory for Optimus. Production volumes exiting 2026 are expected to be extremely small — the hosts suggest it may not even reach 1,000 units total by year-end, let alone 1,000 per week or per month.

3. **Optimus Initial Skill Set and Hardware:** The hosts note that early Optimus units will run on the AI4 chip rather than AI5, which will limit the initial skill set compared to future generations. They expect Tesla to discuss generalities about the third-generation hands and their improved range of motion, but do not expect detailed capability commitments before more testing is completed.

4. **Deployment Path — Factory to External Partners:** The hosts argue that Optimus deployment in Tesla's own factories should not be characterized as "testing" — rather, these are real productive roles. They speculate the external rollout would likely begin with other Elon Musk-affiliated companies, then potentially upstream suppliers (e.g., parts manufacturers, paint companies), before reaching broader commercial customers like Walmart or PepsiCo. After-hours deployment in retail environments (to avoid human-robot safety protocol complexity) is floated as a near-term use case.

5. **Robot Half-Marathon Context:** The hosts reference the second annual robot half-marathon, noting that this year had over 400 robot entries (up from roughly 100 in the first year), and that the winning robot beat the human record by approximately 57 minutes, underscoring rapid progress in robot physical capability.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Unsupervised FSD — Not Expected This Year:** Both hosts agree that unsupervised FSD reaching customer vehicles in 2026 is unlikely. They cite regulatory hurdles and the need for extensive safety data validation as key obstacles. The host notes that Elon Musk has repeatedly promised unsupervised FSD "by the end of next year" going back to around 2012–2014, adding a note of skepticism about near-term delivery.

2. **FSD Version 14.3 — Not the Final Piece:** The hosts confirm that FSD v14.3 has been released to a number of users and remains in supervised mode, and they explicitly state it is not the last version needed before unsupervised operation. They reference a future "V15" as potentially relevant but do not elaborate on its capabilities.

3. **Hardware 3 vs. Hardware 4 for Unsupervised FSD:** The hosts discuss the framing that the better question is not *when* but *how* Hardware 3 cars will reach unsupervised FSD. Tesla's stated approach is to first attempt a "light" version of FSD (referenced as "14 light" or "15 light") for Hardware 3, and if that fails, retrofit with Hardware 4. One host expresses skepticism that a light version can replicate Hardware 4 performance and predicts Tesla will ultimately replace Hardware 3 systems with Hardware 4.

4. **AI5 Chip — Dropped from Vehicle Road Map?:** The hosts address community speculation that AI5 has been removed from the vehicle road map after being confirmed for Optimus and Tesla's supercomputer. Their view is that AI5 has not been dropped from vehicles — it simply isn't critical to achieving unsupervised FSD on AI4, and will be incorporated into cars when AI4 goes out of production and AI5 becomes sufficiently available and cost-effective.

5. **FSD Approved in the Netherlands, Europe Expansion:** The hosts note that FSD has received regulatory approval in the Netherlands and is expected to launch across Europe during summer 2026. They characterize the EU regulatory path as a "patchwork quilt" of approvals, with the Netherlands approval serving as the first domino. Implications for a robotaxi strategy in Europe are acknowledged but not fully answered.

6. **Distributed Compute via Superchargers/Fleet:** The hosts discuss a community question about using Tesla vehicles and Superchargers as a distributed inference network. Based on a conversation with a chip designer, the host flags two key obstacles: data security concerns (customers paying for compute may object to data being distributed across a consumer vehicle fleet) and latency issues from vehicles being geographically dispersed and on variable Wi-Fi connections. Their conclusion is this is a longer-term possibility, not near-term.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi Geographic Expansion — Current Status:** The hosts note active robotaxi activity in several new areas beyond Austin, including Florida, Nevada, and additional locations in Texas. A Cybercab was reportedly spotted in Chicago by Rob Mau on the day of recording. The hosts expect Tesla to discuss expansion into new markets on the earnings call, likely reiterating last year's goal of reaching half the U.S. population.

2. **Revenue Expectations Remain Modest:** The hosts agree that robotaxi revenue in 2026 will be lackluster. They note that meaningful FSD recurring revenue would require unsupervised approval — which they do not expect this year — making near-term robotaxi revenue minor. They also flag NHTSA safety data filings as a topic they hope the earnings call will address, since safety validation may be the primary bottleneck to scaling the fleet.

3. **Robotaxi Expansion Timeline — Skepticism on Pace:** Both hosts maintain skepticism about rapid rollout, with the host noting he was present at the shareholder meeting when ambitious end-of-year goals were announced and immediately doubted the timeline. They expect the earnings call guidance to largely reiterate last year's targets rather than announce major acceleration.

## Energy
1. **Solar Strategy — Waiting on Tesla's Own Solar Fab:** In response to a question about stalled residential solar deployments and whether Tesla will pivot to utility-scale solar or battery farms, the host suggests Tesla is waiting for its own solar fabrication facility to reach higher throughput before aggressively scaling. He argues Tesla has unique advantages in consuming its own solar supply across parking lots, industrial rooftops, and Supercharger canopies, but that the solar fab needs to come online at scale first. The guest notes this is not Tesla's primary current focus and unlikely to be a major earnings call topic.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Vehicle — "Better Than a Minivan":** The hosts briefly address Elon Musk's posts on X teasing a new vehicle described as better than a minivan, speculated to potentially be a "Model Y L" or similar. Both expect Tesla to decline to comment during the earnings call, stating such a reveal warrants its own dedicated event.
