This episode of a SpaceX-focused show covers the upcoming Starship Flight 14 launch (carrying the first commercial Starlink V3 satellites), SpaceX's plan for a 100,000-satellite third-generation Starlink constellation with onboard Nvidia compute, new iPhone Starlink connectivity via four global carriers, and next-generation Starlink hardware — all discussed with guest Larry Goldberg, a venture capital investor.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 prepped for Monday launch:** Ship 41 was on the pad at Starbase for what the host says will be Starship's first orbital flight carrying real Starlink V3 satellites intended for commercial revenue generation, not just testing. The guest noted a recent dress rehearsal successfully fueled the ship and booster with liquid oxygen and methane in about 30 minutes, which he said is faster than fueling a Falcon 9.
2. **Rapid-fire flight cadence announced:** Flight 15 is scheduled for October 19 and Flight 16 for no earlier than October 30, with Flight 16 said to carry Starlink satellites (batch "30-1") as a full commercial launch. The guest noted the spacecraft for Flight 15 has already completed a first round of testing, and both speakers remarked that no one anticipated commercial Starlink launches via Starship this early, before December of this year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **CFO cites AI as driver of Starlink demand:** In a Golden Conference interview, SpaceX's CFO said AI-driven connectivity demand (humanoid robots, autonomous vehicles, "connection points everywhere") will vastly exceed human data consumption, and said Starship is the enabling catalyst for both this demand and SpaceX's coming direct-to-device capability, which he indicated would launch next year.
2. **FCC reviewing 100,000-satellite Gen 3 application:** SpaceX submitted an application (accepted for review around September 18) for up to 100,000 third-generation Starlink satellites across two orbital shells. Elon Musk reportedly said each satellite could deliver roughly 10 terabits of bandwidth in both directions, with a "straightforward" path to over 100 terabits, and that each satellite would carry about 250 kilowatts of power.
3. **Possible onboard Nvidia compute stirs debate:** Musk indicated the Gen 3 satellites may include an Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72-class computer designed by SpaceX, which a named space analyst (Aaron Burnett/Barnett, per the transcript) characterized as a hybrid of Starlink and the "Star Mind" orbital data center concept — a jump in bandwidth and compute density. The guest questioned this rack-vs-chip characterization, believing Elon's original announcement described a single chip for AI-driven satellite functions (navigation, debris avoidance) rather than a full compute rack, and said he considers the "combined" interpretation still unresolved.
4. **New home and mobile terminals in production:** Musk said the Starlink V5 home terminal — smaller, lighter, more energy-efficient, and rated up to 375 megabits per second — is already in production. Separately, Starlink V2 mobile/direct-to-phone hardware was described as delivering 100 times more data density/throughput than V1, intended to enable native connections and 5G-class speeds from space to unmodified phones.
5. **iPhone Starlink support confirmed by four carriers:** T-Mobile, KDDI, NTT Docomo, and Telstra confirmed Starlink connectivity for the iPhone 18 Pro series with no special hardware needed, extending coverage where cell towers don't reach. The guest expressed uncertainty over whether this covers only data or also standard voice calls, noting the difference between data transmission (already feasible on existing bands) and native voice — he could not confirm SpaceX's approach and flagged it as an open technical question.
6. **Guest revises earlier skepticism on mobile Starlink as a business line:** The guest recalled that roughly six months earlier he and the named space analyst had excluded a Starlink-to-mobile business case from their financial modeling, treating it as speculative; he now says recent statements from Musk, the SpaceX CFO, and Gwynne Shotwell indicate mobile Starlink is real and coming sooner than previously assumed, though he remains unsure whether "voice" capability means true native voice or data-based calling.
