This is a pre-event discussion between two futureaza hosts (Brian and Herbert) previewing Tesla's Cybercab launch event, debating how large the initial robotaxi rollout will be, whether the event itself will move TSLA stock, and pushing back on recurring "Tesla bear" narratives about robotaxi and Level 4 autonomy. Herbert reports live commentary while Brian frames the event as a milestone but argues that only sustained earnings growth — not the demo itself — durably moves the stock.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet ramp expectations diverge slightly between hosts:** Herbert expects the initial Cybercab robotaxi rollout to be conservative — 25 cars or fewer in the first week or two, rising gradually — to keep tight operational control during a new vehicle introduction, with rapid scaling only after that. Brian is "somewhat more bullish," setting 25 as his floor (since Tesla could simply swap existing Model Ys for Cybercabs without growing total fleet size) but noting Tesla is now expanding total ride counts, availability, and cutting wait times, suggesting genuine capacity growth rather than a swap.
2. **Texas DMV registrations point to near-term scale:** In the five days prior to the event, Tesla registered 124 new robotaxis with the Texas DMV, including 79 Model Ys and 45 Cybercabs added on August 27th (per Brian). Both hosts treat the 45 Cybercab registrations as a signal of imminent rollout, though Herbert still expects actual first-week ride-hail deployment to start around 25 vehicles even with 45 registered.
3. **Reservations unlikely at the event:** Herbert assigns a "less than 10%" probability that Tesla opens Cybercab reservations or deposits at the launch event, and Brian agrees, arguing Tesla needs full control over network utilization and would lose that control if it sold cars into private hands before a deployment plan is set. Herbert adds it will likely be "well over 2 years" before consumers can buy a Cybercab and put it into personal robotaxi service, and only in specific geofenced markets like Austin at first, with other markets potentially 3+ years out.
4. **On-the-ground Austin sightings ahead of the event:** Herbert cites videos from a source referred to as "Cyber Rima" showing many driverless Cybercabs operating at busy Austin intersections, with a recent report that essentially every car she now sees has no one in it except the rider.
5. **Texas DOT completes Level 4 certification:** Brian states that on the Monday before the event, Texas DOT confirmed Cybercab's certification as a Level 4 autonomous vehicle, which he frames as rebutting recent claims (attributed to a Tesla AI executive on social media, unnamed) that Tesla would never achieve Level 4/5 without AGI.

## Financials
1. **Stock reaction to the event expected to be muted and short-lived:** Both hosts argue product events and demos historically do not durably move TSLA — citing Battery Day, Investor Day, and AI Day 1 and 2 as examples where the stock didn't move, with Cybertruck's reveal cited as having had a negative effect on the stock at the time. Brian says any post-event pop would likely last only a few days before fading, since long-term moves come from earnings results, not events.
2. **What would actually move the stock:** Brian says the market is currently pricing in roughly 1,000–2,000 robotaxis on Texas roads by year-end; a move beyond that expectation — e.g., Tesla expressing confidence in a newer FSD version and pushing toward 1,000 cars within the next month — could be the kind of acceleration that causes real stock movement, as opposed to the launch event alone.
3. **Institutional buying noted:** Brian references a prior show covering buy notices on Tesla stock, including a purchase of $4 billion in TSLA shares by Deutsche Bank, as a sign that institutional investors are "waking up" to the thesis.
