Brian of FutureAzA and Mark of The Tesla Life review recent Tesla Semi news: a new public Megacharger in Texas, HMD Trucking taking delivery of 50 Semis, and the cost case for electric trucking. Their argument is that the Semi program is moving from pilot to real fleet deployments, though more slowly than many hoped, while heavy-truck electrification is accelerating worldwide, most sharply in China.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **First public Semi Megacharger in Texas is live:** A new Megacharger is operating just outside the Dallas metro area. The hosts see it as a standalone site, not a pilot. They say the location makes sense because land is cheaper outside the city, it is near major freight stops and existing grid capacity, and trucks can get on and off the highway easily. Other cited Megacharger sites include Hodgkins (Illinois), Urbandale (Iowa), Ogallala (Nebraska) and Riverdale (Utah). The host is unsure there are yet enough sites to cross the country without another charging network.
2. **HMD Trucking takes delivery of 50 Semis:** HMD has received all 50 Semis for regional and local work in the Chicago area. It registered the first Tesla Semi in Illinois, and the other 49 have since arrived. Mark says HMD took about two months from order to receipt and that customers receiving whole truckloads shows the plant is actually producing. How the trucks were moved to Chicago (driven or hauled) has not been disclosed, and the hosts say HMD has not answered their inquiry.
3. **HMD's cost case: $387 vs. $84 per 400-mile day:** HMD's published calculations put a 400-mile day at $387 on diesel versus $84 with the Tesla Semi. HMD has already begun publishing early operating results. The hosts add that rising diesel prices are drawing more attention to electrification.
4. **Cab is a day cab, with no sleeper:** Mark says the cab is comfortable for a 10-hour driving shift, with a central reclining driver seat. Tesla has not committed to building a sleeper. The hosts expect third-party truck upfitters to offer custom sleeper modules at extra cost, which leaves team-driving and long-haul use unresolved for now.
5. **Production ramp expectations:** Responding to a viewer question, the host says Tesla will not reach a 50,000-unit annual production pace by the end of 2026, citing parts supply as a limit. He still expects Tesla to build over 1,000 Semis in 2026.

## Market & Competition
1. **Megawatt charging expands at California ports:** Forum Mobility is adding megawatt chargers at its Port of Long Beach depot. WattEV is opening a charging station in the Oakland area and says new electric trucks deliver cost parity for fleets. Alpitronic makes chargers rated at 1,000 kW per port and has already deployed many 600 kW units. The hosts highlight that these units can split power between trucks and cars on the same line, and argue pull-through stall layouts suit trucks with trailers.
2. **China's heavy trucks are going electric fast:** In September 2026, battery-electric trucks made up 49.8% of new heavy-duty truck registrations in China (32,800 of 65,800). Only 36 hydrogen trucks were registered, an absolute count rather than a percentage. Battery-electric share rose from 13% in 2024 to 28% in 2025 and nearly 38% in the first nine months of 2026. The hosts frame this as the acceleration that tends to follow once adoption passes roughly 18–19%.
3. **Used EV demand and other electrification signals:** The hosts note that used EVs are selling faster and at higher prices than ever, because the wave of new EV sales from the past 4–5 years is only now reaching the secondary market. A Seattle shipyard has delivered its first all-electric harbor tugboat. They see tugboats, like ferries, as a natural fit because they stay near ports and spend much of their time docked, where they can charge.
