## Robotaxi

- **Qualitative milestone, not a fleet number.** The unsupervised fleet continues to expand geographically; no new city launches were reported today.
- **China pauses new robotaxi license issuance.** On March 31st, 100+ BYD Apollo Go robotaxis froze simultaneously due to a server failure; Chinese regulators responded by halting new expansion licenses and applications, though existing operators continue running.

## Autonomous Driving
- **EU FSD: no vote on May 5th; June 30th is the next opportunity.** Regulators watched roughly one hour of FSD demos but raised objections around speed-limit compliance, wildlife hazards, icy roads, and the "Full Self-Driving" name itself. Roughly 15 of 27 member states must vote in favor to pass; a broad EU rollout this summer is described as "pretty optimistic," with Q4 2026 a more realistic target.
- **Belgium fast-tracking independently.** Flemish Minister of Mobility Annick De Ridder formally requested Tesla submit its FSD dossier and explicitly instructed her administration to explore rapid homologation. Denmark's regulator said Tesla vehicles "performed very well in the complex traffic of rush hour in Copenhagen"; Sweden is "generally positive"; Switzerland's Astra has no objection to Level 2+ systems provided driver responsibility is clearly communicated.
- **FSD China employee trial now includes a ~$732K confidentiality penalty clause.** Tesla added the clause to its internal employee FSD trial policy; broader Chinese approval remains on track for Q3 2026 per Tesla's Q1 earnings guidance.
- **Momenta flagged as the key Chinese FSD competitor.** The decade-old Chinese autonomous driving software company supplies end-to-end neural-net software (similar architecture to FSD) to 50+ production vehicle models representing hundreds of thousands of vehicles; investors include GM, Mercedes, and Tencent; a strategic partnership with Uber targets a European launch.
- **Tesla Semi described as "autonomy ready" at ACT Expo.** A walkaround by Sawyer Merritt at the Las Vegas show noted Tesla's own spec language explicitly calling out the truck's camera suite under that label. Over 62% of Tesla's 10 billion accumulated FSD miles are highway miles — the operating environment where trucks spend most of their time.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Semi high-volume production line live in Nevada.** Tesla Semi's official account posted "First Semi off high volume line" on April 29th, accumulating 2.1 million views. The 1.7 million sq ft Sparks facility is designed for 50,000 trucks/year; 2026 output is estimated at 5,000–15,000 units by analysts.
- **430+ Semi trucks ordered in roughly one week.** Watt EV: 370 units (~$100M), 50 delivering in 2026 with 300+ under a Port of Oakland joint program. Nica Container Freight: 20 units, operational by early 2027. Big F Transport: 40 units after a single demo truck. DHL has additional orders beyond a handful. All short- and long-range variants are priced below the $300K threshold analysts cite as the economic break-even for electric Class 8 trucks.
- **Washington WAZIP rebate cuts long-range Semi price to ~$115K.** The state's new Zero Emission Incentive Program offers up to $175,000 off qualifying heavy-duty electric trucks, dropping the $290K long-range Semi to roughly $115K — approximately $60K below the average new diesel Class 8. California's comparable $200K voucher has already been claimed for 900 Tesla Semis.
- **New Tesla Semi charging suite removes depot infrastructure barrier.** Launched May 1st: the Mega Charger (1.2 MW, 60% charge in 30 min, from ~$188K per two-post setup) targets freight corridors; the Base Charger (125 kW, 60% in ~4 hours, $40K for two units installed) is designed for overnight depot use and can daisy-chain three units on a single breaker without a major utility upgrade. Tesla has mapped 66 planned Mega Charger locations across 15 states; a Pilot Flying J deal targets 46 stations by early 2027.
- **Canada Model 3 RWD launches at ~$29K USD (~$39,990 CAD).** Tesla quietly listed the Shanghai-built, LFP-battery Model 3 Premium on its Canadian site — down from a prior ~$79,990 CAD — enabled by Canada's quota allowing 49,000 Chinese-built vehicles at reduced tariffs in 2026 (growing to ~70,000/year). The same car lists at $42,490 USD in the US. Delivery estimates have already slipped from June to October 2026 on strong order intake; Chinese EV brands have no Canadian sales infrastructure to compete for the quota this year.
- **Model Y L mule spotted on I-280 in North America.** A camouflaged Model Y Long was photographed northbound; the vehicle also received a five-star ANCAP safety rating. A US launch is described as feeling "imminent" by multiple commentators.
- **Tesla brand loyalty #1 in the US at 61.1%.** S&P Global Mobility data for February 2026 places Tesla first, ahead of Subaru, Toyota, and Ferrari — sharply contradicting August 2025 headlines claiming brand damage from Elon Musk's political activities.
- **Cybertruck Premium AWD lease raised ~10–12%.** New monthly rates: $1,100/month with $0 down (up ~10%), $949/month with $5,000 down (up ~12%); Cyberbeast pricing unchanged. Direction of change suggests improved demand or margin management.
- **Model Y ranked #1 globally among EVs in March 2026; #1 overall in Norway Q1 2026.** Norway Model Y: 5,406 units in Q1, outselling the next five non-Tesla vehicles combined; BEVs captured 97.9% market share in Norway for the quarter. Model Y also #1 among all cars in Colombia for two consecutive months.
- **FSD one-time purchase ending in Europe on May 21st.** Affected markets include the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark, and others — consistent with Tesla's North American shift to subscriptions.
- **Lucid Q1 2026: revenue $282M vs. $440M expected; net loss -$1B.** Lucid is cutting production due to elevated inventory. The result reinforces the difficulty competitors face scaling profitably alongside Tesla.

## Energy
- **$225M Megapack BESS under construction in South Australia.** The 200MW / 800MWh Kulonga project (developer: Equis Development / Uno Ekis) is scheduled for commissioning by September 2027, adding meaningfully to Tesla's Energy segment pipeline.
- **Tesla Australia: battery storage now 55% of revenue, up from 15% in 2023.** The shift came despite a substantial fall in battery prices over the period, illustrating volume-driven growth in the Energy segment. Tesla also earned its first $3M in Australian regulatory credits under the country's new vehicle emissions standards, with tightening annual targets expected to grow that income stream.
- **Solar and wind now at cost parity with fossil fuels.** Solar and wind generation costs $54–$82/MWh versus $70–$85 for new Chinese coal capacity and $100+ for new gas globally — a structural tailwind for battery storage demand.

## Market & Competition
- **SpaceX leases Colossus One to Anthropic.** xAI merged into SpaceX and migrated training to Colossus 2, freeing Colossus One's 220,000+ Nvidia Blackwell B200 GPUs and 300 MW of capacity for Anthropic — coming online within May 2026. Bhakdi frames this as the formation of a "magic trinity" (Nvidia, SpaceX, Anthropic) and the first commercial step toward orbital AI compute, which he projects reaches significant scale by 2028 and hyperscale before 2030.
- **Goldman Sachs: autonomous trucking is a larger market than robotaxi by 2035.** Goldman projects robotaxi at $415B and autonomous trucking at $560B by 2035. Cost per mile for autonomous trucks is projected to fall from $8.56 today to ~$2.30 by 2035, with the crossover versus human-driven trucks around 2028.
- **Bhakdi's stock thesis: $400 Tesla = ~$90 auto + ~$310 optionality.** He draws a parallel to the 2014–2019 Tesla plateau (roughly $18 to $17 over five years, then +700% in one year once Model Y scalability was confirmed) and argues robotaxi scaling will trigger a comparable instantaneous re-rating — not incremental repricing. He expects robotaxi at "super scale" by 2027 at the latest.
- **SpaceX Terafab chip fab proposed at $55B–$119B in Grimes County, Texas.** A public hearing is scheduled for June 3rd. Multiple commentators note Elon's public comment on the location is likely a negotiating tactic to encourage competing bids.
- **Nuro receives modified driverless DMV permit covering Lucid Gravity SUVs.** Lucid delivered 75 engineering vehicles to Nuro for testing using Nvidia Drive AGX Thor compute. However, Nuro still requires a CPUC ride-hailing permit and a separate commercial DMV permit before paid rides — the same gap that applies to Tesla. Multiple commentators are skeptical of Nuro's proprietary technology given its history of scrapping its low-speed delivery program.
