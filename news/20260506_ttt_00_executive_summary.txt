## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Semi high-volume production line goes live:** Tesla's Semi-dedicated factory in Sparks, Nevada — 1.7M sq ft, capacity 50,000 trucks/year — posted its first unit off the high-volume line on April 29, 2026. At ~$275K average price, full-ramp output represents ~$14B in annual revenue. 2026 production is estimated at 5,000–15,000 units by analysts.

- **Watt EV orders 370 Semis (~$100M) for Port of Oakland program:** The largest single electric Class 8 deployment in California history. First 50 units deliver in 2026, full fleet by end of 2027; 300+ trucks support a joint Port of Oakland drayage program. Charging depots planned at Oakland, Fresno, Stockton, and Sacramento, with 15 additional sites under development. Watt EV CEO: "The Semi was the easiest choice, based on cost, performance, and availability."

- **Big F Transport and Nika Container Freight add 60 more Semis:** Big F Transport trialed one Semi and immediately ordered 40, citing driver comfort, Scope 3 targets, and TCO versus diesel. Nika Container Freight ordered 20, targeting operations by early 2027. Both fleets will charge at Form Mobility's Rancho Dominguez depot. Total publicly announced orders in the past week exceed 430 trucks.

- **Washington WAZIP rebate makes long-range Semi cheaper than diesel upfront:** Washington State's Zero Emission Incentive Program offers up to $175,000 off qualifying heavy-duty EVs, funded by the Climate Commitment Act ($120M pool, applied at point of sale). The $290K long-range Semi drops to ~$115K — roughly $60K below a new diesel Class 8 at ~$175K, before fuel or maintenance savings. California already has a comparable $200K voucher; 900 of those have gone to Tesla Semi.

- **New Semi charging product suite removes depot infrastructure barrier:** Tesla launched "Semi Charging for Business" on May 1, 2026: a Mega Charger (1.2 MW, 60% charge in 30 min, from ~$188K) for freight corridors, and a Base Charger (125 kW, 60% in ~4 hours, $40K for two installed) for overnight depot use — daisy-chainable up to three units on one breaker without a utility upgrade. Deliveries begin early 2027. Jordan from The Limiting Factor: "With a base charger as an option, I'm no longer concerned that the charging network will be the rate limiting factor for semi production." Tesla has mapped 66 planned Mega Charger locations across 15 states; a Pilot Flying J deal targets 46 stations by early 2027.

- **Tesla Semi confirmed "autonomy ready" at ACT Expo:** A walkaround at ACT Expo (May 4–7, Las Vegas) showed two redesigned Semis with cameras explicitly called out on the spec sheet under "autonomy ready." A 2026 Spring software update also enabled European-style semi-truck visualizations in the FSD display.

- **Canada Model 3 launches at ~$29K USD via Chinese import quota:** Tesla listed a Shanghai-built, LFP-battery Model 3 Premium on its Canadian site overnight at ~$39,990 CAD (~$29K USD) — down from ~$79,990 CAD previously. The gap versus the US price ($42,490 USD) exceeds $13,000. Pricing is enabled by Canada's quota allowing 49,000 Chinese-built vehicles at reduced tariffs in 2026 (growing to ~70,000/year). Delivery estimates have already slipped from June to October 2026, indicating strong order intake. Chinese EV brands (BYD, Geely) have no Canadian sales infrastructure to compete for the quota.

- **Model Y L mule spotted on I-280 in North America:** A camouflaged Model Y Long mule was photographed on I-280 northbound, consistent with expected Q2 2026 testing timing. Tesla has not made a formal US announcement, which the host attributes to avoiding an Osborne effect on current Model Y sales. A five-star ANCAP safety rating for the Model Y L has already been confirmed.

- **Tesla brand loyalty #1 in the US at 61.1%:** S&P Global Mobility February 2026 data puts Tesla at 61.1% retention, ahead of Subaru, Toyota, and Ferrari — sharply contradicting mid-2025 narratives of brand damage.

- **Cybertruck Premium AWD lease raised ~10–12%:** New rates are $1,100/month at $0 down and $949/month with $5,000 down. Cyberbeast pricing unchanged. The direction suggests improved demand or margin management on that trim.

- **Tesla FSD one-time purchase ending in Europe May 21st:** The option to buy FSD outright ends across the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark, and others on May 21, 2026 — consistent with Tesla's North America shift to subscriptions.

- **Norway Q1 2026: Model Y #1 overall, BEV share hits 97.9%:** Model Y sold 5,406 units in Q1 — outselling the next five non-Tesla vehicles combined. Model 3 ranked #2. BEVs captured 97.9% market share in Norway for the quarter.

- **Lucid Q1 2026: revenue $282M vs. $440M expected, net loss -$1B:** Lucid guided for production cuts due to inventory buildup. The severe miss reinforces the difficulty competitors face scaling profitably against Tesla.

## Autonomous Driving

- **EU FSD vote missed at May 5th TCMV meeting:** Regulators watched ~one hour of FSD demos covering European traffic and mixed weather but raised concerns over occasional speed limit exceedances, wildlife hazards (moose in Nordic countries), icy roads, and the "Full Self-Driving" name itself. There was also reported pushback linked to political pressure from Tesla supporters contacting officials. The next vote opportunity is June 30, 2026; if missed, the following window is October. Roughly 15 of 27 member states must vote in favor.

- **Belgium initiates fast-track FSD homologation:** Flemish Minister of Mobility Annick De Ridder formally requested Tesla submit its FSD dossier to the Flemish administration and directed it to explore "rapid homologation." Denmark's regulator stated vehicles "performed very well in the complex traffic of rush hour in Copenhagen." Sweden's transport agency said it is "generally positive on automated driving tech as long as it follows regulations." Switzerland's Astra said it has no issue with Level 2+ systems provided Tesla communicates that the driver remains fully responsible.

- **FSD in China: employee trial with ~$732K confidentiality penalty; Q3 approval targeted:** An unverified account reported Tesla made FSD 14.3.2 available to employees in China with full functionality. Tesla added a confidentiality clause to its employee FSD trial policy carrying a ~$732,000 penalty for violations. Tesla guided on its Q1 earnings call for broader FSD approval in China in Q3 2026.

- **Highway miles dominate FSD training dataset — key for autonomous trucking:** Tesla has crossed 10 billion FSD miles; over 62% are highway miles. The host of brighterwithherbert argues this makes autonomous highway freight a more data-mature problem than urban robotaxi, potentially compressing the autonomous trucking timeline relative to conventional assumptions.

- **China pauses new robotaxi license issuance after Apollo Go mass freeze:** On March 31, 100+ BYD Apollo Go robotaxis froze simultaneously due to a server connectivity failure. Chinese regulators responded by pausing issuance of new robotaxi expansion licenses and new applications; existing approved operators may continue.

- **Bhakdi: robotaxi is the near-term stock re-rating catalyst:** Bhakdi argues the market will not incrementally reprice Tesla as the fleet grows — it will instantly discount a trajectory toward tens of millions of vehicles once scalability is demonstrated, similar to the ~700% move when Model Y's scalability was confirmed.
