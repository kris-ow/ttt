Brand Finance's 2026 Global 500 ranking finds Tesla's brand value fell to $27.61 billion, a 36% drop in 2025 and the third consecutive annual decline, now less than half its January 2023 peak of $66.2 billion.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Multi-year brand value collapse:** Brand Finance's data shows Tesla's brand value falling each year: $66.2B (2023 peak), $58.3B in 2024 (-12%), $43.0B in 2025 (-26%), and $27.6B in 2026 (-36%). The firm bases its ranking on financials, licensing agreements, and consumer surveys across 18 countries.

2. **U.S. recommendation score plunge:** Tesla's U.S. recommendation score fell to 4.0 out of 10, down from 8.2 just over two years ago, indicating American consumers are now unlikely to recommend the brand to others.

3. **Causes cited by Brand Finance:** CEO David Haigh attributed the decline to three factors: a lack of innovative new models, Tesla's relatively high prices versus competitors, and Elon Musk's "overreach" into geopolitics combined with insufficient focus on the auto business, including his political activities such as running DOGE in the Trump White House and endorsing far-right figures in Germany and the UK.

4. **Competitive positioning:** BYD's brand value rose 23% to $17.29 billion, moving opposite to Tesla's trend. Five automakers — Toyota ($62.7B), Mercedes-Benz, Volkswagen, Porsche, and BMW — now rank above Tesla in brand value.

5. **Loyalty holds despite reputation drop:** Tesla's owner loyalty score rose from 90% to 92% in 2025, meaning existing owners remain willing to keep driving their cars, even as new-customer acquisition appears to be suffering.

6. **Stock vs. brand divergence:** Tesla stock gained 11% in 2025 (though it underperformed most major indexes) and hit record highs in December on robotaxi-related enthusiasm, despite the company reporting declining deliveries for both Q4 and the full year — a divergence the article says is widening between brand health and stock price.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the brand decline was predictable given what he describes as Tesla alienating much of its potential customer base, and contends that brand damage — unlike stock price — is very difficult to rebuild once trust is lost. He argues the Tesla vehicles themselves (Model 3, Model Y) haven't gotten worse, but that brand perception is now driven by what he calls a "toxic CEO" rather than the product, a problem he says can't be fixed with a software update. He notes Tesla reports Q4 earnings the same day and says brand value is unlikely to come up on the call but should.
