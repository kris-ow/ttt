Tesla has taken out $30 billion in new credit lines from Citi and Wells Fargo, a move the article ties to declining profitability and rising capital expenditures.

## Financials
1. **$30 billion in new credit facilities:** Tesla filed a regulatory disclosure showing it opened $30 billion in loan facilities from Citi and Wells Fargo, with terms ranging from one to five years. This replaces a previous $5 billion credit line that Tesla had filed for but never drew on.

2. **Revenue and sales growth slowdown:** After roughly a decade and a half of consistent growth, Tesla's growth rate fell from 38% in a prior year to a 1% sales decline in 2024, which the article marks as the turning point for profitability pressure.

3. **Thin recent profits and negative cash flow:** Tesla's profits have been in the "few hundreds of millions" per quarter in recent quarters, and the article states Tesla was cash flow negative last quarter for the first time since Q1 2024 (when it saw a large sales drop), after years of being cash flow positive.

4. **Rising capital expenditures:** Tesla's CapEx more than doubled in the last quarter, and the company expects to spend a total of $25 billion in 2026, up from $8.5 billion in 2025; analysts expect similar CapEx levels in 2027. Tesla says CapEx will remain high given the broader environment of heavy tech-sector spending.

5. **Cash on hand vs. new debt:** Despite holding about $43 billion in cash as of its last quarterly report, Tesla opened the new credit line, which the article attributes to funding operating activities and various product initiatives that may not generate revenue soon.

## Editorial Stance
The author is skeptical of Tesla's spending, characterizing recent capital allocation as chasing "pie-in-the-sky ideas" (steering-wheel-less cars, flying cars with no performance benefit, humanoid robots), and argues Tesla has "little to show" for increased CapEx so far — noting Cybercab, Semi, and Roadster were all announced roughly a decade ago, with Cybercab "still doesn't work," Semi slow to ramp, and Roadster framed by the author as existing mainly to support SpaceX merger hype. The author questions whether any of Tesla's future product bets will pan out or whether more financing will be needed beyond this $30B loan.
