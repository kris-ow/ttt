Electrek reports that Tesla has filed a patent describing a vehicle roof assembly using radio-frequency-transparent materials, which would allow a satellite antenna to be hidden inside the car's roof rather than mounted externally.

## SpaceX — Starlink / Electric Vehicles
1. **Patent details:** The filing, titled "Vehicle Roof Assembly with Radio Frequency Transparent Material," describes using polymer blends such as polycarbonate or acrylonitrile styrene acrylate in the roof structure to let satellite signals pass through, since traditional glass and metal roofs block or attenuate them. The patent states this enables integrating "antennae directly into the roof structure, facilitating clear communication with external devices and satellites."

2. **Starlink link is inferred, not stated:** The patent does not name Starlink directly — patents typically don't name specific commercial products — but the article says the implications point toward SpaceX's satellite network, given it could support the high-bandwidth needs of a connected vehicle. The article notes some owners have already retrofitted Starlink Mini dishes onto Teslas for off-grid use, and speculates a factory-integrated, fully hidden antenna could let a Tesla switch seamlessly between 5G cellular and satellite connectivity depending on coverage.

3. **Purpose unclear:** The article states the reason for this integration "is still unclear."

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's editorial take (via the article's framing and top comment) is skeptical that cars need satellite connectivity, noting autonomous driving is generally not designed to rely on constant internet connections due to reliability risk, and that cellular service is adequate for most driving. The author says such a feature would make most sense as an option for staying connected in remote areas. A highlighted reader comment speculates the motivation could be cost-related — bringing connectivity "in house" so Tesla/Musk-affiliated SpaceX earns the recurring connectivity revenue instead of paying cellular carriers like AT&T.
