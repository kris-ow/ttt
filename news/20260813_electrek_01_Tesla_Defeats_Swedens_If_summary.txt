Sweden's IF Metall union has called off its strike against Tesla after 1,021 days — the longest labor conflict in modern Swedish history — saying it is stopping because Tesla bought out every one of its striking members, leaving the union with no one left to represent.

## Market & Competition
1. **Strike origins and escalation:** The dispute began October 27, 2023, when roughly 120 Tesla mechanics across seven Swedish workshops walked out after Tesla refused to sign a kollektivavtal (Swedish collective bargaining agreement). Other unions joined in solidarity: dockworkers in Sweden, Denmark, Norway, and Finland refused to unload Teslas, PostNord stopped delivering license plates, and electricians and cleaners refused to service Tesla facilities. Tesla routed around the blockade by importing cars through Germany via Trelleborg and obtaining plates through the courts; Elon Musk called the Swedish labor model "insane."

2. **How the strike ended:** IF Metall says Tesla "bought out" all remaining striking members individually until no one remained on the picket line, ending the strike's effect. Conflict measures end at 00:01 on August 19. LO agreement secretary Veli-Pekka Säikkälä told Ny Teknik that Tesla "has gigantic economic resources and has used them, among other things, to buy out the strikers," calling it "unique." Union chair Marie Nilsson called the striking members "union heroes," and contract secretary Simon Petersson said Tesla "is so opposed to collective agreements that they would rather buy out union members than offer secure conditions." Tesla still has not signed a collective agreement.

3. **Sales trajectory in Sweden:** The article states the Model Y was Sweden's best-selling car overall in both 2023 and 2024 throughout the strike, with Tesla registering 21,894 vehicles in Sweden in 2024, its best year there. Registrations then collapsed to 7,252 in 2025, a roughly 67% drop, which the article attributes to political backlash against Musk's activities rather than the labor dispute; Sweden's registration data show Tesla down 63% year-over-year through the first seven months of 2025. 2026 monthly registrations show partial recovery: 512 in January, 553 in February, 1,784 in March, 429 in April, and 858 in May — still well below the pre-backlash peak.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls this a clean loss for IF Metall, arguing Tesla's strategy of individually buying out strikers sets a troubling precedent other foreign employers in Sweden may now emulate. He argues the sales data undermines the union's core theory that the strike would pressure Tesla economically — Tesla's Swedish sales thrived during the worst of the blockade and only collapsed later due to Musk's politics, not labor action. He raises the question of whether this outcome emboldens Tesla to take a similar posture against IG Metall in Germany, where the union has been active around Tesla's Grünheide plant.
