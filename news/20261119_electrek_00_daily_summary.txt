Electrek's only article today covers Elon Musk's DealBook Summit comments about a steering-wheel-free, pedal-free Model Y, and argues the idea makes little commercial or regulatory sense.

## Robotaxi
1. **Driverless Model Y for groups:** Electrek reports Musk said at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday that Tesla plans "an autonomous version of the Model Y that won't have steering wheels or pedals," pitched as the answer to the Cybercab carrying only two people; he described it as good for transporting groups of 6 or 7. No timeline, price or build location was given. Per Electrek, the context includes Tesla's robotaxi fleet being mostly Model Ys with steering wheels, Cybercab personal orders opening at $29,990 plus a $299 monthly subscription, and NHTSA's September Special Order over Tesla's self-certification of the Cybercab against FMVSS 135 (Zoox took the Part 555 exemption route, capped at 2,500 vehicles a year). Citing Cox Automotive's 2026 study, only 10% of US consumers pick fully driverless Level 5 as most appealing (17% in 2016), Level 4 leads at 28%, and 38% would feel safe in a Level 5 vehicle versus 87% for a modern car with basic driver aids. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/19/musk-tesla-model-y-no-steering-wheel-7-seat/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek, in Fred Lambert's "Electrek's Take," calls this "yet another vehicle nobody asked for." It argues that Musk repeatedly answers lineup gaps with autonomy, that keeping a steering wheel costs Tesla almost nothing, and that removing it only adds a federal compliance fight and a geofence-limited market.
