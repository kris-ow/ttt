Tesla raised Cybertruck prices by $5,000 on two of its three trims via a quiet configurator update on August 24, even as the truck sells at a small fraction of its factory's built capacity.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Price increase details:** Tesla updated the Cybertruck configurator on the night of August 24. The Dual Motor AWD rose from $69,990 to $74,990 (a 7.1% increase), and the Premium AWD rose from $79,990 to $84,990; the Cyberbeast stayed at $99,990. No specs changed — the Dual Motor still lists 325 miles of range, 7,500 lbs towing, and a 4.1-second 0-60 mph time. The increase narrows the gap between Premium and Cyberbeast from $20,000 to $15,000.

2. **Weak demand and underutilized capacity:** Gigafactory Texas's Cybertruck line was built for up to 250,000 units/year but is currently selling fewer than 20,000 per year — under 10% of capacity, per the article. Tesla has been throttling production to limit inventory buildup, and roughly 3,000 trucks reportedly remain in US inventory. The article states that in Q4 2025, SpaceX alone bought 1,279 Cybertrucks, and Musk-controlled entities took 1,339 of the 7,071 units registered in the US that quarter; excluding those purchases, the article calculates registrations would have fallen 51% year-over-year. The article also notes Ford sold more F-150 Lightnings than Tesla sold Cybertrucks, despite Ford canceling the Lightning for insufficient sales.

3. **Volatile pricing history:** Tesla previously sold a single-motor Long Range RWD Cybertruck at $69,990 before discontinuing it after 173 sales. In February, Tesla offered a $59,990 dual-motor AWD variant advertised as available "only for the next 10 days," then pulled it almost immediately; that trim later settled at $69,990 before this week's rise to $74,990 — a $15,000 swing on essentially the same vehicle within months.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the price hike is illogical given weak demand and that Tesla should be cutting prices, not raising them; he notes clear demand existed near $60,000 but questions who remains as a buyer at $75,000. He characterizes Musk's earlier earnings-call comments — dismissing a production shutdown and floating commercial cargo use if consumer demand dries up — as avoidance of admitting the product's underperformance rather than a real plan.
