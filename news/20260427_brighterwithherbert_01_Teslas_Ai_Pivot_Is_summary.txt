This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg, a venture capital investor, discussing Tesla's strategic pivot from an automaker to a "physical AI company" following Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call. The core thesis is that Tesla is now firmly positioned as a physical AI company — encompassing FSD, robotaxi, Optimus robots, custom chips, and energy — and that institutional investors have not yet fully priced this in, creating a significant valuation opportunity. The hosts argue that a perceptual shift among mainstream investors is underway but will take 6–12 months to fully materialize, driven by catalysts such as a SpaceX IPO, robotaxi city expansions, and Optimus factory progress.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD as the Consumer-Facing Proof Point:** Larry Goldberg argues that Full Self-Driving is increasingly becoming a reality that the public can witness firsthand, noting that it is very difficult to ignore a robotaxi passing by with no driver in it. He describes FSD adoption as widening and suggests Tesla is now marketing its vehicles as "Full Self-Driving first," with the car described as merely the delivery mechanism for the autonomous experience. The hosts note that Grok integration in Tesla vehicles is rolling out via a spring software update, including a "Hey Grok" wake word, which they believe will further raise consumer awareness of Tesla's AI capabilities.

2. **Robotaxi City Expansion as the Key Catalyst:** Dan Ives of Wedbush is quoted referencing Houston and Dallas as cities being discussed for robotaxi expansion, with the suggestion that adding another five cities would mark a meaningful scaling milestone. The hosts speculate Tesla may target roughly 12 states and 20-plus cities, with an estimated 200 to 500 cars per city by year-end 2026. Larry notes Tesla is currently running a supervised service in Palo Alto and that removing the safety drivers is the next step. He also argues that launching in Silicon Valley is strategically important because that is where institutional investors are concentrated.

## Robotaxi

1. **Fleet Size Projections:** Larry Goldberg predicts Tesla will have a "very large" robotaxi fleet within one to one-and-a-half years — estimating somewhere in the range of half a million to three-quarters of a million cars on the road. He separately estimates that within two years, approximately 50 million people will have ridden in a Tesla robotaxi, which he believes will be the inflection point for mainstream consumer and investor awareness.

2. **X App as the Robotaxi On-Ramp:** The hosts theorize that the X app will become the primary consumer interface for hailing Tesla robotaxis, bundled with Starlink subscriptions and Grok AI. They argue this is how Tesla transitions from a low-frequency car purchase into a high-frequency consumer platform, drawing comparisons to OpenAI's 800 million monthly users and suggesting the combined Tesla/SpaceX/X entity could eventually serve billions of consumers — more than iPhone or Netflix at their peaks.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Factory Conversion Timeline:** Herbert notes that Elon Musk stated the Fremont factory conversion to Optimus production will take approximately four months — two months to dismantle the existing line and two months to build the new one — with large-scale factory preparation beginning "next quarter" (i.e., Q2 2026). Giga Texas is reportedly being designed for a capacity of 10 million robots per year, while the Fremont line is being built for 1 million robots per year. Herbert interprets these announcements as not representing a delay, but rather a deliberate decision to withhold the Optimus Gen 3 reveal until it is closer to production readiness.

2. **Optimus Gen 3 Reveal Timing:** Herbert notes that Elon Musk originally indicated an April 2026 reveal for Optimus Gen 3, then pushed it to June 2026, citing competitive concerns (not wanting competitors to copy the design prematurely) and a preference to reveal it closer to production. Larry expresses some skepticism about the timeline, suggesting "Elon time" means the four-to-six month range is more realistic than the two-month conversion estimate. Walter Isaacson is cited as saying that the physical world AI represented by Optimus and self-driving cars will dwarf what LLMs have delivered so far.

3. **Terafab Chip Plant:** The hosts reference Terafab, described as a large chip-building plant being developed in Austin, reportedly in collaboration between Tesla and SpaceX. A research chip fab groundbreaking was also noted as occurring "this week" (relative to the video's publish date). Dojo 3 is mentioned as already in development. These infrastructure moves are cited as evidence that Tesla is building the full stack of a physical AI company, not just an automaker.

## Financials

1. **Q1 2026 Earnings Highlights:** Canaccord Genuity raised their price target to $450 from $420 following Q1 results, citing strong margins and Tesla posting its highest Q1 order backlog in over two years, aided in part by higher gas prices. Dan Ives of Wedbush maintained a $600 price target and described it as an "overall good quarter," calling Tesla a "physical AI stalwart" now forming. He noted capex is rising but framed this as necessary for the autonomous and Optimus roadmap. A 17% margin figure is referenced in Dan Ives' commentary as something to watch.

2. **Price Targets and Valuation Re-Rating:** Multiple analyst price targets are cited: Dan Ives (Wedbush) at $600, Canaccord Genuity at $450, and Gene Munster quoted as saying traditional auto is no longer Tesla's competition. Larry Goldberg personally targets $550 by mid-2026 (June), calling it "easily attainable," and sees $600 within the coming 12 months. He describes a potential "earthquake" of perceptual change coinciding with the SpaceX IPO, which he believes will cause a halo effect that boosts Tesla's valuation. He estimates the combined Tesla/SpaceX entity could be worth approximately $4 trillion.

3. **AI Acquisition Disclosure:** The hosts note that Tesla disclosed a new AI-sector acquisition in its 10-Q filing, described as appearing in the first line of the filing. Larry characterizes it as not material enough to require a separate announcement, suggesting the acquired company is currently losing money, but notes Tesla obtained an option on the company and its technology.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla as the Only Profitable AI Company:** Larry Goldberg argues Tesla is uniquely positioned as the most profitable AI company on earth, contrasting it with traditional AI companies that generate large losses. He notes that institutional investors are not accustomed to seeing profits from AI companies, which contributes to the market's undervaluation of Tesla's AI assets. Gene Munster is cited as explicitly stating that traditional auto manufacturers are no longer Tesla's relevant competitive set.

2. **SpaceX IPO and Potential Tesla Merger:** Walter Isaacson is quoted saying he "definitely" thinks Tesla and SpaceX will end up merging, pointing to their joint work on Terafab, shared engineering resources, and Elon Musk's pattern of moving engineers and designs between his companies. The hosts theorize a SpaceX IPO could occur around June 2026, followed potentially by a merger announcement a few months later. Larry argues the SpaceX IPO will act as a major catalyst for re-rating Tesla, as it will heighten public consciousness of the broader AI ecosystem Elon is building.

3. **Speculative Apple Stake Thesis:** Larry Goldberg advances a speculative thesis that Elon Musk could acquire approximately 10% of Apple within roughly three months, using an estimated $130–140 billion in available cash from asset sales and company valuations. He argues Apple is "virgin territory" for AI given the weakness of Siri, that Apple is currently buying AI from Google's Gemini, and that a transition in Apple's executive leadership creates a narrow window of opportunity. The hosts frame this as a way to give Tesla/X ecosystem access to Apple's massive user base and private data pool for agentic AI development.
