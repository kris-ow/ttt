This episode of FutureAzA features host Brian and guest Herbert discussing the rapid expansion of Tesla's Full Self-Driving regulatory approvals across Europe, with multiple countries following the Netherlands' lead in quick succession. The core thesis is that European FSD approvals are snowballing — creating a "copy-paste" effect where early approvals make it easier and faster for subsequent countries to sign on. The hosts also discuss FSD's qualitative capabilities, the vision-vs-lidar debate, and broader Tesla market momentum.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Netherlands Approval Triggers European Domino Effect:** The Netherlands became the first European country to formally approve Tesla's Full Self-Driving (supervised), following approximately 150,000 kilometers of testing over roughly a year and a half. According to Case Roland, once the Netherlands approved it, other countries can effectively "copy-paste" the approval, dramatically lowering the barrier for subsequent nations. A broader EU member-state vote — involving approximately 28 countries — is expected in June or possibly September 2026.

2. **Lithuania Approves FSD; Greece, Belgium, and Ireland in Motion:** Lithuania became the second European country to approve FSD shortly after the Netherlands. Greece had been planning to be second but was narrowly beaten out and is expected to formally approve soon. Belgium's Flemish region announced plans to conduct 5,000 km of testing before approving, with the second Belgian region also signaling intent to follow. Ireland has also made noise about moving forward. Tesla is reported to be actively engaging individual country governments to facilitate approvals.

3. **FSD Now Available in Approximately 10 Regions Globally:** Herbert notes that FSD has now been approved in roughly 10 regions worldwide, though Brian clarifies this counts regions rather than strictly sovereign nations (e.g., Puerto Rico counted separately). Herbert describes extensive personal experience using FSD throughout Vancouver, Canada with no issues, and credits those demonstrations as the reason his sister decided to purchase a Model Y.

4. **Vision-Over-Lidar Argument: Intelligence vs. Sensors:** The hosts make a sustained argument that FSD's camera-based, AI-driven approach is superior to lidar-dependent systems like Waymo and Zoox. The key claim is that Tesla's system has a "smarter brain" — it can identify what objects are (a cow, a moose, a cat), understand intent (e.g., a car beginning to turn), and act preemptively, whereas lidar-based systems see objects but may not classify or respond to them as intelligently. Herbert references a Swami (likely referring to a Tesla AI executive) statement from roughly 18–24 months prior, in which he said FSD can identify fog as fog and therefore navigate through it — whereas lidar sees only an opaque wall.

5. **FSD Subscriber Growth and Paid Adoption:** Brian notes that paid FSD subscribers increased by 170,000 last quarter and expects that trend to continue as more units reach more countries.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Market Share Milestones in Emerging Markets:** Brian highlights that Tesla is the leading import in both South Korea and Japan, and — most notably — the number one car overall (not just EV) in Colombia, which he describes as a significant milestone. He expresses surprise at Tesla's dominance in these newer and emerging markets and expects 2026 to be a stronger sales year than 2025.
