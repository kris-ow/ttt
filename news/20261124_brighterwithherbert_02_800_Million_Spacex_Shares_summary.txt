The video examines the December 8 expiry of roughly 800 million SpaceX (SPCX) shares, about $100B at Friday's close, and asks whether to sell ahead of it or buy. The host argues the unlock is the smaller part of the question. The larger part is whether the $100B run-rate guidance is already priced in, and he concludes it largely is at roughly 17x the December run rate.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **What unlocks and why it is 800 million:** The December group is pre-IPO holders, mostly employees and early investors, on the standard 180-day lockup, about 6% of the company. The total is higher than some trackers' roughly 330M because a prospectus bonus early release (triggered if the stock hit about 30% above the IPO price, around $175, in early August) never triggered. The all-time low of about $105 came on August 3, so roughly 455M extra shares rolled into December. Musk's shares sit on a separate 366-day lockup, running to about June 12 next year.
2. **Unlock history:** SpaceX staggered nine unlocks rather than releasing everything at once. The first was August 6 (a little over 900M shares), followed by five of about 330M each and the largest on October 22 (about 1.3B shares). Close to 4B shares are already sellable, almost five times what remains for December. The stock rose 6% on August 6, rose about 16% the next day and finished August up 32%, after dropping hard on the Q2 report two days earlier. It fell 9% to about $145 on October 22, a day after a roughly 8% earnings pop, and was below the $135 IPO price by October 30. The host cites a Field and Hanka study of nearly 2,000 lockup expirations that found an average dip of about 1.5% over three days and trading volume up about 40%.
3. **December is the smallest unlock by size:** August more than doubled the tradable float (about 640M shares plus about 900M unlocked), and October added about 45%. December adds roughly 18%. Afterward about 40% of the company is tradable, with the other 60%, mostly Musk's, locked until next summer. Even if all 800M sold, that would be less than seven days at Friday's volume of about 120M shares. December 8 is also the first day of the Fed meeting, with futures pricing a rate hike as near certain, so any move may not be about the unlock.
4. **Who is selling and buying:** Founders Fund's 13G shows its stake falling from about 427M shares at end of June to about 314M at end of September, roughly a quarter. The filing does not say whether the shares were sold or distributed to its investors. On the buy side, Nvidia added about 7.7M shares in Q3 (now about 130M), State Street raised its stake about 78%, and Cathie Wood's ARK bought 378,515 shares on November 16.
5. **Valuation, and whether $100B is priced in:** At about $128 the market cap is about $1.7T, roughly 17x the December run rate and about 12x the Q1 pace. Analysts expect about $2.74 EPS next year, versus Tesla at around 160x next year's earnings. To justify 25x earnings at today's price, SpaceX would need about $70B of profit, or about $350B of revenue at a 20% margin. That is roughly double analyst 2027 expectations and below Musk's floated $500B to $1T run rate by end of 2027. The host's view is that the guidance is already in the price and the debate is about what comes after.
6. **Guidance and support for the bull case:** CFO Bret Johnsen reiterated the goal of $100B annualized revenue (December revenue times 12) by year-end. He guided Q1 2027 revenue to $33B–$37B. Q3 averaged a little under $5B a month; hitting $100B needs roughly $8.3B in December, and the Q1 midpoint implies about $12B a month. Q3 revenue was $14.6B (up 161% year on year versus Wall Street's roughly $12.7B), and backlog rose from about $47.5B to $63B in one quarter. The average analyst target is around $260. Citi raised its target to $280 from $240.
7. **Bear case:** Over the last 12 months SpaceX lost about $3.4B and burned close to $40B of free cash flow. Sawyer Merritt notes the Q4 capex guide of $35–40B equals Tesla's total capex over the past four years. SpaceX has about $90B of cash and securities against roughly $39B of debt. It sold $32B of AI chip-backed bonds, with a $25.2B tranche at 8.259%, part of a $40B+ financing led by Apollo and Goldman. Q3's $4.4B profit included about $1.6B from interest, investment gains and a tax benefit, leaving about $2.8B underlying. If compute is valued like a landlord business, CoreWeave trades near 1x next year's sales.
8. **Customer concentration:** Per Anthropic's IPO filing, as the host reads it, its monthly SpaceX bill is expected to reach about $37B a year once capacity is delivered in December. That is more than a third of the $100B target. After the first commitment periods end in January and March, either side can cancel on 90 days' notice. Amodei said the compute is high quality and they are enjoying working with SpaceX, without committing to the future. A hosting deal of about $1.1B a month starts December 1, and the CFO says compute deals pay back in under a year. The Q4 report will show whether December hit $100B, and reportedly another group of early holders unlocks right after it.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Ship catch derisks the TAM:** SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage for the first time on November 14. Musk wrote that catching the ship was the last major piece of the puzzle. Morgan Stanley calculates one Starship launch of Starlink V3 satellites pays back its cost in about 13 weeks, and Adam Jonas says the catch materially derisked long-term TAMs across all three segments. Musk reposted the note.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Demand signals:** At DealBook, Microsoft's Satya Nadella said he would lease SpaceX capacity if it were available, and Musk reposted it.

## Financials
1. **Merger angle and HSBC:** At DealBook, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would probably be better together, that a Tesla vote may or may not succeed, and that he should not be compensated for doing a merger. HSBC upgraded Tesla from reduce to hold and more than doubled its target to $359 from $157. The target blends a SpaceX-buys-Tesla-at-a-33%-premium case with a standalone value of about $212, and HSBC says the strategic logic is limited. The host says Bloomberg reported a push to announce in the first half of 2027, which neither company confirmed (an earlier Bloomberg report had pointed to a possible December 3 announcement). The host notes the exchange ratio depends on both stock prices at signing, so SpaceX's December price is one input.
