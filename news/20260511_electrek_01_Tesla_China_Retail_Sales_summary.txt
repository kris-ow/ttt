Tesla's actual retail sales in China fell 10% year-over-year in April to 25,956 vehicles, per China Passenger Car Association (CPCA) final data — contradicting widespread media reports of a 36% surge that were based on wholesale/export figures from Giga Shanghai.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Wholesale vs. retail discrepancy:** Giga Shanghai produced and shipped 79,478 vehicles in April, which generated the "36% surge" headlines. However, 53,522 of those were exports — up 80% year-over-year and the second-highest monthly export total in Giga Shanghai's history. Stripping out exports leaves just 25,956 domestic retail units, down 9.66% from 28,731 in April 2025.

2. **Sequential and cumulative declines:** April retail sales fell 53.7% from March's 56,107 units, though part of that drop reflects seasonal end-of-quarter dynamics. For January–April 2026, cumulative retail sales totaled 138,754 vehicles, down 15% year-over-year. This follows a full-year 2025 retail decline of roughly 5% and a Q1 2026 crash of 16%.

3. **Market share at recent lows:** Tesla captured 3.06% of China's overall NEV retail market in April — its lowest share since November 2025. In the pure BEV segment, share was 4.48%, also a new low since November 2025.

4. **Financing policy change:** Tesla canceled a seven-year low-interest loan option in China in May 2026, retaining only a zero-interest plan for up to five years. The article notes promotional financing has not reversed the demand trend so far.

## Market & Competition
5. **Export surge masks domestic weakness:** In the first four months of 2026, Tesla exported 154,122 vehicles from Shanghai — a 127% surge year-over-year — while domestic retail dropped 15% over the same period. Export destinations include South Korea, Europe, Australia, and Canada (which recently resumed accepting Chinese-made Teslas).

6. **Chinese competitors outpacing Tesla domestically:** In April, BYD posted wholesale volume of 321,123 NEVs (though down 15.5% year-over-year, its eighth consecutive YoY decline). Li Auto delivered 34,085 vehicles, Xpeng delivered 31,011, and NIO delivered 29,356 — up 23% year-over-year. All three exceeded Tesla's domestic China retail figure of 25,956.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that reporting Giga Shanghai's wholesale output as "Tesla China sales" is misleading, since roughly two-thirds of production is now exported. He contends the retail figure is the relevant metric for assessing Tesla's competitive position in the world's largest EV market, which he characterizes as genuinely declining.
