This is a live-stream companion video from the futureaza channel covering SpaceX's Starship Flight 13 launch attempt, hosted by Brian with guest Pres Joe. The stream covers mission objectives, banter, and audience Q&A while awaiting launch, but the countdown ultimately triggers an abort at booster ignition and the flight is scrubbed, with no relaunch occurring during the stream.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 13 mission objectives outlined:** Using an AI assistant (Grok) to summarize the mission, the hosts described primary objectives as liftoff, ascent, and stage separation, plus a Super Heavy boost-back burn addressing prior propellant-settling and grid-fin control issues, followed by controlled re-entry and soft splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico. For the upper stage, objectives included nominal ascent, propulsion reliability, an in-space engine relight (not attempted on Flight 12), and deployment of 20 real V3 Starlink satellites — the first time operational satellites (rather than mass simulators) would be flown, including solar array/antenna deployment and brief laser-link tests with a ground station in South Africa and the existing Starlink constellation, before a controlled re-entry and splashdown in the Indian Ocean. The plan also included heat-shield and load-sensing tile testing.
2. **Launch scrubbed after hold at booster ignition:** The countdown proceeded through a built-in T-minus-1-minute hold with no issues, but during final ignition of the booster's Raptor engines, a hold/abort was triggered, shutting down the engines as they began to light. SpaceX confirmed via its livestream commentary that this was a full scrub for the day (not a brief recycle), with teams moving immediately into propellant offload for both Starship and Super Heavy, and no immediate word on root cause or next attempt timing.
3. **Second Raptor 3 / Starship V3 booster flight:** This would have been the second flight of Starship Version 3 and Booster Version 3 with Raptor 3 engines; total propellant loaded across both vehicles was over 11.5 million pounds.
4. **SpaceX stock dipped in after-hours trading post-scrub:** Hosts noted SpaceX's stock took a hit in after-hours trading following the scrub, which they attributed to emotional retail investors and/or algorithmic traders reacting to negative headlines rather than any indication of a serious problem; one host said they were "not worried" since the vehicle could try again soon. A commenter mentioned SpaceX's IPO price was $135, with a later price around $170 cited by the host as a point some investors may have trimmed at.
