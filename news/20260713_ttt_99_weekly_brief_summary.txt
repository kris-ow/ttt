## Robotaxi

- **Miami went unsupervised July 3**, Tesla's fourth city with driverless operation, using a notably smaller pre-launch safety-driver footprint than Austin's 300+. Field testers found only 2-4 active vehicles, multi-hour wait times, and a geofence roughly 15% the size of Waymo's coverage — early-beta scale despite the "unsupervised" label. Electrek separately reports Austin's unsupervised subset has contracted from a ~25 peak to ~14 active cars.
- **Tesla teased Cybercab "employee rides" at Giga Texas on July 10** — a driverless Cybercab crossing the outbound parking lot, with no confirmed scope beyond a campus loop. Electrek's Fred Lambert calls it not meaningful until Cybercab does unsupervised paid rides on public streets, which he speculates could land around earnings. "Well over 100" finished Cybercabs are stacked at the outbound lot, suggesting hardware is ready while software remains the bottleneck.
- **Tesla's 2025 Impact Report formalized Cybercab as the primary Robotaxi vehicle**: ~165 Wh/mile efficiency, ~4,000 parts (vs. ~10,000 for Model Y), reaction-injection-molded panels replacing a paint shop, and a two-seat layout matching the 85%+ of ride-hail trips carrying two or fewer riders. Tesla claims nearly half the emissions per mile versus a Model 3/Y ride.
- **Tesla's supervised Austin fleet is crashing at roughly 4x the human rate** (~1 per 57,000 miles vs. ~1 per 229,000 human benchmark), per Electrek — a data point that cuts against the safety narrative even as deployment expands.
- **NHTSA head Jonathan Morrison said the agency will "absolutely" consider dropping steering-wheel requirements** for vehicles never meant to be human-driven, calling the current mandate senseless — a direct regulatory tailwind for the wheel-free Cybercab design.

## Optimus

- **Fremont's first Optimus production line is installing on the former Model S/X floor**, with the old lines being torn out to make room; ~40 more sublines remain, each requiring one to two weeks of validation, per Lars Moravy. Low-volume output is suggested possible by Q4.
- **Giga Texas Optimus factory steel is actively rising**, targeting a footprint just under the main car plant and eventual capacity up to 10 million units/year (a separate Fremont-specific figure cites 1 million/year). Tesla has otherwise gone largely quiet on Optimus for months, prompting speculation about schedule slippage versus a differentiated technical path.
- **Competitor 1X unveiled a new tendon-driven hand** (25 DOF, IP68, food-safe, tactile slip-sensing, 8 m/s finger speed), with 10,000 hands/year target capacity — a direct competitive benchmark as China ships an estimated ~90% of world humanoid volume today.
- **Early qualitative signal: Optimus vacuum-robot feature appears operational** per shared media, though no technical details were disclosed. Supply chain localization continues in Texas, with Optimus batteries sourced on-campus and lithium from Corpus Christi.

## Financials

- **Q2 2026 deliveries: 480,126–481,000 units, beating consensus (~406,000–460,000 depending on source), Tesla's best-ever Q2.** Inventory overhang fell from ~50,000 entering the quarter to ~20,000 exiting it. Despite the beat, shares fell roughly 4-8% on report day, a "sell the news" reaction multiple hosts flagged as notable.
- **Analyst targets rose across the board**: RBC to $500 (from $475), explicitly building in a 25-30% premium tied to SpaceX-merger speculation; UBS to $442 (from $364) citing physical-AI potential; JPMorgan reiterated Neutral at $475; Baird set $522.
- **AI5 chip reached tape-out at Samsung Foundry** on a 2nm process, destined for the Taylor, Texas fab for integration into "Tesla's newest products" — no mass-production timeline confirmed.
- **Tesla capped internal AI token spend at $200/week per engineer**, reversing a prior free-for-all era, with exceptions requiring approval.

## Energy

- **Tesla logged over $9 billion in new Megapack orders across six weeks (43+ GWh)** — comparable to a full trailing-12-month deployment total of ~49 GWh — including a new $3B Esyasoft order for 15+ GWh across the UK, Western Europe, GCC, and India.
- **Tesla launched "Tesla Home,"** consolidating solar, Powerwall, Wall Connector, and smart-breaker control into one AI-driven app built on the existing Opticaster engine — a UI/rebrand exercise rather than new hardware, per Electrek.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y was China's best-selling passenger vehicle across all fuel types in June** (38,654 retail registrations) and the Netherlands' best-seller across all fuel types in H1, despite pricing above every other top-10 China model.
- **Model Y Launch Series (Model Y L) is building exclusively at Giga Texas**, not Fremont, with North American deliveries expected to begin September 2026; Fremont capacity is partly constrained by space reallocated to the new Optimus line.
- **Tesla's rental program is expanding to seven states** (California, Maryland, Pennsylvania, Arizona, Kansas, Washington, Florida) at $60/day for Model 3/Y with free Supercharging and FSD included.

## Market & Competition

- **New Jersey bill S.1677/A.3968 would require AVs to use cameras plus two additional sensor types**, effectively barring Tesla's camera-only Robotaxi while leaving Waymo unaffected; backers include Teamsters and a group whose campaign director previously led Tesla Takedown.
- **Grok 4.5 launched** (1.5T parameters), scoring competitively with Anthropic's Opus tier on coding benchmarks at roughly one-tenth the cost; Musk publicly reversed his stance on Anthropic, calling it the current AI leader.
- **Waymo suffered a high-profile SF operational failure over July 4th weekend** — vehicles froze at intersections, some required 3-4 hour tows for dead batteries, others were vandalized — while still operating under 4,000 cars after roughly six years. Waymo separately expanded fully driverless testing to Las Vegas, San Diego, Denver, and Tampa.

## Bear Case of the Week

- **Tesla stock fell despite record deliveries.** Q2's 480-481K unit beat was followed by a ~4-8% share decline across the reporting period, a pattern multiple hosts read as evidence that delivery beats alone no longer move the stock absent tangible Robotaxi scaling.
- **Cybercab "employee rides" announcement is thinner than it appears.** Electrek's Fred Lambert notes the July 10 tease is a parking-lot crossing at Giga Texas, not unsupervised paid rides on public streets — the real milestone remains unmet.
- **Austin's unsupervised fleet is contracting, not growing.** Electrek reports the unsupervised subset down to ~14 active cars from a ~25 peak, even as the city-cited total fleet sits around only 50 vehicles.
- **Tesla's own safety data shows elevated crash rates.** The supervised Austin robotaxi fleet is crashing at roughly 4x the human benchmark rate (~1 per 57,000 miles vs. ~1 per 229,000), per Electrek — a direct tension with Tesla's Impact Report safety claims.
- **New Jersey's proposed sensor-mandate bill would effectively exclude Tesla's camera-only Robotaxi** from the state while leaving Waymo unaffected, continuing a pattern of state-level regulatory friction specifically targeting Tesla's architecture.
