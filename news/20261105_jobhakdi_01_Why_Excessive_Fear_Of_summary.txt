Joe Bhakdi analyzes a broad AI-stock selloff following Democratic midterm election wins, arguing it's driven by fear that Democrats will slow data center buildout nationwide. He contends the selloff is overdone for Tesla and especially SpaceX, since most near-term buildout is already permitted, red states retain control, and SpaceX's space-based compute ambitions make it a long-term beneficiary — while cautioning that neoclouds face a genuine structural threat and that investors should avoid leverage and not panic-sell.

## Market & Competition
1. **Broad AI-trade selloff, not Elon-specific:** Over two trading days the NASDAQ fell roughly 5% intraday (net ~3% across three days), with the S&P down 3.78% and Dow down 2.9%. Tesla was down about 11% and SpaceX about 9% on the day discussed; SK Hynix fell about 11%, Palantir about 10%, Nvidia about 8%, Meta only about 4%, Microsoft about 3%. Neoclouds were hit hardest — CoreWeave and Nebius each fell roughly 15–20% on the day, with Nebius reportedly down around 50% over the prior month. Bhakdi says this spread shows the selloff is a broad AI-infrastructure panic, not specific hostility toward Elon Musk.
2. **Cause: fear of a Democrat-led data center slowdown:** Bhakdi attributes the selloff to market fear that Democrats, after a midterm sweep (including flipping the Texas governorship/Senate seats per the host), will restrict data center permitting; he cites an unnamed analyst's estimate that Democratic policy could remove about 10% of planned data center supply and slow the rest. He argues hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) benefit relatively because they already have capacity, while neoclouds and chipmakers (Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix) are more exposed to a buildout slowdown.
3. **Federal structure limits near-term damage:** Bhakdi argues U.S. federalism means a Democratic Senate majority cannot quickly halt already-permitted projects, that Trump can veto federal legislation against data centers through his term, and that red states (Texas being the main exception/risk) retain authority over permitting — pushing any real impact toward 2028 rather than 2026–27.
4. **Hedges beyond the U.S.:** He points to Poland, Sweden, Finland, and the Baltics as alternative sites with cheap electricity where hyperscalers could redirect buildout if U.S. politics worsens, framing this as a partial hedge against Texas-specific risk.
5. **Memory/chip supply lock-in:** Bhakdi reiterates that Micron and SK Hynix are sold out for roughly 24 months and, per his estimate, Micron has secured about 35% of its revenue through the 2030s via long-term contracts, with Chinese HBM competition unlikely before 2031 — he frames SK Hynix's low forward P/E (quoted around 7, implying roughly 3.5x two-years-forward) as a buying opportunity rather than a risk signal.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX valuation framed as deeply undervalued:** Bhakdi states SpaceX's market cap fell to about $1.7 trillion during the selloff and argues that if the company hits his projected $400 billion+ revenue and $200 billion+ net income next year, it would trade at roughly an 8x forward P/E — a level he calls absurdly cheap given the growth rate. He speculates SpaceX could reach $200 when it next reports earnings (said to be roughly three months out) and reach $400 next year, though these are his own projections, not guidance.
2. **SpaceX seen as relatively insulated from the political risk:** He argues SpaceX benefits from being positioned to move compute to space rather than depend solely on terrestrial data centers subject to new permitting fights, and notes the market sold off SpaceX less than Tesla or neoclouds (down ~9% vs. Tesla's ~11% and neoclouds' 12%+), which he reads as the market partially recognizing this distinction.
3. **Starbase Louisiana flagged as the key risk:** Bhakdi says his main political concern for SpaceX is whether Democrats could target launch site infrastructure (e.g., Starbase Louisiana), calling it the single biggest asset at risk, though he argues this would be an extreme and politically costly move.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship commercial readiness timeline:** Bhakdi states he is confident Starship will be commercially ready with some reusability within 12 months, and likely within 6 months, though not with "endless" reusability yet.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab political risk in Texas:** Bhakdi acknowledges Texas Democrats could theoretically create regulatory friction for Cybercab deployment given the state's political flip, but argues it would be politically toxic for them to specifically target it, and notes continuity at the federal level (DOT Secretary Duffy, President Trump) supports an eventual nationwide autonomy framework.

## Financials
1. **Space-based compute buildout timeline:** Bhakdi projects (his own estimate, not sourced guidance) that SpaceX and Nvidia are planning a first AI satellite for Q4 2027, with roughly 5,000 satellites needed to reach the first gigawatt of space-based compute by around 2029, scaling to tens of gigawatts in the early 2030s — he presents this as a long-term hedge against any terrestrial data center slowdown.
