Electrek published two Tesla-focused pieces today: one on Tesla pulling the plug on Cybertruck in Australia and New Zealand, and one detailing newly approved local tax breaks for Tesla's proposed Texas solar factory.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck killed for Australia and New Zealand:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla has cancelled Cybertruck for Australia and NZ, emailing reservation holders — some of whom put down deposits starting in November 2019 (A$150) — that "plans to produce Cybertruck for Australia and New Zealand have been discontinued." Tesla will fully refund payments within 30 days and is offering a free one-year FSD (Supervised) subscription to those who instead order a Model 3 or Model Y. The article notes the Cybertruck is built only in left-hand drive, and Australia requires its own certification. Lambert estimates Cybertruck sales at 7,000–8,000 units in Q3 2026 (from Tesla's reported 8,295 "Other Models" deliveries), calling that a roughly 30,000-per-year pace versus a line built for 250,000 units annually. Lambert's take: Tesla should have communicated this years earlier rather than letting reservation holders wait since the order page closed in 2022 without refunds, and argues the episode confirms Cybertruck is now "a niche, mostly American product" that Tesla won't invest further in internationally. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/23/tesla-cancels-cybertruck-australia-new-zealand/)

## Energy
2. **Texas solar factory could get 35 years of tax breaks:** Fred Lambert reports Fort Bend County commissioners voted 4-0 to approve four agreements that could give Tesla's proposed $10 billion "Project Crystal Sun" solar factory near Richmond, Texas up to 35 years of local tax breaks on equipment, even though Tesla has not committed to building there. The deal includes a 10-year 100% abatement on county property taxes on equipment (split as $1.52B real property/$8.6B personal property per Tesla's JETI filing), followed by a 20-year period where the county rebates 90% of equipment taxes back to Tesla; hitting a 12,000-average-employee threshold for two consecutive years extends the rebate period to 25 years (project year 35) and makes the first five rebate years a 100% grant. Tesla must start construction by June 30, 2027, begin commercial solar cell production by December 31, 2030, and invest $10 billion by the later of December 31, 2031 or two years after production starts; the jobs ramp targets 9,712 employees at $55/hour average pay by year four. Tesla's consultant Kroll estimated the project would otherwise pay $276.5 million in county general taxes over 38 years. The county's guaranteed minimum under a "true-up" agreement is $2 million/year. Lambert's take: he wants the US-based solar manufacturing to happen but criticizes the deal's weak clawback protections and the pattern of states/counties bidding against each other, and questions whether Tesla is actually committed to Texas or just using the offer as leverage against an unnamed competing state, while citing unspecified sourcing that Tesla is "set on" the Houston-area site. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/23/tesla-solar-factory-texas-35-years-tax-breaks-fort-bend/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is critical of Tesla's handling of both stories: he faults Tesla for years of silence toward Australian/NZ Cybertruck reservation holders and frames the Cybertruck as a fading niche product, while expressing support for Tesla's US solar manufacturing ambitions but skepticism toward the generosity and weak accountability of the proposed tax incentive package.
