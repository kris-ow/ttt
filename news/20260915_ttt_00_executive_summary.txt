## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk declined to rule out a Tesla-SpaceX merger when asked directly by both an All-In Summit interviewer and podcast host Freedberg, saying "who can imagine what action one might take" given the extensive collaboration between the firms — a shift from his more guarded Q2-earnings-call answer. Jo Bhakdi (guest on brighterwithherbert) puts the eventual-merger probability near-certain but sees no concrete timing signal, estimating a 20-30% chance of an announcement this year; a separate host (electrified) points to Tesla's unusually late, still-unannounced shareholder meeting date as a possible tell, floating a personal hope for a buyout north of $500/share (not a prediction).
- Musk praised SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell's "incredible IQ and EQ"; Bhakdi says he believes SpaceX's management/finance bench is superior to Tesla's, while rating Tesla's engineering and factory execution as world-class in its own right.
- SpaceX trading around $1.95 trillion market cap per Jo Bhakdi, who expects a range roughly $135-150 near-term, with a ~328 million share unlock (~15% of float) due in nine days from his recording.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA issued a Special Order (Sept 10, signed by Chief Counsel Peter Simshauser) demanding Tesla justify under oath how the steering-wheel-, pedal-, and mirror-less Cybercab complies with federal safety standards, escalating from an Audit Query opened after commercial service began in Austin Sept 3. Tesla has until Sept 30 to respond; penalties for incomplete or untruthful answers reach $139 million, with criminal exposure up to 15 years for falsification.
- The order specifically challenges Tesla's self-certification under FMVSS 135 (foot-activated brakes), probes whether temporary human-driver controls were used to certify compliance before removal, and notes Tesla holds no Part 555 exemption — unlike Zoox, which received a temporary exemption in July capped at 2,500 vehicles/year through 2028.
- Electrek reports Tesla filed a record 236 driver-assist crash reports to NHTSA in July (up from 135 in March, 207 in May, 209 in June), including four fatal crashes — Upper Marlboro MD (3 deaths), San Mateo CA, Ione CA, and Ashby MN — all marked "Verified Engaged" with narrative, software-version, and operating-area fields redacted in every case. Tesla's report format also logs only one fatality per crash, undercounting the actual death toll (5 deaths recorded as 2-3 across two multi-fatality crashes). Electrek notes Tesla redacts 100% of narratives versus 0% for GM's Super Cruise, and cautions that "engaged" status doesn't prove causation.
- Zurich Insurance became Australia's first insurer offering discounts to FSD (Supervised) users, citing Tesla data of 132M+ km driven with 7x fewer collisions than average; Slovenia's Deputy PM publicly tested FSD Supervised days after regulatory approval, calling it a step toward robotaxis (per Sawyer Merritt).
- Tesla now surfaces FSD-linked insurance savings ("up to $60/mo") directly on its US ordering page.
- Hyundai says it will deliberately delay its autonomy rollout, targeting parity with FSD in roughly five years via a phased 2027-2029 plan; both an unnamed AI engineer and a commentator (Jeff Lutz) argue this strategy misreads how FSD's data flywheel compounds.

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab fleet spotted growing in Pittsburgh (18, then 7 more added per community footage), up from an estimated under-10 count months earlier; Tesla also announced a Cybercab Australia/New Zealand tour starting in Sydney this month through 2027.
- Cybercab rider age rules diverge from Model Y robotaxi: Cybercab bars under-13s (13-17 need an adult); Model Y robotaxi allows 8-17 with an adult and bars under-8s entirely.
- Cost-per-mile analysis (cernbasher, host-constructed, not confirmed Tesla figures): argues Tesla is optimizing lifetime cost per mile rather than sticker price, with hypothetical fleet math showing cost/mile could fall from ~10¢ at 300,000-mile vehicle life to ~3.3¢ at 1 million miles; cites third-party specs (0.2 drag coefficient, 165 Wh/mile, 47.6 kWh battery) as unconfirmed.
- Jo Bhakdi models 1 million robotaxis generating $30-50B in earnings, which he projects could re-rate Tesla shares to $1,500-3,000 — his own projection, not company guidance.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 14 launch window opens 7:15 a.m. Central on September 22: first orbital Starship launch, first Starlink V3 deployment (26 satellites, ~26 Tbps added capacity), and first re-flight of recovered heat-shield tiles; no catch attempt planned. Musk said at the All-In Summit he thinks full reusability with rapid reflight in 2027 is "extremely likely."
- SpaceX announced its first commercial Starfall customer, Luxembourg's Space Cargo, for a 2028 microgravity re-entry mission.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk framed the Tesla-SpaceX Terafab chip project as essential given Taiwan supply risk and near-max capacity at existing fabs; an R&D line at Giga Texas (Tesla-SpaceX collaboration) is targeted to produce "something useful" by end of next year. Nvidia's Jensen Huang publicly backed the effort at the same summit.
- Musk said via tweet he's highly confident SpaceX will launch Nvidia AI computers into space next year; a separate report describes Nvidia developing satellite-specific server racks with solar power and radiators, built on Starlink-adjacent technology.
- Rising Nvidia B200/H100 GPU rental prices (B200 up ~80% since January, ~21% in the last month) are cited as evidence AI compute demand outstrips supply, framing SpaceX's orbital compute ambitions as a major potential beneficiary.
