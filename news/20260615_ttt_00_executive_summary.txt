## Robotaxi

- **102 Cybercabs at Giga Texas outbound lot — record.** Drone footage from Airwave/General Westside confirmed the count; Jeff Lutz (former Motorola/Google/Lenovo supply chain executive) interprets this as a production ramp transitioning from dozens toward hundreds, with fewer than two full months of production elapsed since the late-April start.

- **50 Cybercabs spotted in Dallas; 31 at Houston hub.** As of June 13, 19 of the Dallas units are fully badged with wheel caps, front plates, and decals attached — production-worthy signals that Lutz distinguishes from test-vehicle staging. The Houston hub count rose from ~20 just weeks prior.

- **Cybercabs spotted in Seattle, Mesa AZ, and Las Vegas.** Las Vegas is reportedly preparing dedicated robotaxi support infrastructure. The broad geographic distribution is cited as an indicator of operational deployment rather than localized testing. Per Joe Tegtmeyer, there may be over 1,000 Cybercabs now testing in the wild.

- **New robotaxi depot discovered in Irving, Texas (DFW area).** A permit letter of intent revealed a facility with 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building spaces (speculated to be individual automated-servicing bays), and 17 EV charging stations — closely mirroring a previously reported Las Vegas facility. Panelists on cernbasher note that fleet parking spaces at a depot don't cap fleet size; the depot is a service node potentially covering multiple cities.

- **NHTSA safety data: 18 total robotaxi incidents since Austin launch; Tesla at fault in 7.** The most recent incident (covering April 15–May 15) involved a robotaxi stopped at a red light that was rear-ended by a pickup truck — not Tesla's fault. Analyst "Reigns" (per cernbasher) assessed Tesla's robotaxi at **1.81x safer than human drivers** as of April 2026, with the trend improving since January. Tesla's rolling 3-month crash rate remains better than the human baseline. Note: minor incidents like curb scrapes are mandatory reportable events for robotaxi operators but are routinely unreported by human drivers, likely understating the true safety advantage.

- **Texas DMV data: 69 vehicles registered for Austin robotaxi service** (per Loomis's tracker, pulling directly from the Texas DMV). This is an independent figure covering unsupervised-registered units; it is distinct from the broader active fleet figures in RobotaxiTracker.com.

- **One-year mark since Austin robotaxi launch (June 22, 2025).** As of today, unsupervised service remains in Austin, Dallas, and Houston with no Cybercab yet carrying a paying passenger — all paid rides to date are in Model Y. Herbert and Lutz both acknowledge slower-than-expected progress while pointing to accumulating Cybercab inventory as grounds for H2 2026 optimism.

- **Jo Bhakdi identifies next two weeks as a "local probability maximum" for a major robotaxi announcement**, citing end-of-H1 2026 as a catalyst deadline and referencing Tesla's prior earnings-call guidance to add five more cities by end of H1. He warns he will reduce leverage if Tesla reaches $420–440 without a Cybercab catalyst.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Cybercab EPA-certified May 26, 2026; introduction into commerce May 29.** Full specs from the EPA filing: 3,113-lb curb weight, 163 kW (219 HP) AC 3-Phase Permanent Magnet motor, front-wheel drive, 47.6 kWh battery (326V / 146 Ah), ~154 Wh/kg specific energy. Unadjusted combined range: 418.2 miles; adjusted estimated range ~293 miles. Efficiency: 165 Wh/mi. GVWR: 3,730 lbs (~617 lbs payload). Primary charging: wireless inductive. Electrek notes the 3,113-lb weight is surprisingly heavy for a purpose-built two-seater and characterizes the vehicle as "purposeless" until unsupervised autonomy approval is granted.

- **Reuters/Electrek: Tesla presented misleading FSD safety data to European regulators.** Tesla's November 2024 letter to RDW (Dutch road authority) cited its self-published FSD safety report; a follow-up slide deck emailed to Swedish regulators claimed FSD-equipped vehicles travel 7x farther between crashes than the average U.S. human driver and that FSD could have "saved 32,000 lives" and prevented 1.9 million injuries. Ten of 11 independent traffic-safety researchers called the methodology "misleading marketing." The "7x" figure compares FSD crash rates (airbag-deployment-triggered only) against a U.S. baseline that includes far less severe incidents — researchers say the true inflation factor is ~3x. The "32,000 lives" figure assumes every U.S. vehicle, including freight trucks and motorcycles, is replaced by an FSD Tesla. Norway's roads administration stated Tesla's figures are "self-produced," making it "difficult to find correlation with the authorities' accident statistics." The European Transport Safety Council has sent letters to nearly every non-approving EU member urging a hold until independent peer review is completed. Four countries — Netherlands, Lithuania, Belgium, and Denmark — have already granted national approval.

- **Lithuania approved FSD supervised this week; Greece expected in June; Belgium both regions supportive.** The Netherlands approved April 11 (RDW), Belgium June 10. EU-wide approval requires representatives of 55% of member states covering 65% of the bloc's population. Sweden is still asking basic questions about highway capability. Per Herbert, 13 countries globally now have supervised FSD, 5 in Europe.

- **FSD 14.3.4 rolling out with broadly positive reception.** Jeff Lutz describes it as "a bigger jump than just a dot release," citing smoother lane-keeping, more informed lane changes, reduced nag, materially improved parking (more consistent single-attempt completion), and new destination-approach UI presenting the car as a chauffeur. Jo Bhakdi characterizes it as a "huge leap" relative to 14.3.3. Loomis shares clips of ongoing 14.3 issues — hard braking for a signaling school bus, driving onto a golf course, stopping mid-intersection, brake-stabbing from 44 mph to under 30 mph for a bird, and looping a parking garage repeatedly — and suspects some users still experience regressions on highway navigation.

- **Forbes (Brook Kthers, testing a 2026 Model 3 Premium AWD in LA) declares FSD 14.3 "feels more like a Level 4 driverless ADS than past versions"** and states Tesla is "a headway ahead" of all other automakers pursuing advanced ADAS. GM Super Cruise and Ford Blue Cruise are described as more advanced than Toyota's basic lane-centering but far short of FSD.

- **FSD evasive action video goes viral.** FSD initiated evasive maneuvers in response to an oncoming out-of-control truck before the human driver or the car ahead reacted. A Tesla AI team member confirmed FSD responded at the frame level before the vehicle ahead. A separately attributed reaction time figure of 0.13 seconds was cited by Lutz (attributed to AI Driver and David Moss experiments).

- **NHTSA engineering analysis underway covering 3.2 million vehicles** for 80 identified FSD traffic violations including red-light runs and wrong-lane crossings — the final step before a potential recall. Separately, $20–$50 plastic doll heads sold on Chinese e-commerce platforms are being used to defeat Tesla's cabin camera driver-monitoring system. One driver reportedly completed a 30-minute highway trip at speed with zero safety alerts using a Dwayne Johnson figurine. Electrek's Lambert calls for liveness detection and gaze anti-spoofing and notes this is the second generation of monitoring-system circumvention (the first, steering-wheel weights, was shut down by NHTSA cease-and-desist).

- **FSD China approval expected Q3 2026 per Tesla CFO.** Tesla has rebranded FSD in China as "Tesla Assisted Driving." Rumors online point to approval as early as June 19 (before Dragon Boat Festival); cernbasher and Loomis both expect Q3 as the more realistic window. A leaked video showing FSD operating in China circulated; Loomis speculates Tesla is holding official publication until approval. Tesla has posted ~6 urgent China AI job listings, interpreted by cernbasher as a signal approval is approaching. FSD China price: ~64,010 RMB (~$9,400 USD), one-time purchase; over 2 million Teslas on Chinese roads.

- **Elon Musk on unsupervised FSD for customer-owned vehicles: "probably in Q4"** (2026), with a gradual geographic rollout as each area is confirmed safe.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO priced at $135; trading ~$167–194 by Sunday/Monday.** Jo Bhakdi argues forced index-fund buying (Russell inclusion June 26, NASDAQ 100 inclusion July 6) and lockup mechanics (15–180 days) guarantee near-term upside but predicts a sharp correction to $80–$100 once buying pressure fades — calling it a long-term buying opportunity, not a current one. Gwen Shotwell acknowledged "a lot of synergies" with Tesla and that a merger "would make life easier on Elon." CNBC reported in May 2026, citing a Tesla employee, that the two companies are weighing a combination. Dan Ives puts merger odds at 80%; Kalshi at ~52% within one year. Analysts' combined valuation: ~$3.4 trillion. Bear case: national security review (SpaceX is a major defense contractor; Tesla has major China operations), SpaceX still posting losses, and multi-step board/shareholder approval processes on both sides.

- **Iran ceasefire announced Sunday evening.** NASDAQ rallied ~2.89%, S&P 500 ~1.61%, Dow Jones ~1.0%. Tesla trading ~$411 at time of Bhakdi's session; he targets $440 as resistance, $490 as next level, and $700–$800 by year-end as higher-probability outcome absent merger complications.

- **Waymo acquired the former Apple self-driving proving ground near Wittman, Arizona — ~5,500 acres for $220 million**, recorded June 5, 2026. FutureAzA frames this as evidence Waymo remains committed and well-capitalized. FutureAzA also estimates Waymo costs ~$1/mile more to operate than Tesla's robotaxi due to hardware architecture — a structural unit-economics gap he characterizes as durable regardless of deployment timing.

- **Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe claims supervised point-to-point FSD equivalent coming to all Gen 2 vehicles and R2 later in 2026**, unsupervised in 2027, full driverless for robotaxi use cases thereafter — with 50,000 R2 robotaxis planned for 2028 in partnership with Uber. Lutz and Herbert are skeptical: Rivian has not disclosed the compute infrastructure (GPUs, training data, supervised miles) required to match Tesla's capability, and no independent testing of Rivian's system exists. A commenter identified as Genma (described as working in legacy automaker ADAS) noted Tesla required over 100,000 H100s running 24/7, years of feedback from over 1 million FSD users, and over 10 billion supervised miles.

- **Li Auto CEO claims Mach model could be equivalent to FSD version 14 by Q4 2026**, citing dual Mach M100 chips at 260 TOPS and a claimed 50% improvement in imitation learning scale. Herbert characterizes all Chinese competitors — led by Xpeng, then Huawei, then Li Auto — as approximately two years behind Tesla.

- **ETSC sent letters to nearly every non-approving EU member state** urging a hold on FSD recognition pending independent safety review. Loomis notes ETSC's corporate partners include Toyota Motor Europe and the German Road Safety Council (closely tied to Mercedes, BMW, and VW) and argues the campaign reflects competitive pressure rather than safety-first reasoning. RDW stated it tested FSD "for more than one and a half years on our test track and on public roads" and that FSD "makes a positive contribution to road safety."

- **Several Chinese EV makers posted first profits:** Leap Motors full-year profit $78M (reversed from –$410M); NIO adjusted net profit $104M; Xpeng Q4 2025 net profit $55M (reversed from –$190M). BYD has outsold Tesla in Europe for multiple consecutive months.
