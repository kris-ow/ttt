## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab EPA certification complete.** Certificate of conformity issued May 26, 2026; Introduction into Commerce Date May 29, 2026. The Cybercab clears every federal emissions hurdle but has not received unsupervised autonomy approval — without which, per Electrek, it remains operationally purposeless.

- **Full Cybercab specs confirmed via EPA filing.** Curb weight 3,113 lbs; 163 kW / 219 HP front-wheel-drive permanent magnet motor; 47.6 kWh battery (~680 lbs); unadjusted combined range 418.2 miles, yielding an estimated ~293 miles on the EPA-adjusted cycle; efficiency 165 Wh/mi (~7.8 mi/kWh in lab conditions). Wireless inductive charging is the primary charging method; regenerative braking is front-wheels only.

- **Record 102 Cybercabs in Giga Texas outbound lot**, per drone footage from Airwave/General Westside — the highest single-lot count observed to date. Jeff Lutz (brighterwithherbert) interprets the staging at remote city locations — rather than near the factory — as a signal of growing confidence in both hardware and software readiness.

- **Cybercab inventory building across Texas and Nevada.** As of June 13, 31 Cybercabs were at the Houston robotaxi hub (up from ~20 a few weeks prior); 50 Cybercabs were spotted in Dallas with 19 described as fully badged and ready. Cybercabs were also reported in Seattle, Mesa AZ, and Las Vegas, where dedicated robotaxi support infrastructure is reportedly being prepared. Per Joe Tegtmeyer (cited by Electrified), more than 1,000 Cybercabs may now be testing in the wild; all observed units still carry steering wheels, suggesting supervised testing continues.

- **New Irving, Texas (DFW) robotaxi depot discovered** via a permit filing. The facility includes 212 remote fleet parking spaces, 64 building bays designed for automated servicing, and 17 EV charging stations — mirroring a previously reported Las Vegas facility. Per cernbasher, the depot's parking count is not a fleet cap; it is a regional service hub that could support a fleet many multiples its size.

- **NHTSA safety update: 18 total incidents since Austin launch; Tesla at fault in 7.** The newest incident (April 15–May 15 reporting window) involved a robotaxi stopped at a red left-turn arrow that was rear-ended by a pickup truck — fully the other driver's fault. Per analyst "Reigns" (cited by cernbasher), Tesla's robotaxi is 1.81x safer than human drivers as of April 2026, a figure likely understated because minor incidents mandatory for robotaxi operators are routinely unreported by humans. Texas DMV data cited by Electrified shows 69 Tesla vehicles currently registered for Austin robotaxi service.

- **One-year anniversary of Austin robotaxi launch (June 22, 2025).** The brighterwithherbert host acknowledges that a full year has elapsed with the network still limited to Austin, Dallas, and Houston without widespread deployment — characterizing progress as slower than originally expected but pointing to accumulating Cybercab inventory and safety data as grounds for optimism in H2 2026 or 2027.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.4 rolling out; described as a meaningful step up.** Jeff Lutz (brighterwithherbert) received it on both vehicles over the weekend and calls it "a bigger jump than just a dot release," citing smoother lane-keeping, more informed lane changes, reduced nag, improved end-of-ride behavior, and materially better parking. Jo Bhakdi describes it as a "huge leap" relative to 14.3.3. Early positive reviews are broad, though Electrified's Dillon Loomis suspects some users are still experiencing regressions in highway navigation.

- **FSD 14.3.3 called "ahead, way ahead" of all competitors** in the highest-engagement Tesla post of the day (2.3K likes), per Sawyer Merritt. A Forbes article by Brook Kthers testing a 2026 Model 3 in Los Angeles concluded FSD version 14.3 "feels more like a level four or driverless ADS than past versions" — the strongest third-party endorsement to date.

- **Reuters: Tesla presented misleading FSD safety data to European regulators.** Documents obtained via public records requests show Tesla's November 2024 letter to RDW (Dutch road authority) cited its self-published safety report, and a subsequent slide sent to Swedish regulators claimed FSD could have "saved 32,000 lives" and prevented 1.9 million injuries. Ten of 11 independent traffic-safety researchers called the methodology "misleading marketing." The "7x safer" claim stems from comparing FSD crash rates (airbag-deployment trigger only) against a broader U.S. crash rate — a comparison independent researchers say inflates the margin by approximately 3x. Norway's roads administration stated Tesla's figures "are self-produced," making it "difficult to find correlation with the authorities' accident statistics."

- **ETSC sent letters to transport ministers of nearly every EU member state** not yet approving FSD, urging a halt pending independent safety verification. Electrified's Loomis counters that RDW conducted more than 1.5 years of independent on-road and test-track evaluation and concluded FSD "makes a positive contribution to road safety." He also notes ETSC's corporate partners include Toyota Motor Europe and the German Road Safety Council — closely tied to Mercedes, BMW, and VW — flagging a potential conflict of interest.

- **Lithuania approved FSD supervised this week**, following the Netherlands (April 11) and Belgium (June 10). Greece announced it wanted to be the second European country to approve but was beaten by Lithuania; it is now expected to approve in June 2026. Per cernbasher, the Netherlands is actively seeking EU-wide approval on Tesla's behalf. EU-wide approval requires representatives of 55% of member states covering 65% of the bloc's population to vote yes; a vote may occur in June or September 2026.

- **FSD China approval expected Q3 2026**, per Tesla's CFO on the most recent earnings call (cernbasher). Tesla has posted ~six urgent AI/data-labeling job listings across nine Chinese cities — interpreted by cernbasher as an imminent-approval signal. A video apparently leaked showing FSD operating in China, reportedly intended for Tesla's official Chinese page (Electrified). Online rumors suggest approval as early as June 19, though the brighterwithherbert host expects no earlier than Q3.

- **$20–$50 plastic doll heads sold on Chinese e-commerce platforms defeat Tesla's cabin camera monitoring**, allowing drivers to use Autopilot and FSD (Supervised) without paying attention. One Model 3 owner reportedly drove 30 minutes using a Dwayne Johnson figurine with zero safety alerts. NHTSA has separately identified 80 FSD traffic violations and upgraded its investigation to an engineering analysis covering 3.2 million vehicles — the final step before a potential recall (Electrek).

- **Elon Musk, on unsupervised FSD for customer-owned vehicles:** *"I'm just guessing, but probably in the fourth quarter"* (2026), per Electrified. The brighterwithherbert host expresses skepticism about on-time delivery of this milestone.
