Tesla's Q2 2024 earnings call covered record quarterly revenue, an all-time high in energy storage deployments, and a return to positive free cash flow, alongside extended discussion of FSD progress, the delayed robotaxi unveil, Optimus timelines, and capital allocation questions tied to xAI. Elon Musk framed autonomy (both vehicle and Optimus) as the overwhelming driver of Tesla's long-term value, while CFO Vaibhav Taneja detailed near-term margin and cost pressures from financing incentives, Cybertruck/Model 3 ramp costs, and tariffs.

## Financials
1. **Record revenue and positive free cash flow:** Tesla reported record quarterly revenue and record energy business profits in Q2. Free cash flow returned to positive at $1.3 billion despite restructuring payments, and the company ended the quarter with over $30 billion in cash and investments.
2. **Restructuring charge and opex:** A recent reorg resulted in a $642 million charge recorded as restructuring and other expense in the quarter; excluding such charges, operating expenses declined sequentially despite higher AI-related spend and legal costs.
3. **Auto margins and cost pressures:** Auto margins were flat sequentially, helped by a record quarter for regulatory credit revenue. Attractive financing offers to offset high interest rates affected revenue per unit in Q2 and will persist into Q3. Cost per vehicle declined sequentially excluding Cybertruck's impact, but tariffs on raw materials and finished goods, plus ongoing Cybertruck and Model 3 ramps, are expected to pressure costs in the near term.
4. **CapEx guidance:** Tesla still expects full-year capex to exceed $10 billion as it brings a large GPU cluster online to scale FSD and other AI initiatives.
5. **Tesla's stance on IRA subsidies:** Musk said losing IRA subsidies (raised as a risk if the 2024 election outcome favors repeal) would hurt Tesla "slightly" but would be more damaging to competitors, and could help Tesla long-term; he reiterated that Tesla's value is "overwhelmingly" about autonomy and said anyone who doesn't believe Tesla will solve autonomy should sell the stock. Taneja added Tesla models its business assuming no subsidies.

## Autonomous Driving
6. **FSD v12.5 rollout:** Musk said FSD version 12.5, beginning rollout, has five times the parameters of version 12.4 and finally merges the highway and city driving stacks, which he expects to produce a "step change" improvement for supervised FSD.
7. **Path to unsupervised FSD:** Musk said based on current trends in miles-between-intervention, unsupervised FSD "possibly" by the end of 2024, and said he "would be shocked" if it isn't achieved in 2025, while noting his past timing predictions have been overly optimistic.
8. **FSD pricing and take rates:** Following a revision to FSD pricing in North America, Tesla said adoption rates increased "meaningfully," though Taneja declined to give specific figures, noting the increase started from a low base.
9. **International regulatory approval push:** Musk said Tesla's self-driving capability outside North America lags behind North America, but expects to seek regulatory approval for supervised FSD in Europe, China, and other countries around version 12.5/12.6, and said he thinks approval is likely before the end of 2024.
10. **Dojo doubled down amid Nvidia GPU constraints:** Musk said Tesla is "doubling down" on its Dojo training chip because demand for Nvidia GPUs is so high that sourcing state-of-the-art hardware is difficult; he said Tesla sees a path to being competitive with Nvidia via Dojo.
11. **FSD licensing to other OEMs:** Musk said several major OEMs have expressed interest in licensing Tesla FSD, and Tesla expects more interest over time, but declined to give details. He said adopting FSD would require an OEM to integrate Tesla's AI computer, a gateway computer with cellular/Wi-Fi connectivity, and seven-to-nine cameras for 360° coverage, and that given auto industry timelines it would likely take several years before such a deal showed up in volume/revenue.

## Robotaxi
12. **Robotaxi unveil postponed to October 10:** Musk confirmed the robotaxi/Cybercab product unveil was pushed back roughly two months to October 10, to allow design changes to the vehicle and to add additional product reveals.
13. **Regulatory approach — generalized vs. geofenced:** Musk contrasted Tesla's "generalized" self-driving approach with Waymo's localized, high-density-mapping approach, arguing Tesla's solution works anywhere without geofencing and that regulators are "morally obligated" to approve once safety data (billions of miles) shows it is safer than humans. A Tesla executive added that vehicle-level regulation in the US falls under FMVSS, uniform across all 50 states, though state/local rules may govern ride-hailing deployment.
14. **Cybercab to be produced at Giga Texas:** Musk confirmed the robotaxi vehicle will be produced at Giga Texas (Tesla's Texas headquarters).
15. **Fleet-based deployment model ("Tesla Network"):** Musk described robotaxi deployment as an owner-fleet model likened to "Airbnb on wheels" — owners can opt their car into the Tesla fleet part or full time and share revenue with Tesla — plus a smaller Tesla-owned fleet more akin to Uber. He said the entire compatible fleet (he cited on the order of 7 million vehicles capable of autonomy "soon," growing beyond 10 million then 20 million in subsequent years) could become active essentially instantly via a software update, and that Tesla's vehicle purchase terms restrict use of Tesla vehicles to the Tesla fleet only, barring third-party autonomy use.
16. **GM Cruise comparison dismissed:** Asked about GM cancelling its pedal-less/wheel-less robotaxi vehicle citing regulatory uncertainty, Musk said he believes the real reason is that GM's technology "doesn't work," not regulatory barriers, pointing to Waymo continuing to operate in the same markets.

## Humanoid Bots
17. **Optimus production timeline:** Musk said Optimus is already performing tasks in Tesla's factory, with a limited production version one expected to begin early 2025 for internal Tesla use. He said Tesla expects several thousand Optimus units produced and doing useful work by the end of 2025, ramping production significantly in 2026 when Optimus production version two — the high-volume version for external customers — begins, also to be built in Texas.
18. **Long-term Optimus valuation thesis:** Musk estimated long-term demand for general-purpose humanoid robots could exceed 20 billion units (8 billion people plus industrial uses) and said he believes Optimus's long-term value will exceed that of everything else Tesla combined, several times the roughly $5 trillion market cap figure he cited (attributing that figure to an Ark Invest analysis of autonomous transport) for the robotaxi opportunity alone.

## Energy
19. **Record energy deployments and China expansion:** Energy storage deployments more than doubled sequentially with contributions from both Megapack and Powerwall, driving record energy segment revenue and profit. Musk said the company is demand-, not production-, constrained and is ramping its US factory while building a new Megapack factory in China expected to roughly double (potentially triple) output.
20. **Grid storage demand "underestimated by orders of magnitude":** Musk argued that steady-state power plant operation enabled by battery storage could let a grid produce at least two to three times its current cumulative annual energy output in some cases, because current capacity must be sized for peak demand and sits idle much of the year.
21. **Competitive positioning on storage:** Executives said Megapack faces growing competition from Chinese OEMs but retains an edge via its integrated software stack, power electronics, and site-level controls; Tesla had a "really strong" contracting quarter and is building 2025–2026 backlog, and does not see broad global saturation, only localized pockets.

## Electric Vehicles
22. **4680 battery cell ramp:** 4680 cell production rose 51% quarter-over-quarter in Q2 with significant cost reduction; Tesla now produces enough cells for more than 1,400 Cybertrucks per week and is targeting cost parity with alternatives by year-end. A first validation Cybertruck built with the dry cathode process on mass-production equipment was completed, with dry cathode production launch targeted for Q4.
23. **Roadster and product roadmap:** Musk said most Roadster engineering is complete, with some further upgrades planned, and reiterated Tesla expects to be in Roadster production in 2025; he declined to discuss other future product/variant plans, citing the "Osborne effect" risk to near-term sales from premature announcements.
24. **Advertising and customer base:** Executives said Tesla is doing some advertising and adjusting its strategy but remains focused on product and pricing; over two-thirds of Q2 deliveries went to first-time Tesla buyers.

## Market & Competition
25. **Giga Mexico on hold pending election outcome:** Musk said Tesla needs to see how the 2024 US election plays out before committing further investment in Giga Mexico, citing comments from Trump about imposing heavy tariffs on vehicles made in Mexico; Tesla is instead significantly increasing capacity at existing factories.
26. **EU tariff impact on China-sourced imports:** Tesla was included in the EU's second round of tariff examinations on Chinese-made EV imports (having been excluded from the first round); Tesla is adjusting its import strategy, including shifting more Model Y production for right-hand-drive markets (including UK) to Berlin, more Berlin exports to markets like Taiwan, while continuing to import Model 3 into Europe from Shanghai.
27. **4680 equipment supplier dispute:** Asked about a lawsuit with an equipment supplier related to the roll-to-roll dry cathode process, an executive said Tesla holds a strong IP position, mostly designs/builds equipment in-house, and does not see the dispute as a risk to the 4680 rollout.

## Corporate / Other
28. **xAI compute and hiring clarification:** Responding to a question about redirected AI compute and engineer hiring toward xAI/X, Musk said an earlier report about GPUs being redirected was from six to seven months prior and reflected that Tesla's data centers had no physical space to power on the hardware at the time (the Giga Texas south extension needed to be completed first); he said xAI was formed only after AI researchers who wanted to work exclusively on AGI refused to join Tesla under any circumstances, including an offer to work on AGI there.
29. **xAI/Grok integration and potential Tesla investment:** Musk said Tesla is already benefiting from xAI's work in advancing FSD and building Tesla's new data center, said any Tesla investment in xAI would require shareholder approval which he'd support if shareholders vote for it, and said there are opportunities to integrate Grok into Tesla software.
30. **AI5 chip and distributed inference compute:** Musk said Tesla's next-generation AI5 chip, in inference capability, is comparable to Nvidia's next-gen data center GPU generation, with production expected to begin at the end of 2025 and scale in 2026; he floated eventual distributed compute using idle inference capacity across a large fleet of autonomous vehicles and humanoid robots as "a pretty obvious thing to do," without giving a concrete monetization plan.
