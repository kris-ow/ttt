The video recaps a wide-ranging in-person interview two Cyber Bulls hosts (Alexandra Merz and Jeff Lutz, joined by Cern Basher and host Herbert) conducted with Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy, alongside fresh news on Q2 deliveries, the Model Y L US launch, and Robotaxi/Optimus progress. The overall thesis is that Tesla's core businesses — vehicles, energy, robotaxi, and Optimus — appear to be inflecting upward simultaneously.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries beat expectations:** Tesla delivered nearly 480,000 vehicles in the quarter, described as Tesla's best second quarter ever and roughly 60,000 above the whisper-number consensus of 420,000 (early consensus had been around 406,000–460,000, with individual analysts like Troy and James reportedly closer to 466,000–480,000). One host noted this was achieved with minimal Model S/X volume, calling it effectively the highest quarter ever on a like-for-like basis.
2. **Model Y L launched in the US:** Tesla officially launched the six-seat Model Y L in the US as a limited-edition premium all-wheel-drive variant priced at $61,000 (later corrected from an initially stated 88 kWh to an 83 kWh battery), with US deliveries expected in September (initially misreported as October) and support for power sharing. Hosts view it as a likely strong seller given high parts commonality with the standard Model Y.
3. **Giga Berlin expansion continues amid European competitor weakness:** Giga Berlin is reportedly growing capacity by another 20% (following an earlier 20% increase), with hiring and ramp-up underway, contrasted against reports that Volkswagen may need to lay off up to 100,000 workers.
4. **Stock sold off despite beat; hosts caution against simple narratives:** Tesla stock rose roughly 13% into the print then fell (touching above $430 then below $400), which some commentators attributed to "buy the rumor, sell the news." Hosts pushed back, noting Rivian saw a similar chart pattern despite also beating on deliveries, arguing single-day moves are not reliably explainable.

## Robotaxi
1. **First production Cybercab begins testing in Austin:** Tesla released footage/reports of the first production Cybercab undergoing engineering testing on public roads with no steering wheel or pedals and an occupant not touching any manual controls.
2. **Robotaxi fleet registrations climbing:** Active Robotaxi fleet stands at 750 as of 2026-07-02 per RobotaxiTracker.com (Bay Area 618, Austin 105, Dallas 16, Houston 11); hosts specifically noted the Texas DMV-authorized Model Y robotaxi count rose by roughly 10 vehicles in a day to about 102, which they characterized as a ~20%+ single-day addition to the fleet.
3. **Hosts model a large Cybercab-to-Model-Y ratio:** Based on the assumption that 80–90%+ of rides carry two or fewer passengers, hosts estimated Tesla may eventually deploy roughly 400–900 Cybercabs for every 100 Model Y robotaxis in a market, implying a much larger eventual Cybercab fleet than current Model Y-based operations.
4. **Moravy teased a Giga Texas scaling announcement:** Moravy said "cool news" about "things happening on the campus of Texas" tied to "the scaling effort" would be announced roughly a week after the interview (around July 7); hosts interpreted this as Cybercab-related, though the exact content is unconfirmed. Moravy also reportedly said Cybercab automation readiness is at 90% and that he was surprised there weren't more problems, which hosts read as a strong signal the program is on track.
5. **Moravy reportedly said FSD adoption/demand is very strong ("through the roof"):** Cited as a possible driver behind any capacity-scaling announcement, alongside quarterly FSD adoption growth hosts say has been 10%+ sequentially.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus start of production seen as imminent:** Based on Moravy's comments about automation readiness percentages and "field acceptance testing" occurring at the Fremont facility, Jeff Lutz (an manufacturing-background host) assessed that first production Optimus units and start of production could be "a month or two" away, possibly within the current quarter — while cautioning new problems could still cause delays and that building one unit doesn't confirm scaled production capability (10,000–100,000 units).
2. **Elon Musk reaffirmed initial Optimus production will be slow:** Musk reportedly responded to an enthusiastic social media post about Optimus progress by saying Optimus production will be extremely slow at first, which hosts said aligns with, rather than contradicts, Moravy's comments.
3. **Fremont line changes underway:** Hosts noted Tesla has cleared space previously used for other equipment (referred to as "SNX" tooling) at Fremont, which they believe is being repurposed, at least partially, for new Optimus production equipment.
4. **Moravy defended Tesla's manufacturing edge for humanoid robots:** Asked why an automaker would be well-suited to build consumer robots, Moravy argued Tesla's mechanical/manufacturing expertise transfers well; one host (with a manufacturing background) largely agreed but noted the volumetric density of components in a humanoid's chest/head and non-rectangular form factor pose unique automation and fixturing challenges beyond typical auto assembly.

## Financials

2. **Inventory and margin expectations for the upcoming print:** One host expects Tesla's days-of-inventory to move into the "upper teens," implying a decent cash position, and expects gross margin to be in a "pretty good position," heading into the upcoming earnings report.

## Market & Competition
1. **Rivian delivery beat and similar stock pattern:** Rivian also beat delivery expectations (reported at roughly 12,000 vehicles) and saw a comparable stock chart pattern to Tesla over the prior weeks, which hosts used to argue against simplistic "buy the rumor sell the news" explanations for Tesla's post-earnings dip.
2. **Tesla's flat organizational hierarchy and supply chain vertical integration highlighted as competitive advantages:** Moravy reportedly described Tesla's engineering and supply chain vertical integration (in-house design work typically outsourced at other automakers) and a flatter management hierarchy (fewer layers between engineers and executives) as key to Tesla's fast response to supply shocks like COVID and tariffs, and to supplier relationships that go deep into sub-tier (T3/T4) suppliers.
