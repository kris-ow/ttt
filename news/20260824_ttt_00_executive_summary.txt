## Robotaxi
- Tesla confirmed the Cybercab joins Robotaxi service in Austin on September 3rd, ~23 months after its unveiling; event is invite-only via rider sweepstakes, winners announced Aug 26, livestreamed. Reporting suggests only a small handful of Cybercabs (roughly ten) will be available for rides at launch, not a mass rollout.
- Nevada's Transportation Authority approved Tesla for up to 5,000 robotaxis statewide; Tesla reportedly told regulators it would be "extremely happy" to hit 2,500 deployed by next year — seen by some hosts as a conservative target. Waymo was separately approved for up to 1,000 vehicles in Nevada over 12 months.
- Electrek reports Tesla's active unsupervised fleet has held around 20–30 vehicles across all markets since the June 2025 Austin launch, with 380,000 cumulative unsupervised miles logged through July — versus Waymo's ~3,000 driverless vehicles and 500,000+ paid trips weekly.
- Multiple viral rider complaints surfaced this week: a 2.3-mile Austin trip quoted at 66 minutes (later revised to 16 minutes with no route change), a Dallas rider dropped 3 miles from his car late at night, and a ride requiring manual remote intervention to reach its destination. Tesla's Robotaxi account confirmed a refund was issued after one post hit 630,000+ views.
- Texas driverless robotaxi registrations reportedly grew from ~33 at end of May to over 100 currently, per one analyst; California robotaxis still require a human safety monitor under current law.
- Cybercabs reportedly multiplying across Austin, Dallas, and Houston ahead of the September 3rd event, with sightings in ride-hailing decals and one spotted testing near a previously flagged parking-garage disengagement site.

## Autonomous Driving
- A Hardware 3 vehicle running FSD v14.1 Lite drove over a log across a wet road at 32 mph without braking, per an X video with 800,000+ views; Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla has pushed capable software onto hardware it has acknowledged can't handle full autonomy, though this framing is the article's own assertion.
- HW3 owners have reported thermal faults above 90°C (some reaching 96°C) on v14 Lite, sometimes forcing shutdowns or hardware replacement, alongside phantom braking and hesitation complaints.
- Mainstream press turned more positive: WSJ's Dan Neil called FSD "the best thing about the new Model Y" and said he was "bewitched" after two hours of testing; Edmunds also cited FSD as a key buying reason.
- China's recall covers 5.7 million vehicles industry-wide (Tesla the largest participant, ~3 million cars), effective September 25th — a software/label fix (OTA update plus warning stickers), not a hardware repair.
- Brandenburg, Germany's rural transport ministry confirmed talks with Tesla about deploying driverless shuttles to supplement its on-demand bus system, building on an existing Eifel-region pilot (currently requiring a Tesla employee in the driver's seat).

## Financials
- Tesla's year-to-date revenue through Q2 already exceeds the equivalent period in its prior record year (2023), per analyst James Stephenson, supporting a forecast for a full-year 2026 revenue record.
- Regulatory/ZEV credit revenue is modeled at roughly $100 million now, down to about a quarter of prior levels due to expired U.S. tax credits; both commentators argued Tesla's profitability was never structurally dependent on these credits.
- FSD subscriptions added roughly 170,000 net-new payers in Q1 with continued Q2 growth at $99/month, described as near-pure-margin revenue.
- TSLA closed at $348.95, down 3.83%, against a Nasdaq-100 decline of ~0.97%; SPCX closed at $135, down 1.44%.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- The Starlink constellation surpassed 11,000 satellites, with SpaceX filing an FCC proposal for up to 100,000 satellites long-term; a separate India filing covers nearly 30,000 satellites including direct-to-cell technology, though partnership details remain unconfirmed.
- Grok voice is reportedly resolving over 15,000 inbound Starlink support/sales calls daily and fulfilling 3,000+ orders per week.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
- SpaceX completed its 100th launch of the year, carrying 24 Starlink satellites; Musk said SpaceX now launches over 80% of the world's mass to orbit, targeting ~2.25 million tons/year by 2032 versus ~2,000 tons in 2025 (Musk's own figures).

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 13 was recovered from the ocean near Christmas Island after 24 days adrift; it will not be reflown but will be inspected for heat-shield tile damage. Flight 14 is expected in early September, without ship recovery planned.
- Musk said SpaceX expects to catch and reuse a Starship ship within a few months, with first ship reflight targeted late this year or early next year.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Nvidia announced SpaceX AI will deploy new Vera CPUs for Grok and its first Starmind AI satellite, an orbital rack-scale system based on an optimized Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 design; Musk targets Q4 2027 for orbital launch with significant scale by 2028.
- Nvidia disclosed a roughly $21 billion stake in SpeX and a separate ~$22 billion stake in Intel, a Terafab partner alongside SpaceX and Tesla.
