This video from the "jobhakdi" channel, hosted by Jo Bhakdi, focuses on what he considers the most underappreciated takeaway from Tesla's recent earnings call: the imminent commercial debut and production launch of Optimus Gen 3 (Version 3). Jo Bhakdi argues that this represents a massive "soft catalyst" for Tesla's stock that has been largely overlooked by mainstream media and analysts, and he lays out a bullish case for Optimus sales and deployment scaling in 2026 and beyond.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Gen 3 debut and production timeline confirmed:** On the earnings call, Elon Musk stated that Tesla will go into production and debut Optimus Version 3 — the commercial version — within 90 days, placing the timeline approximately in July 2026. Tesla Ming (a Tesla enthusiast and China-based news source) suggests a midyear debut around June 2026, with mass production ramp starting in July and August 2026. This announcement was corroborated by an official post on Tesla China's Weibo account, which Jo Bhakdi interprets as a significant signal given that Tesla uses Weibo to recruit Chinese engineers and maintain supplier relationships.

2. **Tesla's deliberate silence explained:** Elon Musk explained on the earnings call that Tesla has been completely silent about Optimus for over 9 months due to the highly competitive and sophisticated nature of the humanoid robotics space. Unlike autonomous driving competitors, humanoid robot competitors — many of them Chinese companies like Xpeng — are capable of reverse-engineering Tesla's designs from demo footage. Musk stated Tesla would not show anything about Optimus until the last possible moment, which he defined as the commercial production launch itself.

3. **Chinese supply chain dominance for Optimus components:** Jo Bhakdi notes that key Optimus components — including hands, actuators, and other hardware — are sourced from China, which he describes as a national security risk but an unavoidable reality given China's manufacturing dominance in these fields. Tesla's need to maintain strong relationships with Chinese suppliers and recruit top Chinese engineers is a key reason Tesla China makes official Weibo announcements about Optimus.

4. **Supply chain leaks suggest up to 70,000 units annually:** Jo Bhakdi references a leak reported in approximately December 2025 from Tesla Ming involving Chinese manufacturers who stated they were preparing supply chains for a run rate of up to 70,000 Tesla Optimus units per year for 2026. He interprets this as implying something on the order of over 10,000 units actually produced in 2026, with multiple thousands potentially deployed within Tesla's own operations this year.

5. **Figure robotics used as a competitive benchmark:** Jo Bhakdi cites Figure (led by CEO Brett Adcock), described as Tesla Optimus's biggest American competitor, as a reference point for scaling. Figure reached approximately 150 units per month in April (of its scaling ramp), having begun meaningful scaling in February. Jo Bhakdi argues this demonstrates that if a less-advanced competitor can achieve that scale, Tesla can comfortably deploy multiple thousands of Optimus units in 2026 given Optimus's presumed superiority.

6. **Bullish sales thesis: millions sold before robots are "perfect":** Jo Bhakdi argues that Tesla Optimus does not need to outperform a human in physical labor to achieve massive sales. He believes millions of private households and industrial customers would purchase Optimus at approximately $40,000 with a subscription of approximately $1,000 per month even if it is less efficient than a human, due to its appeal and novelty. He speculates that industries like fast food (citing McDonald's, Burger King, Subway) could place pilot orders for marketing value alone. He expresses confidence Tesla could reach 1 million Optimus units sold per year by 2030 or before.

7. **True human-labor superiority estimated around 2030–2031:** Jo Bhakdi draws a distinction between early commercial sales and the inflection point where Optimus clearly and definitively outperforms humans in specific physical labor tasks. He places that inflection point at approximately 2030–2031, at which point he believes demand becomes effectively unlimited, analogous to demand for large language model AI tools. The hard "forcing function" catalyst for Optimus — similar to what robotaxi scaling represents for autonomous driving — is projected by him for 2027 or 2028.

8. **Optimus as a "soft catalyst" for the stock in the near term:** Jo Bhakdi categorizes the upcoming Optimus Gen 3 reveal and production launch as a significant "soft catalyst" — not a hard forcing function that compels bears to capitulate, but one that motivates bulls and rational middle-ground investors to act. He contrasts this with "hard catalysts" like robotaxi fleet scaling, which directly drives measurable cash flow. He expects the Optimus reveal (assuming a strong demo) to move the stock materially.
