This episode of a Tesla-focused investor show (guest-hosted by Cern Basher, with Larry as guest) surveys Giga Texas's expanding footprint (including the Optimus factory and Cortex 2 data center), regulatory friction over autonomous vehicles in New Jersey, personal anecdotes praising FSD performance, a Chinese fast-charging battery announcement, feature requests for voice-controlled navigation, a running list of 2026 Tesla catalysts, and a segment featuring Andrej Karpathy's comments on Elon Musk's management style.

## Robotaxi
1. **Giga Texas fleet and infrastructure growth:** Drone footage (credited to Joe Tegtmeyer) shows steel going up for a roughly 1-million-unit-capacity Optimus factory near the main Giga Texas plant, alongside a growing fleet of Cybercabs, Cybertrucks, and Model Y/Model Y L units in production and testing, plus a new Megachanger buildout for Tesla Semi and an expanding test track with cobblestone sections.
2. **Robotaxi city rollout status:** A community-made map (credited to a third-party analyst, "Grace K") shows Tesla robotaxis deployed and driverless in four cities (three in Texas plus Miami), with Tampa and Orlando pending, and test-driver operations expanding into additional cities including Las Vegas, Reno, Phoenix, and San Francisco (with drivers); the hosts note Tesla had targeted Tampa, Orlando, Las Vegas, and Phoenix by the end of the prior quarter but only Miami had launched so far.
3. **New Jersey bill threatens vision-only autonomy:** A proposed New Jersey bill would require autonomous vehicles to carry a "redundant safety system" (interpreted by the hosts as effectively mandating LiDAR), which would exclude Tesla's vision-only approach; Tesla has set up an outreach page urging residents to contact legislators, citing its safety data (approaching 12 billion FSD miles) and New Jersey traffic fatality statistics discussed on the show.
4. **Waymo expands to four new cities:** Waymo announced testing of fully autonomous vehicles in Denver, Las Vegas, San Diego, and Tampa. Larry argues Waymo's business model — many cities with few, expensive, hand-built vehicles — can't support surge pricing (which he says drives most ride-hailing profit margin) and therefore can't scale the way Tesla's retail-fleet model can.
5. **Robotaxi rollout cited as top near-term catalyst:** Larry says the most important development to watch this quarter is a clear pattern of Tesla expanding robotaxi/Cybercab operations to new cities, calling that operational rollout the strongest signal of a broader business transformation, more important to him than any single flagged event.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Personal accounts of strong FSD performance:** Both hosts recount recent long highway drives (Florida round trips) with no interventions, including FSD smoothly handling a police officer directing traffic and successfully navigating around road debris (pillows, a stripped tire) amid swerving traffic; both describe the system as feeling highly reliable, though one host still notes parking garages remain a weak spot.
2. **FSD dynamically rerouting around road closures:** One host described FSD autonomously exiting a highway and rerouting around a fully shut-down road/accident scene in Houston without prompting.
3. **Voice control and personalization requests:** Users (cited via social media posts) want FSD to accept verbal, context-based instructions (e.g., identifying a specific house or preferred route) and want parking-location profiles tied to the vehicle rather than the driver; Ashok Elluswamy, head of Tesla's FSD/AI program, reportedly responded that the team is working on it. Hosts also noted general improvement in the car's voice control functionality over the past few years.
4. **Tesla catalyst tracker for 2026:** Hosts referenced a running list of anticipated Tesla milestones including European FSD approval (in progress), FSD supervised approval potentially in China and Israel, a possible Cybercab event and first paid Cybercab ride, roughly 10 million cumulative vehicles produced (expected around July, delivered around August), next-generation Roadster news, advanced technology fab construction progress, an Optimus Gen 3 reveal, a shareholder meeting likely in Q4, Cortex 2 running at full capacity, and Megapack/mega-block/mega-pod developments — noting the "begin construction" milestone for the advanced tech fab is ambiguous and may need redefinition (e.g., first steel erection).

## Energy
1. **Cortex 2 buildout at Giga Texas:** The Cortex 2 data center is estimated to be operating at roughly half of its activated capacity, with Megapacks and cooling infrastructure being installed on the remaining side; a host who last visited about six months prior noted significant progress since then.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chinese ultra-fast charging battery announced:** A Chinese company's "gold brick battery" was demonstrated charging from 10% to 70% in about 4 minutes and to roughly 97% in under 9 minutes, sustaining very high charging power (cited around 500 kW above 80% state of charge). Larry expressed skepticism about real-world importance, calling charging speed largely a concern for non-Tesla-owners rather than actual owners, and noted uncertainty about the battery's cold-weather performance, chemistry, cost, and longevity impact from fast charging; he noted it uses LFP chemistry and a 900V architecture. Hosts noted faster charging could reduce robotaxi charging infrastructure needs if adopted at scale.
