France's Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot said France will not yet authorize Tesla's "Full Self-Driving" (Supervised) "as it currently stands," citing two safety concerns, while framing the delay as a push for EU-wide harmonization rather than outright rejection.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Two specific safety concerns cited:** Tabarot said France found the system allows speeding, and that French data shows FSD "doesn't guarantee an optimal level of driver attention in urban settings and during the most complex maneuvers, such as lane changes, intersections, or roundabouts." He stated the system "is not a self-driving system" and that "the safety trade-offs are not yet sufficient to authorize it as it currently stands."

2. **France seeks EU-wide sign-off, not a Netherlands rubber-stamp:** Tesla received FSD Supervised approval in the Netherlands in April via the Dutch regulator RDW — the first EU country to approve it — with Denmark becoming the fourth country in June. The RDW has been pushing to convert its national exemption into an EU-wide type approval through the European Commission and UNECE. France is declining to activate FSD on French roads based solely on another country's approval, instead wanting a harmonized European framework with common test protocols first.

3. **Ongoing talks and timeline:** Tabarot said France is continuing technical discussions with the Netherlands, other European states, and Tesla; a Ministry of Transport representative traveled to the Netherlands last week to review the data. He plans to convene France's autonomous-vehicle ecosystem this autumn to set direction. He said France supports the technology and has "real cards to play" but won't act "at the expense of safety."

## Market & Competition
1. **France not alone in EU skepticism:** Sweden told the EU in June to reject FSD unless Tesla removes the speeding-allowance behavior; the article notes European regulators have raised similar concerns since spring.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues France's approach is reasonable — a conditional delay with fixable, specific demands (stop allowing speeding, prove urban driver-monitoring works) rather than a rejection of the technology, and characterizes it as France declining to "inherit the Netherlands' homework without checking it."
