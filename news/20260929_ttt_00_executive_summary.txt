## Brief

- Starship Flight 14 reaches orbit, deploys 20 V3 Starlink satellites
- SPCX slides on 328M-share unlock despite Flight 14 success
- Tesla opens $30–34B credit facility, Fitch rates it BBB
- SEC clears Tesla's auto-vote retail shareholder program
- Tesla Roadster unveiling delayed to October 15
- FSD tops 15 billion total miles; approved in Croatia

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 14 completed six orbits, re-entered with heat shield tiles intact, executed the belly-flip, and landed on the water as planned — the vehicle's first full orbital flight, per multiple sources. SpaceX's own blog noted one Raptor vacuum engine shut down early during ascent, but the remaining five engines burned longer to preserve the planned trajectory. One host argues the water landing shows Starship is "already re-landable," with a Mechazilla tower catch now a smaller remaining step; a tower-catch attempt is speculated for Flight 15.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 14 deployed 20 next-generation V3 Starlink satellites, called SpaceX's first "revenue-generating" Starship flight and roughly 10x more powerful per satellite than the prior generation. Starlink Mobile also launched in Kazakhstan, extending direct-to-device coverage to areas without terrestrial mobile service.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX fell despite the Flight 14 success, which one host attributes to a 328-million-share unlock (~15% of float) around September 24, with another 328-million-share unlock due October 9; he's opened short puts near $146 support. A separate host argues SpaceX's ~$2.1 trillion market cap is roughly 2x Tesla's despite SpaceX revenue estimated at only ~30% of Tesla's, calling Tesla "such a bargain" by comparison. Merger speculation continues: Musk has floated more "overlap" between the companies, and Electrek reports Tesla's SEC-approved retail voting program lands amid an unusually delayed 2026 annual meeting (last held November 2025, with a Texas legal threshold for a court-ordered meeting arriving in early December).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's Colossus compute could roughly double in Q4, from an estimated ~2.1 million to ~4.4 million H100-equivalents, as GB300 batches land through November and December, per a host's analysis of Musk's disclosed figures; SpaceX is expected to exit the year near 2 GW of capacity. Anthropic's IPO prospectus reportedly shows new SpaceX AI compute deals reaching up to $84.5 billion through 2029, up from an initial ~$45 billion deal.

## Financials
- Tesla disclosed access to $30 billion in new credit facilities from Citi and Wells Fargo (potentially $34 billion with revolvers), replacing an unused $5 billion line, per an 8-K filing; Fitch rated the company BBB, a tier below S&P and Moody's, citing expected capex. Tesla holds over $40 billion in cash but was cash-flow negative last quarter for the first time since Q1 2024, with 2026 capex guided at $25 billion (up from $8.5 billion in 2025) and similar levels expected in 2027.

## Market & Competition
- The SEC approved Tesla's "Issuer Voluntary Retail Voting Program," letting retail shareholders opt in to auto-vote with board recommendations on future matters (excluding contested elections and M&A votes). Exxon and Goldman Sachs received similar relief; Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev publicly backed the program. Electrek's editorial stance calls the program one-sided since it only lets shareholders pre-commit to the board, not against it, and flags the timing alongside the delayed annual meeting and merger speculation as worth scrutiny.

## Autonomous Driving
- Supervised FSD was approved in Croatia, the eighth EU country (~12.7% of EU population), with an EU-wide vote needing 15 of 27 states and 65% population support expected in December. FSD's mileage counter surpassed 15 billion total miles (5.8 billion city miles), with roughly 7.8–8 billion miles added in 2026 alone — more than the 2020–2025 total combined.

## Robotaxi
- NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison signaled a shift toward performance-based rather than design-based vehicle safety standards, proposing amendments to five FMVSS rules (removing requirements like physical gear indicators, windshield wipers, and manual brakes) that hosts argue improve Cybercab's regulatory odds; a new "Ascend Consortium" with SAE aims to build a national autonomous-driving competency standard. Tesla's self-certification path avoids the 2,500-unit exemption cap, though one host cautions this depends on federal acceptance.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla Semi orders continue growing: a Catalyst Mobility spokeswoman said all 2,500 trucks from the largest US electric semi order will be delivered within 18 months, and trucking firm HMD ordered 50 more Semis. A tracker cited by one host shows 4,397 verified Semi commitments over 18 months. Separately, Tesla raised U.S. financing APRs on several Model 3 and Model Y trims (e.g., base Model Y to 1.99% from 1.49%).
