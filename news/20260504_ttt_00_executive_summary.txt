## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3 is a ground-up runtime rewrite, not an incremental update.** Tesla rebuilt the AI compiler and runtime using MLRI, targeting ~36 guaranteed inference cycles per second. The architectural overhaul — distinct from any model change — is what produced the 20% reaction time improvement and explains the long development cycle before release.

- **Reaction time now estimated at ~0.2 seconds — deep into superhuman territory.** Human safety standards allow ~1.5 seconds; elite sprinters approach the 0.1-second physical limit. Per the cernbasher panel, FSD 14.3's speed advantage is compounded by calibrated precision control, avoiding the overcorrection errors (e.g., wheel-yanking on wet roads) that make fast human reactions dangerous.

- **RL training on real robotaxi incidents was a major component of 14.3.** Safety drivers recorded edge-case incidents — including a minor bumper contact when a lead vehicle stopped suddenly — and fed them back through scenario simulators. Improvements include better rare/low-visibility scenario handling, enhanced 3D geometry, expanded traffic sign recognition, and improved animal avoidance.

- **FSD v14.3.2 rolling out in two staged waves on the same day.** Per Sawyer Merritt, an early wave at 03:52 UTC targeted HW4 owners and included a redesigned FSD popup interface; a broader second wave followed at 20:38 UTC. Per futureaza, the build is already on more than 40% of the eligible fleet and expected to go wide soon.

- **10 billion cumulative supervised FSD miles crossed, with rate accelerating sharply.** The fleet now averages ~28.8 million miles per day (1,000 miles every 3 seconds). It took 5.5 years to reach the first 10 billion miles; the next 10 billion will accumulate in approximately 11.5 months. Per Jeff Lutz, FSD is currently performing at approximately 90% safer than human driving.

- **Elon Musk on FSD version 15: future milestone, not a deployment precondition.** Musk described version 15 as featuring a model ~10x larger than the current small model, and said it will "far exceed human levels of safety even in completely unsupervised and complex situations." The cernbasher panel explicitly pushes back on community interpretation that Tesla is waiting for v15 before expanding robotaxi — arguing unsupervised deployment is proceeding on 14.x variants now.

## Robotaxi

- **Austin unsupervised robotaxi fleet extended to nighttime hours for the first time.** Previously limited to midafternoon, the operational window expansion signals improved system confidence across more challenging low-light conditions — a qualitative milestone beyond raw fleet count.

- **Biggest single-day unsupervised fleet addition on record preceded the current canonical count.** Per RobotaxiTracker.com data cited in sources, seven unsupervised vehicles were added in a single day — approximately 20% daily growth, and roughly 50% fleet growth over ~10 days. Per Jeff Lutz, Tesla appears to be running a probabilistic incident-rate model and adding vehicles as performance metrics improve.

- **Staged Houston vehicles with assigned plates signal continued near-term additions.** Footage of a Houston staging lot shows additional robotaxi units ready to deploy. Jeff Lutz describes internal pace-of-ramp debate as normal for any product launch.

- **San Antonio robotaxi ops manager job posted; multi-city fleet support hiring underway.** Herbert and Jeff Lutz view the San Antonio posting as a strong signal it is the next Texas city to receive service. Separate fleet support postings reference Orlando, Dallas, San Francisco, and Miami. Cybercab prototypes have also been spotted in the San Francisco area.

- **January fender-benders deliberately shaped the conservative ramp cadence.** Five low-speed incidents in January generated outsized negative global media coverage. Tesla slowed unsupervised fleet growth in subsequent months to create narrative separation; per Jeff Lutz, doubling the fleet without first improving the system would have statistically doubled the incident count.

- **California AV traffic law takes effect July 1, 2026.** The law allows tickets to be issued to autonomous vehicles for violations including running red lights or failing to stop for pedestrians; accumulated violations can restrict rollout or suspend a company's driverless license. Jeff Lutz views third-party performance accountability as a net positive for the ecosystem.

- **RobotaxiTracker.com live data during futureaza stream: 91 active riders, average fare $5, average wait ~20 minutes** (as low as 7 minutes earlier that day).

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y and Model 3 ranked #1 and #2 globally among individual EV models in March 2026.** BYD requires eight or nine separate models to appear on the same top-20 list. Per Jeff Lutz, a revenue-ranked chart would skew even further toward Tesla given BYD's model fragmentation and pricing.

- **Model Y was the #1 bestselling vehicle overall in California in Q1 2026** — outselling the Camry, Civic, CR-V, and all other gasoline vehicles in the state.

- **Tesla launched Chinese-made Model 3s in Canada at C$39,940 (~US$29,000)** — the lowest-ever price for the model in that market, more than C$24,000 below the average new car price in Canada (~C$63,000). Per brighterwithherbert, Canada's trade legislation reserved a quota for vehicles with an import price under C$35,000, which Tesla leveraged while also qualifying to sell units at higher price points.

- **Tesla Semi production line completed at Giga Nevada; 50,000-unit annual run rate targeted for H2 2026.** Per Bhakdi's analysis, this would represent ~25% of the US Class 8 market by volume. One new customer ordered 40 Semis at the ACT Expo during the week. Bhakdi frames 50,000 units as the revenue equivalent of ~400,000 incremental Model Y sales per year.

- **Tesla Semi Base Charger unveiled for fleet depot charging.** Specs: 125 kW output, up to 60% charge in 4 hours, full overnight charge, 6-meter cable, 150A continuous, integrated AC/DC converter (no external cabinet), daisy-chainable up to three units, MCS protocol. Deliveries begin early 2027.

- **Tesla customer loyalty: 61%, #1 in the US** per S&P Global Mobility (February 2026), ahead of Subaru and Toyota.

- **Tesla Semi spotted at ACT Expo in Las Vegas** — the largest North American advanced commercial vehicle trade show — with three side-mirror cameras per side and an additional front bumper camera visible.

## Energy

- **SpaceX has begun construction of a solar cell factory in Bastrop, Texas.** Sited at the south end of the Starlink terminal campus, the facility is described by SpaceX Director of Solar Production Noah Kohl (via job postings) as one of the world's most advanced solar cell factories. Larry Goldberg confirmed ground was being cleared nearly a year before the announcement.

- **Elon Musk: both Tesla and SpaceX are independently targeting 100 GW/year of US solar cell manufacturing.** Combined, the two companies would represent 200 GW/year — roughly four times total US solar installations in 2024 (~50 GW). Musk estimates the goal takes approximately three years. The two programs are intentionally separate: SpaceX cells are designed for space (rollable, vacuum-rated, extreme temperature tolerant); Tesla's target rooftop applications.

- **Tesla's solar cells are undergoing a near-total redesign**, moving from 6 manufacturing zones to 18 zones per cell, per Goldberg citing Musk.

- **Tesla's Buffalo solar factory is scaling**, with 350 new jobs added, $350M invested in on-site supercomputers, and production capacity tracking toward 300 MW/year. Goldberg expressed some skepticism that current Buffalo scale is sufficient for Tesla's stated ambitions.

- **Space-based solar is ~5x more productive than ground-based**, per Musk, due to no atmospheric attenuation, continuous operation (no night/weather/seasons), and passive cooling from the cold of space. Musk: orbital AI compute will be the lowest-cost location within two to three years.

## Financials

- **Terafab — joint Tesla/SpaceX chip fabrication — described as Musk's largest project ever, surpassing Starship and Optimus.** Musk's stated goal: over one terawatt of compute per year (logic, memory, and packaging co-located), with an 80/20 split between space and ground applications. Goldberg argues the project is only viable with Tesla and SpaceX operating as a functionally integrated unit, and that cost-sharing effectively halves each company's burden.

- **Tesla capex increase announced**, focused on AI software, AI training, battery, powertrain, chip design, and AI infrastructure. Per futureaza, Nvidia is the near-term primary beneficiary; Tesla is building toward in-house chip manufacturing over time.

## Market & Competition

- **Larry Goldberg: SpaceX–Tesla merger is a "101%" certainty, likely announced in 2026.** He cites operational integration already underway across chips, solar, compute, batteries, materials, and motors — including Dr. Charles Khman serving as VP of Materials Engineering at both companies simultaneously — as making separate corporate structures untenable. He expects the process to extend through mid-2027 to fully resolve.

- **Morgan Stanley raised 2026 hyperscaler capex forecast to $805B** (from $765B prior) across Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle. Per investingagainstthegrain, Musk described today's AI infrastructure build as demand-driven, not speculative — "there are no dark GPUs."

- **Tesla is down ~13% YTD**, making it among the weakest Magnificent 7 performers alongside Microsoft. Alphabet leads the group at +23%, Amazon at +16%. The host of investingagainstthegrain targets $400+ imminently and $450 to be flat for the year.

- **BYD in talks to acquire part of Volkswagen's Dresden plant**, which would qualify it as a domestic European manufacturer and exempt it from EV import tariffs. Per futureaza, Germany offers deep automotive supply chain infrastructure despite higher costs than BYD's other considered site in Spain.

- **Waymo reportedly losing $5–6 billion per year**, per Jeff Lutz, attributed to high part counts, complex sensor arrays, and expensive repair costs — structural unit economics that worsen at scale. Bhakdi sees no credible electric Class 8 truck competitor to the Semi in the US market.

- **Nissan cancelled ~$500M US EV plant in Mississippi**, pivoting back to gas and hybrids amid inability to price EVs competitively.
