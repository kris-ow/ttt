## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3 is an architecture overhaul, not an incremental update.** Tesla rebuilt the AI compiler and runtime from the ground up using MLRI, targeting ~36 inference cycles per second. The redesign was a deliberate pause to lock in guaranteed inference performance before scaling model size.
- **Reaction time: ~0.2 seconds vs. the human standard of ~1.5 seconds.** Tesla AI's official release notes cite a 20% faster reaction time from the runtime rewrite. Community analysis places FSD 14.3 at approximately 0.2 seconds — approaching elite-sprinter territory (Olympic false-start threshold: 0.1 seconds) and far faster than the average driver, especially under fatigue, darkness, or wet conditions.
- **Faster reaction time is paired with calibrated precision, not just speed.** The panel on cernbasher highlighted that FSD modulates its response amplitude simultaneously — contrasting with the human tendency to overreact (e.g., yanking the wheel on a wet-road swerve), which compounds danger.
- **RL training pipeline significantly expanded.** A major portion of the 14.3 development cycle fed real-world robotaxi safety-driver incidents back through scenario simulators, producing improvements in rare and low-visibility scenarios, 3D geometry, traffic sign recognition, and animal avoidance.
- **FSD v14.3.2 rolling out in two waves on the same day.** Per Sawyer Merritt, an early wave (03:52 UTC) targeted HW4 owners with a redesigned FSD popup interface; a second broader wave followed at 20:38 UTC. Per futureaza, the build is already on >40% of the eligible fleet and expected to go wide soon.
- **Elon Musk on version 15: a future milestone, not a deployment precondition.** Musk confirmed v15 will carry a large model (~10x current parameter count) and will "far exceed human levels of safety even in completely unsupervised and complex situations" — but the cernbasher panel explicitly pushes back on the reading that Tesla is waiting for v15 before expanding robotaxi operations.
- **10 billion cumulative supervised FSD miles crossed; pace is accelerating sharply.** Per Sawyer Merritt, the fleet is now averaging 28.8 million FSD miles per day (1,000 miles every 3 seconds). It took 5.5 years to reach the first 10 billion miles; the next 10 billion will take approximately 11.5 months.
- **FSD performing approximately 90% safer than human driving**, per Jeff Lutz on brighterwithherbert, who argues this statistic — not isolated incident counts — should anchor any regulatory or public debate about deployment.

## Robotaxi

- **Austin unsupervised robotaxi fleet begins nighttime operations for the first time.** Previously limited to midafternoon hours, the expansion into evening driving conditions is flagged as a meaningful operational confidence signal.
- **January incidents explain the conservative ramp cadence.** Per brighterwithherbert, five low-speed fender-bender incidents in January triggered outsized global media coverage; Tesla deliberately slowed fleet growth to create narrative separation. The 14.3 reaction time improvement is cited by the cernbasher panel as directly addressing that class of incident (a robotaxi made minor bumper contact when a car ahead stopped suddenly).
- **Houston lot shows additional vehicles staged with license plates already assigned**, signaling continued near-term fleet additions as internal performance metrics continue to improve.
- **San Antonio robotaxi operations manager job posting spotted**, alongside fleet support hiring referencing Orlando, Dallas, San Francisco, and Miami. Brighterwithherbert views San Antonio as the next city to receive service. Cybercab prototypes were also spotted in the San Francisco area.
- **California AV traffic law takes effect July 1, 2026**, allowing tickets to be issued to autonomous vehicles for violations including running red lights or failing to stop for pedestrians. Accumulated violations can restrict rollout or suspend a company's driverless license — a structural enforcement mechanism, not per-incident fines.
- **Robotaxi fleet controller: 91 active riders, $5 average fare, ~20-minute average wait** (as low as 7 minutes) per RobotaxiTracker.com data displayed live on futureaza. Cybercab projected to generate $10,000–$30,000 per vehicle per year in recurring revenue (host estimate).
- **Waymo reportedly losing $5–6 billion per year**, per brighterwithherbert, attributed to high part counts, complex sensors, and elevated repair costs — a structural unit-economics problem that worsens with scale. The cernbasher panel draws a parallel between Waymo/Zoox highway incidents (slow reaction time, not failed detection) and the problem FSD 14.3's 0.2-second response directly solves.
- **Cybercab's Unboxed modular assembly is a structural repair cost advantage.** Jeff Lutz argues the design will translate into modular field disassembly and repair — potentially the most repairable vehicle in the robotaxi space.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model Y and Model 3 ranked #1 and #2 globally among individual EV models in March 2026.** BYD requires eight to nine models on the same list to approach comparable combined volume, per brighterwithherbert and futureaza.
- **Model Y was the #1 bestselling vehicle overall in California in Q1 2026** — beating the Camry, Civic, and CR-V across all powertrains.
- **China-made Model 3 launched in Canada at C$39,940 (~US$29,000)**, the lowest price ever for the model in that market — a 17% cut to Model 3 Performance pricing and approximately 50% cheaper than the long-range variant previously available. Canadian average new vehicle price is ~C$63,000, making the Model 3 over C$24,000 cheaper than the market average.
- **Tesla Semi production line completed at Giga Nevada.** A new customer ordered 40 Semis at the ACT Expo in Las Vegas, where the Semi was also spotted with three cameras per side mirror and a front bumper camera. Per jobhakdi, the Nevada factory targets a 50,000-unit annualized run rate by H2 2026, which would represent ~25% of the US Class 8 heavy truck market.
- **Tesla Semi Base Charger unveiled** for fleet depot use: 125 kW output, up to 60% charge in 4 hours, 6-meter cable, no external cabinet, supports daisy-chaining up to three units, MCS protocol. Deliveries begin early 2027.
- **Tesla Semi fuel economics vs. diesel:** ~$75 in electricity vs. ~$375 in diesel for a 500-mile trip — an ~80% fuel cost reduction — with no credible electric Class 8 competitor identified at scale, per jobhakdi.
- **Tesla US customer loyalty: 61%, #1 in the US** per S&P Global Mobility (February 2026), ahead of Subaru and Toyota.
- **Nissan cancelled ~$500M US EV plant in Mississippi**, pivoting back to gas and hybrids.

## Energy

- **Elon Musk: both Tesla and SpaceX are independently targeting 100 GW/year of US solar cell manufacturing**, each, within approximately three years. Combined, 200 GW/year would be roughly four times the entire US solar industry's 2024 installation output of ~50 GW.
- **SpaceX breaking ground on a solar cell factory in Bastrop, Texas**, on the south end of the Starlink terminal complex, per a job posting from SpaceX Director of Solar Production Noah Kohl. The two companies' solar technologies differ by design: SpaceX cells must roll compactly and withstand vacuum and extreme cold; Tesla's cells target static rooftop use. Scientific knowledge — formulations, coatings — will be shared across teams.
- **Tesla's Buffalo solar factory scaling toward 300 MW/year**, with 350 new jobs added and $350M invested in on-site supercomputers. Tesla solar cells are moving from 6 manufacturing zones to 18 zones — a near-total redesign.
- **Terafab, the joint Tesla-SpaceX chip fabrication venture announced in March 2026**, targets over one terawatt of compute per year (logic, memory, and packaging co-located), with an 80/20 space/ground allocation. Larry Goldberg called it "the biggest project Elon has ever undertaken" — surpassing Starship and Optimus — and argued it is only executable if the two companies operate as an integrated unit.
- **Orbital AI data centers framed as 5–6 years away** by Goldberg, enabled by Starship full reusability dropping launch costs from ~$1,000/ton to ~$30–50/ton. Musk: "The lowest cost place to put AI will be space, and that'll be true within two years, maybe three."

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX-Tesla merger: Goldberg at "101% confidence," with a 2026 announcement possible** and full resolution expected by mid-2027. Cited evidence: Dr. Charles Khman serving simultaneously as VP of Materials Engineering at both companies; shared engineers, chips, batteries, motors, and solar science across Terafab and other projects.
- **SpaceX estimated valuation ~$1.7–$2 trillion**; Goldberg proposes a rolling IPO lockup structure (beginning at 2–5% of shares tradeable two weeks post-IPO, expanding over six months) to prevent a supply flood. He predicts SpaceX stock will be "muted" near-term, but Tesla will rerate toward SpaceX multiples as merger inevitability becomes clear.
- **Tesla down ~13% YTD; needs ~$450 to be flat for the year**, per investingagainstthegrain. Within the Magnificent 7, Alphabet leads at +23% YTD; Tesla and Microsoft are the laggards at ~-13–14%, which the host frames as the logical capital rotation destination.
- **Morgan Stanley raised 2026 hyperscaler capex forecast to $805B** (from $765B) for five major cloud operators. Host characterizes this as demand-driven, not speculative — "there are no dark GPUs."
- **Ford CEO Jim Farley visits China 6–7 times per year** and describes it as "the most humbling thing I've ever seen" while simultaneously lobbying to block Chinese EV imports. Jeff Lutz on brighterwithherbert calls the messaging incoherent; Ford's April sales showed declines across gas, hybrid, and EV segments. Ford is estimated to be 5–7 years behind Tesla on every major strategic move.
- **BYD in talks to acquire part of Volkswagen's Dresden plant**, which would make BYD a domestic European manufacturer and exempt it from EV import tariffs.
- **China dominates current global solar manufacturing**, but Goldberg argues new technology — perovskite cells, automated production, high-efficiency silicon — is shifting the economic case for US-based manufacturing back toward viability.
