## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Tesla disclosed unsupervised robotaxi miles rose from 380,000 to 1 million over six weeks following Cybercab's Austin introduction, per ARK's Tasha Keeney.
- Tesla posted a fleet-buyer interest form gauging demand for purchasing Cybercab fleets; Chamath reportedly signed up publicly, while Ross Gerber argued individual owners shouldn't bear fleet risk Tesla could absorb instead, given Tesla's reported 20–30% cut.
- Cybercab launch-day pricing ran ~35% below Model Y ($7.77 vs $12.10); by the following Sunday a 1.3-mile Cybercab ride cost $15 with a 1-hour-plus wait versus $7.95 for UberX, per a source the host cites as "Tesla Oracle" — suggesting demand outpaced fleet capacity.
- Only 45 Cybercabs were reportedly registered in Austin at launch.
- NHTSA opened an audit query into Tesla's self-certification process the same day the robotaxi service launched; Wells Fargo called the launch "underwhelming" and TSLA fell 6% that Friday, per the host (exact causal link unconfirmed).
- ARK Invest frames Cybercab and "swing capacity" — private FSD owners releasing cars into the network at peak demand — as central to a multi-trillion-dollar robotaxi opportunity; guest Larry Goldberg argues fleet owners without surge capacity are limited to thin margins, while Tesla's dual role as manufacturer and operator gives it an edge ARK's value-chain framework understates.
- ARK's long-term projection of rides as low as 25 cents/mile at scale was flagged by both commentators as likely a cost figure, not a sustainable retail price.

## Autonomous Driving
- Cybercab's "unboxed" parallel-module production process cuts line size ~50% while increasing output; the vehicle uses no rare-earth metals and is Tesla's first with fully electronic brake-by-wire. Cars are currently coming off the line roughly every 10 seconds versus ~34 seconds conventionally, with Musk targeting 5 seconds per vehicle.
- Active FSD subscriptions reached 1.48 million in Q2, up 56% year-over-year at $99/month, an estimated ~$790 million/year in recurring revenue, per the host.
- Cybercab's lack of a steering wheel confines it to areas where unsupervised FSD is permitted — currently parts of Texas and Florida, per the host.
- A drone flyover of Giga Texas reportedly showed Model Y L and Cybercab units equipped with Starlink modules for the first time, intended to keep robotaxis connected when parked away from cellular coverage, per Musk's earlier Q2 earnings-call comments as relayed by the host.

## Electric Vehicles
- Next-gen Roadster unveil is officially confirmed for October 1 in Waco, Texas; invites went to reservation holders and the Roadster site added a countdown. Tesla will hold Cybercab, Semi factory, and Roadster events within a roughly 30-day span.
- Model Y L US deliveries expected within two weeks as VINs are assigned to order holders.
- Tesla Semi Standard Range will launch first in Europe, where short-haul trips reportedly make up ~50% of continental semi-truck activity, per a poster's account.
- Model S and Model X production ended June 30; the six-seat Model Y L (mid-$60,000s) begins US deliveries in October/November.
- A Cybercab ride went viral on streamer IShowSpeed's livestream (cited 175M followers across platforms), introducing the driverless vehicle to a mass audience.

## Financials
- Tesla Insurance is live in 13 states with ~$238 million in Q1 premiums written; carriers were reportedly profitable on underwriting for the first time, per the host.
- AAA pegs full new-car ownership cost at ~$965/month; the host frames this as the benchmark a hypothetical Tesla subscription bundle would need to beat — no such product has been announced by Tesla.
