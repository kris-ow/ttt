This episode covers Tesla's first personal-use Cybercab sale with its subscription terms, a wave of Robotaxi city and fleet expansion data across Texas and beyond, a fast-moving saga over winding down (then extending) Cybertruck production at Giga Texas to make room for Cybercab, SpaceX's 15th Starship flight and its booster/ship recovery attempt, and a busy week of SpaceX revenue, data-center, and Starlink spectrum/phone news, plus renewed Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab now sellable to select owners for personal use:** Tesla opened invitation-only personal ownership of the Cybercab (no steering wheel or pedals) for $29,990 plus a $299/month subscription covering Autopilot, tires, windshields, and maintenance (insurance and charging separate); a $1,000 deposit is required, one per customer, with Texas delivery estimated for December. The car only operates within existing Robotaxi zones and has touch controls allowing movement up to 2 mph for short distances only (credit: Scott's Rule08's breakdown of the terms).
2. **Elon pushes back on the subscription price:** After Marcus Brownlee criticized the $299/month fee, Elon Musk said it should be closer to the ~$115/month FSD-and-connectivity fee charged on other models — though the host notes the $299 fee bundles maintenance and other services beyond just FSD/connectivity, so a direct comparison may understate what's included.
3. **Add-to-ridesharing timeline:** Ashok Elluswamy said on the last earnings call that Tesla owners will likely be able to add their cars to the ride-hailing network around next year's second quarter; currently personal-use Cybercabs cannot earn ride-hailing income and can only be parked in the owner's driveway, per Elluswamy.
4. **San Antonio launch and Austin expansion:** Robotaxi went live in San Antonio as Tesla's eighth city, launching with roughly 275 square miles — the largest day-one footprint Tesla has opened with — and recorded early demand (a reported ride-price surge of $8.32 within 11 minutes) and rain-handling on night one. The same day, per RobotaxiTracker.com, Austin's zone grew by about 170 square miles (+62.5%) to roughly 442 square miles, making Austin Tesla's largest zone again; combined, Tesla's Texas robotaxi footprint grew from about 751 to 1,194 square miles overnight (a 59% jump), reportedly about four times the size of Waymo's Texas footprint despite Tesla launching later.
5. **24/7 operations across all cities:** Tesla's Robotaxi account announced round-the-clock service in all eight live cities (Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Dallas, Fort Worth, Miami, Orlando, Tampa); Elon framed this as a key robotaxi advantage over human drivers who work far fewer hours per week.
6. **Tesla vs. Waymo registered vehicle count in Texas:** Per the host, Waymo had roughly 422 more registered vehicles than Tesla as of September 1, but Tesla briefly overtook Waymo (1,302 vs 1,297) on Tuesday before Waymo pulled back ahead (1,321 vs 1,303) by Friday morning; the host characterizes the narrow gap as not very meaningful given ongoing registration changes in both directions.
7. **Fleet activity metrics:** A tracker (Phil Beisel) reported Tesla robotaxis logged about 5 million unattended miles in one week; a separate Cybercab-spotting service using Austin surveillance cameras recorded 1,544 Cybercab appearances in one day, 1,480 without a driver behind the wheel.
8. **Expansion signals beyond Texas:** Research cited by the host (Tesla Yoda) found robotaxi-related job postings in 42 US cities, with autonomous-testing-operator-only vacancies in 39 cities seen as a precursor to robotaxi launches. In Las Vegas, a Tesla robotaxi test fleet reportedly grew to 53 vehicles; Nevada regulations are said to require Tesla to begin commercial service within a 120-day window whose start date is unclear, putting a deadline in late November or mid-December. Land-permit findings suggest new Supercharger/robotaxi-support infrastructure (totaling a reported 64 planned stations) in the Charlotte, NC area ahead of any public Tesla announcement there. Separately, a report cited by the host says Saudi Arabia is accelerating robotaxi rollout plans with Tesla as a leading candidate, alongside a Department of Transportation slide reportedly showing a Cybercab testing plan for Q3 2027.
9. **Market-size framing from Musk and Palmer Luckey:** Responding to a skeptic's comparison to the ~$175B combined value of the five largest ride-hailing companies, Palmer Luckey argued the real addressable figure is total ride-hailing revenue (over $150B) minus the labor cost of the industry's drivers (cited at 16 million). Elon Musk said he expects Cybercab to eventually provide most passenger transportation in any jurisdiction where not legally prohibited — a vision of capturing most vehicle-miles, not just existing ride-hailing demand.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production line changes at Giga Texas:** Elon announced Cybertruck production at Giga Texas would wind down around mid-next-year to free up capacity for Cybercab production, drawing criticism from some owners; Tesla's former Cybertruck development lead argued the platform's technology lives on in the Cybercab. Musk later said the order book is larger than Tesla can fill before the planned changeover and that an extra shift would be added, then said Tesla would have to refund some deposits, and by midweek reversed again, saying production would be extended until the end of summer 2027 to process the bulk of outstanding orders.
2. **Cybertruck pricing and future changes:** Tesla raised Cybertruck prices by 9% and stopped taking orders for the premium all-wheel-drive variant, leaving only the Cyberbeast orderable at $109,990, with delivery estimates pushed to March–June 2027. Musk said a future Cybertruck version will get a smaller (~16% reduced) battery pack thanks to efficiency gains and will be offered in a "liquid silver" color.
3. **EV sales strength:** Per Kelley Blue Book data cited, Model Y and Model 3 combined outsold all other EVs in the US combined in the recent quarter; in Australia, Model Y was reported as the top-selling passenger car year-to-date, ahead of the Toyota RAV4.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **European and global FSD regulatory progress:** Finland approved FSD Supervised (branded "Tesla Assisted Driving" in Europe) as the 11th European country to do so, with Finland's regulator crediting the rename and stricter speed settings; Finland allows immediate use ahead of an expected Europe-wide vote this winter. The UK Department for Transport reportedly expects new UN rules on supervised autonomous driving to take effect December 24, which could be adopted automatically by around 60 countries. Tesla also says it will launch in Argentina later this month.
2. **Lars Moravy comments on liability (Jay Leno interview):** Tesla VP Lars Moravy told Jay Leno that the vehicle owner bears responsibility under FSD Supervised, but Tesla is considered the "driver" for its own robotaxi service and for future driverless deployments where Tesla wrote the software — a liability framework distinction between supervised consumer use and company-operated autonomy.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus cost and leasing model:** Elon Musk told Ron Baron that Optimus would cost between $20,000 and $30,000, with Tesla planning to lease rather than sell the robots.
2. **Optimus chip memory reduction:** Responding to a report that Tesla reduced memory on the Optimus AI6 chip by about a third, Musk said memory bandwidth was preserved — suggesting a cost reduction without a corresponding performance loss, per the host's interpretation.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15 recovery attempt:** Starship's 15th flight launched after receiving its FAA license, aiming for the first-ever simultaneous catch of both the Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage back at the tower — a milestone not previously achieved (the last booster catch was reported as occurring in March 2025). The booster catch was called off and Super Heavy instead performed a water landing in the Gulf of Mexico; Musk attributed this to the booster running low on fuel. The ship itself reached orbit, deployed all 26 onboard Starlink V3 satellites, and completed roughly 14 orbits with SpaceX reporting the heat shield in good condition; a tower-catch attempt for the ship was planned for the following afternoon with the outcome unknown at time of recording.
2. **Starlink V3 satellite capacity:** SpaceX said the 26 deployed Starlink V3 satellites add bandwidth equivalent to roughly 10 Falcon 9 launches' worth of capacity; Musk said future Starship flights could carry more than 50 satellites at once.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Spectrum acquisition pending:** The FCC accepted SpaceX's application to acquire 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management, covering 546 licenses and nearly all of the US population; the deal still needs final approval, with public comment running through December 1.
2. **Direct-to-phone momentum:** Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon described SpaceX as building a nationwide terrestrial-plus-satellite 5G network. In Brazil, Vivo's Starlink-based phone service reportedly gained 10 million users within two weeks of launch, per Vivo's CEO, providing connectivity (messaging, maps) in areas without standard signal.
3. **Next-gen satellite and hardware plans:** Musk said a future satellite generation would have 10x more power than V3 and, with about 100 lasers per satellite, he believes a constellation of over 100,000 satellites could outpace undersea cables for global data transport, citing light traveling faster through space than through fiber. SpaceX also released the Starlink Mini 2 (priced at $999, with a battery lasting about 4.5 hours), shipping in December. United Airlines said Starlink is now installed on over half of its daily flights.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Revenue targets from Musk at the Baron conference:** On November 6, Musk said SpaceX is on track to reach $100 billion in annualized revenue (one month's revenue times 12) as soon as December, barring a disaster, and put the odds of reaching $1 trillion in annualized revenue next year at about 2% — up from zero previously, in his words. He also said SpaceX's revenue this quarter alone would exceed NASA's full annual budget (cited at roughly $18.8 billion).
2. **Compute revenue estimate:** Deutsche Bank estimated SpaceX's computing business alone could end the year with about $77 billion in contracted annual revenue, separate from Starlink or launch revenue — described by the host as the bank's estimate, not a confirmed figure.
3. **New data center projects:** SpaceX unveiled plans for a $50 billion, up to 6-gigawatt AI supercomputer complex in Cochise County, Arizona (construction starting 2027, no on-site gas turbines, with claimed 10–40% electricity bill reductions for a local cooperative). A separate $24 billion, 444-acre AI supercomputer project near the Port of Brownsville was revealed via a city hearing notice; the site was annexed by Starbase and removed from Brownsville's jurisdiction (which had just imposed a 30-day data-center moratorium) after SpaceX reportedly offered about $220 million for water and sewer infrastructure. Memphis's mayor said SpaceX's local data-center tax payments more than doubled to $54 million, helping the city beat budget.
4. **Solar equipment order for orbital compute:** SpaceX ordered equipment from German company Singulus to produce 100 gigawatts per year of solar cells, which Musk linked to power needs for Starlink's orbital computers.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Tesla-SpaceX merger talk resurfaces:** Bloomberg reportedly said merger talks between SpaceX and Tesla are moving forward, with a possible announcement as early as December, though terms are undisclosed; the host flags this as an unconfirmed report. TSLA closed around $390 and SPCX around $139 that Friday, with Tesla shares rising after the report.
2. **Board change:** Google's Don Harrison resigned from the SpaceX board; Musk cited too many conflicts of interest with Alphabet. Despite this, Alphabet reportedly purchased 3.85 million SpaceX shares last quarter.
