This episode of "brighterwithherbert" covers a dense 24-hour news cycle for Tesla investors, featuring host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist and tech entrepreneur. The discussion centers on the rapid expansion of Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi fleet into Dallas and Houston, accelerating European FSD supervised approval signals from Italy and Spain, Uber's surprising strategic pivot to buy Lucid vehicles instead of Teslas, and the emerging SpaceX IPO details. The overarching thesis is that Tesla's robotaxi deployment is approaching a critical inflection point where scale becomes possible quickly, and regulatory approval globally will follow adoption rather than precede it.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Unsupervised Robotaxi Expansion into Dallas and Houston:** The robotaxi tracker has now identified two unsupervised Tesla robotaxis operating in Dallas and two in Houston, bringing the total number of spotted unsupervised vehicles to 17. The cumulative fleet tracked stands at 611 cars, including inactive units. Jo Bhakdi emphasized that Houston is the third-largest U.S. city and Dallas is also enormous, making these market entries significant milestones, not symbolic gestures. He argued that skeptics who dismiss small numbers are making the analytical error of assuming the past predicts the future — the hockey stick scaling effect means that once proven, deployment could reach millions of vehicles within a couple of years. Both host and guest stressed that if unsupervised operation works in these cities, nothing structurally prevents rapid expansion to tens of thousands of vehicles.

2. **Tesla VP's Safety Confidence and Real-World FSD Incident:** Tesla Vice President of AI Ashok Kaliswami was quoted as saying, "You are unequivocally safer using FSD," reflecting internal confidence in the system's safety profile. A real-world example was shared from Tesla AI Girl, who was using FSD version 14.2.2.5 when a child on a bicycle, distracted by a cell phone, swerved in front of her vehicle. The car spotted the hazard early, was cautious, and came to a complete stop, preventing an accident. The host noted that Tesla's total robotaxi service has recorded zero incidents over the past month, suggesting performance now exceeds human driving safety benchmarks.

3. **Safety as a Regulatory Forcing Function:** Jo Bhakdi made the argument that autonomous driving safety data fundamentally changes the regulatory dynamic — instead of regulators slowing adoption, proven safety statistics create political and moral pressure to approve the technology quickly. He drew a comparison to Waymo's lobbying approach, noting that once the data is clear, regulators cannot justifiably delay approval without being implicitly responsible for preventable traffic deaths. He predicted this forcing function will cause approvals to happen faster than most analysts expect, particularly in the U.S. where individual states can lead without waiting for federal consensus.

4. **European FSD Supervised Approvals Accelerating:** Multiple European countries are showing strong positive signals toward approving Tesla's FSD supervised mode. Italy's Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport has formally requested fast-tracking of Tesla's FSD supervised approval, with the request forwarded directly to the transport department and Italian road authority. If approved, Italy would become the fourth EU market with FSD supervised deployment, adding approximately 16 million people to Tesla's European autonomy footprint, joining the Netherlands and Spain. A mainstream review from the publication Independent described a test drive of FSD in the Netherlands, calling the car's behavior "impeccable" and saying its ability to spot every hazard in a busy city center was "hugely impressive," concluding with a strong recommendation for readers to try it.

5. **Spain's Active FSD Testing Data:** In response to an inquiry, Spain's traffic authority (DGT) confirmed that Tesla currently has 30 vehicles testing on Spanish roads with FSD supervised. These vehicles have driven nearly 50,000 miles (approximately 80,000 kilometers) since November 2025, with zero reported incidents to date. Spain's response was described as very positive in tone, referencing the Netherlands' RDW decision as a potential model and expressing openness to the technology improving mobility for citizens. The host noted that while Spain did not formally approve anything, the tone of the official reply was notably favorable.

6. **EU-Wide Vote Potentially in Late June:** The host shared information attributed to Roland Percher that a key EU meeting is scheduled for May 5th, 2026, at which member states will hear the Netherlands' full report on FSD supervised testing. The session was reportedly extended from 20 minutes to a full 60 minutes to allow a comprehensive presentation. A formal EU vote on broader FSD supervised approval could potentially occur by the end of June 2026, though the host cautioned against expecting a definitive outcome.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas as the Critical Deployment Battleground:** Both host and Jo Bhakdi repeatedly emphasized that what happens in Texas — specifically achieving large-scale unsupervised robotaxi deployment — is the single most important catalyst for Tesla's stock repricing. Jo Bhakdi stated that deploying thousands of unsupervised vehicles in Texas would be "game over," triggering a global domino effect of regulatory approvals and fleet expansion. He argued that European approval, while positive, is secondary — the primary signal investors should watch is the pace and scale of Texas deployment, not regulatory discussions elsewhere.

2. **Robotaxi Pricing Advantage Over Competitors:** The video noted, without providing specific numbers, that Tesla's robotaxi service continues to be priced meaningfully cheaper than both Uber and Waymo's offerings, reinforcing the competitive moat for a scaled fleet deployment.

3. **Scale Projection: Millions of Robotaxis by 2028:** Jo Bhakdi put forward a bold but specific timeline projection: if robotaxi scaling begins in earnest in 2026, there could be millions of Tesla robotaxis operating globally by 2028. He acknowledged this sounds aggressive but argued most analysts underestimate the hockey stick adoption curve once the technology is proven at scale and the regulatory domino effect begins.

## Market & Competition

1. **Uber's Strategic Pivot: Buying Lucid Instead of Tesla:** Uber disclosed an 11.5% stake in Lucid as part of Lucid's $300 million capital raise on April 14th, 2026, and increased its purchase commitment to buy at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles for its robotaxi service. This followed a broader strategy announcement the prior week in which Uber reversed its previous position and committed to buying vehicles directly rather than operating purely as a marketplace. The host and Jo Bhakdi analyzed this move as a hedge — Uber is buying vehicles from capital-starved companies like Lucid and Rivian at likely deep discounts well below production cost, because those companies need to demonstrate demand to their investors.

2. **Why Uber Won't Partner with Tesla:** Jo Bhakdi and the host offered a strategic explanation for why Uber has avoided Tesla: Tesla is a direct existential competitor, not a neutral supplier. Partnering with Tesla on a robotaxi fleet would give Tesla leverage over Uber's margins, customer relationships, and long-term survival. Tesla could theoretically enable FSD for Uber's fleet and then withdraw cooperation or flip to its own service at will, leaving Uber with no competitive position. Jo Bhakdi summarized: "How are you going to compete against Tesla with Teslas? You can't." He concluded that Uber is making the strategically rational decision to avoid Tesla, even if it means working with financially weaker partners.

3. **SpaceX IPO Road Show Underway:** SpaceX has kicked off a private IPO road show, inviting major mutual funds and pension funds for private briefings, including site visits to its launch facilities and the Macrohard data center project in Memphis. The prospectus reportedly targets a valuation of $1.75 trillion with a $75 billion capital raise, which would potentially make it the largest IPO in history. Financially, SpaceX reportedly holds $25 billion in cash, $92 billion in total assets, and $50 billion in total liabilities, with cash reserves doubling from approximately $11 billion at end of 2024 to $22 billion at end of 2025, and revenue reaching approximately $4 billion. The dual-class share structure gives Elon Musk and insiders 10 times the voting power of public shareholders. Jo Bhakdi noted that SpaceX's valuation also implicitly includes Grok as a frontier AI model, an asset he believes is systematically underappreciated.
