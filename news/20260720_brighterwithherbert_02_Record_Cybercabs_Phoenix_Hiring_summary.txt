This episode of the show (guest-hosted by Cern Basher, with a recurring guest referred to as Jeff) covers a cluster of Tesla developments: a record number of Cybercabs spotted at Giga Texas, Phoenix robotaxi hiring, Chinese influencers' FSD testing, Giga Berlin's production and battery expansion, German FSD regulatory delays, upcoming FSD parking/preference memory features, trial-lawyer lobbying against autonomous vehicles, and the accounting treatment of Cybercab inventory.

## Robotaxi
1. **Record Cybercab count at Giga Texas:** Sawyer Merritt spotted a record 245 Cybercabs in the outbound lot at Giga Texas, shared by Tesla's Robotaxi account with the label "gold rush"; the vehicles reportedly lack Cybercab decals, and some observers noted they appeared to have steering wheels. Jeff (the guest) argued this reflects test vehicles being built concurrently with a rapidly increasing production rate, and that the 245 figure will look small within weeks as the line ramps.
2. **Phoenix robotaxi hiring across all shifts:** Tesla is hiring robotaxi fleet support specialists in Phoenix for morning, afternoon, and night shifts, covering vehicle prep, inspections, lot management, and cross-team collaboration. Jeff called hiring across all three shifts a sign that leadership believes hardware and software are close to ready, contrasting it with the hedging behavior (partial-shift hiring, halted production) a company would show if it anticipated a significant delay.
3. **Cybercabs (no steering wheel, with decals) staged in California:** Cybercabs bearing Cybercab decals and no steering wheels were photographed being transported near San Bernardino, California — a state where Tesla is not yet running unsupervised robotaxis. Jeff read this as consistent with a broad, generalized geographic rollout (unlike Waymo's more concentrated city-by-city approach), enabled by Tesla's FSD working across diverse regions already.
4. **Driverless fleet jump cited as a software-confidence signal:** Jeff noted that an increase in driverless robotaxis from roughly 120 to about 180 (a ~50% jump) suggests an FSD software improvement gave Tesla enough confidence to expand the active fleet, implying the company tracks safety/convenience-issue probability thresholds tied to fleet size.
5. **Cybercab inventory/accounting debate:** Responding to a debate started by Warren Redlick (who argued Stellantis-style inventory buildup wouldn't be unusual and Tesla could hold about 10,000 Cybercabs for roughly $200 million in capex), another commentator (Doug) noted Cybercabs will likely be booked as fixed assets rather than inventory. Jeff agreed Tesla could carry some buildup but said it's not in the company's DNA to hold large inventory for long, consistent with its historical approach of minimizing capex-to-return windows.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Chinese influencers say FSD leads China's systems:** Two Chinese automotive influencers testing FSD 14 in Los Angeles reportedly concluded that Chinese systems (Huawei ADS, Xiaopeng's system, Li Auto) still trail Tesla's FSD, urging Chinese firms to "recognize the gap" and catch up. A commentator in the clip said multiple Chinese EV/tech company teams have traveled to the US to trial FSD 14, and argued the gap reflects not just system design but Tesla's accumulated fleet data and engineering approach, which he said is not easily replicable.
2. **Jeff's view: FSD's lead may be widening, not closing:** Jeff argued that relative to the broader auto industry attempting autonomy, Tesla's lead is growing rather than shrinking, with possible exceptions (Xpeng, Huawei, and Nvidia's involvement with an unnamed autonomy effort) that he characterized as "keeping up" but still a couple of years behind.
3. **Wider V14 "light" rollout:** According to a build tracker (Zach), FSD "V14 light" is coming to Model 3, making vehicles on a specific recent software build eligible — described as a broader rollout of the V14 light variant.
4. **Germany slow-walking FSD approval:** Per a source (Ming), Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority and Ministry of Transport are reviewing Dutch-forwarded safety documentation but are also receiving a dedicated Tesla test vehicle in July 2026 for independent evaluation on German roads. Germany is awaiting a coordinated EU-wide technical committee vote, expected around a provisional meeting in October 2026, though disputes over specifics like a speed offset feature could push final approval into late 2026 or 2027.
5. **Upcoming FSD memory for parking/personal preferences:** Responding to user complaints (from accounts including Owen Sparks and others) about FSD not remembering parking spots or lane/carpool-lane preferences, Elon Musk said upcoming releases will let the car remember individual user preferences and specific interventions. Jeff called this a potential major reduction in remaining convenience-related interventions, distinguishing it from safety-critical issues, which he said are already largely resolved with margin (though edge cases like railroad crossing/gate stopping positions remain).
6. **Trial lawyers lobbying against autonomous vehicles:** The American Association for Justice (trial lawyers lobby) has reportedly opposed AV legislation for nearly a decade; the segment cited that the trial lawyer industry earns roughly $180–220 billion annually from auto insurance-related payouts. Elon Musk responded "Wow" to a post highlighting this, and separately reacted "terrible" to a comment from Paul Graham suggesting trial lawyers oppose self-driving cars because they reduce material for lawsuits. Jeff argued this lobbying could slow legislation but that safety data will ultimately prevail, and noted Waymo vehicles are insured at $5 million liability coverage per vehicle.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Berlin production and capacity growth:** Giga Berlin's management report forecasts a significant year-over-year production increase for the 2026 financial year and rising capacity utilization; the plant currently supplies more than 30 markets, with plans to add more. Previously announced capacity expansions include a 20% production increase (twice) bringing output up to 7,500 vehicles per week starting October 2026, alongside 3,500 new jobs.

## Energy
1. **Giga Berlin battery cell investment raised to 18 GWh:** Giga Berlin's battery cell manufacturing capacity target was raised from 8 GWh/year to 18 GWh/year, requiring further investment in the "three-digit million" range, bringing total investment in cell manufacturing to nearly $1.4 billion.

## Market & Competition
1. **Jeff sees a possible trough in Tesla volumes:** Jeff argued Tesla may have hit a trough in vehicle volumes over the past year or two and is now on an upward trajectory, driven by continued software and hardware improvements to the Model Y and Model 3; he said Chinese automakers are gaining share largely through imports/exports rather than new factory commissionings in regions like Europe, while Tesla's Berlin facility continues to ramp.

## Financials
1. **Cybercab fixed-asset accounting and tax benefit:** A recent article (by a commentator referred to as Alexandra, discussing this with Jeff and another guest) argued Cybercabs will be treated as fixed assets rather than inventory, meaning capex spending yields a tax deduction in the first year — potentially creating a reinvestment "flywheel" as the robotaxi fleet scales.
