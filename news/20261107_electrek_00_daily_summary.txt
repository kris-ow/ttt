Electrek's only article today covers a California court ruling finding Tesla liable for fraud over a Solar Roof installation.

## Energy
1. **Tesla found liable for fraud in Solar Roof case:** Fred Lambert reports that a California judge (Cynthia Freeland, San Diego Superior Court) deemed Tesla liable for fraud and negligent misrepresentation in Conroy v. Tesla, after Tesla twice was sanctioned for discovery failures ($15,467.50 in February, $13,500 in June) and then missed a further deadline. Tesla had already stipulated to breach of contract, breach of warranty, and negligence on three of five claims; the original 2020 contract was $82,210 for a 13.1 kW system, later amended to roughly $110,000 for an 18.2 kW roof and six Powerwalls, per the article. The roof was never finished, never passed county inspection, and never produced power, per the complaint. The homeowner estimates repair costs at "well over $2.5 million"; Tesla had offered $5,000 to settle. Internal Tesla messages cited in the exhibit list and complaint reportedly describe the home as an "experiment" and the "99.5 percentile in terms of complexity," with Elon Musk named on the plaintiff's witness list, according to the article — Lambert notes these are the plaintiff's characterizations, not verified transcripts. Tesla is seeking California Court of Appeal review, calling the sanction "unduly punitive." The November 20 trial date has been postponed; a trial readiness conference is set for December 4. Lambert's take: this is "the most damaging Solar Roof case" he has seen, criticizing Tesla's $5,000 settlement offer and arguing the paper trail and years of withheld records reflect poorly on how Tesla ran the Solar Roof program, while acknowledging the appellate sanction challenge "isn't frivolous." ## Editorial Stance
Electrek, via Fred Lambert's "Take," frames this as a damning indictment of Tesla's handling of the Solar Roof business and its treatment of a customer, calling Tesla's $5,000 offer inadequate relative to the alleged scale of harm, while still noting genuine legal uncertainty around the appeal.

Notable quotes and data:
- "well over $2.5 million" — homeowner's estimated cost to fix the damage, per the article
- Tesla's settlement offer: $5,000, per the article
- Discovery sanctions against Tesla: $15,467.50 (February) and $13,500 (June), per court orders reviewed by Electrek
- Judge Freeland: found "ample evidence to support that Defendants have acted willfully"
- Internal Tesla message (per exhibit list, as described by plaintiff's side): the house was in the "99.5 percentile in terms of complexity" and "if we can do this job well, we should be able to do any job well"
- Tesla's appeal argument: the sanction order is "unduly punitive" and amounts to a directed verdict on fraud before trial
