The article compares Wall Street analyst estimates for Tesla's Q3 2026 vehicle deliveries (to be reported October 2), noting a roughly 60,000-unit spread between forecasts and a trend of downward revisions driven by weak August sales in the US and China.

## Financials
1. **Analyst estimates span a wide range:** JPMorgan projects 482,000 (cut from 516,000), Barclays 475,000, UBS 470,000, StoneX 446,500, Goldman Sachs 435,000 (cut from 490,000), and Cantor Fitzgerald 421,758. The median of these six is about 458,000; Visible Alpha consensus is 449,000–454,000, and Bloomberg's consensus is 466,000. Tesla had not yet published its own company-compiled analyst consensus as of the article.
2. **Prediction markets lean more bullish:** Kalshi contracts currently imply Q3 deliveries in the low-to-mid 470,000s, down from roughly 480,000 in mid-September. UBS analyst Joseph Spak said buy-side expectations have risen to a 460,000–480,000 range.
3. **Estimates have trended down over two weeks:** Goldman cut its estimate by 55,000 units on September 16, JPMorgan cut its estimate by 34,000 units on the day of this article, and Cantor's newly issued estimate is the lowest on the Street.
4. **Year-over-year comparison is unfavorable:** Tesla delivered a record 497,099 vehicles in Q3 2025, boosted by US buyers rushing to claim the $7,500 federal EV tax credit before its September 30, 2025 expiration. Every current estimate implies a year-over-year decline — JPMorgan's 482,000 would be down 3%, the median estimate implies an 8% drop, and Cantor's figure would be a 15% decline. Most estimates also imply a sequential decline from the 480,126 vehicles delivered in Q2 2026, with only JPMorgan projecting sequential growth.
5. **Analysts have a recent record of missing badly:** Tesla's company-compiled consensus for Q2 2026 was 406,024 deliveries, but Tesla beat it by 74,000 units; Kalshi prediction-market traders landed almost exactly on the actual Q2 figure.

## Market & Competition
1. **Weak August sales cited as the driver of cuts:** Analysts attribute the downward revisions to a weak August in Tesla's two largest markets — US sales fell 26% year-over-year to about 40,816 units, and China retail sales dropped 12.4% to 50,047 units.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that elevated gas prices tied to the Middle East conflict are offsetting the loss of the US tax credit and could keep US deliveries from collapsing as much as expected, while China discounts and cross-region exports could help September numbers; he personally expects Q3 deliveries to land roughly flat to slightly down versus Q3 2025. He also argues Tesla would be delivering nearly double its current volume if it had proceeded with a $25,000–$30,000 "Model 2" on the unboxed platform instead of pivoting resources toward autonomy, calling that strategic shift a mistake.
