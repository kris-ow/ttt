This video features host Herbert interviewing Jo Bhakdi — an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur — on whether Tesla stock is ready to rally, and what catalysts could trigger a repricing. The central thesis is that Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi expansion in Austin, Dallas, and Houston marks the beginning of a critical inflection window, and that the stock could run significantly once a threshold number of unsupervised vehicles are commercially operating at scale. The conversation also touches on Waymo's fundamental business model weaknesses, the Cybercab rollout, and a speculative SpaceX-Tesla merger scenario.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised robotaxi expansion to Dallas and Houston:** Earlier in the week of the video's publication, Tesla launched fully unsupervised robotaxi service in Dallas and Houston — no safety driver or human in the vehicle. Combined with Austin, this brings the total to at least four unsupervised vehicles operating across three cities, with a stated plan to reach seven cities by the end of June 2026.

2. **Jo Bhakdi's "forcing function" threshold:** Jo Bhakdi identifies approximately 1,800 unsupervised robotaxis in commercial operation as his primary "forcing function" — the point at which robotaxi cash flow represents roughly 5% of Tesla's 2024 annualized free cash flow, compelling even the most resistant analysts to reprice the stock. He notes a secondary, earlier inflection may occur around 50–100 unsupervised vehicles if the growth slope is steep and rapid (e.g., within weeks, not years).

3. **Doubling trajectory and scaling logic:** Bhakdi outlines a doubling logic: from roughly 17 unsupervised vehicles at the time of the conversation, the fleet could plausibly reach 32, 50, and then 100 relatively quickly. He emphasizes that it is not the absolute number alone but the combination of scale and the rate of growth (slope) that would trigger institutional investor repricing. If 50 unsupervised vehicles are reached within a month or two, he believes it could be a game-changing signal.

4. **Cybercab spotted at Giga Texas:** Approximately 14 Cybercabs were reportedly spotted in the Giga Texas lot with no steering wheel or pedals, suggesting they are production units potentially destined for robotaxi service — cited as a sign that Cybercab production is underway this quarter.

5. **Bears moving the goalposts:** The discussion highlights a recurring pattern where skeptics continually raise the threshold for what counts as meaningful progress — from "they have a human in the car" to "it's only one in Houston, who cares?" — making it difficult for incremental data points alone to shift sentiment without a visibly steep scaling curve.

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo's structural disadvantages:** Bhakdi argues that Waymo does not have a path to positive free cash flow at any fleet size under its current model, citing high vehicle costs (estimated at approximately four times the cost of a Cybercab), high fixed and variable costs, and a large remote operations staffing requirement. He contends Waymo would need to acquire an OEM to be viable at scale, drawing an analogy to Google's Android business generating far less free cash flow than Apple's vertically integrated model.

2. **Nvidia as a threat to Waymo, not Tesla:** The hosts note that Nvidia's automotive software partnerships with traditional automakers actually threaten Waymo's potential software licensing model more than Tesla, since Tesla is vertically integrated and does not depend on third-party autonomy stacks.

3. **Bank of America reiterates buy rating:** Bank of America analyst Alex Perry reiterated a buy rating and $460 price target on Tesla, citing "significant embedded opportunity from robotaxi" as a key factor. Perry stated Tesla is in the "early stages of monetization of its autonomy capability" and that autonomous vehicles represent the "most significant change agent in the auto 2.0 landscape."

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybercab as preferred robotaxi unit:** Bhakdi expresses confidence that the Cybercab will work as well as or better than the Model Y for robotaxi purposes, primarily because its lower unit cost produces superior unit economics. However, he frames Cybercab deployment as optically positive but not independently de-risking — what matters is whether unsupervised autonomy technology scales, regardless of vehicle form factor.

## Financials

1. **Stock valuation context and near-term targets:** With Tesla's stock trading approximately $100 below its all-time high (roughly 25% below), Bhakdi characterizes the current price level as having asymmetric upside. He considers a return to the prior all-time high of approximately $490 as "kind of inevitable short term," and suggests a $200 rally is conceivable once a robotaxi inflection is recognized by institutional investors.

2. **Speculative SpaceX-Tesla merger scenario:** The hosts reference analysis from individuals identified as Larry and Alexandra suggesting SpaceX may announce a merger or acquisition of Tesla within weeks of its anticipated June 2026 IPO, with SpaceX reportedly valued at approximately $1.75 trillion. Bhakdi acknowledges this as speculative but notes it could independently push Tesla toward all-time highs. Both hosts treat this as unverified speculation.
