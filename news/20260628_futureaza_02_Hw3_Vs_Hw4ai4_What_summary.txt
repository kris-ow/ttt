This video from FutureAzA features host Brian testing a new web-based tool called Boltwise (boltwise.com) that aims to help buyers, sellers, and fleet investors determine the true market value of used Teslas — factoring in hardware generation (HW3 vs. HW4) and whether Full Self-Driving has been purchased — compared to Kelly Blue Book estimates. The core thesis is that Kelly Blue Book blends values in a way that obscures meaningful differences between otherwise identical Tesla models that differ only in hardware or FSD status, leading buyers to overpay or underpay depending on their FSD intentions. Brian walks through live demos on two vehicles — a 2025 Model Y and a 2023 Model Y — to illustrate how the tool works.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **HW3 vs. HW4 valuation gap:** Brian explains that a 2023 Tesla (particularly Model Y) may have either HW3 or HW4 depending on the exact production date, and that the hardware generation meaningfully affects resale value for buyers who want Full Self-Driving. HW4 vehicles can run the latest FSD versions (Brian mentions "version 14," as opposed to "version 14 Light"), while HW3 vehicles require an already-purchased FSD subscription or license to ensure Tesla has committed to an upgrade path. Tesla has stated and reiterated, per Brian, that HW3 owners who paid for FSD (somewhere between $8,000 and $12,000 or more) have a guaranteed upgrade path.

2. **FSD version 14 and "14 Light" status:** Brian references an anticipated FSD release, distinguishing between a full "version 14" and a "version 14 Light" variant he says "should be out any day." He hedges this with the observation that Elon Musk's timelines typically resolve to either "two weeks" or "end of next year," expressing skepticism about near-term delivery.

3. **Model-specific HW4 rollout timing:** Brian notes that the switchover date from HW3 to HW4 varies by model — for Model S and Model X, HW4 was introduced even earlier than for Model 3 and Model Y — meaning buyers need to verify hardware generation rather than rely on model year alone.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Boltwise tool demo — 2025 Model Y:** Brian tests the Boltwise tool on a 2025 Model Y. The tool reads the vehicle's software screen via photo and determines the car has HW4 hardware but FSD has not been purchased outright — only a subscription with a renewal date. The tool values the vehicle at approximately $37,787, which it flags as lower than Kelly Blue Book's estimate, arguing the KBB figure overstates value given the lack of purchased FSD.

2. **Boltwise tool demo — 2023 Model Y:** Brian then tests the tool on a 2023 Model Y (described as having HW4). The tool reads the vehicle's screens and returns a Kelly Blue Book estimated value of approximately $33,749, but flags the actual value as *higher* than KBB — meaning KBB is underpricing the vehicle because it has HW4 and is therefore FSD-capable. Brian notes that if the same vehicle were a couple of months older (and thus on HW3), the $33,749 KBB figure would be too high, not too low.

3. **Buyer strategy guidance:** Brian advises that shoppers who definitively do not want FSD should seek out 2023 Model Y units produced just before Tesla switched to HW4, as these HW3 vehicles offer the same premium interior and sound system at a lower price, with no meaningful loss for a non-FSD buyer. He notes the HW4 vehicles also include the AMD Ryzen processor (enabling Grok, among other features), whereas older HW3 units use an Intel processor — a distinction he treats as minor for most buyers.

4. **Boltwise platform overview:** Boltwise (boltwise.com) is described as a new, beta-stage product with tiers for buyers, sellers, a buyer/seller combo, a "build a robotaxi fleet" section, and a dealer-facing version with additional tools. Brian notes pricing is subject to change and says he will attempt to obtain a coupon code for viewers. The tool uses photo-based screen reading to extract vehicle data automatically rather than requiring manual input.
