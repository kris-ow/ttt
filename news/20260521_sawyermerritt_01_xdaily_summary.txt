## Key Posts / Themes

1. **SpaceX IPO Filing — Tesla's Stake and Investor Relevance:** SpaceX's 308-page S-1 prospectus became public today, and the filing discloses that Tesla holds 18,990,195 Class A SpaceX shares as of May 1, 2026. With SpaceX planning to list on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX and the filing revealing significant business scale (Connectivity segment including Starlink generated $11.38B in revenue and $3.25B in Q1 2026 alone), Tesla's equity stake represents meaningful balance-sheet optionality that was previously unquantifiable in public filings. Elon Musk retains 85.1% combined voting power via 5.57 billion Class B shares (10 votes/share) and 849.5 million Class A shares. SpaceX also holds 18,712 Bitcoin currently worth over $1.45 billion. The filing's lock-up structure is staggered: up to 20% of eligible insider shares unlock after Q2 2026 earnings, with further unlocks tied to stock price performance milestones (30%+ above IPO price). The Anthropic compute deal — $1.25 billion/month through May 2029 (~$15B/year) — underscores SpaceX/xAI's AI infrastructure monetization, though this is not directly a Tesla catalyst.

2. **FSD European Expansion — Lithuania Approved, Subscription Shift Imminent:** Tesla activated FSD (Supervised) in Lithuania on May 20, 2026, making it the second European country to receive the feature following the Netherlands rollout 39 days prior. Lithuania's Ministry of Transport & Communications issued a supportive statement: *"These technologies can already make a real contribution to safer and more comfortable driving. Our task is to ensure that regulation does not lag behind technological developments."* — a notably constructive regulatory posture. Meanwhile, Tesla is ending one-time FSD purchases across Europe effective May 21, 2026, transitioning to a subscription-only model. This is a significant monetization model change for the European market. Netherlands FSD Supervised usage has reached 9.32 million miles (15 million km) across 39 days, averaging ~239,000 miles/day. Globally, Tesla owners have now driven nearly 10.5 billion miles on FSD (Supervised).

3. **EV Competition — Mercedes AMG GT Electric Unveiled:** Mercedes officially unveiled the production version of its all-electric AMG GT 4-Door Coupe, targeting the high-performance EV segment. Key specs: ~315 miles EPA range, 1,153 hp, 0–60 mph in 2.0 seconds, 106 kWh battery, 600 kW peak charging speed (10–80% in as low as 11 minutes, with 244 miles added in 10 minutes at peak). The car includes artificial engine/exhaust sounds — "exhaust burbles and pops" — a feature Sawyer Merritt highlighted with implicit skepticism. This is a direct competitive entry into Tesla's Model S Plaid territory on performance metrics, though charging infrastructure parity with Tesla's Supercharger network remains an open question.
