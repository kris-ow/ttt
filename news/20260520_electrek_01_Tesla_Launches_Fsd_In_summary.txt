Tesla's FSD (Supervised) software has launched in Lithuania, making it the second European country to permit the system on public roads after the Netherlands approved it on April 10, 2026.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Lithuania adopts FSD via mutual recognition:** Lithuania's Transport Safety Administration recognized the Dutch RDW certification rather than conducting independent testing. Under EU rules, member states can accept another country's type approval, allowing Lithuania to bypass the 18+ months of testing the RDW performed. The Netherlands was the first European approval, under UN Regulation 171 — the EU standard for Level 2 Driver Control Assistance Systems.

2. **Additional European countries in pipeline:** Belgium (Flanders region) is actively testing FSD on public roads. Greece's transport ministry announced an upcoming bill granting the same type approval as the Dutch, which would make it the third country. Ireland's Department of Transport confirmed Tesla is engaging with national authorities including the NSAI to seek approval.

3. **EU-wide approval faces significant obstacles:** The Dutch RDW is pursuing bloc-wide recognition through the European Commission's TCMV, but faces resistance. Sweden, Finland, Denmark, and Norway raised concerns about FSD exceeding speed limits and poor performance on icy roads. EU-wide approval requires 55% of member states representing 65% of the EU population to vote yes. No vote is scheduled; the next TCMV meetings are expected in July and October 2026, putting Tesla's stated goal of EU-wide availability by summer 2026 in doubt.

## Editorial Stance
The author views Lithuania's adoption as a positive but incremental step, noting it reflects bureaucratic recognition rather than independent validation. The author sees Greece's legislative momentum as more significant and speculates Tesla could have FSD operating in roughly half a dozen European countries by fall 2026 even without EU-wide approval.
