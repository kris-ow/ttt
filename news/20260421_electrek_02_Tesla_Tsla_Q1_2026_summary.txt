Electrek previews Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings (releasing April 22 after market close), arguing that headline year-over-year growth figures are misleading because they compare against Tesla's weakest quarter in years, and that the underlying business trends are deteriorating.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Deliveries missed and inventory built sharply:** Tesla delivered 358,023 vehicles in Q1 2026, missing the 365,645 Wall Street consensus by roughly 7,600 units. The 6.3% year-over-year gain from Q1 2025's 336,681 deliveries is characterized as misleading, since Q1 2025 was depressed by Model Y "Juniper" refresh production shutdowns across all four factories. More concerning, Tesla produced over 50,000 more vehicles than it delivered — nearly all in the Model 3/Y category — which the author flags as a demand problem rather than a logistics issue.

## Energy
2. **Energy storage deployment fell sharply:** Tesla deployed 8.8 GWh of energy storage in Q1 2026, a 38% sequential decline from Q4 2025's record 14.2 GWh and well below the analyst consensus of roughly 12–14 GWh. The author notes that energy storage had been Tesla's one consistent growth story over the prior two years, making the sequential drop significant.

## Financials
3. **Revenue consensus split between sources:** Wall Street consensus (Estimize) projects Q1 2026 revenue at approximately $22.3 billion, up roughly 14% from $19.34 billion in Q1 2025, but down sequentially from Q4 2025's $24.9 billion. Tesla's own company-compiled analyst consensus is lower at $21.4 billion. Non-GAAP EPS consensus is $0.37, up 33% from $0.27 in Q1 2025; Tesla's internal consensus is $0.33. Automotive gross margin is flagged as the key metric to watch — a drop below 17% would further pressure the profitability narrative. Over the past four quarters, Tesla has averaged a negative earnings surprise of 7.66%.

## Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi
4. **Shareholder questions center on FSD and robotaxi expansion:** Top investor questions submitted via Say.com focus on milestones for unsupervised FSD expansion beyond Austin and when unsupervised FSD will reach customer vehicles. The author notes the Austin robotaxi pilot has informally extended to Dallas and Houston but characterizes this as a small pilot rather than a real expansion. Questions also probe whether Hardware 3 vehicles can ever achieve unsupervised FSD — the author states flatly they cannot, citing Musk's own prior comments.

## Humanoid Bots
5. **Optimus timeline questions remain unanswered:** The most upvoted shareholder question asks when Optimus v3 will be revealed, when production will start (noting Tesla ended Model S and X production earlier than the mid-year target), and what the expected production rate exiting 2026 will be. The author notes Optimus remains heavily reliant on teleoperation and that production timelines have repeatedly slipped.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla's growth story is effectively dead — the reported year-over-year improvement is an artifact of a depressed base period, while core automotive demand, energy storage, and European sales are all weakening. The author expects Musk to use the earnings call to promote robotaxi and Optimus narratives rather than address deteriorating fundamentals, and expresses deep skepticism about self-driving timelines based on Musk's decade-long track record of missed predictions.
