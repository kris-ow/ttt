This video from The Limiting Factor dissects a Tesla patent application titled "Electrode foil coatings and methods of applying them," filed September 2, 2025, arguing it actually bundles at least three distinct inventions aimed at reducing defects in Tesla's tabless 4680 dry-electrode cell and enabling future higher-energy-density designs. The host concludes the patent as currently worded would likely be rejected as obvious, though it still serves Tesla by clarifying freedom-to-operate relative to other companies' patents.

## Energy
1. **Edge-coating invention already appears to be in use:** The patent describes coating exposed metal between electrode strips (via liquid coating or tape) to prevent short circuits caused by Tesla's tabless 4680 design, where laser-cut "flags" welded top and bottom raise cathode-anode contact risk. The host says his own 4680 teardown (done in collaboration with UC San Diego) showed a white plastic-like stripe on the foil consistent with this coating, suggesting the invention is likely already in production use.
2. **Tapered electrode design targets sharp-edge stress and shorting:** A second invention narrows the cathode coating near its cut edge to create a smooth transition rather than an abrupt "tip," reducing mechanical stress that can otherwise cause inward bending, loss of layer contact (lower energy density), and snagging/cutting that leads to short circuits during charge/discharge cycling.
3. **Electrode sleeve as a possible future-proofing step:** A third invention wraps a plastic sleeve around the electrode foil (either alongside or instead of tapering) to further reduce residual mechanical stress at the foil edge left by tapering alone. The host argues tapering alone is probably sufficient today and that the sleeve is likely preparation for future cylindrical cells with thicker cathodes and/or higher silicon content — changes the host expects to worsen these stress-related failure modes and thus require more mitigation.
4. **Design applies specifically to cylindrical cells, not prismatic/pouch:** The host reasons these fixes target cylindrical cells because their center void must stay round and stable without added support structure, unlike flat-wound or pressed prismatic/pouch cells, which don't face the same core-stability problem.
5. **Patent office deemed the claims not inventive:** The international searching authority found the patent's claims novel and industrially applicable but not inventive (i.e., obvious given existing industrial knowledge), meaning Tesla likely would not receive a patent as currently written — though the search still gives Tesla useful clarity that it can likely use the invention without infringing competitors' patents.

Notable quotes and data:
- Patent title: "Electrode foil coatings and methods of applying them," filed September 2, 2025.
- Named inventors: Yang Shao-yun, Siva Muthukumar, Song Shao-ping, and Li Zhu Chuan.
- Patent application spans 52 pages and, per the host, actually contains at least three separable inventions (one of which, per commentator Study Park on X, was split into its own separate application on electrode narrowing).
- Tesla's electrode coating process is shown using roughly eight coating stripes on the wide foil.
- Battery production lines run at speeds of around 100 meters per minute or more, which the host says makes it unclear how these processes could be manufactured at that speed given the patent's lack of process detail.
- International searching authority verdict: claims are novel and industrially applicable but not inventive — meaning no patent grant under the current wording.
