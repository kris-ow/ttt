On Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call, AI chief Ashok Elluswamy said unsupervised FSD for customer-owned cars is now targeted for Q2 2027, but clarified this means owners adding their vehicles to Tesla's Tesla-managed Robotaxi fleet rather than unsupervised driving anywhere, marking a narrower product than previously promised.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **New timeline, narrower scope:** Responding to a top retail shareholder question (backed by about 1.1 million shares per Electrek's earnings preview), Elluswamy said unsupervised FSD in customer cars is targeted for Q2 2027, framed as owners being able to add their cars to Tesla's own Robotaxi network — meaning unsupervised operation would only work inside Tesla's existing Robotaxi geofenced zones, not anywhere the owner wants to drive.
2. **Safety cited as the bottleneck:** Per the article's characterization of the call, Elluswamy said safety issues are why Tesla currently limits itself to low-speed roads and avoids harder driving environments, and he reportedly guessed (without certainty) that these issues would be resolved by next year.
3. **Prior promises contrast with current framing:** Musk told investors in July 2025 that unsupervised FSD would be available for unsupervised personal use by the end of that year in certain geographies, and a year before that said he'd be surprised if Tesla lacked unsupervised self-driving the following year; HW3 owners who purchased FSD have been told their computers cannot run unsupervised FSD with no clear upgrade path.

## Robotaxi
1. **Current footprint is small:** Tesla's Robotaxi service operates in six US metros, with most service areas covering only small portions of those cities (e.g., the Miami zone covers mostly West Miami and a strip toward Doral and Sweetwater; Orlando and Tampa launched in July with similarly small geofences). Inside these zones, service sticks to low-speed routes, largely avoids highways, and avoids harder driving areas. In the Bay Area, rides are not unsupervised at all — California still requires a safety driver because Tesla has not released data to the California DMV required beyond Level 2 systems.
2. **Mileage and fleet size:** Tesla reported nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised Robotaxi miles by the end of Q3 2026, a month after Cybercab joined the Austin fleet, per Tesla's Q3 2026 results. The article notes third-party tracking this spring showed the number of fully driverless Teslas on the road in the dozens — far below the hundreds of thousands Musk had previously discussed — and notes Waymo was already operating in both Orlando and Tampa before Tesla and has surpassed 3 million miles.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is skeptical that the Q2 2027 date will hold, noting Tesla's long history of missed FSD timelines since 2019, and argues that even if met, the promise has been quietly narrowed from "drives you anywhere while you sleep" to "works as a Robotaxi inside a small, Tesla-controlled geofence" — calling it significant goalpost-moving. He also notes Tesla itself appears uncertain about the timeline, calling Ashok's safety-fix guess just that, a guess.
