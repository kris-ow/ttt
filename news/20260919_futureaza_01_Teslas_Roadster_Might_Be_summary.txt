This futureaza video previews Tesla's upcoming Roadster reveal event, with hosts Brian and Herbert speculating about the product's purpose and design while repeatedly concluding it is a low-priority "halo" vehicle with negligible financial significance for Tesla.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster framed as a halo/marketing product, not a volume driver:** Both hosts agree the Roadster is not meant for mass sales but as a brand-elevating "supercar" showcase, comparable to how automakers use motorsport or concept cars for publicity rather than revenue — Herbert notes the vehicle count given away to early depositors is likely "dozens," not the "thousand" some claim, and that Tesla loses money on early production units regardless.
2. **Hosts expect zero sustained stock impact:** Herbert states that in his review of Tesla's product announcements since its IPO, none have moved the stock in a sustainable way — demos may cause a brief pop but fade, with only earnings reliably driving sustained moves; both hosts predict minimal upside or downside to earnings from the Roadster reveal given its narrow, low-volume nature.
3. **Speculative theory: Roadster tech may seed a future drone/flying-robot business:** Brian floats his own theory (explicitly labeled speculative, "maybe I'm completely wrong") that Tesla and SpaceX's joint work on the Roadster's cold-gas-thruster/hover technology could be a testbed for a future line of flying robots, extending Tesla's existing progression from humanoid robots (Optimus) to driving robots (cars) to tunneling robots (Boring Company).
4. **Comparisons to Cybertruck and BYD supercars:** Herbert pushes back on a comparison calling the Roadster a coming "flop like the Cybertruck," arguing Cybertruck failed only against an extreme-volume goal the Roadster never had, and that Cybertruck's technology (e.g., steer-by-wire, 48V architecture) fed into later products; he also contrasts Tesla's Cyberbeast performance with BYD's dedicated supercars/hypercars (including the YangWang U9) as a competitive reference point.
5. **Tesla positioning as the "last drivable car":** Herbert cites Tesla VP Lars Moravy's remarks at a Tesla Owners Club of Silicon Valley event, where Moravy reportedly described the Roadster as Tesla's intentional send-off for human-driven cars — built to be the best drivable car ever made, explicitly not autonomous or intended for mass/regular-consumer use.
