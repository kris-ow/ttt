The video reports that Cybercab rides went live in Dallas on a "version 1.5" vehicle with Starlink built in, alongside Tesla's growing Austin mileage versus Waymo and a minor Cybercab collision. ## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab goes live in Dallas:** Rides are live in Dallas, the second city after Austin. Tesla's Eric described it as Cybercab version 1.5. It has Starlink built in natively, with visible discoloration on the roof panel that the host guesses is temporary. It also has a new liftgate camera for tracking belongings in the trunk. The host has heard unconfirmed rumors of upgraded seats and suspension. Spencer's count puts roughly 200 badged Cybercabs in Dallas, though the host assumes not all are giving rides yet. One early rider reported a 20-minute trip with a 2-minute pickup, flawless driving through a construction zone, and an $11 fare. The Dallas geofence covers about 388,000 people. The host notes a video claiming Dallas is "flooded" with Cybercabs shows only two.
2. **Starlink in the vehicle:** Tesla was told the Starlink hardware is V5, whose power draw is about half of V4. It is unclear whether passengers can use it or if it is for Tesla's use only. The host expects passenger Wi-Fi eventually. Ashok Elluswamy said Starlink is not required for safe operation and is mainly for navigation, customer service and fleet management.
3. **Customer purchase invites:** More invites for individual Cybercab purchases are going out. The listed range is 279 miles. Tesla insurance is available in the app but not required. The host expects buyers to receive version 1.5 with Starlink. The warranty is 4 years/50,000 miles basic, and 8 years/100,000 miles for battery and drive unit with at least 70% capacity retention. USB power delivery is 100 W across two ports, versus roughly 65 W on the Model 3 and Y. The host is unsure if the 100 W is per port or shared.
4. **Austin mileage versus Waymo:** Per a new analysis from Martin Viecha's physical-AI data company, Tesla went from near zero to passing Waymo in Austin in under four months. Tesla is likely above 450,000 weekly miles, while Waymo has plateaued around 400,000 since April. The host argues the plateau came shortly after Cybercab became available. Nationwide, Waymo still leads roughly 10x. The host hopes Tesla's hockey-stick growth shows up nationally through the first half of 2027.
5. **Cybercab collision with a box truck:** Images circulate of a collision between a Cybercab and a box truck. The host initially thought the truck cut off the Cybercab, then corrected himself after checking street view. He says fault is unknown and likely to surface in NHTSA's Standing General Order data.
6. **Las Vegas launch deadline:** Las Vegas permits give 120 days to launch. With the full permit issued on August 20, that runs to December 18. The host expects a third robotaxi state in the coming weeks but says Tesla could request an extension.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo struggles in San Francisco:** Waymo paused service in San Francisco after vehicles got stuck on flooded streets, and video shows other cars passing through. The host says this echoes an earlier incident this year. One rider reported a 1.5-hour trip with 10+ pull-overs and lost support connectivity. A public rider's first Cybercab ride impressed him, and he predicts one of the fastest adoption curves ever.

## Energy
1. **$3.1B polysilicon refinery application:** Tesla filed a Texas tax-break application for an ultra-high-purity polysilicon refinery. Construction is expected to start in 2027, with completion and operations in 2030. It is the upstream piece of Tesla's vertically integrated domestic solar platform and of the 100 GW-per-year Project Crystal Sun. Tesla told Texas it is weighing other sites and would not build in Texas without the tax break. The filing gives no capacity figure. Over 90% of solar-grade polysilicon supply is made in China. The two US producers have about 40,000 metric tons of capacity (~21 GW). The host adds that the project might qualify for a $3/kg credit depending on sourcing. He sees it as a long-term play that will not move the needle near term.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Terafab equipment talks:** Semiconductor equipment maker GST is in talks to supply scrubbers and chillers to Terafab. Terafab asked how many units GST can deliver on short lead times, and proposed sending a private aircraft to collect samples. Its confidentiality requirements have become stricter.
2. **Compute buildout pace:** Per Musk, SpaceX is bringing roughly 0.5 GW online per month and aims for over 1 GW per month by the end of 2027. The host calculates that would mean over 12 GW incremental in 2028 and that the current pace equals 6 GW per year. Musk said SpaceX builds at a fraction of industry cost, with a team productizing data-center buildout into easily trainable tasks and Memphis running 24/7. The host takes this to mean SpaceX is deploying faster and at nearly half the cost of others.
3. **Anthropic and demand:** Musk thinks it is unlikely Anthropic cancels its lease deals with SpaceX. He thinks Anthropic will want significantly more, most likely an incremental 10 GW. The host says that is over 3x what SpaceX is expected to have online at the end of this year. At the Dealbook Summit, Satya Nadella said he would lease SpaceX capacity if available, and Musk replied "Cool."
4. **Grok training stack:** SpaceX is rewriting its training stack mainly in C and some C++ to get close to bare metal. Musk thinks it may be the best in the world. Starship and Starlink engineers are helping with GPU networking. Musk said Grok 4.8 is not aimed at benchmarks but at usefulness, and test iterations are being used for the Grok-powered bot. Musk also said orbital AI data-center satellites are simpler than Starlink V3.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Direct-to-cell roadmap:** Per Musk, Starlink Mobile V2 starts flying next year with up to 40x the bandwidth of the current direct-to-cell V1. A V4 is planned to connect to ordinary phones with nothing added. He described micro ground stations bundled with Starlink terminals, which he thinks go into production next year, to reach people indoors. He framed it as meeting future AI and robot bandwidth demand rather than putting the big three carriers out of business. He also said offensive military action runs through Starshield, with decisions made by the Department of War or the president, and Starlink is not for offensive drone strikes.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Ship 42:** Musk confirmed Ship 42 will be kept for display and history and will not be reflown.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus demo and timelines:** Musk wants the Optimus demo to be authentic and not staged, but was non-committal on timing ("maybe a month or two"). A test he cited is installing batteries in a toy remote-control car. He expects Optimus to do dishes in about 12 to 18 months. The host notes this conflicts with Musk's January prediction that Optimus would beat the best human surgeons in three years (January 2029), since 18 months lands in May 2028. He concludes Tesla does not really know the pace.
2. **Industrial first, home later:** Musk said 24/7 heavy-industry jobs, such as mining or refining ore, make the most economic sense first, with home use later. The host has reset his expectations and would be happy to buy one before 2030.

## Autonomous Driving

The host says the form factor, such as bench seats or a Model Y L-style layout, is undetermined. Musk also said California Cybercab approval could come perhaps early next year.
