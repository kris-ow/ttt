Title:       Tesla Cybercab orders open at $29,990 + $299/mo, only in Robotaxi zones
Author:      Fred Lambert

Published:   2026-11-14 02:11 UTC

Tesla is now inviting “select customers” to buy a Cybercab for personal use at $29,990, but the car won’t move unless you also pay $299 a month for an “Autonomy Subscription.”

And it only works inside Tesla’s Robotaxi service areas in Texas and Florida, the same geofences that have turned out to be significantly smaller than what Tesla advertises.

## What Tesla is selling

Tesla’s official Robotaxi account announced the invites on X on Friday with screenshots of the order page and the invitation email.

The order page lists the Cybercab at $29,990 before taxes, with an estimated delivery of December 2026 in Austin, Texas. You need to put down a $1,000 non-refundable deposit to order.

 
 
 

The subscription isn’t optional. It’s pre-checked on the order page at $299 per month, with one month included, and the fine print says “Subscription and regular vehicle maintenance required to operate.”

The email is even more direct. Its asterisk reads: “Only available in supported service areas. Subscription required for operation.”

The rest is what we already knew. Two seats, a 21-inch center touchscreen, 20.1 cubic feet of cargo space, and no steering wheel or pedals. Tesla calls it “fully autonomous” and says you’ll “manage your ride in the app.”

Musk had promised a Cybercab “below $30,000” when he unveiled it in 2024. He technically hit it. He just didn’t mention the $3,588 a year it takes to use it. Over five years, that’s about $47,600 for a two-seater, before insurance, maintenance, taxes, and charging.

## “Supported service areas” means Texas and Florida

Tesla runs its Robotaxi service in eight cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth, and San Antonio in Texas, and Miami, Orlando, and Tampa in Florida. That’s where a Cybercab can operate. Everywhere else, it’s a $30,000 sculpture in your driveway.

And those service areas aren’t what Tesla says they are. Independent tracker Koby Pierce tested thousands of addresses in the Robotaxi app and found that the areas where Tesla actually accepts pickups and drop-offs are only 59% to 78% of the advertised geofences in the six cities he checked. In Austin, the very city where Tesla is delivering these cars, it’s about 59%.

Railroad crossings are a big reason why. In Houston, the creator of Robotaxi Tracker found the app quoting about 40 minutes to go two blocks downtown because the vehicle appeared to route around rail crossings. Tesla also lists San Antonio’s airport inside its polygon but doesn’t serve it.

Then there’s the regulatory cloud. NHTSA has ordered Tesla to prove under oath that a car with no brake pedal meets federal safety standards that require one. Tesla answered on October 30 and asked that the entire response be kept confidential. NHTSA hasn’t ruled. Tesla is now selling the car to consumers anyway.

## Electrek’s Take

I think this is going to be a total mess. It looks like Tesla is only doing this by invitation and delivering it in Austin to prove they can deliver an “unsupervised” vehicle in 2026.

Tesla is going to sell these to fans who are excited about owning a self-driving car, and many of them will find out the limitations aren’t worth $299 a month on top of a $30,000 car.

Sure, if your monthly Uber bill is well over $300 and you live and work deep inside the geofence, maybe the math works on paper. Until you realize you have to park it somewhere once you get to your destination. Or that it can’t take you to a friend’s place two neighborhoods over. Or that a 15-minute drive turns into an hour because the car won’t cross a train track. With an Uber or a Waymo, that’s someone else’s problem. With a Cybercab, you’re paying for the privilege.
