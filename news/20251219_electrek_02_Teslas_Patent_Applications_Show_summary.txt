Electrek analyzed roughly 4,200 Tesla patent applications filed from 2014 through 2024, finding a sharp decline in automotive-related patents and a corresponding surge in AI hardware/software filings, which the author argues supports the narrative that Tesla is pivoting away from being primarily an automaker.

## Autonomous Driving / Humanoid Bots (R&D Trends)
1. **Two patent filing peaks reveal shifting priorities:** Tesla's patent filings show a first peak in 2018 dominated by "Industrial"/manufacturing patents tied to Model 3 production ramp-up, and a larger second peak in 2022 where "AI hardware and software" became the dominant category, covering autonomous driving theories/processes and AI computing hardware (including what became the AI4 computer). AI-related patents first became significant during the 2018 peak (tied to Autopilot/FSD work) but only became a majority of filings in the 2020s.

2. **Automotive patents now a small minority:** The article states that less than 10% of Tesla's total patents are now classified as "Automotive," contrasted with legacy automakers like Toyota and VW whose portfolios remain dominated by mechanical engineering (chassis, suspension, combustion). Tesla's portfolio is now roughly 40% AI-related, including filings on neural network training (Dojo), vision-only distance estimation (as a LiDAR replacement), and data labeling/simulation.

3. **2023–2024 filings lean further into AI and robotics:** Despite a 12-18 month lag meaning 2023-2024 patent data is still incomplete, the available filings show a continued majority tilt toward AI hardware/software, with automotive-specific patents (e.g., EV wireless charging) now a small minority. The article notes filings for "electromechanical joints" and "linear actuators," which it ties to humanoid robot development (Optimus), and argues some nominally "automotive" patents (like wireless charging) may actually be motivated by autonomous vehicle needs rather than the core car business.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert states the data vindicates the bear case that automotive R&D is slowing while also giving bulls visual confirmation of Tesla's AI pivot. He discloses he sold his Tesla shares last year, arguing the company's original EV-acceleration mission has been eclipsed by Musk's AI/"age of abundance" rhetoric, and is skeptical of Musk's claim (made in criticism of a philanthropic donation) that AI will "end poverty" and deliver "universal high income," arguing wealth concentration from AI is more likely without redistribution via charity or taxation. A top-voted reader comment adds that Tesla lacks deep AI research credibility compared to dedicated AI research firms, having historically focused on engineering implementation rather than fundamental research.
