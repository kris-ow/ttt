## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD 14.3.2 rumored in China employee rollout.** An unverified online rumor indicates Tesla pushed FSD v14.3.2 — described as "full functionality" — to employees in China. Brian White (FutureAzA) notes the broader China launch has been delayed since March due to regulatory hurdles and the requirement to train on locally processed data. White adds that Xiaomi engineers who ran head-to-head tests described US FSD as "just about flawless" while Chinese FSD (on v13) is not yet at that level; an employee push would signal government approval is approaching.

- **Tesla's cumulative FSD miles: ~10 billion vs. Waymo's ~200 million.** Per Farzad, Tesla is accumulating roughly 1 billion supervised FSD miles per month, doubling approximately every 9 months, putting it ~50x ahead of Waymo on cumulative miles. Farzad applies Wright's Law — cost falls a fixed percentage each time cumulative output doubles — to argue Tesla's compounding data advantage structurally depresses its cost per mile faster than any competitor can match.

- **Morgan Stanley pegs Tesla robotaxi cost per mile at ~$0.81 vs. Waymo's ~$1.36–$1.43.** ARK Invest projects fully autonomous robotaxi cost reaching ~$0.25/mile at scale. Tesla's unified neural network means every customer FSD mile globally trains the same model powering unsupervised robotaxis — a compounding advantage Waymo's city-specific HD-mapping approach cannot replicate at equivalent speed.

- **Waymo's only viable path requires abandoning its entire architecture.** Brian White argues Waymo must transition from its LiDAR/HD-mapping stack to a vision-only neural-net approach — effectively rebuilding "everything" — to reach viable unit economics. Google/Alphabet is projected to absorb ~$5B in Waymo losses in 2026, targeting 1 million paid rides per week, which equates to roughly $100 lost per ride across ~52 million annual rides.

- **FSD in Las Vegas Boring Company tunnels already live.** Testing of FSD inside the Las Vegas loop began in October 2025, running robotaxi software on v14. The Nashville residential loop agreement — Tesla vehicles taking residents from underground parking to the airport in ~9 minutes — extends this integration to a new city, with first segments targeted for late 2026 or early 2027.

- **170,000 new FSD subscribers added**, per Herbert, representing a small but persistently growing recurring revenue stream. White suggested Tesla consider another free one-month trial given v14's quality level.

## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab confirmed FMVSS-compliant from day one**, per Tesla's head of engineering (Lars Moravy, per Farzad). This is a critical regulatory unlock: Tesla is not subject to the NHTSA 2,500-vehicle annual exemption cap that had been a ceiling risk for purpose-built driverless vehicles lacking standard safety equipment. Production ramp at Giga Texas is now constrained only by manufacturing, not regulation.

- **Q1 2026 paid robotaxi miles: ~1.7 million**, nearly tripling from ~610,000 in Q4 2025. Farzad projects Q2 could exceed 4 million paid miles and Q3 could approach 10 million, potentially nearing Waymo's annual commercial volume by end of 2026.

- **Five additional city launches committed for H1 2026:** Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas. Farzad anticipates some will slip to H2 2026 or later.

- **Pony.AI break-even benchmark: ~23 paid rides per vehicle per day.** Farzad flags this as a key competitive threshold — the relevant question is which platform reaches that utilization level first, and at what cost per mile.

- **Uber expanded Lucid commitment to $500M and minimum 35,000 vehicles** (10,000 Gravity SUVs + 25,000 midsize platform EVs), with Nuro providing autonomy software on top of Nvidia Drive AGX compute and additional LiDAR/camera hardware. White counts at least five unproven technology layers in the stack, arguing the combination multiplies rather than mitigates risk. Nuro only recently received permission to test on public roads without a safety operator.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla's 4680 "8L" pack quietly replaced LG cells in European Model Y**, triggering consumer backlash. The 8L pack delivers ~74 kWh usable vs. 82–84 kWh for the LG 5M pack it replaces in the same Premium Long Range RWD trim; WLTP range drops from 661 km to 609 km (−52 km, −8%). Tesla did not disclose the swap on its configurator or order confirmations, prompting cancellations in France and Norway.

- **4680 energy density remains 13% below Panasonic 2170:** 244 Wh/kg vs. 269 Wh/kg. Charging performance also lags: first-gen 4680 Model Y takes >40 minutes for a 10–80% charge vs. ~27–30 minutes for the 2170 Long Range, and Out of Spec testing found the 8L pack's charge curve already declining at 31% SOC from 155 kW — ranked worst in the current European Model Y lineup. Tesla claims up to 250 kW on V4 Superchargers for 2026 Model Y but no independent confirmation exists for the 8L pack specifically.

- **Supply chain write-down confirms 4680 struggles.** South Korean cathode supplier L&F disclosed its $2.9 billion Tesla 4680 contract was written down to $7,386 — a 99.9% reduction — citing a "change in supply quantity." The Cybertruck, the primary 4680 vehicle, is running at an estimated 20,000–25,000 units/year against a 250,000-unit factory capacity.

- **New five-tier Model Y lease launched**, starting at $459/month (standard RWD) through $799/month (Performance), all with ~$4,000 at signing. Strategically, both Herbert and White frame Tesla's lease pricing as a robotaxi pipeline: Tesla reclaims vehicles at lease-end (~30,000–40,000 miles on the odometer) and redeploys them into the fleet — a model that generates margin twice regardless of robotaxi readiness at return.

- **Canadian Model 3 RWD Premium priced at CAD $39,000 (~USD $29,000).** White calls it cheaper than anything "remotely comparable" before accounting for fuel savings. A camouflaged Model Y L was also spotted in the US, which White views as suggesting an earlier-than-stated arrival relative to Musk's "end of year" guidance — White had predicted H1 2026.

- **Refreshed Model Y earns 5-star ANCAP safety rating** in Australia. White describes it as confirmatory given the Model X's structural precedent.

- **Lucid Q1 2026 loss: ~$1B; revenue $282M vs. $440M expected; EPS -$3.46 vs. -$2.64 expected.** The company suspended full-year production guidance and announced output cuts. Stock is down 41% year-to-date. White sees the upcoming midsize SUV platform as Lucid's only viable path to survival, but the chicken-and-egg problem of scale vs. service infrastructure remains unresolved.

## Energy

- **Kulonga battery: 200 MW / 800 MWh Megapack installation in Australia**, valued at approximately $225 million. Brian (FutureAzA) notes Australia has repeatedly validated the Megapack model with payback periods of roughly one to two years, and called for more country-level announcements at this scale.

- **Megapack 3 expected to ship from Giga Texas no later than Q3 2026**, per Brian, with ramp pace dependent on cell supply from LG's new Lansing, Michigan factory. Brian characterized Musk's "this year" framing as sandbagging.

- **IRENA data cited by Sawyer Merritt:** solar and wind with battery storage now costs $54–$82/MWh, down from over $100/MWh in 2020, now cost-competitive with new coal in China and cheaper than new gas globally. A structural macro tailwind for Megapack deployment velocity.

## Financials

- **FSD Europe perpetual purchase ends May 21, 2026**, shifting UK, Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark, and other European markets to subscription-only. The transition converts a one-time revenue event into recurring ARR and mirrors moves Tesla has already made in other regions — a meaningful shift in software revenue mix for long-term modeling.

- **Tesla Semi California HVIP incentive: $120,000–$330,000 per truck**, with over $100M in total funding available first-come, first-served. Per Sawyer Merritt's summary, this materially reduces total cost of ownership for commercial fleet buyers and is an underappreciated near-term demand lever.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX filed for a $55B (up to $119B total) semiconductor fab in Grimes County, Texas**, approximately 2 hours from Austin. Tesla has pledged $20B and SpaceX $20B toward the initial phase. A county hearing is scheduled for early June 2026. Musk confirmed the filing is real but noted multiple locations are under consideration. Proximity to Giga Texas — planned home of Cybercab manufacturing and a 10 million Optimus units/year line — is the cited strategic rationale. If the fab ultimately supplies Tesla's custom AI chips, it has direct long-term relevance to FSD and Dojo compute costs.

- **BYD April 2026 passenger EV sales: ~314,000 units, -15.7% YoY — eighth consecutive month of decline** in the Chinese domestic market. Domestic rivals Xiaomi, Leap Motor, and Zeekr posted new monthly highs. Exports were a bright spot at a record, but White argues domestic weakness will eventually constrain overseas ambitions. BYD has sufficient overcapacity that it likely won't need new factory capex for 8–10 quarters.

- **Mercedes all-electric C-Class priced at €68,000 (~$79,900 USD)**, with 350 miles expected EPA range, 94 kWh battery, 800V architecture, 330 kW peak charging, and 0–60 in 3.9 seconds. Legacy EV competition remains concentrated at the premium end, not the mass market where Tesla has the most structural advantages.

- **Nvidia characterized as an "indiscriminate backer"** across the AV sector — investing in or partnering with virtually every autonomous driving company, then collecting when they buy Nvidia chips. White dismisses Nvidia's portfolio breadth as a self-reinforcing commercial strategy rather than a signal of conviction in any particular partner.
