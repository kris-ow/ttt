Electrek reports Tesla has registered 993 vehicles in India since launching deliveries there in September 2025, with growth accelerating sharply in recent months following a price cut and a new model launch, based on registration data compiled by Indian publication ElecTree.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Slow start, then acceleration:** Tesla registered just 69 cars in its first month (September 2025) and didn't beat that figure for eight months, finishing 2025 with 226 registrations and adding only 202 more through May 2026 (February was the low at 29). Counting only the first 12 months (Sept 2025–Aug 2026), Tesla totaled 699 cars; including September 2026, the 13-month total is 993, with September 2026 alone contributing a record 294 registrations.
2. **Price cut and Model Y L drive the turnaround:** Tesla launched the six-seat Model Y L in India on April 22 at ₹61.99 lakh (about $65,000), with deliveries starting in June — cheaper than the five-seat Model Y Long Range (₹67.89 lakh, about $71,000), which has since been dropped from the lineup. In late May, Tesla cut the entry-level Model Y price by 15%, from ₹59.89 lakh to ₹50.89 lakh (about $53,000). Registrations since then total 565 over four months, versus 428 in the first nine months. The Model Y L accounted for 425 of those registrations and 81% of September's total.
3. **Pricing versus other markets:** The Model Y L's Indian launch price (about $65,000) is within roughly $3,000 of its US launch price ($61,990) despite Indian import duties of up to 110%, a sharp narrowing from a year earlier when Tesla priced the original Model Y at nearly double the US price (~$70,000). It remains well above the roughly $47,000 Model Y L launch price in China, where Tesla manufactures the vehicle.
4. **No Indian factory:** India's Minister of Heavy Industries confirmed in May that Tesla will not build a factory in India, meaning all vehicles sold there continue to be imported and subject to import duties.
5. **Retail expansion:** Tesla has expanded showroom locations beyond its initial Mumbai, Delhi, and Gurugram sites to include Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, and Noida, per ElecTree.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tiny overall share, stronger luxury position:** India registered 35,995 electric cars in September 2026 (up 98% year-over-year, per Vahan data reported by Autocar Professional), with Tata alone registering 14,769; Tesla's record month represents about 0.8% of the overall EV market. In the luxury EV segment, however, Tesla registered 291 cars in September versus BMW's 502 and Mercedes-Benz's 125, making Tesla the second-largest luxury EV brand in India with about 30% share, up from 69 units in the same month a year earlier.
2. **VinFast comparison:** VinFast, which began registering cars in India the same month as Tesla, assembles vehicles locally in Tamil Nadu and prices its SUVs between roughly $19,000 and $24,000. It registered 2,963 cars in India in September 2026 alone — about three times Tesla's entire 13-month total — which the article attributes to the advantage of local manufacturing, while noting the two compete in different price classes.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's own commentary argues that Tesla's original India pricing (roughly $70,000 for a car worth about $40,000) was unworkable and explains the weak initial sales; the subsequent price cut and new model have lifted volume to nearly 300/month. The author notes it's hard to disentangle how much of the improvement comes from pricing versus geographic expansion, but concludes Tesla appears committed to the Indian market despite weak overall performance.
