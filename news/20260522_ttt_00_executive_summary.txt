## Robotaxi

- **Irving, TX depot confirmed via permit filing:** Tesla is remodeling a 35,000 sq ft warehouse in Irving (Dallas–Fort Worth) into a dedicated Cybercab/Robotaxi maintenance facility with 212 vehicle storage stalls, 16 V4 Supercharger stalls, and 24/7 cleaning and tire-replacement operations. The facility mirrors a previously reported Las Vegas hub in size and scope; the 212-stall capacity is far in excess of the current DFW deployment, signaling infrastructure buildout well ahead of fleet scale.

- **Cybercabs spotted with fleet equipment in Dallas and Houston:** Four Cybercabs in Dallas were observed with gold wheel caps and robotaxi front plates. All Cybercabs at the Houston hub have now been fitted with gold wheel caps and robotaxi decals. Sources indicate steering wheel removal is likely the final step before these vehicles formally join the fleet.

- **Imminent Cybercab deployment, but slow initial ramp expected:** Per Dillon Loomis (Electrified), initial Cybercab additions to the network could be imminent but scale will follow slowly — his personal target is approximately 30 Cybercabs in Austin by end of August 2026, plus roughly 10 each in Houston and Dallas. Tesla's quarterly report implies launches in Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas within 38 days of May 22.

- **NHTSA safety data: Tesla robotaxi 1.81x safer than human drivers:** Analysis of 17 NHTSA-reported incidents since the Austin launch (June 2025) found 53% were other-party fault and 41% Tesla's fault — approximately 7 Tesla-fault incidents, all at roughly 4 mph or less, including curb strikes, backing collisions, and sideswipes. Analyst "Reigns" calculated the 1.81x safety advantage as of April 2026. Jeff Lutz (brighterwithherbert) noted that curb rash events are reportable for robotaxis but not for human drivers, suggesting the true safety advantage is understated.

- **Fleet expansion model: discrete step-ups, not a smooth ramp:** Jeff Lutz described Tesla's expansion methodology as mathematically driven — additional unsupervised vehicles are added only when software quality allows the total fleet failure rate to remain within acceptable bounds, producing visible step-changes following software releases rather than a continuous ramp. The panel attributed the current plateau in the unsupervised fleet to this approach.

- **Cybercab fleet economics from 165 Wh/mi efficiency:** At $0.16/kWh average US electricity rates, Cybercab energy cost is approximately $0.026/mile versus ~$0.038/mile for Model 3 and ~$0.048/mile for Ioniq 5. The sub-50 kWh pack also enables faster charging and lower per-vehicle battery cost. Tesla's stated unit price target remains $30,000.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD v14.3: dramatically reduced attention nag:** Version 14.3 began rolling out and all three brighterwithherbert panelists confirmed receipt. Larry Goldberg reported driving extended Texas distances while reading emails without receiving a nag. Jeff Lutz noted one new bug — the system routed to rear alleyways of shopping centers in over 50% of visits — but the panel's overall read is that v14.3 is a meaningful step toward unsupervised operation.

- **Lithuania approves supervised FSD; Belgium approaching threshold:** Lithuania became the second European country to approve supervised FSD after the Netherlands (April 11, 2026). Greece has stated intent to follow, likely June 2026; Belgium has cross-regional support and has now completed more than 2,000 of the required 5,000 test kilometers with three cars active. The full EU member-state vote is expected in June or September 2026.

- **FSD China: Q3 2026 target, not yet approved:** Sawyer Merritt and Dillon Loomis both clarified that full FSD approval in China has not been granted. Tesla posted approximately six urgent China AI job listings, which the brighterwithherbert panel interprets as preparation for a required regulatory data-capture effort. Tesla CFO, per the most recent earnings call, expects approval in Q3 2026.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus pilot production line footage surfaces:** During B-roll at Tesla's Model S/X signature event at Fremont, what appears to be the Optimus pilot production line — described as designed for 1 million bots per year — was briefly visible. Dillon Loomis noted the line appeared further along than public disclosures had suggested. Sawyer Merritt flagged the footage as among the highest-engagement Tesla posts of the day.

- **"Terafab" trademark filed:** Tesla filed trademark applications for the wordmark "Tesla Terafab" and an accompanying logo on May 19, 2026. No details on intended use were disclosed; the name suggests a large-scale manufacturing branding initiative potentially linked to Cybercab or Optimus production.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab rated 165 Wh/mi — most efficient EV ever EPA-certified:** Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy confirmed the figure at the Model S/X signature event. The next closest competitor is the Lucid Air Pure at 230 Wh/mi (+28%), followed by Model 3 and Model Y at 240 Wh/mi. Lead Cybercab engineer Eric stated: *"That 165 Wh per mile is only the starting point."* The design enables the sub-50 kWh pack's claimed ~300-mile range.

- **Model Y recall — missing weight certification label:** Tesla is recalling 14,575 Model Ys (Fremont, November 17, 2025 – April 21, 2026) after an automated vision-scanning tool failed to confirm placement of the required weight certification label on the driver's side door jamb. Tesla estimates ~45% of affected vehicles actually lack the label. Fix requires a physical dealer visit; owner notices mailed July 17, 2026. No field incidents reported (Recall SB-26-19-002). Electrek noted the six-month detection gap as a process quality concern.

- **Model Y hits 100,000 registrations in Norway:** The Model Y became the first EV to reach that milestone in Norway, achieving it in four years. Approximately 1 in every 29 Norwegian passenger cars is now a Model Y.

## Market & Competition

- **Xpeng GX robotaxi enters mass production; pilot ops target H2 2026:** Xpeng's first mass-produced robotaxi — based on the six-seat GX SUV, unveiled at AI Day in November 2025 — rolled off the Guangzhou production line. The vehicle is pure vision, steer-by-wire, fully redundant, and packs 3,000 TOPS across four in-house Turing chips (versus Tesla HW4's ~500 TOPS). Retail price: $28,000. Brian White (FutureAzA), who has toured Xpeng's facilities, noted the high compute figure likely reflects Xpeng compensating for efficiency gaps Tesla has already solved, not a genuine architectural advantage.

- **Xpeng acknowledges Tesla's lead:** Xpeng's CEO personally tested Tesla FSD in Silicon Valley and publicly stated FSD is well ahead of their system, estimating a ~two-year gap. The head of autonomous driving at Li Auto added: *"The only reason that they dare say that is because Tesla FSD hasn't been fully entered the Chinese market yet. Once it truly rolls out, they'll be completely exposed."*

- **Xpeng's GX costs less than Waymo's sensor kit alone:** Brian White estimated Waymo's sensor upfit at approximately $50,000 per vehicle (floor ~$30,000), versus the GX's $28,000 all-in retail price. White speculated a Cybercab-sized Xpeng equivalent could cost as little as $12,000 to manufacture.

- **Waymo pauses freeway rides nationwide; suspends service in four cities:** Waymo paused all freeway rides across the US while completing a software update for flood avoidance, after a vehicle drove into a flood in Atlanta following an earlier patch that had not resolved the issue. Full service is temporarily suspended in Atlanta, San Antonio, Dallas, and Houston.

- **Ford Energy's first major storage deal — built on CATL license:** Ford's energy division signed a 5-year agreement to supply up to 20 GWh of battery storage to EDF. Brian White noted the batteries are manufactured under a CATL license and expressed skepticism about Ford's ability to execute at scale, contrasting it with Tesla's vertically integrated Megapack operation at two facilities with a third under development.

## Energy

- **Tesla Megapacks for Meta/Wyoming data center project:** Ampere and Meta are expanding a clean energy partnership to supply Meta's Wyoming data centers with 365 MW of solar and 1,600 MWh of battery storage — approximately 400 Tesla Megapacks. Dillon Loomis framed the deal as one example of battery storage emerging as a critical grid bottleneck enabler for the AI compute buildout.
