SpaceX's S-1 filing with the SEC, ahead of what could be the largest IPO in history, reveals that Tesla's two hyped joint ventures with SpaceX — Terafab and Macrohard — are in "very early stages" with no binding financial terms, no IP rights allocated, and no committed timelines.

## Financials
1. **$650M in Tesla-SpaceX/xAI related-party transactions in 2025:** SpaceX purchased $144 million in goods and services from Tesla in 2025 (up from $4 million in 2024). xAI — absorbed by SpaceX in February 2026 — purchased $506 million from Tesla in 2025 and another $34 million in the first two months of 2026, primarily Megapack battery systems for xAI data centers. Combined, Tesla received roughly $650 million from SpaceX/xAI entities in 2025. Tesla also holds 18,990,195 shares of SpaceX Class A common stock (less than 1% ownership), converted from its $2 billion xAI investment. Tesla additionally spent $4 million on X advertising in 2025.

## Humanoid Bots / Market & Competition
2. **Terafab: $25B chip fab announcement vs. legal reality:** Musk announced Terafab in March 2026 as a $25 billion semiconductor facility targeting "one terawatt of compute hardware per year," with chips for both Optimus robots/Tesla vehicles and SpaceX's orbital compute infrastructure. Intel was added as a partner in April 2026. However, SpaceX's S-1 describes only "a general framework for the future development of Terafab," with all specific projects, timelines, milestones, and capex subject to separate future negotiations. The filing explicitly warns that "neither Tesla nor Intel are obligated to remain a part of the project" and that definitive agreements "have not yet been determined."

3. **Macrohard: AI platform with no disclosed terms:** Macrohard is described in the S-1 as an "agentic AI platform" designed to fully emulate digital workflows and "create a fully AI-operated software company," being developed jointly with Tesla. As with Terafab, no financial terms, IP ownership structure, or revenue-sharing arrangement has been disclosed, and the same "very early stages" language applies.

4. **Musk's divided attention disclosed:** The S-1 candidly states Musk "does not devote his full time and attention" to SpaceX, citing his roles at Tesla, Neuralink, The Boring Company, and his prior role as Senior Advisor to the President. SpaceX carries no key-person life insurance on Musk, and its charter explicitly renounces certain corporate opportunities that come to Musk — meaning he has no legal duty to bring opportunities to SpaceX first. The same applies to his relationship with Tesla.

## Editorial Stance
Lambert argues that Musk rushed to announce Terafab and Macrohard before the SpaceX IPO without concrete agreements in place, framing the announcements as narrative-building for capital-raising — consistent with a pattern he sees across prior Tesla announcements — while SpaceX's own risk factors warn investors not to count on these projects materializing.
