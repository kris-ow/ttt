China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology has finalized a ban on flush, electronically-actuated EV door handles without mechanical fallbacks, effective January 1, 2027, directly targeting a design popularized by Tesla.

## Market & Competition
1. **Regulation details and timeline:** China first proposed the ban in September; it is now finalized with an effective date of January 1, 2027. Vehicles released after that date must comply, though cars already late in the design/approval process get up to two years of grace period to redesign, according to Bloomberg.

2. **Technical requirements:** The regulation (per a machine translation cited in the article) requires that non-tailgate doors have a mechanical-release door handle usable without tools after an irreversible restraint-system deployment (e.g., airbag) or a battery thermal event, even when electrically locked. Exterior handles must provide a hand-operating space of at least 60mm × 20mm × 25mm relative to the body surface, and markings must clearly indicate how interior controls work.

3. **Direct implications for Tesla designs:** The article states the regulation addresses features found across Tesla's lineup: handles can't be flush to the body (true of nearly all Teslas), must sit in an expected position (unlike the Cybertruck), need mechanical fallbacks for electrical failure (missing on most Tesla doors except the Model S front doors), and must be clearly marked (an issue with early Model 3s, which lacked labeling leading to aftermarket instructional stickers).

4. **Background context:** The article cites a fatal California Cybertruck crash in which three teenagers reportedly could not escape due to hidden mechanical door releases and exterior handles, and reinforced glass that blocked a rescuer — an incident the article says helped prompt NHTSA investigation, proposed Congressional legislation, and a Tesla statement that it would redesign door handles. China is separately reported to be considering (unconfirmed by the article whether finalized) regulations limiting default 0-60 acceleration times unless a sport mode is selected.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's author praises China for acting quickly on a safety issue government elsewhere has been slow to address, argues the aerodynamic/aesthetic benefits of flush handles are too marginal to justify safety risks, and predicts EU/US regulators or automakers will likely follow suit given China's market size — while also criticizing the rest of the world for lagging China in EV regulation and manufacturing generally.
