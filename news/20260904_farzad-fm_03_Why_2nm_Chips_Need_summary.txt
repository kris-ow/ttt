This clip is a technical explainer on extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography used to manufacture 2nm chips, describing the physics of the light wavelength and the precision optics required. It contains no reference to Tesla, SpaceX, or any related companies or products.

## Market & Competition
1. **EUV lithography basics:** At the 2nm node, chip features are too small for conventional light, requiring extreme ultraviolet (EUV) light with a 13.5 nanometer wavelength — 30 to 50 times shorter than visible light (roughly 400–700 nanometers).
2. **All-reflective, vacuum-sealed optics:** At 13.5 nanometers, virtually everything — air, glass, water — absorbs the light, making lenses unusable; the entire optical system must instead use mirrors, and the machine must operate in an ultra-high vacuum with no air present.
3. **Mirror manufacturing:** The mirrors used in these systems are made by Zeiss in Oberkochen, Germany, and are polished to picometer-level flatness (a picometer being one trillionth of a meter).

Notable quotes and data:
- EUV wavelength: 13.5 nanometers, versus visible light's 400–700 nanometers (30–50x shorter).
