## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab testing reaches Arizona**, per Sawyer Merritt — expanding the pre-commercial validation footprint beyond California and Texas. No fleet size or test parameters disclosed.
- **Starlink-equipped Cybercabs confirmed in Houston.** SC Robinson footage showed 14 Cybercabs on site; the Cybercab Spotter account noted multiple tandem sightings all carrying Starlink equipment, confirming it is not a one-off configuration.
- **Bhakdi projects Cybercab production hits thousands per month by June–July 2026**, with mass manufacturing described as only beginning approximately April–May 2026. He argues that since the vehicle has no functional steering wheel, commercial deployment in 2026 is structurally inevitable — not a question of if but when.
- **Bhakdi's end-2026 fleet forecast: 10,000–20,000 Cybercabs, central case ~15,000.** He acknowledges his prior April–May 2026 target of 1,800 unsupervised units was not achieved and attributes the miss to roughly a one-year delay. If 15,000 is reached, he argues 100,000+ in 2027 follows with high probability, generating free cash flow no current Wall Street model captures. He frames perceived consensus at ~3,000 Cybercabs by end-2027 as a significant mispricing.
- **Bhakdi expects "multiple hundreds" of unsupervised robotaxis by August 2026**, which he identifies as the nearest meaningful fleet milestone and a hard catalyst for Tesla's stock re-rating.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus v3 unveiling targeted for July–August 2026.** Elon stated on the April 22, 2026 earnings call that v3 would arrive in ~90 days; Bhakdi calculates the reveal is now ~60 days out. He frames a strong showing as an above-$490 catalyst for the stock.
- **Bhakdi argues Optimus consumer demand is being underestimated on timeline.** He contends a $30,000–$40,000 Optimus — even with limited initial functionality — could sell millions of units to enterprise and consumer buyers purely on novelty, and that selling millions at ~$40K would be revenue-equivalent to doubling Model Y sales.
- **Figure AI signed a commercial deployment agreement with Catalyst Brands** (JC Penney, Aeropostale, Brooks Brothers) to deploy humanoid robots in Reno, Nevada logistics facilities, initially targeting the Joey Pouch sorting system. No pricing, unit counts, or specific timeline disclosed. Noted here as competitive context for Optimus's enterprise deployment race.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.3 cross-Canada attempt: 1,776 of 3,700 miles completed without a single intervention as of Day 3.** David Moss, Spencer, and Deon Olsen are driving a 2025 AI4 Model 3 from Vancouver, BC to Nova Scotia. The only notable event was a bird strike (not a disengagement) and a construction flagger with a stop sign, which the car navigated without driver input.
- **Tesla granted a patent for an autonomous camera lens cleaning system** — a controller detects debris via image quality degradation and triggers a fluid dispenser and contoured wiper blade without human intervention. Noted as critical infrastructure for unsupervised operation in winter and dirty-road conditions.
- **~1 million Tesla vehicles are now paying FSD subscriptions**, out of approximately 4–5 million capable vehicles, per a supply chain executive guest on brighterwithherbert. Framed as the AI revenue flywheel beginning to turn at scale.

## Energy
- **Meta is purchasing $200 million of Tesla Megapack batteries** to power AI data centers in Wyoming. A separate Enbridge/Meta project will pair a 365 MW solar installation with a 200 MW Megapack storage system, also supplied by Tesla Energy.
- **Tesla is moving into longer-duration storage** with a Houston project referencing a product called "Mega Block," described as Tesla's next step beyond the standard Megapack for extended-duration grid applications.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Cybertruck Q1 2026 sales: 4,186 units**, down from ~7,000 in Q1 2025. Overall Tesla Q1 2026 vehicle registrations were ~105,000, down from ~130,000 in Q1 2025. Bhakdi attributes the Cybertruck's weakness primarily to pricing: originally promised at $39,000, it launched at ~$95,000.
- **Cybertruck dual motor AWD VINs now being assigned** for the $59,000 variant, per Sawyer Merritt, with first deliveries expected within weeks. New Premium AWD orders have shifted to August–September 2026 estimated delivery — a 3–4 month wait implying demand ahead of production throughput.
- **Tesla Roadster confirmed for Giga Texas production.** Lars stated on the Ride the Lightning podcast that "a lot of things are unfolding in the next months" but the unveil will not occur in May 2026.
- **Model 3 RWD lease now only $30/month more than a Toyota Corolla Hybrid**, same term and down payment, despite a ~$10,000 higher sticker price. Bhakdi argues Tesla can compress this gap further by layering FSD software revenue on top of car sales to subsidize lease pricing strategically.
- **Tesla opened Supercharger prepaid passes to non-Tesla EVs in China via WeChat.** Trial pass: ~$12 for 50 kWh (~$0.23/kWh); enjoy pass: ~$88 for 400 kWh (~$0.22/kWh). Improves asset utilization and generates upfront cash.
- **Ferrari Luce unveiled at $640,000** with a 122 kWh battery, 280-mile EPA range, 1,050 hp, and Jony Ive interior design. Former Ferrari chairman publicly called the design "hideous" and warned of "the destruction of a myth." Bhakdi notes the Tesla Model 3 Performance at ~$60,000 outperforms it on key metrics at less than one-tenth the price.

## Market & Competition
- **Tesla stock closed at $433.59, +1.78%**, roughly in line with the NDX (+1.76%). Volume was 25% below average.
- **Bhakdi's Tesla fair value for 2026: ~$890**, with a potential overshoot toward ~$1,000 if the Cybercab fleet reaches 15,000 units. He estimates current market consensus sits at $550–$600, implying the market is not pricing a large-scale 2026 robotaxi deployment. Without new catalysts, he targets a return to the prior all-time high of ~$490.

He contends Chinese automakers remain blocked from US and European markets, meaning share shed by failing OEMs flows predominantly to Tesla, with Toyota as the only viable secondary beneficiary.
