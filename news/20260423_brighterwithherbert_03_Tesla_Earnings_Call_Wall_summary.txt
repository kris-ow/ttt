This video features host Herbert interviewing Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and venture capital investor, analyzing Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call and why Wall Street's negative reaction to the capex increase was misguided. The core thesis is that Tesla's decision to raise 2026 capital expenditure from $20 billion to $25 billion — combined with announcements around a research chip fab at Giga Texas, Dojo 3, and a new AI hardware acquisition — signals aggressive investment in physical AI, not financial distress. The hosts argue the market is applying the wrong framework to Tesla, penalizing it for spending that would be celebrated in pure-play AI companies.

## Financials

1. **Capex Raise Misread by Wall Street:** Tesla announced it is raising 2026 capital expenditure from $20 billion to $25 billion — a 25% step-up in a single quarter. Wall Street interpreted this as a sign of spending out of control and sold the stock, which swung from up $15 to sharply negative during the earnings call. Both the host and Larry Goldberg argue this reaction is wrong and that the capex increase should be read as growth investment, not maintenance spending. Bill Ackman was cited agreeing with this interpretation, distinguishing between maintenance capex (which should cause concern if rising) and growth capex (which investors should applaud).

2. **Q1 Capex Underspend and Implied Acceleration:** Tesla only spent $2.5 billion in capex in Q1 2026, meaning they were already behind pace relative to their original $20 billion annual target before the raise was announced. Goldberg argues this means the actual rate of spending acceleration through the rest of the year will be very steep. He also notes Tesla historically has not fully spent its announced capex budgets, and expresses skepticism they will reach the full $25 billion this year — though he expects them to commit to it.

3. **Free Cash Flow Goes Negative — But That's Acceptable:** Tesla reported $1.7 billion in free cash flow for Q1 2026, which surprised many investors. Both hosts say they expect free cash flow to go negative for the next several quarters given the capex ramp, and view this as acceptable as long as the company remains operationally profitable. Tesla is described as sitting on $45 billion in cash, self-funding its AI buildout without tapping shareholders.

4. **Auto Gross Margin Beat:** Auto gross margin came in at 19.2% excluding credits, or above 20% including credits — well ahead of analyst estimates of 17.5% to 17.9%. This was highlighted by Gene Munster as a significant positive that the market's focus on capex overshadowed.

5. **Tax Write-Off Advantage:** Goldberg notes that 2026 is the first year companies can write off all capex on plant, equipment, and buildings, meaning Tesla's aggressive spending this year also provides an enormous tax benefit — making the timing of the investment even more strategically sound.

6. **AI Hardware Acquisition Disclosed in 10-Q:** Tesla's newly filed 10-Q revealed an acquisition in the AI hardware space. Goldberg initially cited it as approximately $1.5 billion (with some uncertainty about whether it was $2 billion), and described it as contributing to the capex jump. He views this as Tesla "bulking up" its AI hardware capabilities.

7. **Capex Trajectory Projections:** Gene Munster projected Tesla's capex will continue rising in 2027 and estimated approximately $40 billion for next year. Goldberg goes further, predicting Tesla will peak at $70 billion in annual capex — more than double the current 2026 level — citing the Terafab buildout, AI infrastructure needs, and the AI hardware acquisition as key drivers.

8. **Energy Revenue Down — A Concern:** Goldberg flags that energy revenue was down in Q1 2026 and that the 10-Q attributed this to tariffs and competition rather than a large volume of work-in-progress, which had been his initial expectation. He describes this as a negative data point in an otherwise positive financial report.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3 as the Capability Milestone:** Elon Musk stated on the earnings call that FSD version 14.3 is "the last piece of the puzzle" for unsupervised capability, but that improvements still need to be made before going to full scale. Goldberg and the host interpret this to mean 14.3 is sufficient to begin unsupervised operations at limited scale, and that waiting for version 15 is not required to get started — version 15 would represent a further leap but is not a prerequisite.

2. **FSD Beginning to Generate Real Revenue:** Goldberg notes the earnings results showed FSD beginning to pay "real dividends" and generate real income, marking a meaningful milestone in monetization of the autonomous driving software stack.

3. **12 States by End of 2026:** Elon stated on the call that Tesla expects to have unsupervised FSD operations across 12 states by the end of 2026, which the hosts cite as evidence there is no fundamental delay to the robotaxi rollout — only a measured, safety-first ramp.

## Robotaxi

1. **No Meaningful Earnings Until Mid-2027:** Elon Musk stated on the earnings call that robotaxi earnings should not be expected to be meaningful until next year (i.e., mid-2027). Both hosts say this was always their assumption and was not a surprise to them, though they acknowledge some retail investors expected 2026 to be the breakout year.

2. **Cybercab in Production, On Track for Volume Scale:** Elon confirmed on the call that Cybercab is in production and on track for volume-scale production. Goldberg expects a few hundred Cybercabs to emerge from production each month in the first couple of months, scaling up from there.

3. **Graduated Deployment Ramp Across Cities:** Goldberg outlines his expected deployment trajectory: unsupervised operations ramping in three cities currently, with Phoenix preparation underway through to Las Vegas. His projection is that before the end of 2026 there will be roughly a couple of thousand Cybercabs in the Bay Area, around a thousand or more in Austin and Dallas (and potentially Houston), and 200–500 in each of the other states in the approved list. He frames this as the right safety-first approach: start with one car, then 5–10, then 100–200, scaling by orders of magnitude at each step.

4. **Robotaxi Economics Are Modelable:** The hosts contrast Tesla's robotaxi business favorably against pure-play AI companies, arguing that robotaxi unit economics (fare per mile, vehicle cost, utilization) are understandable and modelable, while the monetization path for LLM-based AI companies remains unclear.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus as a Key Capital Allocation Target:** Optimus is cited repeatedly as one of the primary reasons Tesla needs to dramatically increase capex. Goldberg references the Terafab (described as currently a "pilot" Terafab) as a major driver of future capex spend, with the implication that full-scale Terafab construction is still ahead. Gene Munster's post-earnings note listed Optimus as one of the key projects the increased spending is meant to fund.

## Market & Competition

1. **Physical AI Is Uncontested Territory:** Gene Munster is quoted arguing that traditional automakers are cutting EV spend and have made only nominal investments in autonomy, while no strong competitor is emerging to challenge Tesla in physical AI. Goldberg agrees, calling it ironic that markets assign enormous valuations to cash-burning software AI companies while discounting Tesla's real-world AI investments.

2. **AI Company Valuation Double Standard:** The hosts observe that companies like OpenAI and Anthropic receive higher valuations the more cash they burn, while Tesla's comparatively modest capex increase triggered a sell-off. Goldberg argues Tesla is being held to a fundamentally different and unfair standard by the market.

3. **XAI and Cursor Partnership:** SpaceX's XAI announced a partnership with Cursor, a coding AI platform that was the first mover in AI-assisted coding. The potential acquisition cost is described as $10 billion (partnership/option) or $60 billion (full buyout). Goldberg argues XAI's key competitive advantage over OpenAI and Anthropic is that XAI actually owns its own data centers, while the others must scramble for compute. He argues Anthropic's throttling of users is evidence of this constraint and that XAI's data center ownership positions it to leapfrog competitors if combined with Cursor's capabilities.

4. **Tesla–SpaceX Merger Speculation:** Goldberg states his belief that Tesla and SpaceX will announce a merger within the next 12 months, with the actual merger completing the following year. He describes having written a board memo-style piece months ago proposing exactly this, arguing it is the natural future for the company and that capex spending may taper around the $75 billion level once the merger combines the two entities' resources.
