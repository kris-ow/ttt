U.S. Rep. Robin Kelly (IL-02) introduced the "SAFE Exit Act," a bill that would require NHTSA to mandate easy-to-find manual door releases and outside first-responder access for vehicles with electronic door latches, explicitly naming Tesla and Elon Musk in its press release.

## Market & Competition
1. **Bill explicitly targets Tesla:** The bill's press release states that Tesla's doors "have trapped people in their own cars" and claims at least 15 people have died in car crashes due to Tesla doors not opening; Rep. Kelly said "Elon Musk and his Tesla designs are not safe, nor efficient, and it has cost people their lives," calling incidents of people trapped during crashes or power loss "a safety failure" rather than innovation. These are the bill sponsor's claims as stated in the press release, not independently verified by the article.
2. **Bill requirements and backing:** The SAFE Exit Act would direct NHTSA to issue a final rule setting performance and labeling requirements for electric vehicle doors, mandating accessible manual releases for each door and a way for first responders to open the vehicle from outside if power is lost. The legislation is backed by Consumer Reports.
3. **Follows a similar Chinese regulation:** The article notes this follows a similar rule change made official in China a few weeks prior; Tesla reportedly was notified in advance and has reportedly been working on a solution, which the article says will likely also apply in the US if this bill passes.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert states he supports the regulation as making sense despite what he calls heated rhetoric, and argues Tesla's manual releases are poorly implemented, especially in rear seats of the Model 3/Y, where few passengers are likely to have read the owner's manual; he says even one death from an undiscoverable manual release is "one too many." The article also includes a highly-upvoted reader comment (not an Electrek staff claim) arguing that two-stage mechanical/electronic door handles are a long-solved industry standard that Tesla skipped to cut costs and at Musk's personal insistence to mimic Rolls Royce-style button openers.
