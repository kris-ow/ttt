This episode of "brighterwithherbert" is a roundtable discussion with panelists Jeff Lutz, Alexandra Merz, and Cern Basher reviewing what was accomplished and missed in April 2026, the current state of Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi fleet, upcoming milestones, and broader topics including the Elon Musk vs. Sam Altman/OpenAI trial and Tesla's corporate governance developments. The panel generally maintains a bullish but measured outlook, acknowledging Tesla missed several high-profile April targets while arguing the foundational work for a major autonomous driving and robotics ramp is being laid. A recurring theme is managing investor expectations relative to Tesla's longer-term, potentially double-exponential growth trajectory.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised Fleet at 26 Vehicles Per RobotaxiTracker.com:** The unsupervised robotaxi fleet now stands at 26 vehicles per RobotaxiTracker.com, up from 17 just a few days prior. The panel acknowledges the tracker may have delays or miscounting, but treats it as the best available public data. Dallas and Houston each have 3 unsupervised vehicles, representing early-stage presence in those cities with teams reportedly hired on the ground. The panel views the recent acceleration in deployment as meaningful, even if absolute numbers remain low.

2. **Android App Launch — Necessary but Limited Impact:** The Tesla robotaxi app is now available on Android, which Alexandra Merz called "the basic minimum" given the iOS app launched approximately nine to ten months earlier. She framed the Android app as part of a third category of requirements for robotaxi success — alongside hardware and software — covering regulatory permits, charging infrastructure, washing, and operational logistics. She noted it sparked some first rides among newly enabled Android users in Austin but expected the excitement to settle quickly given very few vehicles and long wait times.

3. **Cybercab on the Robotaxi Network — Imminent but Uncertain Timing:** The panel discussed when the first Cybercab (VIN zero having already been produced) will be available to hail on the robotaxi network. Jeff Lutz called it "imminent," noting Cybercab validation vehicles are being spotted in multiple cities and states, laying regulatory and operational groundwork. Cern Basher agreed it could be weeks away in Austin. Herbert, however, expressed more caution — suggesting three months or longer — citing the Cybercab as a brand-new form factor with less FSD testing history than the Model Y, and stressing that the first public incidents involving the iconic vehicle would be permanently damaging to brand perception. The panel noted Tesla would likely distribute many of the first hundreds or possibly up to a thousand units internally or to a small trusted user group before broader launch.

4. **Fleet Expansion Strategy — Slow Start, Then Scale:** Jeff Lutz described Tesla's production line for the Cybercab as being engineered to run at several times the speed of the Model Y line (which is capacitized for approximately 1.5 million units per year), with the Cybercab ultimately targeted to be Tesla's highest-volume vehicle. He argued Tesla is building toward a "double exponential" ramp — very slow at the start, then near-vertical — and cautioned against interpreting the current low fleet count as a systemic failure. He predicted Tesla could surpass Waymo's robotaxi count within months in 2026, possibly by 2x or more, if the recent acceleration in deployment continues.

5. **Next Cities on Deck — Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas:** The panel identified Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas as the next five cities likely to receive robotaxi service, potentially at any moment. Herbert noted Miami's F1 race weekend would have been a high-profile opportunity. He speculated the Florida cities (Miami, Orlando, Tampa) may launch roughly simultaneously. Las Vegas was tied to a later F1 event in the year as a potential target window. The panel debated whether launching with only two or three cars in a city constitutes a true "launch," with Cern suggesting Tesla may need a new internal vocabulary to distinguish first-production-unit milestones from a meaningful public launch.

6. **FSD Supervised Approval in China — Rumored Trump Visit Timing:** Alexandra Merz shared a rumor (explicitly flagged as unconfirmed) that FSD supervised approval in China could be announced around a presidential visit by President Trump approximately two weeks from the recording date, with Elon Musk potentially part of the delegation. She said this would be a logical moment to formalize the launch but emphasized it is speculation. Israel was also mentioned as another market where FSD supervised approval is on deck.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3 Rolled Out in April:** FSD version 14.3 did roll out in April 2026 as anticipated. Elon Musk noted on the earnings call that the remaining issues being addressed are not primarily safety concerns but rather convenience and operational quality issues — such as ensuring correct pickup and drop-off locations and eliminating awkward edge-case behaviors. The panel framed this as a positive signal that safety, while always improvable, is no longer the critical gating factor.

2. **10 Billion FSD Miles Milestone Approaching:** Per the FSD miles counter, Tesla is on track to clock 10 billion cumulative FSD miles around May 3, 2026. Cern noted that extrapolating the current counter pace without accounting for growth, Tesla would reach approximately 16.5 billion total miles and about 6.5 billion city miles by year-end 2026. The panel contextualized Elon's prior statement that ~10 billion miles is the threshold at which safety is sufficiently validated for scale deployment — though they stressed safety improvements are continuous (e.g., 5x, then 10x, then 20x safer than a human driver).

3. **Cortex 2 Coming Online:** Part of Cortex 2 (Tesla's AI training supercomputer) came online in April as expected, with fans reportedly already spinning. The panel indicated it may be approaching approximately 50% capacity, though the exact figure is unclear. Full ramp of Cortex 2 remains ongoing.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Gen 3 Reveal Delayed — Production Timeline Pushed:** The planned Optimus Gen 3 demo/reveal did not occur in April as anticipated. The panel treated this as a delay rather than a cancellation. Cern noted that based on Elon's earnings call comments, the Model S/X (SNX) production line sunsetting — necessary to free up factory space — is expected in May, with the Optimus production line build-out then beginning. Jeff Lutz expressed surprise at a July–August start-of-production timeline for Optimus, suggesting "end of summer" is more realistic. The first Optimus units rolling off the line may not come until late Q3 or Q4 2026. Elon described the transition as a "herculean effort."

2. **Competitive Pressure from China on Humanoid Robots:** Jeff Lutz noted that 2026 is shaping up as a convergence year for humanoid robots, with both Chinese and U.S. competitors targeting several thousand units built this year. The panel agreed Tesla must stay on track with Optimus version 3 and meaningful production volume. Alexandra Merz argued Tesla's strategy of showing less publicly is likely intentional given rampant copycat risk, particularly from China — noting that visual similarities can be manufactured even if functional parity does not exist.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi Start of Production — Confirmed or Near-Confirmed:** The Tesla Semi's first unit off the high-volume line was announced, with the panel treating this as effectively equivalent to start of production. A post from Dan Priestley (a Tesla-affiliated account) was cited as using the phrase "start of production," though the panel acknowledged some ambiguity about the precise wording. The panel drew a parallel to the Cybercab's February first-unit-off-line milestone, which was followed by official start of production approximately two months later.

2. **Q1 2026 Backlog and Gross Margins Better Than Expected:** Cern noted that Tesla described its Q1 2026 order backlog as one of the stronger Q1 backlogs in a couple of years. Gross margins were characterized as good across both the automotive and energy segments, with some one-time items — including a warranty true-down — described as constructive rather than concerning. These positives were contrasted with the disappointment around robotaxi fleet growth guidance from the prior earnings call (doubling every 90 days) not being met.

3. **Model S/X Line Sunsetting in May:** Tesla is expected to sunset the Model S and Model X production lines sometime in May 2026 to make way for the Optimus manufacturing line. This was described as a planned transition with significant logistical complexity.

## Market & Competition

1. **Legacy Auto and EV Competitors Struggling:** Jeff Lutz noted that across the broader auto industry, Q1 reporting has been poor for nearly all manufacturers. EV-specific results from competitors were described as "atrocious," with some Western European legacy automakers posting single-digit gross margins — an unusual deterioration. Rivian was noted as a small-volume niche player. Chinese EV names were also described as struggling. Tesla, by contrast, was seen as gaining EV market share. Legacy automakers were criticized for pivoting to hybrids, which Lutz characterized as an inefficient "sugar high" given the cost of dual powertrains.

2. **Vanguard SEC Filing — No Share Sale, Just Restructuring:** Alexandra Merz clarified a Vanguard 13G filing that caused confusion in financial media. Vanguard restructured its operations into two distinct companies; the reporting entity went from 250 million to 210 million Tesla shares, but the second unit (holding the ~40 million shares) did not yet file separately. Total Vanguard holdings are unchanged. The total outstanding share count used by Vanguard in the filing was 3.75 billion, which includes the 2018 restricted shares, the new compensation package, and 2025 restricted shares.

3. **Elon's 2018 Compensation Package — Converted to Restricted Stock:** Alexandra Merz explained that following Tesla's departure from Delaware jurisdiction, the board converted Elon's 2018 stock options (covering 303 million shares, for which all milestones were completed between 2018 and 2022) into restricted stock. These shares are fully accounted for in Tesla's books (no new dilution). Elon now holds full voting rights on these shares. They vest in January 2028, at which point Tesla intends to facilitate a structured repurchase to cover his tax obligation without open-market selling. Elon is required to hold 55% of the remaining shares until 2033. Merz characterized media headlines suggesting Elon was about to sell shares as deliberately misleading.

## Financials

1. **Energy Segment Weak in Q1 — Expected Q2 Recovery:** Cern flagged the energy segment as a miss in Q1 2026, noting management characterized the weakness as "lumpy" rather than systemic. He expressed hope for a strong Q2 energy recovery as a result, while noting it is too early to conclude there is a structural issue. He observed broader demand shifting toward long-duration energy storage.

2. **SpaceX CEO Performance Award — Aggressive Milestones Including Mars Colony:** The board of SpaceX approved a CEO performance award for Elon Musk in January 2026, details of which emerged as SpaceX conducts a roadshow. The ultimate market cap milestone is $7.5 trillion for SpaceX (compared to $8.5 trillion for Tesla — a combined $16 trillion goal). Operational milestones include building a 1-million-person Mars colony and achieving 100 terawatts of compute in space (described as equivalent to 100,000 one-gigawatt nuclear reactors), with 60 million restricted shares tied to the compute milestone. Elon holds 10-to-1 super voting shares in SpaceX. Alexandra Merz speculated that in a Tesla-SpaceX merger scenario, the operational milestones in both packages could potentially disappear while the super-voting rights and market cap milestones would be restructured proportionally — though she noted this is speculation pending review of SpaceX's bylaws.
