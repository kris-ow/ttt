Herbert and guest Brian White of FutureAzA review a week of Tesla robotaxi and FSD news. The headlines are Texas registration data showing Cybercabs now make up more than half of Tesla's robotaxi fleet, a UBS valuation of the US robotaxi business, the FSD v15 rollout, and regulatory movement in California and the EU. Their core argument is that Tesla's data, compute and cost advantages leave competitors, and conservative Wall Street models, well behind.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercabs now outnumber Model Ys in Texas registrations:** Texas DMV data show Tesla registered 49 more Cybercabs, bringing the total to 822 of 1,556 registered robotaxi vehicles in Texas. The Model Y count has stayed flat at 734 for a while. The hosts note the shift happened quickly: through late October, Tesla was registering only Model Ys. They concede these are registrations, not active rides. Brian argues Tesla would not pay to register vehicles it does not intend to use. He also says the existing Model Y fleet is not wasted, because the network will still need cars for three- and four-passenger rides.

2. **Tesla's fleet count passes Waymo's:** On the chart the host shows, Tesla's registered robotaxi count now leads Waymo's by 151. Waymo's fleet is 881 Jaguar I-PACEs and 524 Zeekr minivans from China. The hosts contrast Waymo's two years of growth with Tesla catching up in months. Brian argues Waymo has no clear path to rapid scaling, because retrofitters like Magna would need months to upfit thousands of extra vehicles.

3. **UBS values the US robotaxi business at about $80B:** UBS calls its $80B figure "deliberately conservative." Its model puts Tesla's 2030 robotaxi network revenue at $2.4B, about one-fifteenth of the $36B consensus, and reaches that consensus level only by 2038. UBS still credits Tesla's full stack: it builds the car, owns the software and runs the network itself. UBS says the valuation is too low if the fleet scales faster than about 125,000 vehicles by 2035. The hosts argue UBS is underestimating exponential adoption. Herbert believes analysts lowball on purpose and then raise estimates as data emerges.

4. **Robotaxi hiring keeps spreading:** A community tracker, Tesla Yoda, counts 160 robotaxi job listings across 48 cities. Michigan and Maryland were recently added, with robotaxi fleet support specialist and supervisor roles. The hosts read these fleet-support titles as a sign Tesla genuinely plans to launch in those places. Tesla Yoda has also started a world map because of growing international robotaxi hiring. Brian would not guess which country comes next. He suggests a country where FSD is already permitted, or possibly China. He notes Tesla has not even applied for FSD approval in the UK.

5. **Robotaxi app adds scheduled rides and other features:** The Tesla App Updates account decompiled the robotaxi app and found new features:
   - scheduled rides with editable pickup windows up to a cutoff
   - a button to extend wait time
   - iPhone Live Activities and Dynamic Island cards showing more ride stages
   - confirming rides directly from Android notifications
   - refinements to the in-ride Grok assistant card
   - Bluetooth and ultra-wideband prompts

   Brian says scheduling matters most for early-morning airport trips and for an expanding geofence.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v15 reaches its first customers, including older Model Ys:** v15 is going to a select group, including regular customers rather than only early-access users. It has also started reaching Model Ys without a front bumper camera. Brian cites roughly 850 recipients in the first day or two, out of millions of cars. One highlighted clip, from X user truthtom, shows the car meeting a tree in the road, failing to pass it, backing up and taking a wider line. Brian also points to improved parking-lot behavior, which he says has historically had little training. He expects the annual holiday update in the first or second week of December.

2. **Mileage and the data advantage:** Cumulative supervised FSD miles have reached 18 billion, up from about 6.5 billion a year ago. Tesla's robotaxis have logged 6 million unsupervised miles, now running at 557,000 miles per week. Brian argues no competitor can match Tesla's ability to collect any edge case on demand. He calls the remaining work the "March of Nines." He relays a conversation with an AI doctoral candidate who said simulation falls short because it invents unhelpful edge cases rather than weighting them by how often they really occur.

3. **California moves to cut robotaxi red tape:** Tesla still runs a ride-hail service with a human in the car in California and has not applied for unsupervised operation there. California regulators have proposed rules to:
   - streamline service-area expansion
   - ease airport access
   - drop unnecessary equipment requirements for purpose-built autonomous vehicles

   A vote is set for December 3. Brian attributes the shift to regulators who personally use robotaxis and face high Uber and Lyft prices. Herbert recalls Musk citing the second half of 2027 for California robotaxi approval at one point and, more recently, early 2027. The hosts also say the proposed federal framework sets a safety outcome rather than mandating lidar.

4. **EU vote on supervised FSD slips again:** The EU vote was first set for December 1, then December 7. It has now been pushed back again to mid-December, reportedly per Spain's Ministry of Industry. Per the host, Tesla's European offering is branded as assisted driving with speed limitations. Individual countries keep approving it, most recently Italy. Brian thinks the delay is a scheduling conflict. He is confident the vote will pass now that German automakers such as Mercedes have hands-free systems that fit the same framework.

## Market & Competition
1. **Lucid, Nuro and Uber look costly and behind:** The hosts dismiss the Lucid Gravity robotaxi demo with Nuro and Uber as a demo, not a review. They point to its large roof-mounted sensor rack, the expensive base vehicle, and three companies each needing a margin. Brian says claims that it is only a year behind Tesla ignore the data and compute gap, and that it will fall further behind. Herbert calls Cybercab the opposite, a design built entirely around cost.

2. **XPeng seen as the strongest challenger:** XPeng has moved from an employee-only robotaxi program to a public beta of driverless passenger rides. The hosts consider it probably number two globally. They cite its end-to-end, vision-only neural nets, its own supercomputer and its in-house silicon. Brian, who rode XPeng's robotaxi in China earlier, says the approach is sound and XPeng's data is growing. Creators including David Moss are now testing it.

3. **Visualization comparison:** The hosts review a side-by-side compilation by Nick Cruz Patane of the in-car visualizations from Tesla, Waymo, Mercedes-Benz, BMW and XPeng. Herbert calls Tesla's new FSD rendering almost lifelike and Mercedes' "pathetic." Brian says visualization matters mainly for consumer confidence and as a "flex," and that AI4 hardware runs it easily. He argues no Western company is in second place in autonomy.
