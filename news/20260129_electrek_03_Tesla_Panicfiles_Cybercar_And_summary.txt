Electrek reports that Tesla filed trademark applications for "Cybercar" and "Cybervehicle" 37 seconds apart on January 28, 2026, shortly after Elon Musk used both terms during Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings call, which began at 5:30 PM ET. The article frames this as a reactive, last-minute filing strategy following Tesla's earlier trademark mishaps.

## Robotaxi
1. **Musk's call comments prompted the filings:** During the earnings call, Musk explained that "Cybercab" causes confusion with "robotaxi" terminology, and that some states don't allow the words "cab" or "taxi," suggesting Tesla might instead use terms like "Cybervehicle" or "Cybercar" — while clarifying Cybercab remains the specific two-seat model without a steering wheel or pedals. Tesla's USPTO filings for "Cybercar" and "Cybervehicle" landed at 7:32-7:33 PM, just as the call wrapped, which the article says implies Tesla's legal team raced to file immediately after hearing the terms used publicly.

2. **Background on the Cybercab trademark problem:** The article recounts that Musk unveiled "Cybercab" at Tesla's "We, Robot" event on October 10, 2024; a beverage company called Unibev filed for the "Cybercab" trademark on October 28, 2024; Tesla did not file its own application until November 2024; and in November 2025 the USPTO suspended Tesla's application, giving Unibev priority. Tesla also failed to secure "Robotaxi" as a trademark because the USPTO deemed it too generic. The article notes Tesla already holds a registered "Cybertruck" trademark, which may help "Cybercar" and "Cybervehicle," though it says the USPTO has previously scrutinized Tesla's "Cyber" prefix applications.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla's trademark process is reactive rather than proactive — lawyers scrambling to file after Musk improvises product names live on calls, rather than securing names before public announcements — and contrasts this with standard corporate practice of filing trademarks before announcing names, calling it indicative of a lack of coordinated naming strategy at Tesla.
