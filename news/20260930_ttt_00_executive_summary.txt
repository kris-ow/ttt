## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Musk told CGTN the Cybercab is already in commercial (paid) operation in Texas and coming "soon" to Florida, Nevada, and other states, with California targeted around mid-2027. Florida already runs driverless Model Y robotaxis in Miami, Orlando, and Tampa; Nevada holds a 5,000-vehicle statewide permit with a Cybercab service center under construction in Las Vegas.
- Texas' autonomous vehicle tracker showed 420 Model Y robotaxis and 126 Cybercabs registered, nearly double the 69 Cybercabs registered the prior Thursday — though registrations don't equal active passenger-carrying deployment.
- Tesla confirmed the first Cybercab uses nickel cathode material produced on-site at Giga Texas, cutting supply-chain cost layers.
- A struggling Nasdaq micro-cap, KIDZ AI (formerly Classover Holdings, market cap ~$2M, down ~99.97% over the past year), sent Tesla a non-binding "procurement inquiry" for 500 Robotaxis — a deal that, per Electrek, would cost roughly $15M at Musk's stated sub-$30,000 Cybercab target, far exceeding the company's cash on hand; Electrek frames this as a stock-pump pivot rather than a real order.
- Analyst commentary (jobhakdi) argues Robotaxi will only meaningfully cannibalize car ownership once per-mile pricing falls well below $1, with lower-income buyers burdened by down payments and insurance hit first.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk told CGTN he expects 1 billion humanoid robots within "no later than 10 years," doubling annually thereafter to roughly 100 billion in 20 years; CGTN's own reporting cited a more conservative 10 billion in about 15 years.
- Per a Bloomberg report, Tesla has begun vetting Chinese suppliers and ordered 5,000 Optimus units, targeting 2,000–2,500 robots/week by year-end (roughly 130,000 units/year at that pace).

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 14 completed its first orbital launch Monday. The booster fired all 33 engines but lost one at liftoff, still restarting 31 for a bay splashdown; the ship lost one of six engines during ascent but still reached orbit, completing its first in-space burn, first controlled deorbit braking, and a North Pacific splashdown. All 26 Starlink V3 satellites deployed and were confirmed operational.
- The planned ~10-hour, six-orbit mission was cut to about 3 hours as a precaution after the engine issues.
- Musk said he hopes to attempt a booster/ship catch around the end of next month, with a re-flight of the same ship by early next year; NASA Spaceflight tentatively tracks October 19, though the host flags possible slippage given the engine anomalies. Musk previously put catch odds at 50–60%.
- Separately, Musk said SpaceX expects Starship to reach weekly or twice-weekly launch cadence in 2027.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 14 delivered Starship's first completed commercial mission: 26 Starlink V3 satellites, each adding roughly a terabit per second of bandwidth (~26 Tbps added to the network).
- AT&T's CEO publicly disputed SpaceX's direct-to-cell phone strategy, calling it not "viable" and arguing satellite technology won't beat fiber even a decade out — competitive pushback from a major telecom rival.

## Electric Vehicles
- The EU postponed its vote on Tesla's FSD Supervised approval from October 6 to December; Musk responded with "Sigh." Currently only seven EU countries recognize Dutch approval (~12% of Europe's population), excluding Germany, France, Italy, and Spain.
- FSD Supervised crossed 15 billion global miles driven, up from 14 billion on August 27 — roughly 1 billion miles in about a month. Croatia became the 8th European country to approve FSD Supervised, joining Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, Estonia, Slovenia, and Czechia.
- Tesla opened its Semi manufacturing plant in Nevada and began mass production, designed for 50,000 trucks/year with 500 miles of range fully loaded; PepsiCo, DHL, and US Foods attended the launch. Tesla plans 30+ megacharging stations by year-end, with European Semi deliveries starting 2027. Separately, HMD Trucking (~700 diesel trucks) ordered 50 Tesla Semis.
- Tesla postponed its outdoor Roadster event due to forecast severe weather; new date is October 15.
- Tesla's own Q3 analyst consensus points to 462,000 deliveries and 15.9 GWh of energy storage deployment; other consensus tracking cited ~454,000 deliveries (range 422,000–482,000 across banks).
- Tesla installed its 3,000th Supercharger in Canada, with a special design.

## Financials
- Tesla secured a $30B financing package per an SEC filing: a $20B delayed-draw term loan, an $8B five-year revolving credit facility, and a $2B 364-day revolving credit facility — expanding available liquidity and capex flexibility.
- The SEC cleared Tesla to launch a voluntary retail shareholder voting program allowing a standing default voting preference for future meetings.

## Market & Competition
- Musk said Grok 4.7 trails Anthropic's new Opus 5.5, expecting to catch up with AI "leaders" sometime in 2027; per Artificial Analysis, Grok 4.7 scored 46 vs. 53 for leading models, though Grok remains cheaper ($2/$6 per million tokens vs. Opus 5.5's $4/$20).
- Grok Bot usage is doubling roughly monthly per Musk; Bloomberg's Ed Ludlow reported ~418,000 weekly users, up 24% week-over-week. Grok Bot added bank/card/investment account linking, limited to Tesla's top $300/month subscription tier.
- Musk told CGTN China's AI models are outstanding, with China likely leading in productivity per unit of compute, and estimated China overcomes chip/lithography restrictions in two to three years; he also said China's electricity production now exceeds the US, Europe, and India combined.
- At a White House event, Musk, Jensen Huang, and Gavin Baker discussed power needs and superintelligence; Musk referenced a "superintelligence safety" joint declaration involving mutual AI-model oversight.
