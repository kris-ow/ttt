Tesla is displaying its Cybercab in China for the first time this week as a static showcase, while explicitly stating it will not sell the vehicle there or launch driverless ride-hailing in the country.

## Robotaxi
1. **Static display, no sale or service:** Tesla China said the Cybercab showcase, opening September 17, is meant only to display "product design and technological concepts" and "does not involve the sale of the Cybercab in the Chinese market or any arrangement for driverless ride-hailing commercial operations." The car will be shown at Tesla's Huamao experience store in Beijing through September 27 and at HKRI Taikoo Hui in Shanghai through September 21 — its first public appearance in China.
2. **Regulatory barriers cited by the article:** The article notes the Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals, meaning it cannot be registered and sold to consumers in China (or the US) under current rules. It also notes Tesla only rolled out a limited, supervised FSD version in China in early 2025 and lacks approvals for unsupervised, driverless operation there.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **China FSD status:** Per the article, Tesla's FSD in China remains a limited, supervised release launched in early 2025, with no approval yet for driverless operation.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chinese robotaxi operators ahead:** The article notes that Chinese companies including Baidu's Apollo Go, Pony.ai, and WeRide already operate paid, no-safety-driver robotaxi services across multiple Chinese cities, contrasting with Tesla's static-only Cybercab display.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the China Cybercab showcase is mainly a showroom traffic tactic to drive Model Y sales rather than a meaningful step toward launching the robotaxi vehicle, noting Tesla is "nowhere near ready for deployment at scale" under real regulations already in place in China, unlike in deregulated US markets like Texas and Florida.
