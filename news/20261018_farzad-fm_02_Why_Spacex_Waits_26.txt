Miss your chance
to fly to Mars, and the
next one won't be for another
26 months, not days, but
months.  And Mars is here
, in our sky.  So
why can't a rocket
just launch
when it's ready?  Because
you're not aiming at Mars,
but where Mars will be in the
future.  Imagine
two runners on a
track.  The Earth is on
the inner track,
it makes a circle in a
year.  Mars is on the
outer orbit, and
one circle takes
almost two years.  So,
Earth is constantly
leapfrogging ahead and
catching up with Mars only
once every 26 months.    The
most economical
way to get there is not a
straight line.  You
make a powerful
leap, leaving
the Earth, and then
move by
inertia around
the Sun in a long arc,
ending in the
orbit of Mars.  This
takes 6 to 9
months.  But Mars should
really be there when
you arrive.  Therefore, you
should start
when Mars is a certain
distance ahead of
you on the track.  This
alignment
only happens once
per round.  This is the
launch window, which is
only open for a few
weeks.  Miss
it—and your arc will
no longer intersect with
Mars.  You'd
have to burn
a lot more
fuel or wait for
the Earth to
come full circle again.
That's why Elon talked
about sending Starship to
Mars in whole
flotillas,
launching together when the
window opens.
You can't fly
there all the time, you
need to
move specifically to
these windows.  It would be a
concentration of a thousand
ships, two thousand
or even more.
This means that every
attempt to fly to
Mars is a test that
can only be carried out
once every two or so
years.  Now
compare it to the Moon.    It's
about a
three-day flight away.  If
the test there goes wrong,
you can
go back there in a
few weeks.  If
something goes wrong on
Mars, you'll have to
wait two years for
another try.  Can you imagine
how embarrassing that would be?    To
me, this is the main
reason why SpaceX
put the Moon first this year
.  In technology,
the speed of learning
determines the speed of
success.  So the Moon
is a place where SpaceX
learns quickly, while
Mars lives on its
own schedule.  Because
with Mars there is no "
next week".
