Electrek reports that a Tesla Model 3 using "Full Self-Driving" crashed head-on into another vehicle in China after the system steered into the oncoming-traffic lane during a driver's livestream demonstration.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Livestream crash in China:** A Douyin (TikTok) user livestreaming a Tesla FSD drive in his Model 3 had the vehicle move into the opposing-traffic lane, causing a head-on collision; no one was critically hurt. The driver initially withheld crash footage while seeking compensation from Tesla but later released video confirming FSD was active and had initiated the wrong-direction lane change.
2. **Context on China FSD rollout:** Tesla launched its Level 2 driver-assist "Full Self-Driving" system in China earlier this year; regulators there reportedly required Tesla to rename the system because its original name was judged not representative of its actual capabilities. As in the US, the system requires constant driver supervision despite its name.
3. **Separate reader-reported incident:** The article's top comment quotes a Tesla Model Y owner describing a report filed with NHTSA on Dec 6, stating FSD stopped their car on active San Diego trolley tracks with no warning, requiring the driver to manually move the car off the tracks to avoid a potential collision with an oncoming train.
4. **Grok misinformation:** The article notes that Grok, Elon Musk's LLM, incorrectly claimed the China crash was "staged" and that the driver had been manually driving rather than using FSD.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the crash illustrates the danger of driver overconfidence in Tesla's autonomous features, and criticizes Tesla's marketing (e.g., claims that FSD "gives you time back") and reduced driver-monitoring as encouraging misuse of a system that still requires full supervision.
