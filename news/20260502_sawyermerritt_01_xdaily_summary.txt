## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Megapack Factory Hits 200 Units/Week Production Rate:** Tesla released a new video from its Lathrop, California Megapack factory announcing it is now producing 200 Megapacks per week. This is a concrete production throughput milestone for the Energy segment and represents a meaningful data point for modeling Energy storage revenue and margin contribution going forward. No annualized revenue figure was provided in the posts.

2. **Tesla Semi Charging Infrastructure Launch:** In a significant product expansion, Tesla introduced a new "Basecharger" designed for overnight fleet charging of the Tesla Semi. Key specs: 125 kW charging speed, adds up to 60% of range in four hours, with deliveries beginning in early 2027. For business customers, Tesla also launched a Semi Charging for Businesses page with pricing starting at $40,000 for two Basechargers, or $188,000 for two Megacharger setups (max DC output: up to 1,200 kW shared across two posts, output voltage range 180–1,000 VDC). Additionally, Tesla will charge businesses $0.08 per kWh for use of its chargers at revenue-generating sites, a fee that includes annual preventative maintenance, scheduled service visits, and access to Tesla technicians specializing in Semi charging systems. A separate rebate program in Washington State makes the Tesla Semi eligible for a $175,000 discount — approximately 60% off — materially improving the truck's economics for fleet buyers in that state.

3. **FSD V14.3.2 — Disengagement Popup UI Refinement:** Sawyer received a new build of FSD V14.3.2 and documented a redesigned intervention/disengagement popup window. Changes include moving the popup lower on screen, making it smaller, improving layout readability, and making it non-dismissible until the driver selects an option (while still allowing navigation, HVAC, music, and gear controls). The release notes were unchanged from the prior build. This is an iterative UX improvement rather than a capability milestone, but it signals continued active software development cadence on V14.x.

4. **California to Issue Traffic Tickets to Autonomous Vehicles Starting July 1, 2026:** Beginning July 1, 2026, California will issue traffic violation tickets directly to autonomous vehicles (robotaxis) for infractions such as running red lights or failing to yield to pedestrians. The state framed this as part of its leadership in AV development and adoption. For Tesla's robotaxi operations in the Bay Area — the largest active market per canonical tracker data — this introduces a new compliance and operational cost variable, though the financial magnitude per violation was not specified in the posts.

5. **Tesla Ends One-Time FSD Purchase in Netherlands on May 15, 2026:** Tesla is discontinuing the outright FSD purchase option in the Netherlands on May 15, 2026. Customers have a two-week window to buy FSD for a one-time payment of €7,500. After that date, the only option will be a monthly subscription at €99/month. This continuation of the global shift from perpetual licenses to subscription revenue has recurring revenue implications and is consistent with Tesla's broader monetization strategy for FSD.

6. **Tesla Sold $143M in Vehicles to SpaceX in 2025 (SEC Filing):** A new SEC filing disclosed that Tesla sold $143 million worth of vehicles to SpaceX in 2025, primarily Cybertrucks that replaced SpaceX's gas-powered pickup truck fleet. This is a related-party transaction of note for financial analysts, representing a defined revenue contribution from an affiliated entity. Sawyer noted the shared stainless steel material between Starship and the Cybertruck as a contextual aside.

7. **Nissan Cancels $500M U.S. EV Investment, Pivots Back to Gas Trucks:** Nissan canceled a $500 million EV manufacturing plan at a U.S. plant, reverting focus to gas-powered trucks. This is a modest bullish signal for Tesla's competitive position, as a key legacy automaker retreats from EV investment rather than accelerating to close the gap.
