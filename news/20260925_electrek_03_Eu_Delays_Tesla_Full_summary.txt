The EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles will not vote on EU-wide approval of Tesla's Full Self-Driving (Supervised) at its October 6 meeting, pushing any possible vote to December at the earliest, according to the published draft agenda.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **October vote off the table:** The draft agenda for the 119th TCMV meeting (dated September 11, running 10:00–15:00 in Brussels) lists only a 25-minute "continuation of discussions" item on the Netherlands' Article 39 request to extend its FSD approval EU-wide, with no vote scheduled; the committee does not meet again until December.
2. **Repeated timeline slips:** Tesla Europe posted on X (September 1) that FSD was approved in five EU countries, used by more than 70,000 customers, and that an EU-wide vote could happen October 6 — a date the article notes has now slipped. Previous Tesla-stated targets also slipped: expected Dutch approval in February 2026 (denied by RDW at the time), then April 10 (which Tesla did hit), then Musk's stated goal of EU-wide availability by summer 2026 (TCMV discussed the file in June but did not vote).
3. **Approval math:** Passage requires a qualified majority of 15 of 27 EU member states representing at least 65% of the bloc's population. So far Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania, Slovenia, and Czechia have recognized the Dutch approval, plus the Netherlands itself — seven countries totaling roughly 53 million people, about 12% of the EU population.
4. **Standing opposition:** Sweden has told the EU to reject FSD unless Tesla removes the ability to set the system above posted speed limits; Nordic regulators have also raised concerns about icy roads and driver attention. France has refused to recognize the Dutch approval, citing speeding and driver attention in cities, though its transport minister has reached out to Musk. Germany, Italy, and Spain have not recognized the Dutch approval either; those four countries together hold about 58% of the EU population, making the 65% threshold currently unreachable without some of them.
5. **New safety data:** A Brussels pedestrian safety group reportedly found FSD sped in 55% of the 30 km/h road segments it tested, in a country (unspecified beyond "already approved the system") where FSD is already approved.

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the repeated delays and unresolved objections as evidence that Tesla's and Musk's public timelines for EU-wide FSD approval have consistently proven overly optimistic.
