## Brief

## Robotaxi
- NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened petition file DP26010 on October 9 targeting MY2026 Robotaxis (Model Y and Cybercab) over claims they speed through active school zones; evidence cited is thin — three social videos and four logged incidents, no crashes or injuries — and NHTSA has until roughly January 21, 2027 to decide whether to proceed. NHTSA's filing separately estimates the affected fleet at ~1,000 vehicles, the first federal fleet-size figure, which the article says may reflect DMV-registered rather than active vehicles.
- Tesla registered 150 new Cybercabs with the Texas DMV, pushing the cumulative total to 319 from roughly 169 — nearly double overnight, per Sawyer Merritt. Tesla's own robotaxi app claims the Austin fleet scaled to 300+ Cybercabs one month after launch, an 8x increase in 30 days; Ashok Elluswamy said Dallas service is coming soon, with roughly 300 Cybercabs reportedly already staged there.
- Third-party tracking cited by one channel put Tesla adding Texas robotaxis faster than Waymo (~14.6/day vs. ~9.9/day over three weeks), with Texas totals discussed at 739 Tesla (420 Model Y, 319 Cybercab) vs. 1,185 for Waymo; separately, Austin's ~2,000-person "X Takeover" fan event over the weekend drove heavy Cybercab usage with wait times reported as staying short throughout, and Cybercabs were anecdotally seen giving more rides than Waymo in some clips (unverified).
- Musk framed Tesla's robotaxi strategy as deliberately safety-first, warning a single injury or death could trigger global coverage and regulatory shutdown; Eric Early, who leads the Cybercab program, said Tesla is intentionally pacing slowly now ahead of an expected "meteoric" ramp.
- A long-time Cybertruck owner reported two Cybercab rides as flawless with FSD-like behavior, though he flagged uncomfortable seat material and non-intuitive door/window controls as rough edges.
- Jefferies raised its Tesla price target from $350 to $400, linked by commentators to recent robotaxi momentum.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk said no existing robotic hand can apply human-level precision force (e.g., driving a small screw or opening a jar), calling this the central Optimus design challenge.
- Musk said China's magnet export ban, imposed last year, set Tesla's robotics program back two to three months and temporarily left Optimus units without arms; one commentator noted this was largely a prior-year issue and suggested Optimus team leadership changes also contributed to delays beyond the magnet shortage.

## Autonomous Driving
- A long-term Cybertruck owner with ~120,000 FSD miles said early FSD (v12-era) was rough — curb-clinging, one lane-drift incident — but versions 13-14 marked major quality leaps, with remaining issues now mostly about parking-spot choice rather than safety.
- Destination drop-off accuracy in Austin Cybercabs is improved but not fully solved, per mixed rider feedback cited by one host.

## Electric Vehicles
- Quebec court ordered Tesla Canada to pay insurer Intact C$161,700 over a 2020 Model X fire, rejecting Tesla's unsupported defense that the fire was likely misuse or vandalism; the judge said Tesla's claim relied on "no data whatsoever." Tesla's own safety report cites one fire per 140 million miles (2012-2024), though that rate has worsened from 170 million miles when first reported in 2019.
- Tesla Semi specs disclosed: 500 miles range at full 82,000-lb load, 600 at half load, a new 325-mile standard-range version, 60% charge in 30 minutes, 98% fleet uptime YTD, and 50,000 units/year production capacity.
- A speaker argued Tesla's Q3 deliveries looked weaker only because EV tax credit expiration pulled Q4-normal demand forward, while Tesla still roughly matched prior Q4 levels; the speaker expects a record Q4, "shocked" if Tesla doesn't clear 500,000 deliveries.
- Giga Texas tour notes: Model Y, Model Y L, Cybertruck, and Cybercab are all built there (Model 3 is not); Cybercab is assembled on the building's southeast side.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas flagged Starship Flight 15 (~one month out) as potentially SpaceX's biggest reusability test since the IPO, with Flight 16 (before year-end) framed as potentially even bigger.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX's AI president described "Stargaze," a satellite-based tracking system using over 30,000 in-orbit star sensors to track 17,000+ objects and ~90% of maneuvering satellites crossing Starlink's orbital paths.
- Starship-launched Starlink V3 satellites reportedly deliver 10x bandwidth per satellite versus V2 mini and 20-30x throughput per launch versus Falcon.
- SpaceX secured 800 MHz spectrum enabling entry into mobile telecom, competing with carriers like AT&T and T-Mobile, though one commentator cautioned this will be harder than expected and smaller than the AI/compute opportunity.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- Pivotal Research reportedly raised its SpaceX price target to ~$230 (buy rating); a reported ~$8 billion deal covers spectrum rights for 15,000 direct-to-device satellites.
- Jeff Bezos said he has personally invested $28 billion in Blue Origin, which just raised $10 billion in external funding (oversubscribed); Musk responded "Bravo." A SpaceX investor guest said he remains wary of Blue Origin's execution risk and declined to invest in the round.
