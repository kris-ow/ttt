This episode of brighterwithherbert, with guest Jo Bhakdi, covers a large spotted Cybercab lot in Palo Alto, a new European FSD Supervised approval, a Cybercab manual-control Easter egg, Ashok Elluswamy's vision-only philosophy, NHTSA's audit dynamics around Cybercab, and supply-chain reports of a large Optimus production order.

## Robotaxi
1. **Palo Alto Cybercab lot spotted:** A commentator referred to as Phil Beisel found roughly 100 Cybercabs (engineering prototypes and apparent production vehicles, with California, Florida, and Texas plates) parked in a lot near Tesla's California headquarters in Palo Alto, having earlier spotted six near Page Mill Road. Bhakdi notes Tesla currently can only run ride-hail service (human safety driver, like Uber/Lyft) in California, not driverless robotaxi, and speculates this stockpiling could signal preparation for a California robotaxi permit.
2. **California permit timing:** Bhakdi says Tesla is "very very close" to or past the point where required mileage/data thresholds for California approval should be met, and he "would not be surprised" if Tesla receives California robotaxi approval in Q4 2026, which he calls a potential catalyst.
3. **Cybercab fleet tracker:** Bhakdi's Pioneer Lands tracker shows 41 commercially deployed Cybercabs (updated hourly), versus his pre-launch expectation of 20–30 at launch; growth has slowed to about two added over the prior two days. Separately, he cites 45 Cybercabs registered with the state of Texas as the deployment ceiling Tesla is currently approaching, and speculates a jump to around 100 registered could come by the end of the week.
4. **Ride wait-time squeeze:** Bhakdi says Tesla reduced the ride cancellation wait time from roughly seven minutes to about four minutes since the Cybercab launch, which he interprets as pressure to reuse limited vehicles for more rides and a sign Tesla may need to increase fleet size.
5. **Manual control Easter egg found:** A rider (Joe Tegtmeyer, per the video) found a Cybercab in what appeared to be an engineering validation configuration, showing onscreen virtual manual driving controls (horn button, stop button, swipe-to-shift) that he wasn't able to use to start a ride; both host and Bhakdi treat this as an unintended glitch/Easter egg rather than a normal feature.
6. **NHTSA audit dynamics:** Bhakdi argues Tesla's Cybercab, lacking traditional controls, is currently in violation of FMVSS brake requirements and would fail an NHTSA audit as things stand, but NHTSA has proposed a new rule under review that would resolve the issue; he expects the rule decision by year-end and considers rejection unlikely. He theorizes (his own thesis) that Tesla deliberately timed the Cybercab launch announcement to occur right after the rule's public comment period closed, to avoid Uber and Waymo flooding NHTSA with opposing comments.
7. **1 million unsupervised robotaxi miles:** Ashok Elluswamy announced Tesla has achieved 1 million miles of unsupervised robotaxi operation, which Bhakdi calculates implies roughly 17% week-over-week growth and could put the fleet near 1,000–2,000 cars by year-end, "maybe even more."

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Slovenia approves FSD Supervised:** Tesla announced FSD Supervised approval in Slovenia, the sixth European country after Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, and Estonia. Bhakdi notes the EU-wide vote is October 6, and current approvals represent under 10% of the population versus the 65% threshold required; France and Germany together represent about 33% of the population, and a "no" from both would likely block EU-wide passage. Bhakdi says single countries may still be able to approve FSD independently if the EU vote fails, though some approvals (e.g., the Netherlands, per his understanding) may need to be redone depending on the EU outcome.
2. **France discussions:** The host references a prior report that Elon Musk spoke with the French prime minister about FSD approval, with Musk reportedly saying "I do this quite often"; outcome remains unknown.
3. **German political angle:** Bhakdi highlights a regional election win by the AfD in Saxony-Anhalt, noting Musk's ties to the party and prior discussions with the state of Brandenburg about piloting autonomous vehicles (Cybercabs or Model Ys) to serve remote regions — part of what Bhakdi has separately projected as a large-scale (millions of vehicles) rural robotaxi opportunity in Germany. He argues rightward political shifts in eastern German states could help open the German market to Tesla.
4. **Vision-only philosophy reaffirmed:** In video remarks from the Cybercab event, Ashok Elluswamy argued self-driving is fundamentally an AI/intelligence problem, not a sensor problem, stating cameras provide sufficient information and that lidar/radar-heavy approaches stem from 2008-era DARPA-driven lack of AI capability. Elon Musk reportedly endorsed similar comments. Bhakdi and the host also note that heavy rain does not impair vision-based driving as well as some assume, and that lidar can actually perform worse in rain/fog due to laser reflection off water droplets.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Large Optimus order reported:** Citing supply-chain/China-based reporting (not a Tesla announcement), the host says Tesla has placed an initial order for roughly 5,000 Optimus robots — its first production order in the thousands, following earlier pilot runs of a few hundred units — with a stated delivery target of around 15,000 units this year. Supplier guidance reportedly calls for a production run rate of about 1,000 units per week in September, ramping to roughly 2,500 per week by year-end. Bhakdi says he believes the report given it concerns firm supplier orders rather than general deals, and expects the 5,000–15,000 units to be used mainly for internal testing, with external sales or pilot partnerships more realistic in 2027 (he estimates summer or Q3 2027) and "many thousands" produced in Q4 2026 scaling to "tens of thousands" in Q1 for internal use.
