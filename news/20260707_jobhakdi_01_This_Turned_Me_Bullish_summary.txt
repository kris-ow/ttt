This video from Jo Bhakdi builds a back-of-envelope financial model projecting SpaceX's data center (Colossus 1, 2, 3) EBIT growth through 2027, driven by AI compute leasing to clients like Anthropic and Google, and uses Tesla's and Palantir's forward P/E multiples as comps to derive SpaceX price targets. Tesla appears only as a valuation benchmark and a scale comparison, not as a subject of independent analysis.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX modeled as a vertically integrated data center builder:** Citing Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas, the host argues SpaceX's most underappreciated near-term opportunity is "terrestrial compute" — building and leasing out data centers before space-based compute is ready — enabled by vertical integration across liquid cooling, battery installation, substations, and eventually turbines, solar manufacturing, networking, and compute itself (referencing "Terafab").
2. **Tesla energy tech cited as SpaceX's build-out advantage:** The host attributes SpaceX's data-center speed/cost edge partly to shared capabilities with the Tesla team, including Megapack additions, gas turbine installation, and grid substation connectivity — capabilities the host says traditional data center operators lack, as demonstrated at Colossus 1 and 2.
3. **On-the-ground buildout confirmation:** The host relayed a conversation with Cern Basher, who visited Colossus 2 and 3 and reportedly observed active construction activity on a weekend, taken as evidence the buildout is progressing as modeled.

## Financials
1. **SpaceX EBIT growth model:** The host's model projects SpaceX's EBIT run rate rising from roughly $12 billion currently (on ~$26 billion revenue run rate including Anthropic and Google deals) to $20 billion by the second half of 2026, $31 billion by mid-2027, and $50 billion by end of 2027 — driven by rising installed energy capacity (from 1.2 to a targeted ~2 gigawatts by end of 2027) and a compute multiplier from Blackwell and eventual Vera Rubin GPU deployment.
2. **SpaceX vs. Tesla forward P/E comparison:** At a ~$150 share price and ~$2 trillion market cap, SpaceX's forward run-rate P/E is calculated at ~170x, versus Tesla's ~200x — meaning SpaceX is already "cheaper" than Tesla on this metric, per the host's model.
3. **Tesla-multiple-implied SpaceX price targets:** Applying Tesla's ~200x forward P/E to the modeled EBIT run rates yields implied SpaceX prices of $179 (last half-year), $314 (end of 2026), $478 (mid-2027), and $747 (end of 2027) — the host notes the $314 figure coincides with Adam Jonas's stated $300 price target.
4. **Palantir-multiple comparison as a more conservative case:** Applying Palantir's ~90x forward P/E instead yields SpaceX targets of $81 (current), $141 (end of 2026, near current price), $215 (mid-2027), and $336 (end of 2027) — the host considers this comp more reasonable than the Tesla comp and expects actual outcomes to fall between the two scenarios.
5. **Scale comparison to Tesla robotaxi thesis:** The host frames the projected $50 billion SpaceX EBIT run rate as equivalent in profit contribution to roughly 100,000 deployed Tesla robotaxis operating at run rate.
6. **Host's bottom-line stance:** Despite viewing SpaceX as overvalued at IPO, the host now says the modeled earnings growth is likely real enough to justify buying "a lot" of SpaceX stock, particularly anticipating a price dip when share lockups expire between August and November 2026, potentially pushing shares below the IPO price.
