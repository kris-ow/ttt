## Robotaxi
- **Tesla overtakes Waymo in Texas registrations:** Tesla now shows 1,302 registered robotaxis in Texas versus Waymo's 1,297, per Sawyer Merritt — the first time Tesla has led. These are registration counts, not operational fleet figures; RobotaxiTracker.com's operational data remains stale at 839 total since August 7.
- **Austin sightings surge:** A third-party tracker counted roughly 1,400 Cybercab sightings in Austin on a recent Monday, almost all driverless, up from fewer than 600 about a month earlier. A separate tracker reported Cybercab reaching rough price and wait-time parity with Uber in Austin during a rainy-day geofence expansion.
- **Service expansion:** Austin robotaxi coverage expanded to about 440 square miles, its largest footprint yet; Tesla said Dallas robotaxi rides would begin this month, with customer Cybercab deliveries planned for late 2026.
- **Efficiency gains:** Cybercab currently achieves 6.18 miles/kWh at 3,113 lbs using a 47.6 kWh battery; Musk said future efficiency improvements could enable a roughly 40 kWh pack (~16% smaller), potentially yielding meaningfully more vehicles from the same battery supply.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Cybertruck wind-down date keeps shifting:** Musk has now given three different end-dates for Cybertruck production — "around the middle of next year," then "in about 8 months," now "through the end of summer 2027" — with Tesla citing backlog, per Electrek. The line will be dismantled to expand Cybercab production at Giga Texas.
- **Prices hiked, orders restricted:** Tesla raised Cybertruck prices up to 10% since August (cheapest configuration now $92,490, up from $69,990); the Premium AWD trim is no longer orderable, leaving only Cyberbeast at $109,990–$110,000. New orders are quoted for March–June 2027 delivery, just before the line closes.
- **Demand picture:** Cox Automotive estimates cited by The Tesla Space put Cybertruck US sales at ~39,000 (2024), ~20,000 (last year), and under 12,000 this year — well below the Giga Texas line's 125,000/year design capacity. It remains the top-selling US electric pickup (~11,600 units through September) ahead of Silverado EV (~5,300) and Sierra EV/Rivian R1T (~4,600–4,700 each).
- **Unplugged Performance offers to take over production:** Tesla tuner Unplugged Performance publicly proposed taking over Cybertruck manufacturing at Giga Texas, funded by investors, in exchange for a license fee plus FSD/Robotaxi revenue share; it also pitched a three-row "CyberSUV" and unmanned "CyberCargo." Electrek's Fred Lambert says he doubts Tesla will accept.
- **Roadster reveal draws criticism:** Porsche CEO Michael Leiters said Porsche wants to "completely steer clear from any kind of effects and shows," contrasting Tesla's thruster-powered Roadster flip reveal with Porsche's Mission S unveiling. Electrek's Lambert separately argues the Roadster's cold-gas thruster physics limit it to brief stunts, not real flight.
- **Semi delivery milestone:** Tesla delivered the first 50 of a 650-truck order to Chicago carrier HMD Trucking, a deal reportedly worth about $15 million; HMD's pilot truck averaged 1.74 kWh/mile over 4,000+ miles, in line with Tesla's claims.
- **China sales weak:** Electrek cites China retail sales falling 66% in October to a four-year low.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus Academy framed as core bottleneck:** Musk said on Tesla's October 21 earnings call that the company needs roughly 10,000–20,000 Optimus robots to gain sufficient training experience; Ashok Elluswamy described training starting from human demonstrations before simulation and real-world practice.
- **AI6 chip memory cut, bandwidth unchanged:** Musk confirmed Optimus's AI6 chip memory was reduced from 216GB to 144GB with no change to bandwidth — a change that could allow more robots to be built from the same memory supply.
- **Fremont production underway:** Per Tesla's quarterly report, Optimus production has begun on large lines in Fremont, described as in early ramp-up with equipment and processes still changing. External customer deliveries are expected around the end of 2027.

## SpaceX — Starship
- **Flight 15 targets historic double-catch:** SpaceX plans to launch Starship Flight 15 Friday, November 13 (window opens 4:45 p.m. CT, backups through Nov 19), using Booster 22 and Ship 42, attempting to catch both the booster and upper stage at the tower — a first. SpaceX has caught the booster repeatedly since October 2024 but never the upper stage.
- **Flight 14 recap released:** SpaceX published a 21-minute film on Flight 14 (September 28), the first Starship flight to reach orbit, during which 26 next-gen Starlink satellites were deployed.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- **Google's Don Harrison resigns from SpaceX board:** Effective November 4, Harrison — Google's President of Global Partnerships and a SpaceX director since 2015 — stepped down. Musk cited "too many conflicts of interest with Alphabet," pointing to Google's growing competitive and commercial overlap with SpaceX across AI compute, cloud, satellites, and Waymo robotaxis.
- **Google's stake and exposure:** Alphabet held roughly 555 million SpaceX shares (~$83.7B as of September 30, ~95% of its reported stock portfolio) and added ~3.85 million more shares in Q3; Alphabet's Q3 "other income" showed a $8.2 billion unrealized loss largely tied to SpaceX shares.
- **Merger process remains early-stage:** Both companies' boards have special committees reviewing a potential merger; Musk said he isn't negotiating directly and there's no guarantee of a deal. Reuters says talks are early; Bloomberg suggests an announcement could come in H1 next year. A cited prediction-market estimate put the odds of an announcement this year at ~8.5%, down from ~20% in late October.
- **SpaceX valuation:** Shares traded near $142 as of November 10 (~$1.9 trillion market value), up from the $135 IPO price (~$1.77 trillion) on June 12. SpaceX's Q3 revenue was $14.66 billion, up 161% year-over-year, with Starlink at 13.6 million subscribers generating 85% of operating profit and an AI/"super intelligence" segment contributing roughly $7 billion.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- **Singulus solar deal underpins Starmind buildout:** SpaceX signed a supply contract with Germany's Singulus Technologies for heterojunction solar cell manufacturing equipment to support over 100 gigawatts of capacity at its Bastrop, Texas facility; the first production line (order volume over €60 million) delivers in H1 2027. Singulus shares jumped roughly 31% on the news.
- **Starmind orbital compute concept:** SpaceX's Starmind constellation aims to put AI supercomputers in orbit powered by solar energy; its FCC application for up to 1 million orbital data-center satellites remains pending. SpaceX's updated spec now targets 250 kW peak / 175 kW average power per satellite, up from an earlier 150 kW/120 kW draft.
- **Terafab wafer targets:** Terafab has reportedly told Japanese equipment makers it plans 1 million silicon wafers/month starting in 2032 (~700,000 memory, 300,000+ logic) at a ~100 million sq ft site in Grimes County, Texas; Musk said equipment costs will reach tens of billions by early 2028, potentially over $100 billion long-term.
- **Grok rebuild and real-world performance:** Musk said Grok's training/inference stack was rewritten from scratch in C/C++ after removing JAX and SGLang, borrowing engineering from Starlink; he said Grok 5 "should surpass" Anthropic's Opus 5.5 within two to three months. SpaceX uses Grok for rocket-part debris inspection, reportedly catching defects in ~99.9% of cases versus ~94% for human inspectors.

## Energy
