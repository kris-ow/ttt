This video from Munro Live covers the official launch event for the Slate truck in Los Angeles, where the vehicle's price, specs, and modular design were publicly revealed for the first time. The video features interviews with Slate CEO Eric Kyper, a propulsion engineer named Ben, and Munro Live founder Sandy Munro, who have all been involved with the Slate project since its earliest days. The core thesis is that Munro Live played a foundational consulting role in helping Slate achieve its radically low price point through cost benchmarking, decision-matrix engineering, and parts-reuse methodology.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Slate truck price confirmed at $24,950:** The Slate truck launched at $24,950, making it the lowest-cost truck and the lowest-cost battery electric vehicle currently available in the United States. Eric Kyper confirmed the team held this price target from the very beginning of the program, which began approximately three to four years prior to the launch event. Munro Live was involved in setting subsystem-level cost targets before any designs were committed to CAD.

2. **Key specs announced at launch:** The Slate truck features 205 miles of range, an LFP prismatic cell-to-pack battery architecture, 1,550 lbs of payload capacity, and 2,000 lbs of towing capacity. The powertrain is rear-wheel drive with a 181-horsepower rear electric drive motor, a 12.44:1 final drive ratio, and a single-speed transmission. DC fast charging is supported. Thermal management uses a PTC heater rather than a heat pump, a deliberate cost-driven decision. Zero-to-60 mph is 8 seconds.

3. **Modular SUV/truck conversion system:** The Slate truck is designed to be converted between a two-door pickup and multiple SUV configurations (fastback and squareback) by the owner. The conversion process is error-proofed in a sequential "Russian doll" fashion — the system uses sensors to detect when the roll cage, side panels, and rear seats are installed in the correct order to ensure occupant safety. Curtain airbags are integrated into the roll cage assembly itself, so customers do not perform any airbag installation; the vehicle detects the connection automatically.

4. **Composite exterior and customization platform:** All exterior body panels are injection-molded glass-filled polypropylene, described as highly dent-resistant. If cracked, panels can be repaired with body filler and re-wrapped, or replaced via exterior fasteners. The vehicle supports over 200 color wraps and a broad accessories ecosystem including T-rail mounting points throughout the bed and cabin for tie-downs, tano covers, bike racks, and more. Customers can also remove the Slate emblem and replace it with custom branding.

5. **Frunk highlighted as class-leading:** The front trunk provides 7 cubic feet of storage, which the Munro team characterized as best-in-class relative to the vehicle's footprint — noting that no competitive vehicle of similar shadow size offers a frunk of comparable volume. Achieving this required extensive packaging iteration, including tilting the entire front cooling module rearward to lower the lift-over height.

6. **Suspension and chassis architecture:** The front suspension uses MacPherson struts and the rear uses a De Dion axle. The Munro host noted the De Dion setup offers lower unsprung mass and better isolation of electric drive motor noise from the chassis. Tire pressure is run at approximately 35 PSI. Ride quality during a brief drive was described as smooth and grounded despite a short wheelbase.

7. **Munro Live's role from inception:** Sandy Munro and the Munro Live team were engaged by Slate from the company's earliest days — when Slate had only six employees working out of six cubicles rented inside Munro's own facility. Munro provided benchmarking, decision-matrix consulting, and manufacturing cost analysis. Eric Kyper credited Munro with helping inform key architectural decisions including suspension choice, brake system selection (disc over drum, though drum brakes were initially preferred for cost), thermal architecture, and parts commonality/tooling reuse strategies. Munro Live continues to work with Slate on cost optimization heading into production.

8. **Sandy Munro personally ordered two units:** Sandy Munro stated he ordered two Slate trucks early on, after seeing the vehicle at a prototype stage. He cited the 200-mile range, the vehicle's repairability, and the ease and speed of the conversion process (the team demonstrated converting the vehicle twice in approximately one and a half hours) as his primary reasons for purchasing.
