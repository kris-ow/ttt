This Electrified episode leads with Figure's zero-shot home-cleaning demo as a bullish signal for humanoid robot generalization, then moves through a rumor-debunking on Optimus production numbers, a look at Tesla's post-tax-credit EV market share, Tesla robotaxi safety incidents and an NHTSA regulatory deadline, Tesla Semi deployment news, and a brief SpaceX Starship scheduling update.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Figure's zero-shot home trial as an Optimus bullish signal:** Figure placed its Figure 03 robots in 30 rented homes with no prior training or fine-tuning and tasked them with cleaning tasks (picking up objects, folding towels, making beds). Using its "index" pre-training program (a data-collection effort of paid humans recording real-world tasks), Figure reported overall task success jumping from roughly 8–9% (without index pre-training) to 56% with it — specifically 62% for towel folding, 40% for picking up toys, and 67% for bed-making. The host argues this shows humanoids can learn generalizable skills from human-recorded data rather than needing a large deployed robot fleet to self-collect data first, which could shorten Optimus's training/production timeline; he speculates failure rates could fall to roughly 15–20% "sometime next year" if the trend continues, though he flags this is his own extrapolation, not a Tesla or Figure claim.
2. **Rumored 50,000-unit 2026 Optimus production figure dismissed:** The host says a rumor (via an account he names "Ming" on X) claiming Tesla expects 50,000 Optimus units to roll off the line in 2026 for global Gigafactory deployment is essentially not credible, putting the odds at roughly 0.1%. He separately notes a more plausible version of the rumor — that Tesla executives are in China conducting final factory audits across the Optimus supply chain ahead of possible production starting around October — as believable, but expects any near-term production to be small-scale.
3. **Samsung starts trial AI5 chip production:** Samsung has begun trial production of Tesla's AI5 chip at the Taylor, Texas fab. Full-scale production, and any AI5 chip appearing in a Tesla vehicle, is not expected until well into 2027; per the host, these chips are expected to be used first in Optimus.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's US EV market share rebounds post-tax-credit:** Year-to-date (Jan–Aug) Tesla's US EV market share averaged around 52%, versus roughly 44% over the same period a year earlier. In the post-EV-tax-credit period, Tesla's quarterly market share has stayed consistently above 50%, compared to trending around 45% for most of the prior two years. However, overall US EV sales as a share of all new vehicle sales have leveled out at around 5.8%, which tempers the market-share gain since it comes against a flat overall EV market.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
1. **July robotaxi safety incidents and cumulative tally:** Tesla's July robotaxi safety data showed two at-fault incidents, both in Austin and both described as minor: one robotaxi squeezed around a stopped shuttle bus in a hotel driveway and hit a metal bollard; another turned out of a parking lot into a low-hanging metal chain blocking the exit. The host notes this suggests the current FSD version (described by Tesla as roughly 40% complete on some rollout metric) still hasn't resolved handling of thin horizontal objects like chains. Cumulatively since robotaxi service began, there have been 26 reported incidents: 11 Tesla's fault, 13 the fault of another party, and 2 no-fault (one involving a dog, one uneven pavement).
2. **NHTSA investigation into Cybercab raises regulatory risk:** NHTSA's ongoing probe includes a question (question 21) asking Tesla to explain how the Cybercab complies with FMVSS Part 555 without exemption, after Tesla self-certified arguing certain requirements (e.g., a physical brake pedal) don't apply. The host notes NHTSA has previously stated a vehicle without a brake pedal could not be self-certified, and while NHTSA has more recently agreed brake pedals aren't required for robotaxi operation, it still required Tesla to go through the Part 555 exemption process — which caps annual units at 2,500. The host considers this the biggest regulatory risk facing Tesla's robotaxi program currently. Tesla has until September 30th to respond to NHTSA's questions.
3. **Waymo resumes San Antonio service after flood suspension:** Waymo restarted robotaxi service in San Antonio, five months after a vehicle was swept away in a flash flood; the resumed service covers a roughly 60-mile area including downtown and the airport, with dozens of vehicles. The host notes Waymo did not disclose that it will now avoid or restrict certain higher flood-risk streets during heavy rain, characterizing this as a workaround rather than a genuine fix to the underlying driving problem.

## Electric Vehicles (Tesla Semi)
1. **Forum Mobility charging depots and semi fleet deals:** Forum Mobility is breaking ground on four new electric charging depots in California, with Tesla operating public Megacharger sites at three of them; Forum has separately contracted for fleet offtake of over 330 Tesla Semis. A Tesla Semi representative interviewed separately said a sleeper cab version is "not off the table" once public charging infrastructure matures, and noted the long-range Tesla Semi (200-inch wheelbase vs. 163-inch standard range) has extra cabin storage space; the rep would not give an official bobtail (no-trailer) range figure beyond saying it would go "very far" — the host's own estimate of roughly 900 miles is explicitly his own AI-assisted guess, not a Tesla figure.
2. **IMC Logistics to deploy 50 Tesla Semis:** IMC Logistics, described as the largest marine drayage provider in the US, announced plans to deploy 50 Tesla Semis in California, mixing standard-range units for drayage operations and long-range units for Southern California-to-inland routes. The host notes Tesla's Semi order backlog likely extends well into 2027, meaning all new production units are going to customers rather than demo fleets.

## Energy
1. **Project Crystal Sun solar manufacturing tax incentive approved:** The Lamar School District in Houston unanimously approved a tax incentive agreement for Tesla's Project Crystal Sun, a 100-gigawatt solar manufacturing facility. Construction is set to begin this year, finishing in 2028, with commercial operations targeted for Q1 2029.
