Tesla's EPA certification documents for the Cybercab — filed May 21, certified May 26, 2026 — reveal the robotaxi's full technical specifications for the first time, including a 3,113-lb curb weight, 219 HP motor, and a 47.6 kWh battery pack.

## Robotaxi

1. **EPA Certification Complete, Commerce Date Passed:** The Certificate Summary Information (CSI) for EPA test group TTSLV00.0L1A was certified May 26, 2026, with an "Introduction into Commerce Date" of May 29, 2026 — consistent with the production ramp that began at Giga Texas in April. The Cybercab is cleared for US roads from an emissions and efficiency standpoint, though regulatory approval for unsupervised autonomous driving has not been granted.

2. **Full Powertrain and Battery Specs:** The Cybercab uses a front-wheel-drive, single-speed layout with a 163 kW (219 HP) AC 3-Phase Permanent Magnet motor and regenerative braking on the front wheels. The lithium-ion battery pack is rated at 326V / 146 Ah (~47.6 kWh). The front-wheel-drive configuration is a departure from most Tesla vehicles, attributed to packaging and cost optimization. AC recharge energy is listed at 53.365 kWh (~12% above usable capacity), with wireless inductive charging as the primary charging method.

3. **Unadjusted Range of 418 Miles Supports "~300 Mile" Claim:** The EPA Multi-Cycle Test unadjusted combined range is 418.2 miles; applying the standard ~0.7 EPA correction factor yields approximately 293 miles, consistent with Tesla's prior "close to 300 miles" claim. Highway unadjusted range is 375.4 miles. The filing also confirms a 165 Wh/mi efficiency rating, previously reported by Electrek.

4. **Weight Heavier Than Expected for a Two-Seater:** At 3,113 lbs, the Cybercab is roughly 750 lbs lighter than a Model 3 Standard Range but substantially heavier than comparable two-seat vehicles (Mazda MX-5 Miata: ~2,341 lbs; Honda Civic: ~2,877 lbs). The battery pack alone accounts for approximately 680 lbs (based on the filing's stated specific energy of 154 Wh/kg). GVWR is 3,730 lbs, leaving ~617 lbs of payload capacity.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues that while the EPA filing confirms an impressively efficient and deliberately over-specced powertrain, the 3,113-lb curb weight is surprisingly high for a purpose-built two-seat robotaxi. The author emphasizes that all specs are moot until Tesla obtains unsupervised autonomy approval — characterizing the Cybercab as "a very efficient, but purposeless" vehicle in its current state.
