This video from Dillon Loomis at "electrified" covers Tesla's major expansion of unsupervised robotaxi operations to the entire Austin geofence, a significant milestone in Tesla's autonomous deployment. The video also touches on Tesla Semi infrastructure buildout in Texas, strong global EV sales figures across multiple markets, and a minor FSD software update. The core thesis is that Tesla's robotaxi program may be approaching an inflection point where fleet expansion in Austin — and potentially other cities — could accelerate rapidly.

## Robotaxi

1. **Full Austin Geofence Now Covered by Unsupervised Robotaxis:** Tesla has officially made unsupervised robotaxi operations available across the entire Austin geofence, not just the primary zone where they had previously been operating. Earlier in the same morning, a partial expansion to the south was noted, and then shortly after, the full geofence was confirmed as covered. This is characterized as a clear signal of Tesla's confidence in its unsupervised operations and a meaningful positive development after a period of limited movement on the program.

2. **Safety Monitors May No Longer Be Needed in Austin:** With the full geofence now covered by unsupervised vehicles, safety monitor positions in Austin may effectively be eliminated. The host notes that the only remaining bottleneck to rapid fleet expansion would be backend infrastructure — specifically remote operations — and that if that infrastructure is in place, the "floodgates" in Austin could theoretically open at any time. Tesla may choose to hold fleet size steady for a week or two to validate operations across the expanded geofence before scaling up.

3. **Cybercabs Staging in Dallas:** A photo was shared showing 26 Cybercabs in a Dallas lot, and a second image shared the same afternoon showed over 30 Cybercabs in that lot — a strong indicator that Tesla is preparing for a near-term expansion of robotaxi operations in Dallas. Separately, a ground reporter named Saddok in Houston confirmed the addition of a Model Y as vehicle number nine in the Houston fleet, suggesting fleet additions may not be limited to Cybercabs going forward.

4. **Fleet Counts Referenced:** The video references small but growing fleet numbers across cities, consistent with community-tracked data from RobotaxiTracker.com, including nine vehicles in Houston (one of which is a Model Y).

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 Update Mostly Bug Fixes:** A new version of FSD 14.3 is rolling out, but it is described as primarily containing bug fixes rather than major capability changes. One noted feature is improved driver monitoring sensitivity, including better eye gaze tracking, eyewear handling, and higher accuracy in variable lighting — though the official Tesla account clarified this feature had actually been released in a prior update and simply not highlighted in those release notes.

2. **Railroad Crossing Arm Incident Highlighted:** A video from a user named Platinum Patriarch OnX showed an FSD highway incident involving railroad-style gate crossing arms used to block a road. The host acknowledges this was a supervisor error but emphasizes that truly unsupervised, scalable autonomy requires these edge cases to be reliably handled. He notes that railroad-style gate arms have been a recurring challenge for multiple autonomous driving companies.

3. **FSD 14 Coming to Australia:** Tesla sent an update to Australian owners stating that FSD 14 for Hardware 4 vehicles is in its final stages of local development and testing, with no confirmed release date yet but active work underway.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Record Australia Sales in May:** Tesla posted its best-ever monthly sales figure in Australia in May, exceeding 6,400 units. The Model Y L was cited as a standout contributor by Tesla's Australia country director Tom Drew, who described the result as not isolated. Year-to-date Tesla sales in Australia are up 56% compared to last year. The Model Y was also confirmed as the bestselling vehicle of any kind in Australia for May across all fuel types — the first EV in history to lead Australia's overall sales charts.

2. **Model Y L Spotted in the U.S.:** The Model Y L, which has been driving Tesla's Australia momentum, has been spotted multiple times in the United States, including the previous day on the Fremont test track. The host speculates it could officially launch in the U.S. sometime in the summer.

3. **Strong Norway and China Sales:** In Norway, Tesla achieved over a 21% market share in May (21.5%), making it the bestselling car brand for the month. In China, Tesla's wholesale figure for May came in at 85,982 units, up 8.2% over April and up 39.4% year-over-year — the highest wholesale figure for Giga Shanghai so far in 2026. Year-to-date through May, Tesla China is up 29.4% versus the same period last year, selling over 85,900 more units than in the prior year period. The host notes this is before FSD receives broad approval in China.

4. **Japan and Germany Posting Massive Year-Over-Year Gains:** Tesla Japan saw May sales up 182% year-over-year, with year-to-date sales up 157% — over 8,100 registrations compared to 3,178 in the same period last year — despite overall foreign brand registrations in Japan being down 6%. Tesla Germany registered over 5,100 vehicles in May, up 322% year-over-year, and year-to-date registrations in Germany exceed 21,000 — up 200% versus last year and already 9% above Tesla's full-year 2025 Germany total.

5. **Used Cybertrucks Briefly Appeared on Tesla's Website:** Tesla's inventory page momentarily listed used Cybertrucks for sale, but the inventory appeared to be gone by the following day.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla Semi Dominates EV Truck Voucher Applications in California:** According to California voucher data covering January 2025 through February 2026, Tesla Semi accounted for 965 out of 1,067 total Class 8 tractor voucher applications. Daimler and Volvo combined for fewer than 100 applications. Tesla's voucher applications in this round exceeded the total of all other Class 8 tractor applications combined from 2021 through February 2026.

2. **Tesla Semi Dashboard Launched — 644 Verified Orders:** The host has built and launched an interactive Tesla Semi dashboard tracking 644 verified Semi orders across nine confirmed customers with public confirmation. In the last 18 months, 48 trucks have been delivered — 45 to Pepsi, two to WEV, and one to DHL. Tesla stated earlier in June 2026 that most of its 2026 Semi output is already accounted for, implying production is still only a handful of units per week. The dashboard also tracks 81 total known semi-charging depot sites (operational, under construction, and planned).

3. **Tesla Mega Charger Projects Filed in Texas:** According to filings with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, Tesla has new mega charger infrastructure projects tied to San Antonio (estimated cost $400,000, projected completion May 2027), Laredo (estimated cost $100,000, completion December 2026), and Houston (estimated cost $200,000, start June 22, 2026, completion June 2027). These projects are expected to support Tesla Semi charging in major Texas freight markets. The filings do not specify the number of charging stalls or which fleets will have access.
