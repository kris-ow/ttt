In this video, Jo Bhakdi argues that investors should choose Tesla over SpaceX at SpaceX's IPO, presenting four reasons why Tesla is the superior investment at current prices. His core thesis is that SpaceX is massively overvalued on conventional metrics, laden with near-term "air pockets," and that Tesla is on the verge of its own business inflection point — specifically robotaxi deployment at scale — making Tesla the smarter risk-adjusted bet right now.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO valuation is extreme by any conventional metric:** Jo Bhakdi opens by noting that SpaceX is pricing its IPO at $1.75 trillion ($135/share), which he expects to rise to roughly $160/share and a ~$2 trillion market cap the following day. He calculates that at ~$20 billion in revenue, SpaceX is trading at approximately 100 times sales — well above Palantir, which he cites as the most highly valued publicly traded AI company at roughly 60 times revenue. He further estimates SpaceX's price-to-earnings at roughly 400x, given adjusted EBITDA of approximately $6.6 billion in 2025 and even slower earnings growth. He characterizes this as "a total joke" from a conventional valuation standpoint.

2. **SpaceX's pitch deck is heavy on vision and light on financials:** Bhakdi notes that investors must scroll to slide 47 of SpaceX's pitch deck before encountering any financial growth data — 33% revenue growth from $14 billion to $18.7 billion. He observes that the deck reads like a startup pitch deck, with a total addressable market of $28.5 trillion against a conventional space TAM of only $370 billion (~1% of the total), with the remainder attributed to enterprise AI and orbital compute categories where SpaceX currently generates essentially no revenue. He also flags that free cash flow is being hit hard due to surging capital expenditures.

3. **SpaceX's real forcing functions are years away:** Bhakdi acknowledges that he believes SpaceX's true DCF fair value is between $8–12 trillion — roughly 4x the IPO valuation — driven by orbital compute and Grok/XAI's enterprise AI potential via the "Cursor" deal. However, he argues these revenue streams won't materialize in earnest until the second half of 2027 at the earliest, or possibly the first half of 2028. In the intervening period (from June 2026), SpaceX will be running on "hope fumes" with no strong cash flow floor to prevent a steep correction. He predicts SpaceX will be among the first casualties in any market downturn.

4. **Tesla-SpaceX merger thesis favors buying Tesla now:** Bhakdi contends that a Tesla-SpaceX merger is "pretty much a done deal" and that it must occur at a minimum 1:1 ratio (SpaceX shares to Tesla shares), with Bhakdi arguing it could go as favorable as 1:2 for Tesla shareholders. He reasons that at a 1:1 ratio at SpaceX's $1.75 trillion valuation, Tesla would be implied at roughly $475/share — versus its current price of ~$380–$390. Since Tesla can be bought today at a significant discount to that implied merger price, buying SpaceX at a higher absolute valuation makes no strategic sense from a merger arbitrage perspective. He also notes that Tesla shareholders must vote to approve any merger, giving them leverage to reject an unfavorable deal.

## Robotaxi

1. **Tesla robotaxi at scale is imminent — Bhakdi expects 10,000+ Cybercabs by December 2026:** Bhakdi expresses strong conviction that Tesla will reach robotaxi deployment "at scale" in 2026, defining scale as "thousands and thousands, very likely over 10,000" vehicles. He states he believes Cybercab deployments could begin as early as June 2026 (the current month at time of recording), and that each passing week makes it less likely he is wrong. He references a photo of Cybercabs in Dallas as visual evidence of forward progress. He frames this as Tesla's equivalent of SpaceX's H2 2027 forcing functions — except it's happening now, placing Tesla at a far more favorable point in its inflection timeline.

2. **Tesla's business inflection is now vs. SpaceX's inflection in 2027:** Bhakdi directly compares Tesla's near-term robotaxi and Optimus forcing functions to SpaceX's orbital compute and enterprise AI catalysts. He argues that Tesla's cash flow forcing functions — robotaxi revenue, Optimus — will kick in in H2 2026 (starting in approximately 3 weeks from the video's recording), whereas SpaceX's equivalent catalysts are 12–18 months further out. He frames this as the decisive reason to prefer Tesla: you are buying at the tail end of the air gaps, not the front end.

## Financials

1. **Tesla's current price represents an extreme discount to its all-time high:** Bhakdi characterizes Tesla's current stock price of approximately $380 as "absolutely insane" given that the all-time high in December 2025 was $490. He views this dislocation — driven partly by market correction and partly by capital rotating into the SpaceX IPO — as a compelling entry point for Tesla investors who believe in the robotaxi and Optimus thesis.

2. **SpaceX's financials show EBITDA growth lagging revenue growth:** Bhakdi highlights that SpaceX's adjusted EBITDA grew to $6.6 billion in 2025 but at a rate slower than the 33% top-line revenue growth, with free cash flow falling sharply due to surging capital expenditures. He notes that some confusion surrounds whether losses from the XAI division are properly reflected in these figures. Combined with the 400x implied P/E and 100x price-to-sales, he presents SpaceX's conventional financial profile as deeply unattractive at IPO valuation.
