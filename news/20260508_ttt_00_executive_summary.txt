## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD crosses 10 billion miles.** Tesla emailed customers confirming FSD (Supervised) has surpassed 10 billion real-world miles; the live counter had already moved to 10.133 billion miles at time of reporting. The scale of this training corpus is a core competitive moat argument — replication by competitors is structurally difficult.

- **2026 Model Y passes all eight NHTSA NCAP ADAS evaluations** — the first vehicle to do so. The four new criteria (pedestrian AEB, lane keeping, blind-spot warning, blind-spot intervention) were added for model year 2026 vehicles manufactured on or after November 12, 2025. Electrek notes the "first" framing is partly an artifact of NHTSA delaying full NCAP implementation by one year at the auto industry's request, meaning most OEMs haven't submitted yet.

- **FSD Engineering Analysis running concurrently.** NHTSA is simultaneously conducting an Engineering Analysis covering approximately 3.2 million Tesla vehicles — the stage typically preceding a recall — having found FSD's camera system fails in sun glare and fog conditions and may be under-reporting related crashes. The Electrek author doubts this leads to a recall.

- **FSD 14.3.2 at approximately 50% of the consumer fleet** at time of recording, with full rollout expected within one to two weeks per the brighterwithherbert panel. Ashok confirmed at Tesla's earnings call that the Robotaxi fleet runs a variant of the FSD 14.3 series, likely somewhat ahead of the consumer build.

- **FSD 14.3 architecture significance.** Phil Biceel (ex-Apple, ex-Rivian) explained that FSD 14.3 rewrote the runtime, creating headroom for higher model inference frequency and establishing a reinforcement learning pipeline enabling rapid field-issue fine-tuning. The panel acknowledged ongoing regression risk from fine-tuning but expressed confidence in the tooling to address it quickly.

- **FSD expansion signals in China and Europe.** Rumors surfaced of FSD 14.3.2 being made available to Tesla employees in China under NDA, with concurrent Autopilot engineer hiring suggesting Tesla may seek full China approval near-term. In Europe, the Dutch regulator (RDW) presented findings on FSD to all 27 EU member states and is advocating for bloc-wide approval, though the panel noted the process may still proceed country-by-country. Tesla simultaneously updated FSD purchase terms and one-time pricing deadlines in Belgium, Germany, and Sweden.

## Robotaxi

- **Unsupervised fleet expansion pace.** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised fleet grew 46% in the five days preceding the brighterwithherbert episode (from 26 to 38 vehicles). Dallas and Houston are noted as 100% unsupervised — no safety monitor in any vehicle in either city. The panel's emphasis: rate of change, not absolute count, is the signal at this stage.

- **Cybercab logistics activity accelerating.** Joe Tegtmeyer documented 40–50 Cybercabs now at or near Giga Texas (up from ~20 previously), and a Tesla Semi was filmed transporting a fresh batch. Phil Biceel assessed Cybercabs are already conducting autonomous test runs in Austin but are not yet available for customer hailing. He sees Cybercabs ultimately replacing Model Ys in the fleet.

- **Bhakdi's 2026 deployment assumption: 10,000 robotaxis.** Bhakdi uses 10,000 deployed robotaxis by year-end 2026 as the base case in his DCF, describing it as conservative. His scaling trajectory: 100,000 in 2027 (inflection), 200,000 in 2028, roughly 10 million by 2034–35. He flags 2028 as the year the technical problem should be "thoroughly solved" and regulatory readiness broad enough for mass scaling.

- **Operational constraints, not safety, are now the binding limit.** The brighterwithherbert panel consensus: pickup/drop-off behavior, idle vehicle management, and keeping remote operations interventions at near-zero frequency are the current scaling bottlenecks. Wireless charging — flagged as a future bottleneck — has reportedly not yet received regulatory approval and is significantly delayed.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Tesla communications blackout on Optimus is intentional.** Elon cited competitive intelligence concerns — frame-by-frame copying by rivals — as the reason Tesla stopped releasing Optimus demos. A promised Q1 2026 reveal did not occur; Elon stated the bot was not performing enough use cases at the required level. Phil Biceel drew a direct parallel to Apple's product secrecy culture and expressed comfort with the approach.

- **Fremont factory conversion ongoing.** Herbert countered delay concerns by noting Tesla continues to spend real capital converting Fremont to Optimus production — describing it as a "10 million per year line." He argues that if Tesla were truly shelving Optimus, capex would be deferred as it was with Giga Mexico. The spending is read as near-term intent to ramp.

- **AI5 chip framed as a hard prerequisite.** Phil Biceel stated his view that Optimus will not enter meaningful production until Tesla's AI5 chip is available to power it.

- **Bhakdi's Optimus projections:** 10,000 units delivered in 2026 → 50,000 in 2027 → 200,000 in 2028 → 1.5 million deployed by 2030 (with ~900,000 produced that year). He characterizes these as speculative but reasonable.

- **Optimus 3 referenced** by The Tesla Beat panel as part of Tesla's unprecedented simultaneous product pipeline (alongside Semi, Model Y L, Roadster, potential Cyber SUV). No technical details provided.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Semi officially priced: $290,000 (Long Range), $260,000 (Standard Range).** CARB regulatory filing (Executive Order A-374-0095, April 15) confirmed battery specs: 822 kWh and 548 kWh respectively, both NCMA/4680 chemistry. The Long Range achieves 500 miles at 82,000 lb GCW despite carrying an 822 kWh pack — roughly 80 kWh smaller than Musk's 2022 ~900 kWh estimate — aided by the production unit being ~1,000 lbs lighter than the prototype.

- **430+ units ordered at ACT Expo Las Vegas.** Watt EV ordered 370 Semis (first 50 in 2026, full fleet by end of 2027 — following a prior pilot and published white papers); Big F Transport ordered 40 post-demo; NA Container Freight Lines ordered 20 with first unit operating in 2027. Tesla Semi pricing is approximately 25–35% below competing electric Class 8 trucks ($400K–$500K range).

- **Semi production has begun at Giga Nevada.** The first unit came off the line at the dedicated Semi facility. The FutureAzA panel expects the ramp to be "painfully slow" initially. Aerial footage from Zanegler shows opened crates and packing materials outside indicating recent equipment installation, bridge crane components, silver paint shop vents confirming paint operations are live, and a likely-completed stamping press installation. A new traffic circle behind the facility suggests anticipated vehicle volume increases.

- **Semi efficiency leads the class.** At 1.7 kWh/mile (~0.6 miles/kWh), the Semi is materially more efficient than the Freightliner eCascadia (~0.4 miles/kWh on 550 kWh/230 miles) and Volvo VNR Electric. Both Semi trims share an 800 kW tri-motor drivetrain and support 1.2 MW Megacharger speeds via MCS 3.2 connector. Energy cost ~$0.20/mile electric vs. ~$0.67/mile diesel; estimated 10-year savings exceeding $400,000.

- **Model Y L spotted under cover at Giga Texas.** The Tesla Beat panel reports Giga Texas is preparing for two large SUV variants — the Model Y L and a potential Cyber SUV on the Cybertruck platform. Model Y L is expected to require no new production line build-out, with an unveil possible within 60 days and deliveries potentially starting around October 2026 at approximately $55,000–$60,000.

- **Model S and Model X production has ended.** The last units rolled off the line with a Tesla employee signing ceremony. Model Y L and potential Cyber SUV cited as volume successors.

- **China-built Model 3 now available in Canada at ~$30,000 USD** — described as the cheapest Tesla offered anywhere globally, roughly 35% below the average new Canadian vehicle price. Import cap is 49,000 units for 2026, rising through 2030.

- **Record April sales across multiple markets.** Tesla China-made EV deliveries rose 36% YoY to 79,478 units (best April ever for China ops). South Korea posted a record 13,190 units, outselling BMW and Mercedes combined. Germany posted its best April ever. Norway BEV market share reached ~98%, with Model Y at ~5,400 units and Model 3 at ~2,000 units.

- **Model Y confirmed as the world's best-selling EV in March 2026.** BYD posted its 10th consecutive month of sales decline; Tesla passed BYD in Q1 2026.

## Financials

- **Bhakdi DCF: ~$600/share intrinsic value for 2026**, explicitly conditioned on 10,000 robotaxis deployed by year-end. Methodology applies a probability-weighted risk discount on top of raw DCF output (e.g., a $300–$400 raw robotaxi value discounted by a ~20% failure probability). He pushed original model timelines back 12–18 months from earlier projections, which lowers his 2030 target modestly — but he characterizes it as "still pretty good." He believes the actual market price could significantly exceed $600 if deployment milestones are met, but warns of a subsequent correction back to intrinsic value.

- **Tesla Semi TCO economics.** Analyst projections for 2026 Semi deliveries range from 5,000 to 15,000 units. Megacharger network currently spans 66 locations across 15 states; two-post setup starting at $188,000. Semi facility estimated production ceiling discussed at 50,000 units/year (assumes 2–3 shifts), representing less than 25% of the U.S. Class 8 market.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX–Tesla merger debate at ~70% Polymarket odds.** No merger has been formally proposed; Elon has not publicly stated he wants one. The brighterwithherbert panel framed the debate as "low data." Bhakdi argues any merger at an adequate premium is by definition a fair reflection of known risks; he does not believe Tesla requires a significant premium beyond roughly equal exchange, though he notes SpaceX may be somewhat overhyped at IPO and Tesla slightly undervalued.

- **Terafab filing: $55 billion upfront, up to $119 billion total**, potentially sited in Grimes County, Texas (~2 hours from Austin). Multiple sources flagged domestic chip fabrication as a critical five-year constraint for both Robotaxi and Optimus scaling — without it, chip supply could cap Tesla's growth and compress valuation multiples regardless of software progress.

- **SpaceX–Anthropic compute deal:** SpaceX leased Colossus 1 (~300 MW capacity) to Anthropic, enabling Anthropic to double rate limits and dramatically raise API caps (Tier 1 input tokens/min: 30,000 → 500,000; context: 2M → 10M tokens). Revenue estimates range from $5B–$16B/year (panel discussion) to $20–28B/year (Brian Wang AWS-comparable estimate). The panel read this as strategically weakening OpenAI by strengthening its top competitor.

- **Rivian–Uber robotaxi deal on R2 platform announced** at ACT Expo. Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe also confirmed the "Also" micromobility subsidiary signed a DoorDash food delivery bot deal. Rivian's 30,000+ EDVs on the road with Amazon represent a scaled fleet deployment the panel noted as a real — if narrowly scoped — competitive data point in autonomous delivery.

- **Tesla stock up ~11% on the week**, closing above its 200-day moving average (~$402–$404). Technical analyst "retro tech" on X noted this is the third breakout from a downward channel; the prior two preceded runs to all-time highs.

- **Lexus TZ all-electric three-row SUV unveiled** for the U.S. market: 77 or 96 kWh options, up to 300 miles range, 150 kW peak charging, NACS port. Sawyer Merritt noted the vehicle includes fake gear shifts and simulated engine sounds. At 150 kW peak and 300-mile ceiling, performance benchmarks fall below comparable Tesla segments.
