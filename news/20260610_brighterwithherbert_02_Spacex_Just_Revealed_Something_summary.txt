This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert interviewing Jo Bhakdi — economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur behind pioneerlands.org — on the eve of what is described as the biggest IPO in history: SpaceX's public offering. The conversation centers on newly revealed details from a SpaceX/Elon Musk interview filmed at the Starlink terminal factory in Bastrop, Texas, covering the Terafab, AI satellites, Starship, and Starlink scaling — and whether the SpaceX IPO is a buy, a hold, or a trap. Tesla and its robotaxi/FSD trajectory are used as a parallel throughout.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO pricing and Morningstar bear case:** Morningstar analyst Nicholas Owens published a report valuing SpaceX at $63 per share — a 53% discount to the IPO offering price of $135. Their analysis acknowledges that even in optimistic scenarios assuming a rapidly reusable Starship and commercialized space-based data centers, SpaceX would be worth only $154 per share in the most bullish moonshot case, which they assign a 7% probability. Jo Bhakdi dismisses the Morningstar approach as multiple-based rather than DCF-based, arguing it systematically undervalues disruptive companies.

2. **Jo Bhakdi's short-term vs. long-term IPO thesis:** Bhakdi says he is "highly convinced" the stock will drop after an initial pump, projecting it will likely rise above a $2 trillion valuation shortly after IPO before falling to approximately $1.5 trillion by December 2025 (resolved: December 2026 per publish date of 2026-06-10). He identifies the highest-risk window for SpaceX's stock price as roughly August through January (i.e., August 2026 through January 2027), attributing this to the absence of any orbital AI satellite launches during that window and the delay of S&P 500 index inclusion until June 2027. Herbert suggests the weakest window may be December 2026 through June 2027 for similar reasons.

3. **Terafab scale and joint Tesla/SpaceX project:** For the first time publicly confirmed in this interview, Elon Musk disclosed that the Terafab — a joint project between Tesla and SpaceX — is expected to be approximately 100 million square feet, making it roughly 10 times larger than Tesla's Giga Texas factory. At a target output of 1 terawatt of chips per year, that would represent double the entire annual electricity consumption of the United States (currently approximately 0.5 terawatts). Bhakdi notes the chip design includes integrated memory, reducing dependence on external memory suppliers like Micron.

4. **SpaceX AI satellite (AI1) first public reveal:** SpaceX unveiled the first generation of its AI satellite, designated AI1. When deployed, it has a wingspan of approximately 70 meters — comparable to the 68-meter wingspan of a Boeing 747-8. The satellite is designed for 150 kilowatt peak power and 120 kilowatt sustained average compute payload, with solar array efficiency of 250 watts per square meter and double-sided radiators rated at 1,400 watts per square meter. Elon stated the AI satellite is actually simpler than a Starlink satellite, using solar array technology already being developed for Starlink V3. Bhakdi confirms his earlier (November) projections about AI satellite feasibility were roughly correct, noting his cost and mass-per-kilogram estimates were slightly conservative compared to SpaceX's actual numbers.

5. **Starlink scaling and Terafab production timeline:** SpaceX executives stated that the solar manufacturing facility for the Terafab is already under construction, with the AI satellite production building to follow. They expect solar and AI satellite production to reach "reasonable volume" by the end of 2026 (resolved: end of next year from publish date 2026-06-10). They also described a next-generation Starlink terminal design and projected that ultimately a few hundred million Starlink terminals will be in use globally, supported by a direct-to-cell constellation connecting directly to mobile phones.

6. **Starship reusability and launch cadence targets:** Elon stated that SpaceX has not yet achieved full Starship reusability but expects to do so "later this year" (2026). Beyond full reusability, the goal is rapid reusability — catching, returning to launch stand, and reflying without refurbishment, like an aircraft. Starship Block 3 reportedly produces more than double the thrust of the Saturn V moon rocket; version 4 is projected to reach approximately three times Saturn V thrust. The long-term goal is more than one Starship launch per hour. Bhakdi estimates approximately 50 Starship flights would be needed to put 1 gigawatt of AI compute capacity in orbit by end of 2027, and notes that even without reusability, each flight still delivers payload (as demonstrated in flight 13, which successfully deployed Starlink satellites).

7. **Elon's 1 gigawatt in space by end-of-2027 target:** Bhakdi highlights Elon's stated goal of 1 gigawatt of AI compute capacity in orbit by end of next year (resolved: end of 2027 per publish date), followed by 10 gigawatts the year after (2028). Bhakdi calls this "crazy" but mathematically plausible given Starship flight activity, and notes that even losing every Starship without reuse still delivers the payload. He projects that by 2028, SpaceX's orbital AI business will begin generating billions to tens of billions in free cash flow.

8. **Anthropic and Google as early AI satellite customers:** Bhakdi and Herbert note that Anthropic publicly stated interest in renting SpaceX AI satellite capacity when available, and Google has been cited as lining up to rent capacity. Both see this as validation that demand exists, though Anthropic indicated it is not actively planning around it yet given the ~two-year timeline.

## Robotaxi

1. **Tesla FSD/Cybercab as the SpaceX parallel:** Bhakdi draws an explicit parallel between SpaceX's critical dependency on Starship working and Tesla's critical dependency on FSD working — framing both as binary inflection points. He states: "For Tesla the most important thing is FSD — does it work? And if it does, everything's already... you can see the Cybercab, you can see the scaling." This suggests Bhakdi views FSD viability as the unlock for Cybercab fleet scaling and Tesla's robotaxi business, consistent with the Tesla thesis framework.

## Financials

1. **Jo Bhakdi's DCF methodology vs. consensus:** Bhakdi argues that nearly all Wall Street analysts covering disruptive companies rely on multiple-based valuation rather than discounted cash flow models, which he considers the only intellectually valid approach. He says he is still finalizing his SpaceX DCF model because "I can't believe the numbers," suggesting the model implies SpaceX may be dramatically undervalued even at a $2 trillion IPO valuation. He specifically criticizes Morningstar's analysis for treating SpaceX's non-Starlink businesses (AI satellites, Starship) as having near-zero value — a pattern he says mirrors how analysts historically undervalued Tesla's non-car businesses.

2. **Starlink cash flow trajectory:** Bhakdi describes Starlink as already generating "tens of billions" in cash flow, with a path to "hundreds of billions," serving as the financial foundation that funds the AI satellite buildout. He characterizes Tesla's Model Y and Model 3 as analogous cash-generating "pearls" that funded Tesla's autonomous and robotics ambitions, with Starlink playing the same role for SpaceX's orbital AI compute ambitions.
