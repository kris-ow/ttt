The video covers SpaceX's announcement of Starbase Louisiana, a new 14,000-acre spaceport on the Gulf Coast intended to become the company's high-cadence Starship launch site, discussed by the host and guest Larry Goldberg. The core thesis is that this facility solves Starship's biggest bottleneck — propellant logistics at scale — by co-locating on-site fuel production with the launch pads, and that it materially advances both the interplanetary/Starmind (orbital compute) ambitions and Elon Musk's broader vision.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starbase Louisiana announced:** SpaceX unveiled Starbase Louisiana, a spaceport on 14,000 acres in Vermilion Parish on the Gulf Coast. Elon Musk said it will have over a dozen launch towers and eventually enable more than 30 Starship flights per day, calling it the biggest launch site on Earth. Construction starts next year with the first launch targeted for 2029.
2. **Scale and cost:** The site includes 10 launch sites (five pairs, each pair roughly 1.5 miles/miles apart, per a third-party rendering group called Interstellar GW), a production site over 2 km long, and a "star factory" with over 7.6 million square feet of floor space — described as over four times the area of the existing gigabay design. The whole site spans over 12 miles. Total investment is stated at $100 billion; Goldberg noted combining this with the Terafab project in Texas would put combined investment near $200 billion.
3. **Propellant as the key bottleneck:** A third-party analysis (Aaron Bernat/Mach 33) found that supplying propellant commercially via truck for roughly 1,000 flights a day would require about 1,000 truckloads a day — one truck every 86 seconds — making conventional logistics unworkable. SpaceX will instead produce propellant on-site (pipelines, oxygen plants) next to the natural gas supply. Musk responded to this analysis on X, calling it "mostly correct."
4. **Total launch pad network and capacity:** Including Starbase Louisiana, SpaceX now has 17 Starship launch pads under development, which the host cited as eventually having a combined capacity of 15,000 launches per year (more than twice the total number of rocket launches in all of history) — a figure Goldberg said he hadn't independently verified but thought plausible, even potentially higher.
5. **Interplanetary launches require far more propellant flights:** Goldberg noted that every interplanetary (or lunar) departure requires roughly 10 propellant-tanker launches to orbit to fuel it, meaning claims that a handful of daily launches would suffice (e.g., "seven launches a week") dramatically undercount true launch cadence needs once Moon/Mars missions are factored in. He predicted the number of launch pads will expand well beyond the 10-12 currently discussed, including offshore launch platforms.
6. **Jobs, economic deal, and site context:** Louisiana's state release cites 3,000 direct jobs and roughly 8,100 total jobs (direct plus indirect), an average salary of $92,600 (stated as 192% above the Vermilion Parish average), and a $25 million donation to a local community foundation. The state deal has SpaceX paying $25 million/year for 25 years (with escalator, $20 million upfront), totaling $820 million to Louisiana over 25 years. Musk stated the true jobs number will be "way more" than the official figures once ancillary industries (airport, satellite manufacturing, power infrastructure) are counted.
7. **Texas Terafab agreement also advancing:** Separately, Texas has published a fully signed tax agreement (described as a 34-page document) between the governor's office and Terafab, indicating that project has moved into an agreement phase.
