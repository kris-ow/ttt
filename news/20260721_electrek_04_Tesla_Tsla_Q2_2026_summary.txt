Electrek previews Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings (reporting July 22 after market close), noting deliveries were strong but profitability remains uncertain, especially with regulatory credits fading.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries:** Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2 2026, up 25% year-over-year and about 74,000 above analyst consensus — its best-ever second quarter and first year-over-year delivery growth in two years. Production was 451,758, meaning Tesla drew down roughly 28,000 units of inventory, a reversal from Q1 2026 when it built about 50,000 more vehicles than it sold.
2. **BYD comparison:** Tesla's 25% delivery growth narrowed the gap with BYD, whose battery-electric sales fell about 8% in the same quarter; Tesla remains second but the two companies' trajectories are diverging.

## Energy
1. **Storage deployment growth:** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of energy storage in Q2 2026, up more than 40% from 9.6 GWh in Q2 2025 and up from 8.8 GWh in Q1 2026, though slightly below the roughly 13.8 GWh analysts expected.

## Financials
1. **Revenue estimates:** Estimize consensus puts Q2 2026 revenue at about $25.9 billion; broader analyst consensus is around $26.4 billion, both up 15–17% from $22.5 billion in Q2 2025 and above Tesla's $22.38 billion in Q1 2026. Estimates range widely from about $22.3 billion to over $29 billion, reflecting uncertainty over how much discounting was needed to hit record volume.
2. **EPS estimates:** Analysts expect non-GAAP EPS of about $0.53, up roughly 33% from $0.40 in Q2 2025; Estimize's crowd estimate is $0.52. Estimates range from $0.27 to $0.74, with Deutsche Bank at just $0.36. Options traders are pricing in about a 7.6% stock swing in either direction post-earnings.
3. **Margin and regulatory credit concerns:** Tesla's automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits was around 12.5% in Q1 2026; whether it held at record volume is the key unknown. Regulatory credit sales were $439 million in Q2 2025, already down more than 50% year-over-year, and have kept shrinking since the $7,500 federal EV tax credit expired on September 30, 2025.

## Robotaxi / Humanoid Bots
1. **Shareholder Q&A voting dynamics:** Tesla's Say Technologies shareholder Q&A drew 425 questions from about 1,710 participants representing 8.6 million shares by submission close, up from roughly 300 questions the prior week. The two top questions by shares represented (about 5.3–5.4 million shares each, up from 4.8 million) ask about the status of the Optimus Gen 3 production ramp and the main constraints on expanding robotaxi operations faster — both considered "soft" questions, suggesting a single large holder is promoting them. The most upvoted question by raw votes (732 votes, representing 1.5 million shares, up from under a million) asks why Tesla has missed short-term robotaxi guidance three earnings reports in a row.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the 25% delivery jump is a genuine positive but stresses that volume isn't profit — the critical unknown is whether automotive gross margin (excluding credits) held up or fell as regulatory credits and the federal tax credit wind down. He contends the earnings report will have little stock impact regardless, since Tesla's valuation is tied to Robotaxi and Optimus, and finds it ironic that Tesla's first good quarter in two years was driven entirely by EV sales even as shareholders have downplayed the automotive business.
