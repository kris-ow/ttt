This episode of Electrified covers a cluster of SpaceX Starlink Mobile regulatory wins (FCC approval for a 15,000-satellite Gen 2 constellation and a new nationwide low-band spectrum acquisition), clarifies Elon Musk's comments on the Terafab chip project's structure with TSMC and Intel, and runs through several smaller Tesla items including FSD rollout progress, a CyberCab NHTSA audit extension, and robotaxi performance in rain and at night.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **FCC approves 15,000-satellite Gen 2 constellation for Starlink Mobile:** The FCC cleared SpaceX's application (filed the prior September) to launch and operate 15,000 next-generation satellites for Starlink Mobile, including a waiver letting SpaceX offer wireless service via satellite without a leasing agreement with a ground-based mobile operator. The new constellation is designed to deliver roughly 150 megabits per second per user versus the current T-Satellite (T-Mobile) service's roughly 4 megabits per second using about 650 satellites. The authorization also permits orbital altitudes as low as 326 km (202 miles), modestly lower than the Gen 1 direct-to-cell altitude of about 360 km, and allows use of additional spectrum acquired from AST SpaceMobile's rival EchoStar deal, with full use pending a deal step expected to close in November next year.
2. **SpaceX agrees to acquire nationwide low-band spectrum:** SpaceX announced an agreement to buy a nationwide low-band spectrum license portfolio — previously held by investment firm Grain Management, which acquired it from T-Mobile earlier this year — with some reports pricing the deal around $6 billion (unconfirmed/not officially disclosed). Pending final FCC approval, this spectrum would let Starlink Mobile combine its satellite constellation with a terrestrial network to penetrate buildings, something the host says current direct-to-cell physics can't achieve, directly rebutting recent dismissive comments from T-Mobile's CFO and AT&T CEO John Stankey about satellite-to-phone service.
3. **Market reaction and competitive implications:** On the spectrum news, T-Mobile shares fell more than 6% after hours, AT&T more than 7%, and Verizon just under 7%. The host notes AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile recently formed a joint venture for satellite phone coverage specifically to avoid depending solely on Starlink, but argues owning its own spectrum gives SpaceX leverage so it no longer needs T-Mobile's permission to reach phones directly.
4. **Revenue framing vs. compute-leasing deals:** The host estimates the total US mobile carrier revenue market at roughly $200 billion/year, suggesting a 10–20% Starlink Mobile share could eventually mean $20–40 billion in revenue, but not materializing until the early 2030s, with the actual mobile service launch targeted for end of 2027 "if all goes well." He contrasts this with SpaceX's Nvidia chip compute-leasing deals (around $40 billion), which he says have shown roughly 12-month payback periods, framing Starlink Mobile as a slower, less certain revenue path despite strategic value.
5. **Elon Musk's valuation comment:** Musk said he sees "a path to SpaceX being worth orders of magnitude more than the current Earth economy," a claim the host treats as a long-term, speculative marker to revisit around 2030 rather than a near-term prediction.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute / Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab structure clarified by Musk:** Musk stated SpaceX and Tesla will build and run the Terafab chip facility, with TSMC potentially subleasing floor space but not owning or operating any part of it — a more limited role than some had speculated. The host interprets this as likely mirroring Intel's role as a process/technology partner rather than an owner-operator, with Intel supplying its newer 14A process technology in exchange for a dedicated customer, while any TSMC subleasing would likely come with output reserved for SpaceX and/or Tesla; terms remain unconfirmed.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Ship 40 returns to launch site; false slowdown report denied:** After a multi-week wait, Ship 40 is returning to the launch site with no official plan yet disclosed for its use. Separately, a report claimed SpaceX might be slowing facility construction; Musk responded "False. We are accelerating rapidly."

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD rebranding to "TAD" in the EU:** Tesla's assisted driving has been officially renamed across the EU (no longer called FSD there), with Slovakia reportedly set to approve TAD in the next few days (per the host, sourced from "the word," unconfirmed). The host also flagged a live FSD-in-EU approval tracking dashboard.
2. **FSD 14.3 Light rolling out to Hardware 3:** A still-small rollout of FSD 14.3 Light has begun for Hardware 3 vehicles, with release notes reportedly matching the earlier 14.2 Lite version.
3. **Robotaxi performance in rain and at night:** Ashok Elluswamy posted that the robotaxi service is "ready for service, rain or shine," and the robotaxi account shared video of operation in heavy rain. A reporter covering the Tampa robotaxi service said Waymo's service was paused for weather while Tesla's robotaxi service stayed active. The host calls this anecdotal but notes it as a sign of growing confidence addressing criticism that robotaxis can't handle night or inclement weather.

## Robotaxi
1. **NHTSA extends CyberCab audit deadline:** NHTSA granted Tesla a 30-day extension on its CyberCab audit information request, moving the deadline from September 30th to October 30th; Tesla had requested an 8-week extension (to November 20th) citing the "voluminous nature" of the request, but received only a partial extension. The host reiterates his earlier (labeled as his own speculation) theory that Tesla may be strategically delaying to operate CyberCabs under new FMVSS rules rather than seeking a formal exemption.
