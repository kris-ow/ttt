This video from brighterwithherbert features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi discussing a cluster of Tesla-related developments: new robotaxi hiring signals pointing to Las Vegas and Doral, Florida as next expansion cities; a major EU regulatory meeting on FSD approval; an upcoming EU law mandating driver monitoring systems in every new car by July 2026; and Cybercab production and deployment progress at Giga Texas. The core thesis is that converging signals — FSD readiness, hiring patterns, Cybercab production ramp, and regulatory tailwinds — suggest Tesla's robotaxi network is on the verge of a significant expansion.

## Robotaxi

1. **Hiring signals point to Las Vegas and Doral, Florida as next unsupervised robotaxi cities:** Tesla is actively hiring robotaxi field response specialists in Doral, Florida and Las Vegas, Nevada — the same roles it staffed in Dallas and Houston ahead of those cities' launches. Herbert and Jo Bhakdi interpret this as a strong signal that unsupervised robotaxi service in these two cities is imminent. Bhakdi notes that field operators are only needed once actual deployment begins, reinforcing the view that something is already in motion.

2. **Current unsupervised robotaxi fleet is small but scaling across three cities:** Bhakdi references the current unsupervised robotaxi footprint in Austin, Houston, and Dallas, noting figures of roughly 25 in Austin, and five and six vehicles respectively in Houston and Dallas (consistent with RobotaxiTracker.com data). He characterizes this as "mini scaling" that is happening fast and serves as proof of concept for broader rollout.

3. **Cybercab integration into the robotaxi fleet debated — timeline likely months, not weeks:** Herbert argues that Cybercab cannot enter the robotaxi fleet immediately because, as a brand-new vehicle with no existing fleet data, Tesla will need hundreds or potentially thousands of Cybercabs accumulating real-world miles before a public robotaxi launch. A first-week accident would be catastrophic for brand perception. Bhakdi partially agrees but counters that the camera layout, Hardware 4 chip, and control systems are nearly identical to Model Y, meaning the FSD fine-tuning required is minimal rather than a year-long effort.

4. **Fleet size projections for Cybercab robotaxi by end of 2026:** Bhakdi and Herbert converge on an estimate of 10,000–15,000 Cybercabs deployed as robotaxis by end of 2026. Bhakdi notes that even using the painful Cybertruck ramp as a worst-case analog — which reached roughly 30,000 units in its first nine months — a Cybercab ramp (described as easier to produce) cut in half still yields 15,000 units. Herbert reiterates his consistent estimate of 10,000–15,000 Cybercab robotaxis deployed by year-end.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **EU regulatory vote on FSD approval set for late June or early October 2026:** The European Commission's TCMV (Technical Committee for Motor Vehicles) held a meeting in Brussels where the Dutch RDW presented 18 months and 1 million miles of FSD testing results to EU member states. A vote to approve FSD across the entire EU — without requiring individual country retesting — is expected at either the late June or early October meeting. Approval requires at least 15 of 27 countries representing at least 65% of the EU population.

2. **Belgium's Flanders region moves immediately to fast-track FSD approval:** Immediately following the Brussels meeting, the Minister of Mobility for Flanders, Belgium posted that she had asked Tesla to transfer its FSD test dossier to her administration, with instructions to provide clarity on a rapid homologation process by end of the week. Herbert and Bhakdi note she is responsible for Flanders specifically, not all of Belgium, but characterize the move as a meaningful early signal of regional appetite for approval.

3. **Bhakdi assigns 30–50% probability to EU-wide FSD approval, calls it an upside surprise:** Bhakdi's base case is that the EU rejects the proposal, given institutional inertia, though he puts the odds of approval at somewhere between 30% and just under 50%. He frames EU-wide approval as a significant upside surprise. If the EU rejects it, he expects multiple individual countries — including the Netherlands, Czech Republic, Sweden, and potentially Bavaria — to approve FSD independently in 2026, driven by competitive pressure among regions to attract innovation.

4. **FSD performance in European urban environments highlighted via video:** The segment includes footage of Keith using FSD in Amsterdam, navigating extremely tight streets, cyclists, narrow bridges, and dense urban conditions. Both Herbert and Bhakdi express genuine amazement at FSD's ability to handle environments as varied as Amsterdam's canals, San Francisco, rural South Dakota, and China — characterizing it as evidence of a deeply generalized AI rather than a route-memorization system.

5. **FSD v13/14.3 cited as an important breakthrough enabling faster iteration:** Bhakdi references FSD version 14.3 as a significant milestone, describing it as a revamp that enabled high iteration speed and noting that the system is now proven to work at the level needed for unsupervised deployment.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybercab production at Giga Texas observed at 25–30 units on-site, with vehicles being transported away:** Joe Tegtmeyer's drone footage identified between 25 and 30 Cybercabs around Giga Texas, including at the factory exit point and outbound lot, with many being transported away over the prior week. Vehicles have been spotted on roads, supercharging, and in surrounding areas. Herbert and Bhakdi estimate current production at roughly 10–25 units per day, implying 300–500+ per month, and project this will scale rapidly. Bhakdi believes the cumulative number of Cybercabs already outside the factory is likely well over 200.

2. **Cybercab easier to produce than Cybertruck due to unboxed manufacturing method:** Bhakdi emphasizes that Cybercabs are being produced using Tesla's unboxed manufacturing method and are structurally simpler than Cybertruck, making them easier and faster to ramp. He uses Cybertruck's first-year production of roughly 30,000 units over nine months as a conservative floor for what Cybercab production could achieve, noting Cybercab should be significantly easier.

## Market & Competition

1. **EU mandatory driver monitoring law takes effect July 7, 2026 — applies to all new car registrations:** A regulation stemming from EU law (referenced as EU 2019/2014) requires that every new car registered in the EU from July 7, 2026 onward must include a mandatory driver monitoring system — interior cameras tracking eye movement, head position, and attention levels, with mandatory alerts for distraction or drowsiness. This applies to all manufacturers, not just Tesla. Herbert frames this as creating a bifurcation: either a car drives itself (FSD), or drivers face constant monitoring nags, making FSD a more attractive proposition.

2. **Ford filing driver monitoring patents in the US, including emotion and biometric sensing:** Ford has filed multiple patents related to in-cabin driver monitoring, including a distracted driving detection patent (2020), driver fatigue prevention patent (2022), and a lip-reading, facial expression, and emotion sensing patent filed in April 2026, as well as an in-cabin biometric system patent. A video clip from another creator warns that Ford's patents envision preventing the vehicle from shifting out of park if onboard sensors detect the driver is emotionally unfit to drive.

3. **Broader privacy and civil liberties concerns raised around vehicle AI and government access:** Bhakdi raises substantive concerns about the trajectory of in-car AI surveillance, arguing that driver monitoring systems — while starting benign — create infrastructure that governments could compel automakers to use for tracking, locking vehicles, or routing them. He specifically flags risks in the EU and China, noting that Tesla (and Optimus) operating at scale will face government pressure to cooperate with law enforcement or censorship mandates. Herbert partially pushes back, noting that smartphones and smart TVs already enable similar surveillance, though Bhakdi distinguishes physical vehicle control as a qualitatively new risk.
