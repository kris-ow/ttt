Michael Burry has disclosed a new short position against Tesla (TSLA), entered at $416.22 on June 30, 2026 — shorting into a roughly 10% intraday rally from Tesla's prior close of $379.71. The position is one leg of a broader basket of shorts Burry opened the same day against what he characterizes as an AI and semiconductor bubble.

## Market & Competition
1. **Burry's Tesla short is part of a multi-name basket:** The disclosure appeared in Burry's paid "Cassandra Unchained" Substack post ("Trading Post June 30, 2026"). Alongside Tesla, he also shorted Caterpillar (CAT) at $1,060.98, Nvidia (NVDA) at $198.09, the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) at $642.80, and Applied Materials (AMAT) at $729.40. The bulk of the note focuses on semiconductors: Burry argues the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is trading more than 65% above its 200-day moving average — a level he says has only been seen once before, in 2000 — and cites a price-to-sales ratio above 16x for the index. He rolled his SOXX puts from January 2027 to March 2027 at low-to-mid $400s strikes while holding QQQ January puts.

2. **No size disclosed; history of misreporting:** Burry did not reveal the dollar figure, share count, or options structure for the TSLA short. The article warns against repeating the 2021 error, when press reported his put options tied to 800,100 Tesla shares as a "$534 million" bet — a figure representing notional value (shares × closing price), not actual capital at risk. Burry covered that position by November 2021.

3. **Continued bearish stance on Tesla valuation:** Burry has remained publicly negative on Tesla, previously calling its valuation "ridiculously overvalued" in December and criticizing the ~$1 trillion pay package shareholders approved. He shut down his hedge fund in late 2025 and launched the Substack to discuss the AI bubble freely.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert agrees Tesla is "grossly overvalued" — trading around 295 times earnings on declining profits with ~3.6% annual dilution — but calls shorting it "super risky," arguing the stock is driven by hype and that a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger narrative gives Musk another catalyst to sustain investor enthusiasm. Lambert views Optimus as "vaporware."
