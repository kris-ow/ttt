This video is a casual Turo-rental review of an early Model 3 (VIN 858, built November 2017), with two hosts comparing its build quality, tech, and driving feel against newer Teslas they've owned. The content is anecdotal and nostalgic rather than analytical, with only minor points of interest to investors around legacy hardware/FSD upgrade economics and efficiency.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Early Model 3 holds up well:** The hosts found the roughly eight-year-old Model 3 (near 100,000 miles) to be well-preserved, with strong seats, working A/C, and a cheerful driving character, leading one host to say he'd choose it over many newer cars given used pricing and fuel savings versus gas vehicles.
2. **Efficiency and build variance noted:** The car reportedly achieved 239 Wh/mile driving 75-80 mph in Texas heat, which the hosts called excellent for a car of its age; by contrast, one host's own older Model 3 (assembled during the production ramp, per his account) had noticeably worse road and wind noise, illustrating build-quality variance across early production.
3. **Legacy hardware (HW2.5) and FSD upgrade path discussed:** The car runs on hardware version 2.5 with a beta Autopilot that lacks modern adaptive cruise control and, per one host, still misjudges routine driving situations (e.g., falsely flagging collision risk at green lights). The hosts noted Tesla offers a computer replacement (recalled as roughly $2,000) plus a monthly FSD subscription (recalled as around $100/month) to upgrade such cars to a newer FSD-capable computer, but concluded this isn't worthwhile for a rental car since renters often don't understand or use FSD.
4. **Feature gaps vs. modern Teslas:** The older unit lacks newer software features such as Grok integration and has a smaller center screen with thicker bezels compared to current models; it still built-in Spotify and receives over-the-air updates, and YouTube Music streaming was noted as added to Tesla vehicles in a recent year (recalled as 2023 or later).
