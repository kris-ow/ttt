The host (Jo Bhakdi) explains why Tesla stock cratered after earnings, arguing the real story isn't Tesla-specific weakness but a broad market repricing of growth/AI stocks amid geopolitical risk (the Iran war) and AI-sector uncertainty, using Google's earnings-day selloff and Micron/Nvidia moves as corroborating evidence. He maintains his long-term Robotaxi/Optimus thesis is intact but says he trimmed his Tesla position ahead of earnings and remains cautious near-term.

## Financials
1. **Tesla capex guidance spooked the market:** Tesla stock fell 13.38% the day after earnings, which the host attributes partly to Elon Musk signaling a much bigger capex year ahead without giving specific figures on the call; Wall Street had reportedly modeled around $25 billion capex for this year and $21 billion for next year, which the host calls too conservative given Robotaxi and Optimus scaling.
2. **Google's earnings beat but capex hike triggered a bigger selloff:** Google reported EPS and revenue beats (EPS came in far above estimates; revenue of $119 billion vs. $116 billion expected) with cloud revenue up 82% to $24 billion, yet the stock fell about 7%, worse proportionally than Tesla, after raising planned capex from roughly $185 billion to about $200 billion — evidence, per the host, that the market is punishing capex increases across the board regardless of underlying performance.
3. **Tesla balance sheet remains strong despite capex ramp:** Despite the higher capex outlook, Tesla's cash balance reportedly declined by only about $1 billion, sitting around $39–40 billion, which the host cites as evidence the core business is financially sound even as the stock is being repriced.
4. **Anthropic revenue growth deceleration cited as a systemic AI risk:** The host discusses third-party tracking (posted by another commentator) showing Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate at roughly $69 billion on July 10 and $74 billion by July 22, with growth pace reportedly slowing; he speculates enterprise AI overspend is reversing and warns that a confirmed slowdown could "break the entire AI narrative" for a period, though he flags this as unconfirmed and speculative.

## Market & Competition
1. **Broad market selloff framed as repricing of all growth/AI names:** NASDAQ fell about 2.2–2.5% on the day; Meta, Amazon (down ~4.5%), Nvidia, and Micron (initially) were all hit, which the host uses to argue Tesla's drop is not company-specific but part of a market-wide shift toward demanding immediate cash flow over future growth narratives.
2. **Micron as a relative winner and the host's largest position:** Micron rose about 3.15–3.28% even as the broader market fell, which the host attributes to its dominance (with SK Hynix and Samsung) of roughly 90% of the HBM RAM market critical to AI chips; he says Micron's forward P/E is around 7–8 and that Elon Musk publicly thanked Micron on Tesla's earnings call for a special HBM RAM allocation.
3. **Geopolitical and AI uncertainty are compressing valuations market-wide:** The host argues escalation risk from the Iran conflict (he says he lowered his confidence that it won't spiral further from 90% to 70%) combined with AI-driven uncertainty (citing Kimi K3, an open-weights Chinese model, matching or beating Anthropic's model on some benchmarks) is pushing investors to demand near-term earnings certainty, compressing price-to-earnings multiples across AI-exposed and growth stocks alike.
4. **10-year Treasury yield rising called a major macro headwind:** The host says the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed above 4.7%, calling this "extremely bad" and warning that a move above 5% would create serious problems for markets.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX valuation plunge:** SpaceX's implied share price fell to around $111 (down from about $220 a few weeks earlier and after a decline the prior day as well); the host says he is advising against buying SpaceX for now, preferring to wait for further share unlocks, despite being fundamentally bullish long-term. Later in the discussion he cites a $1.4 trillion valuation for SpaceX versus roughly $1 trillion for Tesla.
2. **Possible SpaceX earnings upside flagged:** The host says he believes SpaceX may have "huge surprises" coming in its own earnings, potentially doubling its EBIT run rate from around $11 billion to $22–25 billion, though he's unsure if that would be enough to lift the stock given current market sentiment.
3. **Merger timing speculation:** Responding to a viewer question, the host says he doesn't believe earnings are being consciously underplayed ahead of a Tesla-SpaceX merger, but says it's fairly clear from Musk's comments that such a merger is being prepared, and that it wouldn't be in Musk's interest for either company's stock to diverge too far from the other — though he frames this as an underlying bias rather than a deliberate plan.

## Robotaxi
1. **Host's conditions for a Tesla rally to $1,000:** The host reiterates that reaching a $1,000 share price this year would require roughly 10,000–15,000 Cybercabs deployed, resolution of the Iran conflict, and a broader sentiment shift away from AI-related fear — and says he now considers this "increasingly unlikely," though he still thinks the Cybercab deployment milestone itself could be achieved.
2. **Earnings call tone described as less confident on Robotaxi timeline:** The host says Musk's comments on the call gave an impression of "we're just going to take all the time in the world" on Robotaxi scaling, which he found not to be a confidence booster, though he maintains this doesn't change his underlying Robotaxi thesis.
