The article (citing a Bloomberg report) states that GM CEO Mary Barra is reportedly considering Sterling Anderson, former Tesla Autopilot head and Aurora Innovation co-founder, as a potential successor, and is testing him in his new role as GM's Chief Product Officer.

## Market & Competition
1. **Anderson's Tesla/autonomy background:** Sterling Anderson led Tesla's Model X program and directed the Autopilot program from 2015-2016, before co-founding self-driving startup Aurora Innovation, which has focused on autonomous trucking.
2. **GM's succession test:** Per Bloomberg's report, Barra is reportedly "gauging" Anderson for the CEO role by giving him responsibility, since joining as Chief Product Officer in June, for GM's end-to-end product lifecycle across gas and electric vehicles, including hardware/software integration. The reported test centers on whether he can execute Barra's push to make GM a tech-first company, including delivering "eyes-off" autonomous driving for personal vehicles — a shift away from former Cruise unit's robotaxi-focused strategy. Barra, 63, has not announced a retirement date; if Anderson succeeds, the article notes he would be the first outsider with a tech background to lead the 117-year-old company.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert notes Anderson's "tech background" is only mostly accurate, given his Tesla, Aurora, and MIT history. He personally recalls Anderson as realistic about autonomy's limits at Tesla, suggesting that may be why he left Autopilot leadership, and credits him with building Aurora into what the author calls a multi-billion-dollar leader in autonomous trucking. He views GM's EV lineup as increasingly strong but hampered by inconsistent commitment to volume, partly due to political/lobbying choices he attributes to GM itself, and says fresh leadership could help.
