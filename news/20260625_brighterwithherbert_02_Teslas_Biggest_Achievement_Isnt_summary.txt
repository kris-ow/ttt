This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel, featuring guest Brian White of FutureAzA, argues that Tesla's most underappreciated achievements are not its headline autonomous driving or robotaxi ambitions, but rather its core contributions to vehicle safety, American manufacturing, and accelerating the global energy transition. The hosts walk through a series of recent data points — FSD safety footage, Cybertruck IIHS results, Tesla Vision airbag technology, and American-made vehicle rankings — to counter what they describe as unfair media narratives against Tesla. The discussion also touches on Goldman Sachs's projection for EV-driven oil demand reduction and the Model Y L's anticipated US launch.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3.4 Sideswipe Avoidance Footage:** A recently circulated real-world clip showed an owner's Tesla running FSD version 14.3.4 executing a lateral maneuver to avoid a vehicle that had drifted into its lane. The owner described it as avoiding a potentially dangerous sideswipe situation and stated they believed no human could have reacted as effectively, calling it "superhuman driving skill." The hosts discuss how the system briefly crossed the double yellow line to avoid the collision — noting there was no oncoming traffic — and contrast this with a handcoded system that might only apply the brakes.

2. **Media Misattribution of a Recent Crash to FSD:** The hosts reference a news story from roughly 48 hours prior (approximately June 23, 2026) in which global media outlets attributed a crash — in which a vehicle traveling at 70 mph in a residential area struck a home and killed a woman inside — to Tesla's FSD or Autopilot. The hosts state that Tesla responded with vehicle data showing the accelerator was pressed fully to the floor, indicating human control at the time of the crash. They argue that flooring the accelerator is not a known failure mode of FSD, drawing an analogy to a blender unexpectedly exploding.

3. **FSD Version 14 Rolling Out to New Markets and Models:** FSD version 14 is described as now rolling out in Australia and New Zealand. Notably, the hosts highlight that the Model Y L received its first-ever FSD activation update, marking the first time that variant could access Full Self-Driving anywhere in the world.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybertruck Earns IIHS Top Safety Pick for 2026:** The Cybertruck became the only pickup truck to earn the IIHS Top Safety Pick Plus — the organization's highest award — for 2026. In the pedestrian front crash prevention test, it avoided every single collision across all scenarios tested: daytime, nighttime, crossing child, and parallel adult. The hosts contrast this with the Ford F-150 and Ram 1500, which rated "very poor" in the moderate overlap front test. The Toyota Tundra also performed well, but the Cybertruck was the only EV in the category.

2. **Tesla Vision Enables Earlier Airbag Deployment:** Tesla released footage explaining a new safety feature in which the vehicle's camera system supplements existing accelerometers to make airbag deployment decisions up to 70 milliseconds earlier. The system works by using exterior cameras to identify when contact will be made and how severe a crash will be, passing that information to the airbag controller before impact sensors alone would trigger deployment. Tesla states this was developed over several years with thousands of simulations and physical sled tests, and that it rolls out as an over-the-air software update to existing vehicles — described as "outside the bounds of the five-star safety ratings."

3. **Cybertruck Real-World Crash Survivability:** An owner reported striking a utility pole head-on at speed while attempting to avoid a child running a red light on a bike. The owner reported the impact was absorbed with no serious injury to occupants and that even the utility pole was undamaged, crediting the vehicle's crumple zone design. Brian White contextualizes this by noting that the Cybertruck's aluminum front rail is scalloped and widens toward the rear, designed to deform progressively rather than buckle uniformly.

4. **Model Y L US Launch Described as Imminent:** The hosts discuss strong rumors that the Model Y L (a slightly longer, taller variant with a larger battery and more interior space) is coming to the United States imminently. Per the host, a source indicated it will be built at Giga Texas, and a Verge article published the prior week reportedly cited multiple sources confirming a Texas build location with potential deliveries as early as August 2026. The hosts frame it as a logical complement to the Cybercab in a future robotaxi network — the Cybercab satisfies most demand, but a larger vehicle is needed for versatility.

5. **Tesla Ranked Most American-Made Vehicle for Sixth Consecutive Year:** According to cars.com rankings for 2026, the Model Y and Model 3 hold the number one and number two spots respectively for most American-made vehicles — across all vehicle types, including gas cars. The hosts note this is the sixth consecutive year Tesla has claimed the top spot and contrast it with popular perception that traditional American brands like Ford have more US-made content.

## Market & Competition

1. **Goldman Sachs EV Oil Demand Projection:** A Goldman Sachs note projects that the global EV surge could reduce oil demand by up to 320,000 barrels per day by late 2027. The hosts note that Goldman itself acknowledged this understates the full picture, as the analysis only accounts for passenger car fleet additions and excludes two- and three-wheeler EVs, which account for 92% of total EV sales in India and 80% in Vietnam. The hosts credit Tesla's entry into China — particularly the Model 3 and Giga Shanghai — as a catalyst for China's EV transition, noting China is now at over 50% battery electric plus hybrid vehicle sales.

2. **LiDAR vs. Vision Debate:** Brian White briefly addresses the ongoing LiDAR vs. camera-only debate, asserting that Tesla without LiDAR performs substantially better than Waymo and other LiDAR-equipped competitors. He argues LiDAR is "not a silver bullet" and that Waymo and others have demonstrated as much in practice.
