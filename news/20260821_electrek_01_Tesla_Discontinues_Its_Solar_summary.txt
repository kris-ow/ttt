Electrek reports, per two sources close to the program, that Tesla has discontinued its Solar Roof tile product, telling third-party installers it will no longer supply solar roof tiles and will instead offer only conventional solar panels. Tesla's own website now redirects tesla.com/solarroof to its solar panels page, and "Solar Roof" has been removed from the Energy navigation menu.

## Energy
1. **Solar Roof discontinued:** Two sources told Electrek that Tesla has informed its certified installer network that solar roof tiles are no longer available to order and only conventional panels will be supplied going forward; one source said Tesla internally concluded the product is not financially viable. Tesla stopped reporting solar roof deployment figures in quarterly reports starting in early 2024, and the product had largely vanished from marketing before this formal wind-down.

2. **Origins tied to the SolarCity merger:** Elon Musk unveiled the Solar Roof in October 2016 shortly before Tesla shareholders approved its roughly $2.6 billion acquisition of SolarCity, a debt-laden solar installer where Musk was the largest shareholder and chairman and which was run by his cousins. The article states that shareholder litigation over the acquisition later revealed the tiles demonstrated at the 2016 event were not yet functioning solar products.

3. **Pricing problems and lawsuit:** Tesla originally marketed the Solar Roof with early quotes around $22 per square foot as cheaper than a new roof plus separate solar panels, but installed prices ended up far higher than quoted, including a 2021 price increase affecting customers who had already signed contracts. This led to a class-action lawsuit Tesla settled for $6 million in 2023; the article cites one customer whose contracted price reportedly rose from about $72,000 to roughly $146,000 before installation.

4. **Production shortfall versus Musk's targets:** Musk repeatedly said Tesla would produce and install 1,000 Solar Roofs per week, a target pinned to late 2019/first half of 2020; Tesla claimed it hit 1,000 units of weekly production in 2020, but actual installations lagged far behind. Wood Mackenzie data cited in the article put peak weekly installations at roughly 21–32 roofs — more than 95% short of the goal — and estimated Tesla installed only about 3,000 Solar Roof systems in the US over roughly seven years.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert writes that he was initially excited about the Solar Roof concept but views its failure as part of a pattern (comparing it to Full Self-Driving) of Tesla announcing a vision years before the product could deliver, with pricing swings that burned early customers. He argues the core economics never worked given manufacturing complexity, labor-intensive installation, and shading limitations, making it viable only for expensive homes with price-insensitive buyers; he notes the failure reflects on Tesla's specific product, not on solar panel economics generally, and mentions hearing unverified accounts of lengthy post-installation servicing delays.
