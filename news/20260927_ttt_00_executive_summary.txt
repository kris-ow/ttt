## Brief

- Cybercab registrations in Austin jump to 126, up from 58 a week prior
- Starship Flight 14 gets FAA approval, targets Monday launch
- Flight 13 nailed relight, V3 sat deploy, soft splashdown
- Grok 4.7 lands third in coding benchmarks behind Anthropic, OpenAI
- Colossus compute buildout could exceed 1.4M GPUs by year-end
- NHTSA rule adoption before December seen as key Cybercab catalyst

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab-specific registrations with the Texas DMV more than doubled in a week, rising to 126 from roughly 58, per Jo Bhakdi's Pioneer Lens tracker; Tesla separately notified Austin users the Cybercab fleet has doubled. Field-spotted deployed units sit at 47, which Bhakdi believes is undercounted by 10–30%.
- Bhakdi calls the pace of Cybercab deployment "a goldilocks moment," noting the September 1 launch is only 27 days old and already outpacing the early Model Y ramp.
- NHTSA is expected to adopt new rules (proposed in June) before December; Bhakdi argues Tesla would currently fail an audit under existing rules, making rule adoption — not the audit — the catalyst to watch. He no longer expects 10,000 units scaled this year due to audit risk.
- Bhakdi reframes his earlier 1,800–2,000 fully-commercial Cybercab figure as the last point analysts would be forced to price in robotaxi margins, not evidence Wall Street is currently ignoring the opportunity; he thinks the stock could move well before that threshold.
- Tesla appears to be centrally coordinating fleet ownership rather than selling Cybercabs freely to individuals, per a web form soliciting interest in "Cybercab fleet vehicle purchasing" and "mobility hubs and infrastructure," per Bhakdi.
- Robotaxi economics won't meaningfully cannibalize Tesla car sales until per-mile pricing drops well below $1, at which point cheaper-car manufacturers get hit before Tesla, per Bhakdi.
- California robotaxi expansion could unlock around mid-next year, per Musk as relayed by Bhakdi — the single biggest state opportunity given population covered.
- A separate speaker attributes long Cybercab wait times (~30 minutes) to fleet size relative to demand rather than pricing, arguing fleet availability — not fare cuts — is the key competitive lever.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 14 received FAA approval and targets a Monday launch as Starship's first revenue-generating mission, deploying 26 V3 Starlink satellites, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Flight 13 delivered multiple firsts in one mission: deployment of demonstration V3 satellite payloads, an in-flight Raptor relight, and the softest, most precise ocean splashdown of the second stage to date; the recovered stage was towed back to shore intact.
- A SpaceX-affiliated speaker says V3 satellites represent a 20x capacity improvement per launch versus V2 on Falcon 9, with each Starship launch (~60 satellites) delivering roughly 61 Tbps of downlink capacity — projecting 1.2 petabytes/year of capacity after 20 launches.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Grok 4.7 launched roughly two months late and landed third in agentic-coding benchmarks behind Claude and OpenAI, a comedown from Musk's earlier claim it would lead the field; Musk said the model may have been "penalized too harshly" in training and called the issue fixable within months.
- Musk says SpaceX's AI effort could take the overall lead in about six months if its improvement rate holds, and is "cautiously optimistic" about a Claude/GPT-6-level model in two to three months (Grok 4.8/4.9); Grok 5, billed as the first AGI model, has no updated timeline.
- Colossus 1 comprises 150,000 H100s, 50,000 H200s, and 30,000 GB200s; Colossus 2 comprises 110,000 GB200 and 440,000 GB300 chips, with further 220,000-GPU batches expected imminently, in November, and possibly December — a build-out that could exceed 1.4 million GPUs by year-end, per one host's estimate, making Colossus 2 potentially the world's largest AI data center at roughly a gigawatt.
- Anthropic and Google are paying SpaceX for Colossus compute — Anthropic a previously reported $1.25B/month through May 2029, Google roughly $920M/month.
- SpaceX plans dedicated orbital AI satellites ("AI 1") launching end of 2027, built at a new Bastrop, Texas factory; a separate "Starmind" compute satellite line (NVL72-class racks on Starlink V3 hardware, ~240kW power) is targeted for readiness around Q4 next year, per Bhakdi.
- One speaker argues space-based compute is nearing cost parity with terrestrial data centers, expects parity around 2029, and predicts space compute undercuts terrestrial by 2035 as terrestrial costs rise; cites avoidance of permitting and regulatory oversight as an immediate advantage.
- Grok integration in Tesla vehicles demoed via Grok Bot voice ordering during FSD driving, currently gated behind a $300/month Grok Super subscription with other tiers promised.

## Financials
- Bhakdi expects Tesla to beat softly on Q3 sales/margin trends but sees no material Cybercab cash-flow impact in the October earnings call, given the program only launched in September; he expects more transparency by January.

## Market & Competition
- Bhakdi is "fully loaded" on Tesla (entry ~$325) running short puts near $367–378 strikes, and says he'd be "very surprised" if Tesla doesn't trade above $500 this year, while flagging market-correction risk in October/November.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Bhakdi notes SpaceX's market cap is roughly double Tesla's despite SpaceX revenue being roughly 70% lower, making him tactically more bullish on Tesla near-term while still bullish on SpaceX over a one-year-plus horizon.
- A viewer's proposed 4.2 SpaceX-shares-per-Tesla-share merger ratio was raised but left unresolved; Bhakdi says Tesla would need a significant premium for any deal to make sense.

## Humanoid Bots
- Bhakdi predicts Tesla will internally test close to 10,000 Optimus V3 units over the next six months ahead of a commercial launch he guesses lands in Q2 or Q3 next year, without a major public reveal event.

## Autonomous Driving
