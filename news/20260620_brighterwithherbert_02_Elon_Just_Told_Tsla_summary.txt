This video from "brighterwithherbert" reviews 20+ business posts made by Elon Musk on X over a five-day window following SpaceX's IPO, framing each post through the lens of what it means for Tesla shareholders heading into the rest of 2026. ## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD destination parking post reframes the intervention mix:** On a Wednesday evening, Elon posted that upcoming FSD releases will remember parking preferences so the car navigates to the correct location at home, office, school drop-off, etc. He stated that destination parking is "by far" the biggest reason people currently intervene with FSD, and crucially added that "critical safety interventions are extremely rare." The host reads this as Elon's first clean public statement about the current intervention breakdown — the safety bucket is already very small, and the remaining interventions are largely convenience-driven. The host argues this is the exact metric regulators examine before unsupervised approval moves forward, and that the upcoming FSD release stripping out parking-preference interventions pushes the unsupervised timeline meaningfully forward — though the host declines to quantify exactly how much, noting Elon's dates tend to slip.

2. **Bear case caveat on intervention data:** The host flags that "critical safety interventions are extremely rare" is Elon's framing, not a regulator's. NHTSA holds the authority to define what counts as a critical intervention, and the timing of unsupervised approval remains uncertain regardless of Elon's characterization. The host treats this as a directional signal, not a confirmed regulatory milestone.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi rear-end confirmation from Elon:** During the IPO weekend, Sawyer Merritt posted that the only crashes involving Tesla's robotaxi fleet in recent months have been other vehicles rear-ending them. Elon replied with "indeed," publicly confirming the pattern. The host notes this mirrors the exact data point AV safety reports use to demonstrate the AV stack is safer than human drivers — if the robotaxi is never the at-fault vehicle, that supports the case for unsupervised expansion and also drives down insurance pricing over time. Since Tesla runs its own insurance through Tesla Insurance, lower insurance pricing feeds directly into robotaxi unit economics and, ultimately, the income statement.

2. **Combined FSD + safety data as unsupervised approval setup:** The host argues that both Tesla-specific posts together — parking interventions shrinking and the fleet getting rear-ended rather than crashing — constitute a meaningful setup for an unsupervised approval headline before year-end 2026. The host explicitly attributes this view to himself and not to any third party. He flags NHTSA's official statement on the rear-ended-only pattern as a key watch item: if the regulator confirms it on the record, the unsupervised approval timeline compresses quickly.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla sales increased after $7,500 EV tax credit removal:** In a Sunday post pushing back on a legacy media piece about government subsidies, Elon stated that when President Trump removed the $7,500 EV tax credit, Tesla sales actually increased because buyers shifted from other EV makers to Tesla. He confirmed the same point in a separate one-word reply ("true") to a post on X. The host reads this as a fundamental data point the bear case got wrong throughout 2025 — the narrative had been that removing the credit would hammer Tesla volumes, but the actual outcome was a share gain, because Tesla's unit economics are strong enough to absorb a $7,500 headwind that damages competitors' margins far more severely.

2. **Bear case on EV credit data:** The host acknowledges this is a short-window data point — one quarter of post-credit-removal share gain is a signal, but four quarters would constitute proof. He will continue watching whether the pattern holds over a full year of data.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX IPO at $2.1 trillion reprices Tesla's AI comps:** SpaceX closed its first day of trading at roughly $2.1 trillion, up approximately 19% from its IPO price in about 6.5 hours. Before the IPO, SpaceX was a private benchmark that analysts could dispute when valuing Tesla's AI and robotics businesses. Post-IPO, it is $2.1 trillion of public market validation. The host argues this directly benefits Tesla's valuation narrative because anyone pricing Tesla's AI, robotaxi, and Optimus businesses now has an undeniable public comp to reference.

2. **Elon's $1 trillion SpaceX revenue call for 2030:** Responding to Morgan Stanley's SpaceX revenue model (which estimated $330 billion for 2030), Elon posted that he thinks SpaceX might reach approximately $1 trillion in revenue in 2030 — roughly three times Morgan Stanley's number — and added seven minutes later that he would be "surprised if revenues not greater than $1 trillion in 2031." Starting from an $18.7 billion actual revenue base in 2025, the host calculates that reaching $1 trillion by 2030 implies roughly a 53x increase in five years. The host flags this as Elon's stated view, not a sell-side estimate, and notes Elon's timelines historically slip.

3. **30–50 trillion valuation thesis endorsed by Elon:** Elon engaged with an analyst post arguing SpaceX's real 5-year comp is $30–50 trillion, premised on Starship reducing cost per kilogram to orbit by 100x (not 20x), which historically has not grown existing markets but spawned entirely new industries (the analyst cited the internet, smartphones, and AI as examples of industries born from 100x compute cost reductions). Viable use cases listed included orbital data centers with continuous solar power and free cooling, microgravity semiconductor and bioprinting manufacturing, point-to-point Earth transit (Paris to Tokyo in 40 minutes), asteroid mining, and lunar tourism. The analyst framed SpaceX as "the toll booth on all of it — AWS but for civilization." Elon endorsed the post with "interesting analysis." Separately, he replied to a question about a $30 trillion valuation as "not impossible, but definitely requires factories on the moon and Mars." The host treats this as Elon signaling the $30–50 trillion comp is the one he wants in public circulation.

4. **XAI / Grok enterprise distribution via Databricks:** On Thursday, Elon posted that Grok models are now available on Databricks' AgentBricks platform, quoting XAI's announcement with the line "bring SpaceX AI's latest models to your enterprise data to power capable AI agents." The host notes Databricks already serves a large portion of the Fortune 1000 as an enterprise data lakehouse, making this XAI's biggest enterprise distribution move to date — compared to the playbook Anthropic ran with AWS Bedrock. The host also flags that the official XAI copy uses the branding "SpaceX AI's latest models," marking the first time the post-merger SpaceX/XAI combined entity branding appears in commercial marketing material (the merger closed in February 2026 per the host). Because Tesla holds an equity stake in XAI (board-approved investment made last year per the host), every commercial deal XAI lands causes Tesla's balance sheet line on that stake to be remarked higher.

5. **Elon's "revenue over benchmarks" framing for AI:** Two hours after the Databricks post, Elon commented on a thread comparing AI model usefulness, writing that Anthropic "has rightly focused on maximizing useful intelligence, which does not show up in benchmarks, but definitely shows up in revenue." The host interprets this as Elon publicly signaling that enterprise revenue — not benchmark leaderboard positioning — is the scorecard he is running XAI against, consistent with the Databricks distribution push.

6. **Space compute thesis and orbital data centers:** On the same Thursday, Elon replied to Brian Armstrong's post on orbital data centers stating it "was always going to be space compute" and that you "can scale a trillion times more than you could on Earth," citing continuous solar power, free cooling, and no earthbound permitting as advantages. The host frames SpaceX as uniquely positioned to execute on this given its launch capacity, with Tesla's XAI stake riding the same curve.

7. **Government subsidies rebuttal with Tesla data point:** Elon's post stated that adding up every government incentive his companies have ever received, the total amounts to less than 2% of the combined value of SpaceX and Tesla. With SpaceX at $2.1 trillion and Tesla's market cap added, the host notes this places the subsidy figure below 2% of something north of $3 trillion in combined value, making the subsidies line a journalistic framing tactic rather than a material fundamentals input.

8. **Starlink infrastructure compounding:** Three Starlink-related posts were flagged. Elon announced a Flow Cayman partnership providing backup connectivity to cell towers in the Cayman Islands, auto-switching to Starlink if fiber is cut during hurricanes — deploying ahead of peak hurricane season. The host draws a parallel to the American Airlines and Delta inflight Starlink deals, calling it the infrastructure becoming "a default." Elon also posted about Starlink connecting schools on a remote island off Haiti, and separately replied to a tweet noting SpaceX has now launched more satellites than the rest of humanity combined, adding that within 5 years SpaceX will probably have five times as many satellites as the rest of the world.

9. **SpaceX investor relations site launch:** On Monday, Elon posted that SpaceX's investor relations site went live at ir.space.com, including financial filings, leadership pages, committee composition, and future earnings reports. The host flags the cadence of disclosure on that site as the next signal for how post-IPO communication will run.
