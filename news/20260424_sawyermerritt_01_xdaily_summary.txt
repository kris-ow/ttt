## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Cybercab Production Ramp Visibly Underway at Giga Texas:** Tesla released a new video showing Cybercabs *without steering wheels* rolling off the production line and driving themselves autonomously to the outbound lot — one of the clearest public confirmations yet that Cybercab manufacturing is live. Separately, glossy gold Cybercabs were spotted out and about at Giga Texas, adding to earlier sightings of matte variants. The no-steering-wheel detail is significant as it confirms Tesla is building the vehicle in its final, fully driverless configuration rather than a transitional prototype form. These posts generated strong engagement (3.6K likes / 310 RTs for the production line video), suggesting viral reach on a meaningful milestone.

2. **FSD V14.3.2 Rolling Out — Key Behavioral and UX Improvements:** FSD V14.3.2 is now in early user hands. Sawyer Merritt's first-drive report highlights that "Mad Max" highway mode no longer hugs the far-left lane and stayed below 80 mph throughout, describing the behavior as "perfect." Actually Smart Summon saw meaningful speed improvements in initiating pull-forward and back-out maneuvers in parking lots. A new UX feature prompts drivers to select a reason when they disengage FSD, presenting a structured menu of options — a data-collection mechanism that could accelerate training feedback loops. These are qualitative improvements; no specific intervention rate or safety metric numbers were cited.

3. **FSD Fleet Miles Reach New Milestone — 28.8M Miles/Day:** Tesla updated its FSD miles tracker to reflect a larger vehicle fleet and increased FSD usage. The fleet is now averaging **28.8 million miles per day** on FSD (Supervised), up from **14.4 million miles per day** a couple of months ago — a **doubling** in roughly 60 days. This equates to approximately **333 miles every second**. This is a critical data point for both safety validation and the autonomous driving investment thesis, as fleet miles are the primary input for training and regulatory confidence.

4. **FSD Subscribers and Revenue: 456K Active Monthly Users, $45M+/Month:** Tesla disclosed that it now has **456,000 active monthly FSD subscribers**, generating over **$45 million per month** in subscription revenue, which Sawyer calculates annualizes to approximately **$565 million per year**. Additionally, Tesla ended Q1 2026 with its highest Q1 order backlog in over two years, signaling demand recovery. These figures provide direct high-frequency revenue visibility for the FSD monetization leg of the thesis ahead of broader robotaxi deployment.

5. **Tesla Acquires AI Hardware Company for Up to $2 Billion:** Tesla announced an agreement to acquire an AI hardware company for **up to $2 billion** in Tesla common stock and equity awards. Approximately **$1.8 billion** of that total is subject to service conditions and/or performance milestones tied to the success of the acquired business. The identity of the company was not specified in the posts. This is a significant strategic move suggesting Tesla is vertically integrating deeper into AI chip/hardware, likely to reduce dependence on third-party suppliers and accelerate compute for FSD and Optimus development.

6. **Optimus Factory Preparations Underway; Walter Isaacson Bullish on Tesla/SpaceX Merger:** Tesla stated in its earnings report that preparations for its **first large-scale Optimus factory** will begin in 2026. Walter Isaacson added public commentary that "millions of Optimus robots and self-driving cars" will be more transformative than LLMs. Isaacson also stated his belief that Tesla and SpaceX will eventually merge, citing the "Terafab" collaboration and Musk's practice of moving engineers between companies — though this remains speculative commentary from an outside observer rather than any official announcement.

7. **Giga Berlin to Add 1,000 Jobs, Target 20% Production Increase by Q3 2026:** Tesla announced it will create **1,000 new jobs** at Giga Berlin by the **end of June 2026** to increase weekly production by approximately **20%** starting from **Q3 2026**. The decision is described as a direct response to increased demand for the Model Y. This is a concrete near-term production ramp signal for the European market.

8. **Model S Signature Edition — Legacy and Product Update:** Hagerty published an exclusive look at the new **2026 Model S Signature Edition**, the final version of the Model S nameplate. Only **3% of parts** are shared with the original 2012 Model S Signature; the new version is **375 lbs lighter** and **40% more efficient**. While primarily a product/heritage story, it signals Tesla continues investing in its premium sedan line.

9. **Supercharger ML Model Update — 9M Miles of Training Data:** Tesla is rolling out an updated machine learning model to better identify vehicles that intend to charge, reducing wait times at Superchargers. The model is trained on **9 million miles** of aggregated and anonymized vehicle trajectory data within Supercharger geofences. This is an infrastructure efficiency improvement relevant to both the current EV business and future robotaxi fleet charging logistics.

10. **Honda Retreating; Gene Munster Defends Tesla's Capex Step-Up:** On the competitive landscape, Honda is exiting the South Korean car market after 23 years, adding to its struggles in EVs and China — framed as evidence of traditional OEM competitive deterioration. Gene Munster argued that Tesla's **25% capex step-up** is bullish, not bearish, as it widens the gap between Tesla and other automakers who are cutting EV and autonomy investment.
