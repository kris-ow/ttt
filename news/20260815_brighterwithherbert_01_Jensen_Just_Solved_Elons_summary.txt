This video features host Herbert interviewing financial analyst Cern Basher about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's proposal to finance AI compute buildouts using institutional capital (pension funds, insurance companies, private equity), and how this financing innovation could solve Tesla's roughly $119 billion Terafab chip factory funding challenge. The discussion covers Wall Street's growing role in financing SpaceX's and Tesla's AI data centers and robotaxi expansion, plus a detailed look at Tesla's Terafab job postings and the physical scale of the planned Texas facility.

## Financials
1. **Compute-as-asset-class financing model:** Basher explains Jensen Huang's thesis that AI compute is becoming a financeable, investable asset class like aircraft leasing or real estate — separating the owner of AI data center infrastructure from the company using the compute, similar to how airlines lease planes rather than own them. Huang's article reportedly cites over $500 billion of outside capital to start, with participation from firms including Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and KKR; Basher believes this figure could ultimately grow into the trillions.
2. **Terafab's estimated $119 billion cost and financing structure:** Basher's research (based on Tesla filings and job postings) estimates Terafab spans over 100 million square feet, requires more than 10 gigawatts of power (comparable to New York City's draw), and could cost around $119 billion for initial phases, with long-term expansion potentially exceeding that. He argues Wall Street financing would not necessarily grant investors equity or profit share in Terafab — deals could be structured like a mortgage with a steady return instead.
3. **Tesla's existing capex and debt plans:** Basher notes Tesla's CFO has said the company plans to raise capital spending to $25 billion and raise $30 billion in debt this year to fund robotaxi and Optimus, which Tesla is currently financing itself since robotaxi cash flow is not yet "predictable" enough for Wall Street to package into financial products the way AI compute now can be.
4. **Robotaxi as a future asset class:** Basher states he has previously written that robotaxi will become a new investable asset class similar to AI compute, once its cash flow becomes predictable enough — potentially financed via debt or structures like an infrastructure fund from a firm such as Blackstone.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX as a leading beneficiary of new compute financing:** Basher argues SpaceX, which he describes as currently the fastest builder of AI data centers with a payback period he says the company has stated is under a year, would be an attractive target for institutional capital under Huang's proposed financing model. He speculates SpaceX could contribute a portion of capital itself (e.g., an illustrative 25%) and raise the remainder from outside investors.
2. **Nvidia-SpaceX/Tesla relationship speculation:** Basher speculates, without confirming, that Nvidia may end up sharing in the revenue generated by Terafab or by SpaceX's data center leasing, suggesting a more intertwined partnership than currently publicly disclosed; he frames this explicitly as his own theory, not a confirmed fact.
3. **SpaceX's short-term compute contracts:** Basher notes SpaceX's current compute agreements reportedly include a 90-day out clause for both parties, which he contrasts with the longer-term "anchor tenant" commitments Wall Street would want before financing a large project.
4. **Cursor acquisition as unmodeled SpaceX upside:** Basher raises the idea (his own speculation) that if SpaceX's pending Cursor acquisition closes, Cursor's coding-focused LLM could compete with Anthropic (which he says makes roughly $100 billion a year) and Cursor (which he states made $8 billion), representing what he calls a significant unaccounted-for revenue opportunity for SpaceX that he has not yet modeled.
5. **Data center efficiency edge from vertical integration:** Basher argues SpaceX and Tesla's data centers (using Megapacks, solar, and Starlink connectivity) may command a premium over generic compute because of bundled advantages, similar to how Nvidia's CUDA software differentiates its chips.

## Robotaxi
1. **Terafab hiring underway in Austin:** Basher's research found Tesla is hiring for roughly 34-35 semiconductor manufacturing roles (34 in Austin, one in Palo Alto), spanning industrial hygienists, environmental engineers, equipment engineers, materials engineers using atomic-scale simulation, and transmission electron microscopy specialists — covering the full chip manufacturing process from factory construction to atomic-level chip fabrication. These roles are initially tied to a smaller "Terafab Advanced" facility already operating at Giga Texas, ahead of a larger planned facility in Grimes County (roughly two to two-and-a-half hours from Austin, per Basher).
2. **Liability and reputational risk as scaling gates for robotaxi:** Basher plans further research on how liability shifts from human driver to Tesla under full autonomy, arguing that even highly capable FSD could face outsized liability exposure at scale, and that reputational risk from poor pickup/drop-off experiences could also gate robotaxi scaling before Tesla can move from thousands to hundreds of thousands or millions of vehicles.
3. **Rationale for selling Cybercabs to fleet operators:** Basher argues Tesla may sell Cybercabs to third-party fleet managers/retail buyers partly to offload financing costs and partly for political reasons — building broad ownership among individuals and large financial players to build support for autonomous transportation's disruption of the driving industry.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Speculation on institutional investors' merger voting incentive:** Herbert speculates that large Tesla shareholders like BlackRock, which he says have been "loading up" on Tesla stock, could be incentivized to vote in favor of a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger in exchange for early access to fund robotaxi rollout or other financing opportunities — explicitly framed as speculation, not confirmed fact.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comparative Terafab scale claims:** Basher describes Terafab's projected footprint using illustrative comparisons — potentially three and a half times taller than the Burj Khalifa if stood on end, larger than Apple Park, the Pentagon, and Mall of America, with an exterior perimeter Basher estimates could reach 15-20+ miles, and one inch of rainfall on a 100-million-square-foot roof yielding roughly 60 million gallons of water (about 91 Olympic pools).
2. **Colossus workforce efficiency claim:** Basher cites reports that Elon Musk's Colossus data center site uses about 3,000 workers versus roughly 10,000 at comparably sized competitor data centers, suggesting Tesla/SpaceX builds faster with a third of the labor.
