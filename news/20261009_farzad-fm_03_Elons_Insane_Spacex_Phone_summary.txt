This video breaks down two pieces of SpaceX news from early October 2026 — FCC approval for 15,000 new direct-to-phone satellites and SpaceX's agreement to buy nationwide low-band (800 MHz) spectrum from Grain Management — arguing that together with SpaceX's prior mid-band spectrum deal, these moves complete the "puzzle" needed for Starlink Mobile to become a full replacement for traditional phone carriers, not just a rural/dead-zone backup.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **FCC approves 15,000 next-gen direct-to-phone satellites:** On October 6th, the FCC approved SpaceX's plan for 15,000 new satellites built to talk directly to regular phones; SpaceX says these next-generation satellites will have more than 100 times the bandwidth of its current generation, enabling a jump from basic texting to high-speed phone service.
2. **SpaceX agrees to buy nationwide low-band spectrum:** On October 8th, SpaceX agreed to buy up to 14 MHz of paired, nationwide 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management (price undisclosed); this deal still requires FCC approval, with the FCC's own target review window being roughly 180 days (~6 months). The FCC itself described the block as a valuable, underutilized source of paired low-band spectrum.
3. **Elon Musk calls it the "last piece" of the spectrum puzzle:** About 20 minutes after the spectrum deal announcement, Musk posted that this was "the last critical piece of the Spectrum puzzle needed for SpaceX to provide complete phone coverage in America," followed two minutes later by "Very big deal." SpaceX previously secured mid-band ("treble") spectrum from Echostar in a deal worth about $17 billion (announced September 2025), adding 65 MHz versus the roughly 5 MHz it uses currently; the FCC also granted SpaceX a waiver this week to use that Echostar spectrum for satellite-to-phone service without a carrier partner.
4. **Low-band spectrum solves building penetration, with limits:** Low-band (800 MHz) signals travel farther and penetrate walls better than mid-band but carry less data; AT&T CEO John Stankey said in September that low-band spectrum gives "decent penetration into some buildings" but won't "penetrate the skin of a high-rise," and satellites are a long way up, which is why SpaceX also plans to deploy the 800 MHz band on ground radios (towers, rooftops, structures) in addition to satellites. SpaceX says most existing mobile devices already support this band, meaning most users wouldn't need a new phone for this piece.
5. **Deployment timeline:** The 15,000 new satellites are not yet in orbit; the host notes SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell said in August that satellite launches would begin the following year with service starting by the end of the year after that, implying a target of roughly end-of-2027 for service. The host frames the usual pattern with Musk-affiliated companies as directionally correct but typically late on timelines, noting SpaceX's first direct-to-cell satellites launched January 2, 2024, with the first texts transmitted six days later.
6. **Competitive and market reaction:** In after-hours trading following the spectrum deal announcement (19 minutes after market close, after all three carrier stocks had closed up that day), AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile shares fell as much as roughly 5–8%. Shotwell said in August the three major carriers generate roughly $600 billion combined annually (their own 2025 reports reportedly totaling about $350 billion), and stated she expects SpaceX to "acquire quite a few" of their customers because the service will be better. On October 1st, AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile formed a joint venture to pool their own spectrum for satellite-to-phone service, seen as a response to SpaceX's moves.
7. **Current service and reach:** T-Mobile currently sells SpaceX's satellite-to-phone service as "T-Satellite" for $10/month, requiring a clear outdoor view of sky and running on about 5 MHz of spectrum through partner phone companies, per Shotwell. SpaceX says that as of January 2024, more than 20% of US land area had no ground-based cell service, and that during network outages more than 1.5 million people were able to communicate via its satellite service.
8. **Speculative next step — a SpaceX-made phone:** The host speculates that the remaining missing piece is SpaceX producing its own phone tied directly to Starlink, framing this as the endgame; this is the host's own forward-looking inference, not a confirmed SpaceX plan.
