The video features investor/entrepreneur Jo Bhakdi discussing venture capitalist Gavin Baker's comments (made on the All-In podcast) that Grok now rivals or beats Anthropic's Claude Opus/Fable 5 on a quality-vs-cost basis, and that investors are broadly ignoring Grok's value when assessing SpaceX. ## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok 4.6 leapfrogs on quality-vs-cost:** Gavin Baker showed a chart plotting AI model quality against cost (on Cursor Bench, per Databricks) where Grok 4.6 appeared ahead of Anthropic's Fable 5 (Claude), calling it "Pareto dominant on a lot of measures" and noting positive real-world reception from figures like David Heinemeier Hansson (37signals/Basecamp) and rising valuations at Databricks ($190B) and Mercor.
2. **Cursor acquisition and SpaceX personnel reshuffle cited as the turnaround driver:** Baker and Bhakdi attribute xAI's rapid competitive catch-up to Elon Musk buying Cursor (which Bhakdi calls "the most important acquisition since YouTube... probably even more important") and to bringing SpaceX executives into xAI's management roughly six months prior — combining SpaceX's infrastructure/compute strength with Cursor's product design and enterprise-sales strength.
3. **Grokbot framed as xAI's enterprise entry point:** Bhakdi argues Grokbot (built by the Cursor team) is xAI's critical move into the large "digital workforce"/enterprise AI market (referenced in a SpaceX deck as a $22 trillion opportunity), distinct from consumer Grok. Elon Musk reportedly stated publicly that Grokbot and Digital Optimus are not the same product, but Bhakdi disagrees strategically, viewing them as targeting the same market. Grokbot pricing was noted as expensive (not usable below $200/month), which Bhakdi called a coding/design choice for revenue, not something SpaceX/xAI necessarily plans to lower urgently.
4. **10-gigawatt-by-2027 compute target debated:** A third-party analysis (referred to as "Sam I analysis" / semi analysis) reportedly concluded Elon Musk's stated goal of reaching close to 10 gigawatts of compute by next year is "within reach" given identified deployment sites, calling Musk "singular" at achieving what doubters see as impossible. Bhakdi is skeptical this specific figure is realistic, modeling a bull case of 5 gigawatts by end of next year instead, viewing the 10GW figure as a stretch goal used to motivate internal teams; both speakers agreed even 5 gigawatts would be highly bullish, with Bhakdi's team calculating that figure at over $300 billion in potential data rental/leasing revenue (versus roughly $500 billion if 10 gigawatts were reached), which would be several times SpaceX's current ~$100 billion run rate reference point discussed in the conversation.
5. **Anthropic's rumored IPO valuation and revenue used as the benchmark for Grok's worth:** Anthropic is reportedly nearing a $2 trillion IPO valuation (rumor) on a rumored ~$65 billion revenue run rate; Baker suggested Anthropic is losing some market share to OpenAI, open source, and Grok despite still growing fast, and speculated a leak from OpenAI showing faster growth could follow amid IPO "gamesmanship." Bhakdi cautions this $2 trillion figure could just as easily be argued down toward "$200 million" if a "Micron-style" narrative takes hold (fast-follower/commoditization risk from cheaper competitors), and that this risk could also depress the valuation of SpaceX's AI vertical if Anthropic's story unravels. He states current enterprise AI revenue tied to Grok/Cursor is around $4 billion, and that proof of massive revenue growth (tens of trillions) is still needed before he would model it into a SpaceX valuation.
