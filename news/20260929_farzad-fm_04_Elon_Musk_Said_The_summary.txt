## SpaceX — Starship

He said he expects Starship to reach a weekly or twice-weekly launch cadence next year.
2. **Starship's role in space-based AI infrastructure:** Musk said Starship is intended to eventually launch substantial annual gigawatt capacity of AI computing into orbit (figures given inconsistently in the transcript, ranging from 200 to 300 gigawatts per year), which he said could represent roughly a 40% annual increase in total U.S. energy consumption if realized at the higher figure — a claim not further reconciled in the transcript.
3. **Long-term Moon/Mars ambitions:** Musk said Starship should eventually deliver enough payload to the Moon or Mars to build a self-sustaining city, estimating roughly a million tons of payload would need to reach the lunar surface for a self-growing city, with a similar rough figure for Mars.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Orbital data centers and solar economics:** Musk said SpaceX aims for very large annual solar production capacity (a specific gigawatt figure was cited alongside Tesla but not cleanly attributable) and argued that space-based solar computing could achieve far higher energy performance than ground-based solar (citing roughly a fifth-to-eighth of output on land, requiring large batteries), making orbital compute economically attractive despite launch costs. He referenced Google having launched (or preparing to launch) TPU-based compute into orbit, and noted an H100 chip was reportedly put into orbit previously.
2. **AI energy-to-GDP link (speaker's framework):** Musk proposed a rule of thumb that each 5 gigawatts of additional steady-state U.S. power generation equates to roughly a 1% increase in usable capacity and, he argued, translates to about a 1% increase in GDP, given rising "intelligence per watt." He estimated each gigawatt could generate roughly $40–50 billion in annual economic output — his own back-of-envelope calculation, not a sourced figure.
