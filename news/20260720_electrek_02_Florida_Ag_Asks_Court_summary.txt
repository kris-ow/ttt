Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier, joined by Alabama and Georgia, filed a July 9 amicus brief asking the 11th Circuit to throw out or reduce the $243 million verdict against Tesla in a fatal 2019 Autopilot crash case.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **The underlying verdict:** In August 2025, a Miami federal jury found Tesla 33% liable for a 2019 Key Largo crash in which a Model S on Autopilot ran a stop sign and flashing red light at roughly 62 mph, striking a parked Chevrolet Tahoe, killing 22-year-old Naibel Benavides Leon and severely injuring Dillon Angulo. The jury awarded roughly $43 million of about $129 million in compensatory damages against Tesla, plus $200 million in punitive damages assessed solely against Tesla. U.S. District Judge Beth Bloom upheld the verdict on February 20, and Tesla has appealed to the 11th Circuit (case No. 26-10858). In October 2025, the trial judge sanctioned Tesla for "willful" and "deliberate" violations after an independent researcher recovered crash data Tesla had told plaintiffs did not exist.

## Market & Competition
1. **States' legal argument:** Florida's brief, joined by Alabama and Georgia, asks the appeals court to reverse the judgment entirely or, alternatively, cut the $200 million punitive award to three times compensatory damages under Florida's statutory 3:1 punitive-damages cap, arguing excessive punitive damages deter beneficial innovation.
2. **Donation-timing questions around Uthmeier:** A Tampa Bay Times investigation (published July 7) reported that Uthmeier's fundraising committee took $25,000 donations from parties involved in civil litigation shortly before his office intervened on their behalf in multiple cases. Investigative journalist Jason Garcia (Seeking Rents) reported that roughly three weeks before the Tesla brief, Uthmeier's committee accepted $25,000 from a political committee tied to the Southern Group, a lobbying firm representing Tesla in Tallahassee that recently began lobbying on ADAS liability; Garcia noted this does not establish the donation was tied to Tesla specifically. Uthmeier, appointed (not elected) by Gov. Ron DeSantis, is now running for a full term and has raised over $10 million; DeSantis is described as a political ally of Elon Musk.
3. **Legislative backup — SB 1616:** Florida Senate Bill 1616, filed by Sen. Tom Leek, would have limited liability for advanced driving system manufacturers and restricted punitive damages to narrow circumstances, overlapping with arguments in Uthmeier's brief. The bill died in committee on March 13 but could be refiled next session; Leek is reportedly in line to become Senate president after the 2030 elections.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the verdict matters because it let a jury weigh Tesla's internal evidence and Autopilot marketing against actual system performance and assign punitive damages accordingly; he views efforts to cap punitives or pass SB 1616 as undermining that accountability mechanism, especially given the sanctions Tesla received for withholding crash data. He calls the donation timing "suggestive and nothing more" but frames it as part of a broader pattern of Uthmeier's office backing donors' civil disputes, now involving a politically connected company tied to Musk and DeSantis. He also notes Tesla is facing up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits and that NHTSA has escalated a visibility probe to an engineering analysis covering 3.2 million vehicles, one step from a forced FSD recall.
