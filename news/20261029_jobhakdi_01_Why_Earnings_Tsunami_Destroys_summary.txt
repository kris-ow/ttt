This livestream from Jo Bhakdi reacts to a broad tech-earnings selloff (Intel, Amazon, Google, AMD, Meta) and argues the market is in an irrational "nubble" (not-a-bubble) panic that is near exhaustion, while using SpaceX's post-earnings unlock-driven dip and Tesla's merger tailwind as the stream's core investment calls.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Stock swings tied to share unlocks, not fundamentals:** Bhakdi attributes SpaceX's recent volatility to share unlock mechanics rather than business news: after strong earnings (stock up ~10%) and a successful Starship flight, a large unlock sent roughly 1.3 billion shares into the market (with another ~300 million the prior Monday), driving the stock down about 9% and keeping it capped between roughly $145–$150. He cites Pioneer Lands' unlock tracker showing the October 22/24 unlocks exceeded 50% of total float, versus an August 20 unlock of 21% of float and an upcoming December 8 unlock he estimates at about 18% of float — meaningfully smaller. He predicts selling pressure will exhaust "early next week" (roughly Nov 2–3, 2026) once roughly 1 billion shares have changed hands, after which he expects a sharp rally back toward $170–180, and holds SpaceX as one of his two largest positions alongside Tesla.
2. **Revenue and valuation framework:** Bhakdi (not a company disclosure) estimates SpaceX revenue next year near $450 billion, against Elon Musk's own range of "$500 billion to a trillion," which Bhakdi calls optimistic. At $450B revenue he estimates at least $250B in earnings for the roughly $2 trillion market-cap company, and argues a 40–50x multiple on that would imply the stock price roughly tripling by end of 2027, driven partly by orbital/space AI compute he expects around 2028.
3. **Potential gigawatt-scale AI compute deals:** Bhakdi says that if SpaceX confirms reaching 3 gigawatts of AI compute capacity, it would be scaling from roughly $26 billion in run-rate revenue (on about half a gigawatt) to around $150 billion in run-rate revenue by year-end, driven by new Vera Rubin and B300 hardware — his own projection, not a confirmed company figure.
4. **Tesla–SpaceX merger seen as a Tesla catalyst:** Bhakdi argues that Elon Musk's confirmation that merger committees exist on both sides signals the market now expects the merger to proceed, which he believes will deliver Tesla a 30–40% valuation premium regardless of where SpaceX trades — a key reason he is most confident Tesla reaches all-time highs by year-end.

## Market & Competition
1. **Intel's 20% drop traced to a one-off fab incident:** Intel beat on revenue (~$16B vs. ~$16.5B estimate) and EPS ($0.52 vs. $0.40 expected), with data center revenue up 78% to $7.3B, but guided Q4 revenue to $15–16B (vs. $17B estimate) and EPS to $0.25 (vs. $0.43). The host attributes the weak guidance primarily to a fab explosion in Israel that shut the facility for two weeks, cutting roughly $300 million from Q4 revenue and about 200 basis points from gross margin — he calls this a one-off, company-specific event unrelated to AI or chip demand, and argues AMD's roughly 4% drop in sympathy (despite reporting no news itself) shows irrational, indiscriminate selling.
2. **Amazon's selloff called unjustified:** Amazon reported revenue of $201B (vs. ~$202B estimate, a roughly $300M/~0.1% miss) and EPS of $1.89 (vs. ~$1.96 expected, about a 3% miss), while AWS revenue rose 39% to $46B, beating estimates. Q4 guidance of $240–247B was below the ~$244B Wall Street estimate at the midpoint but Bhakdi notes the top end exceeds the estimate and still implies ~20% quarter-over-quarter growth, calling the "miss" narrative largely unjustified; the stock fell roughly 4% intraday (on Google-related contagion) before earnings and was down as much as 10% after hours before recovering.
3. **Host's "nubble" thesis — a market overreaction, not a bubble:** Bhakdi's core framing is that AI-adjacent stocks (Google, down as much as ~12% after earnings; Microsoft; Amazon; Intel; AMD) are being sold off irrationally despite strong fundamentals, a pattern he labels a "nubble" (not-a-bubble panic). He argues this is a seasonal, roughly two-to-three-week sentiment cycle he expected in October, now reaching exhaustion, with Meta's post-earnings selloff (from about $697 down to the $630–648 range, where he built a position) cited as an early example of bottoming and recovery.
4. **Portfolio positioning:** Bhakdi says his current holdings, in order, are Tesla and SpaceX (his two largest, roughly tied), followed by Nvidia (third largest), Meta, and Micron; he holds no position in Amazon, Google, or Intel, and says he is "roughly one-to-one" between Tesla and SpaceX exposure.
