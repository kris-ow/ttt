## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab production ramp underway:** Volume production officially began April 24, 2026, with the first unit off the line February 17. Per Herbert Ong, realistic 2026 production is estimated at 15,000–25,000 units, with 30–50% allocated to Tesla's own fleet — implying 5,000–12,000 robotaxi-dedicated Cybercabs.
- **Tesla 10,000+ unsupervised robotaxi forecast by December 2026:** Herbert Ong projects Tesla moving from fifth globally to first in unsupervised fleet by year-end, on the strength of Cybercab production, a 12-state rollout (Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas confirmed for H1), the Model Y AI4 software-upgrade path, and a 56% price advantage ($6.15 Tesla vs. $13.93 Waymo for the same Dallas trip).
- **Q2 earnings (late July) is the first public Cybercab production checkpoint.** Herbert Ong's threshold: below 1,000 units makes the 10,000 forecast too aggressive; above 5,000 is bullish.
- **Waymo year-end target is 3,500 vehicles — not 5,000.** Herbert Ong corrects his prior public statement: Waymo started 2025 with ~1,500 vehicles and announced adding 2,000, not reaching 5,000. Each vehicle costs ~$175,000 ($75K Jaguar I-Pace + ~$100K AV hardware) versus Cybercab's sub-$30,000 target — a ~6x per-vehicle cost gap.
- **China regulatory freeze locks out all domestic robotaxi operators.** A March 31 cloud-dispatch failure caused 100+ Apollo Go vehicles to simultaneously stall on elevated highways in Wuhan; regulators immediately halted all new robotaxi permits across China. Apollo Go, Pony AI, WeRide, and all other China operators cannot add vehicles or expand to new cities while the suspension holds. Tesla's operations are unaffected.
- **California will ticket autonomous vehicles directly for traffic violations beginning July 1, 2026**, including red-light running and failure to yield to pedestrians. This introduces a new compliance cost variable for Tesla's Bay Area fleet — the largest active market per RobotaxiTracker.com.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14.3.2 ships with redesigned disengagement popup:** The intervention window is now smaller, repositioned lower on screen, and non-dismissible until the driver selects an option — an iterative UX improvement with unchanged release notes. Active development cadence on V14.x continues.
- **Netherlands one-time FSD purchase discontinued May 15:** Tesla is ending the €7,500 perpetual FSD option in the Netherlands on May 15, 2026; only a €99/month subscription will remain. Customers have a two-week window to purchase outright.
- **FSD demonstrated on a non-Tesla vehicle:** A Sacramento shop owner retrofitted a 1966 Ford Mustang with Tesla's full camera array and FSD (Supervised), reportedly the first non-Tesla to run the software. The system functioned despite all cameras mounted at non-standard angles and heights — cited as evidence of the neural net's geometric adaptability.
- **HW3 legal exposure crystallizing:** Texas owner Ben Gawiser won a $10,672.88 default judgment against Tesla in small claims court after Tesla failed to deliver Level 5 FSD five years after purchase. Tesla missed the April 22 response deadline; Gawiser has filed a writ of execution. Musk's Q1 2026 earnings admission that HW3 cannot achieve full self-driving without yet-unbuilt factory hardware upgrades was cited directly in the filing. Broader class actions are active in the US, China, Australia, and Europe.
- **Europe FSD expansion:** Netherlands supervised FSD approved April 10, 2026; EU-wide approval targeted for summer 2026 per Herbert Ong.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Fremont Model S/X line shutting down for Optimus conversion in Q2 2026:** Per Cern Basher (Brilliant Advice), Tesla plans to take down the Model S/X production line at Fremont this quarter and convert it to a 1-million-unit-capacity Optimus line. Estimated conversion time: 40–60 days.
- **10-million-unit Optimus factory breaking ground in Texas:** Basher states foundations are being laid for a dedicated Optimus factory in Texas targeting 10 million units/year of capacity, with an estimated 12–15 months to completion. No other humanoid robot manufacturer has announced plans at remotely comparable scale — Figure is cited at ~55 robots/day as a startup-scale reference point.

## Energy

- **Megapack production reaches 200 units/week at Lathrop:** Tesla released factory footage confirming this throughput milestone at its California Megapack facility — a concrete data point for modeling Energy segment revenue and margin contribution.
- **Tesla Semi Basecharger launched with 2027 delivery:** Tesla introduced the Basecharger for overnight fleet charging of the Semi: 125 kW, adds up to 60% range in four hours. Pricing: $40,000 for two Basechargers; $188,000 for two Megacharger setups (up to 1,200 kW shared). Business customers pay $0.08/kWh at Tesla-operated revenue sites, including maintenance and service access. A Washington State rebate makes the Semi eligible for a ~$175,000 discount (~60% off).

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model 3 RWD launches in Canada at record-low $39,490 CAD (~$29,000 USD):** Sourced from Giga Shanghai under the January 2026 Carney-Beijing tariff deal (100% duty reduced to 6.1% on a 49,000-unit annual quota). Specs: 463 km range, 4.2s 0–100 km/h. First deliveries expected May–June 2026.
- **Model 3 Performance cut 17% to $74,990 CAD:** Down from $89,990; 478 km range, 3.1s 0–100. Long Range AWD removed from the Canadian lineup entirely (previously $79,990 CAD).
- **Shanghai-built units excluded from Canada's $5,000 EVAP rebate**, narrowing the effective price advantage against rebate-eligible competitors like the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Chevrolet Equinox EV.
- **~300,000 EV lease expirations in 2026, ~600,000 in 2027** could flood the used EV market and depress used prices, per a community EV cost-of-ownership presentation — a potential headwind for resale values but a tailwind for used EV adoption.
- **Nissan canceled a $500M US EV manufacturing plan**, reverting focus to gas trucks — a modest positive for Tesla's competitive position as a legacy OEM retreats.

## Financials

- **Tesla SEC filing discloses $143M in vehicle sales to SpaceX in 2025**, primarily Cybertrucks replacing SpaceX's gas-powered pickup fleet — a defined related-party revenue contribution for financial models.
- **Tesla's cumulative revenue-to-(capex+R&D) ratio: 6.2x** versus Microsoft 3.5x, Amazon 4.0x, Google 3.5x, per Cern Basher (Brilliant Advice). On a capex-only basis, Tesla's ratio reaches ~10x. Basher argues this positions Tesla uniquely as it enters a heavy capex cycle for robotaxi, Optimus, and AI.
- **Tesla capex plan exceeds $25 billion**, with stated willingness to spend more and access capital markets; robotaxi revenue expected to begin flowing in time to partially self-fund the broader buildout.

## Market & Competition

- **Global unsupervised robotaxi rankings as of May 2026:** (1) Waymo ~3,000, (2) Apollo Go 1,000+ in Wuhan alone, (3) Pony AI 1,400+, (4) WeRide 1,023, (5) Tesla 26 per RobotaxiTracker.com. China regulatory freeze creates an opening for Western operators.
- **Pony AI is the most credible near-term challenger:** Toyota partnership targets 1,000+ Gen 7 units in 2026; Q4 2025 robotaxi revenue +160% YoY, fare-charging revenue +500% YoY; unit economics reached breakeven in Guangzhou and Chengdu within four months of Gen 7 launch. Year-end fleet estimate: 2,400–3,000.
- **Wayve raises $1.5B Series D at $8.6B valuation** (backers: Uber, Microsoft, Nvidia, Mercedes, Stellantis, Nissan, Huawei) but has zero commercial robotaxi rides today — described by Herbert Ong as "a prototype story, not a fleet story" for 2026.
- **Zoox is federally capped at 2,500 vehicles/year** under its current exemption; operating in Las Vegas and San Francisco with ~2M autonomous miles and 350,000+ passengers to date, but structural ceiling limits competitive threat.
