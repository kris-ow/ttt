## Robotaxi
- **Global competitive ranking as of May 2026:** By unsupervised deployed fleet: Waymo ~3,000 vehicles (10 US cities, ~500K paid rides/week), Apollo Go 1,000+ in Wuhan across 26 cities, Pony AI 1,400+, WeRide 1,023 — Tesla currently fifth. Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla's unsupervised fleet stands at 26 vehicles across Austin, Dallas, and Houston.
- **Cybercab volume production officially began April 24, 2026** (first unit off the line February 17). Per Herbert Ong, realistic 2026 Cybercab production is 15,000–25,000 units, with 30–50% allocated to Tesla's own fleet — implying 5,000–12,000 robotaxi-dedicated vehicles. The first public Cybercab production number will appear at Q2 earnings in late July; below 1,000 units would make a 10,000-unit year-end forecast too aggressive, above 5,000 would be bullish.
- **Herbert Ong's year-end forecast: 10,000+ unsupervised Tesla robotaxis** across a dozen states by December 2026, with upside to 15,000–20,000 — roughly three times Waymo's confirmed year-end target of 3,500. The five cited structural drivers: Cybercab unit cost under $30,000 vs. Waymo's ~$175,000 per vehicle, Giga Texas production rate vs. Magna upfit shop at single digits per day, Tesla's 1M+ FSD supervised data flywheel, 12-state regulatory rollout (Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas confirmed for H1), and 80,000+ Supercharger + 1,300 service location infrastructure.
- **Tesla fare documented at $6.15 in Dallas vs. $13.93 for the same Waymo trip** — a 56% discount — per a ride documented by "Merrit."
- **California will issue traffic violation tickets directly to autonomous vehicles starting July 1, 2026**, covering infractions such as running red lights or failing to yield. This introduces a new compliance and operational cost variable for Tesla's Bay Area fleet, the largest active market.
- **Apollo Go cloud failure on March 31 triggered a China-wide regulatory freeze on new robotaxi permits.** More than 100 vehicles simultaneously stalled — some on elevated highways — due to a centralized dispatch system failure. Chinese regulators halted all new robotaxi permits across every operator (Apollo Go, Pony AI, WeRide). Tesla is unaffected, as FSD does not yet operate in China, and its local AI4 chip processing architecture reduces the centralized single-point-of-failure risk that caused the incident.
- **Waymo's year-end 2026 fleet target is 3,500 vehicles, not 5,000** — Herbert Ong corrects his prior public statement, clarifying Waymo announced adding ~2,000 vehicles but started from ~1,500 in early 2025. Waymo is targeting 1 million weekly rides by year-end and is expanding to 11+ new cities including Denver, Las Vegas, Nashville, and London (first international city).
- **Pony AI reached unit economics breakeven in Guangzhou and Chengdu within four months of its Gen 7 launch.** Q4 2025 robotaxi revenue grew 160% YoY; fare-charging revenue grew 500% YoY. The Toyota partnership targets 1,000+ Gen 7 units in 2026 — cited as the most credible OEM production partnership in the industry.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Fremont Model S/X production line to be taken down in Q2 2026** and converted to an Optimus production line with capacity for 1 million units annually. Per Cern Basher, conversion is estimated to take 40–60 days.
- **Ground has been broken and foundations are being laid for a 10 million unit/year Optimus factory in Texas.** Basher estimates 12–15 months to completion, framing active construction as evidence Tesla has reached production-readiness confidence for Optimus. No other humanoid manufacturer has announced plans at comparable scale; Figure is cited at approximately 55 robots/day for context.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD V14.3.2 includes a redesigned disengagement popup:** moved lower on screen, smaller, improved readability, and non-dismissible until the driver selects an option. An iterative UX update rather than a capability milestone, but signals continued active V14.x development cadence.
- **Netherlands approved supervised FSD on April 10, 2026**; Tesla is ending the one-time FSD purchase option there on May 15 (€7,500), after which only the €99/month subscription will be available. Europe-wide approval is targeted for summer 2026, with a second major-country approval (Germany, France, or Italy) identified as the key trigger.
- **A Texas owner won a $10,672.88 small claims default judgment against Tesla** after the company failed to deliver the Level 5 FSD capability he paid $10,000 for in August 2021. Tesla missed both the original hearing and a subsequent April 22 deadline, filing only a procedural extension request with no substantive defense. The owner cited Musk's Q1 2026 earnings admission that HW3 vehicles cannot achieve full self-driving without hardware upgrades requiring new factories as direct evidence against Tesla's defense. A writ of execution has been filed, authorizing Texas law enforcement to seize Tesla property.
- **A Sacramento builder retrofitted a 1966 Ford Mustang with a full Tesla camera array and Model 3 drivetrain**, achieving a working FSD (Supervised) installation — reportedly the first non-Tesla vehicle to run FSD. The system functioned despite all cameras being mounted at non-standard angles and heights, cited as evidence of the neural network's geometric adaptability and potential portability to other vehicle platforms.
- **Cern Basher argues FSD advertising has a faster and more trackable ROI than factory capex**, given Tesla's own Q1 2026 earnings statement that FSD has become a demand driver for vehicle sales. He estimates near-immediate returns at approximately $7,000 vehicle margin per incremental sale, contrasting with multi-year capex paybacks. The average American is described as having no awareness of what FSD is.

## Energy
- **Tesla's Lathrop Megapack factory is now producing 200 units/week**, per a factory video released by Tesla. This is a concrete throughput milestone for modeling Energy segment revenue and margin contribution.
- **Tesla launched the "Basecharger" for overnight Semi fleet charging:** 125 kW, adds up to 60% range in four hours, deliveries beginning early 2027. Business pricing: $40,000 for two Basechargers; $188,000 for two Megacharger setups (up to 1,200 kW shared, 180–1,000 VDC output range). Tesla will charge commercial customers $0.08/kWh at revenue-generating sites, inclusive of annual preventative maintenance and scheduled service visits.
- **Washington State offers a $175,000 rebate on the Tesla Semi** — approximately 60% off — materially improving fleet economics for buyers in that state.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla launched the Model 3 Premium RWD in Canada at $39,490 CAD (~$29,000 USD)**, a record-low Canadian price enabled by a January 2026 tariff deal reducing Canada's duty on Chinese EVs from 100% to 6.1%. The vehicle offers 463 km of range and a 4.2-second 0–100 km/h time; first deliveries expected May or June 2026. Tesla simultaneously cut the Model 3 Performance 17% to $74,990 CAD and removed the Long Range AWD from the Canadian lineup entirely.
- **Shanghai-built vehicles do not qualify for Canada's $5,000 EVAP rebate**, which requires manufacturing in a free-trade-agreement country — partially offsetting the price advantage versus rebate-eligible competitors like the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Chevrolet Equinox EV.
- **~300,000 EV leases expire in 2026, ~600,000 in 2027**, per a community EV ownership presentation. Many lessees are not expected to exercise buyout options, pointing to a coming wave of off-lease used EVs that could push used EV prices below equivalent used gas cars.
- **Nissan canceled a $500 million U.S. EV manufacturing plan**, reverting focus to gas-powered trucks — a modest competitive tailwind for Tesla as a key legacy automaker retreats from EV investment.

## Financials
- **Tesla's cumulative revenue/(capex + R&D) ratio is 6.2x** per Cern Basher's analysis — versus Microsoft at 3.5x, Amazon at 4.0x, and Google at 3.5x. On a capex-only basis, Tesla's ratio rises to approximately 10x. Basher frames this as Tesla being the most capital-efficient manufacturer in America, positioning it well entering a heavy capex cycle for robotaxi, Optimus, and AI infrastructure.
- **Tesla's capex plan exceeds $25 billion**, with stated willingness to spend more and access capital markets. Basher highlights Tesla's expectation that robotaxi revenue will begin flowing in time to partially self-fund the broader robotaxi buildout.
- **Tesla disclosed a $143 million related-party vehicle sale to SpaceX in 2025** via SEC filing, primarily Cybertrucks replacing SpaceX's gas-powered pickup fleet.

## Market & Competition
- **Cern Basher positions Tesla as a physical AI company competing against the Mag 7**, not a car company competing with Ford and GM. His central argument: Tesla's "machine that builds the machine" manufacturing moat is harder to replicate than hyperscaler AI models or data centers. Microsoft and Amazon capex are described as escalating toward $150B+ and $200B+/year respectively, with their cumulative revenue/capex ratios declining.
- **WeRide secured a Lenovo deal for 200,000 autonomous vehicles globally over five years** and plans 1,200 robotaxis with Uber across the Middle East by 2027. Its Geely partnership targets 2,000 purpose-built GXR robotaxi vehicles by year-end. WeRide remains LiDAR/HD-map dependent and its China operations are subject to the permit freeze.
- **Wayve raised a $1.5 billion Series D at an $8.6 billion valuation** with backers including Uber, Microsoft, Nvidia, Mercedes-Benz, Stellantis, and Nissan. It has zero commercial robotaxi rides today and is described as a "prototype story, not a fleet story" for 2026.
