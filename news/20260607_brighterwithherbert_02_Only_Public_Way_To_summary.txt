This video from brighterwithherbert introduces Robo Strategy (ticker: BOTB on NASDAQ), a newly public closed-end fund designed to give retail investors access to private humanoid robotics companies like Figure AI and Apptronik. The host argues that the intelligent robot market represents one of the largest economic shifts in history — bigger than the PC or the internet — and that the value creation is happening almost entirely in private markets inaccessible to ordinary investors. Robo Strategy is presented as a MicroStrategy-style vehicle that bridges that gap.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **The Scale of the Robotics Opportunity:** The host frames physical AI and humanoid robots as a generational economic shift, citing a global labor market of approximately $60 trillion (derived from labor being ~52% of global GDP, with global GDP running around $118 trillion in the prior year). A projected global shortage of 85 million skilled workers by 2030 is cited as a structural tailwind. Industry forecasts place the robotics market at up to $218 billion by 2030 and past $370 billion by 2034, though the host believes these figures understate the ultimate opportunity.

2. **Figure AI's Growth as the Central Case Study:** Figure AI is highlighted as the clearest example of private market value creation being locked away from public investors. It raised at a $2.6 billion valuation in early 2024 and by its Series C in the fall of 2025 was valued at $39 billion — approximately 15x growth in under two years. The host notes that essentially none of that appreciation was accessible to a normal public investor. Figure has also signed a commercial deal with Catalyst Brands (parent of J.C. Penney, Aéropostale, and Brooks Brothers) to deploy humanoid robots at operations in Reno, Nevada, and its production line is reportedly building a robot roughly every 90 minutes.

3. **Apptronik's Momentum:** Apptronik is cited as another private company with significant momentum, having attracted investment from Google, Mercedes, and others. Its valuation is noted to have tripled to over $5 billion in the current year (2026).

4. **Robo Strategy's Portfolio:** The fund's holdings span multiple categories of robotics: Figure AI and Apptronik in humanoids, plus Dino Robotics, Standard Bots, Dexmate, Cocoa Robotics, Path Robotics, and others covering robotic arms, mobile manipulation, and compute infrastructure.

5. **Dr. Scott Walter as Due Diligence Lead:** The host emphasizes the credibility of Dr. Scott Walter — described as a 40-year veteran in humanoid robotics and a recurring guest on the channel — who recently joined Robo Strategy as Robotics Research Diligence Director (internally nicknamed "R2-D2"). The host argues that having a hands-on engineer with decades of experience vetting deals provides a meaningful edge in distinguishing real technology from hype-driven demos.

## Market & Competition

1. **The Private Market Access Problem:** The host identifies a structural problem: the most exciting robotics companies are staying private far longer than in prior technology cycles because abundant private capital means they don't need to go public. He draws parallels to OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX — companies with enormous valuations where the public largely watched from the sidelines. Andrew Kang, the fund's founder, estimates the current private robotics market at roughly $100–$150 billion, with the potential to become a tens-of-trillions industry.

2. **The MicroStrategy Playbook Applied to Robotics:** Robo Strategy explicitly models itself on MicroStrategy's approach to Bitcoin — a publicly traded company that raises capital markets money and deploys it into an asset class (in this case, equity in private robotics firms). The fund is structured as a closed-end fund listed on NASDAQ under ticker BOTB, which went public on May 11, 2026. The permanent capital structure (no fixed fund life) is highlighted as a key advantage over traditional VC funds, allowing indefinitely long holding periods suited to an early-stage industry.

3. **The NAV Premium Flywheel and Its Risks:** The host explains the mechanics in plain terms: when the stock trades at a premium to net asset value (NAV), the fund can issue new shares at inflated prices, use the proceeds to buy more private robotics assets, and incrementally increase NAV per share — a self-reinforcing flywheel. He cites MicroStrategy as precedent, noting it traded at nearly 4x its Bitcoin value at its late-2024 peak before cooling to approximately 1.2x by 2026. The key risk is premium collapse: if sentiment shifts, the fund could trade at a discount to NAV, making share issuance dilutive rather than accretive. Additional risks include the difficulty of valuing and liquidating private holdings, and the possibility that humanoid robot timelines slip relative to current hype.

4. **Capital Raise and Competitive Positioning:** Shortly after listing, Robo Strategy signed a committed equity facility of up to $2 billion with Roth Principal Investments, providing a standing mechanism to issue new shares and deploy capital into future private robotics rounds. The host notes that Andrew Kang's early $19 million investment in Figure AI (made roughly two years ago) and his early entry into Apptronik established a track record, and that Robo Strategy is now described as leading rounds and competing successfully against top-tier venture firms for allocations.

5. **Tesla and Optimus as the Public Baseline:** The host positions Tesla as his single largest holding and acknowledges Optimus as potentially the most valuable thing Tesla ever builds. However, he argues that at over $1 trillion in market cap, Tesla gives only partial, diluted exposure to robotics, making concentrated robotics vehicles like Robo Strategy a complementary rather than redundant holding.
