Tesla has begun circulating a "Robotaxi interest form" seeking fleet buyers, infrastructure partners, and event collaborators for its Robotaxi network, timed to a low-key Cybercab launch event that had no livestream or media access.

## Robotaxi
1. **New interest form targets fleet buyers, not individual owners:** The form, titled "Help Us Build Our Robotaxi Network," collects name, email, phone, and company name, with checkboxes (only one selectable) for Cybercab fleet vehicle purchasing, mobility hubs/infrastructure, event collaboration, or other. The article notes it authorizes Tesla to send marketing about products, services, and regional events.
2. **Cybercab launch lacked typical Tesla hype:** No livestream was provided and media was turned away; the author relied on reports from Tesla fans posting on X. Open questions the article flags but cannot answer include where/when Cybercab will roll out, whether it retains a steering wheel, and how many units will actually appear on roads beyond launch day.
3. **Tesla appears to have scaled back infrastructure ambitions:** Tesla had planned to lease a San Francisco parking garage to build a robo-charging hub for both human-driven and Robotaxi use, but abruptly dropped the plan the same day a planning meeting was scheduled, with no further updates since. The article notes a UK company, Hubber, founded by former Tesla employees, is pursuing similar urban robotaxi charging hubs.

## Autonomous Driving
4. **FSD owners still cannot monetize their purchase:** The article states Tesla sold FSD to owners for up to $15,000 with promises their cars would become "appreciating assets" usable for passive income once autonomy was solved, and that Musk once said Tesla would stop selling cars to individuals once autonomy was achieved in favor of a fleet-owned model. Despite Tesla operating paid robotaxi rides for over a year, the article asserts no private owner has yet been able to profit from FSD as promised. It also notes Tesla has not provided some owners the hardware upgrades needed to run current software, which they already paid for, citing one owner who performed the upgrade himself and called it "easy."

## Editorial Stance
The author frames the fleet-buyer interest form as a betrayal of individual FSD purchasers who funded Tesla's autonomy development over a decade without receiving the promised capability, and is skeptical that the form signals imminent progress given Tesla's history of delayed promises, suggesting it may be more about expanding Tesla's marketing contact list amid slower EV sales.
