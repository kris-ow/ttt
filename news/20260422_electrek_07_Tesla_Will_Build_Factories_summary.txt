Tesla CEO Elon Musk confirmed on the Q1 2026 earnings call that Hardware 3 (HW3) vehicles are incapable of unsupervised FSD, and proposed building urban "microfactories" to retrofit millions of affected cars with HW4 computers and cameras — a potentially costly undertaking for a company already under financial pressure.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Musk confirms HW3 cannot achieve unsupervised FSD:** On the quarterly earnings call, Musk stated HW3 has only 1/8th the memory bandwidth of HW4, and that memory bandwidth is the key bottleneck for unsupervised FSD and autoregressive transformer AI workloads. He said Tesla previously believed HW3 would be sufficient but acknowledged it is not.

2. **HW3 FSD software update still coming:** Tesla said it will offer a version of V14 FSD for HW3 vehicles in June 2026, consistent with its practice of delivering delayed, lower-performing software versions to older hardware.

3. **Precedent of broken hardware promises:** Tesla sold HW2 vehicles with FSD promises, then offered free HW3 upgrades when HW2 proved incapable. The same cycle is now repeating with HW3 → HW4. The article notes Tesla is also developing chips beyond HW4, raising the question of whether HW4 will itself prove sufficient for full Level 5 autonomy.

## Robotaxi
1. **HW4 upgrade tied to robotaxi fleet eligibility:** Musk stated that converting HW3 cars to HW4 is necessary for those vehicles to enter the robotaxi fleet and operate with unsupervised FSD. Tesla is offering HW3 owners a discounted trade-in toward HW4-equipped vehicles, or the option to retrofit their existing car (requiring both a new computer and new cameras).

2. **Microfactory proposal to scale retrofits:** Musk said service centers are too slow and inefficient for the volume of upgrades required, necessitating dedicated small-scale production lines in major metropolitan areas. No timeline, locations, or cost figures were provided.

## Financials
1. **Tesla flagged negative free cash flow for 2026:** Tesla stated it will not be cash flow positive for the rest of the year as it increases capital expenditures amid the broader AI spending race. Net income has been falling for years and the article notes this quarter's results were partially supported by one-time benefits, raising investor concerns about the added cost of building urban retrofit factories.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues the microfactory plan may be a stalling tactic rather than a genuine commitment — designed to encourage HW3 owners to trade in their vehicles voluntarily, reducing the pool of cars Tesla must eventually retrofit. The author also contends the recurring hardware promise failures stem from Tesla's own decision to make premature autonomy claims dating back to 2016.
