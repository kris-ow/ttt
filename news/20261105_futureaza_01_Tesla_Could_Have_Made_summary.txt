This video (a discussion between the channel host and a guest named Herbert) explores why Tesla, despite building substantial AI data center capacity, never entered the business of leasing out surplus compute power the way SpaceX/xAI has — reportedly generating large profits by renting capacity to companies like Anthropic. The hosts debate several explanations: Tesla needs all its compute for FSD and Optimus training now, leasing-as-a-business carries uncertain long-term economics, and Elon Musk's stated long-term view is that Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI will eventually merge into one company anyway, making internal compute allocation decisions less consequential.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX/xAI compute-leasing profits:** The host claims SpaceX makes large profits leasing surplus AI computing power to competitors and AI companies such as Anthropic, which he says rack up substantial monthly bills — though no specific dollar figures are given for this claim.
2. **Colossus build speed:** The guest states xAI built its Colossus 1 cluster in about 4 months, citing this as evidence of Tesla/Musk-affiliated teams' data-center building speed and expertise.
3. **Data center market bottlenecks:** The host argues that despite overwhelming demand for compute, data center operators broadly (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and others) are constrained by power availability and hardware component shortages (e.g., cooling/blade components), with some projects facing waits described as around 18 months; firms reliant solely on leasing revenue reportedly struggle to raise financing (bonds) without diversified income streams — a dynamic the host attributes to a comment from Elon Musk.

## Financials
1. **Why Tesla didn't build a leasing business:** The guest (Herbert) argues Tesla's compute is earmarked for its own highest-ROI use cases — FSD and robotics — making leasing it out premature; he also suggests leasing-as-a-business was viewed internally as unproven/risky, comparing it to compute-sellers like CoreWeave that also face financing difficulties despite the AI boom.
2. **Tesla's investment in xAI was "minuscule":** The guest says Tesla pushed internally (including at the board level) for Tesla to invest more heavily in xAI early on, when xAI/SpaceX AI was valued around $5 billion, but the amount ultimately invested was very small; he says Tesla still profited from the stake it holds, but believes it should have owned more.
3. **Dojo chip development abandoned:** The guest mentions Tesla had been developing the Dojo chip (including a "Dojo2") but shut the program down; he says the experience may have contributed to Tesla's caution about large AI infrastructure bets, though he personally disagrees that this was the deciding factor. He adds that lessons from Dojo fed into the development of Terafab.
4. **Rationale for the xAI spinoff:** The guest explains Elon Musk created xAI as a separate entity specifically to offer equity/stock-option incentives needed to recruit top AI talent competing for "superintelligence" development — talent that would have required paying billions in salaries to retain within Tesla, similar to approaches used by Google and Meta.
5. **Tesla-SpaceX-xAI merger context:** The guest states that in Musk's view, Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI are intended to eventually become one company, and that this anticipated merger is why Tesla has held back from independently building out a leasing-focused AI data center business; he notes the merger "has not materialized" and says it has come "too late" relative to when such consolidation might have been more advantageous.
6. **Cross-company chip/employee movement:** The guest confirms Elon Musk has moved surplus chips and possibly personnel between Tesla and xAI as needed, citing an instance where Tesla had surplus chips that were shifted to xAI use roughly six months prior to the discussion.

## Humanoid Bots / Autonomous Driving
