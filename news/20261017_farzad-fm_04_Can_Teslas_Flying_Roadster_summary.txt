This short video analyzes the physics behind Tesla's claimed flying/hovering Roadster concept, using compressed-air thrust figures to assess whether and for how long the vehicle could actually fly. The core thesis is that while the thrust claims are physically sufficient to lift the car, the compressed-air fuel system limits flight to just seconds at a time, with slow recharging as the main bottleneck.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Thrust vs. weight claims support brief hovering:** The video states Tesla claims the car weighs 1,700 kg (about 17 kN of weight) while its engines can produce over 80 kN of thrust, meaning upward thrust substantially exceeds the weight needed to hover — the speaker concludes traction/lift is not the limiting factor.
2. **Compressed air, not traditional fuel, powers the thrust:** The system is described as using air stored in carbon tanks (250 kg), exhausted at a claimed speed of 700 m/s, rather than a conventional propulsion fuel.
3. **Flight duration is extremely short:** A full "volley" of compressed air is exhausted in about 2 seconds, with a stated maximum hover/flight time of around 10 seconds before the tank is depleted.
4. **Refueling is slow and is the key constraint:** Recharging the air tank currently takes roughly 2 to 6 hours, with an unnamed speaker expressing hope to reduce this to about half an hour; the video attributes the slowness to the energy cost of compression — even a perfect compressor would need around 30 kWh to compress the air charge, equivalent to about 160 km of range in a regular electric car.

- Claimed vehicle weight: 1,700 kg (~17 kN)
