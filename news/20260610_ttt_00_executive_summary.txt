## Autonomous Driving

- **Denmark becomes the fourth European FSD approval.** The Danish Road Traffic Authority concluded FSD "will contribute positively to road safety." Countries now approved: Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, and Estonia. FSD (Supervised) is available in 12 countries/territories globally (~6% of all countries).

- **Belgium publicly signaled it expects to be next**, per Herbert/Bhakdi. Jo Bhakdi describes a predictable domino effect among smaller, more agile European nations — citing Denmark, the Netherlands, and Estonia as the "smartest" early movers — while expecting the UK, France, and Germany to lag. An EU-wide vote covering ~28 member countries could occur by end of summer 2026, though individual countries can proceed independently.

- **Netherlands fleet report — first European real-world FSD safety dataset.** Tesla released data covering two months of operation: 3.5x fewer collisions overall; 3.4x safer on highways with 16.6 million km and zero collisions; 14.9x fewer automatic emergency braking events; 8.8x less harsh acceleration; 7.3x less harsh braking; 8x fewer hard swerves. Bhakdi noted European roads are narrower and slower than U.S. highways, making the results strong validation regardless.

- **US Trade Representative factory visit scheduled.** USTR Jamieson Greer is set to tour Tesla's Fremont factory on approximately June 12, framed around domestic onshoring — a positive optics signal for Tesla's relationship with the current administration amid ongoing trade and tariff discussions.

## Robotaxi

- **Texas DMV Level 4 registrations reach 59 vehicles**, with 8 added in a single day per publicly accessible DMV data tracked by community member Spencer. This is a concrete, publicly verifiable signal of fleet buildout pace.

- **Arizona operating plan lists nine cities for SAE Level 4 autonomy**, per a Tesla first-responder interaction plan appendix dated April 2026. Most observers had anticipated only two cities (Phoenix and Tempe), making this a materially larger expansion than the public had assumed.

- **Cybercabs spotted in Houston fleet lots and Seattle/Bellevue area.** Drone footage by SE Robinson showed a mix of Model Y robotaxi vehicles and Cybercabs in Houston. Friends of Herbert in Washington State have been spotting Cybercabs in Seattle and Bellevue, consistent with Tesla's previously announced hiring activity in Bellevue.

- **Bhakdi's minimum viable fleet density thesis: 300–500 Cybercabs per city** before credible commercial service is possible. He cites Austin users currently experiencing 20-minute wait times and frequent car pullbacks as evidence the density problem is real. This supports a "large simultaneous launch" thesis over a gradual drip.

- **Cybercab manufacturing pace framed as the primary launch signal.** Bhakdi argues production scale creates inherent pressure to deploy commercially — Tesla cannot allow thousands of units to sit idle. He noted Cybercabs in a Dallas lot with wheel caps installed, reading this as a signal of imminent operations rather than pure testing.

- **Bhakdi's stock repricing framework:** ~24 Cybercabs deployed → ~10–15% stock gain toward $490; ~100 vehicles → next meaningful catalyst; 1,000–2,000 vehicles → actual market repricing of Tesla's valuation. He argues an exponential fleet trajectory — not parity with Waymo's ~3,700 vehicles — is what triggers analyst repricing.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Semi enters mass production at a new Nevada facility as of April 2026**, offered in standard-range (~325 miles fully loaded) and long-range (~500 miles fully loaded) variants, both rated to 82,000 lbs maximum legal weight. Megacharger delivers ~60% range in ~30 minutes.

- **PepsiCo real-world Semi data: 1.7 kWh/mile** with 65% of miles driven above 70,000 lbs of load across ~50 trucks in California. Energy cost comes to approximately 25 cents/mile vs. 80–90 cents/mile for diesel at ~$5.60/gallon — a ~65–70% fuel cost reduction. Maintenance estimated at ~6 cents/mile vs. ~19–20 cents for diesel (~70% lower).

- **Tesla Semi is currently the only U.S. Class 8 electric semi below the ~$300,000 fleet break-even threshold**, per FutureAzA host Brian, framing it as a critical competitive moat. Competitor Horizon Motors debuted a new Class 8 electric tractor at ACT Expo with a conventional two-seat cab, but disclosed no efficiency specs.

- **U.S. EV sales rebounded to 85,000+ units in May 2026** — the best month since federal tax credits ended at close of Q3 2025. Average EV transaction price fell to $54,532, down 4% year-over-year (11th consecutive YoY decline). Automakers spent ~$7,600/vehicle on incentives (~14% of ATP), nearly double the industry average.

- **Tesla holds ~50% of U.S. EV market share.** Average Tesla transaction price fell 1% from April and 3.4% YoY. Model 3 ATP: $49,082; Model Y ATP: $51,537. These two models represent 96% of Tesla's May sales, and Tesla's pricing exerts disproportionate downward pressure on industry-wide EV averages.

## Market & Competition

- **Cybercab is 3.4x more energy-efficient than Waymo's I-Pace:** Cybercab operates at 165 Wh/mile (~204 MPGe) vs. Waymo's 562 Wh/mile (~60 MPGe). Herbert argues Tesla would need roughly one-third as many vehicles as Waymo to deliver equivalent service coverage.

- **Waymo acquired Apple's former Arizona self-driving proving ground for $220 million.** The 5,500-acre facility includes a 115-acre city course, 35-acre vehicle dynamics area, 4-mile oval track, and freeway course. Apple had reportedly spent ~$10 billion over a decade on its canceled self-driving program. Bhakdi's interpretation: a company with genuine full unsupervised autonomy across 3,700 real-world vehicles would not need a private proving ground at this scale — suggesting Waymo may be transitioning from LiDAR to a vision-only neural network stack.

- **Bhakdi's Waymo remote-monitoring hypothesis.** He argues Waymo's commercial operations may be supported by extensive remote human oversight, which he frames as structurally impossible for Tesla's FSD given millions of private vehicles in uncontrolled real-world conditions. He cites Waymo's reported ~$100/ride loss as consistent with a heavily labor-subsidized model.

- **California launches $1 billion EV truck incentive program** with rebates from $7,500 to $120,000 covering drayage trucks, semis, box trucks, and delivery vans.

- **Canada established a quota of 49,000 annual Chinese EV import slots**, split into two half-year batches. Effective tariff under quota: 6.1% vs. 100% otherwise. Permits are valid for 60 days maximum, limiting speculative imports.
