## Autonomous Driving
- FSD (Supervised) crossed 12 billion cumulative miles, up from 11 billion on June 9 — roughly a billion miles in five weeks, per Sawyer Merritt.
- A Hardware 3 2020 Model 3 with ~180,000 miles completed a roughly 4,255-mile Key West-to-Washington-state drive on early FSD 14 "light" with no steering, brake, or pedal inputs, per brighterwithherbert; the run is part of a longer cross-country trip, about 60% complete with ~9,000 miles remaining.
- Community member David Moss's intervention-free FSD streak reached ~13,000 miles, part of ~25,000 miles across two streaks at ~99.9% FSD usage, per brighterwithherbert.
- Multiple user videos highlighted FSD handling edge cases: nighttime deer avoidance at ~47 mph, interpreting a police officer's subtle hand signal, backing up to accommodate a long turning truck (FSD 14.3.4), and navigating a blocked-road standoff with a tractor, cyclist, and non-yielding oncoming car — all per brighterwithherbert's roundup of creator videos.
- A Tesla-produced video features a 93-year-old woman whose son says FSD restored her driving independence across San Diego County.

## Robotaxi
- Sawyer Merritt flagged Tesla Robotaxi entering "another new market," though no city or fleet figures were given, making it a directional signal rather than a hard data point.
- Cybercab in-house production cost is estimated at $17,000–$20,000 per unit, which will act as a cash-flow drag for one to three years before the robotaxi flywheel turns cash-flow positive, per Larry Goldberg (futureaza).

## Financials
- Tesla published Q2 2026 sell-side consensus: ~$27.6B revenue and ~$1.3B net income, setting the bar ahead of earnings.
- Larry Goldberg projects Tesla's 2026 cash capex will run closer to $40 billion versus the previously guided $25 billion, driven by Terafab construction, potential space-based data center involvement, robotaxi rollout, and the Optimus ramp.
- With cash on hand near $40 billion against ~$40 billion in projected investment, Goldberg estimates Tesla may need to raise capital — potentially a ~$10 billion bond — in Q1 of next year to maintain a $12–18 billion cash balance.
- Goldberg attributes Tesla's cash discipline to flat management structure and low overhead, contrasting it with Lucid, whose lifetime revenue (~$1.6 billion) he says is dwarfed by a single reported ~$5 billion European Megapack deal.

## Electric Vehicles
- Goldberg cited Tesla's in-house lithium refining plant as an example of selective vertical integration — even at half capacity it supplies only part of Tesla's lithium needs but gives Tesla supplier leverage and process insight.

## Market & Competition
- A Plug In America survey (~4,205 respondents) found 89.8% of EV drivers would choose an EV again, only 0.5% would return to gas, and Tesla owner loyalty was 96.6%; the host argued Tesla is likely under-represented in the sample despite Model Y being the most-cited primary vehicle.
- Survey data showed Tesla Supercharger users reported non-functional chargers ~20-21% of the time versus ~52% for other networks, though the host questioned whether the Tesla figure was too high given his own road-trip experience.
- Goldberg contrasted Tesla's calibrated vertical integration (in-house software, selective battery production) with historical over-integration failures like Ford's rubber plantation venture, calling Tesla's approach best-in-class.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's 13th test flight was scrubbed and is now targeted for Monday, July 20 at 6:45 PM ET, per Sawyer Merritt.
- A separate Starship test attempt aborted mid-flight the prior day, reportedly moving the stock down about $5 (~2.5%); the host framed the abort as a strong engineering result, noting onboard systems detected an anomaly and shut down all 33 engines within milliseconds. Per Elon Musk (relayed by the host), another launch attempt could potentially come as soon as the following Monday, though the host was skeptical of that timing.
- Sawyer Merritt pushed back on investors selling SpaceX (SPCX) shares over the scrub and delay, saying they "probably shouldn't have been in the stock in the first place."

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Analyst Brian (brighterwithherbert) models SpaceX/xAI's AI-compute leasing business at ~$47 billion in 2026 revenue, exiting near a $72 billion annualized run rate (up to $82 billion with more deals), with existing Google, Anthropic, and Reflection AI deals already totaling roughly $1.4 billion/month combined plus Google ramping toward ~$1 billion/month by October.
- Citing Epic AI aerial-photo analysis, Brian estimates Colossus 2 has grown to roughly 440,000 working chips (~330,000 B300s, ~110,000 B200s) as of July 1, up sharply from ~190,000–250,000 a few months earlier.
- Brian's own unconfirmed estimate puts Colossus 2's mobile turbine power at ~1.65 gigawatts, potentially reaching ~2 gigawatts within a month or two, with a further speculative 6 additional gigawatts over three years from Caterpillar/Solar Turbines and other sources.
- Brian projects Q4 2026 consolidated SpaceX revenue of ~$18.4 billion in the base case — comparable to Tesla's quarterly revenue — with operating income modeled at ~$4.4 billion (range $2.3B–$6.8B).
- Grok/X-related AI revenue is already running near $4 billion/year annualized and, per Brian, is on track to double roughly every six months.
