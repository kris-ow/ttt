Jo Bhakdi walks through a Financial Times report that Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to negotiate an all-stock merger, aiming to close before the end of 2026. He argues that the deciding factor is not the bankers' valuation work but the price Tesla shareholders need to vote yes, which he puts at roughly $500 per share if TSLA sits below $420. He also argues that merger expectations explain both Tesla's recent outperformance and SpaceX's share-price weakness, and that the SpaceX discount is overdone given its revenue trajectory.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **FT report on merger committees:** According to the Financial Times, as summarized by an X account the host cites, five independent directors are leading the special committees. On the SpaceX side they are Roelof Botha and Randy Glein. On the Tesla side they are Chair Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs advise SpaceX, and Evercore advises Tesla, with the banks modeling overlaps and the exchange ratio. The report cites a combination value of about $3.5 trillion, which the host says is simply the two companies' current combined value. An insider says the sides are "not close yet," though intensive work keeps a year-end close possible. The deal is expected to face regulatory scrutiny over SpaceX's US defense work and Tesla's China business. The host believes this is manageable.

2. **Voting threshold is the real hurdle:** Approval requires more than 50% of all outstanding Tesla shares, and abstentions count as no. A separate special meeting is expected after Tesla's December 3 annual meeting. The host stresses that with perhaps 80% turnout, a large majority of those voting must say yes. In his view, shareholder satisfaction with the price matters more than any valuation method the banks use.

3. **The host's premium framework:** Bhakdi says the premium must sit within a band:
   - **Ceiling:** no more than about 50% over Tesla's price at the time of the offer. Above that, SpaceX minority holders could sue over dilution, citing Musk's conflict of interest.
   - **Floor:** significantly above 30% while TSLA is 10% or more below its all-time high. He uses roughly $420 as his marker.
   - **Applied:** at a TSLA price of $380, he estimates the minimum winning bid is $500, about a 32% premium.
   - **If TSLA rallies:** at around $500, a premium of roughly 10–20% could suffice. He gives $550 as an example.

   He says the offer should be set as a USD amount per Tesla share and paid in SpaceX shares at consummation. He describes the $500 figure as where shareholder sentiment on X and in his own conversations is converging.

4. **Merger expectations drive relative performance:** The host believes Tesla's outperformance of its Nasdaq beta reflects an anticipated premium. He cites a roughly 12% rally on Monday (November 23). He believes SpaceX's weakness reflects that premium being deducted from it, alongside a broad sell-off in neo-cloud stocks. He puts SpaceX's fair value at $170–180 versus about $130 now, and frames the gap as a roughly 30% merger headwind. A 30% cut from $170–180 actually yields about $119–126.

## Financials
1. **Bullish SpaceX trajectory, per the host:**
   - **Near term:** he expects SpaceX to reach a $120B revenue run rate by December 31.
   - **End of 2027:** he expects a $500B run rate at a 50%+ profit margin. That implies $250B in earnings, which at a 40x P/E (his floor) gives a $10T market cap, about $650 per share and 400% upside.
   - **Merger discount:** applying a 30% merger discount, he arrives at $400. However, 30% off $650 is about $455.
   - **Conservative case:** adding a further 30% risk discount (for example, the 10 GW build-out slipping) takes him to $280. He says this means SpaceX roughly doubles within 12 months even in the most adverse case.

   He expects Q4 earnings in January to validate this trajectory and the stock to re-rate by the end of January at the latest.

2. **Tesla outlook:** The host expects TSLA to move above $400 quickly on the merger premium plus the Cybercab ramp. He expects Tesla earnings to grow much faster than over the past five years, driven by Cybercab in 2027, and exponentially in 2028.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab as a bargaining chip:** Bhakdi argues Tesla shareholders would reject a lowball offer, such as $390, because Tesla is "at the inflection point of scaling Cybercab." A Cybercab rollout plus a market turnaround could push TSLA to $500 by Christmas, which would change the premium math.
