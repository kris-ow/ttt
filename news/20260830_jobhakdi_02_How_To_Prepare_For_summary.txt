## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab launch set for Thursday:** The host frames this week as the launch of full-scale commercial Cybercab passenger service in Austin, Texas, expected to expand to Dallas and Houston, occurring Thursday September 3rd.
2. **Ashok Elluswamy comments on scale:** Ashok Elluswamy (referred to as head of AI/Cybercab/robotaxi) reportedly posted on August 29th that "the street won't be the same anymore"; in a related exchange, a poster asked if Tesla is "about to flood Austin," and Elon Musk replied "yes" — the host is uncertain whether this is meaningful ("empty talk" or "full talk") and flags it as an open question.
3. **Job postings signal a broad rollout:** The host cites a wave of Tesla job listings in Austin — Robotaxi fleet supervisors, night/morning/afternoon shift support specialists, process engineers, and robotaxi operations advisors — as evidence Tesla is preparing a large-scale launch beyond just Austin.
4. **Driverless Cybercab footage circulating:** The host references a video, described as recorded the day before, showing a driverless Cybercab (no one in the vehicle) driving through Austin, and a separate viewer report of a Cybercab sighting in Denver.
5. **Expectations reset low, creating asymmetric setup:** The host's central thesis is that market expectations for Thursday's launch have been driven to near zero or negative, making a large downside surprise unlikely; he estimates roughly 50% odds the launch is essentially "nothing" (e.g., ~10 Cybercabs), with the other 50% skewed toward positive surprise (faster scaling/momentum).
6. **Cybercab count as key catalyst going forward:** The host argues the real trading opportunity comes after the launch, as the fleet scales from a small initial number into the hundreds and then thousands; he cites a specific view that 100–200 Cybercabs added over a month could push the stock back into the mid-$400s, and that scaling into the thousands with clear momentum could support well above $500.
7. **City coverage estimate:** In audience discussion, the host estimates Tesla robotaxi (Cybercab and other) service may already span roughly 10 cities, including Dallas, Houston, Austin, several Florida cities, and locations in California, though he says he isn't certain of the exact count.
8. **Model Y robotaxi role framed as training, not long-term fleet:** A viewer comment stated Model Y robotaxis will be mostly phased out and are designed to help train unsupervised FSD for consumer cars; the host responded with interest but did not independently confirm this.

## Financials
1. **Robotaxi division alone valued far above current stock price:** The host cites his own financial model isolating the robotaxi/Cybercab business, which he says produces a present value of $440 per share for that segment alone (conservative, risk-discounted), excluding Tesla's core auto business, Optimus, and Terafab — implying the ~$348 closing price (Friday) undervalues the company even before counting those other segments.
2. **Long-term price target pushed back ~18 months:** The host says his prior prediction of Tesla reaching $10,000/share by 2030 has been delayed; his current December 2030 present-value/fair-value estimate is roughly $4,500, with $10,000 now expected roughly 18–24 months later than originally modeled (original call was from late 2024/early 2025).
3. **Illustrative earnings scaling math:** The host offers rough illustrative math that roughly a million Cybercabs in operation could generate on the order of $50 billion in earnings (versus current earnings under $5 billion), which he says would bring Tesla's P/E down to around 30 at that scale — explicitly framed as rough/illustrative rather than a forecast.
4. **Stock context and options positioning:** TSLA closed around $348 the prior Friday after a roughly flat week; the host notes Tesla remains about 22% down on the year and has not closed the gap from a sharp drop following earnings roughly five weeks earlier. He describes running high synthetic leverage via short puts, plans to reduce exposure ahead of Tuesday's Fed risk, and states his personal cost basis is around $350.
