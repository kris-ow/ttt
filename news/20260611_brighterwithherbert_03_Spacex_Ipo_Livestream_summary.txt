This video is a live stream from the channel "brighterwithherbert" covering the SpaceX IPO on its first day of public trading. The hosts and guests — including Phil Beisel, Alexandra Merz, Aaron Bernett (Mach 33), Brian Wang, John Gibbs, and Scott (reporting live from Times Square near the NASDAQ tower) — watch and comment as SpaceX begins trading under the ticker SPCX. The core discussion centers on SpaceX's valuation, its AI compute and orbital data center ambitions, the XAI acquisition, Starship milestones, and speculation about a future Tesla–SpaceX merger.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO opens at $150/share:** After a delay of roughly two and a half hours from market open, SpaceX began trading on the NASDAQ. The IPO price had been set at $135 per share, and the stock opened at $150 — approximately 11% above the IPO price. Within minutes of opening it was trading in the $158–$164 range. Phil Beisel had predicted the day prior that it would open at $167, hit an intraday high of $194, and close at $173. The whisper number circulating on CNBC beforehand was $175. The IPO was reported to be five times oversubscribed.

2. **Largest IPO by initial market cap:** Participants discussed whether SpaceX's opening valuation exceeded that of Saudi Aramco's IPO. One panelist noted that Aramco was valued at approximately $1.7 trillion at IPO and traded up to $1.88 trillion, while SpaceX at $161/share was pushing past $2 trillion, making it likely the largest IPO by market capitalization at open.

3. **Retail allocation challenges across brokerages:** Allocation percentages varied widely by platform. Reported figures included: 3% (E*Trade, Phil Beisel), 6% (Scott), 18–28% (John Gibbs), 28% (John Gibbs confirmed), 40% (some international/Australian brokers), 60% (Fidelity, for a family member). Robin Hood and Schwab were cited as relative letdowns. Schwab was noted to be staggering its allocations throughout the morning, with a reaffirmation window that had closed at 7:00 a.m. that day.

4. **SpaceX launched Falcon 9 on IPO morning:** Gwen Shotwell confirmed in her IPO speech that SpaceX launched Falcon 9 with Starlink satellites on the morning of the IPO. Scott on the ground noted SpaceX had completed four launches in the week leading up to the IPO — Sunday from Vandenberg, Monday from the Cape, Tuesday from Vandenberg, and Wednesday morning from the Cape — all with successful booster landings. Shotwell remarked: "What company would do such a thing on the day that they open in the public market?"

5. **Gwen Shotwell's IPO speech highlights:** Shotwell noted SpaceX is 24 years old, founded by Elon Musk in 2002. She cited milestones including first liquid-fueled private rocket to orbit (2008, six weeks after a Falcon 1 failure), Falcon 9 achieving orbit, crewed ISS missions, 165 Falcon 9 launches in the prior year, and Starship development. She stated that following the XAI acquisition, SpaceX as a group now owns "the largest coherent gigawatt-class compute on the planet." She also noted that over half of SpaceX's approximately 22,000 employees bought additional stock in the IPO, totaling almost $1 billion.

6. **SpaceX–Tesla merger speculation:** Alexandra Merz played a clip of Gwen Shotwell being asked about the widely discussed possibility of a SpaceX–Tesla merger. Shotwell said the combination "might make Elon's life a little easier," acknowledged clear synergies and convergence between the two companies, but said she is currently focused on SpaceX's core operations. Dan Ives of Wedbush was cited as putting an 80%-plus probability on a SpaceX–Tesla merger occurring in 2027. One panelist predicted the SPCX ticker would change to X upon acquiring Tesla.

7. **Aaron Bernett (Mach 33) valuation framework:** Bernett described Mach 33's SpaceX model as organized around three revenue lines: Starlink, AI compute, and launch. His published valuation range showed a conservative median of $1.7 (per share or per a normalized unit — not fully specified) and an aggressive median of $4.2, with the recommendation to treat it as "a bond stapled to a call option." Elon Musk had responded to one of Bernett's published models saying SpaceX would "fall far short" of its own goals if Bernett's numbers were the target, which Bernett interpreted as a timing comment rather than a disagreement on the long-term trajectory. Bernett recommended a 5–10–15 year hold and dollar-cost averaging.

8. **Starship milestones and orbital data center timeline:** Bernett relayed details from Gwen Shotwell's interview with Morgan Brennan, noting Shotwell gave unusually definitive timelines: Flight 14 is targeting orbit, Flight 15 is expected to establish a monthly cadence, and Shotwell said it is "very likely" Starship reaches orbit by end of the current year. One launch may originate from Cape Canaveral. To reach the first gigawatt of orbital AI compute, Bernett estimated 100–200 Starship launches would be needed, at roughly 5–10 megawatts per launch and 50 satellites per launch. Elon Musk had stated a goal of one gigawatt in space by 2027, which Bernett considered achievable by 2028–2029 more realistically, noting the use of off-the-shelf Nvidia GPUs (referred to as Rubin/Reuben chips) would pull that timeline forward.

9. **XAI and terrestrial AI compute position:** Brian Wang argued that XAI, now part of SpaceX, is already the number-two AI cloud provider with approximately $26 billion in annualized revenue — nearly double Amazon's approximately $15 billion AI cloud ARR — while Microsoft leads at approximately $37 billion. He argued SpaceX could reach $50–60 billion ARR by fully monetizing existing GPU capacity. He described a third XAI data center location (referred to as "Macrohard," believed to be in Tennessee or Mississippi) that reportedly has capacity for up to 2 gigawatts and has approximately $2.5–2.8 billion in mobile natural gas turbines on order. Wang also discussed how Megapack and solar assets give SpaceX/XAI a grid flexibility advantage — by accepting interruptible power during peak hours (6–9 p.m.), they can secure cheaper and faster grid interconnects.

10. **Elon Musk net worth and trillionaire narrative:** Several panelists noted that if the SpaceX stock rises just 2–3% above the $135 IPO price, Elon Musk's combined holdings across all his companies would make him a trillionaire on paper. Alexandra Merz said she was preparing for "net worth porn" questions from media, intending to reframe the narrative around the employees, families, and investors who have benefited from his companies.
