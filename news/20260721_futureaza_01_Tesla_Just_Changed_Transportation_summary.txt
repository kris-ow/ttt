This video features futureaza host Brian in discussion with Tesla commentator Randy Kirk, debating why Tesla appears to have built hundreds to over a thousand Cybercabs but has not yet rolled them out widely, concluding the delay is most likely deployment logistics rather than safety or technical issues.

## Robotaxi
1. **Large Cybercab production numbers observed but unexplained delay:** Drone footage (from Joe Tegtmeyer, clipped by Sawyer Merritt) reportedly showed hundreds of Cybercabs sitting in an outbound logistics lot; Randy Kirk estimates the built fleet is likely around a thousand or more, though he stresses this is speculation with no hard data. Both hosts push back on a report (attributed to an unnamed writer) claiming Tesla has bet its entire company fortune on the robotaxi program, calling it inaccurate.
2. **Kirk's logistics-pacing theory for the rollout:** Kirk argues the holdup isn't safety or fear of city-wide traffic stalls (the kind he says have dogged Waymo), but rather Tesla testing how fast it can deploy cars into a new city without idle vehicles hurting public perception. He cites a recent batch of 55 vehicles dispatched at once as a likely test of how quickly riders adopt the app and use the cars, informing future scaling decisions for hundreds or thousands of units.
3. **Confidence Cybercab will work if Model Y robotaxi already does:** Both hosts argue that because unsupervised Model Y robotaxis are already operating, and the Cybercab is smaller in all dimensions, there's very high confidence (Kirk estimates well under a 0.001% chance) it will fail to work similarly. They note current robotaxi fleets include supervised cars in the Bay Area and unsupervised cars elsewhere, with NHTSA data reportedly showing the vehicles operating without at-fault incidents.
4. **Debate over remote-operator staffing ratios:** The hosts discuss the ratio of remote supervisors to deployed robotaxis as a key scaling variable. One argument raised is that Tesla should overstaff remote operators now even if the ratio moves from 1:1 toward more cars per operator, since scaling the fleet will require more, not fewer, human overseers in the near term. Kirk personally believes far fewer support staff will ultimately be needed per vehicle (he speculates ratios could reach 10-to-1 or even 100-to-1) for logistics like cleaning, charging, and repairs, though a human will likely always be needed for emergency response situations.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Camera-based "computed LiDAR" via stereoscopic triangulation:** Responding to a video allegedly showing Waymo vehicles approaching a burning car too closely (LiDAR reportedly struggling to detect flame, which lacks solid reflective properties), the hosts discuss a third-party analysis (possibly by a commentator referred to as "Tesla Ming," per Kirk, unconfirmed) arguing that Tesla's multi-camera array can triangulate distance similarly to LiDAR via stereoscopic vision. Brian and Kirk agree this is plausible — comparing it to human binocular vision — while Kirk notes he's not technically expert enough to confirm the claim.
