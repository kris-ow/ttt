This video is a recording of SpaceX's Q3 2026 earnings presentation, featuring remarks from Elon Musk, President Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Brett (last name not given), covering financial results, Starship/Starlink progress, the super-intelligence compute buildout, and an analyst Q&A touching on chip fabrication plans and a possible SpaceX–Tesla merger.

## Financials
1. **Q3 revenue and profitability surge:** SpaceX reported Q3 2026 revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% year-over-year from $5.6 billion; net income of $4.4 billion, an improvement of $5.5 billion from a prior-year net loss of $1.1 billion; and adjusted EBITDA of $8.8 billion, up 306% from $2.2 billion.
2. **Segment breakdown — Connectivity and SI:** Connectivity (Starlink) revenue was $5.2 billion, up 69% year-over-year and 22% sequentially. The super intelligence (SI) compute segment was the standout, with revenue rising to $7.6 billion — up $5.0 billion sequentially and $6.7 billion year-over-year — driven by cloud services agreements ramping.
3. **Capex and backlog:** Total company capex in Q3 was $19.6 billion, primarily for SI compute buildout, Starship infrastructure, and satellite production. Backlog stood at $63 billion as of September 30, including $17.5 billion recognized as deferred revenue; roughly $42 billion of backlog is expected to be recognized within the next year. The company reiterated its goal of $100 billion in annualized run-rate revenue (based on December's run rate) by year-end.
4. **Compute pricing economics:** The CFO said new compute deployments are currently generating payback in under a year, with pricing tracking slightly above the high end of a previously discussed $30–$50 per watt range.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 reaches orbit, Flight 15 to attempt full recovery:** Starship Flight 14 reached orbit for the first time and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites. Flight 15, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and ship — which Musk said would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle if successful; he called it "more likely than not" to succeed, with FAA engagement expected to support a return-to-launch-site and tower-catch license for both stages.
2. **Louisiana Spaceport and flight cadence ramp:** SpaceX announced development of a Louisiana Spaceport, which Musk said could eventually have on the order of 20+ launch towers and support transport of millions of tons per year to orbit. Starship cadence is targeted to reach weekly flights in the first half of next year, rising to twice-weekly or more by the end of next year as reusability improves.
3. **Q3 launch cadence:** The quarter included 35 total launches: 21 Starlink Falcon 9 flights, two Starship flights, a Falcon Heavy mission (NASA's Roman Space Telescope), and 12 other customer missions including six national security launches. In early October, SpaceX flew three Falcon missions across three launch sites within 13 hours, including Crew 13 to the ISS, a Falcon Heavy NRO mission, and a rideshare mission carrying 130 payloads.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and bandwidth growth:** SpaceX reported 13.6 million Starlink consumer subscribers (one figure given as "just under 14 million"), adding 1.6 million in Q3, plus hundreds of thousands of additional enterprise and aviation subscribers. Starlink Mobile surpassed 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter.
2. **Long-term bandwidth ambitions:** Musk said Flight 14 alone deployed Starlink bandwidth equivalent to 10 Falcon 9 launches while only about half-loaded. He projected that within roughly three years, Starlink could have about 100x its current bandwidth via an order-of-magnitude more satellites (potentially on the order of 100,000) each with 10x+ the bandwidth of today's satellites, alongside direct-to-cell and terrestrial network buildout. He further speculated that in 3–5 years, bandwidth demand could be a thousand times current levels, driven by AI/SI agents.
3. **Plans for refueling and next-gen satellites:** SpaceX will attempt on-orbit refueling early next year and plans to begin flying mobile V2 and Starlink satellites in 2027, with spectrum integration targeted for early 2028.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Massive compute buildout underway:** SpaceX ended Q3 with 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate compute capacity, with additional capacity already online in early October. Musk said the company is on pace to bring roughly 200,000 GPUs online per month for the rest of this year, continuing into next year with next-generation compute.
2. **2027 compute targets:** Musk said he is "fairly confident" of adding 10 GW of incremental compute in 2027 (on top of roughly 2.5–3 GW online by end of this year), implying a total of at least 13 GW by end of next year. He described a (unspecified, proprietary) plan that could add up to 20 GW of incremental power/cooling infrastructure next year, putting total power potentially around 23 GW — but called this "difficult but not impossible" and said he does not expect "everything to go right."
3. **Casting bottleneck as competitive moat:** Musk said turbine blade and vane casting is currently 12–18 months behind the rest of turbine production industry-wide, and claimed this bottleneck means SpaceX can bring power online 12–18 months faster than competitors unless rivals build equivalent advanced casting capacity.
4. **Grok/Grokbot growth and enterprise rollout:** Musk said Grokbot is seeing the fastest product growth he has ever witnessed, with per-user usage increasing over time rather than declining. Enterprise deployment is already underway with trusted customers and is expected to reach wide availability within a few weeks. He speculated Grokbot "may end up being what uses most of" SpaceX's super-intelligence compute, and the CFO noted Grok enterprise customers with over $1 million in run-rate revenue (including Cursor) grew 50% quarter-over-quarter on a pro forma basis; a new bundled plan combining Grok, Cursor, and X Premium was recently announced.
5. **Implied long-term ARR range:** Musk said the compute buildout implies SpaceX annual recurring revenue by the end of 2027 "somewhere between $500 billion and a trillion" dollars — a speculative projection, not guidance.
6. **Chip fabrication (Terafab) timeline:** In response to an analyst question about EUV machine orders, Musk said SpaceX is aiming to produce its first functional test chip around May or June of next year at a research lab in Austin, with an aspirational target of reaching volume production around Q3 2028 at the Terafab facility at Gibbons Creek — explicitly caveating these as aggressive, uncertain internal targets with "big error bars."

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Musk addresses SpaceX–Tesla merger speculation:** Asked about merger prospects, Musk said both SpaceX and Tesla have formed special committees of independent directors to discuss the idea at shareholders' request, and that he — as a conflicted party — cannot participate in those discussions. He said it would be "overstating it" to call a merger a certainty, noted any Tesla-side approval would require a vote of 50% of all shares outstanding (not just those voted) — calling that "quite a high hurdle" — and said the special committees have not yet reached agreement on terms.
2. **Dominant launch market share claim:** Musk said SpaceX holds roughly 90% of global mass-to-orbit market share, versus roughly 6% for China and about 4% for the rest of the world combined (including the rest of the US).
