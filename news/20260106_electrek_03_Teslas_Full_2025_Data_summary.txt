The article reports full-year 2025 European vehicle registration data showing Tesla's sales fell sharply across nearly every major market, with the author describing it as a "bloodbath."

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Overall European decline:** Tesla's total European registrations fell from roughly 326,000 units in 2024 to just over 235,000 in 2025, a 27.8% year-over-year decline. The article attributes early-year weakness partly to the Model Y refresh limiting supply, but notes that even after production ramped up, Tesla failed to reach 2024 volume levels, indicating no pent-up demand backlog from the refresh.

2. **Country-level breakdown:** Tesla's sales declined in every European market in 2025 except Norway. Germany, previously described as Tesla's growth engine, saw registrations crash 48.4% (from over 37,500 to just over 19,000 units). France saw a 37.5% drop, which the article attributes to new "bonus écologique" rules that effectively disqualified the Chinese-made Model 3 Highland. Sweden and Belgium — markets that reduced EV incentive support — saw drops of 66.9% and 53.1%, respectively. The UK remained Tesla's largest European market but still posted a significant year-over-year decline.

3. **Norway's temporary exception:** Norway was the sole market where Tesla grew in 2025, but the article says most of that growth came in the final two months of the year, as buyers pulled purchases forward ahead of 2026 changes to Norwegian EV incentives that will make more expensive EVs, including Tesla's, ineligible for some tax benefits. The article states this demand pull-forward should make 2026 difficult for Tesla in Norway.

## Market & Competition
1. **Rising Chinese competition in UK:** The author expects Tesla's UK decline to accelerate in 2026 as Chinese competitors increase their market presence.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) characterizes the 2025 European decline as "an impressive demand cliff" reflecting significant brand problems, attributed to a mix of CEO Elon Musk becoming "highly toxic" and Tesla's EV lineup growing stale amid tougher competition. The author is skeptical that cheaper Model 3/Model Y versions will reverse the trend in 2026, personally predicting sales could be "about flat" at best. A highlighted top reader comment (by Michael Wolf) argues Musk no longer believes Tesla can compete with Chinese manufacturers and has shifted company focus to AI/robotics while "coasting" on vehicles, compounding reputational damage from Musk's behavior.
