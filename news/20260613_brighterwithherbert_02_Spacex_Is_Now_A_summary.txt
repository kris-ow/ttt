This video covers the SpaceX IPO on Friday, June 12, 2026, which the host and guest Larry Goldberg describe as the largest public offering in the history of capital markets. The core thesis is that SpaceX is vastly underappreciated as an AI and cloud computing company — not merely a rocket company — and that Starlink's total addressable market and terrestrial data center ambitions make it a generational investment. Elon Musk is noted to have rung the NASDAQ bell for the second time, the first being for Tesla's IPO in June 2010.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO size and debut performance:** SpaceX priced its IPO at $135 per share and closed its first trading day up 20% at $161, giving it a market capitalization of approximately $2.1 trillion. The offering raised approximately $86 billion (including a green shoe overallotment), which is described as roughly three times the size of Saudi Aramco's record-breaking IPO debut in 2019. By number of shares traded on debut day, SpaceX ranked second to Facebook (580 million shares vs. SpaceX's approximately 518 million), but in dollar value of a single stock traded in a single day, SpaceX set an all-time record.

2. **SpaceX's position among the world's largest companies:** Following the IPO, SpaceX is ranked the seventh-largest company in the world at $2.1 trillion. Tesla is cited as the tenth-largest company at $1.46 trillion. Elon Musk now owns two of the top ten largest companies on Earth, with both having gone public at NASDAQ — Tesla in June 2010 and SpaceX on June 12, 2026, exactly 16 years later.

3. **SpaceX as an AI and cloud computing company:** Guest Larry Goldberg and early SpaceX investor Gavin Baker of Atradus both argue that SpaceX is fundamentally misunderstood as a rocket company. Baker notes that a month prior to the IPO, SpaceX had no cloud computing business, but by IPO day it had become the fourth-largest cloud provider by some measures, surpassing Oracle — an achievement accomplished in one month. Baker points to SpaceX's ability to bring gigawatts of terrestrial compute online, reuse Starship to deploy Starlink V3 satellites, enable direct-to-cell service, and eventually develop orbital compute as a series of investor-trackable milestones.

4. **Colossus data centers and chip strategy:** The discussion references Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 data centers. Colossus 1 was originally intended for Tesla's use, but latency issues led to a shift toward Colossus 2, which is associated with Vera Rubin chips in a deal with Google. Colossus 1 was then made available to Anthropic. Larry Goldberg argues that SpaceX/xAI will always prioritize its own compute needs first and rent out capacity secondarily — a strategy he says will make them the leading AI entity in time. He also suggests Grok and Cursor will merge into a single product.

5. **Starlink total addressable market revision:** Cathie Wood of ARK Invest bought approximately 3.3 million SpaceX shares — worth close to $500 million — on the open market on the day of the IPO across four funds. She explains that ARK originally sized the Starlink opportunity at $34 billion, interpreting it as a rural/maritime connectivity play. SpaceX itself now sizes the total addressable market at $1.6 trillion, encompassing all of telecommunications and the internet, enabled by SpaceX's decision to manufacture its own phased array antennas and drive hardware costs down dramatically. A phased array antenna previously cost approximately $27,000; SpaceX's in-house manufacturing has collapsed that cost curve.

6. **Starlink terminal manufacturing scale:** Larry Goldberg describes visiting the Starlink terminal factory in Bastrop, Texas (near Austin), where fully automated manufacturing produces phased array antennas with no human workers on the floor. He notes that this already enormous factory was doubled in size after his first visit, and that SpaceX plans to build four factories at Bastrop, some up to three times the size of the current facility, to manufacture data satellites at scale.

7. **Analyst coverage and investor demand:** Oppenheimer initiated coverage on SpaceX with an Outperform rating and a $190 price target, which would imply a valuation of approximately $2.5 trillion. The firm's thesis is that SpaceX will leverage its terrestrial compute expertise as a bridge to becoming the largest communications, cloud, and AI company in the world, citing a very large total addressable market with high barriers to entry. Black Rock reportedly put in an order for $5 billion of SpaceX shares. Options trading on SpaceX is set to begin Tuesday, June 17, 2026, and both the host and Goldberg warn of extreme volatility and danger for retail options traders.

8. **Competitive moat and SpaceX's lead over rivals:** Goldberg argues SpaceX's competitive moat is essentially insurmountable in the near term. He notes that China has not yet successfully returned a single booster to Earth, and Blue Origin — the only other company to have done so — is approximately nine to ten years behind SpaceX. SpaceX's Starship is described as ten times larger than competitors' rockets and designed for 100% reusability. Goldberg attributes SpaceX's technological lead to its willingness to push the frontier aggressively rather than waiting years between tests.

9. **SpaceX employee wealth creation:** Approximately 4,000 SpaceX employees, including blue-collar workers such as welders, reportedly became millionaires or multi-millionaires as a result of the IPO, due to SpaceX's practice of granting stock options to all employees.

10. **OpenAI and Anthropic IPO expectations:** The host notes that both OpenAI and Anthropic are expected to go public soon and that rumors suggest each could raise approximately $100 billion, which would surpass SpaceX's $86 billion raise and make SpaceX's record temporary.
