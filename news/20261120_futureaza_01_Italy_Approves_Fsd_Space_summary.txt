The Tesla Beat podcast (episode 170) reviews the week's Tesla and SpaceX news. The hosts see the Cybercab going on sale for personal use, Italy's FSD approval and the Giga Berlin Cybercab investment as signs of Tesla's autonomy push. They treat the first Starship upper-stage catch as a landmark and debate how a Tesla–SpaceX merger would have to be structured. Both stocks were down about 5% midday on the recording day.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger debate after Musk's DealBook comments:** The hosts relay that Musk, at the DealBook Summit, said the companies don't need to be combined but would be better together, citing AI and robotics convergence and the AI software and chip design groups at both. He also said a deal needs a shareholder vote that may not succeed at Tesla, and that he can't drag SpaceX holders into a bad deal. One host says Musk is effectively negotiating against himself, since he has more equity and voting control in SpaceX. The host argues that either a Tesla premium or no premium invites lawsuits from one side. Another host notes Tesla holders would also give up their voting rights and so will want to be paid for that. A third host calls a deal mostly a matter of timing, and another says it must be a remarkable deal to make sense.
2. **Terafab construction shifts to SpaceX:** Matthew observes that construction of the Grimes County Terafab project, once under Tesla's purview, has been transferred to the SpaceX side. He cites it as evidence of growing overlap between the companies.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab on sale for personal use:** Tesla is selling the Cybercab for personal use in the Austin geofence at $29,990 before taxes, with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit. It requires a $299 monthly subscription that includes maintenance, and the vehicle does not work without it. The hosts say the first month is included and deliveries start in December. They see this as an early-adopter launch series, not the final deal. They also see it as proof of a mass-produced unsupervised vehicle sold to individuals.
2. **Fleet participation and ownership terms:** Matthew says Musk said select customers will be allowed to add their own Model Y to the robotaxi fleet. He expects Cybercab owners to follow and speculates the $300 subscription could come with a larger revenue share than a split on a $0 FSD plan. He also describes the order agreement as strict: Tesla can buy the car back at its market rate, and owners can't service it, disable it or use it commercially elsewhere. He doubts that is enforceable and thinks leasing would be cleaner. Another host raises open insurance questions, and another speculates the restrictions will soften over time.
3. **Dallas launch and Texas registrations:** The Cybercab is now live in Dallas, with Fort Worth expected soon. A robotaxi tracker listed 192 vehicles for Dallas in a first-responder lookup. The host says Tesla has now passed Waymo in registered autonomous vehicles in Texas, with the Cybercab now ahead of the Model Y within that count. The hosts argue Waymo's upfit-an-existing-car approach can't match vehicles that drive straight out of the factory.
4. **Regulatory pacing in the US:** Matthew says Nevada requires Tesla to deploy by December 18 or seek a new permit. He says most states neither prevent nor permit deployment, so Tesla is waiting for explicit permission before building out.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Italy approves FSD:** Italy is described as the 12th European country to approve FSD. The host believes a majority of EU states, 14 or 15, is needed for EU-wide approval, so two or three more are required. Matthew says it has taken six years for FSD to reach Europe after launching in the US.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Berlin Cybercab hall:** Tesla announced a $3 billion investment in a Cybercab production hall at Giga Berlin, with capacity of up to 1 million Cybercabs per year. The host believes production is targeted for 2029 and thinks EU timelines may slip. Matthew says Tesla expects roughly two years to build and begin production, and that Giga Shanghai took less than half that. He adds that the Cybercab's small size suits European roads, and that European cities will compete to approve it.
2. **Giga Shanghai and Musk's visit:** Musk visited Shanghai for the first time in three years. The host says the factory has built 810,000 vehicles this year against capacity of up to 950,000. Brian expects Q4 output to exceed 950,000 handily, because China's holidays fall in Q1 rather than around Thanksgiving and Christmas. The hosts note China's car market is soft and competitive, with exports hitting records and Tesla entering new markets such as Argentina and Chile.
3. **Semi ramp:** Over 50 Semis were sold to a customer in Illinois, an EV fleet took delivery of a long-range Semi in San Bernardino, and there are seven Megacharger sites in seven states. Brian says the factory's robotics line is working, the weld and fairing wrinkles are fixed, and units are shipping nationwide. He says Megachargers use modified V4 cabinets, so they can deploy quickly.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus demo and business model:** Joe says Tesla promised an authentic, non-scripted Optimus demo at the TomorrowX Summit within a month or two, showing real hands and forearms. The hosts expect Optimus to be rented or sold under strict terms, similar to the Cybercab, and not freely sold to consumers. They believe regulation will slow it less than robotaxis. They cite a crowded field: Genesis AI raised $500 million at a $3.5 billion valuation, Boston Dynamics plans another Atlas test in 2027, and Figure is pursuing home robots on a lease model.

## Energy
1. **Tesla Powercore launch:** At RE+ in Las Vegas, Tesla introduced Powercore, a solid-state medium-voltage transformer, inverter and breaker in one unit. It delivers 7.4 MW continuous at 34.5 kV AC, with 98.86% peak efficiency and a warranty of up to 25 years. It is 100% made in the US, and first units operate in 2027. It has a built-in solid-state breaker that clears faults in 100 microseconds and uses no flammable oil. The hosts see it as a way around transformer shortages that delay Supercharger and grid projects, and as a possible product to sell to others.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **First Starship ship catch:** Mechazilla caught the Starship upper stage after 14 orbits, about 22.5 hours after launch, at 4:20 Central. Brian calls it monumental and says it will cut launch costs again. He says it also lets Starship carry a full load of 60 Starlink V3 satellites, which he says adds a million users of capacity per launch. He claims Starship would already rank as the second-largest US launch provider in 2026, having passed ULA with two flights. The hosts also note the positive media coverage and speculate about future point-to-point passenger travel.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute payback and demand:** The host recounts CFO Bret Johnsen saying compute investment pays back in under a year, sometimes much faster. He also recounts Johnsen saying Anthropic wants 10 more gigawatts. Matthew worries that many compute buyers are unprofitable, so rising spend could be a house of cards.
