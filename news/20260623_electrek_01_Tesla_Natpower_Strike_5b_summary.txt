Tesla and UK-based independent energy firm NatPower have struck a deal to deploy 25 GWh of Megapack battery storage across Italy and Britain, the first phase of a program worth up to $5 billion.

## Energy
1. **Deal structure and scope:** The multiyear agreement covers five initial projects totaling 25 GWh across Italy and the UK, with a construction cost of $4–$5 billion. Beyond hardware, Tesla is also supplying its energy trading software to manage charging and discharging cycles for grid arbitrage — embedding its software stack into NatPower's operations. NatPower CEO Fabrizio Zago framed the arrangement as "an ecosystem that enables alignment between capital and execution" that can be replicated across markets.

2. **Long-term program ceiling:** NatPower says the broader program aims to exceed 100 GWh of total storage capacity, with revenue potentially topping $15 billion over 20 years. The article notes the 25 GWh phase-one figure alone exceeds half of Tesla's entire global energy storage deployment in 2025.

3. **Context — Tesla's energy growth:** Tesla deployed a record 46.7 GWh of energy storage in 2025, up roughly 48% year-over-year. Analysts pegged Q1 2026 deployments at a record ~14.4 GWh. In September 2025, Tesla unveiled the Megablock and Megapack 3, pre-integrated systems designed to reduce installation time. The article notes Tesla secured a separate multi-billion-dollar Megapack contract in 2024.

## Financials
1. **Market reaction:** TSLA was down approximately 3.3% in pre-market trading Tuesday, attributed to separate reports of a ransomware group leaking confidential documents tied to Tesla and Apple — not to the NatPower deal itself.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert calls this "a meaningful win" in Tesla's strongest business segment, highlighting the software component as an underrated, stickier, higher-margin moat. He cautions that "up to $5 billion" and "$15 billion over 20 years" are headline figures dependent on execution, permitting, and grid interconnection — noting Europe's interconnection queues are "notoriously slow."
