Electrek reports on a newly published Tesla patent application describing a two-piece active rear wing capable of up to 700 kg of downforce, speculating it may be tied to the next-generation Roadster set to be unveiled October 1.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Patent details:** US patent application 2026/0285418 A1, titled "Deployable and Active Rear Wing Assembly with Safety Features," was published by the USPTO on September 24, filed by Tesla in August 2025 as a continuation of an abandoned March 2025 filing, with named inventors David Lemire and James Michael Arthur Crook. The wing has a main element plus a leading-edge slat positioned 3–15 mm ahead of it, sits flush with the deck lid in "road mode," and deploys via a four-bar linkage (with alternate versions using linear rails/lead screws). Tesla states it can go from stowed to fully deployed in under 3 seconds and change angle in under a quarter second.

2. **Downforce and drag figures:** The filing states the wing produces 0 kg of downforce in DRS mode and up to 700 kg of downforce in "high downforce mode" at speeds above 100 mph. Deploying the wing adds 350 drag counts (0.035 Cd) and 1,000 downforce counts; rotating it flat into DRS removes 250 drag counts. For comparison, the article notes McLaren's Senna is rated at 800 kg of downforce.

3. **Control and safety systems:** A controller adjusts the wing based on speed, acceleration, steering angle, brake input, GPS location, and "autonomous driving sensor data" (cameras, radar, lidar per the filing), and can switch to track mode automatically via GPS. Two safety systems are described: mechanical latches that release above a set impact force threshold (folding the wing back flush with the car's rear) and a predictive system that begins retracting the wing ahead of an anticipated rear collision. Non-back-drivable actuators hold the wing's position without drawing power.

4. **Link to the Roadster:** The patent doesn't name the vehicle, but the article notes Figures 2A/2B show a low, fastback 2+2 with a large rear diffuser resembling the Roadster's shape, filed after Musk said in late 2024 Tesla was "close to" finalizing the Roadster's design, and following a separate underbody-duct aero patent from last August that the article says also lines up with the Roadster.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert says the wing's sophistication (DRS, automatic track mode, crash-retraction safety) suggests Tesla engineers are building a genuine track-focused car, which he prefers to earlier "flying-car" style Roadster marketing talk. He cautions that it is only a patent, that Tesla files patents for features that never ship, and that nothing in the filing explicitly confirms it is for the Roadster — this will become clear at the October 1 unveiling.
