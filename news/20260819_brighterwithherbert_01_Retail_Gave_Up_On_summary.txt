This video features a conversation between the host and guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist and investor, about why retail investors are abandoning Tesla stock while institutions are buying near 12-month lows, framed around the upcoming Cybercab launch event and its implications for Tesla's robotaxi ramp and share price.

## Market & Competition
1. **Retail exodus from single stocks, institutions buying Tesla dip:** Research cited from Alexandra Merz shows retail investors broadly have been shifting out of individual stocks into ETFs since around January, while retail ownership of Tesla specifically has fallen to 15.8% of the float; the host and guest note institutional buying of Tesla accelerated in the most recent quarter even as the stock sat near 12-month lows.
2. **Context of a broad momentum-stock selloff:** Bhakdi describes the prior month as the worst for momentum stocks in 30 years, citing Micron, SanDisk, and Nebius as similarly hard-hit, attributing the rout partly to Citadel and what he called "the Ashen Brener debacle" (a market event he references but does not fully explain); he argues Tesla's weakness must be understood in that broader context rather than as Tesla-specific.
3. **Volume decline described as a "ghost town":** The host cites Trendpider's characterization that Tesla trading volume dropped to its lowest level since 2016, which the host and guest interpret as a sign retail has largely walked away from the stock.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab launch event announced with a rider lottery:** Tesla announced a Cybercab launch event is coming soon; riders taking robotaxi trips between August 23rd (dates as stated) have a chance to win tickets to attend the event, with winners announced by August 25th. Bhakdi calls this a major milestone, noting the commercial Cybercab launch has been anticipated for roughly a decade.
2. **Cybercab production ramp estimates (Bhakdi's projections, unconfirmed):** Bhakdi estimates roughly 2,000 Cybercabs have been produced to date and projects a production run rate of about 2,000/month by September, potentially exceeding 3,000 by mid-September — which he claims would put Tesla's Cybercab count above Waymo's vehicle count, though not yet deployed. He also projects over 10,000 Cybercabs produced by year-end, calling this "extremely likely," while stating deployment/in-service numbers are far less certain.
3. **Deployment pace debated — dueling estimates:** A third-party commentator referenced by the host ("Genma," an engineer at another auto company's FSD-equivalent team) forecasts the initial commercial launch involves only a few dozen vehicles, no more than a few thousand Cybercabs in operation by December, and a base case of only about 1,000 vehicles in service by next year. Bhakdi agrees deployment will stay cautious and slow near-term but pushes back on a flat 1,000-vehicle year-end figure, arguing that if thousands are already produced and tested, more than 1,000 will likely be placed into service by year-end (without committing to a specific number).
4. **Austin robotaxi charging hub permit includes wireless chargers:** New permits filed near Austin (across from Tesla's Elmo service center) call for 48 charging stalls in a vehicle storage lot, with a second phase reportedly including plans for 80 wireless vehicle chargers (V4 charging cabinet) — the first known instance of wireless charging infrastructure being planned for robotaxi use, following long delays to a wireless charging feature previously discussed publicly (referenced remark about Franz von Holzhausen speaking about it in an interview with Jay Leno).
5. **Existing Supercharger capacity seen as sufficient near-term:** Bhakdi argues existing Supercharger infrastructure in Austin (citing roughly a dozen-plus chargers at some sites) could handle charging needs for the current, still-small Cybercab fleet overnight with minimal staffing, downplaying concerns about charging bottlenecks until fleet sizes reach much larger scale.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Cybercab production ramp interpreted as confidence in FSD readiness:** Bhakdi's central thesis is that Tesla would not aggressively scale Cybercab production and hire robotaxi operators/event staff if the company's leadership (including Elon Musk, the CFO, COO, and head of AI) believed FSD was not on track — arguing it's far more likely critics are wrong about FSD's readiness than that a sophisticated organization would knowingly overproduce non-functional vehicles.
2. **FSD progress framed as engineering buildout, not open-ended research:** Bhakdi contends Tesla's FSD development resembles a predictable AI/compute scaling problem (analogous to improving a large language model) rather than an uncertain scientific breakthrough (like nuclear fusion research), implying Tesla can more reliably forecast and plan improvements and edge-case resolution over time.

## Financials
1. **Guest's short-term and year-end TSLA price targets:** Bhakdi states high confidence in a short-term move to the $370s (mentioning $379/$380 area) within about three weeks, driven largely by the Cybercab launch event, and argues a return to prior all-time highs — referencing a $488–$490 level from around December of the prior year — is plausible by the end of this year if the market perceives large-scale Cybercab deployment as imminent within roughly six months.
