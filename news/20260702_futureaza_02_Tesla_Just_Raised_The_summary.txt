FutureAzA host Brian interviews Herbert, who recently sat down with Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy for an in-depth discussion about Cybercab manufacturing, AI-driven quality control, and the Unboxed production platform. The video covers key engineering revelations from that meeting — including the "Full Self-Hearing" quality system, the burn-in test track at Giga Texas, and Cybercab production automation levels — as well as speculation about a July 7th Tesla announcement related to Cybercab and/or robotaxi city expansion.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **"Full Self-Hearing" — AI-powered NVH quality detection:** Lars Moravy revealed that Tesla has developed a system — internally called "Full Self-Hearing" — in which Cybercabs autonomously drive over a squeak-and-rattle test track after leaving the production line. The cars use their onboard microphones (the same ones used for voice commands) combined with machine learning to detect, localize, and classify noise, vibration, and harshness (NVH) issues. The AI in the car then communicates directly with the factory AI system to flag problems and trace them back to the root cause on the production line — potentially all the way back to the stamping stage. Lars described this as a genuine two-way AI-to-AI communication loop that accelerates the iteration cycle. He characterized it as his favorite example of how Tesla is deploying AI in manufacturing.

2. **Cybercab self-driving end-of-line logistics:** Beyond NVH testing, Cybercabs are already autonomously self-driving from the end of the production line to the logistics yard, with no human driver required. This is presented as a live example of FSD capability being used operationally within the factory environment itself.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab manufacturing line 90% automated:** Lars Moravy disclosed that the Cybercab manufacturing line is already 90% automated — further ahead than he personally expected. He attributed this to lessons learned from prior vehicle launches, allowing the team to avoid past pitfalls and move faster than normal. He stated that the vast majority of Cybercabs currently being produced are being used for FSD testing rather than customer robotaxi deployment, which is the primary reason the paid robotaxi service has not yet fully launched.

2. **Burn-in test track at Giga Texas:** A dedicated test track has been built at or near Giga Texas where Cybercabs autonomously drive in loops to accumulate mileage in a controlled, on-site environment. Herbert noted this replaces the traditional practice of hiring human driver crews to burn miles in loops around cities (a practice he witnessed firsthand with Ford engineers). The track allows Tesla to run thousands of miles of durability and FSD testing simultaneously and efficiently, including multiple passes over the NVH section during each run.

3. **Scale ambitions for Cybercab — 2 million to 10+ million units per year:** Lars repeatedly emphasized that analysts — including both Wells Fargo (characterized as deeply underestimating) and ARK Invest (characterized as the most bullish but still conservative) — have not grasped how efficient the Cybercab production process is, from raw material to finished vehicle. He stated that the Cybercab platform is a platform unto itself given planned production volumes, suggesting a target of 2 million units per year is "almost a given" and that he is thinking potentially 10-plus million units of this single form factor over time. Different form factors built on this platform are possible but may not be necessary given the scale planned for the Cybercab alone.

4. **Unboxed platform: 20–25 manufacturing cells vs. ~200 in traditional auto:** Lars disclosed that traditional automobile manufacturing setups use approximately 200 production cells, while Tesla's Unboxed platform for the Cybercab uses only 20 to 25. Herbert noted that the range (20–25) suggests the definition of "cells" may differ from the traditional concept. The Unboxed architecture has been in conceptual development since 2014 — approximately 12 years before reaching production scale.

5. **Cybercab efficiency — 165 Wh/mile energy consumption, 300–400 mile range:** The Cybercab achieves approximately 165 watt-hours per mile, enabling roughly 300 to 400 miles of range on a relatively small battery pack.

6. **July 7th announcement speculation — robotaxi city expansion:** Lars hinted at a "cool" announcement related to Cybercab scale, expected around July 7, 2026, though he declined to elaborate. Herbert and Brian speculate this is most likely a robotaxi launch in multiple new cities rather than a new vehicle or factory announcement. The host notes Tesla had previously indicated it would expand to five cities by the end of June 2026 but did not meet that deadline. Cities discussed as likely candidates include Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Miami — with a comment on X cited (unconfirmed) suggesting Nevada regulators are expected to issue a final permit around July 7. Tesla has reportedly applied for 5,000 robotaxi permits in Nevada.

7. **Miami fire department training on Cybercab:** The hosts reference a report (described as from the prior day) that the Miami fire department has been conducting training exercises involving the Cybercab, citing this as a signal that Miami is a near-term robotaxi launch city.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Panel gap quality — third-best per JD Power:** Lars shared that Tesla now ranks third among all cars tested by JD Power for panel alignment and fitment quality. He attributed this improvement to AI-powered vision cameras on the production line that instantly detect panel gaps after assembly and trace the cause back up the line — including all the way to the stamping stage — so issues are caught and corrected in near real-time.

## Market & Competition

1. **Unboxed as an industry-shifting manufacturing model:** Herbert and Brian discuss how Tesla's Unboxed manufacturing approach — like gigacasting before it — may eventually become an industry-wide standard. Lars framed the decision to abandon the traditional optimized assembly line as a significant risk that the team took deliberately, and one that is now paying off at scale. Herbert expresses confidence that other automakers will eventually adopt similar approaches, just as many followed Tesla's lead on gigacasting.
