This episode of "Electrified," hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers the current state of Tesla's robotaxi rollout and why fleet growth has stalled, along with a detailed breakdown of Elon Musk's actual real-world voting power at Tesla versus the commonly cited beneficial ownership figure. The video also touches on Megapack 3 deployments in Europe, EU EV sales data, Optimus factory construction progress, FSD updates, and several SpaceX-related developments.

## Robotaxi

1. **Fleet Growth Has Stalled Since Mid-May:** According to the third-party tracker (RobotaxiTracker.com), fleet growth effectively paused in all three Texas cities around May 9th. Of the 26 active vehicles tracked over the past 30 days, 21 are unsupervised, and just under 80% of rides over the past 7 days have been unsupervised. A photo shared by a community member shows 13 Cybercabs parked in Dallas that "appear to be asleep for now," suggesting they are on-site but not yet deployed.

2. **Houston Depot Activity Suggests Preparation:** A community observer named Aron noted that many Model Y vehicles at the Houston robotaxi depot have their hazard lights on, speculating they may be undergoing retrofits (possibly for a rear washer camera). Some of these Model Ys are launch edition vehicles not available for sale. Separately, employees were observed removing carpeting and parts from trunks. Last week, all Model 3s, Cybertrucks, and non-robotaxi vehicles were removed from the lot, leaving only Cybercabs and Model Ys. Dillon interprets this as Tesla being in "all hands on deck" mode preparing Cybercabs for imminent deployment.

3. **Cybercab Production Timeline and Deployment Thesis:** Tesla announced the first Cybercab off the production line at Giga Texas on February 17th, with a broader production announcement on April 23rd. The pause in Model Y fleet expansion appears to coincide with the arrival of Cybercabs in Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Dillon's personal expectation is approximately 30 Cybercabs in Austin and 10–15 each in Dallas and Houston over the coming weeks. He believes serious scale will wait until a future FSD version (referred to as V15), expected later in 2025.

4. **Five New Cities Must Launch Within 33 Days:** Tesla's own slide deck commits to launching robotaxi service in five new cities, and as of the video's date, that deadline is approximately 33 days away. Dillon emphasizes that commitments in Tesla's official slide deck should carry significantly more weight than off-the-cuff comments Elon makes in interviews. He believes Tesla is hyperfocused on meeting these city-launch milestones and does not want to face the backlash of missing them.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 Mixed Performance:** Dillon reports that FSD version 14.3 demonstrated impressive supervised FSD behavior in a complex traffic scene in the Netherlands. However, it also showed a concerning parking failure where the vehicle would have driven into a shopping cart stall without human intervention. Dillon has also experienced some phantom braking on 14.3 in limited testing. He emphasizes that the most significant aspect of 14.3 is Tesla's rewrite of the compiler and runtime from the ground up, positioning it as a foundational building block for future releases rather than a polished end product.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Factory Steel Structure Goes Up:** Joe Tegtmeyer reported that the first steel structure has been erected at the construction site for an Optimus factory with a stated capacity of 10 million units per year, and that the second phase of land reclamation is underway. A screenshot was shared showing the first steel going up at the site.

## Energy

1. **Megapack 3 Heading to Belgium:** Tesla's newest Megapack 3 will be deployed in one of Europe's most significant battery storage projects, with Belgian company Spy installing 180 units on the site of a former power plant. Installation and grid connection work is scheduled to begin in November 2025, with testing and commissioning expected to be completed by end of 2027. The project will have a storage capacity of several hundred megawatt hours and a total project value exceeding $116 million. This will be one of the first large-scale Megapack 3 deployments in Europe.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla EU Sales Up 45.8% Year-to-Date:** New ACA data through April shows Tesla sold 89,429 vehicles in the EU, EFTA, and UK year-to-date, up from 61,343 in the same period last year — a 45.8% increase. Tesla's overall market share rose from 1.4% to 1.9%. Dillon cautions that comparisons are distorted because Q1 and Q2 of last year saw Tesla transitioning to the refreshed Model Y and Model 3. He hopes a broader supervised FSD rollout later in the year will accelerate momentum further.

2. **Chinese Automakers Outpacing Tesla in Europe:** BYD sold over 101,000 vehicles in EU/EFTA/UK markets in the first four months of the year, up nearly 144% year-over-year, exceeding Tesla's volume. Chery Auto also outsold Tesla in this period with sales growth of 338% year-over-year. Dillon notes that lower price points and more available models contribute to these figures, but flags the Chinese automaker surge as a significant storyline. Hybrids continue to dominate EU consumer preference at 38.2% market share versus full BEVs at 19.7%.

## Market & Competition

1. **Elon's Real-World Voting Power Is 11%, Not 20%:** Dillon provides a detailed breakdown of the difference between Elon Musk's SEC-reported beneficial ownership and his actual real-world voting power. The 13G/A filing shows approximately 717 million shares classified as beneficial ownership (~20.3%), but this includes 303 million unexercised options from the 2018 compensation award that do not currently carry voting rights. Elon's actual real-world voting power today is based only on the ~413 million shares he fully owns in a revocable trust, divided by Tesla's actual outstanding share count of approximately 3.75 billion — yielding an 11% real-world voting percentage.

2. **Options Exercise Window Opens Path to 17.6%:** Per a new implementation agreement following the Delaware court case, Elon's window to exercise the 303 million 2018 award options runs from May 21st through August 15th of this year. If Elon doesn't exercise them himself, Tesla will exercise them on his behalf. Once exercised, those shares will be added to both the numerator and denominator: 717 million divided by approximately 4.059 billion outstanding shares would give Elon a 17.6% real-world voting stake. The tax event for those shares is expected to be pushed to 2028 when shares vest, not at the time of exercise, with a 5-year holding period extending through 2033.

3. **2025 Award and Path to 28.1%:** The newer 2025 compensation award covers approximately 423 million shares already included in Tesla's outstanding share count. These shares are currently subject to a voting proxy given to Tesla's secretary, voted proportionately to other shareholders — meaning Elon has no voting control over them now. The award has 12 tranches, each worth approximately 35 million shares. As Elon earns each tranche by hitting market cap and operational milestones (implying Tesla reaching approximately an $8.5 trillion market cap for full vesting), voting rights transfer to him. If all tranches are earned, Elon's total voting control would reach approximately 28.1% — near or above his stated goal of 25%, estimated to occur around tranches 8 or 9.

4. **SpaceX Merger Speculation:** Peter Diamandis, a friend of Elon Musk and early SpaceX investor, stated that a post-IPO merger between SpaceX and Tesla is "inevitable" and a question of timing, primarily because Elon would want to consolidate voting power across his companies. Elon currently holds 85.1% voting control at SpaceX (prior to IPO filing). The FTSE Russell indices announced fast-entry enhancements allowing qualifying IPOs to join relevant indices just 5 days after IPO, changes Dillon notes were largely put in place for SpaceX, enabling more forced institutional buying shortly after listing.

5. **Rivian R2 Launch June 9th:** Rivian announced the R2 will launch on June 9th with order invites, first customer deliveries, and demo drives beginning. Dillon characterizes the R2's success or failure as likely a make-or-break moment for Rivian.
