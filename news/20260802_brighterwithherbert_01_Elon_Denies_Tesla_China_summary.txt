## Market & Competition
1. **WSJ report on Tesla China sale denied:** The Wall Street Journal reported, citing one unnamed source, that Tesla was weighing a sale of its China business to pave the way for a potential SpaceX merger. Elon Musk called the report "absurdly fake news," said it had never even come up in discussion, and said people should assume news is fake until proven otherwise; Tesla China separately denied the report. The guest argues China has strong incentive to keep its Tesla relationship intact and lacks a real mechanism to block a Tesla-SpaceX merger, characterizing the report as WSJ "fishing" and part of a pattern of stories preceding SpaceX/Tesla developments.
2. **US-China tech restrictions escalating:** Over the past several months the US has moved to restrict Chinese drones, EVs, and (most recently) humanoid robots from the US market. The host cites a third-party commentary (attributed to "Farzad") warning that fully autonomous robots are a national security issue, that China won't hesitate to position itself against the US, and that as SpaceX's Starship dominance grows it further disadvantages China technologically — though Tesla and SpaceX are expected to weather this. The guest frames US-China tech relations as a long-running cycle of mutual bans and cooperation that will likely continue indefinitely short of a Taiwan invasion scenario.
3. **China's open-source AI push and DeepSeek:** China is promoting open-source AI models (e.g., a new DeepSeek version reportedly matching Anthropic's Opus on benchmarks) at very low cost, which could pressure US labs' pricing power. A podcast host (Jason, All-In podcast) is cited predicting tokens will be ~100x cheaper within two years and that a large share of compute will run locally/unmetered. The guest pushes back, arguing frontier models continue improving faster than open-source can catch up, that enterprise workloads will still require frontier-model compute run in data centers, and that inference — not training — is where the real spend will occur, ultimately shifting to space-based compute per Musk's own framing (90%+ of AI compute service-side for now, long-term ~99% in space).
4. **CXMT IPO and memory chip shortage:** China's largest memory chip maker, CXMT (Changxin Memory Technologies), went public and jumped 466% on its first trading day, reaching a roughly $500 billion valuation, spooking the AI trade; Yangtze Memory Technologies is also cited as going public amid a global AI-driven memory shortage. The guest argues this is not purely a threat since more global chip supply is needed regardless, though it does add competitive pressure — reinforcing why Tesla/SpaceX's Terafab initiative to build proprietary memory and semiconductor chips is strategically important.
5. **NHTSA/Trump administration AV policy moves:** NHTSA (under Secretary Sean Duffy) announced a partnership with SAE Industries Technology Consortium funding a 3-year, $5 million effort to create the first national AV performance standards, aimed at replacing the current patchwork of state regulations. Separately, NHTSA granted Zoox (Amazon) a temporary exemption to commercially deploy up to 2,500 robotaxi vehicles annually for two years — a cap the host says reflects Zoox's manufacturing/testing limitations (Zoox failed self-certification crash testing for its backward-facing seating), whereas Tesla's Cybercab has already passed self-certification and, per Lars Moravy (referenced as reconfirming this over the past weekend), faces no cap on deployment numbers. Additional NHTSA changes include allowing pre-exemption-manufactured vehicles to qualify for commercial deployment exemptions and streamlining Part 555 exemption applications.
