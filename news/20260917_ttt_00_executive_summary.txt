## Brief

- NHTSA sets Sept 30 deadline for Cybercab scaling answers
- Morgan Stanley lifts Tesla bull case to $840 on Semi autonomy
- Tesla to operate public Megachargers at 3 Forum Mobility depots
- IMC Logistics orders 50 Tesla Semis for California drayage

- Starship Flight 14 now targeted September 28, first V3 Starlink batch

## Robotaxi

- NHTSA's formal question list to Tesla, due September 30, asks how Cybercab scaling and Part 555 compliance work without a brake pedal exemption; the exemption process caps annual units at 2,500, which one commentator calls the biggest near-term regulatory risk to the program.
- Cybercab began production in Texas in Q2 2026, with Tesla targeting at least 2 million units/year at full capacity from a single facility, per one source — though a separate host cites a 125,000-unit/year cap from Tesla's latest earnings, illustrating disagreement on the real production ceiling.
- Debate over Cybercab's endgame: one commentator insists Tesla will eventually add a steering-wheel variant to soak up excess capacity if regulation caps deployment; another argues removing all driver controls is a deliberate regulatory strategy clearing the way for a cheaper, steering-wheel-less consumer lineup (potentially under $30,000) in roughly two years.
- A Palo Alto depot sighting near Tesla's engineering campus showed roughly six Cybercabs in test driving plus an estimated ~100 more parked, alongside ~25–30 robotaxi-labeled Model Ys — read as an active dispatch/engineering hub rather than evidence of imminent wider deployment.
- Active Robotaxi fleet remains 839 (Bay Area 657, Austin 135, Dallas 22, Houston 25) and unsupervised fleet 48, per RobotaxiTracker.com — both stale for over a month.

## Autonomous Driving

- Tesla's July robotaxi safety data showed two at-fault incidents in Austin (a bollard strike, a collision with a low-hanging chain), both described as minor; cumulative incidents since launch stand at 26 (11 Tesla's fault, 13 other-party, 2 no-fault).
- Waymo resumed San Antonio robotaxi service five months after a flash-flood incident, covering roughly 60 miles including downtown and the airport; one host notes Waymo has not disclosed whether it will avoid flood-prone streets going forward.
- Autonomous Semi development will follow robotaxi progress, per a statement attributed to Ashok Elluswamy, implying no autonomous trucking deployment this year or likely next.

## Electric Vehicles

- Tesla officially unveiled its European Semi variant: 342 miles (550 km) range, camera-based side mirrors, and roughly 1.6 kWh/mile efficiency versus 1.8 for the US version — over a 10% gain that one analyst says likely reflects new underlying architecture; European deliveries start 2027.
- Tesla's Nevada Semi factory, designed for 1,000 trucks/week, ramps toward a September 24 rollout event; current output is estimated at only 10–20 units/week, with 471 open job listings signaling a hiring push.
- Tesla's US EV market share averaged ~52% year-to-date (Jan–Aug) versus ~44% a year earlier, though overall US EV sales share has flattened at ~5.8% of total vehicle sales.
- IMC Logistics, the largest US marine drayage provider, will deploy 50 Tesla Semis in California; Tesla's Semi order backlog reportedly extends well into 2027.
- Tesla Semi executives say fleet mileage has reached 11.8 million miles (19 million km) to date, with 400,000 km logged in European pilots.

## Financials

- Morgan Stanley raised its Tesla bull-case price target to $840 (from $820), holding base case at $400, citing autonomous Semi software economics it estimates could generate $12,000–$18,000/truck/month versus ~$100/month for consumer FSD; the firm remains rated equal-weight. One commentator called the per-truck revenue estimate too high while the 82,000-unit 2040 deployment assumption seemed too low.
- TSLA closed at $366.20, up 2.27%; SpaceX (SPCX) closed at $154.81, up 2.6%, versus the Nasdaq-100 up 1.73%.
- Ron Baron reiterated a bullish Tesla stance, saying "the time to buy the stock is now," citing 55% FSD attach rate among new buyers.

## Energy

- Tesla's proposed $10.1 billion "Project Crystal Sun" solar factory near Houston cleared a local hurdle as the Lamar CISD school board unanimously approved a tax incentive agreement; the 100-gigawatt facility is set to begin construction this year, finish in 2028, with commercial operations targeted for Q1 2029.

## Humanoid Bots

- Figure placed its Figure 03 robots in 30 rented homes with no prior training, reporting task success jumping from ~8–9% to 56% using its "index" human-data pre-training program (62% towel folding, 40% toy pickup, 67% bed-making) — cited as a bullish signal that humanoids can learn from recorded human data rather than needing large deployed fleets first.
- A rumored 50,000-unit 2026 Optimus production figure was dismissed as not credible by one host, who instead flagged a more plausible report of Tesla executives conducting factory audits in China ahead of possible small-scale production starting around October.
- Samsung has begun trial production of Tesla's AI5 chip at its Taylor, Texas fab; full-scale production isn't expected until well into 2027, with Optimus cited as the chip's likely first application.

## Market & Competition

- Tesla is installing over 100 Megachargers across Germany, France, UK, Sweden, Netherlands, and Belgium, with businesses in five countries now able to install their own Semi chargers on-site.
- Tesla will operate public Megacharging at three Forum Mobility truck depots in California (Ontario, Oakland x2), collecting $0.08/kWh — about 20% below its Supercharger for Business rate — while Forum owns the real estate; the open MCS 3.2 standard also allows Daimler, Volvo, and Scania trucks to charge there.

## SpaceX — Starship

- Starship Flight 14 is now targeted for September 28 (shifted from an earlier September 22 estimate), aiming to be the first flight to reach full Earth orbit and deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites — satellites only launchable via Starship, not Falcon 9.
- The flight will reuse two heat-shield tiles recovered from a prior ship, the first such reuse for Starship; Elon Musk said a successful Flight 14 sets up a ship-catch attempt on Flight 15.
