Looking at Lars comments more broadly, he s&nbsp;

Let s take a closer look&nbsp;&nbsp;
at each factory that Lars mentioned:
First, at Giga Berlin, they re shifting to&nbsp;&nbsp;
using 4680 battery cells that were produced in&nbsp;

which cuts margins to third party suppliers,&nbsp;&nbsp;

&nbsp;
Second, at Giga Nevada, Tesla s upgrading battery&nbsp;
pack production lines that were first stood up&nbsp;&nbsp;
back in 2017 to 2018. Now, with almost a decade of&nbsp;
experience, they re well overdue for an upgrade,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which might also include improvements to the&nbsp;
design of the battery pack. That, in turn, should&nbsp;&nbsp;
mean lower production costs for those battery&nbsp;
packs as well as higher production rates.
&nbsp;
Third, at Giga Shanghai, as has been widely&nbsp;
reported, Tesla has started purchasing battery&nbsp;&nbsp;
cells from Sunwoda and manufacturing&nbsp;
the modules themselves in-house.
&nbsp;
Up until now, most of Tesla s LFP battery&nbsp;
supply for vehicle in China came from CATL,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which manufactured both the battery cells and&nbsp;
modules. The Sunwoda cells are likely cheaper&nbsp;&nbsp;
than the battery cells they purchase from&nbsp;
CATL, and of course assembling the modules&nbsp;&nbsp;
in-house that go into the battery&nbsp;
packs will also likely save money.
&nbsp;
What all this means is that over the&nbsp;
next couple of years, thanks to those&nbsp;&nbsp;
cost savings on the battery pack which is the&nbsp;
most expensive assembly the vehicle Tesla can&nbsp;&nbsp;
either reduce the cost of their vehicles&nbsp;
or take the additional vehicle margin.
