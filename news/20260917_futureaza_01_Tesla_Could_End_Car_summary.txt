This is a co-hosted discussion (futureaza with guest Herbert) speculating that Tesla is moving away from one-time car sales toward an all-in-one subscription model — bundling vehicle access, FSD, insurance, entertainment (via Starlink), cleaning, and charging into a single monthly fee, similar to Apple One or Netflix. The hosts frame this as their own speculative interpretation of "breadcrumbs" (e.g., Tesla ending one-time FSD purchases) rather than anything Tesla has confirmed, and debate pricing, insurance economics, and whether this displaces car ownership entirely or coexists with it via tiers.

## Electric Vehicles / Robotaxi
1. **Subscription bundle thesis (speculative):** Herbert argues Tesla is building toward an Apple One/Amazon Prime-style bundle: a single monthly fee could give a customer access to ride-hailing (robotaxi), a car parked in their garage on demand, the ability to swap vehicle types (e.g., a Cybertruck for a weekend move, a Model Y L for a family event), plus FSD, insurance, entertainment (Netflix/YouTube Premium via Starlink-equipped cars), automated cleaning, and charging — all under Tesla's existing city-by-city Robotaxi rollout and its multi-city car rental program. He explicitly states this is inference from Tesla's behavior (e.g., discontinuing one-time FSD purchases priced as high as $15,000/$12,000/$8,000), not anything Tesla has announced.
2. **Insurance tied to FSD usage:** Herbert describes his own Tesla insurance (via Metro Mile/Lemonade) as usage-based, and notes FSD-specific insurance discounts now exist in six U.S. states, where insurance costs drop the more FSD is used versus manual driving. He cites Lemonade's view that a future safer FSD version could push insurance costs toward near-zero, reinforcing the case for a bundled subscription where FSD usage lowers the effective monthly fee.
3. **Cost comparison and pricing tiers:** Per Herbert's analysis, total monthly car-ownership costs (insurance, registration, maintenance) run around $1,000/month for some owners, and he argues a Tesla bundle priced below that would be a "no-brainer." He also floats a lower ~$500/month tier and separate cheaper monthly plans for apartment-dwellers who'd only use ride-hailing, similar to Uber/Lyft subscription passes; Brian pushes back that even a lower tier may be too high for many potential users, who typically buy used rather than new cars.
4. **Precedent of failed "car on demand" services:** Herbert notes that Cadillac, Mercedes, and other automakers previously tried similar car-swap/subscription services ("Cadillac on demand") and they failed, because using human drivers to shuttle vehicles between customers was cost-prohibitive; he argues Tesla's advantage is that self-driving cars could handle swapping, cleaning, and charging autonomously, making the model economically viable at scale.
