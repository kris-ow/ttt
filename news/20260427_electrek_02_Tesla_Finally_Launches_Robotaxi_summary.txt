Tesla has launched its "Robotaxi" app on the Google Play Store for Android users, nearly a year after the iOS version debuted in September 2025. The Android release arrived April 24, days after Tesla expanded its robotaxi service from Austin to Dallas and Houston.

## Robotaxi
1. **App features and availability:** The app allows users to request rides from Tesla's fleet of specially configured Model Y vehicles, displaying the approaching vehicle's license plate upon confirmation. Riders can adjust climate controls, seat positioning, music, trip progress, and drop-off location, or request a pullover. The Android version adds Google Pay integration. The app is free, available in over 25 languages, and currently limited to the United States.

2. **Service scope remains very limited:** Tesla's robotaxi currently operates in Austin, Dallas, and Houston. Houston's geofence covers roughly 12–15 square miles; Dallas covers approximately 30–35 square miles. Crowdsourced data shows Tesla deployed just one vehicle in each new city at launch, with a second added shortly after. Austin has roughly 40 active vehicles, but only about a dozen operate fully driverless — the vast majority still carry human safety monitors. Tesla had planned to expand to eight U.S. cities but has delayed five, now listed as "preparations underway" with no specific timelines.

3. **Pricing data:** Dallas pricing is $3.00 base fare plus $1.40 per mile. Austin pricing is $3.25 base fare plus $1.00 per mile. Service operates daily starting at 6 a.m.

## Market & Competition
4. **Waymo comparison:** Waymo operates in 10 U.S. cities with approximately 3,000 vehicles, delivering over 500,000 fully driverless rides per week with no safety monitors, no chase cars, and 24-hour availability. Waymo launched in Dallas and Houston in February 2026, two months before Tesla's arrival in those cities.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert characterizes Tesla's robotaxi as "a marketing exercise designed to justify Tesla's valuation as an 'autonomy company'" rather than a genuine autonomous service, arguing the gap between Tesla and Waymo is widening, not closing.
