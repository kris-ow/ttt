This Electrek opinion piece catalogs a string of Elon Musk's recent inflammatory social-media posts and political moves — from white-nationalist-adjacent statements to an xAI lawsuit against a Minnesota deepfake law — and argues this behavior poses a growing risk to Tesla's brand ahead of the midterms.

## Market & Competition
1. **Musk's political posts escalate after an Economist interview:** On July 23, Economist editor Zanny Minton Beddoes interviewed Musk and pushed back on his claims about UK crime and immigration; Musk conceded, "I think I got a little too involved in politics. I got carried away, frankly." The article notes Tesla executives had already told an earnings call that "political sentiment" was hurting the brand.
2. **Series of controversial posts detailed:** The article recounts Musk posts including a "Hitler was a socialist" claim (111 million views) that was corrected by his own company's Grok chatbot via a community note; attacks on director Christopher Nolan over Odyssey casting, calling him "an anti-White racist"; a reply calling opponents of "remigration" (mass deportation) "traitors" (3.1 million views); support for a woman convicted of racist remarks toward a child; criticism of NYC mayor Zohran Mamdani (8.5 million views); and praise for a Shopify CEO defending a wealth-weighted voting proposal.
3. **xAI sues Minnesota over deepfake law:** On July 28, Musk's xAI sued Minnesota's Attorney General to block a law (effective August 1, with penalties up to $500,000 per image) targeting AI-generated nonconsensual sexual images, arguing it is unconstitutional; the article notes xAI faces its own related lawsuit from Ashley St. Clair over Grok-generated images.
4. **Musk reviving political PAC:** Axios reportedly said Musk is reviving America PAC to fund Republican midterm turnout, run by adviser Chris Young; he spent more than $260 million through the PAC in 2024.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues Musk's escalating political rhetoric mirrors the 2025 DOGE-era backlash that hurt Tesla, citing a prior study attributing over a million lost US sales to Musk's politics, collapsed brand loyalty, and a $140 billion single-day stock drop after Q2 results, and warns renewed political involvement risks further damaging Tesla ahead of the midterms.
