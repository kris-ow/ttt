This Electrified episode covers Tesla's summer software update rolling out with FSD14.3.8 and a new Grok voice model (ThinkFast 2.0), robotaxi service expansion, weak first-half Cybertruck registration data alongside a price hike, Giga Berlin hiring signals, Tesla China's denial of FSD pullback rumors, and a deep dive into the humanoid robot data/training race involving Figure and a company called Skild, plus a Boring Company Vegas Loop update.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Grok ThinkFast 2.0 voice model rolling out:** Tesla's summer release, bundled with FSD14.3.8, integrates SpaceX's newer speech-to-speech Grok model (ThinkFast 2.0), replacing the slower speech-to-text approach; the host says it benchmarks as the best speech-to-speech model currently available and is also used for Starlink's customer service chatbot. Grok can now execute multiple simultaneous in-car commands (climate, glove box, wipers, mirrors, launching the self-driving app), though the host says older Intel-based Teslas won't get the full command suite — only Ryzen-chip cars, which additionally gain phone calls, music search/playback, and vehicle Q&A. The host speculates in-car FSD voice commands may be coming next and notes Grok conversations now appear to sync across car, phone, and desktop.
2. **FSD avoids thin linear object:** A shared clip showed FSD successfully avoiding a thin, linear object near eye level, which the host called encouraging given past challenges Tesla FSD has had with this type of obstacle detection.
3. **Tesla China denies FSD pullback rumors:** Tesla China stated rumors it was withdrawing FSD development and vacating its Shanghai data center are false, saying the data center operates normally, hiring for driver-assistance roles is accelerating, and it reported the rumors to police. Tesla still has not launched FSD subscriptions in China or announced a rollout timeline. A Li Auto executive reportedly said no autonomy system besides Tesla's FSD ranks in the first tier.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi hours extended, fleet growing:** Tesla said Robotaxi now operates 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., seven days a week, across all six active cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa), with an unsupervised fleet described as larger than before and improved vehicle distribution/routing reducing wait times; Tesla did not disclose a specific unsupervised fleet number, which the host attributes to day-to-day fluctuation. He argues extending hours into late-night (10 p.m.–1 a.m.) would capture strong bar/nightlife demand.
2. **Cybercabs spotted in Las Vegas:** A shared video reportedly showed roughly 30 Cybercabs now stationed in Las Vegas; a separate source said even on FSD 14.3.8, some parking issues persist.
3. **Boring Company Vegas Loop expansion:** The Vegas Loop received approval for 19 additional stations, bringing total entitlement to 123 stations; the network currently runs seven boring machines with 11 expected by year-end. The host expects Cybercabs (and eventually Optimus/"the rebove," likely a Robovan reference) to eventually use the tunnels.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck registrations fall sharply:** Citing S&P Global data, the host says 8,400 Cybertrucks were registered in the US in the first half of this year, down from 12,800 in the first half of last year, against a 125,000-unit annual production capacity — figures the host attributes to a named source's insight ("Mars"). He cites supplier/legal production challenges in H1 and expects a boost from a lower-priced trim in H2, but says the trend suggests Cybertruck may be settling in as a low-volume "halo" vehicle rather than the high-volume seller Tesla originally hoped for. The host also shares a personal, hedged concern about potential harassment risk associated with the vehicle as a possible demand headwind.
2. **Cybertruck price increase:** Tesla raised prices on both the dual-motor and Premium All-Wheel Drive Cybertruck variants by $5,000 each — entry-level now starts at $75,000, Premium at $85,000. Estimated delivery for the entry-level trim is listed into 2027, while Premium is October–November this year, which the host suggests may reflect a supply limitation rather than pure demand strength.
3. **Giga Berlin hiring signals possible new production prep:** A Tesla-watcher reported "25 Guns" engineers (described as an elite internal team) are being brought to Giga Berlin with urgency, and one Tesla figure noted a focus on "cell production," which the host speculates could indicate early Semi production prep or simply scaling of Model Y and local 4680 cell output.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Third-party enthusiasm for Optimus:** Unnamed commentators speaking about a recent Optimus demo (details undisclosed by the host) made bullish predictions — including that Optimus could win the majority of contests against other robots at a robotics competition, that there will be roughly 1 billion Optimus units in 10 years, and that Optimus could become the best-selling product in history, exceeding the iPhone's value — with one claiming Optimus's hardware is generations ahead of and its "brain" further along than competitors' humanoids being built in China.
2. **Skild's S1 training breakthrough vs. Figure's data-gathering approach:** Pittsburgh-based Skild (founded 2023, valued at $14 billion) announced a technique where a pretrained robot model learns new tasks from a single demonstration video without adjusting model weights, achieving a 66% average per-step success rate versus 9% for text-prompted (VLA-type) commands on unseen tasks — showing video demonstrations are far more effective than language instructions for novel tasks. However, the host cautions the 66% is a per-step figure with human correction after each failure, so multiplying independently across a 10-step task implies only about 1.6% full-task success, and Skild has reportedly not yet run S1 on an actual humanoid robot. Separately, Figure announced its "Index" data initiative: 264,000 app downloads across 108 countries, 44,000+ weekly active users, over 16 million videos uploaded, an ingest rate of about 30 minutes of video per second (equating to roughly 4.9 years of human work uploaded daily), $15 million paid to data contributors so far with a stated goal of 100x that, and a commitment to spend over $1 billion on data and compute in the next year (split undisclosed). Figure said outside data vendors couldn't meet its quality/throughput needs, prompting it to build its own pipeline covering tasks like cooking, cleaning, laundry, logistics, and office work; the host notes not all uploaded video makes it into training after fraud/duplicate filtering.
