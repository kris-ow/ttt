This video is a pre-show and live-reaction coverage of SpaceX's Q3 2026 earnings call, featuring a panel of SpaceX-focused analysts and commentators discussing financial results, AI/compute buildout projections, Starship/Starlink progress, and Elon Musk's remarks on a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger. The core thesis from the panel is that SpaceX's "super intelligence" (SI) compute business is inflecting dramatically faster than consensus expected, with guidance implying annual recurring revenue potentially reaching $500 billion to $1 trillion by the end of 2027.

## Financials
1. **Q3 2026 results beat expectations across the board:** SpaceX reported Q3 revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% year-over-year; net income of $4.4 billion (versus a net loss in the prior-year period); and adjusted EBITDA of $8.8 billion, up 306% year-over-year. Analyst Larry Goldberg noted the company beat his high-case estimates on nearly every line item, with diluted EPS of $0.35 against his high case of $0.22 and consensus of $0.13. Capex was $19.6 billion and free cash flow was roughly -$10 billion; cash plus marketable securities ended the quarter at $89.9 billion.
2. **SI segment overtakes connectivity as largest business:** SI (super intelligence/compute) revenue reached $7.6 billion, up $5 billion sequentially and $6.7 billion year-over-year, driven by cloud services agreements; SI adjusted EBITDA was $5 billion. CFO Brett Johnson said compute deployments are currently generating payback in less than a year, with pricing tracking slightly above the $30–$50 per watt range discussed last quarter.
3. **Backlog and forward guidance:** Total backlog stood at $63 billion as of September 30, with $17.5 billion recognized as deferred revenue and roughly $42 billion expected to be recognized within the next year. Management reaffirmed a goal of $100 billion ARR by end of 2026 and gave new guidance of $33–37 billion in total company revenue for Q1 2027. Q4 capex guidance was raised to $35–40 billion.
4. **Connectivity segment growth:** Connectivity revenue was $5.2 billion, up 69% year-over-year and 22% sequentially; Starlink consumer ARPU rose 5% sequentially to $69/month. Enterprise and government revenue grew 91% year-over-year, driven by Star Shield and aviation.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Elon Musk raised compute capacity targets:** Musk said SpaceX is on track for roughly 2.5–3 gigawatts of nameplate compute by year-end (above prior ~2GW guidance), expects to be "highly confident" of reaching 10 incremental gigawatts in 2027 (13GW total), and said there is a "non-zero" — though still small — probability of exceeding 20 gigawatts of SI compute online next year, contingent on power/cooling infrastructure.
2. **Casting capability as a competitive moat:** Musk said SpaceX's advanced metal-casting expertise (developed for rocket engines) is being applied to manufacture turbine blades and vanes, which he said are typically a 12–18 month bottleneck for turbine suppliers industry-wide — giving SpaceX a claimed 12–18 month power-deployment advantage over competitors.
3. **Compute monetization strategy:** Musk said SpaceX aims for roughly a 50/50 split between internally-used and externally-rented compute, favors short-term/spot-market contracts over long-term deals on the belief that useful intelligence per watt (and thus compute value) will keep rising, and said the company is "hounded relentlessly" by hyperscalers and smaller companies for more compute capacity.
4. **Grok/Grokbot and Cursor momentum:** Musk said Grokbot is growing faster than any product he's seen, with usage per user increasing rather than declining over time; the company reported 800,000 paid weekly Grokbot users (more than doubling in the last month) and said enterprise customers with over $1M run-rate revenue (including Cursor) grew 50% quarter-over-quarter. SpaceX plans a training cluster of 440,000 Vera Rubin compute units online by end of Q2 2027, which Musk said should make Grok "extremely competitive," if not necessarily the best model yet.
5. **Panel analysis of data center buildout pace:** Independent analyst Brian (panelist) presented analysis suggesting SpaceX is completing roughly one "mini-hard" (mini data center) data center build per month, with a leaked report cited (unconfirmed) suggesting Texas and Tennessee/Mississippi sites could host roughly 4GW of grid power, 4GW of behind-the-meter natural gas, and solar/battery capacity. He speculated full build-out could reach a trillion-dollar run rate by late next year if fully monetized with Vera Rubin chips, though he flagged this as highly dependent on execution and chip economics.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 reached orbit; Flight 15 to attempt full recovery:** Musk said Starship Flight 14 reached orbit for the first time and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites. Flight 15, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and ship — which, if successful, would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle. Musk called this "more likely than not" to succeed but not guaranteed.
2. **Louisiana spaceport announced:** SpaceX is developing a new Starship spaceport in Louisiana, which Musk said could eventually feature "on the order of 20 or more" launch towers; the company's internal goal is a first launch from the site in two years (2028), with COO Gwynne Shotwell saying 2029 is "highly likely" for launching at scale.
3. **Cadence targets:** Management expects Starship to fly weekly in the first half of next year and twice-weekly or more by the end of next year as reusability improves.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and bandwidth growth:** Starlink added 1.6 million consumer subscribers in Q3, reaching just under 14 million global broadband consumer subscribers (13.6 million per the earnings deck). Starlink Mobile surpassed 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter. The company is active in 174 markets.
2. **Aviation and enterprise expansion:** Starlink is now contracted for roughly 12,000 planes (nearly a quarter of available aircraft), with agreements signed with three of four major US carriers; aviation production rate has quadrupled since the start of the year and is expected to double again by year-end.
3. **Mobile/spectrum expansion:** SpaceX received FCC approval to launch 15,000 mobile V2 satellites and announced a low-band spectrum acquisition intended to support Starlink Mobile becoming a major US mobile carrier, with service targeted for early 2028.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Q3 launch cadence:** SpaceX flew 35 launches in Q3, including 21 Starlink Falcon 9 missions, two Starship flights, a Falcon Heavy (carrying NASA's Roman Space Telescope), and 12 other customer missions, including six national security launches. The company signed 18 new National Security Space Launch missions and three additional Dragon human spaceflight missions extending through 2028.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger process confirmed as ongoing but undecided:** Musk confirmed that special committees of independent directors have been formed at both SpaceX and Tesla to evaluate a potential merger, but said he cannot participate in their discussions due to his conflict of interest, and that the committees "have not reached agreement on the terms of any transaction." He said a Tesla shareholder vote would require support from a majority of all shares outstanding (not just those voting), calling that "a high hurdle." Panelists characterized this as confirming the process has begun but is likely a year-long process, with no announcement expected this year.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Panelist expects Optimus repositioning within merger talks:** One panelist (Larry) argued that as Tesla's robotaxi fleet scales (he expects by mid-2027) and Optimus matures, Tesla will shift from being perceived as the "stepchild" of a potential merger to a company with its own massive cash-flow and optionality — though this is the panelist's own forward-looking view, not a company statement.
