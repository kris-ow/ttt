## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first lockup expiration passed quietly: stock closed up ~5% despite a hyped 900M-share overhang, with panelists on the brighterwithherbert roundtable crediting heavy pre-positioning via options and employees reportedly holding rather than selling after the earnings call.
- Institutional price targets mostly exceed $200 (Wolf Research low at $175, Raymond James high at $800), contrasting with more bearish retail sentiment and a reported ~34% short interest as a percentage of float.
- Merger sentiment has flipped: a poll cited by panelist Alexandra Merz showed over 50% of 813 respondents favoring an immediate Tesla-SpaceX merger, versus heavy pushback weeks earlier. Merz modeled a ~4.2-to-1 exchange ratio with a suggested Tesla share-price floor of at least $600, funded via SpaceX share dilution if needed, with a possible shareholder vote targeted around November — her own analysis, not confirmed.
- Cash and capex comparison: Tesla holds ~$43.5B cash vs. SpaceX's ~$100B post-IPO; Tesla's H1 capex was $8.3B ($25B full-year guide) vs. SpaceX's $18B in a single quarter.

## Financials
- SpaceX Q2 (first as a public company): total revenue $7.8B, adjusted EBITDA ~$3.5B, total capex $18.3B ($15.8B AI-side). Space division revenue ~$0.9B (op. loss ~$0.5B); Starlink/connectivity ~$4.2–4.3B (op. income $1.66B); AI ~$2.5B (op. loss $1.2B, adj. EBITDA +$1.15B).
- Musk says SpaceX is on track for $100B+ annualized run rate by December — call it a floor, since it's "what we'd achieve if we basically did nothing" — and that internal projections for $1 trillion in revenue moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029.
- Only ~28% of the $100B ARR target is tied to named customers (Anthropic ~15%, Alphabet ~11%, Reflection AI ~2%); the remaining ~72% (~$6B/month) is unannounced, with some additional deals reportedly signed in early Q3 for disclosure around October's earnings.
- SpaceX's CFO called AI compute capex "almost bucket-able as COGS" given sub-one-year payback, distinct from traditional decades-long infrastructure spend — a framing panelists say the market hasn't priced in.
- Terafab's leaked $16.8B initial-phase investment figure (per Sawyer Merritt) is the confirmed number; a separately reported $119B figure was called out by panelists as implausibly low relative to the facility's scale, without resolution.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Compute buildout: SpaceX expects to exit 2026 with 2+ gigawatts online, scaling toward ~10GW by end of 2027 (Musk floated a tentative 20GW target for power/cooling by end of next year, which panelists discounted toward 6–15GW as more realistic).
- Named compute sites (Colossus ~340MW, Colossus 2 ~1GW, two Macrohard-style builds ~350MW each) account for well under a quarter of the ~8–10GW 2027 target, implying many unannounced sites still needed.
- Musk said compute cancellation clauses in Anthropic/Alphabet contracts were requested by SpaceX itself, preserving optionality to redirect capacity to internal uses (Grok, Optimus inference) if external demand or pricing shifts; only ~10% of compute is currently earmarked for training.
- Memory, not chips, is the binding constraint per Musk: output growing ~20%/year against demand growing 200%+/year.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk said SpaceX could attempt catching the ship (second stage) as soon as the next flight, aiming to catch both stages of the upcoming Starlink V3 mission this year. Flight 13's booster was lost but the ship splashed down intact with heat-shield tiles holding up despite a harder ascent profile.
- Musk said Falcon will deliver 80–90% of all mass sent to Earth orbit this year (~2,500 tons), with a long-term ambition to scale to 1–10 million tons/year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said it's plausible Starlink delivers a majority of internet traffic within under 10 years in markets where it operates, aided by major bandwidth gains from V3 satellites (~20 deployed suborbitally on the recent Starship flight).
- Shotwell described a "femtocell" strategy — rooftop Starlink-connected stations for mobile connectivity without heavy spectrum capex — which Musk said could beat existing cellular bandwidth.
- Airline anecdotes (relayed by Shotwell and Andrew Ross Sorkin) describe passengers choosing flights specifically for Starlink connectivity, though no revenue or subscriber figures were disclosed.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla admitted pre-2026 Cybertruck power conversion system (PCS) electronics "do not meet our reliability standards" and extended warranty coverage to 8 years/150,000 miles; the 2026 Cybertruck's redesigned PCS carries a 7-year/70,000-mile warranty. Owners who already paid for replacements will be reimbursed, and a software update now allows Supercharging even after PCS failure.
- Electrek's July owner poll found 91 of 223 Cybertrucks (~41%) needed a PCS replacement; out-of-warranty repairs ran $5,000–$7,200 before Tesla informally discounted them to ~$1,000. Electrek's Fred Lambert credits the fix but faults Tesla for acting only after public pressure.
- Ford announced its "Fathom" midsize EV truck starting at $28,350, pre-orders early 2027, deliveries late 2027 — a future competitor to Tesla's lower-price EV lineup.

## Robotaxi
- Zoox (Amazon) began paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas, priced at a "comfort tier" 20–40% above standard ride-hailing, under an NHTSA exemption capped at 5,000 vehicles over two years. A Futureaza guest argued Tesla deliberately avoided a similar capped exemption, instead pursuing broader regulatory approval to scale Cybercab beyond that limit — calling it a bigger near-term risk but larger long-term upside.
- Tesla's robotaxis were shown testing unsupervised at night in Austin (~9 PM local), an incremental expansion of operating conditions.
- A ride-quality comparison (one rider's own account) ranked Tesla's robotaxi as most human-like, Waymo more robotic, Zoox most robotic. None of the three companies' robotaxi economics were shown to be clearly profitable; Tesla bundles robotaxi costs into broader expenses with no standalone visibility.

## Humanoid Bots
- A podcast host laid out a thesis (his own framework) that Optimus's "mind" will live in SpaceX's Starmine compute network, delivered via Starlink with ~15ms round-trip latency, positioning the robot as a thin edge device rather than a standalone computer — floating a shared/leased Optimus model as an alternative to individual ownership.

## Energy
- Tesla began Megapack 3 production at its Texas Megafactory (5 MWh per unit, ~86,000 lbs, 28-foot enclosure), its third operational Megafactory globally. A separate $125M, 250MW/500MWh Tesla Megapack facility in Needville, Texas went live on ERCOT, co-located with a 430MW solar project.

## Market & Competition
- DeepSeek released V4 Flash, priced at $0.14/$0.28 per million input/output tokens — an 18x–100x cost discount to Claude/GPT-class models — with a speaker arguing cheap, high-volume "agentic" AI could outcompete costlier frontier models; no substantive Tesla/SpaceX linkage was made beyond a passing mention.
- China has roughly 100 companies holding robotaxi permits with regulators now imposing a moratorium on new ones, per a Futureaza guest, who does not expect significant new US robotaxi entrants in the next couple of years given capital requirements and China's market exclusion.
