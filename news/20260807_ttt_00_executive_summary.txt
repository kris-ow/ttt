## Brief

- Terafab: SpaceX-Tesla $16.8B Texas chip fab confirmed by Tesla
- Tesla extends Cybertruck PCS warranty to 8 years after defect admission
- SpaceX targets $100B ARR by December, floor not ceiling
- Zoox launches paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas
- Tesla robotaxi tests unsupervised driving at night in Austin
- Merger sentiment poll flips to over 50% favoring immediate deal

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first post-IPO lockup expiration was a non-event: stock closed up ~5% despite media hype around a 900M-share unlock, per brighterwithherbert panel, who attributed the muted move to heavy pre-positioning via options.
- Institutional price targets skew bullish (mostly above $200, Raymond James high at $800, Wolf Research low at $175) versus more bearish retail chatter and reported ~34% short interest as a percentage of float.
- A poll cited by panelist Alexandra Merz found over 50% of respondents (813 votes) now favor an immediate Tesla-SpaceX merger, under 8% opposed — a reversal from prior weeks. Merz proposed an illustrative ~4.2-to-1 exchange ratio with a Tesla share price floor of at least $600, funded via SpaceX share dilution rather than cash, with a shareholder vote potentially targeted around November; this remains her own speculation, not confirmed.
- Merz noted Tesla cash (~$43.5B) trails SpaceX's (~$100B post-IPO), and Tesla's H1 capex ($8.3B) is dwarfed by SpaceX's single-quarter capex ($18B).

## Financials
- SpaceX's Q2 results as a public company: total revenue $7.8B, adjusted EBITDA ~$3.5B, total capex $18.3B (of which $15.8B was AI-side). Space division revenue ~$0.9B (op. loss ~$0.5B); Connectivity ~$4.2–4.3B (op. income $1.66B); AI ~$2.5B (op. loss $1.2B, adj. EBITDA +$1.15B).
- SpaceX's CFO said AI compute capex pays back in under a year, calling it "almost bucket-able as COGS" versus traditional decades-long infrastructure capex.
- Musk said SpaceX expects to exceed $100B ARR by December — which he later clarified is a floor, "what we'd achieve if we basically did nothing" — and that internal projections for $1 trillion in revenue moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029. Disclosed named customers (Anthropic, Alphabet, Reflection AI) cover only ~25-28% of the $100B target; the rest is unannounced.
- Terafab, the joint SpaceX-Tesla chip fab in Grimes County, Texas, carries an initial-phase investment of $16.8 billion (Tesla's own figure) — different from a $119B figure reported earlier in the week by other sources, which panelists called implausibly small versus the facility's apparent scale; the discrepancy remains unresolved. Facility will employ at least 3,000 people, with future expansion phases pushing investment higher.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX said it will end this year with over 2 gigawatts of compute, with a tentative target (per Musk) of 20 gigawatts of power/cooling capacity by end of next year — though panelists flagged this as hedged, suggesting 15GW or even 6–8GW as more realistic guard-banded outcomes.
- Named compute sites (Colossus ~340MW, Colossus 2 ~1GW, two Macrohard-related builds ~350MW each) account for well under a quarter of the ~8-10GW 2027 target, leaving most of the buildout unannounced.
- Musk said memory, not chips, is the binding constraint on AI growth, with output growing ~20%/year versus demand growing 200%+/year.
- Musk framed cancellation clauses in Anthropic/Alphabet compute contracts as a hedge SpaceX itself requested, preserving optionality to redirect compute to internal uses like Grok or Optimus inference.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk said SpaceX could attempt a ship-catch as soon as the next Starship flight, with plans to catch both stages of a Starlink V3-carrying Starship this year. Flight 13's booster was lost but the ship reportedly splashed down intact and is being towed for inspection; heat-shield tiles held up well despite a harder ascent.
- Musk said Falcon will deliver 80-90% of total Earth mass to orbit this year (~2,500 tons), with long-term ambitions of scaling to 1-10 million tons/year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said Starlink could plausibly carry a majority of the world's internet traffic within under 10 years in permitted markets, aided by bandwidth gains from new V3 satellites.
- Gwynne Shotwell relayed an airline reporting passengers choosing shorter connecting flights to guarantee a Starlink-equipped aircraft — an anecdote the airline said it had never seen before.

## Robotaxi
- Zoox began its first paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas, priced roughly 20-40% above standard ride-hailing (comfort tier), operating under an NHTSA safety-standard exemption capped at 5,000 vehicles over two years. A Futureaza guest argued Tesla deliberately avoided a similar capped exemption, instead pushing for broader regulatory approval to scale Cybercab beyond that ceiling.
- Robotaxi economics remain opaque across all three players; a Futureaza guest assumes Tesla's robotaxi unit isn't yet breaking even given fleet size versus infrastructure costs, and questioned whether Zoox can reach profitability given low-volume, hand-built production.
- Tesla's unsupervised Model Y robotaxis were shown operating at night in Austin (~9 PM local), described as incremental expansion of operating conditions beyond daytime.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla admitted the power conversion system (PCS) in pre-2026 Cybertrucks "do not meet our reliability standards" and extended warranty coverage to 8 years/150,000 miles, with the redesigned 2026 Cybertruck PCS covered for 7 years/70,000 miles. Owners who already paid for replacements will be reimbursed, and a software fix now allows Supercharging even after PCS failure.
- Electrek's July owner poll found 91 of 223 Cybertrucks (~41%) needed a PCS replacement; out-of-warranty repairs ran $5,000-$7,200. Fred Lambert (Electrek) credits the fix but criticizes Tesla for acting only after owner and press pressure rather than proactively.

## Humanoid Bots
- A CernBasher host laid out a speculative thesis that Optimus's "intelligence" will live persistently in SpaceX's Starmine compute network, delivered via Starlink, with the physical robot as a thin edge device (~15ms round-trip latency) — his own framework, not a company statement. The same source floated a shared/leased Optimus model (delivered by Cybercab, billed hourly) as an alternative to individual ownership.

## Market & Competition
- Panelists on brighterwithherbert flagged that a Tesla robotaxi safety incident could carry greater reputational risk than a SpaceX rocket failure, given Tesla's consumer-facing exposure versus SpaceX's more insulated government/B2B customer base.
- DeepSeek released "V4 Flash," claimed to cut cost-per-task by up to 99% versus frontier models at $0.14/$0.28 per million input/output tokens — a speaker's framing that cheap, high-volume AI agents may outcompete costlier frontier models; only a passing, unsubstantiated mention connected this to Grok or SpaceX.
- Ford announced a new midsize EV truck ("Fathom") starting at $28,350, with deliveries in late 2027 — a future competitive entrant against Tesla's lower-price lineup.
