## Robotaxi
- Tesla Robotaxi expanded to 24/7 operation across all eight active service cities (Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) this week.
- San Antonio launched as the eighth robotaxi city, with hosts on the Tesla Beat podcast describing a roughly 275-square-mile service zone; airports are excluded pending separate port-authority rules.
- The Tesla Beat podcast cited fleet figures of roughly 1,300 Tesla robotaxi vehicles (729 Model Y, 573 Cybercab) versus Waymo's roughly 1,297 (874 Jaguar, 423 Zeekr) — materially different from RobotaxiTracker.com's stale counts, so attributed to the show rather than the tracker.
- Florida's Tampa/Orlando geofences remain comparatively small despite easier road geography; panelists on Herbert Ong's show attributed this to operational readiness rather than software limits.
- One panelist flagged that Tesla had not added Cybercabs to Texas registrations since around October 27–28, though another host doubted any NHTSA-driven restriction and suggested new-city Model Y launches as a likelier explanation.
- Robotaxi-related hiring spans 42 U.S. cities, led by Texas with 14, per a jobs-tracking account cited on Herbert Ong's show.
- Europe: Finland reportedly became the 11th EU/European country to approve robotaxi-related rules, per one Tesla Beat host; roughly 15 of 27 countries are seen as needed for ~65% EU population coverage.
- California robotaxi launch remains unfiled, with Elon Musk previously saying service there is unlikely before roughly mid-2027.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's US configurator now offers only the $109,990 tri-motor Cyberbeast after Dual Motor ($82,490) and Premium AWD ($92,490) trims were greyed out; the Cyberbeast price rose from $99,990 to $102,490 to $109,990 in about a week, a 10% jump and 57% above the $69,990 cheapest Cybertruck available in early August, per Electrek's Fred Lambert. No new Cybertrucks were found in Tesla's inventory within 200 miles of Los Angeles at check time.
- Lambert estimates Q3 Cybertruck deliveries at 7,000–8,000 against installed capacity he puts above 125,000 units/year, and frames the price hikes and trim cuts as a deliberate wind-down monetizing a self-created "backlog" narrative — an editorial view, not confirmed by Tesla.
- Separately, Tesla Beat hosts put the production cutoff at end of August with an added factory shift, and reported an unverified acquisition offer for the Cybertruck line from Unplugged Performance, which hosts were skeptical could succeed given Tesla's casting-machine capacity and shared 4680 cell constraints across Cybertruck, Optimus, Semi, and Model Y.
- Germany Tesla registrations reportedly up 291% year-over-year, with a host-cited (self-flagged as possibly high) year-to-date figure of ~47,000 units; Giga Berlin is reportedly still ramping shifts to cut wait times.

## Humanoid Bots
- Elon Musk said at a Barron's conference that Optimus will "probably" be rented rather than sold, at least initially in major metros, paralleling planned rental models for Cybercab and Semi.

## Financials
- Bloomberg (Ryan Gold, Siddhartha Philip, Dinesh Nair) reports Tesla-SpaceX merger talks are progressing, with an announcement possibly in December or slipping to early January 2027; terms are still reportedly being finalized. One panelist speculated the timing could align with Tesla's December 3 shareholder meeting or, more likely, shortly after SpaceX's early-investor lock-up expiration (estimated around December 8–12, unconfirmed).
- A SpaceX board member representing Google, Don Harrison, recently left SpaceX's board; one commentator speculated — explicitly as her own inference — this reflects growing conflicts of interest as SpaceX, Tesla, and Google/Waymo compete, and tied it to merger speculation.
- TSLA traded near $397 on November 12, up from roughly $390 intraday the prior session; SPCX was up roughly 5% over the trailing week per the Tesla Beat hosts.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX announced plans to invest more than $74 billion in two AI supercomputing campuses: a $24 billion facility near Starbase, Texas, and a $50 billion, up-to-6-GW, 9-building campus in Cochise County, Arizona, with Arizona construction starting in 2027. SpaceX's own materials claim combined projects create more than 10,000 construction jobs, with the Arizona site alone cited at 6,500 jobs (1,000 permanent), up to $103M/year in new property tax revenue, and a projected 10–40% cut in local electricity bills.
- The Arizona campus plan includes 24 GWh of grid-connected battery storage; a poster's own calculation (not a SpaceX-stated figure) puts this at over $5 billion in potential Tesla Megapack purchases, contingent on the project moving forward.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 14 delivered 26 Starlink V3 satellites, adding downlink capacity roughly equal to 10 Falcon 9 Starlink launches.
- A Starship "double catch" test flight is scheduled, with the Super Heavy booster catch expected within minutes of liftoff and the Starship upper stage returning after about 22 hours; FAA final sign-off was still pending as of recording. A successful double catch would mark Starship's first full-reusability milestone.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas wrote that the market does not fully appreciate the significance of a successful booster-and-ship catch on Flight 15, calling it a likely material positive for SPCX stock and an even bigger positive for the underlying business.
- SpaceX also disclosed a planned Starbase site in Louisiana, targeting first launch in 2029.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Elon Musk predicted at SpaceX's October earnings conference that bandwidth demand could grow a thousandfold in three to five years, driven by AI agents; one host cautioned this shouldn't be read as implying thousandfold growth in Starlink satellite traffic specifically, since much agent traffic stays within data centers.
- Starlink subscribers reached 13.6 million at the end of September 2026, up from 7.2 million a year earlier, with corporate and government revenue also growing per the company's latest results.

## Market & Competition
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned effective immediately amid personal-conduct allegations (one panelist's characterization as involving a "possibly a Chinese spy" was explicitly framed as speculation); CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO. Elon Musk said in response to a social media post that it "would be an honor" to hire Huang, which multiple sources stressed is speculation, not a confirmed plan.
- Panelists on Herbert Ong's show argued Tesla and SpaceX are largely insulated near-term given existing multi-year Nvidia chip allocations, contrasting them with neo-cloud/LLM providers reliant on vendor-financing structures; Oracle was named as more exposed.
- One commentator argued Nvidia's Delaware incorporation and Huang's low (~3.5%) voting stake leave it exposed to shareholder derivative suits, contrasting this with Tesla's post-reincorporation shareholder structure, and cited Palantir's founder-control share class as a precedent.
