The Monday news roundup leads with the FSD v15 rollout and a record Cybercab registration day in Texas. It then covers Tesla's solar and energy moves, EV adoption data, and SpaceX's AI-chip financing and its challenge to telecom carriers. The host's thesis is that Tesla's autonomy and energy businesses keep advancing while incumbents and policy headwinds lag. He also notes the market's moves seem disconnected from fundamentals.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v15 reaches first customers:** The host says v15 is out and cites his own video on the new visualizations. He also cites a drive-through of Los Angeles on Mad Max mode by Kyle Conner, which he says tests far more than the visualizations. He says only Model Y Juniper cars with the bumper camera appear to be in the first wave, and he frames that as his own read of the rollout.
2. **Waymo California setback:** The host says the California DMV banned Waymo, a story he describes as almost a week old. He argues Waymo needs a better "brain" because its training data is concentrated on surface streets rather than highways, and he doubts claims of billions of miles.

## Robotaxi
1. **Record Cybercab registration day:** Tesla registered 200 Cybercabs to its Texas robotaxi fleet in a single day, about four days before the video. That pushed the Texas total past 750. The host says the fleet now runs 24/7 in many locations and that anyone in a registered area will see the cars everywhere. A viewer comment says expanded service area matters more to them than the registration count, and the host agrees.

## Energy
1. **Solid-state power unit:** The host highlights a new Tesla solid-state unit that puts transformers and inverters in one box with no oil. He says it makes sites faster to deploy and keeps quality in-house rather than relying on separately purchased pieces and third-party installers.
2. **$3.1B polysilicon refinery:** Tesla plans a $3.1B polysilicon refinery in Nueces County, Texas, making ultra-high-purity polysilicon for high-efficiency solar cells. The host presents it as vertical integration of the solar supply chain, driven by an inability to source the material at the needed price, quantity and quality.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Global EV share at a record:** EVs reached a record 31% of global passenger vehicle sales, with Europe driving growth. The host says China's overall auto sales are dipping while EV share keeps rising. A headline he cites projects China's EV adoption topping 70%, and he notes that figures often include plug-in hybrids.
2. **US EV sales weak:** Cox Automotive data shows October US EV sales up only 3% year over year. The host calls this very bad, since last October was the post-credit hangover. Used EV sales are doing very well, which he partly attributes to expiring leases.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **First Starship catch:** The host notes SpaceX successfully caught a Starship for the first time, yet SpaceX shares were still down over the five-day window. He calls the market irrational.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **$32B AI-chip debt sale:** SpaceX sold $32B of AI-chip debt at yields topping 8% to buy more chips. The structure is designed to keep it off the balance sheet and may become a model for similar financings. The host says a commenter's point that 8% is not high given a possible 100% return on investment in a year is fair.
2. **Lenders want chips kept on the ground:** Lenders aim to bar SpaceX from launching the roughly $40B of chips, since they could not repossess hardware in orbit. The host calls this a fair point he had not considered.
3. **Google picks Blue Origin for orbital AI:** Google selected Blue Origin's New Glenn to launch AI computing satellites in 2029 under Project Suncatcher. The host says Blue Origin is good at winning contracts but less proven at delivering on them.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Verizon CEO vs. SpaceX:** Verizon's CEO says SpaceX would need over $300B to compete in mobile service. The host disputes this, saying SpaceX already has the bandwidth and only needs antennas it can launch. He argues carriers have no structural cost advantage, since SpaceX avoids having to carpet the planet in fiber. He is unsure whether SpaceX will sell direct to consumers or to other providers.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia leadership reversal:** Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, though not chairman, effective immediately. The board cited input from senior US government levels that his return poses no national security risk. The host notes the stock is up about 6% over the past week but still down on the month, six-month and year-to-date views.
