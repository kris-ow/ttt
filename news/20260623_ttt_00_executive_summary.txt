## Autonomous Driving

- **Fatal Texas crash — FSD was engaged:** Tesla's Head of AI Ashok Elluswamy confirmed that FSD was active when a Model 3 in Katy, Texas reached 73 mph in a residential area and struck a home, killing a 76-year-old woman. Elluswamy's account holds that the driver manually overrode the system by pressing the accelerator to 100%, with the pedal remaining floored after impact. NHTSA has opened a Special Crash Investigation to independently pull vehicle logs; a separate Engineering Analysis already covers ~3.2 million vehicles.

- **The complacency counter-argument:** Electrek argues Tesla's "driver overrode it" framing does not settle the question. The most plausible failure mode, per the article, is that FSD did something unexpected, prompting an emergency intervention in which the driver — feet off the pedals because the system was driving — hit the accelerator instead of the brake. The Florida precedent is relevant: a federal jury in August 2025 held Tesla 33% liable for a 2019 Autopilot crash, resulting in a $243 million judgment upheld in February 2026, on the grounds that Tesla's marketing fostered driver complacency.

- **FSD V14.3.4 — zero disengagements in NYC:** Sawyer Merritt drove throughout New York City on FSD V14.3.4 with zero disengagements, including a self-driven trip from New Haven to New York City. Behaviors noted include stopping for a child on a scooter entering the street and navigating tight urban spaces. NYC represents one of the most challenging validation environments for the system.

- **Spain approval expected ~June 30; UK timeline late 2026 at best:** FSD version 14.2.2.6 reportedly shows Spain and the UK in the software. A Tesla testing fleet in Spain has grown from 19 to ~30 vehicles and driven ~80,000 km since November 2025; employees cited by brighterwithherbert expected regulatory approval around June 30, 2026 (unconfirmed). The UK is assessed by Tesla Jigsaw's Will as "the last domino to fall" — late 2026 or early 2027 at best — due to post-Brexit type approval requirements and Tesla apparently prioritizing EU momentum first via the Dutch RDW framework.

- **Australia and New Zealand on FSD V14:** Both right-hand-drive markets received FSD version 14 on June 19, 2026. FutureAzA's Brian speculates that right-hand-drive operational data from those markets may benefit the UK approval process.

- **UK FSD subscription currently delivers only Enhanced Autopilot-equivalent features:** Will from Tesla Jigsaw tested the UK FSD subscription and found it provides only summon, navigate on autopilot, and lane changes — hobbled by regulation requiring a driver steering-wheel touch within 3 seconds during any system-initiated lane change or the maneuver aborts. He canceled his subscription and called the constraint dangerous, attributing it to regulation rather than Tesla's system design.

- **UK Department for Transport AV safety consultation underway:** A consultation on draft safety principles for automated vehicles opened June 17, 2026, with a closing date of September 9, 2026. These principles will feed into permanent UK rules covering supervised FSD, unsupervised FSD, and robotaxi operations.

## Robotaxi

- **Austin fleet transitions to fully unsupervised on one-year anniversary:** Sawyer Merritt noted that Tesla's Austin robotaxi fleet has transitioned to unsupervised operation, coinciding with the one-year anniversary of Tesla's first robotaxi rides. This is a qualitative milestone reflecting Tesla's internal confidence threshold for full driverless operation in that market. The active robotaxi fleet stands at 739 vehicles across Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston per RobotaxiTracker.com as of 2026-06-23.

- **Largest Cybercab formation yet spotted at Giga Texas:** Joe Tegtmeyer captured approximately 10 Cybercabs testing together in formation on what appears to be a newly built test track at Giga Texas — the largest group he has observed on the track at any one time.

- **Cybercab lots growing in multiple cities:** Per brighterwithherbert, ground reporters on X documented Cybercab counts in Houston and/or Dallas parking lots climbing from ~45 to ~55 units (unconfirmed, single source). Jo Bhakdi estimates Tesla's Cybercab production run rate at approximately 1,000 units per month (his own unverified figure) and argues that distributing 50–60 units across distributed city lots is unambiguous preparation for commercial deployment, not testing. He stated that a source told him Cybercabs will not be sold to individual consumers — consistent with a fleet-only robotaxi model — and noted a viewer comment suggesting a commercial announcement by June 30, 2026, though Bhakdi did not personally confirm that date.

- **Four months without at-fault incidents:** Brighterwithherbert cites Tesla robotaxi safety data showing four months without any at-fault incidents, which the hosts argue supports the case for expanded deployment.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus factory steel structure rising at Giga Texas:** Drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer shows ongoing steel assembly at Tesla's dedicated Optimus factory at Giga Texas, with a structure appearing to span at least four floors. Substantive completion is targeted for late 2027 per Tegtmeyer. The facility is planned for a target capacity of 10 million units per year at full ramp, per brighterwithherbert.

- **New supplier deals for Optimus 3 mass production:** Per Taiwan Economic Daily, cited by brighterwithherbert, Tesla has signed agreements with Merl Automation (harmonic reducers, joint modules — with a new joint venture in Thailand) and Asia Optical (spherical and aspherical lens components for the robot's vision system). These parts are slated to enter mass production in H2 2026 and into 2027. The scale of Tesla's orders is large enough that suppliers are establishing new joint ventures and factories.

- **Jo Bhakdi on Optimus scale strategy:** Bhakdi argues Elon Musk's key insight with Optimus is the same as with xAI's Colossus compute: bet on manufacturing scale rather than guaranteeing best-in-class underlying technology. His thesis is that the world's largest humanoid robot factory will eventually force any technologically superior competitor to partner with or be acquired by Tesla.

## Energy

- **Tesla–NatPower: $4–5B deal for 25 GWh of Megapack:** Tesla and UK-based NatPower have signed a multiyear agreement covering five projects totaling 25 GWh of battery storage across Italy and the UK, with a construction cost of $4–5 billion. Tesla is also supplying its energy trading software for grid arbitrage, embedding its software stack into NatPower's operations. The 25 GWh phase-one figure alone exceeds half of Tesla's entire global energy storage deployment in 2025.

- **Longer-term program ceiling: >100 GWh, >$15B over 20 years:** NatPower's stated broader program target is more than 100 GWh of total storage capacity, with revenue potentially exceeding $15 billion over 20 years. Electrek's Fred Lambert flags these as headline figures dependent on execution, permitting, and Europe's notoriously slow grid interconnection queues.

- **Context on Tesla's energy growth trajectory:** Tesla deployed a record 46.7 GWh of energy storage in 2025 (+~48% YoY). Analyst estimates peg Q1 2026 deployments at a record ~14.4 GWh.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla European registrations up 107.9% YoY in May 2026:** Per Jo Bhakdi, Tesla registered 28,610 vehicles in Europe in May 2026, up 107.9% year-over-year — four consecutive months of gains. Bhakdi flagged at least one outlet running a misleading headline suggesting a "hit to Europe sales" while the article itself reported these figures.

- **UBS raises Tesla delivery forecast to ~405,000:** A UBS estimate cited by Bhakdi bumped Tesla's delivery forecast to approximately 405,000 vehicles, up ~5% year-over-year and ~13% versus two years prior.

- **Lucid cuts 18% of U.S. workforce:** Lucid announced an 18% reduction in its U.S. workforce and the immediate departure of COO Marc Winterhoff, citing the need to align production with demand and reduce inventory — a continuing sign of stress among EV challengers.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla closes down ~5.8%; macro context:** Tesla closed down approximately 5.79% on June 23, against a NASDAQ down ~2.1% and S&P 500 down ~1.38%. Jo Bhakdi attributes the majority of Tesla's decline to misleading media headlines on the Texas crash and a NASDAQ-wide selloff driven by the South Korean KOSPI falling ~10% in a single session (the KOSPI was up ~90% year-to-date in 2026). Bhakdi assesses less than half a percent of Tesla's drop as attributable to the crash headlines directly.

- **SpaceX–Reflection AI compute deal: ~$150M/month, up to $6.3B through 2029:** SpaceX has signed an AI compute lease with Reflection AI giving it access to Colossus 2 infrastructure and NVIDIA GB300 chips at approximately $150 million per month starting July 1, 2026. Either side can terminate with 90 days' notice after the first three months, meaning the $6.3B headline figure is not guaranteed revenue. SpaceX has now signed compute agreements with Anthropic, Google, and Cursor in addition to Reflection.

- **SpaceX lockup expiration flagged as Tesla tailwind:** Bhakdi warns that approximately 400% of SpaceX's current float will come to market between August 11 and November 2026 as lockup periods expire, creating significant selling pressure on SpaceX. He states he will not take a SpaceX position until November at earliest and believes this dynamic has been a persistent headwind for Tesla as investors rotate into SpaceX.

- **Waymo targeting London public robotaxi rides by September 2026:** Per Tesla Jigsaw's Will, Waymo is testing in London under the UK's Autonomous Vehicles Act of 2024 and was targeting public robotaxi rides by September 2026 (Will flagged this may have changed). This operates under a different regulatory framework than Tesla's supervised FSD, which requires separate type approval.
