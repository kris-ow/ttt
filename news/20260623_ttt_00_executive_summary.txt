## Autonomous Driving

- **Fatal Texas crash — FSD was engaged:** Tesla VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy confirmed that FSD was active when driver Michael Butler pressed the accelerator to 100%, reached 73 mph in a residential neighborhood in Katy, Texas, and struck a home, killing 76-year-old Martha Avila Mantilla. Elon Musk had initially posted that "FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets," a framing his own company's data then contradicted. NHTSA has opened a Special Crash Investigation and maintains a separate Engineering Analysis covering ~3.2 million vehicles.

- **The complacency counterargument:** Electrek argues Tesla's "driver overrode it" defense doesn't constitute exoneration — the most plausible scenario is that FSD behaved unexpectedly, triggering an out-of-the-loop emergency intervention where the driver hit the accelerator instead of the brake. A Florida federal jury in 2025 found Tesla 33% liable on identical complacency grounds, resulting in a $243 million judgment upheld in February 2026.

- **Media framing vs. technical record:** Sawyer Merritt explicitly called out the *New York Times* for implying Autopilot caused the crash before any data was public — a post that drew 7.6K likes. Jo Bhakdi argued the headlines are "outright lies" and potentially actionable. The hosts across multiple sources agreed this illustrates Tesla's recurring problem: the narrative sets before the technical record surfaces.

- **FSD 14.3.4 — zero disengagements in NYC:** Merritt drove throughout New York City on FSD 14.3.4 with zero disengagements, including stops for a child on a scooter and a full unsupervised trip from New Haven to New York City. A viewer in Jo Bhakdi's live session independently cited 14.3.4 as a notable capability leap.

- **Spain approval expected ~June 30; UK timeline late 2026 at earliest:** FSD version 14.2.2.6 reportedly includes Spain and UK in its software. Employees cited by the FutureAzA host expected Spanish regulatory approval around June 30, 2026; a testing fleet there has grown from 19 to ~30 vehicles and covered ~80,000 km since November 2025. The UK is further behind — no formal VCA type-approval application has been confirmed, and the most optimistic realistic timeline is late 2026 or early 2027. Australia and New Zealand received FSD version 14 on June 19, 2026.

- **UK regulatory state:** The UK Department for Transport launched a consultation on automated vehicle safety principles on June 17, 2026, closing September 9, 2026. UK customers currently paying for FSD receive only Enhanced Autopilot-equivalent features, further constrained by regulations requiring a steering-wheel touch within 3 seconds of any system-initiated lane change.

## Robotaxi

- **Austin fleet now fully unsupervised:** Sawyer Merritt marked the one-year anniversary of Tesla's first robotaxi rides and noted the Austin fleet has transitioned to fully unsupervised operation — a qualitative confidence threshold that suggests the unsupervised count tracked by RobotaxiTracker.com (last updated May 9) is now materially stale.

- **Largest Cybercab formation spotted at Giga Texas:** Joe Tegtmeyer captured ~10 Cybercabs testing together on what appears to be a newly built track at Giga Texas — the largest group he has observed there. He expressed hope they would soon be taking paying passengers.

- **Cybercab lots expanding in multiple cities:** Jo Bhakdi reports Cybercab counts in parking lots across Houston, Dallas, and other cities are climbing, with one lot growing from ~45 to ~55 units per a ground reporter on X (unconfirmed). Bhakdi estimates a production run rate of ~1,000 Cybercabs per month (his own unverified figure) and argues distributed city parking is unambiguous evidence of imminent commercial deployment. He floated June 30, 2026 as a possible commercial announcement date, though he does not personally confirm that timeline.

- **Cybercabs will not be sold to consumers:** Bhakdi says he heard from an unspecified source that Tesla plans to keep Cybercabs off the retail market, consistent with his own unit-economics modeling favoring the fleet revenue model.

- **Four months without at-fault incidents:** The brighterwithherbert host states Tesla's robotaxi safety record shows four months with no at-fault issues — cited as a foundation for expanded deployment.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus factory steel structure rising at Giga Texas:** Drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer shows steel assembly well underway, with a structure appearing to span at least four floors. Substantive completion is targeted for late 2027. The planned nameplate capacity is 10 million units per year at full ramp.

- **New supplier agreements for Optimus 3 mass production:** Per Taiwan Economic Daily, Tesla has signed deals with Merl Automation (harmonic reducers and joint modules) and Asia Optical (spherical and aspherical lens components). Merl has established a new joint venture in Thailand specifically for this production. Parts are slated for mass production in H2 2026 and into 2027.

- **Scale-as-moat framing:** Jo Bhakdi and Jeff Watts (supply chain executive, brighterwithherbert) independently made the same argument: Tesla's decisive bet is on manufacturing scale rather than guaranteed best-in-class technology — mirroring the xAI compute playbook. Watts characterized Tesla's leads in FSD and Optimus as "measured in half and full decades."

## Energy

- **Tesla–NatPower $5B Megapack deal:** Tesla and UK-based NatPower signed a multiyear agreement to deploy 25 GWh of Megapack storage across five projects in Italy and the UK, with construction costs of $4–$5 billion. Tesla is also supplying its energy trading software for grid arbitrage — embedding its software stack into NatPower's operations, which Electrek's Fred Lambert flags as the stickier, higher-margin component. The broader program targets >100 GWh and >$15 billion in revenue over 20 years. The 25 GWh phase-one figure alone exceeds half of Tesla's entire 2025 global energy storage deployment of 46.7 GWh.

## Electric Vehicles

- **European May registrations: +107.9% YoY:** Tesla posted 28,610 European registrations in May 2026, up 107.9% year-over-year — four consecutive months of gains. Jo Bhakdi flagged at least one outlet running a "hit to Europe sales" headline while the article itself reported these numbers, citing this as a contributor to retail investor confusion.

- **UBS raised delivery forecast to ~405,000:** Per an article cited by Bhakdi, UBS bumped its Tesla delivery estimate to approximately 405,000 vehicles, representing ~5% growth year-over-year and ~13% versus two years prior.

- **Lucid cutting 18% of U.S. workforce:** Lucid announced the cuts alongside the immediate departure of COO Marc Winterhoff, citing production-demand misalignment and elevated inventory — continued stress among EV challengers.

## Market & Competition

- **Tesla -5.79% on the day; macro and crash headlines both cited:** Tesla closed down ~5.79% on June 23 against a NASDAQ decline of ~2.1%. Bhakdi attributes the macro drop primarily to South Korea's KOSPI falling ~10% in a single session (though up ~90% year-to-date), which crushed Micron (-13-14%) and semiconductor names. He attributes less than half a percent of Tesla's decline to the Autopilot crash headlines — and most of the rest to the FSD narrative overhang. SpaceX closed +5.16% on the same day.

- **SpaceX IPO creating persistent Tesla selling pressure:** Bhakdi warns of significant Tesla selling pressure through November 2026, when SpaceX lockup expirations begin on August 11 and bring approximately 400% of current SpaceX float to market. He says he will not take a SpaceX position until price discovery is more complete after November.

- **Reflection AI signs $6.3B compute deal with SpaceX:** Reflection AI agreed to pay ~$150M/month for access to SpaceX's Colossus 2 infrastructure and Nvidia GB300 chips starting July 1, 2026, totaling up to $6.3B through 2029 (terminable with 90 days' notice after the first three months). SpaceX disclosed $100.88B in cash as of June 19, per an SEC filing tied to a proposed senior unsecured notes offering.
