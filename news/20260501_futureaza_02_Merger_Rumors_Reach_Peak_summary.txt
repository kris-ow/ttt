This weekly Tesla news roundup from the "futureaza" channel covers a broad range of topics including the OpenAI trial involving Elon Musk, Cybercab production updates, FSD V14 rollout plans, the Samsung chip factory ceremony in Taylor, Texas, Giga Berlin milestones, Tesla Semi production ramp, and Optimus building permits at Tesla's north campus. The hosts — joined by co-hosts Brian and Matthew — maintain a broadly bullish thesis on Tesla's autonomous driving and robotaxi future while expressing skepticism toward competitors like Waymo and GM's self-driving timeline claims.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD V14 Light Coming to Hardware 3 and International Markets:** Tesla posted on X announcing that following the future rollout of FSD V14 Light for Hardware 3 vehicles in the US, the company plans to expand V14 to additional international markets. This update is intended to ensure Hardware 3 vehicles continue receiving ongoing software updates. The hosts confirmed that as of the recording, V14 Light had not yet rolled out to Hardware 3 in the US. The international rollout is subject to regulatory approval, and Tesla stated it cannot provide definitive dates at this time.

2. **GM's 2028 Full Self-Driving Claim Met with Heavy Skepticism:** GM announced that its cars will drive themselves by 2028. The hosts were dismissive, noting that virtually every automaker and software company made similar claims targeting 2020 back in 2012–2016 — including Nissan — and none delivered. The panel argued GM has no credible path to achieve this and that the AI landscape is evolving so rapidly that whatever GM is building now will likely be obsolete by 2028.

3. **Tickets for Autonomous Vehicle Violations:** The panel discussed emerging policy around issuing traffic violations to autonomous vehicles. A recent incident involving a Waymo being part of a multi-car collision — and appearing to drive away — was cited as a key example. The hosts argued it is reasonable and necessary to hold autonomous vehicles accountable for traffic law violations, just as human drivers are held accountable.

4. **FSD on Cybertruck Through San Francisco:** The host shared a personal experience using FSD on his Cybertruck on a challenging route through downtown San Francisco, including Lombard Street, noting the system performed smoothly on steep inclines that limit the driver's own line of sight. The anecdote was used to illustrate FSD's sensory advantages over human drivers.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Is in Production:** The hosts confirmed that Cybercab production has begun, with an estimated roughly a dozen Cybercabs being produced per day currently. One host argued this already exceeds the daily rate at which Waymo is adding vehicles to its fleet. The expectation expressed is that production will scale to 100 vehicles per day and eventually 1,000 vehicles per day. Many units are reportedly rolling off the production line without a steering wheel or pedals, which the hosts interpreted as a signal that Elon Musk is confident in FSD's readiness for unsupervised public road deployment.

2. **Cybercab Individual Ownership Debate:** The panel debated whether Cybercabs should be sold to individual customers. One host argued they are too valuable to sell privately and should be kept in the fleet. Another saw a limited case for selling to individuals in remote or rural markets where Tesla has no interest in operating its own fleet — such as small hotels or rural areas — or to customers who simply want personal ownership. A third host noted that Elon Musk has publicly commented that the Cybercab would be available for sale by end of next year, with the panel suggesting that realistically means 2028 at the earliest, with a handful of private sales potentially starting in 2028–2029.

3. **Waymo's Fleet Scaling Limitations Highlighted:** The hosts argued that Waymo, despite having a larger current fleet, faces a fundamental manufacturing bottleneck: its vehicle platform (the Jaguar I-Pace) is out of production, has no finalized next design, and Waymo has no scalable pipeline to add new vehicles. By contrast, Tesla's Cybercab is purpose-built for mass production at scale.

4. **Unsupervised Robotaxi Expansion Underway:** The hosts noted that Tesla is actively launching unsupervised robotaxi operations in new cities, referencing three cities in Texas where unsupervised FSD is currently active using Model Y vehicles with Hardware 4. They acknowledged that regulatory hurdles remain significant but expressed confidence the expansion is progressing.

5. **Cruz/Uber Failures as Cautionary Backdrop:** The hosts referenced Cruise's shutdown following its incident where a vehicle dragged a pedestrian, noting the real failure was the cover-up rather than the accident itself. Uber's earlier exit from autonomous driving after a single serious incident was also cited. These were framed as cautionary examples of how fragile public trust is in this space.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Building Permits Filed at Tesla North Campus:** Joe Tegtmeyer reported that building permits have been filed in Travis County related to groundbreaking activity at Tesla's northwest campus, specifically for Optimus production facilities. The hosts expressed surprise at the speed of land preparation, noting the site appeared to have been cleared and prepped very rapidly.

2. **Neuralink Patients Control Robotic Arm:** The hosts highlighted a recent demonstration in which Neuralink patients were able to control a robotic arm, performing tasks such as putting on hats. This was framed as a significant milestone in human-machine integration, building on the freedom Neuralink has already provided to paralyzed patients.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Giga Berlin Reaches 1 Million Drive Units Built:** Giga Berlin announced it has produced 1 million drive units — a milestone the hosts celebrated as evidence of Tesla's continued global manufacturing scale, even if Giga Berlin's volumes remain well below Giga Shanghai's.

2. **Tesla Semi Production Has Officially Begun:** Tesla Semi production has commenced, with footage from Zanegler confirming the facility has moved from a dirt lot to an active manufacturing floor. The hosts noted that at a landed cost of around $300,000, electric semis make financial sense for fleet operators, while legacy competitors like Freightliner are pricing their electric semis in the $400,000–$500,000 range. Freightliner's eCascadia production reportedly dropped from approximately 1,000 units in the prior year to a target of around 100 units this year.

3. **Model X Is the Fastest-Selling Used Vehicle in the US:** Used Model X vehicles are reportedly selling faster than any other used vehicle in the United States, including gas-powered cars. The hosts attributed this partly to tax refund season and the growing consumer appeal of a vehicle that can drive itself, especially as older used Tesla models become more affordable.

4. **Model Y Long (Model Y L) Coming to US and Expanding Globally:** Strong signals suggest the Model Y L — first launched in China — is coming to the US, based on a Tesla webpage listing and recent visits by prominent Tesla YouTubers (Kyle Connor and others) to Chinese showrooms. The hosts also noted the Model Y L is now available in India. They speculated Tesla is deliberately not announcing the US launch yet to avoid cannibalizing current Model Y sales ("Osborning" the product).

5. **Supercharger Virtual Queue Feature Launched:** Tesla has built out a virtual queue feature for Superchargers, allowing owners to join a digital queue when a Supercharger is at capacity rather than waiting in a physical line. The system will manage charge order based on when vehicles joined the queue. The hosts noted this has been a long-requested feature and will require user adoption and in-vehicle prompting to work effectively.

## Market & Competition

1. **Samsung Equipment Move-In Ceremony for AI4 Chips:** Samsung held an equipment move-in ceremony at its Taylor, Texas chip plant, formally installing chip-making machines that will begin producing Tesla's AI4 self-driving chips and products for other customers later in 2026. The facility represents a nearly $37 billion investment and was approximately two years delayed after breaking ground in late 2022. Elon Musk had reportedly toured the factory with Samsung executives prior to the ceremony.

2. **SpaceX Solar Cell Factory Revealed in Bastrop, Texas:** SpaceX is constructing what is described as one of the world's most advanced solar cell factories in Bastrop, Texas. A job posting for a SpaceX Director of Solar Production was cited as evidence of active recruiting. The hosts found this surprising and noted it adds credibility to ongoing Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation, as solar is a natural fit for Tesla Energy's product ecosystem.

3. **Tesla-SpaceX Merger Speculation Remains Elevated:** Merger rumors between Tesla and SpaceX have intensified following the Terafab announcement (combining xAI and SpaceX data infrastructure) and now the SpaceX solar factory news. One host noted that new SpaceX compensation guidelines for Elon — including goals around a data center constellation and putting a million people on Mars — make the merger puzzle harder to solve, since you don't need to merge companies to do business together. Elon reportedly liked a post on X by a user making a detailed case for why a merger is feasible.

4. **Boring Company to Take Over Montreal Infrastructure in Las Vegas:** The Boring Company has announced it will take control of the existing Las Vegas Monorail infrastructure when it reaches end-of-life in a few years. Plans include removing the existing rail tracks, laying precast road on top, and integrating it into the Vegas Loop network. The hosts noted this will dramatically increase the visibility of Tesla vehicles operating in the Vegas Loop, serving as a major advertisement for Tesla given the volume of international visitors.

5. **Union and Legislative Pushback on Autonomous Vehicles:** A union for Uber drivers in Seattle has formed and is protesting autonomous vehicles. Montana legislation was also referenced that would require semi-trucks to maintain a human driver behind the wheel. The hosts acknowledged this as a near-term political reality while arguing such restrictions should not become permanent policy.
