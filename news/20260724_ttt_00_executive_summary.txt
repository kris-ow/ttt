## Financials
- TSLA dropped roughly 13% (~$50) in a single day, which jobhakdi's host attributes partly to higher-than-expected capex guidance — a signal he frames as bullish for long-term buildout (Terafab, Optimus, Cybercab) despite the market's negative reaction.
- Tesla's Q2 10-Q confirmed a $1.95 billion AI hardware acquisition paid entirely in stock and equity awards, with $1.73 billion contingent on milestones Tesla itself called "improbable" — meaning no stock-comp expense was recognized this quarter. Only $222 million was allocated to recognized assets (a patent and developed technology). The acquired company remains unnamed; market speculation points to DensityAI, per Electrek, unconfirmed by Tesla.
- Tesla is establishing a new $30 billion credit facility, up from roughly $7 billion previously, which panelists on brighterwithherbert read as a sign Tesla can't fully fund its scaling plans from operating free cash flow alone.
- Operating cash flow of about $4 billion fell short of nearly $6 billion in capex, pushing free cash flow negative for the first time in about a decade, per Matt Smith.
- Tesla's share count rose ~198 million shares in H1 2026 for equity awards and acquisitions; stock-based compensation rose to $2.18 billion, up ~80% YoY. SpaceX separately purchased $405 million of Megapacks in H1 2026.
- Delaware Supreme Court appeal reopens litigation many considered closed, per Tesla's 10-Q, after a vice chancellor dismissed related cases in April.

## Robotaxi
- Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla has driven over 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents, and that fleet growth has run at double-digit week-over-week rates since January, expected to continue through year-end — though panelists disputed what base rate that implies for absolute fleet size.
- Elluswamy described a shift toward operating by state rather than city, with new-city launch time trending to zero; Matt Smith countered that Tesla's own materials still list upcoming individual city launches.
- Current robotaxi fleet runs early builds of FSD v15, with roughly 40% of seven planned improvement tracks merged, per Elluswamy.
- A chart of cumulative robotaxi miles showed a declining monthly rate of additions, unexplained by Tesla on the call; one third-party theory cited by Matt Smith blames the transition to v15 software.
- Elluswamy called Cybercab "a phenomenal product," saying manufacturing targets are now aligned to the projected growth rate of unsupervised miles, with the same FSD v15 stack powering both Model Y and Cybercab.
- David Moss (FSD YouTuber) plans to attend Tesla's Miami robotaxi launch, then travel to Austin to ride in a Cybercab, per futureaza.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's newly released safety data shows FSD-supervised driving at 5,690,675 miles per major collision versus 2,076,014 for manual driving with active safety features and 698,781 for the U.S. average, per Sawyer Merritt's roundup.
- Fleet-wide FSD usage is now running at an annualized 13.7 billion miles/year, up 241% YoY, averaging 37.5 million miles/day — up from 28.8M/day a few months ago.
- Alexandra Merz warned any publicized FSD safety incident could jeopardize European approval, since a bloc of countries meeting a population threshold could block continent-wide clearance even where provisional approval (e.g., Netherlands) already exists.
- NHTSA denied a petition for a defect investigation into Model 3 manual door releases (covering 179,031 MY2022 vehicles) but granted a separate petition to begin industry-wide rulemaking on emergency door-egress standards. Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the denial isn't a clean win given parallel open investigations, pending lawsuits, and a Congressional bill citing 15 deaths tied to door-handle access.
- Real-world FSD anecdotes (cernbasher): a 93-year-old San Diego driver regained independence via FSD; a Cybertruck owner completed a difficult off-road parking-escape test; dashcam footage showed FSD swerving to avoid a deer at night; FSD correctly interpreted a police officer's subtle hand signals in traffic.
- David Moss reports ~50,000 FSD miles since last September and finds Hardware 3 (running ~15% of Hardware 4's compute) delivers largely comparable functionality; he plans FSD tests in Australia (version 14.33) and China, a 50-state FSD road trip in seven days later this summer, and a Hardware 3 coast-to-coast attempt around Labor Day.
- Aurora launched second-generation driverless trucks for 10 Sun Belt routes; Ford partnered with Apple to integrate Apple Maps into its upcoming self-driving platform; IIHS released a study recommending standardized AV safety-data reporting — all relevant regulatory/competitive context for Tesla's Robotaxi program.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus manufacturing facilities are being built out rapidly, per jobhakdi's host, cited alongside Terafab as evidence of continued operational progress despite the stock's sharp drop.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Merger speculation intensified around SpaceX's August 4 earnings call, with Alexandra Merz (explicitly speculative) suggesting SpaceX could be the "absorbing entity" in a Tesla-SpaceX merger; she cited Tesla General Counsel Brandon Ehrhart's unusual presence on the earnings call as a possible signal. Musk deflected an analyst's merger question on the call, saying an earnings call wasn't the right venue.
- Diverging share prices (SpaceX ~$118 vs. Tesla ~$319–320, per Merz) complicate merger exchange-ratio math versus her prior scenario; she suggested Musk might need a price floor in any offer.
- Matt Smith warned that eroded retail trust from missed Tesla milestones (robotaxi timelines, Optimus, Solar Roof) could require a major roadshow — potentially a Terafab groundbreaking, Roadster event, and Optimus presentation — to secure sufficient retail votes for any merger.
- Bloomberg reportedly noted SpaceX considered merging first with either Tesla or xAI as early as January, per Merz.
- Musk told The Economist one reason he took SpaceX public was to let retail investors own a stake, since private companies face shareholder-count limits; he also called retail investors "very insightful" with "long-term views."

## SpaceX — Starship
