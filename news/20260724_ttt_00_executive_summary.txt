## Brief

- Tesla stock fell ~13-20% this week on capex/margin concerns
- 10-Q reveals secret $1.95B AI hardware acquisition, unnamed target
- FSD collision rate: 1 per 5.69M miles vs 698K manual average
- NHTSA denies Model 3 door-release defect petition, opens rulemaking
- Merger speculation swirls around SpaceX's August 4 earnings call
- FSD v15 now running on robotaxi fleet, ~40% of tracks merged

## Robotaxi

- Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla has driven over 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents, citing this as validation of the camera-only approach; commentators disagreed on whether these miles are fully unsupervised.
- Elluswamy described fleet growth at double-digit week-over-week rates continuing through year-end and a shift toward operating by state rather than city, though panelists split on whether city-by-city launches are actually ending — Tesla's own materials reportedly still list upcoming city launches.
- FSD v15 is now running on Model Y robotaxis (not yet Cybercab) with roughly 40% of seven planned improvement tracks merged, per Elluswamy.
- Tesla received zoning approval for a dedicated robotaxi charging facility in San Antonio with 56 stalls; panelists speculated (unconfirmed) this points toward wireless charging given earlier FCC approval for positioning antennas.
- Starlink hardware is now integrated directly into the Cybercab rather than bolted on, per images shown at Tesla's Santana Row store — the first official confirmation of this integration.
- Musk said Tesla will rely on direct bookings via its own Robotaxi app rather than partnering with Uber/Lyft; panelists said fleet size, not demand, remains the current bottleneck.
- Active Robotaxi fleet: 791 (Bay Area 643, Austin 109, Dallas 20, Houston 19) per RobotaxiTracker.com; unsupervised fleet last reported at 39, unchanged since 2026-05-09.
- A shareholder question challenged why investors should trust Tesla's robotaxi timeline predictions; panelists said Tesla never directly addressed the credibility gap on the call.

## Autonomous Driving

- New Tesla-published data: FSD-supervised driving now shows one major collision per 5,690,675 miles vs. 2,076,014 for manual driving with active safety features and a 698,781 U.S. average.
- Fleet-wide FSD usage is running at an annualized 13.7 billion miles/year, up 241% year-over-year, with the fleet averaging 37.5 million FSD miles per day, up from 28.8M/day a few months ago.
- 55% of car sales now ship with FSD subscriptions, with subscription growth up 56% year-over-year, per the earnings call.
- NHTSA denied a defect-investigation petition into hidden manual door releases on 179,031 model-year 2022 Model 3 vehicles, finding only one matching complaint (traced to the petitioner's own VIN), but granted a separate petition to begin industry-wide rulemaking on emergency door-egress standards. Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the denial isn't a clean win since NHTSA wouldn't start rulemaking over a non-issue, and notes parallel open Model Y/Model 3 investigations, pending lawsuits, and a Congressional bill citing 15 deaths.
- FSD user David Moss reported roughly 50,000 miles of real-world use since last September and said Hardware 3 delivers largely the same FSD functionality as Hardware 4 despite using about 15% of the compute; he plans an attempt to cross all 50 states on FSD in seven days later this summer.

## Financials

- Q2 revenue reached $28.24 billion, up 26% year-over-year and roughly $3 billion above consensus, with the first-ever rolling 12-month period above $100 billion in revenue and services/other revenue up 50%.
- Free cash flow turned negative — roughly $4 billion of operating cash flow against nearly $6 billion of capex — a first in about a decade per one commentator; Tesla is establishing a new $30 billion credit facility, up from roughly $7 billion previously.
- Tesla's 10-Q confirmed closing of a $1.95 billion AI hardware acquisition paid entirely in stock and equity awards, with $1.73 billion contingent on milestones Tesla itself called "improbable" and only $222 million allocated to recognized assets. The acquired company remains unnamed; market speculation points to chip startup DensityAI, unconfirmed by Tesla. Electrek's Fred Lambert criticized the lack of disclosure and called it shareholders funding deals they can't evaluate.
- Share count rose ~198 million shares in H1 2026 for equity awards and acquisitions; stock-based compensation rose to $2.18 billion, up ~80% year-over-year.
- Panelists attributed TSLA's roughly 13-20% weekly decline primarily to higher-than-expected capex guidance rather than deteriorating fundamentals, drawing a comparison to Google's smaller post-capex-announcement drop.
- Delaware Supreme Court appeal of previously dismissed litigation resurfaced in Tesla's 10-Q, reopening a matter many considered closed.

## Humanoid Bots

- Optimus 3's hand design emphasizes full dexterity versus competitors' simpler grippers; an unnamed Tesla engineer reportedly said the robot will look more like "a human in a superhero suit." Panelists read unusually low leak volume around Optimus 3 as a bullish secrecy signal.
- Optimus manufacturing facility buildout is accelerating alongside Terafab construction, cited as evidence of operational progress continuing despite the stock drop.

## Energy

- Energy deployment was up 41% year-over-year, the second-best quarterly figure to date, alongside the launch of Tesla's first public Megawatt Charger site for the Semi in Bloomington, California (six stalls), tied to freight routes serving the Port of Long Beach.

## Market & Competition

- Canada EV momentum: Model Y deliveries up 98% quarter-over-quarter (~6,000 units, +28% year-over-year in H1); Cybertruck deliveries up 116%.
- Aurora launched second-generation driverless trucks across 10 planned U.S. Sun Belt routes; Ford announced a partnership with Apple to integrate Apple Maps into its upcoming Universal EV Platform and self-driving development.
- A Comcast executive downplayed Starlink as a competitive threat while AT&T's CEO said new entrants are "coming to the game very late"; panelists were skeptical, calling it a "Steve Ballmer reacting to the iPhone" moment.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- Merger speculation intensified around SpaceX's August 4 earnings call, with one analyst (explicitly speculative) citing Tesla General Counsel Brandon Ehrhart's presence on Tesla's earnings call as a possible signal of active legal groundwork; Musk deflected an analyst's merger question on the call, saying an earnings call wasn't the right venue.
- Diverging share prices (SpaceX ~$118, Tesla ~$319-320) were said to complicate exchange-ratio math versus a prior "Goldilocks" scenario.
- A Bloomberg report cited discussions in January over whether SpaceX should merge with Tesla or xAI ahead of a public listing.
- SpaceX purchased $405 million of Megapacks in H1 2026, per Tesla's 10-Q.

## SpaceX — Starship

## SpaceX — Starlink
