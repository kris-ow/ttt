This episode of the Tesla Beat Podcast features hosts Brian and Joe (Summon King) alongside returning special guest Hans Nelson, covering a wide range of Tesla and broader EV/tech news from the week of June 26, 2026. The discussion spans Tesla's American-made dominance, an FSD-related media controversy, Semi progress, Megapack energy deals, competitive EV landscape deterioration, and broader market context with Tesla trading around $377. The hosts maintain a bullish long-term thesis on Tesla while acknowledging near-term stock volatility and the difficulty of scaling complex manufacturing.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla tops American-Made Index for sixth consecutive year:** The Model 3 and Model Y topped the American-Made Index for the sixth year in a row. The hosts note that Tesla also leads local-content rankings in every region where it manufactures — including China and Europe — crediting Tesla's deliberate strategy of sourcing from local supply chains near each Gigafactory.

2. **Giga Berlin production ramp:** Per the hosts, Giga Berlin announced a production increase from 5,000 to 6,200 Model Y units per week, with a further ramp to 7,500 per week targeted by November or Q4 2026. The hosts say this expansion will create approximately 2,000 additional jobs.

3. **Cybertruck earns top safety honors:** The Cybertruck received a Top Safety Pick+ designation from IIHS (referred to as "IHS" by the hosts) during the week of broadcast. The hosts frame this as further evidence that Tesla's vehicles are among the safest on the market.

4. **Polestar ban and EV competitive landscape:** The hosts discuss Polestar's effective exit from the U.S. market, attributed to a ban on its Chinese-origin software (developed by Geely). Brian raises concern that regulators may not have fully considered second-order consequences — including potential trade war implications — given that Volvo, also Geely-owned and manufactured in South Carolina, uses related software. He notes that very few Polestar units are sold in the U.S., making the practical impact small but the precedent potentially significant. The hosts also note ongoing struggles at Rivian (recent layoffs, per the hosts) and Lucid (continued large per-vehicle losses).

5. **Slate EV truck priced at $24,950:** The Slate EV truck officially launched at $24,950 for the base model, confirming earlier leaks. Brian expresses continued skepticism about whether the company can be profitable at that price point, though he notes the assembly process is straightforward and the factory is small. Deliveries are expected late 2026 or Q1 2027. The hosts note Slate's CEO has described a "sell at a loss, make money on accessories" model akin to a printer company, and Brian flags the risk that third-party accessory makers could undercut that strategy.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD falsely blamed in media crash report:** A widely circulated news story (covered by ABC News, per the hosts) blamed Tesla FSD for a crash. Ashok Elluswamy publicly confirmed that the driver had manually overridden FSD by pressing the accelerator to 100% of pedal travel, reaching 73 mph in a residential area, with the accelerator still pressed after the crash. Brian explains that FSD's known failure mode is to slow and park — not accelerate — and that in an electric vehicle, any brake pedal application cuts power entirely, unlike in an ICE vehicle where throttle and brake can fight each other. Senator Blumenthal demanded Tesla accountability, per the hosts. The hosts praise Ashok Elluswamy's public response as appropriate transparency from Tesla's engineering leadership.

2. **NHTSA moves toward removing pedal requirements for robotaxi-class vehicles:** Regulators proposed adjusting brake pedal requirements for vehicles designed to be ridden rather than driven. Brian frames this as a rare instance of U.S. regulators getting ahead of the technology curve rather than reacting after the fact. He argues that removing pedals from purpose-built robotaxi vehicles like the Cybercab eliminates a source of human-error input rather than a safety feature, citing Elon Musk's principle that "all input is error."

3. **FSD version 14 referenced:** Brian mentions FSD version 14 in passing, noting he has not observed certain earlier failure modes (such as wheel jitter) on that version, though they existed on earlier versions.

## Robotaxi

1. **Waymo accident involving wet cement:** The hosts discuss a Waymo incident in which a vehicle drove into wet cement while staying between construction cones. Hans Nelson notes the incident appears software-related and offers measured commentary — acknowledging Tesla FSD has also had some freeway edge cases — but points out that the Jaguar I-PACE platform used by Waymo seems to be involved in most of the incidents observed, not the Zeekr-based vehicles. The hosts treat this as a minor but notable quality-control issue for Waymo.

2. **Amazon Zoox entering production:** Amazon's Zoox robotaxi is going into production. Hans Nelson acknowledges Amazon's AI infrastructure advantages but expresses concern about the onboard compute requirements implied by Zoox's heavy sensor suite, suggesting it may consume disproportionate energy relative to its range and drive up operational costs. He does not view Zoox as a near-term competitive threat to Tesla's robotaxi at scale.

## Energy

1. **Nat Power signs $5 billion Megapack deal:** European utility Nat Power signed a $5 billion agreement to purchase 25 gigawatt-hours of Megapack storage. Brian contextualizes the scale by noting this single deal is roughly three times the size of Lucid's entire lifetime revenue (~$1.6 billion). He also states that 25 GWh represents approximately 50% of the Megapack factory's projected full annual output. Brian emphasizes that the software layer — not just the hardware — is the key differentiator, enabling millisecond-level grid optimization including anticipatory responses to distant grid events such as wildfires.

2. **Megapack's grid integration advantages:** Hans Nelson elaborates that the new Megapack can emulate various types of electrical generation equipment (hydro, gas turbines, etc.), allowing seamless integration into existing grid architectures without requiring custom bridging solutions. The hosts describe Tesla's vertical integration in energy — from solar panels to Powerwalls to vehicle-to-load — as creating a compelling virtual power plant offering for residential customers as well, with Joe noting he receives an annual payment from Tesla for energy contributed back to the Texas grid.

## Electric Vehicles — Tesla Semi

1. **Semi ice-road testing in Alaska:** Tesla Semi testing footage in Alaska, filmed by Dan Priestley, showed the new-version Semi loaded with concrete and steel maintaining a stable front section even as the trailer fishtailed significantly on icy roads. Brian notes the Semi has been running routes over Donner Pass for approximately eight to nine years and that both the long-range and standard-range variants are now being manufactured in the Sparks/Reno, Nevada area, though not yet in large quantities.

2. **Semi efficiency and total cost of ownership advantage:** Brian states Tesla Semi is EPA-rated at 1.8 kWh per mile versus over 2.1 kWh per mile for competing electric semis (e.g., Freightliner eCascadia, Volvo). A recent Oklahoma pilot study by a fleet operator that subsequently ordered additional semis achieved 1.55 kWh per mile — approximately 25% more efficient than competing electric trucks. Brian says the Tesla Semi's purchase price comes in under $300,000 (the threshold at which he argues operators can be profitable), versus $400,000–$500,000 for the eCascadia. He suggests Tesla and BYD (which manufactures Class 8 trucks in Long Beach for Port of Long Beach use) are the only electric semi makers likely to survive without subsidies. He also floats the idea of a potential third "port range" variant with a smaller battery for short-haul port operations.

3. **ArcBest purchases two Tesla Semis:** After successful testing in California, ArcBest purchased two Tesla Semis, with the hosts noting that more orders could follow — consistent with their view that fleet operators who trial the Semi tend to expand their orders.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla stock and broader market context:** Tesla was trading at approximately $377 at the time of recording, down from around $400 the prior week. SpaceX, which had recently gone public, was trading around $153, down from a post-IPO high near $185. The hosts attribute the softness to macro uncertainty including Fed rate concerns, geopolitical tensions around Iran, and general post-IPO digestion. Brian cautions against expecting individual good-news items to move the stock, noting that much of Tesla's upside is already priced in by informed investors.

2. **Volkswagen restructuring:** VW is reportedly weighing up to 100,000 job cuts and four plant closures in what the hosts describe as its biggest overhaul yet. Brian notes VW is down only about 5% over the week despite this news, which he finds irrational — using it as an example of how stock prices are often disconnected from underlying business reality. The hosts frame VW's struggles as validation of a thesis they say they've held for five or six years.

3. **Starlink aviation and maritime adoption:** Starlink connectivity was highlighted across multiple transport sectors: Southwest Airlines flew its first Starlink-equipped flight; Liberia airlines installed Starlink on planes; Alaska Airlines is adopting the service; and Oldenorf shipping has installed Starlink on more than 80 vessels. The hosts view this as evidence of Starlink's compounding network effects and reliability advantage over legacy in-flight and maritime connectivity solutions.
