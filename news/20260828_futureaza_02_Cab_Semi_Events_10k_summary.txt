This episode of The Tesla Beat (#158) covers a busy news week: an upcoming Cybercab launch event and a Tesla Semi event in late September, a Cybertruck price increase amid apparent production constraints, Rivian's CFO departure read as a warning sign, SpaceX's Starbase Louisiana jobs announcement, and a Starship booster/ship surviving reentry and being recovered by barge. Hosts also discuss Neuralink's humanitarian impact, Model Y L cabin space, and Tesla Semi fleet order progress.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck price hike amid apparent production slowdown:** Tesla raised prices on the dual motor and premium Cybertruck trims by about 5%. One host cited a claim (attributed to a poster named Omar) that first-half-year Cybertruck sales were just over 8,000 units, which the hosts characterized as down roughly a quarter to a third versus the prior year. Hosts debated causes — rising steel costs and cross-border tariffs (with steel sourced partly from Canada), possible loss of economies of scale, or reallocation of batteries/staff toward Cybercab production — with one host noting Tesla could be shifting some capacity/attention to the Model Y L instead. One host said Tesla executives have previously indicated Cybertruck costs less to produce than the Model X or Model S.
2. **Model Y L cabin space impressions:** Hosts shared hands-on impressions of the Model Y L, saying third-row legroom felt notably better than the Model X's third row (one host, about six feet tall, could fit his feet under the second-row seats, unlike in his Model X). An NBA player was also shown in a social media clip praising the Model Y's interior space.
3. **Tesla windshield subscription price cut:** Tesla is dropping its windshield-related monthly subscription/warranty-style offering from $35 to $25, effective October 1st, alongside newer offerings like tire evaluation service and daily vehicle rentals.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab sightings and scaling ahead of launch:** Hosts reported increasing real-world sightings of Cybercabs testing in areas including Seattle, mostly with a driver and steering wheel present (no fully unsupervised units observed there yet). One host who viewed the Cybercab production line (without disclosing details) said the vehicle uses fewer parts and a smaller line footprint than Cybertruck's, calling it a strong step toward a low-cost-per-mile vehicle; the launch was expected in the following week, with unsupervised operation already underway in six cities and Austin set to be first for a pedal-less, steering-wheel-less version. Tesla has reportedly built over 2,000 Cybercabs so far (per the host, unconfirmed). A host also noted hearing (secondhand, unconfirmed) that Tesla may be seeking a vehicle storage facility in the Seattle area.
2. **Design detail spotted:** A host observed the Cybercab's wheel covers feature rubber edging similar to early Cybertruck wheel treatment, visible in recent official photos.

## Market & Competition
1. **Rivian CFO departure seen as a warning sign:** Rivian announced CFO Claire's departure shortly after the R2 launch, which hosts called a potentially critical growth moment akin to Tesla's "Model 3 hell" period. Hosts speculated (without confirmation) this could reflect financial strain, cash burn concerns, or personal reasons, drawing a parallel to a Waymo co-CEO's earlier departure ahead of a major expansion. One host suggested Rivian's ongoing cash burn problem remains its central risk and called the R2 the company's likely last major shot, with no fallback plan if it underperforms.
2. **Bank of America flags SpaceX wireless ambitions:** Hosts referenced a Bank of America note suggesting SpaceX's wireless ambitions could benefit T-Mobile and cell tower companies; hosts speculated Starlink-direct-to-device could eventually challenge or partner with traditional mobile carriers globally, citing a roughly $300–$400 cost for US carriers to acquire a new customer (unsourced figure from a host) and a new 10-year Starlink license granted by the UAE.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship survives reentry, recovered by barge:** A Starship vehicle unexpectedly survived reentry and splashdown rather than breaking apart, and SpaceX is recovering it by barge (described as heading to the Gold Coast). Hosts noted the extensive data gained and speculated it might eventually be displayed, possibly in a "rocket garden."

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Starbase Louisiana jobs announcement:** SpaceX announced Starbase Louisiana with plans to hire 10,000 people and a stated investment described by a host as around $100 billion (unconfirmed figure from host commentary). A host cited Gwynne Shotwell noting 60% of Starbase Texas staff were local hires, suggesting similar local hiring in Louisiana. Hosts framed multiple launch sites as valuable for weather resilience and interstate competition, similar to Tesla's Texas/California dynamic.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD credited with avoiding pedestrian collision:** A widely shared video showed a person jumping in front of a Cybertruck traveling around 40 mph while on FSD; the vehicle swerved and avoided the pedestrian. The vehicle's owner said he didn't notice the person until the truck swerved and believed FSD likely prevented a serious or fatal collision; the incident appeared to hosts as a possible intentional attempt to be hit.

## SpaceX — Launch Business / Electric Vehicles (Tesla Semi)
1. **Tesla Semi deployment plans and state incentives:** A Tesla executive reportedly confirmed plans to deploy 75 of 500 ordered Tesla Semis, with the remaining units by the end of 2027; the 500-unit order was placed in 2026. Hosts noted large state incentives in Washington and California that could reportedly reduce consumer cost to around $100,000 or less, in one case cheaper than a Cybertruck (host claim, unconfirmed). A Tesla Semi event was flagged for the end of September.
