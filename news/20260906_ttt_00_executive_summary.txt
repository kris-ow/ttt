## Robotaxi
- Cybercab commercially deployed count sits at 37-38 units per Pioneer Lens tracker (jobhakdi), which the host calls "right in between good and excellent" versus his 20-30 pre-launch estimate; 50+ would have exceeded expectations, and staying stuck near current levels for a month would be a bad sign.
- Tesla VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy disclosed Cybercab has surpassed 1 million unsupervised self-driving miles, growing 17% week-over-week — within the 10-15% range guided on the earnings call, per brighterwithherbert/Cern Basher.
- Cybercab became hailable anywhere in Austin as of the Friday after the launch event, no longer geofenced; Tesla is reportedly targeting 24/7 service via a "next month or so" software update tied to the V15 plan.
- Tesla released a fleet-purchase interest form (fleet vehicles, mobility hubs, event collaboration); 73,000 people clicked through, though commentators expect actual third-party fleet sales are a year to 18 months out at minimum.
- Cybercab drive unit disclosed: bar-wound stator, sub-10-second automated production cycle, and per Musk, no rare-earth metals while maintaining range. Brake system uses individual electric actuators per caliper, no hydraulic lines.
- Cybercab designed for total lifetime cost, not lowest unit cost — target battery/component life of 500,000+ miles, with a Tesla robotaxi program lead saying the vehicle is built to last five years or longer.
- One Cybercab is estimated operationally equivalent to roughly three Waymo vehicles due to charging-return downtime, per a Tesla robotaxi program lead; a viral cost comparison (~$18,000/Cybercab vs. $125,000/Waymo Gen 6) doesn't capture lifetime differences, per Cern Basher.
- 35 Cybercabs spotted near Miami International Airport, suggesting staging or expansion activity beyond Austin (per Sawyer Merritt digest).
- Texas DOT Executive Director, after attending the launch event, highlighted accessibility benefits for blind and wheelchair-using riders — a notable third-party endorsement relevant to regulatory goodwill.
- Dillon Loomis (Electrified) argues current multi-city vehicle deployments signal a genuine launch plan, expecting each new city to start with 5-10 vehicles before scaling to "hundreds" sometime next year; he also cites a sensor-suite cost edge (~$10,000-$20,000 vs. Waymo's reported $25,000-$30,000 upfit).

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla has launched an always-on active collision avoidance feature that can take over the vehicle regardless of FSD engagement; unclear if it's FSD-exclusive or fleet-wide.
- Cern Basher and Herbert dispute a Bloomberg report (forwarded by analyst Gary Black) claiming Tesla hasn't applied for a required federal exemption and would be capped at 2,500 vehicles; they maintain Cybercab is self-certified to federal safety standards without steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors, so no exemption or cap applies, citing reaffirmation from Tesla's Lars Moravy and Eric Early.
- jobhakdi assesses Tesla is likely to technically fail NHTSA's current-rules audit but expects NHTSA to approve updated rules that let Tesla pass, calling the post-event stock drop overreaction to this audit news rather than "sell the news."

## Humanoid Bots
- Dillon Loomis says Optimus won't generate meaningful Tesla revenue until near 2030, though a few thousand units could ship as early as late next year; he points to Tesla's 10-million-unit-capacity facility as a signal of internal confidence despite sparse recent public demo footage.

## Financials
- Loomis attributes TSLA's roughly flat share price over the past four to five years to stagnant profitability, with capital continually redirected to autonomy, Terafab, and Optimus rather than near-term earnings.

## Market & Competition
- futureaza and Loomis criticize Tesla's communications, noting an earlier Musk claim about the robotaxi fleet doubling on some cadence "was never close to" being met, and that Tesla's PR department was eliminated in favor of Musk handling comms directly ($100,000/year stated compensation).
- jobhakdi frames Uber as having no survival path once Cybercab scales, and notes a NASA/government contract awarded to Blue Origin as deliberate policy to avoid SpaceX overdependence; a German commercial space company reportedly launched an orbital rocket, called a positive but distant sign for European competition.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- futureaza argues SpaceX's stated terrestrial-first data center approach is the right call over orbital "Starmind" compute, citing repairability, cheaper solar, and no radiation/micrometeorite shielding needs on the ground.
- Host's core objection: at the proposed high-altitude compute orbit, satellites face minimal atmospheric drag and could remain in orbit for hundreds to thousands of years (versus 3-7 years for Starlink), with de-orbiting requiring ~20x more energy than reaching a graveyard orbit — risking an "antique mall" of stranded obsolete hardware that also can't be recycled.
