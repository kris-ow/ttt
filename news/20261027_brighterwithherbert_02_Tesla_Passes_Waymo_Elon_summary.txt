This video (host joined by guest Brian Wang of Next Big Future) covers Tesla's robotaxi expansion overtaking Waymo in Austin on weekly driverless miles and service-area coverage, new fleetwide FSD mileage and safety statistics, and Turo's launch of FSD-supervised rental cars. The core thesis is that Tesla's robotaxi growth is now exponential while Waymo's is comparatively flat, driven by vehicle count growth and operational efficiency that Waymo reportedly cannot match in the near term.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla overtakes Waymo on weekly driverless miles in Austin:** Tesla robotaxis are driving roughly 430,000–438,000 miles per week in Austin versus Waymo's most recently reported ~46,000 miles per week (a Q2 figure, reported with a roughly 3-month lag per the host). Elon Musk captioned a chart of the divergence "log curve in blue, exponential in red," and separately called the Austin race "never a real contest."
2. **Guest attributes Waymo's gap to fleet size, not technology:** Brian Wang says Waymo operates roughly 4,000 vehicles total (about 2,300 in California, 900–1,000 in Texas, ~500 in Austin) and that its mileage growth is capped by how many vehicles it can add — he estimates Waymo could grow total mileage only 10–20%, while Tesla has been growing weekly mileage roughly 15–20% week-over-week per crowd-tracked data. He also says Waymo has had recurring issues blocking traffic/intersections in busy areas (e.g., San Francisco), a constraint he says Tesla does not face, which he believes lets Tesla scale ride density further without the operational limits Waymo has hit.
3. **Tesla's Texas service area now roughly double Waymo's:** Tesla's combined Texas robotaxi service zones (Austin, Houston, Dallas) are now about 600 square miles versus Waymo's 307 square miles, driven partly by a roughly 10x overnight expansion of Houston's geofence (Austin ~272 sq mi, Houston ~254 sq mi per maps shown). All six Tesla robotaxi cities are described as running fully unsupervised. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis publicly asked Tesla to extend the Tampa and Miami zones to include the beaches.
4. **Dallas Cybercab staging ahead of next-month launch:** Spy footage shows large numbers of Cybercabs staged in a Dallas parking lot ahead of Tesla's stated plan to add Cybercab to the Dallas robotaxi fleet next month; Wang estimates roughly 300 vehicles staged between Dallas and Houston combined, guessing Houston may launch first (within two weeks of its large geofence expansion) with Dallas following, both likely in the first week of November.
5. **Guest's fleet growth forecast — up to ~2,000 vehicles by year-end, possibly 5,000:** Wang projects Tesla could reach close to 2,000 robotaxi vehicles across Texas by the end of November or first week of December, with 5,000 vehicles by year-end requiring a sharp acceleration in the final two weeks of December — which he thinks Tesla may instead delay into January to avoid straining staff over the holidays. He frames this as a large upward revision from expectations six months ago, when forecasts were far lower.
6. **Florida fleet likely larger than official tracker shows:** Wang says crowd-sourced tracking shows roughly 20–30 vehicles in Tampa (close to Houston's tracked level) but believes actual Tampa fleet size could be 40–50 vehicles since few riders in Florida log rides to the tracker; he expects Tampa could scale from current levels to a much larger fleet within about a month once Tesla decides to "turn it on."
7. **FSD version 15 and unsupervised rollout timing (speaker's estimates):** Wang puts roughly a 30% chance that a public version of FSD v15 ships before year-end, expecting capability to climb further by end of January and February; he believes Tesla will likely achieve broader unsupervised capability with v15 before extending current-generation (v14) unsupervised status to non-robotaxi owners. Ashok Elluswamy reportedly said (per the host) that the current software is not yet fully complete, with some remaining issues to solve.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD mileage tracker raised to 316.8 million supervised miles/week:** Tesla's FSD miles tracker was updated from 16 billion to 16.7 billion cumulative miles, with the fleet now logging about 50 million FSD miles per day (1,000 miles every 1.8 seconds), up 35% since July; cumulative driverless-only miles are cited at 3.7 million. Separately, Phil Beisel's tracked FSD-supervised weekly mileage rate rose from 244 million to 316 million miles per week.
2. **New safety data shows FSD supervised at 6 million miles per major collision:** Tesla's data shows FSD-supervised driving averaging 6 million miles between major collisions, versus 2 million miles with active safety features only, 845,000 miles under fully manual Tesla driving, and a 660,000-mile U.S. average — implying roughly 10x versus the manual Tesla rate and about 9x versus the U.S. average, per the host. Total major collisions logged for FSD-equipped Teslas stands at 1,579. The data covers all road classes (highway and non-highway) across North America, addressing earlier criticism that comparisons were highway-skewed.
3. **FSD subscription growth:** Overall FSD subscriptions rose by 260,000 in Q3 (from Q2), reaching 1.74 million subscriptions, a roughly 15% increase; Wang notes new-vehicle attach rate in the U.S. is around 75%, while attach rate on the older AI4-hardware fleet (~4 million vehicles) is around 30% (~1.2 million vehicles), which he expects to rise sharply if a future FSD version improves capability further.
4. **Possible passive collision-avoidance even without FSD engaged:** Wang says recent FSD updates appear to include a background collision-avoidance capability that can intervene even when FSD Supervised is not actively engaged, which he links to the jump from 845,000 to 2 million miles between collisions for non-FSD-engaged driving; he expects Tesla may eventually release this as a standard safety feature across all vehicles regardless of FSD purchase.

## Market & Competition
1. **Turo launches FSD-supervised rental verification:** Turo, in partnership with Tesla, launched (effective around October 20, per the segment) the first verified FSD-supervised Tesla rental experience in the U.S., allowing renters to search for and book Teslas with FSD Supervised activated, verified via direct Tesla API integration once a host links their account. Turo's CEO cited survey figures that 55% of Americans and 71% of Gen Z are interested in self-driving tech for travel.
2. **Barclays upgrades Tesla, cites multiple catalysts:** The host notes Barclays' recent upgrade (TSLA cited as moving from $370 toward $450 in the target) citing improving vehicle demand, stronger energy growth, accelerating robotaxi adoption, and potential upside from a SpaceX merger.
