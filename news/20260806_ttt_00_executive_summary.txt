## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first earnings call as a public company: Q2 revenue $7.81B (+66% QoQ), net loss $541M, adjusted EBITDA $3.53B, capex $18.3B, cash ~$100B, backlog $47B. CFO Brett Johnson guided to over $100B annualized recurring revenue by December; Musk said internal trillion-dollar revenue targets moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" in 2029.
- SpaceX's first major post-IPO share unlock (roughly 900M shares, lifting float from 639M to ~1.5B) landed August 6 without triggering a selloff — SPCX closed up 6.14% at $114.92 per one source, up ~2.6% per another. Analysts flagged this as a bullish signal but want to watch price action over the coming week; resistance is expected near $130-140, close to the $135 IPO price.
- Terafab, the Tesla-SpaceX chip megafactory, was officially confirmed for Grimes County, Texas, on the site of a former coal-plant reservoir. Initial phase capital investment is pegged at $16.8B combined — down from the $25B figure Musk cited in March — with "future expansion phases" pushing total cost much higher; Electrek notes SpaceX's own May S-1 filing described Terafab as a non-binding "general framework" with no finalized IP split, a tension with the confident tone of today's announcement. Facility targets 100M+ sq ft and 3,000+ jobs, aiming for 1+ terawatt of compute/year for Optimus, Cybercab, and space-based data centers.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX targets over 2 gigawatts of compute by year-end, potentially 8-10 gigawatts by end of 2027, built exclusively on Nvidia hardware (Vera Rubin/Reuben GPUs) under an exclusive partnership. AI capex reportedly pays back in under a year. AI segment revenue came in at $2.5-2.56B (beating a $2.1B estimate) but posted a $1.2B operating loss for the period.
- A third-party analysis (Cern Basher) modeled an 8-gigawatt buildout as a potential $500-600B/year rental opportunity by end of 2027 — flagged by multiple hosts as speculative ("a big if"). One SpaceX-focused analyst pushed back, saying he expects SpaceX to fall short of the 5-10 gigawatt guidance.
- A separate commentator (Bhakdi) claimed SpaceX/xAI had to cut planned Colossus 2/3 energy capacity roughly in half due to government-related constraints, requiring ~1.2GW of buildout mostly to replace rather than add capacity — illustrating compute scaling is energy-constrained.
- Electrified pushed back on claims (from a rival channel) that SpaceX's Colossus data-center economics are a "scam" driven by circular Google investment, arguing the comparative cost/revenue-per-megawatt figures cited unfairly compare SpaceX's brownfield sites to competitors' greenfield builds. The host did concede that SpaceX's Anthropic/Google compute contracts carry 90-day rolling cancellation terms with no long-term guarantee.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink added 1.7M net subscribers in Q2 (best quarter yet), reaching ~12 million total, per SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell; ARPU held at $66/month across 167 markets. Constellation stood at ~10,200 satellites as of June 30 (~9,600 broadband, ~800 Tbps downlink).
- Shotwell announced Starlink Mobile will launch at the end of 2027, moving from ~5MHz to ~65MHz of bandwidth via EchoStar spectrum plus ~10x satellite density, targeting the ~$600B/year US "big three" wireless carrier market with voice, video, and disaster resilience.
- Viasat has lost 81% of its US fixed broadband subscribers since Starlink's 2020 launch (603,000 to 115,000), with further Q2 2026 losses reported (exact figure not given). Hughesnet is reportedly going out of business this week, attributed to LEO competition from Starlink.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk declared the Starship heat shield problem solved based on inspection of a recovered ship, saying he sees no remaining technical obstacles to full, rapid reusability — though iterative fixes continue, with current reuse potentially limited to 3-10 flights.
- SpaceX completed two successful V3 flights in the last 90 days; flight 14 will carry the first Starlink V3 satellites to operational orbit. A booster-and-ship catch attempt is planned this year, though a booster catch with the new engines hasn't yet been demonstrated.

## Energy
- Megapack 3 entered production at Tesla's new Brookshire, Texas Megafactory (16 months groundbreaking-to-production, 50 GWh/year design capacity). Each unit holds ~5 MWh, up 28% from the Megapack 2 XL's 3.9 MWh, with thermal connection points cut 78%. The new "Megablock" bundles four units (~20 MWh) with pre-integrated transformer/switchgear, claiming 248 MWh/acre density and 1 GWh deployed in 20 business days.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the Megablock's factory-integrated wiring is the more important innovation than raw cell density, but flags a risk: filling a 50 GWh factory requires large-format cells at a scale Tesla's own cell production hasn't reached, with energy-segment gross margins already pressured by tariffs on imported LFP cells.
- Tesla says it recovers 90%+ of key battery materials (nickel, cobalt) from recycled cells, processing ~3,000 metric tons in Nevada last year and a similar amount expected in Texas this year, with recycled output projected to rival North American mining output by 2030.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla is hiring for roughly 280 Optimus-related roles versus about 30 for Cybercab — a ~9x gap — per a hiring tracker cited on brighterwithherbert, including specialized thermal and actuator engineering roles.
- The Optimus factory building at Giga Texas is sized for a reported 10-million-unit-per-year line, per Joe Tegtmeyer; he attributes Optimus's roughly one-to-two-year timeline slip to Tesla vertically manufacturing motors and actuators in-house rather than sourcing off-the-shelf.
- Investor Joe Bhakdi projects humanoid robots reach commercial scale within roughly a year but places Tesla's Optimus-driven stock "inflection" more likely in 2027-2028, calling it unlikely within 6-12 months but "very likely" within 24.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi service is now running limited night hours in Austin (until roughly 9-9:30 PM), per commentator Abby Manu; vehicles still avoid highways, with supervised rides continuing alongside.
- A Tesla executive reportedly told a podcast guest (Randy Kirk) that Tesla intends to build a million Cybercabs rather than a million Model Ys, with weekly production growth described ambiguously as "double digits."
- Amazon-owned Zoox will begin its first paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas next Monday, priced near "comfort"-tier ride-hailing — a notable competitive milestone as rivals move toward commercial monetization alongside Tesla.
- Bhakdi argues robotaxi is a nearer-term catalyst than Optimus, expecting scale-up "definitely" within 12 months and "probably" within 6.

## Financials
- Musk reportedly gave a rough compute-allocation estimate for Terafab output: ~25% for Tesla Optimus, ~75% for AI/spacecraft use — implying close to zero allocation to vehicle FSD compute, which is expected instead from AI5 chips via TSMC/Samsung.
- One host estimates the full Terafab project could eventually exceed $100B in capex, tying likely funding to Tesla's existing $30B debt facility; TSLA closed at $319.53, down slightly on the day.

Angstrom's countersuit alleges Cybertruck sales have been "extraordinarily disappointing" and disputes that backlog reflects genuine consumer demand.
