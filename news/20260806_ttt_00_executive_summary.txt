## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's first earnings call as a public company showed Q2 revenue of $7.81B (up 66% QoQ), net loss of $541M, adjusted EBITDA of $3.53B, capex of $18.3B, cash of ~$100B, and backlog of $47B (a separate source cites ~$45B). CFO Brett Johnson guided to over $100B ARR by December; Musk said internal trillion-dollar revenue projections moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" in 2029.
- SpaceX's first major post-IPO share unlock hit August 6, expanding tradable float from 639 million to roughly 1.5 billion shares (~12% of total). Rather than selling off, shares closed up — one source puts it at +6.14% to $114.92, another at +2.6% — against a Nasdaq-100 down 0.39%. A Business Insider profile of three early shareholders found none currently planning to sell at current levels.
- Terafab, the joint Tesla-SpaceX chip megafactory, was officially confirmed for Grimes County, Texas, on the Gibbons Creek Reservoir. Initial phase cost is $16.8B (down from the $25B figure Musk gave in March), targeting over 1 terawatt of compute per year for Optimus, Cybercab, and space-based data centers; planned footprint exceeds 100 million sq ft with 3,000+ jobs. Electrek notes SpaceX's own May S-1 filing described Terafab as a non-binding "general framework" with no finalized IP split — an inconsistency with the confident tone of today's announcement. Musk reportedly estimated Terafab compute output would split ~25% Tesla Optimus / ~75% AI/spacecraft.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX plans to exceed 2 gigawatts of AI compute by year-end, with cumulative compute potentially reaching 8–10 gigawatts by end of 2027, built exclusively on Nvidia hardware (Vera Rubin/Reuben GPUs) under an exclusive partnership. AI capex was $15.8–18B of the quarter's total capex, with the CFO citing sub-one-year payback on AI infrastructure.
- AI segment revenue came in at $2.5–2.56B (beating a $2.1B estimate per one source) but posted a $1.2B operating loss for the period; connectivity segment was profitable with $1.66B operating income.
- One host pushed back on claims (from a rival YouTuber) that Google's dual stakes in SpaceX and Anthropic create circular, inflated data-center revenue, conceding the fair point that SpaceX's Anthropic/Google compute deals carry 90-day rolling cancellation terms and are not guaranteed long-term.
- A third-party analysis (Cern Basher) estimated an 8-gigawatt buildout by end-2027 could represent a $500–600B/year rental opportunity — flagged explicitly by multiple hosts as speculative.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink added 1.7 million net subscribers in Q2 (best quarter yet), reaching roughly 12 million total; ARPU held at $66/month across 167 markets. Connectivity revenue was $4.29B, beating a $3.8B estimate.
- SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell announced Starlink Mobile will launch at the end of 2027, moving from ~5 MHz to ~65 MHz of bandwidth via EchoStar spectrum plus ~10x more satellites, targeting the ~$600B/year US "big three" wireless carrier market.
- Viasat has lost 81% of its US fixed broadband subscribers since 2020 (603,000 to 115,000), underscoring continued Starlink share gains; Hughesnet is reportedly going out of business this week.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk declared the Starship heat shield problem solved on the earnings call, citing data and inspection of a recovered ship, with no remaining technical obstacles to full rapid reusability — though iterative improvements continue, with reuse currently limited to as few as 3–10 flights.
- SpaceX completed two successful Starship V3 flights in the past 90 days; flight 14 will carry Starlink V3 satellites to operational orbit, and a first/second-stage catch attempt is planned this year, with a ship catch possibly as soon as the next flight.

## Energy
- Tesla began Megapack 3 production at its new Brookshire, Texas Megafactory (16 months groundbreaking-to-production, 50 GWh/year design capacity). Each unit holds ~5 MWh, up 28% from the Megapack 2 XL's 3.9 MWh, with thermal connection points cut 78%.
- The new Megablock design bundles up to four Megapack 3 units (~20 MWh) with pre-integrated transformer and switchgear, enabling 248 MWh/acre density and 1 GWh deployed in 20 business days — Electrek's Fred Lambert calls this factory-integrated wiring, not cell density, the more important development, though he flags that filling a 50 GWh factory requires cell production scale Tesla doesn't yet have.
- Tesla says it recovers over 90% of key battery materials from recycled cells, processing ~3,000 metric tons in Nevada last year and a similar amount in Texas this year, targeting output that rivals North American mine production by 2030.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla is currently hiring for 280 Optimus-related roles versus about 30 for Cybercab roles, per a hiring tracker cited by one host. The Optimus factory at Giga Texas is reportedly sized for a 10-million-unit-per-year line, per Joe Tegtmeyer, who says no other company is building at this scale.
- Tegtmeyer attributes Optimus timeline slips of roughly a year or two to Tesla vertically manufacturing motors and actuators in-house rather than sourcing off-the-shelf.
- Investor Joe Bhakdi expects humanoid robots to reach commercial scale (not perfection) within roughly a year, but places Tesla's Optimus-driven stock re-pricing "inflection" more likely in 2027–2028, calling it guaranteed within 24 months but unlikely within 6–12.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi service is now running limited night hours in Austin (until roughly 9–9:30 PM), per commentator Abby Manu; vehicles still avoid highways, with supervised rides continuing alongside.
- Bhakdi argues robotaxi scale-up is a nearer-term catalyst than Optimus, expecting meaningful progress "definitely" within 12 months and "probably" within 6.
- A Tesla executive reportedly told commentator Randy Kirk that Tesla intends to build a million Cybercabs (not a million Model Ys), with weekly fleet growth described ambiguously as "double digits."
- Amazon-owned Zoox will begin its first paid robotaxi rides in Las Vegas next Monday, priced similarly to "comfort"-tier ride-hailing — a notable competitive data point as rivals move toward commercial monetization.

## Financials
- Terafab's funding structure remains unclear: it's unspecified whether the $16.8B initial phase is combined or per-company, and one host connects it to Tesla's existing $30B debt facility as a likely funding source, calling the project potentially Tesla's largest-ever capex commitment.
- Electrek's Fred Lambert questions how much of Terafab's cost lands on Tesla's balance sheet given Tesla's car business has stopped growing and it already committed $16.5B to Samsung for AI6 chip supply.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's Cybertruck-parts dispute with supplier Angstrom continues: Tesla won a court order to recover production equipment, but Angstrom's bankruptcy filing triggered an automatic stay. Angstrom's countersuit alleges Cybertruck sales have been "extraordinarily disappointing" and that backlog reflects Tesla-affiliate purchases rather than public demand.
- Ford unveiled the "Fathom" midsize electric truck, starting at $28,350, targeting cost-competitiveness with Tesla and Chinese EV leaders; deliveries begin later in 2027.
- Tesla Model Y topped Canadian EV search rankings (April 2025–April 2026); EVgo will add Tesla Superchargers to its network with automatic in-car routing.
- Tesla is offering 2-month free trials of FSD (Supervised) V14 to some owners in Denmark and the Netherlands.
