This video features FutureAzA host Brian and car dealer Jack at Plat Auto, reviewing the refreshed Hyundai Ioniq 5 as a competitor to Tesla's Model Y. The video blends a hands-on test drive with commentary on the Ioniq 5's specs, value proposition, and how it stacks up against Tesla and other EVs in the used and new market. A side discussion covers a 2018 Model X with prepaid Full Self-Driving and a BMW i3 on the lot.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Refreshed Ioniq 5 Now on NACS:** The 2025 Ioniq 5 facelift switched from CCS to the North American Charging Standard (NACS), aligning it with Tesla's charging network. The refresh also redesigned the center console, added physical buttons for heated seats and steering wheel, upgraded to wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and improved the infotainment UI with larger, easier-to-read icons.

2. **Range and Charging Performance:** The two-wheel-drive test vehicle showed approximately 316 miles of estimated range at 99% charge. Peak DC fast charging is rated at 230 kW, described as competitive but not class-leading. The all-wheel-drive variant has roughly 40 miles less range. During the spirited test drive, the vehicle returned approximately 3 miles per kWh, noted as good efficiency for a spacious two-row SUV.

3. **Ioniq 5 as a Value-Oriented Model Y Alternative:** Jack describes the Ioniq 5 as consistently one of the dealership's best sellers alongside the Model Y. The Ioniq 5 sells at a discount to comparable Model Y configurations while offering similar charging speeds and range, making it a strong "spreadsheet" choice for buyers prioritizing EV specs per dollar. The Ioniq 5N variant is also mentioned, featuring simulated gear changes and a simulated torque curve for enthusiast driving.

4. **Hyundai/Kia Warranty Advantage:** Original owners receive a 10-year/100,000-mile battery and powertrain warranty plus a 5-year/60,000-mile bumper-to-bumper warranty. For second owners, the powertrain warranty drops to 5-year/60,000-mile coverage. This is noted as among the best in the industry, broadly competitive with Tesla's warranty, though Tesla offers slightly higher mileage limits on certain models like the Cybertruck.

5. **ADAS Features Included at No Extra Cost:** The Ioniq 5 offers lane centering and adaptive cruise control as standard, with the ability to run them independently — lane centering without adaptive cruise, or vice versa. The system is hands-on and will alert the driver if hands are removed. No subscription fee is required for these features.

6. **2018 Model X with Prepaid FSD on the Lot:** A 2018 Model X 100D with approximately 57,000–58,000 miles and prepaid Full Self-Driving (believed to be Hardware 2.5) is listed for around $30,000. The prepaid FSD transfers with the vehicle. Elon Musk has indicated that customers who paid for FSD will receive value from it, though a Hardware 4 retrofit for this vehicle is considered uncertain. This is framed as an unusual value proposition — a large Tesla with a big battery and FSD for life at that price.

7. **BYD Blade 2.0 Battery Briefly Discussed:** Brian mentions the new BYD Blade 2.0 LFP battery in China, reportedly capable of charging at 1 megawatt, allowing a 10–80% charge in approximately five minutes — verified by EV reviewer Kyle Connor. The tradeoff discussed is a reduction in battery longevity (roughly half a million miles vs. a million miles). BYD is noted as deploying Megapack-equivalent battery energy storage at its charging stations to support these rates.

## Market & Competition

1. **Ioniq 5 Competes Directly with Model Y on Value:** Jack explicitly positions the Ioniq 5 as selling at a discount to the Model Y with broadly comparable EV performance, making it a top seller at the dealership. Brian personally expresses preference for more SUV-like options (Cadillac Lyriq, Nissan Ariya) or sportier ones (Ford Mustang Mach-E) despite acknowledging the Ioniq 5's objective advantages.

2. **EV Cost-Per-Mile vs. Gas in the Pacific Northwest:** The hosts discuss electricity costs in the Pacific Northwest as among the cheapest in the nation, equating home charging to roughly $0.80/gallon gasoline equivalent. Supercharging at approximately $0.35/kWh equates to roughly $3.50/gallon equivalent — which used to be comparable to gas prices but is now significantly cheaper as gas prices have risen. The hosts note prices may take six months or more to normalize even if supply issues resolve.

3. **American EV Efficiency Criticized:** Brian notes that domestic EV offerings (citing the Cadillac and Wagoneers by name) have stated efficiency figures that are worse than most imports in real-world driving, contrasting with the Ioniq 5's solid real-world numbers.
