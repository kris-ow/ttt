This is a conversational deep-dive between the host (Brian, of futureaza) and Dillon Loomis (Electrified) assessing whether Tesla still has meaningful upside catalysts, covering the state of robotaxi deployment, FSD adoption trends, Optimus timelines, and criticism of Tesla's communications around missed forecasts. Both hosts remain long-term bullish but acknowledge investor frustration with unmet short-term promises and a lack of financial profit growth in recent years.

## Robotaxi
1. **Vehicle deployment seen as pre-launch signal, not final scale:** Dillon Loomis argues Tesla is positioning tens to over a hundred robotaxi-equipped vehicles across multiple cities — numbers he says far exceed a demo — as evidence of a genuine launch plan rather than a symbolic rollout, though he expects each new city to start with only five to ten vehicles for weeks or months before scaling to "hundreds" sometime next year.
2. **Pick-up/drop-off precision is a data problem, not a safety flaw:** Loomis says the software struggles with precise pickup/drop-off locations because Tesla lacks training data for that specific behavior (regular FSD drivers aren't dropping off "passengers"), and both hosts expect this to improve as more real-world robotaxi miles accumulate.
3. **Sensor suite cost seen as a durable cost advantage:** Loomis states Tesla's sensor suite could cost roughly $10,000–$20,000 versus a vehicle COGS around $20,000–$25,000, giving Tesla a structural cost edge; the host cites a Wall Street estimate that Waymo's vehicle upfit costs roughly $25,000–$30,000, partly due to needing custom engineering across multiple vehicle platforms and third-party (e.g., Magna) integration costs.
4. **New-city deployments generate outsized learning value:** Loomis argues that even small initial fleets in new cities generate valuable, city-specific training data (differing intersections, demographics, usage patterns) that accelerates the broader rollout even if it doesn't immediately produce large visible fleets.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscriber growth flagged as the more "material" near-term signal:** The host notes rising monthly FSD subscriber take rates in already-established markets (separate from new-country expansion) as a concrete, current business win that Loomis says no competitor can currently match.
2. **Most current complaints are preference, not safety:** Loomis says most current FSD criticism (including from his own wife, in a video he referenced) is about driving style/comfort rather than safety, and that this distinction gets lost in online reactions; both hosts argue such friction will become less relevant once driving is fully unsupervised, since riders stop caring about route/style once they're not paying attention to the road.
3. **Tesla's FSD geographic footprint framed as a unique moat:** Loomis frames Tesla's FSD (unsupervised claim aside) as operating in far more countries than Waymo, which he says operates in only a handful, positioning Tesla's generalized approach as structurally advantageous even though full unsupervised capability isn't there yet.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus won't generate meaningful revenue until near 2030, per Loomis:** Loomis states plainly that he doesn't expect Optimus to generate revenue for Tesla until closer to 2030, though he allows Tesla "might sell a few thousand units" possibly as early as late next year, while noting personal doubt about even that.
2. **10-million-unit capacity facility cited as a confidence signal:** Loomis points to Tesla building a facility with 10 million unit capacity as evidence Tesla internally believes the humanoid robot bet will work, even though the public has seen little recent demo footage (he notes the last Optimus video shared was months prior, only about eight seconds long).
3. **Host argues Tesla's automotive background transfers directly to robotics:** The host says Tesla's automotive-honed tools — e.g., crash-test simulation methods used in Megapack and early Optimus engineering — give it a structural edge in building out humanoid robot manufacturing and engineering that competitors lack, regardless of any specific robot design.
4. **Market seen as big enough for multiple winners over 5–10 years:** Loomis says the humanoid robotics market is large enough that several competitors, including Chinese robot makers he says he doesn't follow closely, can succeed over the next 5-10 years without any one company monopolizing it.

## Financials
1. **Stock described as roughly flat over recent years due to stagnant profitability:** Loomis attributes TSLA's flat share price performance over the past four to five years largely to a lack of profitability growth, saying Tesla posted similarly sized profitable quarters years ago as it does now, as capital continually gets redirected toward future bets (autonomy, Terafab, Optimus) rather than near-term earnings.

## Market & Competition
1. **Criticism of Tesla's reduced PR/communications function:** Both the host and Loomis criticize Tesla for weak investor communication around missed timelines (e.g., an earlier-year expectation, attributed to Elon Musk, that the robotaxi fleet would double on some cadence, which the host says never came close to happening) and note Tesla's PR department was eliminated, leaving Musk — whose stated compensation for this role was $100,000/year — handling comms directly despite language barriers in markets like China and Germany.
2. **Cybertruck production cited as slower than desired but improving:** Loomis notes Cybertruck production is increasing, though slower than Tesla would like, without giving specific volume figures.
