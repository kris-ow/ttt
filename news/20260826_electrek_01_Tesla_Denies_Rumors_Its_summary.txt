Tesla publicly denied Chinese social media rumors that it had abandoned its Shanghai data center and withdrawn its "Full Self-Driving" push in China, saying the facility is operating normally and reporting the claims to police, while China's FSD (Supervised) subscription rollout remains stuck without a published timeline or pricing.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Rumors and Tesla's denial:** Chinese social media claimed Tesla's Shanghai data center — built to support a "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" launch — had been vacated with liaison teams withdrawn and related positions cut. Responding to Chinese outlets Tencent Tech and National Business Daily, per CnEVPost, Tesla said the data center "is operating normally," that recruitment for driver-assistance roles is accelerating, and that work is "progressing steadily." Tesla also reported the rumors to police.
2. **China missing from official market list:** China does not appear on Tesla's updated list of 12 markets (including the US, Canada, Australia, South Korea, and several European countries) where FSD (Supervised) subscriptions are offered, even though Tesla's US website still lists China as a market where FSD Supervised is "available for use." Tesla announced FSD availability in China in May 2026, then renamed the software "Tesla Assisted Driving" on its Chinese site, but has never published subscription pricing or a broad rollout timeline for Chinese customers. Electrek notes it previously reported in February that Tesla secured AI training capability inside China (a data-localization requirement) and in January that Chinese regulators dismissed Musk's claim FSD approval was coming "next month."

## Market & Competition
1. **Local rivals advancing faster:** Chinese automakers Xiaomi and Huawei are rapidly expanding urban driver-assistance features operating on city streets across China. Xpeng is preparing to launch an upgraded level 2 driver-assist system already described as competitive with Tesla's limited version of FSD in China.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues there's no solid evidence Tesla shut down its Shanghai data center and that reporting rumors to police is standard practice in China, but says Tesla's denial doesn't address when Chinese owners will get full FSD access. He characterizes Tesla's "accelerating" and "progressing steadily" language as the same forward-looking rhetoric used for over a year, and concludes Tesla likely remains stuck in regulatory limbo — data localization solved, but a broad urban FSD release still gated by Beijing's approval process.
