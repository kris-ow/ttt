## Market & Competition

1. **AI displacing entry-level cognitive work now:** The host presents data showing the unemployment rate for recent computer science graduates (ages 22–27) stands at 6.1% as of the time of recording, with computer engineering majors specifically at 7.5% — comparable to arts majors. A Stanford Digital Economic Lab study published in November 2025 ("Canaries in the Coal Mine") tracked software developer employment for ages 22–25 and found it down nearly 20% from the late 2022 peak through September 2025, even as the tech industry's revenue was rising. The mechanism identified is that senior developers with AI coding tools no longer need to delegate to junior hires.

2. **Corporate layoffs explicitly citing AI are accelerating:** According to Challenger Gray and Christmas, roughly 55,000 U.S. layoffs in 2025 explicitly cited AI as the reason. In Q1 2026 alone, tech layoffs crossed between 50,000 and 78,000, with AI cited in roughly half. In March 2026 alone, 15,000 cuts were announced explicitly tied to AI. Named examples include: Snap cutting 1,000 jobs (16% of workforce) in April 2026 citing AI; Block cutting 4,000 jobs (nearly half its headcount); Microsoft laying off 15,000 workers across 2025; Amazon cutting 14,000 corporate roles; Oracle cutting between 20,000 and 30,000; and Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff stating in September 2025 that headcount was reduced from 9,000 to about 5,000.

3. **Anthropic's Economic Index quantifies task-level AI coverage:** Anthropic published their Economic Index in March 2026, analyzing Claude usage data and finding that 49% of sampled jobs had at least 25% of their tasks being performed by Claude. For computer programmers specifically, task coverage was 75%; for data entry keyers, 67%. The host notes these are not fully automated occupations yet, but that when a quarter of tasks disappear, companies typically eliminate workers who did that quarter rather than proportionally reducing headcount.

4. **Broader workforce disruption projected:** A Brookings Institution report by Molly Kinder and Mark Muro (October 2024, updated with Yale Budget Lab in 2025) found that more than 30% of all U.S. workers could see at least 50% of their occupational tasks disrupted by generative AI, and roughly 85% of workers could see at least 10% disruption. The report also noted that industries most exposed to AI historically have the lowest union representation.

5. **Klarna cited as a real-world workforce contraction example:** Klarna's CEO stated their OpenAI-powered chatbot handles the work of roughly 700 full-time customer service agents. The company's total workforce went from over 7,000 in 2022 to about 3,000 in early 2026, with the CEO publicly projecting fewer than 2,000 employees by 2030.
