This "news of the dumb" segment from futureaza features hosts mocking sloppy or misleading automotive journalism, with most of the substance centered on debunking a Curbed article's factual errors about Tesla's Cybercab, alongside a Waymo executive's vision-only critique, a Model 3 safety rating, and a Cybertruck vehicle-to-load anecdote. The tone is comedic/reactive rather than analytical, with the hosts fact-checking claims point by point.

## Robotaxi
1. **Curbed article's Cybercab errors debunked:** The hosts dissect a Curbed article claiming Tesla's driverless Cybercabs operating in Florida and Texas have no brakes and no side mirrors; a city transportation department spokesperson quoted in the same article contradicts this, stating the Cybercabs are testers equipped with a steering wheel and gas/brake pedals — which the hosts call an internal contradiction exposing the article's sloppiness.
2. **Host's cost comparison of Waymo vs. Cybercab:** One host claims that Waymo's safety record is roughly on par with Cybercab's ("about equal from what I can tell"), unsupervised, and asserts (without citing a source) that Waymo's sensor upfit costs as much as or more than a Cybercab — an unverified personal estimate.
3. **Laser windshield-wiper claim debunked:** The hosts push back on a CarBuzz article suggesting Cybercab's lack of windshield wipers relates to a laser-based debris-removal patent from around 2021; they clarify that patent concerned only small debris removal, not converting rain/water to steam, calling the article's framing incorrect.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Waymo executive's vision-only critique:** A Waymo software executive reportedly argued cameras alone aren't sufficient for autonomous driving, without naming Tesla directly; the hosts dispute this, citing real-world vision-only deployments (Tesla, and China's Xiaopeng, which one host says he has personally driven and found "quite good," though not as good as Tesla FSD in the US) performing well across varied weather conditions.
2. **Host's personal FSD experience:** One host describes testing a recent FSD version (referred to as 14.3.9) for an hour and a half on personal errands, saying it handled a long, narrow driveway with a hedge without issue and found no remaining trouble spots to test it against; he notes it still won't enter his garage.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model 3 five-star safety rating:** The Model 3 received a five-star rating from a safety-testing organization (not a Tesla-run outlet), sweeping all individual crash test categories and meeting active safety recommendations; Tesla highlighted this on X, noting the car "swept every individual crash test category, securing five stars as usual."
2. **Cybertruck vehicle-to-load vs. Powerwall cost comparison:** A host argues that Cybertruck's vehicle-to-load feature can be cheaper than buying a Powerwall for backup power, since owners can drive the truck to a location with power to recharge during an outage rather than relying on a stored battery or generator.

## Market & Competition
1. **Volkswagen and Mercedes concept vehicle criticism:** The hosts mock Volkswagen for developing an efficiency concept vehicle unlikely to enter production while cutting roughly 100,000 jobs and closing plants, and similarly criticize Mercedes for building a fully road-legal concept car capable of long range in hot desert conditions with no production plans.
