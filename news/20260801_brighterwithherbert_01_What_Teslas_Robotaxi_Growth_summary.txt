The video features analyst Cern Basher walking through scenario modeling for Tesla's Robotaxi business, built around Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 earnings call comment about "double-digit" weekly compounding growth in unsupervised (paid autonomous) miles. Using conservative (11%), base (14.5%), and optimistic (17%) weekly growth-rate scenarios that taper toward 11% over time, the analyst projects fleet size, cumulative miles, and revenue through end-2026 and end-2027, then checks those figures against Tesla's stated Cybercab production capacity to assess whether manufacturing — not demand — could become the binding constraint.

## Robotaxi
1. **Guidance interpretation drives the whole model:** The analyst anchors his model on Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 call statement that the fleet has grown at "double-digit growth rates" and a "high compounding rate" week-over-week and expects to continue "through the rest of this year," treating this as official company guidance and deliberately downweighting Elon Musk's separate ~10% weekly figure, which he views as referring only to a specific recent week. A third-party analysis (Jenma JP, cited by the analyst) interpreted "double-digit" as implying roughly 11–17%, with a realistic central estimate around 14–15%; the analyst adopted 11%, 14.5%, and 17% as his three scenarios.
2. **Conservative scenario (11% weekly growth):** Starting from an estimated ~64 vehicles and ~45,000 weekly miles today, this scenario reaches roughly 500 vehicles (range ~450–550) and ~500,000 weekly miles by end-2026, with cumulative miles near 5 million and an annualized revenue run rate of about $60–65 million (at an assumed $2.50/mile). Extended to end-2027, this grows to roughly 80,000 vehicles, ~110 million weekly miles, over 1 billion cumulative miles, and a $14–15 billion annualized revenue run rate.
3. **Base case (14.5% tapering to 11%):** By end-2026, this yields roughly 900–1,000 vehicles, ~900,000+ weekly miles, ~8 million cumulative miles, and about a $120 million revenue run rate. By end-2027, this scales to roughly 200,000–230,000 vehicles, over 300 million weekly miles, over 3 billion cumulative miles, and a roughly $40 billion annualized revenue run rate — requiring Cybercab production capacity to reach around 1 million units to avoid constraining growth.
4. **Optimistic scenario (17% tapering to 11%):** By end-2026, this implies roughly 1,200–2,000 vehicles, ~1.4 million weekly miles, ~11–12 million cumulative miles, and about $180 million in annualized revenue. By end-2027, vehicles reach roughly 450,000–500,000, weekly miles exceed 600 million, cumulative miles reach ~6.5 billion, and revenue run rate hits roughly $80 billion — requiring Cybercab production capacity near 2 million units by that point.
5. **Current fleet cross-check against RobotaxiTracker.com:** Active unsupervised Robotaxi fleet: 48 (Austin 32, Dallas 6, Houston 6, Miami 1, Tampa 3), as of 2026-07-30, per RobotaxiTracker.com — broadly consistent with the analyst's model estimate of ~64 vehicles currently in the unsupervised fleet, and the site's own tracker (using a ~10% weekly growth assumption) shows cumulative unsupervised miles around 432,000, close to the analyst's 11%-growth estimate.
6. **Miles-per-vehicle assumption is a key unknown:** The analyst assumes vehicles started at roughly 50 miles/day and are ramping toward 200 miles/day over about 80 weeks (currently modeled at ~100 miles/day), explicitly flagging this as a guess rather than disclosed data; he contrasts this with other analyses that assume a flat 50 miles/day, which produces much smaller fleet estimates (one such comparison cited elsewhere suggests up to 2,000 cars by year-end versus his ~500).
7. **Utilization may not need to stay high at scale:** The host and analyst discuss that as the fleet scales, high utilization may become less necessary for profitability — due to potential "distributed inference" revenue when cars are parked, and added services like package/food delivery — meaning the 200-miles/day plateau assumption could eventually decline rather than hold steady.
8. **Cybercab production capacity is the likely constraint only in the high-growth case:** Comparing yellow (vehicles-needed) bars against Tesla's currently stated Cybercab production capacity (125,000/year, i.e., ~2,400/week), the analyst finds capacity is not a binding constraint in the 11% or 14.5% scenarios (needing ~500,000 and ~1 million capacity respectively, deemed plausible with a mix of Cybercab and Model Y). In the 17% scenario, reaching ~2 million units of Cybercab capacity by 2027–2028 is characterized as "a bit of a stretch" if confined to U.S. Cybercab-only production, though feasible if Model Y supplements it or Berlin production comes online.
9. **Valuation implication — avoid PE ratios for exponential growth:** The analyst argues Robotaxi earnings in 2027 will look small on any curve, making a near-term P/E-based valuation misleading; he advocates a discounted cash flow approach with probability-weighting, arguing that delays hurt near-term earnings optics but barely affect long-run discounted value, and that rising confidence in execution likely increases present value over time.
10. **Regulatory footprint needed for large fleets:** Discussion estimates that servicing roughly 50% of the U.S. population (a figure Musk has previously cited) would require on the order of 300,000 vehicles across roughly 60 cities (assuming ~5,000 cars/city), contingent on a federal regulatory framework rather than city-by-city approval.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Version 15 (next major FSD version) framed as a key inflection point:** The analyst and host discuss Ashok Elluswamy's description of an upcoming FSD version as "ridiculously safe," framing it as potentially resolving many current edge cases (e.g., detecting chains/obstacles in parking lots, ignoring false stop-sign triggers) and enabling a step-change ("stair-step") in growth rate rather than smooth continuation of the current weekly growth trend. Neither speaker treats this as confirmed — the host notes Tesla itself won't know the true impact until the version is complete.
2. **Edge cases and third-party actions remain the harder problem:** The analyst distinguishes between FSD's own driving safety (which he says is already superhuman in reaction time and hazard avoidance) and the separate challenge of other drivers' unsafe actions (e.g., red-light runners, crashes into Robotaxis), arguing the latter will be an ongoing multi-year development challenge regardless of how safe Tesla's own driving becomes.
3. **Waymo's scaling problems cited as a competitive contrast:** The analyst references New Street Research's finding (from a prior show) that Waymo struggles to scale profitably because expanding its fleet erodes the premium fares it currently captures, and cites reports/videos of Waymo vehicles (he states roughly 3,500 vehicles across planned/current metros) getting stuck or blocking intersections, arguing Tesla's cost structure and safety approach could avoid these two scaling failure modes, while both face the shared challenge of other road users' behavior.
