This video from "brighterwithherbert" features host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and active venture capital investor, discussing three major Tesla rumors that emerged last week alongside confirmed news about the AI5 chip tape out. The core thesis is that Tesla may simultaneously be pursuing three potentially trillion-dollar projects — Terafab (next-gen chip manufacturing), Optimus humanoid robot production at Giga Shanghai, and a new smaller/cheaper EV — though all three rumors are treated with significant skepticism while the AI5 tape out is confirmed fact.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **AI5 Chip Tape Out Confirmed:** Elon Musk announced that the AI5 chip has completed tape out, thanking TSMC and Samsung for their help. The AI5 is described as delivering five times the useful compute of a dual AI4 system-on-chip setup, eight times the compute power, nine times the memory, and five times the bandwidth versus the current generation. A single AI5 chip is claimed to match Nvidia H100 performance for Tesla's workloads, and a dual setup is said to rival Blackwell at roughly 10% of the cost — though Larry Goldberg expressed strong skepticism about the Blackwell comparison, particularly for training workloads, arguing AI5 is optimized for inference, not training.

2. **AI4 Sufficient for FSD, AI5 Targeted at Optimus:** Elon stated that AI4 is sufficient to achieve "much better than human safety" for FSD, and that AI5 is targeted at Optimus and supercomputer clusters rather than cars. However, Larry Goldberg disagreed, arguing that future in-car use cases — including real-time map updating, Grok integration, and potential car-as-inference-computer functionality — will demand AI5 in vehicles as well. He predicted Tesla will be "very lucky" to see AI5 in production before the end of 2027, consistent with Elon's typical pattern of optimistic timelines.

3. **AI Chip Roadmap Beyond AI5:** Elon mentioned the team is now moving on to work on AI6, Dojo 3, and other chips following the AI5 tape out. Larry emphasized that in his nearly 60 years in the hardware industry, improved hardware has always been immediately absorbed and then overwhelmed by demand, making it virtually certain that AI6 and beyond will follow rapidly.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Giga Shanghai Robot Manufacturing — Executive Confirms Capability, 100K Unit Rumor Disputed:** Tesla China President Alan Wang Hao stated that "like other Tesla factories, Giga Shanghai can shoulder important responsibilities in manufacturing all new products including robots" and expressed confidence in "welcoming the arrival of a new era of robots." Following this, unnamed Chinese securities firms circulated rumors claiming Tesla is targeting annual production capacity of 100,000 Optimus units at Giga Shanghai by end of 2026. Larry Goldberg flatly rejected the 100K figure, citing analyst Brian White's assessment that Giga Shanghai has no floor space available for robot manufacturing without stopping Model 3 production, and argued the manufacturing design for humanoid robots differs fundamentally from car factories.

2. **Fremont First, Then Global Rollout:** Larry argued that Tesla is still working through core product-to-engineering-to-delivery challenges at Fremont and that the design is not yet ready to be replicated across multiple factories. He estimated the 100,000 bots planned at Fremont for the near term will use AI4 chips, with AI5 arriving roughly a year later — potentially just in time for mass production scaling. Once the Fremont design is proven, he expressed near-certainty that Tesla will roll out robot manufacturing to both Giga Berlin and Giga Shanghai.

3. **China Robot Manufacturing — Geopolitical Parallel to EVs:** Larry drew a parallel between Tesla's EV manufacturing in China and potential robot manufacturing there, arguing that China values Tesla as a technology transfer vehicle — teaching Chinese industry how to design and build cars, battery packs, and now potentially robots. He expressed 100% confidence that Tesla China will eventually build humanoid robots, comparing it to how China allowed Tesla's EV and battery manufacturing despite broader protectionist policies.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Reuters Reports New Smaller, Cheaper Tesla EV:** Reuters, citing four unnamed sources with knowledge of the matter, reported Tesla is developing a new compact SUV that would be smaller than the Model Y and built in China. The host largely dismissed this rumor, arguing Tesla's manufacturing capacity should be prioritized for Cybercab and Optimus factories rather than a new conventional vehicle. Larry Goldberg was more open to it, noting Elon did not publicly deny the Reuters report — which he typically does for fabricated stories — and that a four-to-five-seat SUV variant could serve a legitimate robotaxi use case for the roughly 4–5% of trips requiring more passenger capacity.

2. **Model 3 Replacement Logic:** Larry suggested that if a smaller, cheaper EV is in development, it could serve as a replacement for the Model 3 in China — especially if a new dedicated robot factory were built nearby, freeing up Giga Shanghai capacity. He expressed skepticism about sedan demand generally, favoring an SUV form factor for both consumer and robotaxi applications.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab Factory Priority:** The host argued that building new factories for Cybercab should be Tesla's top manufacturing priority — ahead of both robot factories and any new conventional EV program. Larry partially agreed but noted a four-seat robotaxi SUV would complement the two-seat Cybercab for use cases requiring more passenger capacity, suggesting the smaller EV rumor and robotaxi strategy may not be mutually exclusive.

## Market & Competition
1. **Terafab — Next-Gen Chip Manufacturing Rumor:** Bloomberg reported that Tesla's Terafab project team has been reaching out to chip industry suppliers including Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, Lam Research, and Samsung Electronics, and is allegedly offering to pay considerably above quoted prices to secure supplier priority. The project is estimated to cost between $5 trillion and $13 trillion. Larry cautioned that suppliers contacted by Bloomberg have every incentive to confirm engagement with Tesla regardless of actual status, and that the project is only 3–6 months old at most. He confirmed the hiring of Terafab-related engineering roles (TPMS engineers, wet etch, dry etch, etc.) is real and verifiable.

2. **Intel Partnership and Potential Acquisition:** The video noted a confirmed partnership between Tesla and Intel in connection with Terafab. Larry outlined three reasons Tesla would partner with Intel: US-based and US-owned, deep semiconductor expertise, and a management situation that has left Intel hungry for direction. He stated he "would not be shocked" to see Intel acquired by Tesla, SpaceX, or some combination of the two entities, citing remaining technology value and goodwill within Intel.

3. **Samsung's Reported Pushback:** A rumor circulated that Samsung is pushing back on participating in Terafab and instead wants Tesla to focus on expanding existing Samsung facilities. Larry noted this is unverifiable and advised against reading press reports on Terafab at all given the lack of cross-checkable sourcing.

4. **Chinese EV Competition and Ford's Warning:** The conversation briefly touched on geopolitical dynamics in EV competition, with Ford's CEO reportedly sounding alarms that if Chinese EVs are allowed into the US market, domestic automakers would be devastated. Larry noted Europe's decision to allow Chinese car imports while historically restricting US vehicles as a puzzling strategic mistake.
