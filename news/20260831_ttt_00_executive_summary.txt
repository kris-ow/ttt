## Robotaxi
- Multiple Cybercabs filmed driving fully unsupervised on Austin public streets with no one inside and no steering wheel or pedals. Asked if Cybercabs are "about to flood Austin," Musk replied "Yes"; Tesla AI VP Ashok Elluswamy said "the streets won't be the same anymore."
- Tesla began registering Cybercabs with the Texas Motor Carrier Credentialing System, climbing from 7 to 45 vehicles in a single day ahead of Thursday's launch — well above most analysts' expectation of roughly 10–30 (per jobhakdi). VIN numbers cited (up to ~2,400 range) suggest Cybercab production already exceeds 2,000 units, per the same source.
- Dallas robotaxi service area expanded roughly 2–3x its prior coverage, with operating hours across all six robotaxi cities now 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily.
- 25 Cybercabs were photographed staged in a parking lot in Tampa; Sawyer Merritt says a purpose-built, steering-wheel-free Cybercab will offer North American public rides "next week," predicting the fleet will exceed the Model Y robotaxi fleet size seen in Austin in June 2025.
- Commentators (cernbasher) argue Tesla's slow, geofenced rollout is a deliberate data-accumulation strategy, leveraging 13.6 billion supervised FSD miles and citing ~$25 camera-repair costs as a cost advantage over Waymo's lidar/radar stack.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek's SGO analysis found Tesla's own NHTSA filing on a May 2026 fatal Clute, TX crash (Model 3, 104 mph) lists automation status as "Verified Engaged" — a detail absent from all police and news reports, which cited a possible medical episode. Tesla redacted the crash narrative, software version, and operating-area status, as it does in a reported 99.9% of SGO filings.
- Electrek separately matched two fatal October 2025 crashes — one in Florida (I-4, rear-ended by a semi) and one in Arizona (Loop 202, rear-ended by a pickup) — where Tesla's NHTSA filings show driver-assist "Verified Engaged" while the car sat stopped at 0 mph in a live freeway lane. Neither crash was reported by police or local news as Tesla-related; Electrek flags phantom braking as one possible explanation but says only Tesla's data can confirm cause.
- Musk said pothole avoidance is "coming soon," a claim Electrek notes he first made in 2019 without it ever shipping.
- Musk dismissed an OpenAI executive's claim that affordable Level 5 self-driving requires AGI and remains 5-10 years away with a laughing emoji; he separately predicted AI will handle any digital task not requiring "shaping atoms" at superhuman level by the end of next year.
- Tesla's new software update rolls out Grok-based in-car voice control for climate, glove box, charging, and lighting settings.

## Humanoid Bots
- Jason Calacanis (All-In podcast) claimed, per his own secondhand account of a private demo, that Optimus hardware is "a generation and a half" ahead of Chinese humanoid robots and its software roughly five generations ahead, forecasting 1 billion Optimus units worldwide by 2036 — characterized by hosts as unconfirmed personal opinion.
- Tesla is reportedly building a ~1M-unit/year Optimus line at Fremont plus a larger-capacity line at Giga Texas; commentators disagree sharply on near-term output, with estimates ranging from 2,000-3,000 to 20,000-30,000 units in the first year.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's heat shield tiles survived reentry at speeds exceeding 16,000 mph, enabling a controlled soft splashdown in the Indian Ocean, with the vehicle remaining afloat afterward.
- A vehicle at orbital velocity carries roughly 30 million joules of kinetic energy per kilogram; Starship must dissipate trillions of joules during reentry — equivalent to roughly 700 tons of TNT — through its heat shield within minutes.
