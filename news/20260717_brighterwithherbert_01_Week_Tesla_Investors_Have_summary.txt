This panel discussion (filling in for the usual host) previews Tesla's upcoming earnings call, dissects three top shareholder-submitted "Say" questions (Optimus ramp, missed robotaxi guidance, and a merger-related compensation question), covers a Tesla executive's fixed-asset/capex framing of the Cybercab fleet, discusses Elon Musk's personal purchase of a power-generation company, and closes with SpaceX coverage of an imminent Starship test flight, a new Starlink terminal version, and merger-premium speculation from a prominent investor's CNBC appearance.

## Financials
1. **Earnings expectations set ahead of Wednesday call:** Panelists expect Tesla revenue in the $27–28 billion range and adjusted EPS possibly near or above $0.60, with healthy auto and energy margins; one panelist flagged Q2 production above 450K units (drawing down inventory) as a positive cash signal, while cautioning that free cash flow this quarter could be negative given heavy capex across new factories, Optimus, and Terafab compute investment.
2. **Cybercab fleet as a capital asset:** One panelist (building on a point he raised earlier in the week) argued that Tesla's Cybercab robotaxi fleet will be capitalized as fixed assets rather than inventory, each unit costing roughly $20,000, implying $20 billion in capital outlay per million units produced annually — a meaningful cash use, though eventually providing a large tax shield (via bonus depreciation under recent tax legislation) once Tesla becomes strongly cash-generative in a few years.
3. **SpaceX earnings call timing:** The panel expects SpaceX's first public-company earnings call one to two weeks after Tesla's (either July 29 or August 5), with August 9 as the outside deadline tied to 10-Q filing requirements (barring a 5-day NT10Q extension).

## Robotaxi
1. **Say question on missed robotaxi guidance:** A shareholder question — representing close to 900,000 shares — accused Tesla of missing short-term robotaxi guidance three quarters running (from 50% state coverage by end of 2025 to seven new cities in H1 2026). Panelists largely felt the underlying question (why is predictability so hard) was fair but the confrontational tone would likely get it dismissed or ignored on the call.
2. **Texas unsupervised fleet jumped roughly 50%:** One panelist highlighted a recent unsupervised robotaxi fleet increase in Texas of about 50% in a single move (following earlier ~20% increases), which he read as a sign operations and confidence are scaling into an exponential phase; he predicted that roughly seven years after Tesla's first supervised robotaxi ride (~a year-plus ago), the fleet could reach on the order of 3.7 million vehicles, contrasting this with Waymo's reported 3,700 cars after six years.
3. **Tesla employees riding in Cybercab:** A Tesla executive's announcement (a few days later than one panelist expected based on an earlier interview) that employees are now riding in Cybercab was described as treating the vehicle like a near-production service undergoing final testing, seen as a signal that customer rides are imminent; the same executive reportedly emphasized in a recent interview that Cybercab volumes will eventually be far larger than any prior Tesla vehicle, aiming for nationwide and eventually global scale.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Gen 3 production timeline debated:** In response to a shareholder question about Optimus Gen 3 production ramp and 2027 task capability, one panelist combined a Tesla executive's recent comments on production readiness with Musk's remarks about possible July production activity to predict a "first-off" production unit appearing around late summer to late September (plus or minus about 30 days), with actual production ramp timing left to Tesla's discretion since Optimus units are used internally rather than shipped to dealers.
2. **Generalization, not manufacturing, seen as the real bottleneck:** The same panelist argued Optimus's manufacturing ramp will be relatively manageable for Tesla compared to competitors, and that the true gating factor is Optimus's ability to learn and generalize tasks via AI rather than teleoperation — a KPI he felt investors underweight relative to production-line milestones.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Supervised mileage in Europe:** Tesla FSD Supervised has surpassed roughly 50 million kilometers (about 31 million miles) driven across the Netherlands, Estonia, Belgium, Lithuania, and Denmark, just three months after first regulatory approval in the region — described as a strong adoption rate.

## Market & Competition
1. **Physical AI moat argument:** Panelists argued that no other automaker currently offers a comparable point-to-point autonomous driving experience, framing Tesla as building a generalized platform rather than model-specific engineering; one panelist contended Tesla's competitive lead in "physical AI" (autonomy plus robotics) will widen rather than narrow, arguing few competitors can pass through the combined filter of product definition, manufacturability at scale, and generalized AI — and that this should command a valuation premium.
2. **Grok neutrality study:** A chart from an organization called Neutrality Org, measuring large language models on a political spectrum, reportedly placed Grok as the only model near a neutral midpoint among 18 models tested, with the other 17 skewing left; Musk reposted the study calling Grok the most politically neutral and truth-seeking AI. Panelists used this to argue against proposed AI regulatory bodies, contending such measures would slow US AI development while adversaries (including China) continue unconstrained, including in offensive cyber capabilities.

## Energy
1. **Musk's personal $1B+ purchase of APR Energy:** Elon Musk personally (not through Tesla or xAI) acquired APR Energy, a mobile gas/diesel turbine fleet capable of reaching full power in under 10 minutes and scaling to hundreds of megawatts, for over a billion dollars — a deal surfaced via an SEC filing rather than public announcement. Panelists speculated the purchase (giving access to over a gigawatt of fast-deployable capacity) is linked to power needs for xAI's Colossus supercluster in Memphis amid grid connection delays, and that Musk likely structured it as a personal/private deal for speed and to avoid burdening Tesla's or SpaceX's balance sheets or free cash flow; panelists called it a tactical, non-mission-aligned but pragmatic move given turbine supply shortages.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship Flight 13 imminent:** The show was timed ahead of a Starship test flight (Flight 13), notable as the first attempt to deploy commercial satellites, with the booster targeting a splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico (no catch attempt this time) and the upper stage targeting the Indian Ocean. One panelist who has watched five prior launches in person predicted the next launch could occur within about a month if this flight succeeds, with cadence potentially accelerating to every two-to-three weeks thereafter.
2. **Falcon 9 booster reflight milestone:** SpaceX achieved its 600th reflight of a reused Falcon 9 booster two days before the show, contrasted by panelists with China's recent achievement of landing a single reusable booster.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink V5 terminal launch:** A new, smaller and more power-efficient Starlink terminal (V5) has launched, with a peak data-rate spec reportedly lighter than the V4 dish; it's produced at a highly automated factory in Texas that panelists described as a rare example of vertically integrated electronics manufacturing in the US. One panelist cited production of roughly 170,000 Starlink terminals per week (about 8.5 million per year).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger premium speculation from CNBC appearance:** A prominent investor (unnamed venue detail beyond "CNBC Tuesday morning") reportedly discussed the industrial logic of a SpaceX-Tesla merger, suggesting a fair-deal premium for Tesla shareholders could need to be as high as 50% given Tesla's volatility and litigation risk, and also called Grok among the best five LLMs and floated the idea of a Starlink-branded direct-to-consumer mobile phone. Panelists were split on whether these remarks were a deliberate "trial balloon" for Musk (one panelist disagreed with that framing, calling instead for objective valuation analysis) versus the speaker simply being provocative.
2. **Shareholder question implies merger is under consideration:** A Say question asked Musk to commit to achieving at least half of his 2025 compensation plan milestones before considering any Tesla acquisition/merger offer; panelists expect Tesla to decline to answer, noting last year's compensation proxy already specifies that if a change of control occurs, operational milestones would be cancelled while market-cap-linked milestones are preserved (with any milestones achieved between merger announcement and consummation still counting) — a provision panelists said was written to protect Musk/Tesla generally, not crafted for this specific scenario.
