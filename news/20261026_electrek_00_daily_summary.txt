Electrek's Tesla coverage today spans FSD distribution strategy, another Robotaxi geofence stunt, and a possible Canadian Model 3 sourcing shift.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Tesla partners with Turo to surface FSD-enabled rental cars:** Starting October 28, Turo will let US renters filter specifically for Teslas with Full Self-Driving (Supervised) active, verified via a direct API integration with Tesla; Turo says "thousands" of FSD-enabled Teslas are already listed, and eligible hosts get a free one-month FSD trial before Tesla's $99/month subscription applies, per Electrek. Tesla disclosed 1.74 million active FSD subscriptions in Q3, a metric tied to Elon Musk's 2025 CEO Performance Award's 10-million milestone; author Fred Lambert calculates that at last quarter's pace Tesla would need about eight more years to reach it, and argues the Turo deal is effectively a supervised version of Musk's long-promised "Tesla Network." Lambert's take also raises concern that Turo's "self-driving"/"driverless" marketing language, despite FSD being a Level 2 system requiring driver responsibility, risks encouraging complacency among first-time renters, citing a recent Fairfield, California crash where a driver reportedly told police he was asleep with FSD engaged. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/26/tesla-turo-full-self-driving-test-drives/)

## Robotaxi
2. **Houston Robotaxi geofence redrawn into a rocket shape:** Tesla expanded its Houston Robotaxi service area today, announcing it on X with "Houston, we have liftoff," and the new zone — stretching from the original ~25-square-mile northwest Harris County area down to downtown and Bellaire — forms a shape Electrek says many on X also compare to a phallic image, echoing a similar Austin geofence controversy in July 2025 that Musk leaned into publicly. Robotaxi Tracker (RobotaxiTracker.com) puts the new Houston service area at about 254 square miles — roughly 10x the launch zone — while the unsupervised fleet is only 34 Model Ys (up from 22 three months ago), with no Cybercabs yet; in Austin, the tracker now counts 397 driverless vehicles, over half Cybercabs. Author Fred Lambert contrasts this with Tesla's own statement last week that safety remains its biggest Robotaxi expansion bottleneck, arguing a map drawn "for a joke" undercuts that claim, and notes Waymo has run fully driverless Houston rides since February and doubled its own service area ahead of the World Cup. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/26/tesla-robotaxi-houston-geofence-expansion-shape/)

## Electric Vehicles
3. **Tesla Canada website specs suggest return of US-built Model 3:** Tesla Canada updated the Model 3 Premium Rear-Wheel Drive listing to show 584 km of range and 0-100 km/h in 5.2 seconds, matching specs of the Fremont-built Long Range RWD Tesla sold in Canada until 2024, rather than the Shanghai-built version (463 km, 6.2 seconds) that has been unorderable since early October due to import quota limits, Electrek reports, citing a Tesla owner who first spotted the change. Canada's 25% counter-tariffs on US-built vehicles remain in effect per the Department of Finance's October 9 update, and author Fred Lambert estimates that if Tesla applies a similar tariff markup as before, a Fremont-built Premium RWD could cost roughly $65,000–$67,000 CAD, above the $50,000 cap for Canada's EV Affordability Program; Lambert views this as a likely stopgap after Tesla's Shanghai-built quota ran out, but doubts Canadian buyers would pay that price given a Model 3 Performance is currently the only orderable variant at $74,990. Electrek's Fred Lambert takes a skeptical line across all three pieces: questioning whether Tesla's FSD-rental push on Turo risks driver complacency, mocking the Houston geofence as a safety-undermining meme, and casting the possible Canadian Model 3 sourcing change as an awkward stopgap rather than a real solution.
