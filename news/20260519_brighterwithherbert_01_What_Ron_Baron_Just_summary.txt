This video features host Herbert and guest Jeff Lutz — a multi-decade supply chain executive and CEO of a high-tech manufacturing consulting firm — discussing the implications of the upcoming SpaceX IPO for Tesla investors, including Ron Baron's comments on a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger. The core thesis is that while SpaceX represents a compelling investment opportunity, Tesla shareholders should not panic-sell Tesla to buy SpaceX, and any merger speculation is premature pending the S1 filing and deal structure details. The conversation also touches on Tesla's underappreciated physical AI advantage and the long-term potential of robotaxi, Optimus, and broader physical AI deployment.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO Details:** SpaceX is scheduled to go public on June 12th on the NASDAQ under the ticker SPCX. Shareholders have approved a 5-for-1 stock split, bringing the share price down from $527 to $105. The S1 was expected to drop the Wednesday following the video's recording. Anthropic and SpaceX are described as confirmed IPOs for 2026, with OpenAI's status uncertain. Combined, these mega-IPOs (Anthropic, SpaceX, OpenAI) are projected to raise more than all US IPOs in 2025 combined, with a combined estimated valuation of $1.6 trillion — though SpaceX alone is estimated by some at $1.75 trillion, and prediction markets have it as high as $2.2–$2.4 trillion.

2. **Major Investors Lining Up for SpaceX:** Elon Musk confirmed he will not be selling any of his SpaceX shares at IPO. BlackRock has announced plans to invest $5–$10 billion. Ron Baron stated he has placed a $1 billion IPO order. Jeff Lutz noted that SpaceX IPO demand in illiquid pre-markets is oversubscribed by double-digit percentages, and that early pre-market trading implies a $2.2–$2.4 trillion valuation.

3. **Tesla Shareholders and SpaceX Allocation:** Alexandra Merz ran a poll of her predominantly Tesla-investor audience asking how they would fund SpaceX purchases. Results (from approximately 400 respondents): 15% would sell Tesla, 6% would sell many holdings including Tesla (~21% total selling Tesla), 10% would sell other holdings but not Tesla, and the vast majority said they would use available cash. Both Herbert and Jeff Lutz stated they would consider buying SpaceX but would not sell Tesla to do so, and both said they wanted to read the S1 first.

4. **Ron Baron's View on Tesla-SpaceX Merger:** Ron Baron, when asked about a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger, said he would not bet on it — clarifying that when he doesn't know something, his answer is simply "I don't know." He acknowledged there are good reasons for and against a merger, noted that Elon operates based on what's best for his broader mission rather than personal financial gain, and said whatever Elon decides will likely have good rationale. Herbert interpreted Baron's comments as more of an "I don't know" than a firm "no."

5. **Ron Baron's SpaceX and Starlink Valuation:** Baron described SpaceX as potentially the largest company on the planet, projecting Tesla could reach $2,000–$2,500 per share over the next 10 years. He stated Starlink alone could be worth $14–$15 trillion, citing approximately $1 trillion in potential annual revenues at 70–80% profit margins. He noted Starlink currently has around 15 million customers in 2026 (up from 9 million in 2025), growing at approximately 50% per year, with a potential path to 300 million customers in 10 years. Starlink currently has 10,000 satellites and is on its way to 20,000.

6. **SpaceX's Orbital Dominance:** As of 2026, SpaceX accounted for approximately 87% of total global mass sent to orbit — 841 of 969 metric tons. The rest of the world combined accounted for 13%. Starship is designed to transport over one megaton of payload to space per year. Jeff Lutz emphasized this is not a close competition, and that rivals such as Blue Origin are years behind.

7. **Terafab Chip Manufacturing:** Ron Baron discussed Elon's plan to build a chip fabrication facility called Terafab, designed to produce chips at a fraction of the cost of Nvidia's chips, aiming for 50 times the world's current compute output. Baron noted Elon currently uses only 3% of what Nvidia provides and, given Nvidia's ~80% profit margins, believes he can manufacture chips cheaper, faster, and simpler in-house. He said Elon will never stop buying Nvidia chips but sees an opportunity to supplement at massive scale.

8. **Grok and AI Market Size:** Baron drew a parallel between Grok (XAI) and Anthropic, noting Anthropic grew from $9 billion in annualized revenues to $40–$45 billion in four to five months. He put the total AI opportunity at approximately $27 trillion per year — described as the first time a specific number of that magnitude had been publicly cited on the show.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla's Physical AI as an Underappreciated Moat:** Both Herbert and Jeff Lutz argued that Tesla's real-world physical AI is significantly undervalued relative to LLM-based AI companies. They cited a quote from a senior Tesla AI executive (identified as "Yantasai" in the transcript) who stated: "Making seamless real-world agents is a lot harder than digital world agents, especially since the compute and energy budgets are extremely frugal compared to an H200. I have great respect for Tesla AI engineers pulling rabbits out of the hat over and over again. It may not be considered a frontier lab, but it is truly frontier." Jeff Lutz added that he knows of no company doing physical real-world AI at Tesla's scale.

2. **FSD Fleet Scale:** Jeff Lutz cited 1.3 million cars currently running Tesla Full Self-Driving, with customers paying a monthly subscription. He contrasted FSD's requirement for near-perfect decision-making (described as needing to be right 99.9999% of the time) with LLMs, which can hallucinate or produce wrong answers without consequence.

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Deployment Philosophy vs. Competitors:** Jeff Lutz argued that Tesla's slower-than-announced robotaxi rollout reflects a deliberate strategy to build the best cost structure and most scalable system, rather than rushing to deploy a few hundred or few thousand vehicles unprofitably as competitors might. He suggested critics who wanted Tesla to meet its original timeline would have gotten a robotaxi service that was likely not profitable. He framed this as a longer-term competitive advantage.

2. **Tesla Robotaxi as a Near-Term Value Catalyst:** Herbert noted that Tesla shareholders are anticipating a potential 10x value increase from the robotaxi business alone, and that any merger speculation is premature because Tesla is "just now about to get our robotaxi due." Jeff Lutz agreed that both robotaxi and Optimus represent unmet demand "for many years if not measured in decades," framing them as non-zero-sum opportunities.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus and the Broader Physical AI Opportunity:** Herbert argued that Tesla's physical AI will not be limited to cars and humanoid robots — it extends to any physical device with a chip and a camera, including smart home devices, agricultural machinery, factory equipment, and more. Jeff Lutz agreed, noting that any machine in any factory or farm could be given a chip and camera and become intelligent, posing the rhetorical question of what TAM one would assign to all factory and industrial equipment globally. He referenced Jeff Bezos's startup Prometheus as pursuing a similar concept of AI-enabling existing factories.

## Financials

1. **Tesla Price Target from Ron Baron:** Ron Baron projected Tesla stock could reach $2,000–$2,500 per share over the next 10 years, with the company potentially worth $10–$30 trillion or more. He stated: "Now is the time for Tesla."

2. **Merger Timeline and Deal Structure Uncertainty:** Herbert noted that according to a source named Larry Goldberg, boards of both Tesla and SpaceX could announce an intention to merge without immediately disclosing deal structure terms, meaning the actual deal terms may not be known for 8 months to a year after any such announcement. He added that neither Tesla nor SpaceX reportedly knows yet what the relative valuations or deal structure would look like.
