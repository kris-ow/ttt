## Brief

- Cybercab launch event teased, rider lottery runs through Aug 23

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Cybercab launch event is imminent; riding through August 23 enters a lottery for tickets, with five winners announced August 25. Multiple hosts read this as confirmation commercial launch is near, following Tesla's typical employee-first, public-shortly-after pattern.
- Cybercab production estimates (Bhakdi, unconfirmed): roughly 2,000 built to date, a run rate near 2,000–3,000/month by mid-September, and "extremely likely" over 10,000 by year-end — though deployment/in-service numbers remain far more uncertain, with a rival forecaster ("Genma") projecting only about 1,000 vehicles in service by next year, a figure Bhakdi disputes as too low.
- Tesla VP Lars Moravy told Sawyer Merritt in person there is no 2,500-unit cap on Cybercab, pushing back on a widely cited deployment-limit narrative.
- New Austin permits near the St. Elmo Tesla service center show a charging hub starting construction September 7 (phase 1 complete January) with 12 V3.5 cabinets supporting 48 posts; a "80 wireless chargers" reference appears to be a design-firm error per analyst Marco, with true wireless-charging hardware likely a later phase, not before Q1 next year.
- Persistent Robotaxi navigation complaints continue in Austin: riders report looping at pickup/drop-off adding 5–15 minutes, a mid-intersection drop-off, a 10-minute ride turned 47-minute detour, and one vehicle overshooting a drop-off near 70 mph before a 40-minute looping episode.
- Cybercabs spotted in Baltimore, suggesting geographic testing beyond current markets; roughly 97 Cybercabs seen at a Houston lot, with unconfirmed rumors of a Houston geofence expansion by month-end.
- Tesla posted a Palo Alto "events manager for Robotaxi" job listing to scale events and media partnerships, compared by one host to Waymo's Raiders partnership.

## Autonomous Driving
- Bhakdi argues Tesla's aggressive Cybercab production ramp implies leadership confidence FSD is on track, framing FSD progress as a scalable engineering buildout rather than open-ended research.
- FSD subscriber growth cited at 170,000 new paid subscribers in Q1 and roughly 150–160k in Q2, per one host; FSD v14 described as a major usability improvement over prior builds.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's flight 13 vehicle tipped over into the Indian Ocean but stayed afloat instead of exploding — a first for the program. After roughly 24 days at sea and rough conditions that at one point had Musk posting recovery was "not looking good," the ship was towed to calmer water off Christmas Island for inspection before transport back to Starbase.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Former Tesla global sales president John McNeil said an upcoming Roadster demo — reportedly using a SpaceX cold-gas thruster system at McGregor, Texas — could be an attempt to "knit Tesla and SpaceX's futures together," predicting the companies "are going to come together probably sooner rather than later." Unconfirmed and treated skeptically by one host regarding timing and whether the car will be manned.
- The Information reports Tesla could unveil a redesigned Roadster as early as this month: a limited SpaceX-branded thruster edition (sub-1-second 0-60 per Musk) costing hundreds of thousands of dollars, plus a separate consumer version without thrusters. Unconfirmed; Roadster has a long history of missed dates.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX completed its acquisition of Cursor, with the team joining SpaceX's AI org to work on Grok, Grok Build, Grok Bot, and Grok API.
- Musk floated 2029 as a timeline for orbital AI compute, calling it "probably" the only way to scale AI given Earth power/permitting constraints — a hedge one host flags given Musk's history of missed dates.
- Grok bot launched in public beta, described as a persistent AI agent workforce (Salesforce, Slack, LinkedIn, email access); priced around $200/month standalone or bundled at $300/month "Super Grok Heavy." Investor Gavin Baker called it "another Claude Code moment."
- Grok 4.6 tied for #1 on a community agentic benchmark (score 59, level with Claude Opus 5 Max); Microsoft shipped Grok 4.6 into GitHub Copilot. Musk conceded Anthropic's model is "smarter overall" but claimed Grok is faster/cheaper, predicting Grok 4.7 (2.1T parameters, weeks away) "has a good chance of exceeding all current models."
- Terafab site: Tesla/SpaceX own ~6,640 acres with 2,300 more in final recording and an estimated 2,000–3,000 acres still needed for the 4+ mile facility; Phase 1 alone estimated near $17B, full project up to $120B.

## Electric Vehicles
- Einride ordered 500 Tesla Semis — its largest deployment — tripling Einride's fleet from ~250 to 750 trucks, deliveries September 2026 through September 2028; Einride's 2028 breakeven plan reportedly needs 1,500–2,000 trucks, hinting at further orders. This pushes total tracked Semi orders above 1,100, up from ~686.
- Edmunds tested the Model Y L Premium at 358 miles, 38 miles (12%) beyond its 320-mile EPA estimate, calling it the longest range of any Model Y tested.
- Musk highlighted a domestic-content ranking putting Model 3 and Model Y at the top with 84% American-made parts.

## Energy
- Sweden's 1,021-day IF Metall union standoff ended with no collective agreement signed; over 250 dark Supercharger stalls across 20 locations are now switching on, expanding Tesla's Swedish network over 20%. Strike participation had fallen from ~130 to a handful before the action was called off.
- LG Energy opened a $4B Lansing, Michigan plant supplying LFP cells for Tesla's Megapack 3, targeting 35+ GWh annual capacity.
- A third Megapack factory is coming online in Texas; Megapack margins described as settling in the "lower 20s" percent, per one host.

## Market & Competition
- Retail TSLA ownership has fallen to 15.8% of float per cited research, with institutional buying accelerating near 12-month lows; trading volume reportedly hit its lowest since 2016.
- Waymo's Ohio-built vehicle now serves all riders in SF, Phoenix, and LA; reporting pegs its all-in cost near $100,000/unit ($38K base plus ~128% tariff plus ~$25K hardware/software) versus an expected $20,000–$25,000 for Cybercab.
