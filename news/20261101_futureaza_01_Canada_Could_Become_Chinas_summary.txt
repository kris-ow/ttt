This episode of futureaza covers the competitive landscape for Chinese and Chinese-made EVs entering Canada, including Chery's potential manufacturing plans, Kia's affordable China-built EV offering, and a brief Tesla-specific thread about Canadian import quotas and a reversed Model 3 spec-page change.

## Market & Competition
1. **Chery weighing a Canadian factory:** A host says there is no actual Chery factory in Canada yet, but discussions are underway to build one, with cars potentially going on sale in Canada around Q2–Q3 2027 under a new agreement with China; he frames this as Canada getting closer to Chinese cars that have "never been here before."
2. **Canada's Chinese-EV quota barely used:** Canada's second quota window for Chinese EVs has been used at under 1%; per the hosts, 15,931 of 49,000 available permits were used in the first year. The hosts dispute Tesla's claim that it halted shipments from China to Canada because its quota was exhausted, calling this "not true," though they acknowledge the government doesn't disclose manufacturer-level data and they lack confirmed information on exactly how permits are allocated.
3. **Canada courting a real factory, not just assembly:** One host suggests Canada is trying to attract a Chinese automaker to build an actual local factory (using local workers and suppliers) rather than a kit-assembly plant, and may be withholding permits from manufacturers like Tesla that aren't planning to localize production — though he stresses this is speculative ("we're not really sure what's going on").
4. **Prime Minister's quota comments:** Per one host, Canada's Prime Minister said in a New York speech that the vast majority of cars imported into Canada under the deal are Teslas, but that he expects this won't remain the case indefinitely.
5. **Kia's affordable China-built EV:** Kia plans to bring an electric model (referred to as the EV3) to Canada priced under 40,000 Canadian dollars, built in China and sold through Kia's existing Canadian dealer and service network — pitched as a way for Canadians to get a Chinese-made EV without waiting for direct Chinese brands to arrive.
6. **US border comments clarified:** A headline claiming "Chinese cars still won't be able to cross the US-Canada border" was corrected; a host quotes a US official (Pete Hoekstra) as saying such cars may come from China to Canada but won't cross into the US, meaning they can't be registered there — not that it's physically impossible. The hosts characterize this official as not a reliable source based on past inaccuracies.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Model 3 Canada spec page briefly reverted:** Tesla's Canada website briefly showed an updated Model 3 specification resembling American-built cars before reverting hours later; the hosts say the Chinese-built version sold in Canada uses LG (not Panasonic) batteries with a shorter range than the US rear-wheel-drive spec (cited around 363 miles for the US version). They clarify this was a site update error, not a sign that American-built Model 3s are returning to Canada, and that US-built cars remain subject to a 25% tariff that makes them uncompetitive there.
2. **Tesla pausing new Canada orders:** Per the hosts, Tesla's website indicates it is not accepting new orders in Canada until March 2027, when it may receive new import permits.
