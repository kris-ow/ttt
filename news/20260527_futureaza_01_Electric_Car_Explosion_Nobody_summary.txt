This video is a lighthearted, comedic review by Brian (FutureAzA) and Mark (The Tesla Life) of Top Gear's list of 44 most exciting upcoming electric vehicles. The hosts work through the list car by car, offering quick reactions, design critiques, and skepticism about whether many of the featured vehicles will ever reach production in their rendered form. The core thesis, stated at the outset, is that the EV market has matured dramatically — where 10–15 years ago affordable options barely existed, today there is a wide and growing global selection, even if many are not yet available in North America.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **EV market maturity framing:** The hosts open by noting that while a decade or more ago it was genuinely true that affordable EVs were scarce, today there is a broad and expanding global selection. The caveat is that many of the most interesting options are not yet available in preferred markets, colors, or brands — but the variety is real and growing.

2. **Peugeot E208 GTI (No. 1 on Top Gear's list):** Shown only as a rendering, the car is noted as having 276 horsepower. The hosts are skeptical of render-only reveals and note that many vehicles on the list claim up to 300 miles of range on the European test cycle, which they address more fully later.

3. **European vs. EPA range cycles explained:** When discussing the Mercedes C-Class electric's claimed 473-mile range (on the CLTC Chinese cycle), the hosts clarify that non-EPA range figures are not inherently dishonest — CLTC is calibrated for slower, more congested Chinese urban driving, and WLTP is calibrated for typical European driving conditions. They argue these figures reflect real-world expectations for their respective markets.

4. **Hyundai Ioniq 3:** Noted as claiming 305 miles of range on the European cycle. The hosts caution that charging from 10–80% would reduce usable range by roughly a third, and that real-world figures would be lower still. They suggest it could be competitive if priced well, given the popularity of small SUVs.

5. **Hyundai Staria Electric:** Highlighted as significant because it fills a gap — an electric minivan segment largely absent in North America. Mark notes Hyundai also has a smaller van (the PV4 or PV5) coming to Canada later in the year (i.e., later in 2026). The hosts confirm the Staria is not made in China, with production across Korea and two final assembly plants elsewhere.

6. **BYD Denza 79 GT:** The hosts are genuinely enthusiastic about this vehicle, citing 952 brake horsepower, crab-walk capability, and instant charging. Already on sale in China and heading to Europe. They discuss its fastback/shooting brake design and note its camera-based side mirrors, confirming that Europe has permitted camera mirrors for years.

7. **Rivian R2 (No. 16):** The hosts express surprise it ranks so low given that manufacturing has already begun and the vehicle has been seen publicly. They drove it previously and found the slightly smaller size appealing, noting it could be a strong fit for both European and North American markets. They flag that it lacks even a cramped third row. Rivian will be present at the Michigan summer meetup offering test drives (of other models, not the R2).

8. **Volkswagen ID Polo / ID2 / ID Golf:** The hosts call it an early winner on nostalgia alone, noting it closely resembles a Golf. No pricing or specs confirmed yet, but they believe the heritage branding will drive strong sales.

9. **Polestar 2 Gen 2 and Polestar 4 Estate:** The Polestar 4 estate variant appears to add a rear window absent from the standard model. On Polestar generally, the hosts note that US pricing has been too high relative to perceived brand value — "it's hard to charge Mercedes pricing without the Mercedes logo."

10. **Jaguar concept:** The hosts are harshly critical of Jaguar's strategy, noting the brand has had nothing to sell for over a year and a half to two years while running brand-image advertising. They question what Jaguar dealerships have been doing during this period. Despite this, Top Gear apparently gave the prototype a drive and called it "a proper Jag of the highest order."

11. **Render quality and concept skepticism:** A running theme throughout is that a significant portion of the 44 vehicles are either pure CGI renders, early-stage concepts, or designs the hosts believe will look substantially different in production. Several entries (BMW hydrogen, Bentley Urban SUV, Ford Universal EV, Alpine A3, and others) receive particular mockery for appearing to be low-quality renders or outright placeholder images.

## Market & Competition

1. **China-origin vehicles expanding to Europe:** Multiple vehicles on the list — including the BYD Denza 79 GT and MG Cyber Stir Coupe — are already on sale in China and are now moving into European markets. The hosts note that Chinese manufacturing enables lower price points and that MG's heritage as a non-premium brand means the Cyber Stir coupe does not need to be expensive.

2. **Stellantis/Jeep credibility gap:** The hosts express skepticism about the Jeep Recon, citing Stellantis's track record of announced products that underdeliver. They predict the Jeep Recon will likely be "an overpriced, oversized pavement princess" given its apparent weight and cost trajectory.

3. **Toyota/Subaru partnership noted:** The Subaru E Outback is identified as still being a Toyota platform cousin, with the hosts noting that the latest generation of Toyota EVs has meaningfully improved in horsepower, efficiency, and range for the 2027 model year.
