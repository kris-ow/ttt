This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers a wide range of topics spanning Tesla's energy storage business, FSD regulatory progress in Europe, Optimus supply chain developments, robotaxi infrastructure updates, and a SpaceX Starfall spacecraft debut. The video's core argument is that Tesla is quietly making significant progress across multiple fronts — particularly in energy (a landmark Megapack deal) and autonomous driving (European FSD approvals) — even as the two highest-valuation business lines, robotaxi and Optimus, remain relatively quiet publicly.

## Energy
1. **Historic Tesla–Nat Power Megapack Agreement:** Nat Power, a European energy infrastructure platform, has entered into a multi-year supply and execution agreement with Tesla covering more than 25 gigawatt hours of battery storage across European markets. Projects will initially be sited in Italy and the UK, with a total capacity target of more than 100 gigawatt hours across five initial projects. The host notes this is by far the largest energy storage deal Tesla has ever signed — the previous record was in the neighborhood of 15 gigawatt hours. For context, Tesla deployed roughly 46 gigawatt hours of battery storage for all of 2025, meaning this single deal represents more than half of that figure.

2. **Deal Scale and Financial Implications:** The agreement's aggregate construction value is estimated at $4–5 billion, with expected project revenues exceeding $15 billion over 20 years. To reach 25 gigawatt hours, the host estimates Tesla would need to supply over 6,400 Megapacks, and at roughly $1 million per unit (potentially less given economies of scale), this could represent over $6 billion in revenue for Tesla over the next few years. The host notes that the exact meaning of "construction value" is uncertain.

3. **Autobidder Revenue Warranties:** The agreement includes long-term revenue warranties through Tesla's Autobidder platform, with Tesla effectively guaranteeing Nat Power a specified level of revenue generated by Autobidder software managing the battery storage units. The host frames this as a signal of Tesla's confidence in its Autobidder software.

4. **Structural Innovation in Energy Procurement:** The host highlights that this deal is structurally significant because it is the first time battery storage has been contracted, financed, and executed across multiple jurisdictions under a single integrated framework. It simultaneously addresses manufacturing slots, grid connections, permitting timelines, and financing — breaking the traditional "silo effect" that has historically slowed energy storage deployment.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Finland and EU-Wide FSD Approval Progress:** Finnish transport authority Traficom is considering whether to recognize the Netherlands' prior approval of Tesla's supervised Full Self-Driving before a broader EU-wide TCMV vote. That official TCMV vote is now expected in October 2026, though Traficom is prepared to move faster if sufficient information is obtained after summer. The approval would apply only to Teslas with the latest generation hardware, of which there are approximately 6,500 in Finland. Finnish authorities described the system positively: it can manage challenging weather, does not require lane markings to operate, often makes safer decisions than human drivers, and has faster response capabilities in many situations. The TCMV is set to next discuss the FSD approval process on June 30, 2026.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab NVH Validation Automated:** Following footage of Cybercabs on the test track in Austin shared by Joe Tegtmeyer, a commenter asked whether the vehicles' high-quality microphones and accelerometers could automate their own noise, vibration, and harshness (NVH) validation drives. Wes Moral confirmed, "Yes, this is happening." The host interprets this to mean Tesla has moved beyond manual data gathering for NVH — Cybercabs are autonomously collecting audio and vibration data on test tracks, sending it back to Tesla's central hub, where machine learning is used to analyze and quantify the data for iterative improvements, creating faster feedback loops by removing humans from the manual processing step. The host acknowledges some of this is speculative inference.

2. **Robotaxi Delay Theory Dismissed:** The host directly addresses circulating speculation that Elon Musk is intentionally delaying the robotaxi rollout to suppress Tesla's stock price so SpaceX can acquire it at a discount. The host assigns essentially a 0% probability to this theory, noting it would likely constitute securities fraud. The host's simpler explanation: Full Self-Driving's pickup, drop-off, and navigation performance still needs work; Tesla is building out robotaxi service infrastructure; there was a recent incident of a remote operator driving a Tesla robotaxi into a fence; and Tesla is continuing to address the long tail of real-world edge cases while internally testing what the host believes is v15 of FSD, which Tesla is reportedly pleased with. Elon's approximately 20% ownership stake in Tesla — meaning a $500 billion swing in market cap equals a ~$100 billion swing in his net worth — is cited as a structural argument against deliberate suppression.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus 3 Supply Chain Taking Shape:** A Chinese news source, IT Home, reported that Tesla has begun collaborating with companies including Merl Automation and Asia Optical to advance mass production of Optimus 3. Allied Automation is said to primarily supply harmonic reducers and joint modules, and is jointly producing these components alongside Shenzhen Kadali and Rayong. Because the project includes Kadali's existing factory, reports suggest it will soon enter mass production at that supplier level. Asia Optical is reportedly responsible for the robot's eyes, utilizing spherical and aspherical lens technology, with new products expected to enter mass production in the second half of 2026 and into 2027.

2. **Mass Production Timeline Tempered:** The host cautions that true mass production of Optimus at Tesla itself will not happen in 2026 — 2027 at the earliest is the host's view. However, the host reads these supply chain reports as a signal that Tesla is getting components sorted to begin low-volume production later this year. The host also acknowledges that Optimus has effectively produced zero public news for months, calling it "a complete zero," and expressing anticipation that this silence will break in the coming months, given Optimus is estimated to represent roughly 80% of Tesla's future valuation.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Tops Cars.com American-Made Index:** Tesla's Model 3 ranked #1 and Model Y ranked #2 on the 2026 Cars.com American-Made Index. The top five is rounded out by the Jeep Gladiator (#3), Jeep Grand Cherokee (#4), and Honda Ridgeline (#5). The first Ford vehicle on the list is the Ford Expedition at #13, and the first GM vehicle is the GMC Canyon at #25. The host notes that the Cybertruck does not appear on the list because heavy-duty trucks do not receive fuel economy ratings or the detailed assembly information required for inclusion.

2. **European EV Sales — Comps Caveat:** Tesla's sales in the EU/EFTA/UK region were up 57% year-over-year for January through May 2026, but the host repeatedly emphasizes this is measured against a soft comparable period in 2025 when Tesla was transitioning to refreshed Model 3 and Model Y. The host also flags that Chery Auto is outselling Tesla on raw unit volume year-to-date in the region by over 4,000 units, with Chery up 316% year-over-year.

3. **Tesla Free Supercharging Competition:** Tesla has launched a free Supercharging competition, with the prize being free Supercharging for life (for as long as the winner owns their Tesla). Winners will be determined by Supercharger network usage throughout 2026, and Tesla will celebrate its nine billionth Supercharger user in January 2027.
