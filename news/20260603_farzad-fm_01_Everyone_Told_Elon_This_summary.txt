## Market & Competition
1. **Anthropic Renting Compute from SpaceX AI:** In May 2026, Anthropic — widely regarded as the builder of the best AI models in the world and Elon Musk's direct AI competitor — was announced as the first major external customer for SpaceX AI's Colossus supercomputer. The video frames this as extraordinary: a frontier AI competitor forced to rent compute from a company nobody considers an AI infrastructure provider, because there is simply not enough available compute elsewhere.

2. **SpaceX AI / xAI Merger and Colossus Supercomputer:** xAI merged with SpaceX in February 2026 (per the video) and was rebranded as SpaceX AI. In 2025, SpaceX AI spent nearly $8 billion in the first 9 months and built the Colossus supercomputer — described at the time as the world's largest AI supercomputer — in 122 days, then doubled it to 200,000 GPUs in another 92 days.

3. **AI Inference Cost Collapse:** ChatGPT-4-level inference cost approximately $36 per million tokens at launch in early 2023 and fell to under $1 per million tokens by 2025 for equivalent capability — a 36x cost reduction in roughly two years. DeepSeek's R1 model was trained for approximately $6 million while American labs were spending hundreds of millions for comparable performance. The video argues AI cost curves are steeper than Moore's Law and show no signs of flattening.

4. **Keyman Risk — Elon Musk:** The video identifies Elon Musk as the single biggest risk to the entire system. Without him, Tesla remains a strong car and energy company and SpaceX retains Gwynne Shotwell for operations — but the cross-company integration strategy and the long-range vision connecting all pieces to a Mars colony in three decades would be lost. The video calls this "the biggest keyman risk of any company in the history of business."

5. **Terafab Chip Foundry:** Tesla and SpaceX are building a chip foundry called Terafab targeting production of between 100 and 200 billion custom AI chips per year — representing roughly one full terawatt of compute power annually, described as approximately the size of the entire US electrical grid. Approximately 80% of that output is earmarked for space applications.

6. **Starship Cost to Orbit:** The cost to put 1 kilogram into orbit on the Space Shuttle was between $10,000 and $54,000. Falcon 9 brought that down to approximately $2,700. Starship's target is $10 to $100 per kilogram — a 30x to 300x reduction from Falcon 9 prices and over 1,000x cheaper than where the industry was a decade ago.

7. **Starlink Scale:** Starlink has over 10,000 satellites in orbit today, with FCC filings for up to 1 million. New V3 satellites deliver 1 terabit per second of bandwidth each, with 60 fitting into a single Starship. SpaceX is targeting approximately 6,000 V3 deployments per year. Monthly subscribers exceed 10 million and annual revenue exceeds $10 billion.
