Electrek's Tesla/SpaceX coverage today spans an ongoing FSD driver-complacency safety story and a major report on an accelerating SpaceX–Tesla merger timeline.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Another sleeping Tesla driver ticketed in Canada:** Ontario Provincial Police charged a Tesla driver with careless driving after spotting them asleep with eyes closed and hands off the wheel on Ottawa's Highway 417 during rush hour, per Electrek citing OPP's account; the charge carries a $490 set fine and six demerit points, with Highway Traffic Act maximums up to $2,000, six months in jail, and a two-year licence suspension if it reaches court. Fred Lambert catalogs a string of similar Canadian incidents this year (British Columbia cases in March, July, and two in August) plus U.S. incidents including a September Colorado report, a Congressional inquiry citing 43 videos of apparently sleeping Tesla drivers (NBC News-sourced, per the article), and an October Fairfield, California crash where a driver said he was asleep on FSD before hitting a parked patrol car, injuring two officers. Lambert argues this reflects "automation complacency" from FSD improving enough to let supervision lapse, contrasts it with Tesla's camera-based monitoring (which per Tesla's owner's manual doesn't require full eye visibility and still functions with sunglasses on) versus GM's infrared eye-tracking Super Cruise, and calls Tesla's "Drowsiness detected. Stay focused with FSD" alert and lenient strike system inadequate; he calls for stricter detection and enforcement from Tesla, which NHTSA already recalled over 2 million vehicles for in 2023 regarding Autopilot monitoring. ## Financials
2. **SpaceX-Tesla merger could be announced in December, Bloomberg reports:** Citing Bloomberg sources, Electrek reports talks between Tesla and SpaceX have moved to negotiating price and control, with a possible announcement as soon as December 3 (Tesla's annual shareholder meeting date) or early 2027 as an alternative target; one open variable is how many SpaceX shares Tesla investors would receive, implying SpaceX would absorb Tesla. SpaceX's valuation is over $1.9 trillion following its June IPO versus Tesla's roughly $1.5 trillion, and Fred Lambert notes a deal would be the largest merger ever, dwarfing Vodafone-Mannesmann's $183 billion and SpaceX's own roughly $250 billion xAI purchase. Neither company commented; Tesla stock rose as much as 1.9% on the report. The article notes Musk owns about 20% of Tesla but roughly 85% of SpaceX's voting power per its S-1, and that change-in-control terms in Musk's 2025 CEO award would let earned shares vest immediately, with a deal valuing Tesla at $2 trillion (about a 33% premium) clearing the first market-cap tranche instantly; his 286 million restricted shares from the 2018 package would also accelerate. Bloomberg, per the article, cites ring-fencing Tesla's China business (which shipped 67,829 vehicles from Giga Shanghai in October alone) as a key obstacle, quoting a JPMorgan analyst calling SpaceX's government/defense work and Tesla's China footprint "key dynamics that are at odds with each other." The article also notes the new Democrat-controlled Congress has signaled scrutiny of Musk's companies, and that the companies reportedly want to close before Trump leaves office. Lambert's take: he calls the boards' "independent committees" not credible, argues Tesla shareholders may not care given four years of stock underperformance, and characterizes the merger as folding an underperforming car/energy company into a premium-valued rocket/satellite/AI company under one person's control. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/12/spacex-tesla-merger-announcement-december-report/)
