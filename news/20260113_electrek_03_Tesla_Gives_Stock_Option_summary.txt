Tesla granted Senior Vice President of Automotive Tom Zhu a new stock option package, per an SEC filing, as part of a long-term retention structure.

## Financials
1. **Zhu's option grant:** A SEC filing shows Zhu received 520,021 stock options at a strike price of $435.80, granted January 8, 2026; the face value of underlying shares is approximately $226.6 million, though the article notes options have zero intrinsic value at grant since the strike equals the market price at that time.
2. **Vesting schedule:** Per the filing, 1/48th of the options vest starting April 5, 2027, with 1/48th vesting monthly thereafter, fully vesting by March 2031 — a roughly five-year retention structure requiring Tesla's stock price to rise for the package to have realized value.
3. **Comparison to Musk's package:** The article contrasts this with Musk's 2025 CEO Performance Award and ratified prior packages, which shareholders approved in late 2025 and which could be worth hundreds of billions and up to $1 trillion if aggressive targets are met.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's shrinking "critical exec" ranks:** Tesla now reports only two executive officers besides CEO Musk — Zhu and CFO Vaibhav Taneja — down from more at other large tech peers (Google has 3, Apple and Nvidia 4 each, Microsoft 5), which the article calls unprecedented for a company of Tesla's size.
2. **Zhu's background:** Zhu has worked at Tesla over a decade, led the Gigafactory Shanghai ramp-up, later oversaw North American sales and the Gigafactory Texas ramp, and was reported in 2023 to have become effectively Musk's No. 2 managing global automotive operations; 2024 reports suggested he might return to China, though his current day-to-day role is described as unclear.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues Zhu is arguably more important to Tesla's operations than Musk yet receives a far smaller package at a near all-time-high strike price; he calls Musk's compensation unjustifiable by comparison and criticizes Tesla's board as enabling a "stock-pumping machine" primarily benefiting Musk. A top reader comment disputes using face value for options, noting the package could be worth far less — or zero — depending on share price performance.
