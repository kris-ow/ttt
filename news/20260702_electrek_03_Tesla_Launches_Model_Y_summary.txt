Tesla has launched the Model Y L — a stretched, three-row, six-seat SUV — in the US and Puerto Rico, priced at $61,990 for the initial "Launch Series" trim, higher than analyst expectations and above both key EV rivals.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y L specs and configuration:** The Model Y L extends the standard Model Y's wheelbase by 150 mm (to 3,040 mm) and overall length by roughly 180 mm, accommodating a 2+2+2 six-seat layout. Second-row captain's chairs include heating, ventilation, powered armrests, and one-touch fold; third-row seats add heat, power recline, and child-seat anchors. Tesla claims 0–60 mph in 4.4 seconds and 325 miles of EPA-estimated range. Additional features include 89 cubic feet of cargo, adaptive damping, staggered tires, upgraded acoustic glass, a 19-speaker audio system, a second-row 8-inch touchscreen, 50W cooled wireless charging, and FSD Supervised with integrated Grok AI.

2. **Pricing above expectations:** Analysts had anticipated a US price around $54,000, based on the ~$4,000 premium the L commands over the standard Model Y in China (where it launched in August 2025 at roughly $47,000). Instead, Tesla opened US orders at $61,990 — approximately $4,000 above the Model Y Performance ($57,990) and $22,000 above the base rear-wheel-drive Model Y ($39,990). Tesla has used "Launch Series" pricing before to lead with a loaded trim ahead of cheaper configurations.

3. **Lineup context:** Tesla added a cramped third-row option to the standard US Model Y in January 2026 and launched a seven-seat Model Y in Europe in February 2026, but neither offered genuine adult-usable third-row space. The Model Y L is described as Tesla's first real answer for buyers needing six usable seats.

## Market & Competition
1. **Three-row EV rivals undercut the Launch Series:** The Kia EV9 starts at $54,900 (up to 304 miles of range) and the Hyundai Ioniq 9 starts at $58,955 (up to 335 miles) — both below the Model Y L's $61,990 opening price. The Tesla counters with a faster 0–60 of 4.4 seconds.

## Editorial Stance
The author views the Model Y L's 2+2+2 layout and specs as genuinely compelling for family buyers, but flags the $61,990 Launch Series price as a "Tesla tax" at a moment when Hyundai and Kia are proving competitive alternatives. The real test, the author argues, is what a standard (non-Launch Series) Model Y L will cost once initial demand is absorbed — and whether it lands near the $54,000 the market expected.
