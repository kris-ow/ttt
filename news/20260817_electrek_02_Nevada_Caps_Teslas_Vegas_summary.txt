Nevada's Transportation Authority granted Tesla a permit to operate driverless vehicles in Las Vegas but capped the fleet at 10 cars, far below the 5,000 Tesla requested in its application.

## Robotaxi
1. **Permit details and restrictions:** Tesla Robotaxi, LLC filed docket 26-05015 in early June seeking an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit covering all of Clark County and up to 5,000 vehicles in the first year, naming Harry Reid International Airport and Henderson Executive Airport as pickup points. The Authority approved the permit on July 27 but limited it to 10 fully autonomous vehicles within an Authority-approved geofence, a 45 mph speed cap, no airport pickups, mandatory "robotaxi" vehicle labeling, passenger notification of driverless operation, and required "appropriate human supervision." Expanding beyond 10 vehicles requires new Authority approval. The article notes Nevada regulators have not publicly explained the gap between the 5,000-vehicle request and the 10-vehicle permit.

2. **Competitor permits in Nevada:** Amazon's Zoox holds Nevada AVNC Permit 001, amended July 16 (11 days before Tesla's) to allow up to 100 vehicles; Zoox is currently running roughly 50 vehicles on the Strip and began charging for rides this month after clearing a federal hurdle. Waymo filed a Nevada application in June (docket 26-06007) and went fully driverless in Las Vegas in early July; Uber's Aviari Services subsidiary filed in July. Both remain pending. Tesla is described as the third autonomous operator to reach the Strip, requesting an allocation roughly 50 times larger than Zoox's authorized cap.

3. **National fleet context:** The article states Tesla's entire unsupervised Robotaxi fleet across the US is roughly about 20 vehicles operating concurrently, with Austin having peaked near 25 in late April before drifting down. Ten additional vehicles in Las Vegas would represent roughly a 50% increase to Tesla's national driverless fleet. The article argues the software, not permits or hardware, is the binding constraint, noting Tesla is building Cybercabs faster than it can deploy them, and that Elon Musk has said the robotaxi program won't see material revenue until at least 2027, a timeline Musk has pushed for roughly a decade.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls Tesla's 5,000-vehicle request "delusional" given its actual deployment capacity, arguing the ask exceeded even the total number of taxi permits in Las Vegas and made no market or operational sense. He contends that if Tesla could scale unsupervised robotaxis, it would first max out existing lightly-regulated Texas and Florida markets, but it cannot — evidenced by only around 20 Tesla robotaxis operating at any time. He predicts the Las Vegas launch will be another small-scale demonstration while Tesla awaits a further technical breakthrough to enable real scaling.
