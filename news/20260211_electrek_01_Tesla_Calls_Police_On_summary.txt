Tesla called police and filed a criminal complaint against an IG Metall union representative accused of secretly recording a closed works council meeting at Giga Berlin, three weeks before a works council election covering roughly 11,000 employees. IG Metall denies the accusation and calls it a fabricated smear campaign ahead of the vote.

## Market & Competition
1. **Recording incident disputed:** Tesla plant manager André Thierig said an external IG Metall representative was caught recording a non-public works council meeting on a laptop; the representative allegedly refused to hand it over, prompting works council chairwoman Michaela Schmitz to call police, who seized the laptop. IG Metall's Tesla Workers GFBB group, the largest single faction on the works council with 16 seats (not a majority), called the accusation "a brazen and calculated lie" and said it would pursue legal action over what it termed a smear campaign. The article notes the outcome hinges on what investigators find on the laptop.
2. **Escalating anti-union campaign:** Tesla management has campaigned against an IG Metall majority for months: in late December Thierig drew a "red line" against the union's push for a 35-hour workweek and warned US leadership would halt plant expansion if IG Metall wins the election; Tesla also held a concert for employees (described by the article as widely seen as anti-union propaganda) and announced a 4% pay raise without works council or union involvement.
3. **Works council election context:** The March 2–4 election could reshape the plant's future. In the 2024 election, IG Metall won the most individual votes (about 39.4%) but non-union lists secured a combined majority of seats; the union has organized to flip that outcome this time. Giga Berlin is Germany's only non-union automotive plant.
4. **Workforce cuts and denials:** A Handelsblatt report found Tesla quietly cut roughly 1,700 jobs at Giga Berlin over the past year, reducing headcount from 12,415 to 10,703 (a nearly 14% decline), while Thierig repeatedly denied any staff reductions were occurring.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **European sales collapse:** Tesla's European sales fell 28% in 2025, with Germany down 48%; January 2026 saw registrations drop 44% across five key European markets. A Nordic reputation study ranked Tesla last among 30 companies in Germany, citing the largest reputational collapse the survey has recorded in over a decade; a separate survey found 94% of Germans would not consider buying a Tesla.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that regardless of who is telling the truth about the recording incident, Tesla's pattern of threats, an anti-union concert, a sidelining pay raise, and now a criminal complaint suggests a company unsure of its workforce's support. He speculates Tesla may use the election outcome, whichever way it goes, as a pretext to scale back or close Giga Berlin given shrinking European demand, and expresses personal concern about this possibility.
