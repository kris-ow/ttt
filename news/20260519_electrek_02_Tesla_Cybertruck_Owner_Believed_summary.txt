A Tesla Cybertruck owner drove his vehicle into Grapevine Lake in Texas to test the "Wade Mode" feature, became stranded, and was arrested on multiple charges. The incident is the latest in a pattern of similar events tied to Elon Musk's past claims about the Cybertruck's water capabilities.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Grapevine Lake incident:** On Monday evening around 8 PM, Grapevine police and fire were dispatched to Katie's Woods Park Boat Ramp, where a Cybertruck had been intentionally driven into the lake to test Wade Mode. The vehicle became disabled and took on water; passengers self-evacuated and the fire department's Water Rescue Team recovered the truck. The driver was charged with operating a vehicle in a closed section of park/lake, having no valid boat registration, and multiple water safety equipment violations, and remains in jail as of the article's publication.

2. **What Wade Mode actually does:** Despite Musk's 2022 claim that the Cybertruck would be "waterproof enough" to serve as a boat and cross rivers, lakes, and even seas — with a stated goal of crossing the ~360-meter gap between SpaceX's Starbase and South Padre Island — the production vehicle's Wade Mode only pressurizes the battery pack and raises ride height to support a maximum wade depth of approximately 32 inches from the bottom of the tire, intended for shallow creek crossings.

3. **Pattern of similar incidents:** Prior incidents include a Cybertruck stuck in water in Truckee, California (prompting the CHP to note "Wade Mode isn't Submarine Mode"), a Cybertruck that sank in Ventura harbor during a jet ski launch requiring Coast Guard involvement, and a European-imported Cybertruck stuck in a lake in Slovakia. Tesla's warranty does not cover off-roading or water damage.

## Editorial Stance
The author squarely blames Musk's repeated public claims for giving owners false confidence, but also states bluntly that owners who take Musk at his word on such claims "probably deserve a night in jail and whatever fine that comes with it."
