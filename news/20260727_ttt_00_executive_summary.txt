## Brief

- Coast-to-coast FSD "cannonball" run hits zero disengagements, 20,000 miles
- Musk: Semi self-driving targeted for late 2026/early 2027
- 245 Cybercabs spotted at Giga Texas outbound lot, a record

- Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation intensifies, unconfirmed by either company
- TSLA slides toward $300 amid broader AI-stock selloff

## Autonomous Driving
- David Moss's team ran a fully autonomous, zero-disengagement cross-country Cannonball drive of ~2,800 miles in 47h15m39s on FSD 14.3.6, beating the prior record by ~2h40m and pushing the car's total to 20,000 FSD miles. Tesla VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy called it a "touring test" that has become so routine it "went breezing by."
- Musk told LightShed's Walter Piecyk on the July 22 earnings call that self-driving on the Tesla Semi is targeted for "probably around the end of this year or early next year," with Semi autonomy work taking "a bit of a backseat for the next six months" while engineering focuses on Model 3/Y/Cybercab. Electrek notes Tesla has missed roughly ten prior self-driving deadlines since 2013 and contrasts Tesla's 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles with Aurora's 250,000+ fully driverless highway miles.
- Elluswamy said FSD version 15 has begun testing in Austin Robotaxi operations, with an estimated three of seven planned new features currently integrated.
- Tesla restarted FSD 14 Light rollout for Hardware 3 vehicles with brake-confirmation options, destination parking changes, UI updates and "streaks"; one reviewer called it not yet good but improving.
- Tesla confirmed FSD is moving toward personalization — remembering individual driver habits and intervention patterns rather than one-size-fits-all behavior.

## Robotaxi
- A record 245 Cybercabs were spotted in the Giga Texas outbound lot (per Sawyer Merritt); none had Cybercab decals and some appeared to retain steering wheels, read by commentators as test/validation units ahead of wider geographic rollout rather than a red flag.
- Fleet sightings expanded beyond Austin: Houston's visible fleet grew to ~13 cars within an hour with 130+ Cybercabs reported at a Houston Tesla facility lot; new rides appeared in Tampa; and Cybercabs/instrumented Model Ys were spotted in Charlotte, NC and undergoing winter testing in New Zealand. One poster speculated (unconfirmed) that Tesla's total North American Cybercab test fleet may be "approaching 1,000."
- Driverless Cybercabs were reported near Austin suburbs, alongside apparent employee pickups on public roads outside Giga Texas — characterized as validation-stage testing preceding wider rollout.
- Tesla reported zero critical incidents over 380,000 Robotaxi miles, though one commentator cautioned the sample size is too small to establish a real safety rate.
- Uber and Waymo are ending their multi-year ride-hailing exclusivity in Atlanta and Austin, read by one commentator as a sign of Waymo's growing leverage and Uber's structural weakness.
- Multiple commentators noted Tesla still hasn't published objective criteria for a wider Robotaxi rollout, relying on vague metrics like "10% weekly compounding miles."

## Financials
- TSLA fell toward the $300–313 range after earnings (from mid-$300s/$400s days prior), part of a broader AI-stock drawdown; one host's earnings-week hedge (20 puts, $345 strike, ~$5,000 cost) closed for ~$21,000 profit, with no shares sold.
- Commentators cited a 55% FSD attach rate in countries where FSD is offered, from the Q2 earnings call.
- The AI-sector selloff dragged comparably-exposed names down sharply from highs (Tesla ~37%, Palantir ~41%, Marvell ~41%), with one host framing the move as an oversold buying opportunity rather than a fundamental Tesla problem.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- On Tesla's earnings call, Musk did not deny Tesla-SpaceX synergies when asked directly, describing overlaps in Starlink, Grok, and the Terafab chip project before Tesla's general counsel confirmed an investment and framework agreement between Tesla and SpaceX signed earlier this year covering Terafab and Digital Optimus.
- SpaceX's first-ever earnings call is scheduled for August 4; commentators generally doubt a merger announcement will be tied to that date.
- Merger-timing speculation varies widely and is explicitly unconfirmed: one guest predicts an exploratory committee announced between Q3 earnings and year-end with possible completion by mid-2027; another host guesses August; a third argues a merger is unlikely before 2027 given SpaceX shares are still in price discovery post-IPO. One host remains skeptical of any near-term deal, citing Tesla's institutional shareholder base and control concerns.
- SpaceX shares are down roughly 34–35% over the past month and about 49% from their post-IPO peak; Musk reportedly said the decline reflects SpaceX prioritizing Moon/Mars funding over near-term profitability and may continue to miss earnings quarters.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk described "Digital Optimus" as AI that reads and reacts to computer screens like a human, with Grok orchestrating its tasks — distinct from tools that merely replay demonstrated actions. A Tesla executive reportedly framed this as "full self computer," applying FSD's vision-in/action-out approach to computer use.
- One commentator argued manufacturing isn't Optimus's core bottleneck — the harder problem is achieving generalized, useful task performance without one-to-one human control, a capability not yet reached.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's 2026 Summer Update is rolling out with route-memory navigation, deeper Grok voice integration (glovebox, climate, calls, music), a "karaoke" parking-score feature, new traction-control modes, and an ambient-light-matched intro animation; full destination-level memory remains a distinct, unreleased feature Musk has said is coming.
- Tesla officially launched in Uruguay.
- Tesla released a non-motorized kids' balance bike (ages 2–5, magnesium frame, 35kg capacity) restricted to existing owners; one resold on eBay for ~$1,200.
- Tesla reportedly rose to #9 in a Consumer Reports reliability ranking, attributed to Model S/X exiting the lineup and improved Cybertruck/Model Y quality.
- A Bloomberg piece argued Cybertruck could rank among the biggest sales flops in auto history versus the Ford Edsel's 200,000 units; one host called the framing overstated but agreed sales remain weak.
