This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and Tesla journalist Brian White (of the YouTube channel Futurasa) analyzing the Tesla Q1 2026 earnings call, which many investors found confusing or discouraging. The core argument is that the negative reaction was driven largely by Elon Musk's tone rather than the substance of what was announced, and that the actual content — on Cybercab production, FSD adoption, and robotaxi expansion — was broadly positive. The hosts walk through key audio clips from the call to clarify what was and wasn't actually said about delays and timelines.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybercab enters production at Giga Texas:** Tesla officially announced that Cybercab production has begun at Giga Texas. Elon Musk cautioned that, as with any new product involving a completely new supply chain, the initial production will follow a slow S-curve before ramping exponentially toward the end of 2026 and into 2027. Tesla's earnings deck confirmed that Cybercab, Tesla Semi, and Megapack 3 are all on schedule for volume production starting in 2026.

2. **Cybercab to eventually replace the Model Y as Tesla's highest-volume vehicle:** Tesla confirmed in their earnings deck that once in production, they expect the Cybercab to begin replacing the existing Model Y fleet and to become the largest-volume vehicle in the fleet over time. Elon has also reiterated that eventually 90% of all Tesla vehicles will be Cybercabs, with the only driver-operable vehicle being the Roadster.

3. **Tesla Semi production ramp also underway:** Tesla Semi production has not yet formally begun, but Elon confirmed it is starting soon. Brian White noted the Semi has accumulated enough road miles for Tesla to be confident in proceeding, though the 4680 battery supply — given that each Semi requires the equivalent of 10–12 cars' worth of batteries — may be a remaining bottleneck. Tesla Semi is priced approximately 40% below competing electric semi offerings, making it highly competitive.

4. **Hardware 3 cannot achieve unsupervised FSD:** Elon confirmed that Hardware 3 vehicles are not capable of running unsupervised FSD. Tesla is offering two remedies: a discounted trade-in for customers who want to upgrade to an AI4-equipped vehicle, and the ability to upgrade the car's computer and cameras in place. Elon noted that to do this efficiently, Tesla will need to set up micro-factories or small factories in major metropolitan areas, as service center upgrades would be too slow and inefficient. He indicated it would eventually make sense to convert all Hardware 3 cars to Hardware 4, enabling them to join the robotaxi fleet. A limited "FSD version 14 light" for Hardware 3 cars is planned for release in June 2026, which will be supervised-only but functionally close to version 14 for most point-to-point trips.

5. **Inventory and EV demand tailwinds:** Brian White noted Tesla currently sits at approximately 27 days of inventory, which he views as well-timed given rising global gas prices that may push more consumers toward EVs. He also noted that in the UK, EVs are already cheaper to operate than petrol alternatives.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD adoption accelerating sharply — 28.8 million miles per day:** Tesla's FSD fleet is now driving an average of 28.8 million miles per day, up from 14.4 million just a couple of months ago — a doubling in a very short period. The hosts note this equates to roughly 1,000 miles every 3 seconds across the fleet.

2. **FSD subscription take rate exceeding 50% on new deliveries:** Analyst Cern compiled data showing that the incremental FSD take rate — defined as the change in active paid FSD subscriptions over incremental deliveries in the most recent quarter — has now exceeded 50%. This figure explicitly excludes free trials. The quarter-over-quarter increase in subscriptions was approximately 170,000, equating to roughly $16.8 million per month or $50 million per quarter in additional revenue just from the increase. Total FSD revenue including upfront purchases is estimated at approximately $375 million per quarter, described as essentially pure margin.

3. **FSD version 15 confirmed for AI4 hardware — targeting end of 2026:** Elon confirmed that FSD version 15 will run on AI4 hardware. He described version 15 as a complete overhaul of the software architecture, intended to enable unsupervised FSD anywhere in the world where it is legally permitted. He said this version will "hopefully" be available by the end of 2026. Both hosts are currently running FSD version 14.2, which they describe as extremely close to a comfort level suitable for unsupervised use, with Brian citing a drive to California at over 98% FSD engagement.

4. **FSD version 14.3 is the current ceiling — a newer architecture needed before global scale:** Elon stated that FSD version 14.3 represents the last piece of the puzzle for the current software generation, but that there are known major architectural improvements that would significantly improve safety. He indicated it would not make sense to deploy unsupervised FSD or robotaxi at large scale while those known improvements remain unimplemented. The hosts debated whether this implied a wait until version 15, or whether it only referred to truly global scale — concluding that expansion to a dozen states can proceed on the current trajectory while version 15 is developed.

5. **AI4 confirmed sufficient for unsupervised FSD; AI5 going to Optimus first:** Elon confirmed that AI4 hardware is sufficient to achieve unsupervised FSD and that there is no pressing need to move to AI5 in cars. AI5 will go into Optimus first, and it may be some time before it reaches vehicles. An "AI4 Plus" variant — featuring upgraded RAM (from 16 GB to 32 GB per chip, or 64 GB in the dual configuration) and approximately a 10% increase in compute — is planned to go into production in mid-2027, contingent on Samsung's manufacturing capability.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi targeting a dozen or so states by end of 2026:** Elon said he expects unsupervised FSD or robotaxi to be operating in "a dozen or so states" by the end of 2026. He noted Tesla has had no injuries and no fatalities with unsupervised FSD and robotaxi to date, and that Tesla is taking a cautious rollout approach to maintain that record.

2. **Robotaxi revenue will be material but not "super material" in 2026 — significant in 2027:** Elon stated that unsupervised FSD and robotaxi revenue will be material in 2026, but not "super material." He expects it to be material "in a significant way" in 2027. Brian White interpreted this as the revenue in 2026 not being sufficient to offset the infrastructure, operations, insurance, and buildout costs incurred to date — meaning the net financial impact won't be clearly positive until 2027.

3. **The "delay" debate — scale vs. global expansion:** A central debate among investors following the earnings call was whether Elon's comments meant robotaxi was delayed. The hosts' interpretation is that it was not delayed for near-term U.S. multi-state rollout, but that "large scale" in Elon's framing likely means global or near-universal deployment — not the initial dozen-state expansion. The Cybercab production ramp, even at its initially slow pace (hundreds of units per week in the early weeks), means unsupervised robotaxis will be operating in meaningful numbers well before FSD version 15 is released.

4. **Operational challenges are now the main barrier — not safety:** The hosts emphasize Elon's framing that the barrier to wide-scale robotaxi deployment is now operational, not safety. Key operational issues cited include edge cases like a bus blocking a lane that causes the fleet to stall — situations requiring remote operator intervention. The ratio of remote operators to vehicles was raised as an open question. Elon also described an "infinite loop" problem where vehicles sit idle rather than route around obstacles, which needs to be solved before broader scale.

## Market & Competition
1. **Cybercab unit volumes will dwarf current EV competitors from day one:** Brian White noted that even a painfully slow Cybercab ramp of hundreds or a few thousand units per week will likely outsell many of Tesla's competitors who periodically announce "strong months" with much smaller volumes. He drew the comparison to Model Y production in Shanghai, where Tesla quickly localized and scaled its supply chain under factory leadership.
