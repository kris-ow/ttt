## Executive Summary
Tesla's robotaxi build-out showed a visible data point with a record single-day batch of Cybercab registrations in Texas, while Tesla also unveiled its first transformer product, Powercore, aimed at off-grid data centers. On the SpaceX side, CFO Bret Johnsen described strong compute demand and fast paybacks that let SpaceX fund its buildout from internal cash flow, and the Starlink competitive position was reinforced by Delta's CEO comments. A California DMV order restricting Waymo on freeways is a competitive development for the autonomy space.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Record Cybercab registrations in Texas:** Per Sawyer, citing the Texas DMV, Tesla registered a record 200 Cybercabs in a single day for its Robotaxi fleet, bringing the total registered to 773. He calls it the largest single-day increase in Cybercab registrations and speculates that Dallas rides might go live soon. Registrations are a regulatory/fleet-prep step and do not by themselves show vehicles in service or the size of the operating fleet.

2. **Tesla Powercore transformer:** Tesla officially unveiled its first-ever transformer, Powercore, positioned to enable fully off-grid data centers. Listed specs: a solid-state medium-voltage transformer, inverter and breaker; peak efficiency of 98.86%; 9 MVA / 7.4 MW power, with grid-forming support for 10 MVA of peak power. The post drew strong engagement (8.8K likes, 842 reposts). It extends Tesla's energy business toward AI data-center power infrastructure.

3. **SpaceX compute economics and demand:** In a new interview, CFO Bret Johnsen said SpaceX can fund its compute buildout with internal cash flows, citing a payback of less than one year, "sometimes dramatically less than one-year payback," on hosting deals for terrestrial compute. He also said SpaceX sees nothing other than increased demand on the compute side, across almost every player in the industry, and that it can bring up compute and act as a spot market player.

4. **Starship catch sentiment:** Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas, in a new SpaceX note, said there were "crickets after catch": SpaceX achieved one of the most important milestones in spaceflight history, yet the market reaction was quiet, with one of the quietest days noted amid a lot of other news going on.

5. **Starlink competitive position:** Delta's CEO, in a new interview, said Delta has been the leader in free, fast Wi-Fi, expects more than one LEO product in the world besides Starlink, and is confident Amazon can do a great job. He described Viasat, with two new satellites in the sky, as "not Starlink caliber but pretty darn close." Sawyer disputes this, saying it is not remotely close to Starlink. The commentary shows large airline customers actively courting alternatives to Starlink.

6. **Waymo freeway restriction:** The California DMV ordered Waymo to stop operating its autonomous vehicles on California freeways, citing concerns that they pose an imminent public safety hazard. Waymo said it is working diligently and responsibly with the DMV and other state officials. This is a regulatory setback for a key robotaxi competitor.

## Signal Strength: **Mildly Bullish** — A record Cybercab registration batch, the Powercore launch and strong SpaceX compute economics are positives, tempered by the fact that registrations are not deployments and Starlink faces emerging competitor narratives.
