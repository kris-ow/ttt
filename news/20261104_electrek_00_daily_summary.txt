Electrek's two pieces today both carry political/regulatory implications for Musk's companies: one on the fallout from Texas's Democratic election sweep for Musk and his Tesla-SpaceX merger ambitions, the other on a Korean consumer complaint alleging Tesla marketed FSD to buyers who can't legally use it.

## Market & Competition
1. **Democratic sweep in Texas raises questions for Musk's legal and political standing:** Jameson Dow reports that Democrats swept Texas's statewide races in the midterms, including the Senate race where James Talarico defeated Ken Paxton 55-43 in what the article calls the most expensive Senate race in US history; Musk spent $212 million supporting Paxton, part of $474 million he put into the election cycle overall, per the article. Democrats also won the Attorney General race (Nathan Johnson), the Governor's race (Gina Hinojosa defeating Greg Abbott), and all seats on the 15th Court of Appeals and Texas Supreme Court. The author notes Abbott and Paxton had given Musk favorable treatment — relaxed autonomous-vehicle rules, "business courts," and a hand-picked 15th Court of Appeals — and argues the flip of that court (which recently halted an inquiry into redacted Abbott-Musk emails) could weaken the favorable Texas legal environment the author says Musk's proposed SpaceX-Tesla merger scheme may depend on. The author characterizes the merger as likely detrimental to both companies' shareholders and serving mainly to give Musk more control and personal wealth, and speculates Musk may clash with Texas's new leadership given his heavy spending against the EV-unfriendly party drew contrast with his companies' interests. ## Autonomous Driving
2. **Korean consumer group alleges Tesla sold unusable FSD to most buyers:** Fred Lambert reports that Citizens United for Consumer Sovereignty plans to file a complaint with South Korea's Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) alleging Tesla marketed "Full Self-Driving" to customers whose cars can't legally run it, per Reuters, which reviewed the complaint. Only US-built Model S, X and Cybertruck qualify for FSD (Supervised) in Korea under KORUS; Shanghai-built Model 3/Y, which make up most Korean sales, lack approval. Tesla Korea's country manager testified FSD works on about 20% of registered Teslas; Tesla has sold over 170,000 Shanghai-built vehicles in Korea since 2023 with sales growing more than fivefold 2023–2025, per data given to the National Assembly. The complaint says Tesla charged 9.043 million won (~$6,726) one-time, later a 150,000 won (~$112) monthly subscription, while a "coming soon" promo and demo video didn't disclose eligibility limits; group official Choi Young-suk is quoted saying Tesla "generated substantial revenue by selling an expensive driver-assistance system to mass-market buyers who cannot use it for even a single day." The KFTC has already opened a probe and received written responses from Tesla, per a Reuters source; Tesla did not respond to Reuters. Tesla only filed for FSD certification in Korea around October 22, despite testifying weeks earlier it had never formally requested approval; the transport ministry says current FSD doesn't comply with local hands-on-wheel rules. A Seoul court last month dismissed a refund lawsuit, blaming Korean regulations rather than Tesla. Despite this, Tesla passed BMW this year to become Korea's top imported car brand, the article notes.
