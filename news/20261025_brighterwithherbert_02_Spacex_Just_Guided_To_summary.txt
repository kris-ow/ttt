This video breaks down SpaceX's latest quarterly earnings call, focusing on explosive growth in its AI/compute ("SI") business, Starlink's aviation and mobile expansion, Starship's upcoming capture attempt, and the $40 billion debt raise funding it all. The host's core argument: CFO Bret Johnson's guidance (100B ARR by December, $33-37B revenue in Q1 2027) should be treated as reliable, while Elon Musk's much larger claims (13 gigawatts of compute next year, $500B-$1T annual revenue by end of 2027/2028) are speculative and should be tracked separately.

## Financials
1. **Record quarter, first GAAP profit since IPO:** SpaceX reported $14.6 billion in quarterly revenue, up 161% year-over-year and beating Wall Street's ~$12.7 billion estimate. Net income was $4.4 billion versus a $1.1 billion loss a year ago — the first profitable quarter since SpaceX's IPO — though only about $2.8 billion came from operating activities; the rest was interest income, tax breaks, and $561 million in gains from SpaceX's stake in The Boring Company (which raised funds in September), per CNBC's reporting.
2. **Segment breakdown — SI now bigger than Starlink:** Per Sawyer Merritt's breakdown of the call, SpaceX's own launch segment brought in $1.77 billion (+6%), Starlink communications $5.25 billion (+69%), and the SI (superintelligence/compute) segment $7.56 billion (+830%) — the first time SI has exceeded Starlink in revenue, driven mostly by renting compute capacity to other companies.
3. **Guidance: $35B quarter ahead, $100B ARR target reaffirmed:** CFO Bret Johnson reaffirmed the company remains on track for $100 billion in annualized revenue run-rate by December, and newly guided Q1 2027 total revenue to $33-37 billion — roughly $140 billion annualized at the midpoint, well above the December ARR target. He attributed the longer-range guide to growing visibility into the compute contract backlog, which rose from $47.5 billion to $63 billion quarter-over-quarter, with about $42 billion expected to convert to revenue over the next 12 months.
4. **Elon's "crazy" trillion-dollar projection:** Musk said annual revenue by the end of 2027 could reach $500 billion to $1 trillion, immediately calling it a "crazy assessment" himself; the CFO did not repeat or endorse the figure. The host notes JPMorgan, currently the most bullish bank on the stock, models only about $254 billion in 2027 revenue — far below Musk's range.
5. **Capex surge and $40B debt raise:** SpaceX spent $19.6 billion on capex in the quarter (nearly 3x a year ago) and guided to $35-40 billion in Q4 capex alone. Through nine months it took in ~$13 billion in cash while spending ~$48 billion on capex, holding ~$90 billion in cash/investments against ~$39 billion in debt. The company is completing a $40 billion debt raise led by Apollo and Goldman Sachs — a riskier tranche yielding ~8.3% and a safer, AI-chip-backed tranche at ~7.75%, both over a point higher than SpaceX's June bond issuance (which averaged under 6%). JPMorgan's Doug Amuth reported management told him SpaceX is not currently considering an equity raise.
6. **Analyst price target moves:** JPMorgan raised its target from $240 to $500, Barclays to $300, Morgan Stanley held at $300, and Bank of America raised to $275.
7. **SpaceX-Tesla merger — no decision yet:** Musk said the situation is a "strange kabuki dance" due to his conflict of interest, pushed back on the idea a merger is "inevitable," but said yes when asked if he thinks it would be a good idea; SpaceX's Thursday report stated no decision has been made and gave no terms.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute capacity scaling ahead of prior guidance:** SpaceX ended September with 1.7 gigawatts of nominal compute capacity (up from 0.6 a year ago), expects to reach ~2.2 gigawatts by the end of October, and 2.5-3 gigawatts by year-end — above the "2+ gigawatts" guided in August. Musk said he would not be surprised if the company has 13 gigawatts connected by the end of next year, with an internal plan for 20 gigawatts of new power and cooling capacity, adding the caveat that he doesn't expect everything to go perfectly.
2. **In-house turbine manufacturing as a power-buildout edge:** Musk said SpaceX casts turbine blades and vanes itself using its existing rocket-engine foundry, avoiding supplier delays turbine makers quoted at 12-18 months, letting SpaceX bring power capacity online faster than competitors; he declined to detail an additional proprietary advantage, citing patented technology.
3. **Grok and Cursor driving internal compute demand:** Grok's agentic "Grokbot" product reached 800,000 paid weekly users as of early this month (more than double the prior month), starting at $30/month, with Musk saying he expects it to eventually consume most of SpaceX's compute for superintelligence; a business-tier rollout is expected within weeks. The breakdown between individual and business subscribers was not disclosed. Separately, the Cursor coding-tool acquisition (closed August 14 for $60 billion) generated $608 million in revenue in roughly seven weeks.
4. **Compute payback and leasing economics:** Bret Johnson said new compute capacity pays for itself in under a year, with pricing just above the $30-50/watt range discussed last quarter; Musk said next year's payback period could fall to as little as 6 months. SpaceX aims to lease out about half its compute externally and use the other half internally, deliberately keeping leases short to capture rising spot prices rather than locking into long-term contracts. Musk described being "constantly attacked" by hyperscalers and smaller firms seeking to rent capacity, illustrating acute market-wide GPU scarcity with an anecdote about SpaceX itself struggling to rent chips elsewhere.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and revenue growth:** Starlink added 1.6 million consumer subscriptions to reach 13.6 million (excluding airlines, ships, government), with enterprise/government revenue up 91% and Starlink operating profit nearly doubling to $2.4 billion.
2. **Aviation push:** Gwynne Shotwell reported Starlink now has contracts for 12,000 aircraft (about a quarter of all aircraft), with production quadrupled since the start of the year and expected to double again by year-end; roughly 3,800 aircraft had Starlink installed as of end-September, per Sawyer Merritt's recap of the call.
3. **Starlink Mobile and spectrum acquisition:** Direct-to-phone Starlink Mobile reached 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter. The FCC approved 15,000 next-generation mobile satellites, and on October 8 SpaceX agreed to purchase a nationwide 800 MHz spectrum block valued by Reuters at roughly $8 billion. Shotwell said the service is targeted to launch in early 2028 (pushed from a prior late-2027 target), and Musk said there is a clear path to several hundred billion dollars in annual connectivity revenue, with a dedicated presentation on the plan to come.
4. **Tower acquisition talks and carrier pushback:** Bloomberg reported SpaceX is seeking to acquire at least 80,000 cell tower sites (about half of all US towers, per industry estimates), in talks with Crown Castle among others (American Tower, Vertical Bridge also named); nothing is signed. Crown Castle shares rose Friday while AT&T and T-Mobile fell 7-8% intraday. AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile jointly lobbied the FCC on October 20 to preserve rules requiring satellite operators to get carrier permission before using their spectrum.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 reaches orbit, first revenue-generating launch:** Starship's 14th flight reached orbit for the first time and carried 26 new V3 Starlink satellites; SpaceX says the launch added roughly 10x the Starlink capacity of a Falcon 9 Starlink mission.
2. **Flight 15 to attempt full booster-and-ship capture:** Musk said Flight 15, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and ship — which would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle; he called success "likely" on this attempt though not guaranteed. NASA Spaceflight reported Flight 15 is scheduled for mid-November from the second Starbase site, pending FAA approval for dual-stage capture.
3. **Cadence targets and second Starbase site:** Musk said SpaceX aims for weekly Starship flights in the first half of next year, rising to twice weekly or more by year-end; Shotwell said SpaceX wants to relaunch a reused ship and attempt in-orbit refueling early next year. A second Starbase site in Louisiana is planned with an official first-launch target of 2029 (internal target 2028) and roughly 20+ launch towers.
4. **New contracts and lunar refueling debate:** Shotwell listed 18 more national-security launch contracts and a contract to land a payload on the Moon via Starship. On lunar refueling, a NASA safety committee member estimated ~22 launches would be needed to support a Starship Moon landing; Musk responded that the real number will be closer to six or eight. NASA's updated Artemis schedule shows fuel-transfer/cabin-systems testing in Q1 2027, landing-gear tests in Q2 2027, Artemis 3 (Starship-Orion docking test) in Q3 2027, an uncrewed lunar landing in Q4 2027, and the first crewed landing (Artemis 4) in 2028 — with NASA still undecided between Starship and Blue Origin's lander for that mission.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab chip timeline:** Musk said a first test chip (a simple memory chip) at the Austin research line should be running around May-June next year, with serial production targeted around Q3 2028 at the main Terafab site — both dates given with large margins of error; he said there are potential manufacturing breakthroughs he wouldn't fully disclose.
2. **Tesla-SpaceX financial ties and other items:** SpaceX purchased $151 million of Megapacks from Tesla last quarter and $480 million year-to-date. Separately, Musk said he plans to give one SpaceX share to every child in America next year (the host calculates ~72 million children, ~$10.5 billion in shares), and SpaceX is reportedly proposing a 400-acre expansion at Kennedy Space Center including a Starship test bed.
