## Brief
- Reuters: Tesla pressured Dutch regulator on FSD review, emails show
- FCC approves 15,000-sat Starlink Mobile Gen 2 constellation
- SpaceX to acquire nationwide low-band spectrum, ~$6B reported
- Telecom stocks fall 6-7% after-hours on Starlink spectrum news

## Autonomous Driving
- Reuters investigation (via Electrek): emails show Tesla pushed RDW to shrink its FSD review scope, disputed a €365,000 invoice, and resisted in-person test witnessing; RDW invited Tesla to suggest reducing "required effort" after the billing dispute. RDW says its process stays independent but has redacted much of the email record and hasn't published its own test data.
- Seven safety researchers told Reuters Tesla's EU "evidence dashboard" compared small samples of professional-driver FSD miles against surrogate behaviors (honking, hard braking) rather than crash data — the same methodology that produced Tesla's original US "10x safer" claim, later revised to 7x.
- EU-wide FSD vote now expected no earlier than December; eight countries have approved FSD (Netherlands, Lithuania, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Slovenia, Czechia, Croatia) but Germany, Italy, Spain have not, and Sweden wants speed-limit functionality removed before backing approval.
- FSD rebranded "TAD" across the EU; Slovakia approval reportedly expected within days (unconfirmed). FSD 14.3 Light began a small Hardware 3 rollout; FSD (Supervised) V14.3.11 also rolling out with unchanged release notes.
- Robotaxi service reportedly stayed active in heavy rain in Tampa while Waymo paused for weather, per a reporter covering the market; Tesla posted footage of rain operation.

## Robotaxi
- NHTSA granted Tesla a 30-day extension (to October 30) on its Cybercab audit information request, less than the 8-week extension Tesla sought.
- Cybercab tracker cited by one host shows 75 vehicles deployed (two added in 24 hours) and 169 registered with the Texas DMV; host ties a ~2,000-unit fleet milestone to a potential TSLA move above $500.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- FCC approved SpaceX's 15,000-satellite Gen 2 Starlink Mobile constellation, targeting ~150 Mbps per user versus ~4 Mbps on the current ~650-satellite system; waiver also lets SpaceX offer satellite wireless service without a ground-carrier leasing agreement.
- SpaceX agreed to acquire nationwide low-band spectrum (previously held by Grain Management, originally from T-Mobile), reportedly priced around $6 billion though not officially disclosed; pending FCC approval, it would let Starlink Mobile combine satellite and terrestrial networks for indoor coverage.
- T-Mobile fell over 6%, AT&T over 7%, Verizon just under 7% after-hours on the spectrum news, directly rebutting prior dismissive comments from T-Mobile's CFO and AT&T's CEO on satellite-to-phone viability.
- Musk: "I see a path to SpaceX being worth orders of magnitude more than the current Earth economy" — a long-term, speculative marker.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- A 328 million share unlock is scheduled for October 9, with another 328 million on October 24 and a larger 1.3 billion-share unlock in late October/November. One host reports SPCX began sliding two days ahead of the October 9 unlock, earlier than the one-day lead seen before the prior unlock, and expects roughly 30-60 million shares to be sold into the unlock.
- SPCX closed at $160.57, down 4.19% on the day, alongside a broader Nasdaq 100 decline.
- One host assigns roughly 80% odds against a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement at the October 15 event (expected to center on Tesla's Roadster reveal), versus 20% odds for one, with an expected ~30% premium to shareholders if it happens.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk clarified the Terafab chip facility will be built and run by SpaceX and Tesla, with TSMC potentially subleasing floor space but not owning or operating any part of it — a more limited TSMC role than some had speculated.
- Futurist Brian Wang (Next Big Future) argues SpaceX can stand up a gigawatt-scale AI data center cluster in roughly 4-5 months versus 2-4 years industry-wide, and models — explicitly as speculative scenario math, not guidance — a path to 10+ gigawatts by end of 2027 and a ~$500B annual revenue run rate, versus a prior $100B run-rate target cited for this year. Figures are unconfirmed and sourced to an unverified "Q&A" document.
- Ship 40 is returning to the Starship launch site with no disclosed plan yet; Musk denied a report of slowing facility construction, saying "we are accelerating rapidly."

## Energy
- Tesla Powerwalls delivered a record Virtual Power Plant event in California: 85,774 homes participated, supplying 601 MW during peak demand with the fleet peaking at 634 MW — the largest residential VPP event reported to date.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla filed a Clark County, Nevada permit for a dedicated Semi service center in Las Vegas (3780 N Las Vegas Blvd), status "Awaiting Plans" — construction drawings not yet submitted, so not an imminent opening. The I-15 corridor location sits within the Standard Range Semi's 325-mile rating and addresses a Nevada Megacharger gap (only 2 of 64 planned sites in the state).
- Semi volume production is underway at the new Sparks factory (claimed 50,000 trucks/year capacity); Einride ordered 500 trucks in August. Tesla has outlined plans for roughly 20 dedicated Semi service locations nationally.
- Tesla shares fell modestly in today's broader Nasdaq selloff, roughly in line with the index's ~1.25% drop; one host frames this as investors recognizing a separate semiconductor-sector headline had no real Tesla relevance.

## Market & Competition
- A reported OpenAI revenue figure (~$50B annualized run rate, down from a previously cited $70B) triggered a broad chip-sector selloff — Micron -4.79%, Nvidia -3%, Broadcom -4.3%, AMD -3.9% — which one host calls a misleading accounting-comparison story (gross vs. net revenue reporting) rather than a genuine miss.
- Separately, OpenAI's own investor materials reportedly project $840B cumulative revenue against ~$856B in compute/infrastructure spend through 2030, implying roughly $150M/day in net burn, per Financial Times reporting relayed by a host.
- Dodge Charger Daytona EV sales collapsed to 767 units January-September (down 89% YoY), with just 233 in Q3, underscoring weak EV demand at some legacy automakers even as BMW (iX4) and Jaguar (Type 01) push new premium EV entrants.
