Tesla published European specifications for its Semi electric truck ahead of its formal launch at the IAA Transportation show in Hannover (September 15-20), and the numbers differ notably from the version already sold in the US.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **European Semi specs revealed:** Tesla Europe posted specs via X and its German website: up to 550 km (342 miles) of range at 40 tonnes fully loaded, 1 kWh/km energy consumption, Megacharger charging up to 800 kW recovering ~60% of range in 30 minutes, ePTO up to 25 kW, 40-tonne gross combination weight, and 9,100 kg curb weight. Tesla has not disclosed European pricing or an on-sale date.

2. **Spec mismatch with US version:** The US Long Range Semi is rated at 500 miles (805 km) on an 822 kWh battery at a US gross weight of 82,000 lb (37.2 tonnes), lower than Europe's 40-tonne standard for zero-emission trucks. The European truck's curb weight of ~9,100 kg (~20,000 lb) is lighter than the US Long Range truck (~23,000 lb) and matches the US Standard Range spec, leading the article to suggest the 550 km figure may reflect a standard-range battery pack rather than the Long Range version — something the article says needs clarification at Hannover. Charging is also lower in Europe (800 kW vs. up to 1.2 MW demonstrated in the US), though the efficiency figure (1 kWh/km ≈ 1.6 kWh/mile) is roughly consistent with real-world US fleet data.

3. **Manufacturing location unresolved:** Tesla currently builds the Semi only at its new high-volume factory next to Gigafactory Nevada, and the article notes it's unclear whether European trucks will be imported from Nevada or built locally; Musk has previously said Tesla would build the Semi at its Berlin factory.

## Market & Competition
4. **Crowded European electric truck market:** Mercedes-Benz, Volvo Trucks, MAN, Scania, DAF, and Renault already sell battery-electric heavy trucks in Europe with established service networks. Mercedes' eActros 600 offers around 500 km of range on a 600 kWh battery, making Tesla's 550 km/40-tonne figure competitive but not a clear leader.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the key story is what Tesla omitted — price, delivery date, and battery configuration clarity. He interprets the 9,100 kg curb weight as pointing to a standard-range or EU-modified truck, meaning European fleets may get shorter range than the headline suggests, and views the 800 kW charging cap as likely reflecting Europe's Megacharger rollout status rather than a truck limitation, but says it still changes the duty-cycle math for operators. He believes Tesla's best way to compete with entrenched rivals like Volvo and Mercedes is on pricing.
