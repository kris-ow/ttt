The hosts (Cern Basher, Herbert and Phil Beisel) discuss what stands between Tesla and a very large robotaxi fleet. Their view is that the constraints are software maturity, operational build-out within each geofence and map and navigation integration, rather than Cybercab production cost. They read management's "millions" scaling comment as evidence of a plan, not an imminent outcome.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab sales to outside owners:** The hosts note Tesla said at its conference that it is working on selling Cybercabs to customers by the end of the year. Herbert assumes the first buyers would be fleet owners of perhaps 10 or more units, operating inside the existing Austin geofence within the current robotaxi service. Whether they own the cars is, in his view, a funding question, and it does not let Tesla field more cars than the current limits on robotaxi numbers allow. One host adds that if Tesla produces more cars than the fleet needs, selling to private owners and fleets is a way to use capacity. Herbert counters that Tesla may face shortages around March or April and could need the cars itself.
2. **Production cost is not the issue:** The hosts argue Cybercab does not create a production-cost problem for Tesla. Tesla's total capacity is about half a million units per quarter, and Cybercab would use only a small slice, with peak Cybercab capacity of roughly 25-30 thousand units per quarter. Using half of that, about 15 thousand units, would be a very small share of fixed-cost absorption. They add that launch problems hit every product and that Cybercab is cheaper with fewer parts.
3. **Geofence operations are the bigger constraint:** Phil says there are two fundamental constraints: autonomy software and fleet operations. For each new area Tesla must certify the geofence and then solve the operational questions of car positioning, charging timing and cleaning. He thinks Tesla is improving but only starting that process, and he suspects Tesla wants to keep full control of operations. He cites the Austin geofence expansion to cover the F1 race area, which required effort to get cars to line up effectively. Herbert's open question is whether externally owned fleets would have Tesla handle charging, cleaning and maintenance, or push some of it to operators, which would let Tesla expand faster.
4. **Houston expansion and highway driving:** The hosts note Houston's service area grew roughly tenfold. A video showed a Cybercab driving on an interstate in the rain, which one commentator presented as Tesla now driving on highways in Austin. Herbert reads it as implying higher top speeds, though nobody announced it, and he calls it an auspicious development.
5. **Management's "millions" comment:** On the Q3 call, in answer to a Bank of America analyst on how fast Cybercab can scale, Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla can scale for the foreseeable future with no obstacles in the way, and that there is a "clear path to scaling to millions." He used the word "millions" twice in the answer. The hosts were surprised, since a timeline of a few months is implausible, and read "clear path" as a defined problem-solving plan that still needs execution, not a million cars arriving soon. They add that his body language has looked confident.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Software gaps remain:** Ashok said at the last financial conference that work remains: reaching 24/7 operation, driving at higher speeds on highways and improving automated parking. Phil says the v15 software is getting closer on safe predictive trips and can keep being improved virtually indefinitely.
2. **Learning loop and the long tail:** Phil praises the autonomy foundation, which exposes more decision points in the stack so Tesla can pick scenarios, apply reinforcement learning and solve open-ended situations that recur daily. He asks what the margin of safety is. Each fix needs data collection, a reinforcement learning scenario, regression testing and a fleet rollout. He says Tesla's cycle time is likely improving, but the long tail never stops being long.
3. **Maps moving into the core stack:** Phil sees maps and navigation as the weak link, with Tesla trying to integrate mapping into the center of the autonomy stack instead of treating it as a separate component. He says this should bring better interactivity and resolution but takes time and is not done yet. The hosts read Ashok's v15 comment as confirming this: the start and end of trips already use the technology, and it will take over all navigation, parking and destination handling over the next few months.
