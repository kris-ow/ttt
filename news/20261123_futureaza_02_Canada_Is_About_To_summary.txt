The hosts discuss how Canada's EV market may change as Chinese automakers gain entry under a trade arrangement, and many Canadians are waiting for cheaper cars before buying. They also cover tariff pressure on Canada's auto sector and a run of grid battery storage developments in Canada and globally. Tesla appears only briefly, through Model Y/Model 3 supply in Canada.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Canadians waiting for cheap Chinese EVs:** The hosts say many Canadians are holding off on purchases until more affordable Chinese EVs arrive, which they expect in early to mid-next year, though nobody knows exactly when. They cite a survey in which 48% of respondents want an electric car and half of those want to wait and see what happens with Chinese cars. The cheapest EV currently on the Canadian market is around 35,000, or roughly 30–31 thousand after a $5,000 credit. The hosts expect Chinese cars to cost more than the 15–20 thousand seen in Chinese reports, but say a capable car at 20–30 thousand would be a big shift.
2. **Tesla supply in Canada:** A report claims Tesla's import quota was limited by the government, forcing it to stop taking Model 3 Premium orders. The hosts note the source only says the minister did not answer a question, so they want written confirmation. They do say Tesla has stopped taking orders for these cars in Canada. One host says there is a real shortage of cars and that he found only about seven Model Ys, in one or two trims, after earlier finding none within 200 miles. The Model Y shipped from Berlin, premium version, is cited at about 65 thousand Canadian dollars.

## Market & Competition
1. **Import terms and first entrants:** Under the agreement the Prime Minister reached with the US, the hosts describe an arrangement in which half of imported vehicles are priced up to 35,000 wholesale at the port and the rest can be priced at any level. They speculate that Geely-owned brands could enter under a different name to fit the limits. A report on November 17 says Chery's first EV in Canada will arrive next spring, priced around 60–70 thousand with a CCS1 connector. Canada has also approved a second Chinese automaker for imports but has not named it; Chery, Geely and Dongfeng are known applicants. The host says BYD probably won't arrive until 2027.
2. **Tariffs and USMCA leverage:** Prime Minister Carney says Canada could work with Chinese automakers if trade talks with the US fail, because auto-sector tariffs are hurting the industry. The hosts note that Ontario and Quebec have the workers, expertise and parts suppliers built around the US market, and that this base is now in limbo.

## Energy
1. **Ontario storage projects:** Ontario completed three energy storage projects on time and on budget and is considering battery storage in various parts of the province to support the grid.
2. **Salt battery pilot:** Medicine Hat completed a $4.6 million project using Canada's first salt battery. It is still being commissioned, so performance and economics are not yet established.
3. **Storage economics:** The hosts cite a report that 4-hour battery storage is now cheaper than open-cycle gas turbines in 43 markets worldwide, and say batteries are typically designed to last 15–20 years. They also cite a ten-year Norfund and FinDev Canada partnership to 2030 to mobilize 10 billion in climate finance for storage and grid infrastructure.
4. **Distributed storage and grid build-out:** Canada's 2026 budget includes more than $50 billion to double grid capacity by 2050. A speaker says each neighborhood needs about $20 million in upgrades, whereas a single battery smaller than a parking space can power 190 homes for a couple hundred thousand dollars. The hosts argue local batteries reduce the need for new long-distance power lines. A Canadian company is also repurposing used battery packs for grid and backup storage.
