This is a panel-style Tesla/SpaceX news roundup (episode 161) covering the upcoming Tesla Roadster reveal, the Cybercab ride-hailing launch and its muted market reaction, a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger debate, and rapid-fire SpaceX and competitive news, ahead of Tesla's "We, Robot"-style event in Austin.

## Robotaxi
1. **24/7 robotaxi service expected next month:** A panelist relayed that Ashok Elluswamy said on September 4th that round-the-clock robotaxi service is coming next month, alongside a next-generation FSD version being tested against the current build; current service reportedly runs 6am–10pm.
2. **Unsupervised FSD passes one million robotaxi miles:** A panelist noted FSD unsupervised/robotaxi mileage has now hit one million miles, which they said should support expansion into more cities, including colder climates.
3. **Muted Wall Street reaction to Cybercab ride-hailing launch:** Per a panelist citing a report, TSLA stock fell about 6% on September 4th after Tesla's robotaxi-related invite; the event was not live-streamed, offered no details on production, pricing, or fleet size, and Elon Musk did not appear. Panelists attributed the market disappointment to lack of production/rollout details rather than doubts about the technology.
4. **Two-seater capacity debate:** One panelist said roughly 80% of rides involve two or fewer people, arguing the Cybercab's two-seat layout is not a limitation, and noted Model Y is also used as a robotaxi for larger groups.
5. **Uber liability contrasted with autonomous safety case:** A panelist cited a report that Uber was ordered to pay $40 million after a driver pulled over on a highway and a passenger was killed, using it to argue for the safety case of removing human drivers from ride-hailing.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Personal FSD anecdotes praised as evidence of capability:** Panelists shared anecdotes (a two-hour supervised drive with no issues logged, a car autonomously returning to a specific remembered parking spot) as informal evidence that FSD/vision-only approach "works," contrasting this with public skepticism.
2. **FSD testing in Spain reaches 460,000 km:** Tesla has reportedly logged 460,000 kilometers of supervised FSD testing in Spain, a market where Tesla isn't yet fully live, framed by panelists as evidence of expanding international testing.
3. **Tesla vs Waymo sensor philosophy debate:** Panelists discussed the camera-only (Tesla) vs. multi-sensor (Waymo) debate, noting Waymo/Cruise personnel have historically been trained to believe camera-only autonomy is unachievable, while Tesla engineers reportedly are comfortable riding in their own vehicles — used as an informal credibility signal for Tesla's approach.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Upcoming Starship flight to reuse booster-catch capability, deploy V3 satellites:** Panelists said Elon Musk stated at the All-In Summit that the booster could have been caught on the previous flight but they ran another test to confirm reliability before catching it on the next (15th) flight; the current flight will instead deploy the first Starship V3 satellite payload.
2. **Previous Starship flight achieved intact splashdown:** A panelist noted the prior flight, which was suborbital, achieved a soft splashdown without breaking apart — for which they express strong prior skepticism — and said it was being recovered from ocean waters, expected to yield unusually rich engineering data.
3. **Starship flight timing near suborbital threshold:** A panelist claimed the prior flight was roughly two seconds of additional thrust away from reaching full orbital velocity, and that the deliberate suborbital profile was a safety choice pending confirmed reliability of a system.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **V3 satellites bring major capacity increase:** A panelist described the upcoming Starship flight's ~26 first-generation V3 satellites as roughly twice the size of current satellites but with roughly ten times the capacity per satellite, framed as a major cost and profitability improvement for SpaceX.
2. **FCC approves Starlink Mobile international telecom services:** The FCC has granted SpaceX approval to provide international telecommunications services supporting Starlink Mobile; panelists speculated about pricing and coverage but had no confirmed details.
3. **Southwest adopts Starlink; Ryanair declines:** Southwest is adding Starlink to its fleet, following other airlines; Ryanair reportedly declined, citing brand concerns about the antenna's appearance. A panelist noted Starlink doesn't yet work over India but approval is expected, and in-flight pricing has been cheap or free on flights they've used.
4. **Constellation size estimate reduced:** A panelist claimed the planned satellite constellation, once envisioned at up to 150,000, could realistically cover global population with about 14,000 satellites given added bandwidth and the new V3 satellite design — presented as their own estimate, not an official figure.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Speculation on a Tesla–SpaceX merger:** Panelists discussed Elon Musk's comments at the All-In Summit as strongly hinting at an eventual merger without confirming one. One panelist relayed that investor Ron Baron said he'd support whatever Musk decides regarding a potential merger, while believing the companies can also succeed separately.
2. **Investor argues against a "merger of equals":** A self-identified long-term Tesla and SpaceX shareholder argued a merger should involve a real premium for Tesla shareholders (citing a hypothetical acceptable price in the $650–750/share range) rather than a no-premium "merger of equals," and suggested SpaceX's valuation has risen far faster than Tesla's in recent years, making an equal-split merger inequitable to Tesla holders.
3. **Speculation on rival bidders for Tesla:** A panelist speculated that Alphabet or Amazon could theoretically make a competing bid for Tesla to drive up its price, given their existing investments in Waymo and Zoox respectively, though this was framed as pure speculation, not reported fact.
4. **Tesla and SpaceX sue over "Terafab" trademark:** Tesla and SpaceX are reportedly suing a small Illinois firm in federal court over a trademark dispute after cease-and-desist orders; panelists expect a modest settlement payment, calling it immaterial to Tesla financially.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Tesla Roadster reveal expected around a delayed date:** Panelists discussed an upcoming Roadster-related reveal, referencing a graphic showing what may be an inverted Roadster; the reveal date has reportedly been pushed multiple times, with the panel citing September 28th as the latest expected date, close to Tesla's separate Austin event.
2. **Pricing and production speculation on Roadster:** Panelists noted the Roadster was originally announced years ago at a $250K price point but now speculate (unconfirmed) it could exceed $1 million, with uncertainty about whether a lower-cost, non-SpaceX-package version will be offered.
3. **Cybercab excluded from China robotaxi sales:** Tesla is reportedly bringing Cybercab to China but ruling out sales or robotaxi service there for now, which a panelist attributed to Tesla's data/camera restrictions in China; they speculated (unconfirmed) about possible future local manufacturing for APAC or European markets.
4. **Volkswagen EV concept compared unfavorably:** Panelists reacted to a new Volkswagen EV concept, comparing its design to a Cybercab, and expressed skepticism about Volkswagen's ability to compete effectively in EVs at this stage.
