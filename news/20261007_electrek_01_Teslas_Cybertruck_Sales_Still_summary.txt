Electrek estimates Tesla delivered roughly 7,000–8,000 Cybertrucks in Q3 2026, based on Tesla's "Other Models" category falling to 8,295 deliveries — a five-year low and down 48% year-over-year — now that Model S and Model X production has ended.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **"Other Models" category hits five-year low:** Tesla delivered 8,295 "Other Models" in Q3 2026, down from 15,933 a year ago and 12,364 last quarter — the lowest since Q2 2021. Production of this category was 7,004 units, down 40% from 11,624 a year ago. The category now consists mainly of the Cybertruck, plus small numbers of Semi and Cybercab units and a shrinking leftover pool of Model S/X (production of both ended in April, with about 600 cars in inventory plus a final run of 350 Signature cars still to deliver).

2. **Cybertruck estimate derived by subtraction:** Since Tesla doesn't break out Cybertruck deliveries, Electrek estimates 7,000–8,000 units by subtracting small estimated contributions from leftover Model S/X (a few hundred) and the Semi (estimated in the hundreds, since Tesla only announced volume production and customer deliveries on September 25, five days before quarter-end) from the 8,295 total. The Cybercab is called out as an unresolved variable — Tesla has reportedly produced "a few thousand" but it's unclear whether any are counted as "delivered" given it's delivering to its own robotaxi fleet.

3. **Pace still far below planned capacity:** At 7,000–8,000 per quarter, Tesla's run-rate is about 28,000–32,000 Cybertrucks a year, versus a planned 250,000/year production line and over 125,000 units of installed capacity cited in Tesla's latest shareholder letter — roughly 13% of the original plan and about a quarter of installed capacity, by the article's framing.

4. **Factors behind the improved quarter:** The article attributes the uptick to the cheaper dual-motor trim reaching customers in late June, elevated gas prices lifting EV demand broadly, and a delivery backlog — Tesla told a court in July (during litigation against its Cybertruck casting supplier) it had "several thousand Cybertrucks already committed to customers" it couldn't build without its tooling, implying some Q3 deliveries were orders that would otherwise have shipped earlier. Tesla then raised Cybertruck prices by $5,000 in August, with the dual-motor now starting at $74,990.

5. **Market context:** The article cites Cox Automotive data showing the Cybertruck was the best-selling electric pickup in the US in Q2 2026 at 3,744 units, ahead of the discontinued Ford F-150 Lightning (2,421), Chevy Silverado EV (2,266), and Rivian R1T (1,219). Separately, S&P Global Mobility counted 7,071 Cybertruck registrations in Q4 2025, of which 1,339 went to SpaceX and other Musk companies.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues this is the first quarter giving a genuinely clean read on Cybertruck volume, and that even the improved 7,000–8,000 figure required a cheaper trim and a gas-price-driven demand boost — undercut by Tesla's subsequent $5,000 price hike. The author expects this clarity to be short-lived, predicting that as Semi production ramps at a factory with stated capacity of 50,000 trucks/year, growth in "Other Models" will again mask Cybertruck's true sales figures.
