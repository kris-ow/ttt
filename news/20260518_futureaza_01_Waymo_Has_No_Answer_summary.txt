This May 18, 2026 live stream from FutureAzA covers approximately 20 news stories spanning Tesla's robotaxi crash data release, Waymo's ongoing technical and PR struggles, Tesla FSD and vehicle updates, EV market developments, and broader automotive industry news. The host's core argument is that Tesla's autonomous driving approach (neural net-based) is fundamentally superior to Waymo's hand-coded system, and that Waymo's recent stumbles — combined with Tesla's relatively benign crash data — reinforce this thesis.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Robotaxi Crash Data Released — Less Alarming Than Portrayed:** Tesla reportedly released previously unredacted crash data for its robotaxi program, which the host suggests may have been accidental. Reviewing the 17 reported incidents, the host argues the vast majority are trivial: vehicles being rear-ended while stopped at stop signs or red lights (other drivers' fault), a mirror lightly touching a truck, a tire contacting a curb while parking, and a metro bus's bike rack making contact with the vehicle. The host also notes that one incident involved a pothole destroying a tire, which he argues should be classified as an incident rather than an accident. The host characterizes these as events that most drivers would not report to police and says none would concern him.

2. **FSD Version 14.3.2/14.3.3 Rolling Out with Spring Update:** A new FSD version — described as either 14.3.2 or 14.3.3 — has begun rolling out alongside a spring software update. The host notes one vehicle received FSD 14.3.2/14.3.3 and another received the spring update separately, calling the split deployment unusual. Smart Summon top speed has been increased from 6 mph to 8 mph. The host also notes that FSD's maximum speed is 85 mph (or 87 mph if the readout is switched to kilometers, as the cap is 140 km/h).

3. **Waymo Red Light Running Incident:** A Waymo vehicle was captured on video running a red light on a left turn, which the host describes as "terrifying" and says he doesn't know how Waymo fixes this. He contrasts this with Tesla's neural net approach, arguing that a vision-based system learns from observing other vehicles' behavior, whereas Waymo's rule-based system fails to generalize to novel edge cases.

4. **Waymo Flood/Standing Water Software Recall:** Waymo issued a software recall after vehicles in Texas were swept away by floodwaters. The host explains the underlying problem as a hand-coded rules conflict: vehicles were programmed both to avoid water and to proceed through it in certain conditions, leading to inconsistent behavior. He argues a neural net would have observed other cars safely navigating around standing water and followed suit, illustrating a structural advantage for Tesla's FSD approach.

5. **Waymo Neighborhood Training Loop Issue:** Waymo was observed sending approximately 50 cars repeatedly through a single residential cul-de-sac, apparently to collect training data. The host mocks the inefficiency and suggests it reflects a compute limitation rather than a perception limitation — noting the cars get stuck when a "slow children" sign is posted, congregating without resolution.

6. **Tesla Hiring for China FSD Road Testing:** A series of job postings attributed to Tesla in China signals significant expansion of FSD development activity there. Roles include data labelers, intelligent driving testers (with requirements for flexible domestic and international travel and on-site public road testing), and technicians for city-specific smart driving tests. The host frames this as a major signal of FSD momentum in China.

## Robotaxi

1. **Uber Publicly Criticizes Waymo's Expansion Strategy:** Uber executives have been taking direct shots at Waymo, criticizing its AVON (autonomous vehicle operations) model and warning that scaling proprietary AV technology while bypassing ride-hailing platforms is misguided. Waymo has expanded its service to five more cities without Uber. The host dismisses Uber's moral framing, pointing out Uber itself has retreated from many markets due to high costs and that Waymo's premium pricing also limits its reach to wealthier neighborhoods.

2. **Waymo Drunk Passenger Incident Raises Design Questions:** A passenger reportedly climbed into a Waymo's driver's seat while intoxicated. Waymo revoked the rider's access. The incident prompted discussion about why Waymo vehicles retain a driver's seat and steering controls at all, with the host suggesting this creates unnecessary confusion and risk. He contrasts this with a separate carjacking attempt where Waymo used remote communications to prevent the vehicle from moving until police arrived.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Model Y Price Increase:** Tesla raised prices on the Model Y: Rear-Wheel Drive and All-Wheel Drive variants each went up $1,000, while the Performance trim increased by $500. The host notes that even with this increase, the price remains lower in real terms than after the April 1, 2024 price increase when adjusted for inflation (~6% cumulative).

2. **Two New Paint Colors for Model 3 and Model Y in the US:** Tesla introduced Marine Blue and Frost Blue as new color options for Model 3 and Model Y in the United States. Canada receives only one of the two colors, which the host explains is due to differences in which factory supplies the Canadian market.

3. **California $1 Billion EV Truck Rebate Program:** California announced a billion-dollar rebate program for electric trucks. The host argues Tesla stands to capture a disproportionate share because no other manufacturer has the volume or competitive pricing to take full advantage of the program.

4. **BYD Flash Battery Demand Outpacing Supply:** BYD reported that battery supply is tight due to accelerating demand for its flash-charging EVs. The host notes an online debate in China over thermal management, referencing a live-stream test that recorded battery surface temperatures of 76°C during high-rate charging sessions. He questions whether most consumers actually need megawatt-level charging speeds.

5. **Congress Bill to Permanently Block Chinese Vehicles from US:** A bipartisan bill introduced by Michigan legislators (one Democrat, one Republican) would permanently ban vehicles from Chinese automakers if they contain China-developed software or connectivity systems. The host attributes the Michigan origin to domestic auto industry protectionism rather than genuine data-privacy concerns, and is skeptical the bill will advance.

## Market & Competition

1. **Toyota $2 Billion Texas Assembly Plant Expansion:** Toyota announced plans to invest up to $2 billion expanding its existing San Antonio facility, creating approximately 2,000 new jobs. The host notes the relatively modest job count reflects this being an expansion rather than a new greenfield factory.

2. **Volkswagen-Backed Gotion Launches Sodium-Ion Battery:** Gotion (Volkswagen-backed) launched a sodium-ion battery claiming up to 261 Wh/kg for the high-energy version and 162 Wh/kg for the low-temperature version, described as mass-production ready. The host, while generally skeptical of "miracle battery" announcements, notes Gotion is a real company with credible (non-outrageous) claims, though he flags that sodium-ion cells are bulkier than comparable lithium cells despite competitive weight.

3. **TSMC Arizona Expansion:** TSMC announced a $20 billion expansion of its Arizona semiconductor facility, though the host notes the project faces water shortages, labor shortages, and complications from visa rules.

4. **Delta Airlines Rejected Starlink, Now Behind United:** Delta rejected Starlink in-flight internet because it required branding as a "Delta portal" rather than a generic Starlink connection. United Airlines adopted Starlink and is now years ahead in connectivity. The host frames this as Delta prioritizing brand optics over customer experience, at significant competitive cost.

5. **Ethiopia EV Import Surge:** Ethiopia imported a record 19,000 Chinese EVs in 2024 and has doubled that figure to 44,000 in 2025, driven by expanding domestic hydroelectric power capacity. The host highlights this as a model for energy security through domestic electricity production.

6. **GM 2027 Chevy Bolt Production Efficiency:** GM announced a production improvement for the 2027 Bolt involving batching cars by color and trim (30 units at a time) to reduce paint shop downtime and cleaning frequency between color changes. The host jokes that GM has rediscovered basic assembly-line principles.

7. **Mazda EV Delay:** Mazda announced a two-year delay on its next EV while effectively admitting development had not yet begun. The host references the Mazda MX-30 (100 miles range, $35,000) as an example of Mazda's historically weak EV commitment.
