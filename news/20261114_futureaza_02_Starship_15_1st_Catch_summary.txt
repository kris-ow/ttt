This is a livestream co-hosted by futureaza, "John" (Dr. Noal), and later Casey Green, covering SpaceX's flight 15 mission in real time — the first-ever attempted catch of a Starship vehicle (ship 42) at the Starbase tower, following the previous day's booster launch and its diverted water landing. The stream intersperses live mission commentary with discussion of Tesla's newly announced Cybercab pricing and reservation terms, and ends with the successful historic catch.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Historic first Starship catch succeeds:** Ship 42 (flight 15) was successfully caught by the Starbase tower ("Mechazilla") after completing 14 orbits and a roughly 22.5-hour mission — the first time SpaceX has ever caught a Starship upper stage, following earlier successful booster catches. SpaceX's own broadcast team called it one of the most significant engineering milestones in the company's history, calling it "the holy grail of spaceflight" and the "missing link" in rapid reusability.
2. **Booster diverted to water landing after low-fuel alarm:** During the prior day's launch, the Super Heavy booster's boost-back burn ended early due to what SpaceX described as a low fuel mass alarm; guidance then detected it was out of position for a tower catch and it autonomously diverted to a safe offshore water landing, where it triggered its flight termination system as designed. Posted comments attributed to Elon Musk (via a chat with a third party, unconfirmed by hosts) said the booster was burning copper (indicating engine erosion), had depleted its regenerative cooling and gone oxygen-rich in the main combustion chamber, and that cutting the engine about one second earlier would have prevented the issue; Musk reportedly said losing the tower/launch mount would cost two to three months, and that SpaceX has the next booster nearly ready to fly.
3. **Re-entry performance and technical details:** Starship re-entered at over 26,000 km/h, reaching peak heat-shield temperatures described as around 2,600°F, before slowing for the landing burn (three Raptor engines down to one) and a "belly flop"/swoosh maneuver — the first time that maneuver had been performed in some time per SpaceX commentary. The hosts noted Starship re-enters roughly five times faster than the booster, which by their rough math (velocity squared) implies roughly 25x the heat load rather than any exact figure cited on-stream.
4. **Mission also deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites:** All 26 satellites deployed from the ship during the mission were reported healthy and raising to operational altitude; the hosts noted Starlink's network was used to relay live video and telemetry during re-entry, when normal communications are typically blacked out by plasma.
5. **Possible reflight of this same booster/ship:** The hosts relayed Musk's stated intent (unconfirmed specifics) that if the booster/ship came back in good condition, SpaceX could attempt to refly it on the very next flight (flight 16).

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab reservations opened with monthly fee structure:** Tesla opened Cybercab orders/reservations, drawing criticism in the chat that a cited $299/month fee is too high; the hosts noted an alternate interpretation that the price is closer to $115/month when including premium connectivity (matching FSD/connectivity costs on other models), and debated that the $299 figure may reflect a corporate/fleet rate rather than personal use. Per the hosts, the Cybercab is geofenced to Texas, restricted to personal/household use only (not commercial fleet use, e.g., not usable by a real estate agent driving clients), does not include insurance, and carries restrictions such as no wrapping, painting, tinting, or custom tires.
2. **MKBHD (referred to by hosts without the garbled name, but understood as the tech reviewer previously betting on Cybercab pricing) reportedly received and attempted to use a reservation invite:** Per the hosts, he was initially told the invite wasn't valid in his region (New Jersey) but later posted that he was able to complete an order, which the hosts said satisfies the literal terms of an earlier public bet regarding availability under a certain price by year-end — though they noted the current rollout doesn't reflect broad public availability.
3. **Hosts' own addressable-market estimate:** One host estimated the near-term Cybercab total addressable market, given Texas-only geofencing, at roughly 1,000–5,000 (up to a generous ceiling of 10,000), characterizing current buyers as the earliest early adopters.
