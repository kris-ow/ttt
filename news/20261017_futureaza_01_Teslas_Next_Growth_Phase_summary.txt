This futureaza video (hosted by Brian, joined by Herbert) runs through several Tesla growth vectors the hosts believe could inflect over the next few quarters: Megapack/energy storage, Cybercab/robotaxi scaling, the Tesla-SpaceX Terafab chip venture, Semi trucks, and FSD-driven demand. The core thesis is that Tesla has multiple businesses approaching simultaneous growth phases, with execution (not stock price) as the stated focus.

## Energy
1. **Megapack deployments seen as lumpy, due to catch up:** The hosts say Q3 Megapack numbers disappointed analysts who expected higher figures, which they attribute to the inherently uneven pace of deployment/commissioning versus production. They note production capacity of roughly 10 GWh at Lathrop, 10 GWh at Shanghai, and over 10 GWh at Giga Texas, with actual achieved output around 13.8 GWh (approximate, per the host). They expect deployment figures to catch up and potentially overshoot in Q4 or Q1, and note battery cell supply (LFP cells, mostly sourced from China, with some from LG Energy in the US) is not currently a constraint.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab scaling cited by Eric Earley and Elon Musk:** At the X Takeover event, Eric Earley (described as head of the Cybercab project) reportedly said current progress feels slow but exponential growth is expected "in the coming months" — language the host says Musk echoed. The host cites a jump from under 40 to over 150 Cybercabs added in a single day around the event, which he attributes partly to preparing for an influx of attendees; he says the system did not slow down despite the surge.
2. **Mixed rider experience on drop-off accuracy:** The host personally experienced accurate final-destination drop-offs during Cybercab rides in Austin, but says other attendees reported the vehicle did not drop them at the expected location — suggesting operational kinks remain even as safety/scale looked solid at ~150 vehicles.
3. **Host's own projection for fleet growth:** The host speculates the fleet could reach roughly 2,000 vehicles by the end of this year, calling it plausible given recent momentum, but says reaching 10,000–50,000 next year is harder to call, with "higher" odds based on recent-quarter trends — explicitly framed as his own guess, not a confirmed figure.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Demand narrative inverted per Ashok Elluswamy and Elon Musk:** The hosts relay remarks attributed to Ashok Elluswamy and Elon Musk on the latest earnings call describing a shift where buyers now choose which car to buy based on wanting FSD, rather than FSD being an afterthought to liking the car.
2. **FSD subscription attach rate:** The host cites a previously reported 55% rate of new Tesla buyers choosing the monthly FSD subscription, and expects this to keep rising as FSD improves and awareness grows.
3. **Host's own timeline skepticism on unsupervised FSD:** The host states he does not believe Tesla will achieve full unsupervised FSD (e.g., legally sleeping in the back seat) even next year, estimating it will take at least one to two more years, and predicts any early unsupervised capability will be geofenced to a limited area like Austin rather than nationwide.
4. **Upcoming FSD version anticipated:** The host says he is anticipating a newer FSD version (referred to as "version 15") that he expects to bring a notable safety improvement, citing Ashok Elluswamy, with a possible arrival this year or, failing that, next quarter — framed as his own expectation, not a confirmed release date.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi truck production ramp observed:** The host says a recent site visit counted over 40 (roughly 45-46) Semi trucks appearing ready to ship, describing this as outside formal production in a loose, observational sense.
2. **Roadster and drones framed as lower-conviction bets:** The host is skeptical the Roadster will be a high-volume product, though he believes its R&D benefits other models. He separately speculates Tesla could enter the drone market, citing a reported comparison of a $10,000 drone versus a $21,500 "American version" that police are required to buy, but says he's unsure Tesla has the resources for this and would be only "a little" surprised either way.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Tesla-SpaceX Terafab chip venture construction underway:** The host describes active, round-the-clock construction (cranes running at midnight) at two Terafab sites — a smaller research/testing facility near Giga Texas and a larger facility in Grimes County, Texas — as a joint Tesla-SpaceX chip manufacturing initiative, with foundation work already completed at the Grimes County site and projected to shape the company over a three-to-five-year horizon.
2. **Partnership list and strategic rationale:** The host names Intel, Samsung, and TSMC as partners, with ASML described as in talks or already joined (uncertain per the host). He frames the partner companies' promises to boost their own production as implicit pressure not to let Terafab reach full self-sufficiency, i.e., using Terafab as leverage to secure better terms/supply rather than a certain full build-out — his own interpretation, not a confirmed company statement.
3. **Nvidia relationship tied to data center chip allocation:** The host says Tesla's AI4/AI5 chip collaboration involves Samsung and TSMC, and separately claims Nvidia will reserve its newest "Vera Rubin" chips for Tesla/SpaceX data centers, expected to materialize in the first quarter — presented as the host's understanding of the relationship rather than a quoted company commitment.
