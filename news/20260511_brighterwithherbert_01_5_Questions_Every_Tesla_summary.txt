This video features host "brighterwithherbert" interviewing Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and active venture capital investor, to address the five key questions Tesla investors are asking. The conversation covers Tesla's auto sales rebound, the robotaxi rollout, the SpaceX IPO as a major catalyst, and the biggest underpriced catalyst for Tesla stock. Larry's core thesis is that Tesla stock will surpass its all-time high this year, driven primarily by a successful SpaceX IPO rather than any specific robotaxi fleet milestone.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Auto Sales Rebound Is Real But Modest:** Larry characterizes Tesla's auto business as a mature, legacy business undergoing modest growth rather than explosive expansion. He expects new form factors (such as an enlarged Model Y in the US), continued evolution of the Cybertruck, and new models tied to the robotaxi fleet and a "robo van." He does not view the auto business as Tesla's growth engine, but sees it as a stable, cash-flow-generating foundation that underpins the robotaxi business.

2. **Strong Regional Sales Data Cited:** The host highlights specific April sales data: China up 36% (described as its highest ever), Denmark up 102%, Sweden up 111%, and France up 112% in volume of Tesla car sales. Larry acknowledges the rebound is real and timely, coinciding with FSD rollout and the early stages of robotaxi scaling.

3. **Full Self-Driving as a Demand Driver:** Both the host and Larry agree that FSD is already becoming a demand driver for vehicle sales, even without significant marketing or advertising. Larry argues that once broader awareness of FSD's capabilities spreads — as it is beginning to do in Europe — a massive shift in consumer demand will follow. Larry frames the shift as Tesla no longer selling cars but selling self-driving capability, with the car model being a secondary choice.

4. **FSD's Competitive Advantage Is Time-Limited:** Larry notes that FSD is currently roughly three to four years ahead of the market, but cautions that this advantage will erode over that same timeframe. He therefore argues Tesla's true long-term business is robotaxi, not FSD itself, which he sees as years — potentially a decade — ahead of competitors as a business model.

## Robotaxi
1. **No Magic Fleet Number for Stock Movement:** Larry pushes back on the idea that a specific unsupervised robotaxi fleet size — whether 500, 1,000, or surpassing Waymo's scale of roughly 3,000 vehicles — will trigger a stock re-rating. He argues there is instead a singular "magic moment" of public realization, similar to the moment during Tesla's production hell when registration data began moving despite widespread skepticism. The precipitating fact of that moment is unpredictable and cannot be engineered by hitting a particular milestone number.

2. **Robotaxi Scaling Not Expected to Be Complete This Year:** Larry explicitly states he does not believe robotaxi will be "scaled" by end of 2026, contrary to some investor expectations. He anticipates continuous and ongoing progress but cautions against expecting a fully scaled network within the year. The host references a current figure of approximately 38 unsupervised robotaxis, and Larry suggests the fleet may be in the range of 100 to 200 by the time the SpaceX IPO occurs.

3. **Unsupervised Personal Use as an Underpriced Catalyst:** The host references a Piper Sandler thesis that unsupervised personal use — the ability for existing Tesla owners to use their personal vehicles in a fully driverless capacity — is the biggest catalyst not yet priced into the stock. Larry acknowledges Piper Sandler's deep market knowledge on this point but personally ranks the SpaceX IPO as a larger near-term catalyst.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Expected to Show Meaningful Capability by Q3–Q4 2026:** Larry states he expects Optimus to "really roll out" and demonstrate genuine capability by the third or fourth quarter of 2026, describing it as one of several simultaneous catalysts — alongside robotaxi progress and the SpaceX IPO — that could drive Tesla's stock higher this year.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX IPO as the Single Biggest Tesla Stock Catalyst of 2026:** Larry identifies a successful SpaceX IPO — which he expects in June 2026 — as the single most important catalyst for Tesla stock this year. He outlines three reasons: (1) a successful IPO will reawaken public perception of Elon Musk as a visionary, moving past the political environment that damaged his image; (2) it will help investors understand how Tesla and SpaceX work together as complementary businesses; and (3) it will validate Elon's credibility on robotaxi promises, since SpaceX's success proves his ability to deliver on audacious claims. Larry estimates Tesla stock could move 30–40% on the back of a successful SpaceX IPO.

2. **SpaceX Valuation and IPO Dynamics:** Larry discusses SpaceX's IPO pricing expectations, suggesting an offering at approximately $1.75 trillion with a successful outcome defined as the stock rising to $2 trillion by end of day. He notes SpaceX was valued at roughly $800 billion just months ago, and that the Anthropic deal has materially changed the valuation picture. He expects a staggered lockup period, likely as long as six months, meaning any Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement or exploration would not come until after the lockup ends — likely after the end of 2026.

3. **Tesla-SpaceX Merger Discussion:** Larry believes a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement is possible but will not occur around the time of the IPO or during the lockup period. He speculates that discussions, if happening at all, would be extremely private. He describes a potential merger announcement as "extraordinarily positive" for Tesla stock and expects it would be a landmark event, though he aligns with the view that it would be locked in step with SpaceX's post-IPO trajectory.

4. **Anthropic Deal as a Proxy Signal:** Both the host and Larry point to Tesla's stock rising approximately $10 following SpaceX's deal with Anthropic — a deal with no direct Tesla connection — as evidence that investor instincts, not spreadsheet analysis, drive stock movements. Larry uses this as an analogy: if a tangential SpaceX deal moved Tesla $10, a successful SpaceX IPO could move it $50.

5. **Tesla Stock Expected to Surpass All-Time High in 2026:** Larry states his expectation that Tesla stock will surpass its all-time high this year, closely tied to SpaceX IPO progress. He notes the prior all-time high was reached at the time of Tesla's S&P 500 inclusion, which he characterizes as "artificial," and anticipates the next high will be more fundamentally driven by the confluence of events — auto rebound, FSD adoption, robotaxi progress, Optimus rollout, and the SpaceX IPO.
