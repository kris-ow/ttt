This episode of FutureAzA features host Brian and correspondent Randy Kirk discussing a cluster of Tesla news items from the week, including the end of the Model S and Model X era, a postponed delivery event, battery technology developments, brand loyalty data, and Tesla/SpaceX's partnership with Intel on a semiconductor fabrication facility. The hosts offer context on whether each headline materially matters to Tesla's stock and long-term thesis, generally concluding that strong sales volume and the robotaxi ramp are the dominant drivers of the current stock rally.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **End of the Model S and Model X Era:** The final Model S has been produced, marking the end of the SNX lineup. The hosts note that the last car was reportedly graffitied before delivery to its buyer, Steve Jurvetson, who they speculate will apply clear PPF wrap to preserve it. Randy argues the retirement is a natural business decision: as Tesla scales, products that once represented a major revenue line become "a rounding error," and maintaining the factory space and cost structure for low-volume models is increasingly hard to justify. The hosts suggest Tesla still has halo-car appeal through the Cybertruck, Cybercab, and the forthcoming Roadster.

2. **Delivery Event Postponed with 72 Hours Notice:** A scheduled celebration and handover event for the final Model S/X vehicles, planned for May 12, 2026, was postponed with only 72 hours notice and no new date provided. The hosts speculate the likely reason is Elon Musk's expected participation in a presidential trade trip to China alongside other tech executives including Tim Cook. They acknowledge this is inconvenient for loyal customers who booked non-refundable travel, but conclude it will have zero impact on the stock price.

3. **Giga Shanghai Posts Strongest April Ever:** Tesla's Giga Shanghai recorded its strongest April on record, delivering approximately 80,000 vehicles in the month. The hosts note this puts the factory on an annualized run rate of close to one million units. They highlight that while China domestic sales have been somewhat soft, Tesla is increasingly exporting from Shanghai to markets including Canada, Europe, and South Korea, describing a broad surge in global demand.

4. **Tesla Brand Loyalty Still Above 60%:** A brand loyalty study found Tesla owners return to buy another Tesla at a rate above 60%, still ranking first among automakers, though slightly lower than historical highs. The hosts attribute the modest decline primarily to political backlash (including incidents of vandalism at Tesla locations) earlier in the year, which they say has since largely faded. They view the retention figure as consistent with Tesla's positioning as a premium product with demonstrable advantages in safety and ADAS.

## Market & Competition

1. **4680 Battery Underperforms vs. LFP and 2170:** Reports surfaced that the 4680 cell has fallen short of expectations on both energy density (watt-hours per kilogram) and charging speed, failing to surpass the older 2170 cell on either metric. Meanwhile, LFP chemistry has advanced significantly — offering higher charge/discharge rates, more cycles, cheaper materials, and now competitive fast-charging speeds. The hosts discuss a battery deep-dive interview on Dillon Loomis's show with a guest named Iona covering Tesla batteries and Megapack technology. Randy notes Tesla remains the leader in high-nickel (NMC/NCA) chemistries, but acknowledges LFP is closing the performance gap, particularly relevant in China where apartment dwellers depend on public fast charging. Tesla has reportedly adopted a very fast-charging LFP battery (described as 5C or 3C) for the Chinese market.

2. **China Battery Competition Intensifying:** Chinese manufacturers including CATL and BYD are aggressively scaling battery production, overbuilding factory capacity and driving down prices. The hosts frame this as a familiar pattern of Chinese industrial overinvestment creating intense price competition, while noting Tesla retains a differentiated position on the high-nickel chemistry side.

## Financials

1. **Stock Up ~4% on the Day of Recording:** Tesla's stock was up approximately 4%, or $16.73 per share, on the day of recording (Monday, May 12, 2026). The hosts note the stock is now within approximately 10% of its all-time high of $495, meaning nearly all current shareholders are in a profit position. They attribute roughly 50% of the recent rally to strong car sales numbers, with the robotaxi ramp cited as the other major catalyst expected to drive an all-time high later in 2026.

## Autonomous Driving / Market & Competition

1. **Tesla and SpaceX Partner with Intel on Terafab Semiconductor Fab:** Elon Musk toured Intel's semiconductor fabrication plant in Hillsborough, Oregon, ahead of a formal Tesla and SpaceX partnership with Intel to build a chip fabrication facility called the "Terafab." The hosts recount that Tesla first approached Samsung (who allowed a factory tour but apparently didn't satisfy Musk's ambitions) and then TSMC (who declined to partner), before landing on Intel, which agreed to share its new chip architecture and manufacturing methodology. The first phase of the Terafab is planned to cost $55 billion, split between Tesla and SpaceX, making the financial burden more manageable for both. The hosts argue the deal is arguably even more significant for SpaceX than Tesla, as it de-risks a key question ahead of SpaceX's anticipated IPO. They also note that Samsung subsequently announced it would dedicate more chip capacity — suggesting the Intel deal sparked competitive pressure.
