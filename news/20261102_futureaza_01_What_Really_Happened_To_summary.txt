This video from futureaza investigates a Tesla Semi fire that occurred on a California highway, arguing that sensational headlines overstated the incident and that context — the vehicle's age, fire statistics for commercial trucks generally, and Tesla's own safety updates — paints a far less alarming picture. The host frames the piece as a deliberate, slow, fact-based correction to rushed news coverage rather than breaking news.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi fire in Hayward, CA was an old pre-production test unit, not a current model:** A Tesla Semi caught fire around 5:30–5:45 p.m. local time on a Monday in Hayward, California, blocking a highway in both directions for roughly 10 hours before being extinguished; there was no collision, accident, injury, or death. The host identifies the vehicle as a Frito-Lay-liveried, hand-assembled early test/pilot truck — not a current production unit — using an older Panasonic 2170 battery cell rather than the 4680 cells used in newer Tesla Semi designs, and says the battery pack design has since been changed/made smaller, though he notes comprehensive details on the current pack aren't available. Preliminary reports cited by the host indicate the fire was concluded (based on early information) to have started in the battery pack, with no confirmed cause from California Highway Patrol as of publication; initial speculation about the trailer's refrigeration unit was reportedly not supported by visual evidence.
2. **Tesla updated its first-responder guidance within hours of the incident:** The host found that Tesla's existing first-responder document for the Semi was a four-page 2022 rescue briefing simply advising responders to pour water; a few hours after his own post about this, a new 37-page response guide appeared, specifying roughly 8,000 gallons of water fed directly to the battery, advising against foam, and warning of hydrogen fluoride exposure — which he characterizes as a quick, appropriate correction by Tesla.
3. **Tesla's fire rate compares favorably to broader vehicle and commercial-truck fire statistics:** The host cites general data showing commercial truck/large-vehicle fires occur roughly every 20–21 million miles and gasoline passenger vehicle fires roughly every 17 million miles, versus Tesla's own safety data showing (per the host) roughly a 20-times-lower fire probability than the comparison figures. He also notes a separate dataset showing about 7,000 commercial truck fires over a five-to-six-year window (2003–2008), i.e., over a thousand per year, to argue such fires are common and routinely under-covered by media compared with Tesla incidents.
4. **Prior Tesla Semi fire (earlier incident) was accident-related, unlike this one:** The host states a previous Tesla Semi fire (referenced as occurring in a prior year) was caused by an accident/collision, in contrast to this latest Hayward fire, which he says had no accident and was likely linked to an old, non-upgraded battery rather than a design flaw in current production vehicles. He also dismisses viewer suggestions that sodium-ion batteries are a needed fix, arguing their lower energy density makes them unsuitable for trucks requiring long range.
