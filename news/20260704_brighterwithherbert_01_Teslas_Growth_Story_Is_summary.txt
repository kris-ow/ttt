This episode features analyst Cern Basher walking through Tesla's Q2 2026 results, framing it as a broad-based improvement across deliveries, energy deployments, FSD adoption, and China/Europe demand, while contrasting Tesla's scale against struggling EV competitors Lucid, Rivian, and Ford. The core thesis is that Tesla's growth engine is reaccelerating simultaneously across its major business lines ahead of the robotaxi and Optimus ramp.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries beat estimates by a wide margin:** Tesla delivered 480,000 vehicles in Q2, its fourth-highest quarterly total ever and a new Q2 record, beating Wall Street expectations by roughly 60,000 units; Cern Basher noted Wall Street estimates are generally poor and that community forecaster James Stephenson was closest at a prediction of around 482,000.
2. **China posts best month of 2026, extends streak:** Tesla China had its strongest month of 2026, up 24% year-over-year, extending an eight-month growth streak and following a 39% year-over-year surge the prior month (unverified/approximate, per the discussion).
3. **Europe demand rebounds sharply:** Regional sales recovered broadly across France, Sweden, Denmark, and Spain (Norway roughly flat, already near-saturated), attributed largely to high fuel prices sustaining EV demand versus a more incentive-driven US market.
4. **Giga Berlin raising output again:** Plant manager Andre Thierig said Giga Berlin is increasing weekly production toward 7,500 vehicles per week starting in October (a further ~20% increase on top of a previously announced 20% increase), adding roughly 1,000 more jobs (bringing total new hires discussed to about 3,500 across vehicle and battery manufacturing).
5. **Production/delivery efficiency remains tight:** Trailing 12-month production and delivery curves track closely together; Cern Basher calculated inventory days of about 16, implying Tesla is effectively running near a negative two days of inventory once average delivery lag is considered.
6. **Factory utilization near 77%:** Stated production capacity is 2.25 million units annually; trailing 12-month production implies about 77% utilization, though Basher cautioned the capacity figure itself is uncertain.
7. **Approaching 10 million cumulative vehicles:** Cumulative production stood at about 9.861 million and cumulative deliveries about 9.721 million; Basher speculated (explicitly as a guess) that production could cross 10 million in late July and deliveries in late August.
8. **Model 2/affordable models scrapped in favor of autonomy focus:** The hosts discussed Tesla's earlier decision to cancel a sub-$25,000 compact model and shelve promised additional affordable vehicles in favor of concentrating resources on Cybercab and autonomy, which they believe hurt near-term unit sales but was the right long-term capital allocation call.
9. **Competitor Q2 results dwarfed by Tesla:** Lucid delivered about 4,000 vehicles (produced ~4,700) in Q2, up 20%, while replacing its CFO and citing supply chain issues amid continued large losses; Rivian delivered about 12,000 vehicles, up 14%, while raising guidance; Ford sold about 9,000 EVs in Q2, with the Mach-E down 31% year-over-year and F-150 Lightning discontinued following loss of incentives — Basher argued Ford's EV commitment is now severely challenged.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD framed as a demand driver:** Discussion centered on Tesla executive commentary (referred to as "Lars," understood to be Lars Moravy) describing FSD subscription growth as very strong, though Basher stressed his specific take-rate figures were his own aspirational estimates, not confirmed data.
2. **Speculative FSD subscription targets for Q2:** Basher said he would like to see total active FSD subscriptions reach 1.6 million (up from 1.28 million in Q1), implying a cumulative take rate of about 16.5% of ~10 million cumulative vehicles and an incremental take rate near 37% of vehicles sold this quarter — explicitly flagged as his own hoped-for/guessed numbers, not official or predicted figures, pending Tesla's July 22 earnings report.
3. **Hardware generation affects FSD quality:** Older Hardware 3 vehicles get a materially weaker FSD experience than newer Hardware/AI4 vehicles (available in Model 3 and Model Y), which Basher expects to become "even more amazing" in coming quarters.
4. **Rivian's in-house self-driving effort questioned:** Basher expressed skepticism that Rivian can independently develop a fully autonomous system comparable to Tesla's, suggesting Rivian may reach a supervised driver-assistance level at best and questioning why it didn't seek to license Tesla's technology instead.
5. **FSD pricing philosophy tied to insurance integration:** Basher argued Tesla's $99/month FSD price is not meant to be a major profit center but a demand driver; he noted Tesla is rolling out its own insurance (cited as coming to Washington state soon) with FSD-linked discount tiers similar to Lemonade's approach, which could make the FSD subscription cost roughly offset by insurance savings.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab positioned as focal strategic bet:** The hosts discussed Tesla's strategic pivot toward Cybercab/robotaxi over new affordable vehicle models, viewing Cybercab as potentially the most profitable part of the auto business, with EV ownership possibly serving as a "gateway" to robotaxi adoption (or vice versa).
2. **Owner-fleet robotaxi timeline seen as distant:** Basher said he is conservative/pessimistic on Tesla allowing customers to add personal vehicles into a robotaxi fleet, estimating this may not happen until late next year or later, though he sees long-term merit in distributing charging/cleaning infrastructure to individual owners and in building a broader stakeholder base to blunt political/monopoly risk.

## Energy
1. **Second-highest energy deployment quarter ever:** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of energy storage in the quarter; trailing 12-month deployment reached a new high of 49 GWh, and cumulative lifetime deployment is approaching 140 GWh (estimated around 132 GWh currently).
2. **Capacity utilization estimated around 54%:** Based on Basher's capacity estimates (Lathrop at 40 GWh/year fully scaled; Shanghai megapack capacity possibly reduced from 40 GWh to a lower figure, and Houston's mega-block capacity expected online around Q3), deployments imply roughly 54% utilization, though Basher cautioned this lags actual production and may understate true utilization.
3. **Combined auto+energy framing shows new highs:** Converting energy deployments into EV-equivalents (using 75 kWh packs) yields about 653,000 vehicle-equivalents over the trailing 12 months; combined with actual EV deliveries (~1.753 million trailing 12-month, below the prior peak of 1.808 million), total EV-equivalents hit a record 2.406 million. Measured instead in energy terms, autos contributed about 132 GWh and energy storage about 49 GWh for a combined new-high total of about 181 GWh.
4. **Approaching 1 terawatt-hour lifetime deployment:** Basher estimated Tesla will cross 1 TWh of cumulative energy deployed within about three quarters, guessing (explicitly speculative) this could occur around early 2027.
