This video features analyst Cern Basher walking through three deep-dive breakdowns of Tesla's Q2 earnings call: Ashok Elluswamy's comments on robotaxi safety and scaling, an analysis of whether Tesla is being too cautious with robotaxi rollout versus Waymo, and 13 takeaways from Elon Musk's remarks. The core thesis is that demand and safety are no longer the bottleneck for Tesla's autonomy push — the constraint is now execution speed and scaling — and that investors focused on short-term linear progress are missing a nonlinear, exponential growth pattern that both Musk and Elluswamy described.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Ashok Elluswamy details unsupervised safety record:** Elluswamy stated Tesla has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents, framing this as validation of Tesla's camera-only approach without lidar, radar, or HD maps. He noted any reported incidents involved other actors impacting stationary vehicles.
2. **Double-digit weekly growth in unsupervised miles:** Elluswamy said unsupervised miles have grown at a double-digit rate week-over-week for several months and are expected to continue at that rate through the rest of the year; Musk separately said he believes it's "at least over 10%." Basher cautions this isn't necessarily a smooth 10% weekly rate — it could be higher, lower, or occur in stair-steps, and even the lowest interpretation implies large compounding growth (roughly 12x over six months, ~140x over a year at a steady 10%/week).
3. **New-city expansion effort declining, trending toward statewide operation:** Elluswamy said the engineering effort required to launch a new city is falling and should trend toward zero, with Tesla's eventual goal being to operate across entire states rather than city-by-city — a comment Basher calls the first explicit mention of state-by-state expansion.
4. **FSD V15 (10x parameter model) already partially deployed in robotaxi fleet:** Elluswamy said Tesla planned roughly seven major improvement tracks for V15, with about 40% already merged into early builds running in the robotaxi fleet — a detail Basher calls a surprise, noting it's still an early, incomplete version with no timeline given for full completion (Musk separately mentioned upcoming memory/destination-reasoning features, per Basher, to be taken with appropriate caution given the source).
5. **FSD attach rate cited as a demand driver:** Musk-era data cited: 55% of new vehicles delivered in North America had FSD attached at time of delivery, versus 42% globally, which Basher/Herbert frame as a major and underappreciated demand engine for vehicle sales.
6. **Tesla vs. Waymo architecture comparison:** Waymo has reportedly logged about 220 million fully autonomous miles — roughly 580 times Tesla's unsupervised mile count — with published safety stats (94% fewer serious/fatal crashes, 82% fewer injury-causing crashes than human driving) across cities including LA, San Francisco, Phoenix, Austin, and Atlanta. Basher notes Waymo's architecture defaults to inaction with remote-operator intervention when uncertain, which can cause stranded-vehicle incidents (e.g., during power outages), whereas Tesla is aiming for AI-driven reasoning without remote operators to enable scaling beyond a few thousand vehicles.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab production aligned to unsupervised-mile growth rate:** Elluswamy said Tesla has aligned Cybercab manufacturing targets to roughly match projected unsupervised-mile growth. Basher and Herbert both infer (their own interpretation, not a direct quote) that this implies Cybercab will likely be added to the paid robotaxi network within a couple of months, since scaling production only makes sense if the vehicles enter revenue service rather than remaining employee-only test units.
2. **Multi-city rollout intended to prove generalization, not permanent city-by-city strategy:** Elluswamy said expansion across cities (rather than doubling down on one) is meant to prove the driving stack generalizes without heavy per-city engineering; once proven, Tesla's stated goal is to move to state-by-state and eventually near-instant activation, unlike Waymo's continued city-by-city approach.
3. **Assessment: Tesla not being "too cautious," per Basher:** Basher concludes Tesla's slower-than-hoped rollout reflects a deliberate long-term strategy — managing political/regulatory risk (citing a pending EU vote on supervised FSD where France has said it will vote no, and provisional approvals in five countries that could be revoked), waiting for Cybercab validation and Starlink connectivity integration, and avoiding Waymo/China-style backlash from stalled vehicles — rather than excess caution. He expects actual mass scaling more around mid-2027 rather than this year.
4. **Fleet mile accumulation and safety statistics context:** Beyond the 380,000 fully unsupervised miles, Basher/Herbert cite that Tesla has accumulated roughly 2.5 million robotaxi miles overall (including supervised safety-monitor miles) with no fatalities, and that a Cybercab is expected to log about three times more daily miles than a Waymo vehicle due to battery/charging efficiency, potentially accelerating mile accumulation once Cybercabs enter service.
5. **RobotaxiTracker.com fleet context:** (Not directly cited by number in transcript beyond general fleet discussion) Tesla's active robotaxi fleet stood at 791 vehicles as of 2026-07-24 per RobotaxiTracker.com, with the unsupervised (driverless) subset last reported at 39 as of 2026-05-09 (stale, unchanged for ~77 days).

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus timing remains least certain, deliberately slow-walked:** Musk reiterated Optimus has enormous opportunity but the least certain timing, citing all-new components and no existing supply chain. Basher notes Musk is not making the aggressive short-term promises here that he has for robotaxi, and interprets Tesla's approach as building for generalized robot intelligence (training via video/demo) rather than rushing narrow-task deployment — suggesting broad Optimus deployment won't occur until a version several generations from now.
2. **Major capex commitment to Optimus manufacturing capacity:** Tesla shared photos of the Model S/X line being repurposed for million-unit-capacity Optimus production, and a new large-scale factory in Texas is under construction. Basher argues this level of capital commitment is a strong long-term signal of confidence, arguing Musk would not build such capacity if he expected years of delay.
3. **Digital Optimus (software-only agent) framed as a potential independent revenue stream:** Musk said training a digital version of Optimus also aids physical robot training, since a humanoid robot needs to operate human-designed interfaces (touchscreens, terminals, machinery). Basher and Herbert discuss this as a possible major, non-manufactured revenue opportunity (compared informally to Anthropic's Claude Code), though its competitive advantage over existing AI agents from other companies remains unproven and undemonstrated.

## Energy
1. **Megapack framed as critical AI data-center infrastructure:** Musk discussed Megapack's role in smoothing power delivery to AI data centers, which Basher notes SpaceX has already proven out; falling Megapack cost per kilowatt-hour is expected alongside less price-sensitive data-center customers, a favorable margin dynamic going forward.
2. **Solar manufacturing and autonomous Semi mentioned without new specifics:** Musk referenced upcoming solar manufacturing plans (location unconfirmed) and an autonomous Semi, which Musk suggested could arrive around late 2026 or early 2027 (Basher suggests adding a buffer to that timeline).

## Financials
1. **Terafab framed as a major strategic value driver:** Musk emphasized chip production (Terafab) as a strategic constraint Tesla is trying to solve. Basher reiterates his own prior view that Terafab could represent roughly a trillion dollars in present value for Tesla if it works, even though the market isn't currently pricing that in.
2. **Micron memory allocation secured despite tight supply:** Musk indicated Tesla has secured a memory allocation from Micron that he seems satisfied with, despite Micron being largely sold out for the next year or more and other major customers (e.g., Apple) reportedly struggling to get supply.
3. **Capital efficiency being relaxed in favor of speed:** Musk indicated Tesla is prioritizing speed of execution somewhat over maximum capital efficiency at this stage of growth, a shift from Tesla's historically strict capital discipline.
