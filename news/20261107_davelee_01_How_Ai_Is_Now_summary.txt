This video summarizes a report from the non-profit AI evaluation organization Meter, which spent 11 weeks embedded at Anthropic examining whether AI is now accelerating the pace of AI development itself. The report concludes Anthropic is likely progressing at roughly twice its prior rate, with much of that speedup attributed to AI tools being used in the research process — but the video has no substantive connection to Tesla or SpaceX and is included here only because it was submitted for review.

## Market & Competition
1. **Meter's acceleration finding:** Meter's report states that Anthropic's internal capability benchmark score, which rose about 15 points annually from early 2024 through fall 2025, began rising at about 29 points per year starting around October 2025 — nearly double the prior rate. Meter attributes roughly 80% of this speedup to AI itself, with a plausible range of 1.3x to 3x depending on scoring methodology choices.
2. **Anthropic researcher and leadership commentary:** Anthropic researchers reportedly told Meter they feel like they have "postdocs and graduate students" available and can run many parallel experiments on the cloud at near-zero cost; Anthropic staff self-reported being about 12x faster with new models, a figure Meter discounts given a prior study where programmers believed they were 20% faster with AI but were measured as 19% slower. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had estimated in February that AI was speeding internal work by 15-20%, growing over time; Meter's October findings show roughly double that.
3. **Transparency and disclosure limits:** Anthropic redacted much of the detailed data Meter found most useful, including forecasts and models of future capability and slowdown risk; Anthropic co-founder Sam McCandlish said fuller disclosure would give competitors a roadmap. A related Meter comparison found Anthropic's public safety report understated its internal "progress multiplier" (public figure under 2x versus a private figure near 1.97, which Meter treats as approximately 2x), and Meter says Anthropic may have crossed its own disclosure threshold as early as this past July; Anthropic says it will release further statistics on this in the coming weeks.
4. **Methodology caveats and competitive context:** Meter notes the acceleration figure could look very different (from modest to nearly 3x) depending on scoring assumptions, that comparing frontier models across companies on the same chart would reduce the apparent speedup by about 1.4x, and that Google has stated it sees no real acceleration in its newest model. Meter itself used Claude to help analyze the underlying data, and found no evidence this biased the results or that Anthropic tried to mislead investigators.
5. **Host's broader framing:** The host speculates that frontier AI labs' practice of keeping their most capable models in-house before public release could constitute a durable competitive advantage or even a form of "monopoly" on cutting-edge intelligence, raising antitrust questions he considers unresolved — but this commentary concerns the AI industry generally, not Tesla's or SpaceX's AI efforts specifically.
