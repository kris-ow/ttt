## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Texas robotaxi/Cybercab fleet reached roughly 1,080 units per RobotaxiTracker.com, cited live during one discussion, up from 1,051 with 81 added in a single day; Austin breakdown was ~554 Cybercabs and 507 Model Ys, meaning Cybercabs now outnumber Model Ys in the fleet. A second source cited 573 Cybercabs vs. 507 Model Ys with 110 added in one day — the two counts differ, underscoring the figures are directional, community-tracked estimates rather than precise.
- Tesla's self-driving fleet logged 438,000 miles in one week, exceeding Waymo's earlier weekly Austin average, though Waymo's national mileage remains roughly 7x Tesla's; one host says Tesla could pass Waymo's statewide Texas fleet total (just over 1,200 cars) as soon as next week.
- Cybercab production is reportedly running ahead of deployment needs: per Lars Moravy (relayed across both podcasts), Tesla is deliberately running then pausing the line because the fleet "can't use as many vehicles as they can make right now." Stated production specs: 78% automation, 25-step final assembly (5–10x fewer steps than Model Y), 50% less floor space, and 125,000 units/year capacity.
- Ashok Elluswamy told a Bank of America analyst on the Q3 earnings call that Tesla has "a clear path to scaling to millions of units," with vehicles "available in the next few months" — panelists characterized this as reflecting a plan in place rather than a near-term fleet-size claim.
- During Austin's F1 race weekend, a 19-mile trip cost $78 (Model Y Robotaxi, 4-min wait) or $76 (Cybercab, 40-min wait) versus $112–$172 on Uber under surge pricing — framed as a successful real-world test of Tesla's dynamic pricing.
- Tesla reportedly plans to begin selling Cybercabs to fleet owners by year-end and open the network to customer-owned cars in specific areas around Q2, per panel readings of earnings commentary; Dallas service is expected to launch in November, and Houston's geofence was recently expanded tenfold.
- A new independent tracking tool shows Tesla's self-reported mile counters are periodically adjusted rather than live: supervised-mile rate jumped from 244M miles/week (Oct 24) to 316M miles/week (Oct 26), while unsupervised Robotaxi miles were tracked at 557,000/week — just 0.023% of total FSD miles. Week-over-week Robotaxi mile growth has been accelerating (17.1% → 17.7% → 20.7%, per Tesla's own disclosed milestones), though one host's extrapolation to ~2.5M fleet vehicles and ~$230B annualized revenue a year out is explicitly flagged as unlikely to hold.
- Alphabet's "Other Bets" segment (including Waymo, Google Fiber, Wing) posted a ~$2B quarterly loss, up from ~$1.8B prior quarter and ~$1.4B a year ago; panelists assumed Waymo was the largest contributor but the figure isn't Waymo-specific.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V15 is reportedly consolidating start/end-of-trip navigation (parking, destination handling) into a unified mapping/autonomy stack over the next few months, per Ashok Elluswamy's earnings-call remarks — intended to fix weak "last mile" navigation.
- FSD safety data now shows 9–10x safer than the average human driver, up from a previously cited 7x, per one host.
- A social-media claim endorsed by Elon Musk pegs FSD's potential annual U.S. economic impact at over $1.5 trillion, based on $2T in estimated annual traffic-accident social costs and FSD's claimed 9x safety gain — this is the claim's own framing, not independently verified.
- EU FSD approval sits at 10 of 27 member states (13% of EU population), short of the 15 states/65% population threshold needed; Italy's Deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister voiced political support for approving Tesla's rebranded "Tesla Assisted Driving," though this is an endorsement, not a regulatory clearance. Best-case approval timeline cited is December–February, more realistic February–April next year.
- Only Mercedes-Benz, Nuro, and Waymo currently hold California driverless approval; Tesla has not yet applied, per the panel, though Musk reportedly told Chinese media robotaxi approval in California is expected mid-next-year — treated as speculative.

## Electric Vehicles
- A Tesla Semi caught fire on I-880 in Hayward, CA on October 27, closing both directions for an estimated 15–18 hours; no injuries involved. Vehicle version (pre-production vs. mass production) is unconfirmed by Tesla, and the panel called for root-cause disclosure given plans to manufacture up to 50,000 Semi units.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX secured $40 billion in financing — called the largest GPU financing deal ever — managed by Apollo Global Management with Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, and KKR as partners, to buy Nvidia chips.
- Anthropic raised its monthly SpaceX compute spend commitment from $1.2B to $3.1B, securing compute through 2029 starting around December 2026, with 90-day termination rights after an initial period ending early 2027.
- Projected SpaceX compute revenue mix by end of December (per one host's breakdown): Anthropic 37%, an undisclosed customer 13% (~$1.1B/month), Alphabet ~11% (~$1B/month), Reflection AI ~2%, and ~37% from still-unannounced customers.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink's constellation has grown to 11,161 active satellites across 423 launches since 2019, with 916 terabits of total downlink capacity, 5.2 terabits added this month alone, per one host's independent tracking tool.
- A single Starship launch carrying Starlink V3 satellites (only half-full) added 26 terabits of capacity in one shot — about 2.8% of total system capacity — which the hosts say exceeds the combined capacity of all other competing satellite internet providers.
- The FCC voted to advance new satellite-to-phone rules described as benefiting Starlink Mobile, despite lobbying against parts of the proposal by AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile; SpaceX separately accused T-Mobile and other carriers of pushing "outdated, restrictive" rules to undermine Starlink Mobile's expansion.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Elon Musk plans to give one SpaceX share to every child in America starting next year via 530A ("Trump") accounts, per a host's relay of Musk's post ("this will be the first of many"); Musk is currently locked up until around mid-2027, explaining the delayed timing.

A pre-IPO governance structure grants a Class F share controlled by an entity of seven co-founders 50.1% of voting power.
