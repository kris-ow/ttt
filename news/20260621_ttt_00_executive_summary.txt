## Autonomous Driving
- **Fatal crash in Katy, TX under Autopilot investigation.** Around 8 p.m. June 20, a Tesla failed to make a right turn, drove through a brick wall, and killed a 76-year-old woman inside her home. The 44-year-old driver told Harris County deputies the car was on Autopilot; investigators have not confirmed the claim and no charges have been filed.
- **Active NHTSA scrutiny compounds the incident's weight.** A separate NHTSA probe covering ~2.9 million Tesla vehicles for FSD running red lights and wrong-way driving was upgraded to an engineering analysis in March 2026 — the final step before a potential recall. Tesla also faces a parallel investigation for allegedly under-reporting Autopilot and FSD crash data.
- **FSD 14 end-of-June deadline met with skepticism.** Herbert Ong noted Tesla promised FSD version 14 "light" to owners by end of June 2026, adding: "none of us are holding our breath on that one." Bhakdi separately described version 14.3.4 as "the biggest leap so far" but it has not yet reached viewers in markets like Belgium, where one user reported multiple serious interventions over ~200 km on 14.2.26.
- **10,000-mile hands-free FSD streak set.** Tesla owner David Moss became the first to cross 10,000 miles on Tesla's FSD streak counter — tracking continuous miles without touching the steering wheel or pedals — equivalent to 40% of Earth's circumference, per Sawyer Merritt.
- **Cortex 1 continuously training FSD; Cortex 2 partially operational.** Per FutureAzA guest John, Cortex 1 in Austin runs FSD training around the clock, with marginal improvements becoming harder to isolate as the system matures. Cortex 2 is already partially operational and expected to serve primarily as Optimus training infrastructure.

## Robotaxi
- **Cybercab accumulating in multiple cities; market unrewarded until deployment.** Bhakdi confirmed Cybercabs are being spotted in Houston, Austin, Nevada, Florida, and other locations, suggesting serious production scaling. He is explicit that Wall Street will not reward Tesla for units in parking lots — the stock catalyst requires operational deployment. He still believes a June deployment is possible but acknowledged his earlier timeline has so far been wrong.
- **Bhakdi's deployment trigger: ~50 units could send Tesla to $490.** He stated even a small-scale confirmed rollout — "even to only 50 or something" — would be sufficient to catalyze a move to $490 from current levels near $400.
- **Dedicated service centers expected; Dallas real estate acquisition cited.** Bhakdi said existing Tesla service centers lack capacity for robotaxi maintenance volume and that Tesla's real estate acquisition in Dallas is likely evidence of purpose-built robotaxi service infrastructure being developed.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus Version 3 reveal targeted ~late July 2026; merger could push it to 2027.** Bhakdi noted Tesla indicated a Version 3 reveal roughly one month out (from June 21), but said if merger proceedings overlap with that window, Tesla may delay the reveal into 2027 to avoid complications with a shareholder vote.
- **Optimus supply chain: at least 60% China.** A viewer estimate Bhakdi agreed with — and suggested may even undercount — flagging this as a structural risk in the context of any merger with ITAR-governed SpaceX.
- **Cortex 2 framed as "Optimus University."** FutureAzA's John described the cluster as the primary training ground for Tesla's humanoid robot, with both FSD and Optimus training running simultaneously across the Cortex infrastructure.

## Energy
- **Tesla files "Megapod" trademark for distributed compute at Supercharger sites.** Bhakdi described the concept as micro data centers deployed across hundreds of existing Supercharger locations, leveraging existing electrical permits to bypass multi-year data center permitting delays. He argued the filing under Tesla rather than SpaceX signals a merger is imminent — otherwise it would logically be a SpaceX business. He estimates a few gigawatts of such compute could double or quadruple Tesla's current ~$3.5–4B annual free cash flow.

## Market & Competition
- **SpaceX–Tesla merger: Grok models highest probability window at late July–mid-August.** Both Herbert Ong and Bhakdi converged on the same timing thesis. Grok analysis cited by Ong assigned 40–50% probability to an announcement around Q2 earnings (~August 11), framing it as the strongest setup because fresh financials justify a premium. An earlier window (July 7–25) scored 35–45% probability; a post-unlock window in September–October scored 15–25%.
- **Bhakdi's proposed merger structure: $600/share for Tesla with a ratchet mechanism.** He argues Tesla shareholders will only vote yes if the offer includes a ratchet ensuring they receive proportionally more SpaceX shares if SpaceX's market cap exceeds the acquisition value by vote day. He will publish detailed merger math on June 22. A successful deal at $600 would represent ~50% upside from current levels.
- **SpaceX structurally over-owned near-term; Bhakdi tactically bearish.** With only ~5% of SpaceX shares currently in float, the August 11 unlock alone adds ~20% of float — a 400% increase in potential sellers. Bhakdi is selling naked calls at $210–$250 strikes and says he cannot see buying SpaceX before November 2026. He argues SpaceX would trade at ~$35 if priced on Tesla's revenue multiple.
- **ITAR is the single largest merger obstacle.** SpaceX's defense contractor status under ITAR bars certain foreign investors and conflicts with Tesla's deep Shanghai manufacturing presence (~one-third of production). Both Ong and regulatory logic suggest a full close likely slips to 2027 even if an announcement comes this summer.
- **Terafab: Tesla's domestic chip fab co-funded with SpaceX.** FutureAzA described Tesla's plan to build a full-stack chip fabrication facility in Texas — raw materials through packaging — in partnership with Intel's non-EUV, 1–2nm process approach. Samsung and TSMC will continue building Tesla chips in the interim. FutureAzA's John was skeptical of a sub-one-year operational timeline regardless of construction speed.
- **Rivian's FSD-level autonomy claim for end-2027 met with skepticism.** FutureAzA's John characterized Rivian's current driver assistance as equivalent to Tesla Autopilot from ~five years ago — highway-only, no traffic signals, no turns — and said he "seriously doubts" Rivian can close that gap in roughly 18 months given insufficient training data and no visible large-scale compute infrastructure.
