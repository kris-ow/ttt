Futurosa host Brian and drone photographer Joe Tegtmeyer discuss the rapid expansion of SpaceX's Bastrop, Texas manufacturing campus (near the original Boring Company/Starlink site, about 15 minutes from Giga Texas), arguing the combined build-out will exceed Giga Texas in total production footprint and is central to SpaceX's satellite, AI-in-orbit, and Starship strategy.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Massive Bastrop expansion dwarfs existing Starlink factory:** The site near Bastrop, Texas — originally a Boring Company location that later added a Starlink terminal (dish/modem) factory and the public "Boring Bodega" — is undergoing a large new construction project that Joe Tegtmeyer says will make the site's total production building potential larger than Giga Texas combined.
2. **Existing facility is a major circuit board/terminal manufacturer:** The already-built portion of the site manufactures Starlink dishes and modems; Tegtmeyer states it is one of, if not the largest, circuit board manufacturing plants in the country, work he notes was traditionally done in Japan or China. He separately notes Redmond, Washington remains the primary hub where the Starlink satellites themselves (he cites a figure of 15,000 built there) are produced.
3. **New facility built specifically for "Star Mind" AI satellites:** The new construction is intended to support a next-generation satellite line (referred to as "Star Mind"/"Starman" satellites) as well as continued Starlink and Starshield production. These satellites reportedly will use Nvidia GPUs and are intended for a sun-synchronous (polar) orbit with continuous sunlight, meaning the facility needs no dedicated battery plant — though Tegtmeyer notes newly released satellite specs suggest the satellites will carry a small amount of onboard battery, slightly increasing mass.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **New satellites are too large for Falcon 9, require Starship:** Tegtmeyer says the satellites being built at this facility are large and massive enough that they cannot fly on Falcon 9 and must launch via Starship, tying the facility's usefulness directly to Starship's success and to SpaceX's broader revenue plans.
2. **Shared "form factor" links satellites, Starship deployment, and ground-based AI data centers:** The new satellites use the V3 Starlink chassis in a flat, "pancake"-style form factor designed to fit Starship's payload dispenser (described as a "PEZ dispenser" that releases satellites in pairs through a small payload door). Tegtmeyer highlights that this same chassis/form factor is shared with the terrestrial AI data center racks (the orbital versions add solar panels and radiators), meaning SpaceX can use common manufacturing, parts, and dispensing systems across space and ground-based AI compute products — which he argues should benefit margins and manufacturing efficiency.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Orbital data center rationale tied to solar power access:** The hosts discuss SpaceX's stated interest in placing AI data centers in orbit specifically to use continuous solar power in the satellites' polar, sun-synchronous orbit, avoiding the need for battery storage.

## Market & Competition / Other
1. **Adjacent but separate Tesla solar facility:** A separate, newly filed Tesla project near Houston is for terrestrial solar panel manufacturing (ingots, wafers, solar cells) intended for residential/business solar capacity on Earth — explicitly distinct from the Bastrop satellite-focused facility.
2. **Road and infrastructure upgrades reflect growth:** The access road to the Bastrop site has already been widened from a narrow country road to roughly four lanes to handle increased truck and delivery traffic, with north and south access points; further widening may be needed as the satellite production facility nears completion.
3. **Applied Engineering Lab and X headquarters presence:** A University of Austin-Texas-affiliated "Applied Engineering Lab" near the site offers hands-on engineering experience with SpaceX/Boring Company; X's headquarters address is also located nearby, though its current active use is unclear following recent X/SpaceX/xAI organizational changes.
