This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert in a panel discussion with three Tesla bull investors — Alexandra Merz, Jeff Lutz, and Cern Basher — covering the three topics most discussed among Tesla investors that week: robotaxi expansion, the SpaceX IPO and its implications for Tesla, and competition in autonomous driving and humanoid robotics. The panel's core thesis is that a significant convergence of Tesla catalysts is building for the second half of 2026, potentially amplified by a Tesla-SpaceX merger that Alexandra Merz believes could be announced as early as end of June through mid-August 2026.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised Robotaxi Expanded to Greater Austin Area:** Tesla VP of AI Ashok Kaliswami officially announced that unsupervised robotaxi service is now available across the entire greater Austin metro area, no longer confined to a smaller geofenced zone. Jesse Richard was credited as the first to discover the expanded boundary by testing the service limits. The panel noted the exact number of vehicles operating in this expanded zone is unknown — potentially still a small handful — and that the RobotaxiTracker app appeared to be lagging more than at launch, possibly due to increased activity.

2. **Panel Mixed on Pace — "Baby Steps" vs. "Imminent Launch":** Cern Basher characterized the Austin expansion as a "baby step," noting that nearly a year into the service, expansion to other cities has been slow and wait times remain too long for an urban ride-hail product — with one observer suggesting waits under 20 minutes are still too long. However, Jeff Lutz argued the buildup of inventory, hiring, and infrastructure feels like a product launch in progress, comparing it to supply chain readiness before a factory launch, and said he believes deployment is "imminent." He argued Tesla may have underestimated the operational complexity of running a ride-share service from scratch.

3. **Texas Permit for Unsupervised Operation — A Regulatory Milestone:** The panel noted that on May 28, 2026, Texas granted Tesla a permit to operate unsupervised robotaxi vehicles, clearing what the panel described as one of three key requirements for full robotaxi deployment: (1) regulatory approval — now done in Texas; (2) hardware/software readiness — described as largely ready or getting there; and (3) operational infrastructure — cleaning, charging, on-time logistics — still being built out.

4. **Infrastructure Buildout Signals Scale:** The panel highlighted several infrastructure developments: three large facilities (described as approximately 36,000–151,000 square feet) for car wash and charging being built or approved in the Dallas/Austin area, and a new 51,000-square-foot delivery center in Grand Prairie, Texas (between Dallas and Fort Worth). Jeff Lutz warned that if Tesla builds out this OPEX-heavy infrastructure and then doesn't deploy high volume, it would be a serious financial drag — which he takes as evidence Tesla is planning a large deployment. Cern Basher noted there are currently 36 job listings for robotaxi-related roles.

5. **Edge Cases Still Being Resolved:** The panel discussed recent testing footage showing a Model Y repeatedly practicing recognition of a chain in a parking lot, and a separate incident where FSD failed to detect a lane-closure gate on a highway and struck it. Jeff Lutz and others agreed these edge cases must be resolved before wide rollout — and that resolving them takes iterative development cycles. Jeff noted Tesla has speed-profile "dials" it can adjust post-launch to reduce risk during edge conditions.

6. **Cybercab Production Units Spotted:** A video from content creator Tech Danny was shown featuring a production Cybercab in a new color called "Gleaming Gold," with self-opening McLaren-style doors, two seats, a large center console, no rear-view mirrors, no sunroof, and no rear window. The panel discussed the Cybercab's unconventional door mechanism as a potential manufacturing risk at scale, noting the concern about misalignment or sticking at higher volumes.

7. **Six-City Expansion Timeline Under Scrutiny:** The panel acknowledged Tesla had previously suggested expanding to six cities by end of Q2 2026. As of filming (Thursday, June 5, 2026), active supervised and unsupervised robotaxi service remains in Austin, Dallas, and Houston, with California still not operating as a true robotaxi service. Cern Basher expressed belief the other three cities could still be added by end of Q2 (approximately 21–26 days away at time of filming), and the panel broadly expects a more concerted ramp through summer 2026 (July–September).

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised Now Approved in 11 Countries:** Ashok Kaliswami presented a slide showing FSD supervised self-driving approved in: United States, Canada, Mexico, Netherlands, Estonia, Lithuania (and broader Europe listed), China, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, and Puerto Rico. Estonia was noted as having approved FSD supervised that same week. The panel noted some nuance — China's inclusion reflects FSD being "shipped to customers" in certain cities/regions, not a formal nationwide approval, and Ashok's language ("supervised self-driving shipped to customers in China") was parsed as technically accurate but potentially misleading.

2. **Pending Approval in 13+ Countries Covering 36% of World Population:** Kaliswami's slide showed FSD pending regulatory approval in Japan, India, Israel, Bangladesh, Thailand, Taiwan, Malaysia, Philippines, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Ethiopia, Colombia, Chile, and the rest of Europe. Nick Cruz Betain was credited for compiling the list. The panel noted 36% of the world's population lives in these pending-approval regions. Cern Basher flagged that Bangladesh and the Philippines did not appear to be highlighted on Ashok's actual map, and noted that Ethiopia was a surprise since Tesla currently only operates in Morocco within Africa. Belgium was noted as likely next in Europe, awaiting homologation data from Dutch authorities.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **202 Optimus Job Listings vs. 36 Robotaxi Listings:** The panel highlighted that Tesla currently has 202 job listings related to Optimus, compared to 36 for robotaxi roles, signaling the company's significant internal focus on humanoid robotics. An Optimus manufacturing facility is reportedly being built at Giga Texas, described as nearly as large as the main Giga Texas factory.

2. **Optimus Gen 3 Reveal Delayed, Now Tied Closely to Production Readiness:** The panel discussed that Optimus demo dates were pushed back multiple times — from December 2025, to January 2026, to March 2026 — and Elon Musk has now said the reveal will happen as close to production readiness as possible, rather than as a standalone demo event. Jeff Lutz predicted a Q3 2026 reveal; Cern Basher suggested the second half of September to first half of October; Alexandra Merz suggested it could come sooner if tied to a merger announcement. The panel interpreted the delay as Elon wanting a high-quality demo, and drew a comparison to Apple's approach of revealing products close to availability.

3. **Competition from Figure and OpenAI — Panel Unworried:** The panel addressed Figure's impressive live-streamed demo over 7+ days showing humanoid robots doing pick-and-place tasks at human-comparable speed, and OpenAI's announcement of entering robotics. Jeff Lutz argued there aren't enough companies building capable humanoid robots, and that the real filter — AI brain capability, right product specs, and mass manufacturing — will leave very few viable competitors. He cautioned that no company will produce a million useful bots in the near term, drawing a parallel to the 5-year smartphone supply chain ramp from one million to one billion units per year. The panel noted the supply chain for humanoid robots is more complex than smartphones, which benefited from a globalized assembly ecosystem.

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo Comparison Called "Bizarre" and "Ignorant":** Jeff Lutz pushed back strongly on CNBC comparisons between Tesla's robotaxi program and Waymo's approximately 3,700-car fleet, calling the framing inaccurate and almost purposefully misleading. He argued that when Tesla is at scale, the two services will be fundamentally different, and that the broader problem is there aren't enough autonomous ride-share companies — not too many. The panel agreed competition in this space is healthy and that Tesla does not fear it, historically publishing patents openly to encourage the ecosystem.

2. **Tesla Sales Strong Internationally — Q2 Expected Solid:** Jeff Lutz cited South Korea at 11,000 car sales in a single month as an example of organic demand growth, not just easy year-over-year comps. He attributed some of the demand tailwind to oil prices and the retreat of other automakers from EVs in Western Europe and the US. Global sales data (excluding US, where rapid data was unavailable) showed net growth of approximately 27–28% over the prior year. Q2 deliveries and production numbers are expected around July 3, 2026, with a full earnings report expected in the third or fourth week of July.

3. **Cybercab Available for Public Purchase by End of 2026 — Per Elon:** Jeff Lutz noted Elon Musk has stated Cybercabs will be made available for public sale by end of 2026, and discussed how customer-owned Cybercabs could help Tesla scale the robotaxi network faster by offloading charging, cleaning, and maintenance infrastructure burdens.

## Financials

1. **SpaceX-Tesla Merger Thesis — Alexandra Merz's Detailed Scenario:** Alexandra Merz laid out her theory that SpaceX will IPO on June 12, 2026, followed by a merger announcement with Tesla between end of June and mid-August 2026. Under her scenario, a Tesla shareholder vote would occur in November 2026, with the actual merger completing in Q2 2027. At the moment of merger consummation, the merged entity would replace the Tesla ticker in the S&P 500, triggering mandatory index buying — which she estimates could exceed $60–100 billion from S&P alone, with an additional $15–20 billion each from FTSE, CRSP, and NASDAQ 100 indices. She described this S&P inclusion event as a major financial motivation to complete the merger sooner rather than later.

2. **S&P Dow Jones Declines to Change Index Inclusion Rules:** Breaking news during the filming (June 5, 2026) confirmed that S&P Dow Jones officially announced it would not change its index inclusion rules to fast-track profitable-company requirements from 12 months to 6 months. This means SpaceX on its own (without a merger) cannot be included in the S&P 500 until approximately June 2027. Jeff Lutz predicted S&P would eventually be forced by market forces to change these rules, as competing indices (NASDAQ, Russell, CRSP/Vanguard) stand to absorb capital flows from SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, and other large companies excluded from S&P.

3. **Starlink Hits 12 Million Subscribers:** The panel noted Starlink announced 12 million subscribers, up from 10 million the prior month, as evidence of SpaceX's rapidly building profitability. Goldman Sachs (co-lead on the SpaceX IPO) projected SpaceX 2030 revenue of $322 billion; Mach 33 (Aaron Bernett and Larry Goldberg) was cited as projecting approximately $305 billion for 2030, with AI data center revenue not materializing significantly until 2031.
