This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers Tesla's new "Megapod" trademark filing and its implications for a distributed AI compute network tied to digital Optimus and the Macrohard project, alongside updates on Tesla's robotaxi operations in Texas and California regulatory developments. The video also touches on FSD safety data from Spain, an Autopilot-related crash controversy in Texas, and SpaceX's new financial disclosures and compute leasing deals. The core thesis is that Tesla's combination of AI 4-equipped vehicles, Supercharger power infrastructure, and Megapod hardware positions it for a highly capital-efficient AI inference business — though Loomis cautions that material impact may not arrive until late 2027 or 2028.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Spain FSD Public Road Testing Milestone:** Spain's National Road Authority reported that Tesla's supervised FSD has driven over 171,000 miles of public road testing without a single serious incident. Tesla's 30-car fleet has been running since November 2025 and is the most active tester on Spain's registry by a wide margin — the next largest figure reported by any other operator is the bus group Alsa at approximately 1,456 km (~900 miles). The only other company testing level-two passenger cars in Spain is Wave with just three vehicles; the rest of the list consists of autonomous buses and shuttles at SAE Level 4, typically operating one or two at a time. Loomis notes this dwarfs Belgium's public road testing aim of approximately 3,000 miles.

2. **Texas Crash / Autopilot Misreporting:** A Tesla in Texas crashed into a home at high speed, fatally injuring the occupant, with initial media reports blaming Autopilot. Loomis was confident the data would show otherwise, and Elon Musk publicly stated the crash made no sense for FSD given that FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets. A post by "A Shouk" (per the transcript) stated the driver manually overrode self-driving by pressing the accelerator to 100% of pedal travel, reaching 73 mph in a residential area, with the accelerator still pressed after the crash. Loomis criticized the media coverage and the driver for deflecting accountability.

3. **Tesla Semi Ground Truth Validation Spotted:** Community member Danny shared photos of a Tesla Semi equipped with ground truth validation equipment in Sunnyvale, California, alongside two Cybercabs spotted within the same five-minute window. Loomis interprets this as a positive sign but does not expect unsupervised FSD for the Tesla Semi to be in serious consideration until 2028 at the earliest.

4. **Grok Voice Integration in Tesla Vehicles — ~3 Months Out:** Elon Musk stated that the ability to converse with Grok inside a Tesla vehicle — for commands like directing drop-off locations or parking — will arrive in approximately 3 months (implying roughly September 2026). Loomis noted he will add this to his Elon tracker dashboard at lumis.com.

## Robotaxi

1. **California DMV Name Change — Administrative Only:** Tesla's name on California's DMV autonomous testing list was changed from "Tesla" to "Tesla RoboTaxi LLC." Loomis clarifies this is purely administrative — Tesla has not received any new autonomous vehicle permits in California. He acknowledges permits could come soon but cautions the name change alone is no guarantee.

2. **San Francisco Airport Limousine Permit — Not an AV Permit:** A permit shared by The Kilowatts showing a Tesla authorized limousine permit expiring end of January 2027, issued from San Francisco and SFO, was clarified by Loomis to cover only Tesla's existing supervised ride-hail service accessing the airport — not a new CPUC autonomous vehicle permit, which Tesla still needs.

3. **Austin Robotaxi — One Year of Operations, Safety Record:** Per the robotaxi dashboard, Tesla has now operated its robotaxi service in Austin for one year. Over that period there have been 18 incidents total, of which 8 were attributed to other parties, not Tesla. Tesla's last at-fault incident in Austin was in February 2026. Loomis calls the first year "very disappointing relative to expectations" and says it has recalibrated his outlook for Optimus timelines as well.

4. **Five-City Expansion — 7 Days Remaining, Looks Unlikely:** At the time of recording, Tesla had 7 days remaining to hit its stated target of launching robotaxi service in the next five cities. Loomis says this "feels very doubtful" and states he is now confident Tesla will not catch Waymo this year. He directly challenges the credibility of anyone still forecasting 10,000 robotaxis for Tesla in 2026.

5. **Houston Robotaxi Hub Activity and Expansion:** On-the-ground observer Sladoch counted 55 Cybercabs at the Houston robotaxi hub, up from 45 two days prior, with the lot full of Cybercabs and Model Ys. Robotaxi rides in Houston are now available as early as 7:00 a.m., with the same reportedly true in Dallas, though Loomis notes service hours have been fluid. Tesla also erected a black privacy screen along the fence at the main Houston robotaxi lot.

6. **New Tesla Construction Project Registered in Houston:** Tesla registered a new project with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation on June 16, 2026, with a start date of October 19, 2026 and a completion date of April 15, 2027. Estimated cost is $5.2 million; the work is categorized as renovation/alteration of a 38,900 sq ft building located just south of central Houston. Loomis notes this is a different location from the previously reported robotaxi cleaning/service/maintenance hub (~45 minutes away), and a separate location from Tesla's new Megapack factory near Houston (~1 hour away). The specific purpose (robotaxi or Megapack-related) is unconfirmed.

7. **Cybercabs at Giga Texas Test Track:** Joe Tegtmeyer shared video footage from Giga Texas showing approximately 10 Cybercabs on the new test track.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Factory Construction Progress at Giga Texas:** Joe Tegtmeyer shared images of the Optimus factory under construction at Giga Texas — the facility planned for an eventual 10 million unit per year capacity. Construction is fully underway and Loomis estimates the facility could be completed sometime toward the end of 2027.

2. **Digital Optimus and Macrohard — Capability Is Here, Deployment Timeline Uncertain:** Elon Musk has described "Macrohard" (also referred to as "digital Optimus") as a joint xAI and Tesla project. Grok serves as the reasoning layer ("system two"), while digital Optimus acts as the instinctive execution layer ("system one"), processing the past 5 seconds of real-time computer screen video and keyboard/mouse actions. Elon stated it works in all AI 4-equipped cars, meaning a car can do office work when not driving. Loomis says the software capability exists today but that material deployment may not be until late 2027 into 2028, given Tesla's current robotaxi focus.

## Energy

1. **Megapod Trademark Filing — Modular AI Compute Hardware at Superchargers:** Tesla filed a new trademark for "Megapod" as an intent-to-use filing. The filing describes modular data center hardware systems for AI computing, comprising computer servers, AI processing hardware, networking hardware, electrical power distribution units, and cooling systems — sold as a unit with downloadable software for monitoring, managing, optimizing, and regulating modular AI compute hardware. Loomis describes these as self-contained, portable data centers analogous in form factor to a Megapack. The filing's "sold as a unit" language raises the possibility of eventual third-party sales, though this is not confirmed.

2. **Supercharger Infrastructure as AI Power Backbone:** Elon Musk stated Tesla has approximately 7 gigawatts of available power at Supercharger locations. Loomis argues this gives Tesla a structural advantage — bypassing utility interconnection queues that can delay new compute capacity by years — and that deploying Megapods at Superchargers with existing solar and Megapack installations enables energy arbitrage. The distributed architecture is also presented as an advantage for latency, bandwidth, and privacy vs. centralized data centers.

3. **Marginal Cost Advantage for Tesla Inference Compute:** Loomis argues that because Tesla has already paid for AI 4 chips, cooling, and power infrastructure, the marginal hardware cost for inference compute via its vehicle fleet is effectively zero. The only incremental cost is electricity to power the chips. He frames this as a significant structural advantage in an AI landscape increasingly focused on cost-per-token efficiency.

## Financials

1. **SpaceX 8-K: $100.8 Billion in Cash and Equivalents:** SpaceX filed a new 8-K as of June 19, 2026, disclosing approximately $100.8 billion in cash and cash equivalents — a figure that includes IPO proceeds. SpaceX announced a new notes offering (amount unspecified) with proceeds intended to fully repay its outstanding bridge loan facility. Loomis frames this as refinancing short-term higher-rate debt (approximately $20 billion) into longer-term lower-rate debt, while retaining the full cash balance for capital deployment, with Terafab cited as a major anticipated use of funds.

2. **SpaceX Investment-Grade Credit Rating:** Fitch assigned SpaceX an investment-grade credit rating for its new bond offering, making all three major rating agencies now rating SpaceX as investment-grade. The post was reposted by "Eamon" per the transcript.

3. **SpaceX Compute Leasing Revenue Run Rate — ~$28 Billion Annually:** SpaceX signed a new compute agreement with Reflection AI: $150 million per month starting July 1, 2026, running through 2029, totaling approximately $6.3 billion if the full term is completed. Either party can exit with 90 days notice after the first 3 months. Combined with Anthropic ($1.2 billion/month) and Google (just under $1 billion/month), Loomis calculates a roughly $28 billion annual revenue run rate from these three deals alone. He notes deals have also been signed with Cursor.

4. **SpaceX Compute Economics — Per ARK Invest:** ARK Invest data cited by Loomis suggests SpaceX's cost to build roughly 1 gigawatt of compute is approximately $26 billion, while the Google compute leasing deal implies revenue of over $53 billion per gigawatt — representing approximately a 2x return on investment and over 100% return.

5. **Tesla and SpaceX Stock Prices:** Tesla closed at $405.05, up 1.14%. SpaceX closed at $154.60, down 16.43%, on a day when the Nasdaq was down 0.19%.

## Market & Competition

1. **Reflection AI — Open-Source Frontier Model Competitor:** Reflection AI, founded by two former Google DeepMind engineers, is focused on American-based open-source models and describes its mission as building "frontier open intelligence." Loomis positions Reflection as an alternative to closed-source frontier models and a competitor to open-source models from China.

2. **SpaceX Cursor Acquisition Accelerates Digital Optimus:** SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor (a coding AI tool) was cited by Elon Musk in the context of AI achieving "Stockfish-level coding and generalized computer use" — referencing Stockfish as the most advanced computer chess engine. Loomis says this acquisition will accelerate progress toward digital Optimus / generalized computer use, while noting SpaceX still has significant work to do on enterprise code.
