This video from Jo Bhakdi covers New York State's one-year moratorium on large (50+ MW) data center permits, President Trump's public criticism of the move, and the resulting selloff in semiconductor stocks (Micron, SanDisk, Intel, AMD) and SpaceX-adjacent sentiment. The host argues the ban is a near-term overreaction — data center demand remains strong and red states will absorb the buildout — but expects political friction to escalate materially by 2028-2029. Tesla content is minimal, limited to Cybercab deployment sightings and speculation about European FSD approval.

## Market & Competition
1. **New York bans large data center permits:** Governor Kathy Hochul signed an executive order pausing approval of data centers using 50+ megawatts for one year while a regulatory framework is developed; New York's state legislature separately passed broader related legislation not yet signed. Trump publicly criticized the move, saying companies would instead go to Alabama, Florida, Texas, and Arizona.
2. **State-by-state patchwork on data center legislation:** The host cites a list of state actions — moratorium/restriction bills failed in Maine (vetoed), Maryland, Minnesota, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, South Dakota, and Wisconsin, while New York's passed; the host argues no single state can halt the national buildout given competing red-state incentives.
3. **Semiconductor selloff tied to the news:** On the day covered, the host notes sharp drops in SanDisk (~15%), Micron (peak ~11%, intraday touching a level he describes as around $876 versus roughly $1,000 the prior day), Intel (~9%), and AMD (~7%), while Nvidia held up better; he attributes this partly to data-center policy uncertainty rather than demand concerns.
4. **Host turns bullish on Micron:** Citing a large market-cap decline (he cites a figure of roughly $94 billion in a single day) and what he estimates as a very low forward price-to-earnings multiple (he estimates under 8x on 2026/2027 earnings), the host says he has gone long Micron and sold cash-secured puts, viewing the selloff as overdone given ongoing supply constraints and demand growth.
5. **Data center buildout framed as supply-constrained, not demand-constrained:** The host cites a claim that data centers could consume roughly 12% of US electricity by 2028 (versus a stated current ~7%), and that developers face permitting, land, water, and grid infrastructure bottlenecks rather than lack of demand; he references a stat that the data center backlog would take roughly 11.8 years to clear at current buildout rates.

## Financials
1. **SpaceX stock under pressure, host trims position:** The host notes SpaceX/SPCX stock is down roughly 10% over the prior week including declines on the day covered; he says he sold short calls at a $180 strike and de-risked half his position due to elevated short interest and fear of a short squeeze. He frames SpaceX's real near-term catalyst as data center/compute revenue (Colossus 1, 2, 3) rather than Starlink, and speculates SpaceX could reach roughly $30–40 billion in EBIT next year (versus a stated current run rate of about $11 billion), which he argues would make a $1–2 trillion valuation reasonable at a ~100x P/E — though he stresses this is contingent on multiple unresolved factors and he is timing entry around November rather than targeting a specific price.

## Autonomous Driving

2. **Speculation on FSD approval in Germany:** The host says he believes Full Self-Driving approval in Germany will happen this year (his own expectation, not yet confirmed) and predicts it would meaningfully boost Tesla sales in Europe.
