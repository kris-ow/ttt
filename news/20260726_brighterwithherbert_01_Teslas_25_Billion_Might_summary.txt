This video features a discussion between host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg debating whether Tesla's ramped-up capital expenditure — including a new debt facility — is sufficient given the scale of its simultaneous buildouts (robotaxi fleet, Cybercab production, Optimus lines, the Terafab chip plant, lithium and cathode refineries, battery cells, solar manufacturing, and Cortex data centers). Goldberg argues Tesla is unusually capital-efficient and that its "just-in-time," speed-over-scale approach to spending is a deliberate strategy rather than underinvestment.

## Financials
1. **Capex guidance likely to be exceeded:** Tesla guided capex toward roughly $25 billion for the year, but Goldberg predicts the company will exceed this figure by year-end (reversing its historical pattern of coming in under guidance), potentially even seeing capex roughly double from prior levels, driven by Cybercab buildout, Cortex 2 completion, and the new Terafab chip plant.
2. **Q2 capex and cash position:** Tesla spent $6 billion in capex in Q2, with cash declining by only $1.2 billion as the operating business funded much of the investment; Tesla holds close to $40 billion in cash and is generating $3–4 billion per quarter, which Goldberg expects to rise to $5–6 billion per quarter next year.
3. **New $30 billion debt facility:** Tesla secured a new $30 billion borrowing facility, which Goldberg says was within his expectations; he predicts long-term borrowing could rise to roughly $50 billion within a year, particularly to fund the Terafab, absent a Tesla–SpaceX merger (which he expects would instead resolve into a larger, longer-term combined loan facility).
4. **Robotaxi revenue already appearing, not delayed:** Goldberg pushes back on the idea that robotaxi revenue is delayed, saying it is already showing up in Tesla's services line and will scale from hundreds of thousands of dollars currently to millions next quarter, then hundreds of millions and eventually billions within roughly a year; he argues each robotaxi vehicle (a $30–40k investment) is easily financeable given fast payback.
5. **Micron memory supply deal framed as competitive advantage:** Citing an excerpt from an article by Cern Basher, the host notes Elon Musk thanked Micron and Nvidia on the earnings call for securing Tesla a significant, reasonably priced memory allocation for the foreseeable future despite what Musk called insane current memory pricing — framed as a supply-chain risk other "Mag 7" companies may not have managed as well.
6. **Capital efficiency vs. speed philosophy:** Musk told his team he prioritizes speed over maximum capital efficiency; Goldberg interprets this as Tesla optimizing for the combination of speed and cost together, arguing that faster execution lowers effective cost even if per-unit spending is sometimes higher, and cites Tesla's manufacturing time-to-market advantage over Ford and GM's EV programs as evidence.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **xAI's data center cost efficiency vs. hyperscalers:** Goldberg argues xAI (referred to as SpaceX AI in the discussion) has been able to sell compute capacity at premium prices despite lower investment than rivals like Google, because building data centers faster lets it deploy cutting-edge chips roughly a year ahead of competitors using conventional subcontracted buildouts, and those chips are lasting far longer than expected (around six years versus a predicted two to three).
2. **Tesla's own chip use for inference lowers costs:** Goldberg says Tesla/Cortex uses its own AI4 chips (with AI5 and AI6 upcoming) for inference rather than third-party GPUs, which he estimates cost three to four times more than building proprietary silicon, contributing to Tesla's low-capex efficiency.
3. **Prediction of another data center compute announcement before SpaceX's earnings:** Goldberg speculates that another company (he guesses Microsoft) may announce a deal similar to prior xAI compute arrangements before SpaceX's upcoming earnings date, but explicitly frames this as his opinion, not confirmed information.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Cortex 2 buildout and FSD version 15:** Cortex 2 is reported at over 115 megawatts of production capacity, with compute roughly doubling over the recent quarter; Goldberg says Cortex 2's first half is currently being used for FSD (not Optimus), enabling rollout of a newer FSD version with a roughly 10x parameter increase, with a comparable magnitude of improvement expected from this version as was seen in the prior major FSD upgrade. The second half of the Cortex 2 building is still under construction and expected to roughly double total capacity to about a quarter-gigawatt by year-end.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Tesla's factory-first approach to Optimus:** The host argues Tesla appears behind in the humanoid robot race only because it hasn't shown flashy demos, while it is actually ahead in building manufacturing capacity — a roughly 1-million-unit-per-year line at Fremont and a next-generation, higher-capacity line planned at Giga Texas. Goldberg adds that Tesla is deliberately cautious given that robot usability involves far greater degrees of freedom than FSD, contrasting this with an unnamed competitor's robot project reportedly torn out by a client for not working.
