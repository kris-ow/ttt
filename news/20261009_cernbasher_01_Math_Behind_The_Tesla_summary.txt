This video analyzes the Tesla Semi's economics following its delivery event, modeling fuel-cost savings versus diesel trucks across a range of electricity and diesel price scenarios, and comparing the Semi's price and range to competing electric trucks from Peterbilt, Volvo, Freightliner, and Kenworth. The hosts conclude Tesla has priced the Semi attractively enough to deliver rapid payback periods while undercutting rivals on both price and range.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi fuel savings modeled across scenarios:** Using Tesla's own example (diesel at $7/gallon, 7 mpg ≈ $1/mile; electricity at 20 cents/kWh, 1.7 kWh/mile ≈ 34 cents/mile), the savings is about 65 cents per mile, or roughly $66,000/year at 100,000 miles. The host built a sensitivity table varying electricity cost (8–30 cents/kWh) and diesel price ($4–$9/gallon): at 30 cents/kWh the savings falls to about $49,000/year, while at 10 cents/kWh it rises to about $83,000/year; at $9/gallon diesel (cited as roughly today's California price) with 20-cent electricity, savings reach about $95,000/year. The host noted the diesel-vs-electricity cost comparison is also far less volatile for electric, reducing the need for fuel surcharges and easing cost planning for trucking companies.
2. **Estimated Semi pricing and payback period:** The host's best estimate (unconfirmed, not yet officially revealed by Tesla) puts the long-range Semi at around $290,000 and the standard-range version around $260,000, compared to diesel Class 8 sleeper trucks at $180,000–$240,000+ and diesel day cabs at $140,000–$180,000. Depending on which diesel comparison is used, the host estimates the price premium is recouped via fuel savings in under a year to a couple of years, with multiple additional years of savings over an assumed roughly million-mile truck lifespan.
3. **Projected aggregate savings from production scale:** The host calculated that if Tesla produces 50,000 Semis with average fuel savings of $66,000 each, that implies about $3 billion in annual fuel savings across that fleet — a hypothetical/illustrative figure rather than a company projection.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla Semi vs. competing electric trucks on price and range:** The host compared the long-range Tesla Semi (~$290,000, ~500-mile range) against the Peterbilt 579 EV (~$428,000, ~200-mile range), Volvo VNR Electric (~$436,000, ~275-mile range), Freightliner eCascadia (~$460,000, ~220-mile range), and Kenworth T680E (~$514,000, ~150-mile range), concluding Tesla significantly undercuts rivals on price while roughly doubling or more their range. The hosts expressed concern this could make it hard for competing electric truck makers to sell units, while noting it's unclear whether those trucks still beat diesel economics on their own terms.
