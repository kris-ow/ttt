This video from brighterwithherbert reviews a 37-item list of expected 2026 Tesla catalysts, reports that 21 are now complete (including the newly-confirmed Megapack 3 production start), and runs through the 16 remaining items — Roadster, Q3 earnings, Optimus production/presentation, robotaxi expansion in Las Vegas/Phoenix/existing cities, FSD regulatory approval in Europe/China/Israel, unsupervised FSD for personal cars, a purchasable Cybercab, a federal AV framework, Cortex 2, Digital Optimus, and a shareholder meeting — assigning rough probabilities to each ahead of a 12-week window the host calls pivotal.

## Robotaxi
1. **Las Vegas launch window closing fast:** Nevada granted Tesla a 12-month, up-to-5,000-vehicle robotaxi permit in Clark County on August 20, with Tesla telling the state it hoped to reach around 2,500 cars sometime next year; under Nevada rules Tesla must begin operating within 120 days of permit issuance, putting the deadline between November 24 and December 18 (per the host's reading of the rules, unconfirmed by Nevada). A visitor to the Las Vegas robotaxi lot on October 3 reported most Model Ys gone and roughly 30 Cybertrucks newly parked there. The host rates Nevada launch likely, given permits are secured and vehicles are staged.
2. **Phoenix less certain:** Tesla holds only a driver-operator taxi permit in Arizona and has filed an emergency response plan for Phoenix-area cities as a precursor to driverless mode; Phoenix also appears on a list of cities where Tesla is hiring robotaxi operators. However, when Elon Musk listed upcoming Cybertruck robotaxi markets to Chinese outlet CGTN on September 25 (Florida, Nevada, and others), he did not mention Arizona — leading the host to estimate Phoenix's odds slightly below 50/50.
3. **Fleet scaling and city-level data:** Active unsupervised Cybertruck robotaxi registrations in Texas reached 589 (169 of them Cybertrucks), up from 45 at the start of Cybertruck trips in September — a materially different, fresher figure than RobotaxiTracker.com's stale canonical counts, so attributed to the source cited. Paid Cybertruck rides remain Austin-only; a flyover source reported "a couple hundred" Cybertrucks around Houston, and a poster said Dallas Cybertrucks are now being driven without anyone inside. Austin operating hours were extended to 11 p.m. from 10 p.m., which Musk attributed to avoiding running over "gray kittens on gray asphalt in the dark."
4. **24/7 operation and broader scaling goal:** Tesla AI head Ashok Elluswamy said on September 4 that the technology enabling 24/7 robotaxi operation would arrive "in the next month or so," tied to the FSD version 15 rollout. Measured against Musk's April goal of robotaxis in "a dozen or so states" by year-end, the host calls a narrower win (a new city, extended hours, or 24/7 service) a 50/50-or-better bet, but calls the literal "dozen states" goal highly unlikely with only two states currently unsupervised.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Germany shifts position on FSD:** Germany agreed Tesla's FSD can exceed the speed limit by a maximum of 10% (down from a prior 50% ask) and suggested branding the product "Tesla assisted driving" domestically since Germany considers the FSD name misleading. German Transport Minister Stefan Bilger said he would seek EU-wide approval quickly and that Germany would consider unilateral action if the EU path fails; Musk responded "Danke schön."
2. **EU-wide vote still pending:** Slovakia's transport minister reportedly expects approval within days, which would make it the ninth EU country to approve FSD. The EU regulators' committee met October 6 but only discussed the topic for about 25 minutes without voting, with the next meeting in December. EU-wide approval requires 15 countries representing 65% of the population; the eight already-approved countries represent about 12.5%, rising to roughly 31% with Germany added — still short of the threshold, with France and Sweden voicing concerns. The host puts odds of an eventual EU-wide approval above 50%, with December plausible, followed by required European Commission sign-off.
3. **China approval stalled, Israel uncertain:** Tesla's CFO said in April the company hoped for Chinese approval by Q3, which has now passed without approval; China's new driver-assistance rules take effect January 1 for new approvals, leading the host to rate 2026 China approval odds as very low. Israel approved driverless testing in February and its transport minister hinted in March that client approval was coming, but none has materialized, and Israeli elections on October 27 leave the ministry operating on a temporary basis.
4. **German demand signal despite no FSD approval:** Tesla registrations in Germany rose 268% in September even though FSD is not yet legalized there, which the host frames as evidence of demand upside once approval lands.
5. **Unsupervised FSD for personal cars unlikely this year:** Musk said in April he expected unsupervised FSD for owners "probably" in Q4, contingent on FSD version 15 (AI4-only hardware), which he separately said would ship by year-end or early next year. Customer cars currently run version 14.3; robotaxis use early v15 builds. The host estimates only a 10–15% chance of any form of unsupervised FSD reaching owner cars by year-end.
6. **Federal AV framework unlikely near-term:** NHTSA announced September 17 it is developing a federal safety standard based on driving performance rather than specific features, proposing changes to five safety regulations. Progress is stuck in Congress — a House bill has not moved since clearing a subcommittee in February — with midterm elections November 3 and transportation funding expiring December 11 adding pressure. Tesla VP Lars Moravy said in July there is nothing at the federal level currently blocking Cybercab's launch.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus 3 production reportedly ramping:** The Information reported (unconfirmed by Tesla) that Fremont Optimus production rose from a few dozen robots per week in Q2 to several hundred per week in August, with managers targeting over 1,000 robots per week by year-end; hand/forearm assembly (over a hundred small parts each, currently manual) is described as the bottleneck. Musk said on October 1 that Tesla halved RAM on the Tesla AI5 chip specifically to ensure sufficient volume for Optimus production at lower cost. The host considers pilot production likely, expecting confirmation via the factories table in Tesla's Q3 shareholder report.
2. **Optimus 3 presentation deliberately delayed:** Musk said in April that Tesla is delaying the Optimus 3 reveal until closer to production because competitors are "doing frame-by-frame analysis and copying everything we do" (his characterization). J.P. Morgan reportedly met Tesla in Fremont in August and wrote that the Gen 3 reveal would be timed to coincide with start of production; the same J.P. Morgan note reportedly said commercial sales could begin as early as the second half of 2027. The host sees hints (app renders, a Halloween teaser, a new touch-sensor patent) but calls the presentation timing essentially unpredictable and not expected to be part of Thursday's robotaxi-adjacent event.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Roadster event postponed again, now October 15:** Tesla postponed the Roadster demo from October 1 to October 15 citing high winds, marking a sixth postponement within the year. The Information reportedly described the planned demo as a crewless, remotely controlled cold-gas-engine launch near SpaceX's McGregor test site (unconfirmed by Tesla); FAA flight restrictions over McGregor are cited as a supporting clue. Lars Moravy stated production would begin 12–18 months after the presentation (no earlier than 2027), and the only official specs remain from 2017; reservation deposits were recently reopened at $5,000 plus $45,000 by bank transfer with no price or production date specified.

## Financials
1. **Q3 earnings call set for October 21:** Tesla's Q3 report is scheduled for October 21 at 2:30 p.m. Central Time, broadcast on X; deliveries reportedly already exceeded Wall Street expectations by about 24,500 cars. The host expects several list items (Optimus production status, robotaxi city plans, updated mileage figures, possibly a shareholder meeting date) to be addressed on this call.
2. **Energy storage volume below expectations:** Q3 energy storage deployment was 13.7 gigawatt-hours — the second-best quarterly result on record but about 14% below analysts' expectations.
3. **Shareholder meeting date still unset:** As of this week, Tesla has not filed a proxy statement or set a 2026 annual meeting date; an April filing said the board had not yet set one. Last year's meeting was held on November 6, implying a 13-month bylaw deadline around December 6 this year, and roughly two months are typically needed between filing and meeting. Separately, the SEC approved on September 29 a Tesla program letting small shareholders set a standing vote preference, and Tesla arranged a $30 billion credit line it says it does not plan to use this year; the host notes online speculation linking the voting tool to an unannounced vote but says nothing is confirmed. The host puts meeting-date-announcement odds at 50/50 and the meeting actually occurring this year as less likely still.
4. **Cortex 2 capacity ambiguous:** Tesla's Cortex 2 training supercomputer was said in July to be operating above 115 megawatts with capacity to ramp through the rest of the year, against an earlier Musk comment (December) targeting roughly half a gigawatt by mid-next-year; the host calls reaching "full power" this year roughly 50/50, leaning no.
