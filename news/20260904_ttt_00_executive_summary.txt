## Brief

- Cybercab opens to the general public in Austin
- NHTSA opens audit into Cybercab's brake-pedal self-certification
- Tesla claims 1 million unsupervised Robotaxi miles, up from 380K
- Tesla courts fleet buyers, leaves individual FSD owners without payout
- New rare-earth-free motor debuts in Cybercab
- FSD v14.3.9 adds steer-to-avoid collision feature

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab launched publicly across Austin's full geofence, with no steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors; riders describe a smooth, quiet ride and Grok-powered cabin controls, per multiple ride-along accounts. Tesla staff said over 80% of rides are two-passenger trips and cited a sub-0.2 drag coefficient as central to the design.
- Tesla disclosed 1 million unsupervised Robotaxi miles at the launch event, per VP Ashok Elluswamy — up from 380,000 at the July 22 Q2 call, a roughly 620,000-mile jump in six weeks; Electrek cautions this partly reflects Tesla only recently starting to log unsupervised miles rather than pure fleet growth. Waymo, by contrast, has logged 200 million total miles across 14 cities.
- NHTSA opened audit AQ26002 on September 3, covering ~1,000 Cybercabs, examining how Tesla certified a vehicle lacking human-usable brakes, pedals, and mirrors under FMVSS. One commentator (Bhakdi) argues the audit is likely to find Tesla's certification invalid given brake-pedal requirements, drawing a parallel to Waymo's 2022–2024 audit that ended in a 2,500-vehicle exemption cap; he flags a pending NHTSA rule change (comment period closed Aug. 26) as Tesla's likeliest mitigating path.
- Only ~45 Cybercabs are currently registered for use in Texas, roughly one-tenth the number of Model Y robotaxis registered in Austin, per Electrek.
- Tesla launched a "Robotaxi interest form" and a separate website section soliciting fleet buyers and infrastructure partners for bulk Cybercab purchases — a shift some commentators read as Tesla moving toward third-party fleet ownership rather than an entirely company-owned model; pricing speculation (unconfirmed) ranged $30,000–$50,000 per unit.
- Tesla dropped its earlier plan to lease a San Francisco parking garage for a robo-charging hub, per Electrek, with no update since the plan was scrapped same-day as a scheduled planning meeting.
- Tesla published rider-guide and compliance details: 20.2 cu ft trunk capacity, 220 lb trunk weight limit, 16.5" step-in height, service-animal and wheelchair-space accommodations, and a minimum rider age of 13 for Cybercab (vs. 8 for Model Y robotaxi).

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD Supervised v14.3.9 is rolling out with a new active safety feature: FSD can now take steering control during manual driving to avoid a collision when automatic braking alone isn't sufficient, and may also intervene if it detects a heavily distracted driver or an accidental FSD disengagement. Tesla hasn't said whether the feature can be disabled.
- The robotaxi/Cybercab fleet is reportedly running early builds of FSD v15, separate from the consumer v14.3 branch, per in-car Grok responses relayed by a rider — unconfirmed by Tesla.
- Electrek reports FSD owners who paid up to $15,000 still cannot monetize their purchase via the robotaxi network, and some have not received promised hardware upgrades, even as Tesla courts fleet buyers.

## Humanoid Bots
- Elon Musk said he'd bet serious money on at least a billion humanoid robots within 10 years, each roughly five times as productive as a human — output exceeding all of humanity combined — calling it a conservative estimate.
- Jason Calacanis said Musk showed him a new, non-public Optimus video, quoting Musk confirming "this is real" and not CGI; Calacanis separately speculated (his own view) that Optimus could win a majority of humanoid-robot Olympic-style events versus competitors shown at Beijing's robot games.
- Filings reviewed by Joe Tegtmeyer suggest the Giga Texas Optimus factory will be just under 7 million sq ft; the structure looks largely complete but may be only about half of final steel, with ~6 months of construction remaining. Optimus production is expected to start in Fremont late this year or early next, with Texas ramping by end of next year.

## Market & Competition
- A joint Tesla-SpaceX "Advanced Technology Chip Fab" foundation (~490,000 sq ft) is under construction at Giga Texas, described as a pilot/R&D line that one commentator argues will house Tesla's most advanced chip technology, ahead even of the larger planned Terafab.
- Terafab site clearing in Grimes County, Texas is paused by a countywide burn ban until fall conditions ease.
- Rare-earth-free permanent-magnet motor debuted in Cybercab (18% smaller, 25% lighter, more efficient per Tesla), which Musk said avoids rare earth metals; Electrek frames it as a real but incremental achievement given EV motors are already 90%+ efficient, motivated by China's rare-earth export controls.
- Refreshed Model Y (MY2026) earned an IIHS Top Safety Pick+ award.

## Financials
- Debate continues (futureaza) over a possible Tesla-SpaceX merger: one commentator values Tesla near $1 trillion and SpaceX near $2 trillion, arguing SpaceX is "more proven" via Starship and compute-rental revenue while Tesla hasn't yet monetized robotaxi/Optimus at scale; the other counters Tesla is already profitable while SpaceX's AI-compute revenue durability is unproven. No consensus reached; one participant frames merger urgency as being about Musk consolidating control rather than synergy.

## SpaceX — Starship

- SpaceX secured 444 additional acres near the Port of Brownsville plus a reported $220 million water infrastructure deal; intended use is unconfirmed, with speculation ranging from vertical integration to propellant infrastructure.
