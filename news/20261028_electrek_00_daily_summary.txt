Electrek's Tesla coverage today spans a Semi fire incident, Ford's gigacasting catch-up to Tesla, a confirmed Vietnam market entry, and criticism of Tesla's Houston Robotaxi service map.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi catches fire with no crash, shuts I-880 for 10 hours:** A Tesla Semi caught fire on I-880 in Hayward, California Tuesday evening with no crash involved, per Fred Lambert; the driver was unhurt but CHP closed the freeway, initially warning the battery could burn 15-18 hours, though it was fully reopened by about 3:30 a.m. Wednesday. The cause is under investigation; Tesla hasn't commented. Lambert notes this is the second Semi fire to shut a California interstate in about two years (after an August 2024 NTSB-investigated crash-related fire), and argues that with Tesla's Semi fleet still small (PepsiCo had about 86 trucks in 2024) and volume production just started, a no-crash fire is "a big data point" that Tesla should investigate and publish findings on, especially as WattEV (370 trucks) and a 2,500-truck ZET SCALE order loom. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/tesla-semi-fire-i-880-hayward-no-crash/)

2. **Tesla confirms Vietnam entry to challenge VinFast's dominant share:** Tesla has begun hiring in Vietnam for five roles including sales manager, service manager, and a charging project developer, with a Tesla recruiter posting that the company is "officially coming" to the country, per Fred Lambert. Tesla quietly registered Tesla Motors Vietnam LLC in September with about $3 million in charter capital. VinFast holds roughly 36% of Vietnam's new car market year-to-date (177,697 units), more than its next five competitors combined, and builds locally while Tesla would need to import, likely from Giga Shanghai, facing high tariffs; Vietnam's EV registration fee exemption through 2030 helps imports too. Lambert notes Tesla has followed this same hiring-then-launch playbook in Thailand and the Philippines before. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/tesla-vietnam-entry-vinfast/)

## Robotaxi
3. **Tesla's rocket-shaped Houston Robotaxi map includes a "fake" dead zone downtown:** Ethan McKanna, creator of Robotaxi Tracker and a former Tesla employee, rode Tesla's newly expanded Houston Robotaxi service area (shaped like a rocket after Monday's expansion) and found the app refuses pickups in parts of downtown inside the published polygon, calling it "by far the most egregious 'fake' AV service area" he's seen, per Fred Lambert. One trip quote showed roughly 40 minutes to travel two blocks, with McKanna suspecting the routing avoids downtown railroad crossings; he said Tesla likely drew the rocket shape first and that service otherwise "feels really good" with better driving than recent Waymo rides. Separately, Robotaxi Tracker found Waymo's Orlando service area shrank 8.4% to 109 square miles after an airport drop-off option wasn't yet live in the app. Lambert's take: Tesla "drew a rocket first and figured out where the cars could actually go second," calling the faked map "completely stupid." [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/tesla-robotaxi-houston-rocket-service-area-fake/)

## Market & Competition
4. **Ford casts 5,000 "unicastings" for Fathom EV truck, following Tesla's gigacasting approach:** Ford has produced over 5,000 large aluminum unicastings for its upcoming $28,350 Fathom midsize electric pickup, each truck using two castings (front and rear) to replace 146 individual parts, per a Ford blog post cited by Fred Lambert. The castings are made at Ford's Shelbyville Technical Center and shipped to Louisville Assembly Plant, with Ford also working on "modular repairability" to address crash-repair cost concerns that have made some automakers (Lucid) avoid gigacasting. Lambert notes Tesla pioneered this with its 2020 Giga Press (70 parts replaced on Model Y) but has since scaled back its own casting ambitions, including reportedly dropping the front casting on some Model Y production; he argues Ford's front-and-rear approach is "catching up" to Tesla rather than leapfrogging it, while competitors like Volvo, Xiaomi, Xpeng, Honda, and Toyota pursue larger presses and others (Stellantis, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Hyundai) hold back. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/ford-fathom-5000-unicastings-tesla-giga-press/)
