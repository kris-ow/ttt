This video features FutureAzA's host visiting Platt Auto, a used car dealership with a significant Tesla inventory, to test drive and review a 2025 Jeep Wagoneer S from the perspective of Tesla owners and EV enthusiasts. The core thesis is that the Wagoneer S, available used at roughly half its new price (~$40,000), offers a compelling premium EV option for buyers who prioritize interior quality, ride comfort, ground clearance, and light off-road capability — though it has notable calibration and software shortcomings compared to Tesla. The video is casual and conversational, with dealership rep Jack providing hands-on context.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Jeep Wagoneer S specs and pricing:** The 2025 Jeep Wagoneer S on the lot is priced at approximately $40,000 used — described as "almost half off" its new price. It features a ~100 kWh battery pack, stated range of just over 300 miles, all-wheel drive, and NMC (not LFP) battery chemistry. Both the vehicle and batteries are described as made in the US. The car has approximately 4,000 miles on it, making it a very low-mileage used example.

2. **Performance and handling characteristics:** The Wagoneer S is described as "rocket ship fast" in a straight line, with the front end lifting noticeably under hard acceleration — a "boat-like" feel the hosts find acceptable given the vehicle's weight (just under 6,000 lbs curb weight). Sport mode automatically disables traction control, which both hosts criticize as a poor design decision, as the front wheels visibly spin out.

3. **Regenerative braking calibration issues:** The host's primary complaint is the throttle/regen pedal calibration in high-regen mode, describing the transition from acceleration to regeneration as an abrupt on/off switch with no granularity or smoothness. He notes that Nissan has handled one-pedal driving more gracefully for over a decade, and that he resorts to the lower regen mode to achieve acceptable drivability.

4. **Interior quality and features:** Both hosts praise the interior, highlighting plush, well-bolstered ventilated seats, high-quality leather feel, thoughtful dash patterns, a square-ish steering wheel that improves dashboard visibility, and high-definition surround-view cameras. The interior is described as more premium-feeling than comparable Teslas, which is cited as the primary reason cross-shoppers are seriously considering it.

5. **Practicality and off-road capability:** The Wagoneer S has approximately 8-point-something inches of ground clearance, compared to the Model Y's ~6.5 inches. The hosts consider it well-suited for light off-roading such as forest service roads and trail heads, with the 300-mile range sufficient for regional trips (e.g., Portland to Mount Hood at 80 miles). It seats five with a flat rear floor, generous legroom, and a large trunk — but is deemed too small for a practical third row.

6. **Exterior pedestrian sound alert:** At low speeds (switching on around 26 mph), the Wagoneer S emits a loud exterior pedestrian alert sound, similar to Chevy/Blazer EV behavior, which the host finds so intrusive it creates an urge to accelerate past the threshold just to silence it.

## Market & Competition
1. **Cross-shopping dynamics at the dealership:** Customers showing interest in the Wagoneer S are primarily cross-shopping against top-trim Model Y's and Chevy Blazer EVs — vehicles of similar size in the large two-row SUV segment. The Wagoneer S attracts buyers seeking a more premium interior experience than either of those alternatives.

2. **Competitive comparison to other EVs:** The hosts compare the Wagoneer S to the Hyundai Ioniq 5 (similar range, slightly better charging, but much lower ground clearance making it less capable on rough terrain) and loosely to Mercedes GLE/GLS EVs and BMW X5/X7 in terms of pricing tier and positioning. The Wagoneer S is described as sitting at the top of the two-row SUV size class, just below vehicles large enough to offer a meaningful third row.

3. **Tesla Autopilot removal noted:** The hosts briefly discuss that Tesla has removed Autopilot from new vehicles entirely (not just the base trim), attributing the decision to Tesla not wanting to maintain a separate software stack alongside FSD. This leaves new Tesla buyers with either basic cruise control or full FSD — no intermediate Autopilot tier.

4. **Platt Auto's Tesla inventory:** The dealership carries a broad Tesla lineup including a 2020 Model S, a 2018 Model X, Highland and older Model 3s, and multiple Model Y's — described as "the whole sexy arrangement," missing only a Roadster and Cybertruck.
