This video, hosted by Herbert with guest Brian White (of the FutureAzA channel), is a deep dive into newly discovered Cybercab features and specs, arguing that Cybercab's core advantage is a radically lower cost per mile — enabled by rare-earth-free motors, a streamlined "unboxed" production process, and purpose-built design — rather than price alone.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab DMV registrations climbing:** The number of Cybercabs registered with the Texas DMV reached 49, up from 45 a day earlier, according to the hosts.
2. **Cybercab pricing vs. Model Y and rivals:** Within Tesla's robotaxi app, a sample Cybercab ride was quoted at $10 (4-minute arrival) versus $17 for the same trip in a Model Y — roughly 40% cheaper — and the $17 Model Y fare was said to undercut Waymo, Uber, and Lyft on that route. A third party cited comparative per-mile costs (New York taxi ~$3.50/mile, Waymo ~$3.16/mile, Uber/Lyft ~$2.80/mile, cost of owning a car ~77 cents/mile); Brian White cautioned he's skeptical Tesla operating cost can fall below 77 cents/mile but said it doesn't need to — only to undercut car ownership cost to reshape transportation, which he thinks starts happening meaningfully around $1.50/mile and accelerates below $1.
3. **Unboxed production slashes line size and cycle time:** Tesla says the "unboxed" manufacturing process assembles modules in parallel and frames the car in one step, halving production line size and enabling production over five times faster than conventional auto manufacturing — under 10 seconds cycle time (long-term goal of 5 seconds) versus 34 seconds for Model Y. White noted this required brake-by-wire, which he says he identified from the wiring harness at Tesla's shareholder meeting in November.
4. **Gene Munster's fleet growth guess:** Analyst Gene Munster reportedly said he guesses Tesla adds roughly 300 Cybercabs to Austin over the next month; White called this "optimistic but not crazy" and said he wouldn't be surprised either way given Tesla's apparent preference for a slow, low-scrutiny rollout.
5. **Flexible ownership model (host's speculation):** Herbert speculated (his own assumption, not confirmed by Tesla) that future Cybercab access could work as a "slider" between full personal ownership and full ride-hail availability, letting owners flex between private use and fleet earnings month to month.
6. **Subsidized 40% discount vs. Model Y validated informally:** Both hosts treated the ~40% cheaper Cybercab-vs-Model Y ride pricing as an early but real signal of Tesla's cost advantage playing out in the live Austin robotaxi app.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Grok voice control reduces screen dependence:** Cybercab has deep Grok integration allowing natural-language control of climate, YouTube, music, and destination changes (which also push a notification to the rider's phone) without touching the screen; Herbert noted this mirrors a similar voice-navigation change feature he has used via his own vehicle's assistant.
2. **Manual joystick driving mode discovered:** A rider found an on-screen virtual joystick allowing low-speed manual driving (reported by the rider as roughly 8 mph or less), which White interprets as intended for precise positioning (e.g., showroom placement, service bay maneuvering) or as a manual override if cameras were blocked or damaged; the feature appeared in what White described as an engineering validation configuration.
3. **Adaptive door-opening via camera obstruction detection:** Cybercab's automatic doors only open as far as needed and check cameras for obstructions before opening, stopping short if something blocks the path.
4. **EU regulatory approval optimism:** Elon Musk replied to a German rider that Cybercab-style service is "hopefully soon" coming to Europe; the hosts noted an EU vote is scheduled for October 6 and referenced Musk reportedly discussing FSD supervised approval with a French government official (unconfirmed by the hosts). White argued that since Waymo is pursuing EU permits without a steering wheel, driverless approval logically follows FSD approval already granted for Model Y-type vehicles.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybercab specs and efficiency:** Cybercab is Tesla's narrowest vehicle at 69 inches wide with the most legroom of any Tesla, over 6.1 miles per kilowatt-hour efficiency, curb weight around 3,000 lbs, front-wheel drive, and a 22-inch touchscreen (the largest in any Tesla) — capped at that size, per Tesla engineer Eric Early, to preserve space for airbag deployment. White noted he was originally told a lower efficiency figure (5.5 miles/kWh) by an engineer early on and says the real figure came in higher.
2. **Rare-earth-free motors as a strategic advantage:** White called Cybercab's use of motors without rare-earth magnets the "headline" feature, framing it as a supply-chain independence advantage given China's dominance in rare-earth mining, refining, and magnet production — a risk he says no other automaker has solved.
3. **Design details for durability and accessibility:** White said Cybercab is engineered for a much longer service life than typical consumer cars (designed to run in the hundreds of thousands of miles rather than ~150-200k) via easily replaceable wear parts. Reviewers also found the ride height matches standard wheelchair height for easy transfers (one paraplegic rider rated ingress/egress 10/10), doors close automatically when the seatbelt is fastened, and the classic Autopilot engagement chime was retained.
4. **Starlink hardware now built into production units:** Cybercabs are reportedly coming off the production line with Starlink connectivity hardware installed in the hatch (discovered by a source identified as Joe Tegtmeyer), suggesting the feature has moved from testing into standard production tooling; Elon Musk replied to the discovery saying he'd missed announcing it. Tesla's own messaging still describes "V5 Starlink" as "soon to be equipped," which the hosts noted is already behind what's being observed in production.
