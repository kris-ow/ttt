This video features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi — economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur — discussing several Tesla-related stories: the unveiling of Ferrari's first all-electric car (the Ferrari Luche), Tesla's Cybertruck sales struggles, the narrowing price gap between the Model 3 and Toyota Corolla hybrid, and a live coast-to-coast FSD demonstration across Canada. The overarching thesis is that legacy automakers are structurally disadvantaged against Tesla, and that the Cybercab ramp is the most reliable signal of imminent large-scale robotaxi deployment.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Ferrari Luche: A Brand Mismatch at $640,000:** Ferrari officially unveiled the Luche, its first all-electric car, priced at $640,000 and co-designed by Apple's former head of design, Jony Ive, with US deliveries targeted for Q2 2027. Jo Bhakdi argues the car is not necessarily bad design in isolation — it might work as a Hyundai or an Apple product — but it is a fundamental mismatch with the Ferrari brand identity. He contrasts it unfavorably with the Tesla Model S Plaid at approximately $95,000, which he notes is roughly one-sixth the price yet faster with more range, and with the Model 3 Performance at roughly $60,000 — less than one-tenth the price — which also bests the Luche on key performance metrics. The host speculates that a Tesla Roadster could be available by Q2 2027, which would make the Luche even less competitive at launch.

2. **Cybertruck Sales Slump:** Tesla sold only 4,186 Cybertrucks in Q1 2026, down from approximately 7,000 in Q1 2025. Jo Bhakdi frames the Cybertruck as a symptom of Tesla's tendency to fall in love with engineering novelties — comparing it to the Model X's Falcon Wing doors — and argues it was fundamentally mispriced: originally promised at $39,000, it launched at approximately $95,000 due to manufacturing difficulties, which he says is the core reason for weak sales. Overall Tesla vehicle registrations in Q1 were cited as approximately 105,000, down from approximately 130,000 in Q1 of the prior year.

3. **Model 3 vs. Toyota Corolla Hybrid — Near Price Parity on Leasing:** The Tesla Model 3 rear-wheel drive is now available to lease for only $30 more per month than a Toyota Corolla Hybrid, under the same term and same money down, despite carrying a roughly $10,000 higher sticker price. Jo Bhakdi says he predicted approximately 18 months ago that 2027 would be the "year of reckoning" for OEMs. He argues Tesla's ability to layer FSD revenue on top of car sales gives it the flexibility to reduce lease pricing even further, potentially using competitive intelligence to strategically undercut rivals at critical moments to accelerate their decline.

4. **Jo Bhakdi's Case for Product Concentration:** Jo Bhakdi argues strongly that Tesla's competitive advantage comes from concentrating production on very few models — citing the analogy of commodity TVs dropping to $199 through massive scale. He argues that the Cybercab, designed as the world's highest-production vehicle, could reach a cost of goods sold (COGS) of approximately $10,000 in the long run through economies of scale, and that producing variations (four-seater, six-seater, van, etc.) would undermine that advantage. He contrasts this with BYD, which he notes has 43 trims, calling it a cautionary example of excessive model proliferation.

## Market & Competition

1. **Legacy OEM Structural Decline:** Jo Bhakdi describes the auto industry as having extreme operating leverage in reverse — a 5% sales decline can be "close to death" given the fixed cost base of giant capex and manufacturing sites. He singles out GM and Volkswagen as being in serious trouble, calls Ford "not too bad," and says Toyota may be the best-positioned legacy OEM for survival. He argues that Chinese automakers (primarily BYD) remain blocked from the US and European markets, meaning the market share shed by failing OEMs will flow predominantly to Tesla, with Toyota as the only possible secondary beneficiary.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Coast-to-Coast FSD Drive Across Canada:** David Moss, Spencer, and Deon Olsen began a coast-to-coast attempt across Canada three days prior to the video, driving a 2025 AI4 Model 3 on FSD version 14.3, targeting 3,700 miles from Vancouver, British Columbia to Nova Scotia with zero interventions. At the time of the video, they had covered 1,776 miles — approximately halfway — entirely on FSD with no disengagements. The only notable incident was a bird strike (which did not constitute a disengagement) and a tense encounter with a construction flagger holding a stop sign, which the car ultimately navigated without driver intervention.

2. **FSD Maturity Beyond Austin:** The host emphasizes that FSD supervised is being used across Washington State, throughout the United States, and now cross-country in Canada — not just in the Austin robotaxi zone — as evidence of broad geographic capability. A Tesla team member named "Gentai" is quoted saying that FSD supervised "shrinks your idea of a long trip" and enables road trips anywhere, handling driving, charging, and parking autonomously.

## Robotaxi

1. **Jo Bhakdi's Cybercab Fleet Forecast for 2026:** Jo Bhakdi, referencing agreement with "Cern" (likely Cern Basher) from a recent podcast, forecasts 10,000–20,000 Cybercabs deployed by end of 2026, with 15,000 as his central estimate. He previously predicted 1,800 unsupervised Cybercabs by April–May 2026, which he acknowledges did not materialize, and he attributes this to roughly a one-year delay in the overall trajectory. He notes his confidence in the eventual outcome has increased, not decreased, due to the evidence of Cybercab production ramping and infrastructure buildout across Dallas, Houston, and Austin.

2. **The "Thousands" Heuristic for Commercial Launch:** Jo Bhakdi's core logical argument is that it is not feasible for Tesla to have thousands of Cybercabs driving around indefinitely without putting them into commercial service. He estimates that within three to four months from the time of the video (i.e., approximately August–September 2026), Tesla will have thousands of Cybercabs produced, at which point the economic and logistical pressure to launch commercial operations becomes irresistible. He expects perhaps three to six months of pre-commercial testing once that threshold is reached, but argues the ramp cannot be halted without it constituting a catastrophic failure signal.

3. **2027 and Beyond — Exponential Scaling:** If 15,000 Cybercabs are reached in 2026, Jo Bhakdi argues 100,000+ in 2027 becomes near-certain, with massive free cash flow implications. He projects millions of Cybercabs before or around 2030 and tens of millions by the end of the 2030s — numbers he says no conventional Wall Street analyst has on their forecast, which he interprets as a significant mispricing of Tesla's future cash flows.

4. **Market Consensus Underestimates the Ramp:** Jo Bhakdi estimates that mainstream analyst consensus expects perhaps 3,000 Cybercabs by end of 2027, and forecasts of 100,000–200,000 units around 2040, which he calls "totally off." He argues that if his trajectory is correct, current Tesla stock prices reflect a dramatic underestimation of robotaxi cash flows.

## Financials

1. **Tesla Margin vs. Legacy OEM Margins:** Jo Bhakdi references Tesla's automotive gross margin of approximately 15.9% as something critics have complained about, but notes that legacy OEM margins are deeply negative on EVs — framing 15.9% as a position of overwhelming strength by comparison. He uses this to argue Tesla is "printing money" while competitors are being "hammered and destroyed."

2. **Stock Price Targets:** Jo Bhakdi states his fair value for Tesla this year is approximately $890, and believes the stock could overshoot toward approximately $1,000 if the Cybercab fleet reaches 15,000 units. He contrasts this with what he characterizes as the market consensus of perhaps $550–$600, arguing the market is not pricing in a large-scale 2026 robotaxi deployment.
