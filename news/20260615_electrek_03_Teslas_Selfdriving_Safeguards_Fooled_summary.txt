Chinese e-commerce platforms are selling $20–$50 plastic doll heads designed to fool Tesla's cabin camera driver-monitoring system, allowing drivers to use Autopilot and FSD (Supervised) without actually paying attention. The devices represent an escalating pattern of safety-system circumvention with potentially fatal consequences.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **How the bypass works:** Tesla's cabin camera monitors head position and eye movement to ensure driver attention during Autopilot and FSD (Supervised) use. Chinese Tesla drivers are mounting miniature figurine heads — including celebrity likenesses — near the rearview mirror to satisfy the camera's detection criteria. One Model 3 owner reportedly drove 30 minutes using a Dwayne Johnson figurine with no safety alerts while eating sunflower seeds and filming video one-handed at highway speeds. Sellers also offer screens displaying blinking eyes and custom headrest-mounted solutions.

2. **Broader pattern of safety system circumvention:** This follows an earlier generation of steering-wheel torque-sensor weights (e.g., "Autopilot Buddy," shut down by NHTSA cease-and-desist), which prompted Tesla to add cabin camera monitoring — now itself defeated. The article notes Tesla recently had to crack down on over 100,000 vehicles using hacked FSD-enabler devices in unauthorized markets, and Tesla just launched FSD (Supervised) in China while facing a fraud lawsuit from 10 Chinese owners over "Full Self-Driving" claims.

3. **Regulatory and safety record context:** NHTSA has identified 80 FSD traffic violations including running red lights and crossing into wrong lanes, and has upgraded its investigation to an engineering analysis covering 3.2 million vehicles — the final step before a potential recall. The article cites documented crashes including a Tesla on FSD going through a railroad gate before a train arrived and a crash during a live FSD demonstration.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that buyers of these devices are endangering themselves and others, sellers are enabling potentially fatal behavior, and Tesla bears responsibility for deploying a camera-based monitoring system defeatable by a static plastic object. He calls on Tesla to implement liveness detection, gaze-tracking with anti-spoofing, and body-pose correlation, and criticizes Tesla for undermining its own safety messaging through official videos that promote FSD misuse. He also argues that branding like "Autopilot" and "Full Self-Driving" set expectations the technology cannot meet.
