Electrek's lone Tesla/SpaceX-relevant article today examines Tesla's newly opened Cybercab orders and the controversy around them.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab orders launch amid MKBHD bet controversy:** Electrek reports Tesla opened invite-only Cybercab orders at $29,990 for select customers, requiring a mandatory $299/month Autonomy Subscription and a $1,000 non-refundable deposit, with estimated delivery in December 2026, limited to Tesla's Texas Robotaxi cities. Per Electrek, the car only operates inside Tesla's existing Robotaxi geofences, and Tesla's Cybercab Autonomy Subscription Agreement states the car will not operate without the subscription, bars owners from ride-hailing use, and lets Tesla shrink the operating area at any time. Electrek calculates this totals $47,631 over five years before insurance, registration, and charging. The piece ties the launch to a bet between Elon Musk and YouTuber Marques Brownlee (MKBHD), who argued in September that Tesla would not ship such a vehicle to regular customers for $30k before the end of 2026 and would shave his head on camera if wrong; Musk called him "a chicken" after the order page launched. Musk, replying to Brownlee, said the car "will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval" but that "the monthly fee is too high" and "should match the cost of Tesla FSD," which Musk said is "more like ~$115/month." Author Fred Lambert argues this looks like Tesla rushing out a half-baked product mainly to win a Twitter bet, comparing it to Tesla's single 2025 "autonomous delivery" stunt, and contends the offer makes little sense since a Model Y with FSD at $99/month gives more capability for less money without Tesla's ability to brick the vehicle or change terms. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/15/tesla-cybercab-mkbhd-bet-musk-chicken/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is sharply critical, characterizing the Cybercab personal-use offer as "completely broken," a product designed mainly to let Musk win a bet rather than deliver real consumer value.
