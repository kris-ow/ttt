This is a post-earnings roundtable from the futureaza channel reacting to Tesla's latest quarterly report and the roughly 20% weekly TSLA drawdown (and ~10% SpaceX secondary-market decline per the host), debating whether the selloff reflects deteriorating fundamentals or market skittishness over rising capex. The panel broadly argues fundamentals are strong or improving even as they critique Tesla's Q&A handling of robotaxi-timeline skepticism, then move through product updates spanning FSD, Optimus, Robotaxi infrastructure, and Starlink.

## Financials
1. **Capex increase seen as main driver of the selloff:** One panelist (Matthew) argued the stock drop was driven primarily by rising capex and guidance that capex will not decrease, comparing it to a smaller drop Google saw after similar large capex announcements; he called this expected for long-term holders but unsettling for shorter-term investors.
2. **Quarterly results highlighted as strong despite stock reaction:** A host cited record deliveries up 25% year-over-year, revenue growth of 26% to $28.24 billion (beating consensus by roughly $3 billion, per the host), the first-ever rolling 12-month period above $100 billion in revenue, and services/other revenue growth of 50%.
3. **Panelists frame stock price as decoupled from near-term fundamentals:** One panelist (John) argued that Tesla's stock reflects future growth expectations rather than current fundamentals, and that a dip signals reduced market confidence in the pace of future growth, not a deterioration in execution; he noted profit was still roughly a billion dollars and pushed back on the framing that Tesla "missed" when it was Wall Street analyst estimates that missed.

## Robotaxi
1. **Panel critiques Tesla's non-answer on robotaxi timeline credibility:** Matthew said the top shareholder question asked why investors should trust Tesla's short-term robotaxi predictions, and that Tesla preempted it with a robotaxi update but never directly addressed why guidance should now be trusted, calling this a missed opportunity for a more transparent response about what has slowed expansion.
2. **Robotaxi expansion continues across cities:** The panel noted Robotaxi service now operates in about seven cities, with two additional unsupervised launches this week in Orlando and Tampa; one host noted anecdotal reports of the first Robotaxi-involved accidents (e.g., other vehicles backing into parked cabs) appearing at service centers.
3. **San Antonio robotaxi charging facility zoning approved:** Tesla received zoning approval for a dedicated robotaxi charging facility in San Antonio with 56 charging stalls; panelists speculated (without confirmation) that this points toward wireless charging, citing an FCC approval earlier in the year for ultra-wideband positioning antennas needed for precise pad alignment.
4. **Starlink connectivity now integrated into Cybercab:** A host said Tesla's Santana Row store this week displayed images confirming Starlink hardware is now integrated into the Cybercab (rather than the earlier bolted-on antenna seen during testing), which the panel called the first official confirmation of this integration and a step toward eliminating connectivity dead zones for the autonomous fleet.
5. **Elon Musk says Tesla won't partner with Uber/Lyft:** Per the panel, Musk stated Tesla will rely on direct bookings through its own Robotaxi app rather than partnering with ride-share providers, with panelists noting Waymo and Uber reportedly ended their partnership in Phoenix; panelists said Tesla's current bottleneck is fleet size/ramp rather than demand.
6. **Active fleet metrics:** Active Robotaxi fleet: 791 (Bay Area 643, Austin 109, Dallas 20, Houston 19) per RobotaxiTracker.com; unsupervised fleet last reported at 39 (stale since 2026-05-09).

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD mileage and adoption metrics from the earnings call:** The panel cited cumulative FSD miles around 12.6 billion and roughly 380,000 robotaxi miles that a host believed (with some hedging) were unsupervised; they also noted 55% of car sales are now shipping with FSD subscriptions, which the host called an inflection point given FSD subscription growth of 56% year-over-year.
2. **New FSD/software versions rolling out:** Panelists discussed recent FSD versions (one host mentioned versions in the 1435–1436 range fixing local navigation issues) and noted V15 is now running on the robotaxi fleet on Model Y vehicles (not yet Cybercab), which they read as a positive signal since it means the newer software reaches other vehicles before Cybercab.
3. **Grok integration expanding vehicle capabilities:** Upcoming software updates include Grok gaining abilities to make calls, search/play music, adjust climate, open the glovebox, and answer vehicle questions; other planned features include FSD stats in the app, custom wrap uploads without USB, karaoke score-tracking, smarter route-learning navigation, and supercharger search by name.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus hand dexterity highlighted as an engineering leap:** A host discussed comments attributed to Musk about the human hand's complexity and said Tesla's Optimus 3 hand design emphasizes full dexterity and flexibility versus competitors' simpler two/three-finger grippers, with an unnamed Tesla engineer reportedly saying the robot will look more like "a human in a superhero suit."
2. **Panel reads Tesla's secrecy on Optimus 3 as bullish:** One host argued the unusually low leak volume around Optimus 3 is itself a bullish signal, and another host attributed (per Musk, according to the panel) the secrecy to concerns that competitors could copy demonstrated capabilities to inflate their own valuations.

## Energy
1. **Energy deployment growth and new charging infrastructure:** The call reportedly showed energy deployment up 41% year-over-year (second-best quarterly figure to date), alongside the launch of Tesla's first public Megawatt Charger site for the Semi in Bloomington, California (six stalls), which panelists linked to freight routes serving the Port of Long Beach.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comcast downplays Starlink threat; panel is skeptical:** A Comcast executive was quoted saying Starlink is not currently seen as a meaningful competitive factor but that the company isn't complacent and expects it to become more competitive over time; panelists called this a "Steve Ballmer reacting to the iPhone" moment, arguing Starlink already dominates rural broadband and will erode telecom margins as Starship enables larger V3 satellites.
2. **Canada EV sales momentum:** Model Y sales in Canada were said to have nearly doubled in the most recent quarter (up 98%), with roughly 6,000 vehicles delivered and Model Y deliveries up 28% year-over-year in the first half; Cybertruck deliveries in Canada reportedly surged 116%.
