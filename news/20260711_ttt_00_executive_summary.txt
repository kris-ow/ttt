## Robotaxi
- Tesla announced July 10 that Cybercab "employee rides" at Giga Texas are "starting soon," showing a driverless Cybercab (no wheel, no pedals) crossing the outbound parking lot. Scope is unclear — could be a real shuttle across the campus or just a lot loop — and it is not the same as Cybercab joining the paid Austin fleet, which the Austin fleet still runs via Model Y with safety monitors (city estimates ~50 vehicles added per year). Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the announcement not meaningful news until a Cybercab does unsupervised paid rides on public streets, which he speculates could happen around Tesla's upcoming earnings.
- "Well over 100" finished Cybercabs are already stacked at the Giga Texas outbound lot per Electrek, suggesting production/hardware is ready while software readiness remains the bottleneck; Tesla has acknowledged FSD needs a "ground-up rewrite" to scale to unsupervised operation.
- Tesla's supervised Austin robotaxi fleet is crashing at roughly 4x the human rate (~1 per 57,000 miles vs. ~1 per 229,000 human benchmark), per Electrek.
- Tesla has a commercial filming permit for Wrigleyville, Chicago (July 13) with two Cybercabs delivered nearby last week; Illinois currently has no Waymo or Tesla robotaxi operations and is described as not particularly AV-friendly.
- Miami robotaxi service remains early-beta: field testers found only 2-4 active vehicles and multi-hour wait times; Tesla's Miami geofence is roughly 15% of Waymo's coverage area. None of the observed vehicles carried official Cybercab badging.
- Cost analysis (futureaza): Cybercab's ground-up redesign — no steering wheel/pedals, two-seat layout, injection-molded frame, actuated doors — is argued to cut weight, battery size, and durability engineering costs versus a retrofitted consumer car; Tesla's domestic 4680/lithium supply chain is cited as insulating Cybercab from tariffs.
- Bull case (jobhakdi): host argues Robotaxi is "extremely likely" to be a major new earnings driver as soon as Q3 2026, projecting a hypothetical fleet path from ~100,000 units by 2027 to 1.5 million longer-term — explicitly the host's own speculative model, not company guidance, and well above Morgan Stanley's cited estimate of 30,000 by 2030.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's Optimus factory near Giga Texas is under active construction (steel erection, site expansion visible via aerial footage), reportedly targeting up to 10 million robots/year with a footprint just under the Giga Texas car factory, per brighterwithherbert; a separate report (Sawyer Merritt) cites a 1 million/year capacity figure tied specifically to the Fremont retooling.
- Fremont's Model S/X production lines are being torn down to install Optimus manufacturing equipment, consistent with prior comments that gear is arriving from Tesla Automation in Germany requiring 1-2 weeks validation per line across 40+ lines, with possible low-volume Optimus output suggested by Q4.
- Competitor 1X unveiled a new Neo robotic hand: 25 degrees of freedom, IP68-rated, food-safe, tactile slip-sensing, finger speed up to 8 m/s; hundreds already built with a target of 10,000 hands/year capacity. 1X plans to gather real-world data via teleoperated home deployments, contrasting with Tesla's in-house factory training approach. Figure also touted robot progress including the "first humanoid robot in the White House."
- China ships roughly 90% of the world's humanoid robots today per Peter Diamandis; Musk responded "For now."
- Tesla has gone "completely radio silent" on Optimus for months amid unverified reports of schedule slippage, per electrified's host, who believes Tesla is likely pursuing a different technical path rather than falling behind.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14 Lite began early-access rollout to HW3 Model 3/Y owners in South Korea — the first international expansion of this version, with commentators noting most North American HW3 owners still lack it.
- Grok conversational AI is rolling out for in-vehicle search in Japan, demonstrated switching fluidly between Japanese and English.
- Sawyer Merritt floated wanting the Unsupervised Robotaxi "Pull Over" button feature added to personal FSD — a feature request, not an announced product change.

## Market & Competition
- New Jersey bill S.1677/A.3968 would require AVs to use cameras plus two additional sensor types (e.g., LiDAR, radar), effectively barring Tesla's camera-only system while leaving Waymo unaffected; backers include Teamsters and a group called SAVE US whose campaign director previously led Tesla Takedown. Sponsor Sen. Zwicker frames it as safety-driven (citing ~600 NJ traffic deaths last year); Sawyer Merritt called it "anti-competitive favoritism."
- Musk publicly reversed his stance on Anthropic, calling them the current AI leader and saying he would not cut off their compute access despite competing, citing Tesla's open-sourced patents and Supercharger network as precedent.
- Musk said Grok 4.5 isn't yet using SpaceX's internally developed C++ inference software matched to GB300 hardware, and that doubling current speed "is probably achievable." Perplexity's internal benchmark reportedly found Grok 4.5 outperforming rivals at roughly half the cost of Opus 4.8.
- Mag7 valuation debate: one host cites Nvidia trading at ~15.7x forward P/E despite ~26.2% projected growth, below the NASDAQ's 23.68x average, arguing Tesla and peers are undervalued relative to growth.
