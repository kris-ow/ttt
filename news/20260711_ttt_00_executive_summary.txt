## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Tesla posted a clip of a driverless Cybercab (no wheel, no pedals) crossing the Giga Texas parking lot, captioning "Cybercab employee rides at Giga Texas" starting soon; Tesla gave no detail on route, fleet size, or whether this is a real shuttle service versus a lot loop. Electrek's Fred Lambert calls it non-news until a Cybercab runs unsupervised, paying rides on public streets — something he speculates could land in Austin around this month's earnings. Electrified's host reads it as a pre-launch milestone, guessing 2-3 weeks before network deployment.
- "Well over 100" finished Cybercabs are already stacked in the Giga Texas outbound lot, per Electrek — hardware/production looks solved, with software readiness now the binding constraint given Tesla's acknowledged need for a "ground-up rewrite" of the FSD stack before unsupervised scaling.
- Tesla holds a commercial filming permit for Wrigleyville, Chicago on July 13, with two Cybercabs already delivered nearby; Chicago has no current Waymo or Tesla robotaxi operations and Illinois is described as not AV-friendly.
- Miami robotaxi service remains early-beta: field testers waited from ~10am to ~3pm for a ride with only two to four active vehicles observed; Tesla's Miami geofence is roughly 10-15 sq mi, ~15% of Waymo's coverage there. A spotted Cybercab lot near the airport is not seen as signaling imminent ramp-up — vehicles lacked official Cybercab badging.
- Austin's officially registered fleet is ~102 vehicles, but only an estimated 30-50 are active at any given time — called a "mostly disappointing first year" by one host.
- Cybercab's cost advantage stems from ground-up redesign (no steering wheel/pedals/glove box, two-seat layout, injection-molded frame, actuated doors that skip slam-durability engineering), reportedly targeting a 500,000-mile design life under heavy taxi utilization, per Tesla's impact report.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla's Optimus factory near Giga Texas is expanding southward with active steel erection, reportedly targeting 10 million robots/year capacity (per aerial footage analysis) — footprint just under the Giga Texas car plant. Separately, Sawyer Merritt's feed cites a Fremont-tied capacity figure of 1 million Optimus/year once that retooling ramps.
- Fremont's Model S/X lines are being torn down to make way for Optimus tooling, consistent with equipment arriving from Tesla Automation in Germany; low-volume Optimus production has been suggested for Q4.
- Competitor 1X detailed its Neo robot hand: 25 degrees of freedom, IP68-rated, food-safe, finger speeds up to 8 m/s, tactile slip-sensing, with hundreds already produced off a Hayward, CA line rated for 10,000 hands/year (5,000 robots); a larger plant opens later this year. 1X plans to gather data via employee teleoperation once units ship to customer homes, contrasting with Tesla's in-house training approach.
- China reportedly ships ~90% of the world's humanoid robots (per Peter Diamandis); Musk responded "For now." Tesla has stayed silent on Optimus specifics for months amid unverified reports development lags plan.

## Autonomous Driving
- FSD V14 Lite is rolling out as early access to HW3 owners in South Korea — the first international expansion cited for this version; hosts note most North American HW3 owners still lack it.
- Tesla's supervised Austin robotaxi fleet is crashing at roughly 1 per 57,000 miles versus a ~1 per 229,000-mile human benchmark, per Electrek, alongside Tesla's own acknowledgment that FSD needs a "ground-up rewrite" to scale to unsupervised operation.
- Grok conversational AI is rolling out for in-vehicle search in Japan, demonstrated switching fluidly between Japanese and English.

## Market & Competition
- New Jersey bill S.1677/A.3968 would require AVs to use cameras plus two additional sensor types (e.g., LiDAR, radar), effectively barring Tesla's camera-only system; backers include Teamsters and a group called SAVE US led by a former Tesla Takedown organizer. Sponsor Sen. Zwicker frames it as safety-driven (~600 NJ traffic deaths last year); Sawyer Merritt calls it "anti-competitive favoritism." Waymo, which uses LiDAR/radar, would be unaffected.
- Waymo continues fully driverless, paid rides at scale across multiple US cities, a contrast Electrek draws against Tesla's factory-lot employee rides.
- Musk said he was "clearly wrong about Anthropic," calling it the current AI leader and pledging not to cut its compute access despite competing with it, citing Tesla's open-sourced patents and Supercharger network as precedent.
- A host argues Mag7 names including Tesla are undervalued, citing Tesla's ~198x forward P/E against a base-case 2026 EBIT of ~$4.5B; his own (non-guidance) projections model Robotaxi fleets reaching 100,000 by 2027 and up to 1.5 million longer-term, versus Morgan Stanley's cited estimate of 30,000 by 2030.

## Electric Vehicles
- Model Y L is in production at Giga Texas with North American deliveries expected from September 2026; European spy shots suggest a configurator launch is approaching.
- A China regulatory filing suggests Model Y Performance may return there, becoming a fifth Model Y variant offered in that market.
- Tesla's rental program is expanding to Maryland, Kansas, Washington, and Pennsylvania (plus prior states), at $60/day for Model 3/Y and $75/day for Cybertruck, with free Supercharging and FSD included; launch timing unspecified.

## Energy

- Musk announced Starlink can deliver reliable 10 Gbps symmetric connectivity globally, demonstrated at up to 20 Gbps via bonded gateways in a remote Alaska deployment.
