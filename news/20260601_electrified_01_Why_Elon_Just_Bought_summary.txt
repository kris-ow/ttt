This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers a wide range of Tesla and broader tech developments including the stalled robotaxi expansion in Houston and Dallas, FSD 14.3 performance impressions, Tesla's European sales surge, the Terafab land acquisition in Texas, and competitive moves from BYD and OpenAI in autonomy and robotics. The core thesis is that while Tesla's supervised FSD is showing strong real-world performance globally, the unsupervised robotaxi rollout appears to be lagging expectations, creating a confounding near-term picture for the autonomy thesis.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas Self-Certification and Official Registry:** Tesla officially self-certified its robotaxis as Level 4 under a new Texas law that went into effect last week, allowing continued robotaxi operations in the state. This is described as largely a formality and procedural change — no new capability was added. The Texas Motor Carrier website now lists Tesla's robotaxi service with 42 VINs registered across 42 rows. For comparison, Waymo has 619 VINs listed in Texas, and AVID has 317 VINs registered.

2. **Houston and Dallas Fleet Contraction:** On-the-ground reporters indicate the Houston robotaxi fleet has dropped from six operational vehicles down to two, a situation that has persisted for several weeks. In Dallas, Spencer reported being unable to get a robotaxi two days in a row, with the app showing "no rides available nearby" — indicating no vehicles operating in the fleet rather than high demand. Dillon Loomis noted he warned on April 18th (four days before the earnings call) that if the Houston and Dallas launches didn't scale beyond a few vehicles within weeks, they may have been timed for positive earnings momentum rather than genuine readiness.

3. **Cybercab World Cup Speculation and Caution:** Many are speculating Tesla will debut the Cybercab on the robotaxi network in Houston and/or Dallas around June 14th when World Cup games begin. However, Dillon Loomis cautions that Tesla has been historically cautious and is unlikely to rush a Cybercab debut for marketing exposure. Ashoke stated that "soon the Cybercab will be driving itself into Austin City reporting for duty," though Dillon notes "soon at Tesla has many different interpretations."

4. **Supervisor Hiring for New Robotaxi Cities:** Tesla currently has six job listings for a robotaxi fleet support supervisor in Palo Alto, Tampa, San Antonio, Orlando, San Francisco, and Miami. Three of those cities — Miami, Orlando, and Tampa — match three of the five cities Tesla is reportedly targeting for robotaxi service launch by the end of June 2026. The role involves ensuring vehicles are in excellent condition for 24/7 operations, including vehicle prep, inspections, and lot management.

5. **Dallas Facility Repurposed for Autonomous Vehicles:** A court document reveals Tesla is building a 51,000 sq ft EV sales, service, and delivery center in Grand Prairie, Texas (between Dallas and Fort Worth, near Interstate 20). A revised operational plan in the document states the facility will be used for charging, cleaning, repair, and general maintenance of Tesla-owned autonomous vehicles. This signals that Tesla's existing service center and gallery infrastructure is insufficient for planned robotaxi scale. The facility is currently under construction and will not be open in time for the World Cup.

6. **RobotaxiTracker Hiring Watch List Update:** The RobotaxiTracker.com hiring watch list now shows 41 entries, including 33 active listings of cities where Tesla is currently hiring for an AI safety operator role. Historical cities where Tesla previously hired for this role are still visible, suggesting those positions have been filled.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 Real-World Performance — Positive Signals:** Multiple noteworthy FSD demonstrations have surfaced. Joe Anderson drove through all 48 contiguous US states plus DC on FSD version 14.3.2 — over 7,200 miles across 9 days — with FSD driving every mile. Tesla's official account congratulated him. Separately, a team drove coast-to-coast across Canada — over 3,700 miles on version 14.3 — with zero disengagements of any kind. The head of research at Silicon Data was quoted saying "self-driving has been quietly completely solved with steady daily improvements." A tester in Germany drove for almost an hour through villages, country roads, and highways with no issues. FSD has also gone viral in China with testers pushing extreme scenarios. Tesla's FSD is now approved in the Netherlands, Lithuania, and Estonia across the EU.

2. **FSD 14.3 Personal Experience — Mixed:** Dillon Loomis shares his own experience with 14.3, describing it as "very far from a banger" despite Elon's public praise. He reports persistent jittery behavior, micro-braking events that would make passengers uncomfortable, poor navigation in his area, and notably poor parking behavior — the car has parked in entirely different parking lots from the intended destination on multiple occasions. He attributes some of this to Tesla rewriting the compiler and runtime from the ground up, acknowledging a few steps back may be expected. He also notes that Elon's credibility for touting upcoming FSD improvements is "as low as it's ever been" in his view.

3. **Hardware 3 Version 14 Light Expected in June:** Dillon notes that hardware 3 owners should be expecting a "version 14 light" sometime in June 2026 if Tesla delivers on its timeline.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **European Sales Surge:** Tesla sales in Europe have surged significantly in May 2026, partly reflecting low year-ago comparables from the Model Y and Model 3 changeovers. Country-level data for May: Portugal up ~350% year-over-year (January–May up 58%, outpacing a 33% rise in all EV sales); Sweden up 71%; Denmark up 136% (Model Y was Denmark's best-selling vehicle in May); Spain up 113%; Norway up 29%; France up 655%. Giga Berlin is expanding output capacity, and a broader supervised FSD rollout across the EU later in 2026 is expected to further accelerate sales.

2. **Canada Chinese-Built EV Quota:** Tesla has already used approximately 12% of Canada's available quota for Chinese-built EVs in the first allocation window. Nearly all of the roughly 2,900 Chinese-built EVs imported so far are Model 3s from Giga Shanghai. The current quota window allows up to 24,500 Chinese-built EVs to be imported between program launch and August 31st, 2026.

3. **Tesla Semi Grapevine Test:** Covenant conducted a two-week real-world test of the Tesla Semi with a major California customer on the Grapevine route — described as one of the most demanding real-world routes for any truck due to steep grades, heavy load management, and heat. The driver reported being "amazed" at the Tesla Semi's performance and felt a level of confidence hard to match in a diesel truck. A summary from Covenant is described as coming soon.

4. **Graphite Supply Chain Update:** Australia's Syrah Resources announced Tesla has withdrawn a notice of intent to end their graphite supply deal, accepting that Syrah is now producing conforming samples. Tesla had originally issued a default notice in July 2025 citing conformity issues with active anode material samples from the Vidalia facility. Dillon flags the importance of securing anode material suppliers outside of China.

## Market & Competition

1. **BYD Autonomous Driving Liability Policy:** BYD announced a new policy offering comprehensive coverage for damages incurred while its "God's Eye" autonomous driving system is engaged. BYD will cover all losses with no cap for vehicle repairs, third-party property damage, and personal injury liabilities, and the policy will not raise the owner's regular commercial insurance for the following year. The policy is valid for one year. God's Eye is technically a Level 2 system. Dillon notes a precedent: when BYD introduced a similar guarantee for automatic parking in July 2025, usage jumped from ~21% to over 90%. He flags this as a signal that automakers will increasingly need to take liability for their autonomous systems.

2. **OpenAI Robotics Entering Humanoid Space:** Sam Altman announced OpenAI Robotics is now hiring to program and manufacture robots. OpenAI's stated near-term focus is on robots to support skilled workers building infrastructure; long-term vision is a personal robot for everyone. Dillon recaps the history: OpenAI invested in Figure in 2024 and partnered to develop AI models for Figure robots, but Figure ended that partnership roughly a year later after claiming a major in-house AI breakthrough. OpenAI has since been doing data collection and teleoperation work with hands but has not yet revealed a full humanoid prototype. In January 2026, OpenAI issued an RFP seeking humanoid manufacturing partners in the US for components like gearboxes, actuators, and motors. Dillon expects partnerships given OpenAI's lack of manufacturing experience and views this as a very long game over decades.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Terafab Land Acquisition Near Austin:** SE Robinson reported that Witt LLC — a purchasing entity sharing the physical address of XAI in Palo Alto — is buying over 3,100 acres in Grimes County, Texas (at the Gibbons Creek Reservoir), just over a two-hour drive from Giga Texas. A public hearing is scheduled for June 3rd, 2026, with SpaceX listed as the property owner for the tax abatement session. The project is described as a "multi-phase next-gen vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing and advanced computing fab facility." Estimated capital investment for initial phases is $55 billion, with a total potential investment of $119 billion if additional phases are built. XAI's general counsel James Burnham has signed on behalf of Witt LLC. Dillon identifies this as the land for the Terafab, noting Elon uses tech entities tied to his companies to acquire land ahead of formal approvals, which are still pending.

2. **Macrohard and Digital Optimus Context:** Dillon briefly references Macrohard and "something like a digital Optimus" as potential internal uses for SpaceX's compute capacity (Colossus), noting that SpaceX retaining optionality to reallocate GPUs for internal Optimus-related projects represents asymmetric long-term upside.
