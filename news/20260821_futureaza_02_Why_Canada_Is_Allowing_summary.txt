This episode of futureaza, featuring a guest from The Tesla Life, covers Canadian trade policy toward Chinese-made EVs — knockdown-kit assembly rules, tariff negotiations with the US, and Canada's EV import quota system — with Tesla surfacing only as one of the companies already shipping Shanghai-built vehicles into Canada under that quota.

## Market & Competition
1. **Canada blocks knockdown-kit assembly for Stellantis's China EV plans:** Canadian Industry Minister Melanie Jolie reportedly ruled out Stellantis using knockdown kits (pre-built components shipped for local assembly) for Chinese EV production at its factory; any plan must have provincial government and union support and support the local supply chain, per the discussion. The hosts cited a Los Angeles BYD bus factory as an example of this "assemble, not build" model that Canada wants to avoid.
2. **Chinese and other new EV entrants face a value/service risk in Canada:** The hosts discussed whether Chinese EVs newly entering Canada can hold resale value, given uncertainty about long-term parts, service, and warranty support; they cited VinFast (Vietnam) as a cautionary example, saying it closed roughly 80% of its Canadian dealerships about a year and a half after entering the market, which the host argued scares off buyers and value.
3. **Canada–US tariff/USMCA turmoil clouds auto trade:** The host (from The Tesla Life) described USMCA as suspended amid new tariffs on Canada, including a reported 50% tariff on various Canadian goods approaching around August 19, with intense last-minute negotiations underway at time of filming and an uncertain outcome.
4. **Kia EV3 pricing highlighted as aggressive:** The Kia EV3 was described as the least expensive EV in Canada at roughly $40,000 CAD all-in, an SUV-style vehicle with about 517 km (over 300 miles) of range, qualifying for an additional $5,000 federal EV rebate that could bring the price down to around $33,000 CAD (roughly $24,000 USD). One host questioned whether Kia is making money at that price and called it potentially unsustainable.
5. **Canada–China exploring clean-energy cooperation:** Following the lifting of a Chinese EV import blockade for a 49,000-unit quota, Canada and China are reportedly exploring broader clean-energy cooperation, pairing Canadian minerals with Chinese manufacturing; one host noted a company ("CL") has participated in three large-scale grid storage projects in Ontario and suggested room to expand that cooperation.
6. **Feasibility of Chinese-EV assembly in Canada (à la Ford's Spain plant) questioned:** A host argued replicating Ford's approach of building Chinese EVs in Spain isn't straightforward for Canada, noting a new plant typically needs to produce around 200,000 vehicles to be profitable, while Canada's ~2 million annual vehicle purchases are spread across many models — meaning surplus production would need an export market like the US, which is complicated by the current tariff uncertainty.
7. **Canada's 49,000-unit EV import quota mechanics and Tesla's early position:** Canada's Chinese-EV import quota (roughly 2–3% of the market, described inconsistently in the discussion as both "too many" and "not enough") launched this spring; a host said slightly over 50% of the first-half quota has been used, with unused allocations rolling over rather than being lost. Companies that had already been exporting China-built vehicles — including Tesla (Shanghai-built units) and Polestar — were able to use the quota immediately, while other Chinese manufacturers are expected to start entering around 2027 per the host's expectation. One host speculated Tesla has already shipped and will likely continue shipping Model 3s from China to Canada under this quota, and noted lead times "have been increasing," though no specific figures were given.
