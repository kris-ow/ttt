The article (an opinion-inflected news piece from Electrek) reports that Tesla and SpaceX have each formed special board committees to explore merging the two companies, as disclosed by Elon Musk on SpaceX's quarterly earnings call. The author frames this as the first formal step toward a merger and argues it would dilute both companies' shareholders and, by extension, retirement savers exposed to major indices.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Special committees formed:** Musk said on SpaceX's earnings call (its second ever as a public company) that both Tesla and SpaceX have formed "special committees" of "independent board members" to review the possibility of a merger. Musk stated he thinks a merger would be a good idea because "we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity" — a claim the author disputes and calls irrelevant to an automaker.
2. **Approval process:** Musk is reportedly not officially involved in the committees' discussions, nor are non-"independent" members like his brother Kimbal Musk. After any committee sign-off, shareholders of both companies would need to approve the merger. The author notes SpaceX approval is likely assured because Musk controls more than 50% of SpaceX's voting shares. For Tesla, approval requires 50% of all outstanding shares (not just those voting), and Tesla has introduced an "auto-vote" feature letting shareholders default their votes to board recommendations — which the author argues is designed to boost passage odds given Tesla's high proportion of retail shareholders.
3. **Pay package tie-in:** The article states that Musk's Tesla and SpaceX pay packages include provisions under which his stock grants are automatically delivered in the event of a merger, regardless of whether operational milestones (e.g., robot, robotaxi, or Mars colonization targets tied to the packages) are met.

## Financials
1. **Dilution concern for retirement savers:** Both Tesla and SpaceX are large enough to be included in major stock indices; the author notes SpaceX was added to the Nasdaq 100 within days of its IPO after what the article says were rule changes Musk obtained, meaning many retirement portfolios may already hold SpaceX exposure. A merger giving Musk a larger ownership share would, per the article, dilute the value of index-linked holdings in both companies.
2. **Musk's current Tesla ownership:** The article states Musk's Tesla ownership is around 22% following what it describes as the restoration of his "illegal" pay package.

## Editorial Stance
The author is strongly critical, characterizing the merger push as a continuation of a pattern of Musk using high-profile, improbable promises (robots, Mars colonization, superintelligence) to secure shareholder approval for deals that primarily benefit him, drawing an explicit parallel to Tesla's 2016 SolarCity acquisition and the Twitter/X debt situation. The author also argues Tesla's board has consistently acted in Musk's interest rather than shareholders', citing the board's approval of his prior pay package as evidence against its independence, and closes with pointed criticism of Musk's personal conduct and political statements.
