This Munro Live episode is a hardware teardown of the Inspire Robotics RH56DFQ dexterous robotic hand, a low-cost six-degree-of-freedom hand, with hosts Mike Kasein and Paul Turbo (Munro & Associates) explaining its actuator design, motor construction, and gearing. The video does not mention Tesla, Optimus, or any Tesla/SpaceX product or executive; its relevance to the Tesla thesis is only as general background on the humanoid robotics component-supply landscape that Optimus competes within.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Inspire hand's mechanical design is simple and low-cost relative to more sophisticated dexterous hands:** The RH56DFQ has six actuators driving six degrees of freedom (four fingers, one thumb, one wrist), each finger curling as a linked two-joint motion via a push-rod linear actuator rather than a tendon-pull system; a single microprocessor in the palm coordinates the six actuators, and the design is described by the hosts as an economical, no-frills approach with no sensors or wiring in the fingertips themselves.
2. **Force sensing is inferred at the actuator base, not at the fingertip:** Instead of fingertip sensors, a pressure sensor sits at the back of each linear actuator, translating force transmitted through the finger back to the actuator base; the hosts note this means the hand can sense force when closing/gripping but cannot detect resistance or obstruction when a finger is opening/extending.
3. **Actuators use coreless brush DC motors paired with planetary gearing and a lead screw:** Each finger actuator uses a small coreless brush DC motor (a long-standing, cheaply mass-produced motor type dating to 1980s pager/phone vibration motors, retailing for roughly a dollar) driving a two-stage planetary gearset (roughly 2.4:1 per stage, ~4.8:1 combined) that turns a lead screw to convert rotational motion into linear push force; the motor design avoids iron core losses and cogging torque, though some commutation-related torque ripple remains.
4. **Actuator force output involves an explicit trade-off between speed and strength:** The hosts state the underlying actuator mechanism can be engineered to output up to roughly 70 newtons of force, but Inspire's implementation in this hand is capped around 30 newtons, a deliberate trade-off favoring faster movement to better simulate human hand motion.
5. **Industry framed as early-stage with divergent design philosophies:** The hosts characterize the humanoid-hand space as still in early "wild west" stages, with companies choosing between simple pincer-style grippers and fully articulated hands with independent finger control; they note this Inspire hand cannot move individual fingers independently (only combined curling motion) and represents one lower-cost point on that spectrum.

Notable quotes and data:
- Six degrees of freedom (four fingers, thumb, wrist) driven by six actuators; roughly half a Newton of force resolution cited for the hand overall.
- Planetary gear set: 36 teeth on the ring gear, 12 teeth on the sun gear, ~2.4:1 ratio per stage, two stages combined for ~4.8:1.
- Motor produces on the order of a couple hundred millinewton-meters of torque at up to two amps of current, geared up to roughly one newton-meter of output torque.
- Actuator mechanism capable of up to ~70 newtons of force; this specific hand's actuators are capped at about 30 newtons for speed.
- Coreless brush DC motor design traced back to 1980s pager/phone vibration motors, mass-produced "in the billions," retailing for about a buck.
- Host quote: "It's the wild west... we're just getting started on humanoid robots and this is a great starting point for people to get their feet wet with robotics and hands."
