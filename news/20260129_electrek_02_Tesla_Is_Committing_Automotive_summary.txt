This is an Electrek opinion/editorial piece by Fred Lambert arguing that Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings call revealed a strategic pivot away from being a traditional automaker and toward "transportation as a service," which the author characterizes as self-destructive given Tesla's declining core vehicle business.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model S/X discontinued, no new mass-market models:** On the Q4 2025 earnings call, Tesla said Model S and Model X production will end next quarter, with the Fremont line converted to build Optimus robots. VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy said Tesla should be thought of as "providing transportation as a service more than the total addressable market for the purchased vehicles alone," and the article states there are no plans for new mass-market models, including the previously promised $25,000 Tesla.
2. **Current lineup reduced to two successful models:** The article assesses Tesla's lineup as: Model 3 and Model Y (successful but in decline), Model S and Model X (being killed), Cybertruck (described as a commercial failure selling roughly 20,000-25,000 units/year against a stated 250,000 capacity), and Tesla Semi (still not in volume production after years of delays).
3. **Automotive revenue and delivery declines:** Tesla's automotive revenue declined 10% in 2025 and deliveries fell 9%, with the company losing its position as the world's largest EV maker to BYD, per the article.

## Robotaxi / Humanoid Bots
4. **Musk's comments on autonomy's future role:** Elon Musk said on the call that "the only vehicles that we'll make will be autonomous vehicles" long-term, predicting less than 5% of miles driven (possibly as low as 1%) will involve a human actually driving in the future.
5. **Current state of robotaxi and Optimus described as thin:** The article claims Tesla's Austin robotaxi fleet has roughly 30-60 vehicles actually operating despite claims of "well over 500," and that Optimus robots are doing zero useful factory work "by Musk's own admission." It also notes the CyberCab is about to enter production without a steering wheel while Tesla "hasn't solved autonomy," per the author.

## Financials
6. **2026 capex guidance and profit decline:** Tesla announced plans to spend a record $20 billion in capital expenditure in 2026, with most directed at robotaxi, humanoid robots, and supporting infrastructure/training compute. Tesla's 2025 non-GAAP net income was under $6 billion, down 26% year-over-year and over 50% from its peak a few years prior. The article contrasts this with Tesla's automotive business having generated $80 billion in revenue and nearly $15 billion in profit at its peak.
7. **Mission statement change:** Tesla's 10-K SEC filing updated the company's mission statement to "building a world of amazing abundance," which the article calls vague.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla is "committing automotive suicide" by abandoning a proven, profitable car business to chase unproven autonomy and robotics ventures, and contends Tesla could have pursued both (e.g., via a corporate split) rather than sacrificing its automotive business. The piece also quotes a reader comment (148 likes) arguing robotaxi service will be a worse experience than car ownership, and frames the "transportation as a service" shift as part of a broader, "depressing" societal trend toward subscription-based ownership.
