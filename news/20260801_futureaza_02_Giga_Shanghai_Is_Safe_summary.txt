This episode of the Tesla Beat podcast covers a rumor debunked about splitting Tesla China, a widely discussed Elon Musk interview with The Economist, Starship's latest test flight and recovery, FSD's V15 rollout and safety data, Cybercab fleet expansion, a Cybertruck brake control system (PCS) fix and reimbursement program, and new Megapack LFP cell production milestones, alongside lighter items on Rivian R2 range testing and Volvo dropping LiDAR in favor of vision-only driving.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Robotaxi fleet already running FSD v15:** Per Tesla executive Ashok Elluswamy, the current robotaxi fleet is already running FSD version V15 software, a jump the hosts say is a significant step up from prior V14 point releases; one host reports his personal build (a V14 variant) has become "boring" because it has fixed nearly all of his prior navigation complaints (correct bridge/exit selection), though unsupervised operation is not yet active for consumer vehicles.
2. **Unreleased software spotted at Model Y L event:** Attendees testing the Model Y L, including a host, were running an unreleased FSD build (newer than the version publicly available at the time), suggesting Tesla is already validating newer software ahead of wider release.
3. **FSD attach rate and paid-software growth:** FSD attach rate on new vehicle sales is now around 55%, with hosts describing a growing shift where Tesla stores (citing a Santana Row concept store) now focus sales pitches on the autonomy experience rather than the vehicle itself. FSD paid sales reportedly rose by roughly 180,000 in Q1 and a further roughly 150,000 in Q2 (host-cited figures, exact sourcing not specified in transcript).
4. **European FSD safety data:** Tesla's Europe/Middle East/Africa account reported FSD supervised has been over 5.2 times safer than manual driving across roughly 65 million kilometers in five EU countries over the last four months (per Tesla's own posted data); a host notes total European FSD supervised mileage cited in the study is about 40 million miles, and attributes the lower multiplier versus the US (where a roughly 7.4–7.6x figure was cited) to smaller sample size and software not yet fully trained on European roads.
5. **Long-distance coast-to-coast FSD drives becoming normalized:** Hosts note that individuals (referenced: David Moss, Josh West, Bearded Tesla) have completed multiple unsupervised-style coast-to-coast FSD drives, arguing these feats are becoming so routine they're under-covered relative to their significance.

## Robotaxi
1. **No cap on Cybercab fleet size confirmed:** At a Tesla event, a Tesla executive (referenced as "Lars" by one host) confirmed there is no fleet-size cap on Cybercab production/deployment, addressing speculation about a roughly 2,500-unit limit; hosts describe the vehicle's self-certification under FMVSS as settled, comparing it favorably to Zoox's waiver-based regulatory path.
2. **Fleet visibly scaling in testing regions:** Hosts describe frequent, near-continuous Cybercab traffic observed in Palo Alto and at Giga Texas test tracks, alongside deployment to Tesla sales/service center hubs across the US and Canada for regional testing.
3. **Federal autonomy framework could ease state rollout:** The Trump administration's proposed federal autonomy framework (vs. state-by-state rules) is expected to help autonomous vehicle deployment, though a host cautions it may require additional legislation to force adoption in reluctant states, and skepticism remains given past similar announcements that stalled.
4. **California excludes Tesla from state EV incentive:** A host reports (from a conversation with an unnamed Tesla test-team contact) that Tesla is excluded from a California EV credit (cited at $3,750) because Tesla lacks a headquarters in California, while Lucid and Rivian qualify; hosts argue this has minimal sales impact given Tesla's volume advantage.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus/Tesla production milestone — 10 million vehicles:** Tesla announced hitting 10 million vehicles produced (outside Fremont), reaching this from 1 million six years prior; the milestone was marked with footage from the Model 3/Model Y "tent" (sprung structure) assembly area, which hosts credit as pivotal to Tesla's earlier scaling and profitability.

## Energy
1. **Domestic LFP Megapack cells begin shipping:** Tesla shipped its first Megapacks using domestically made LFP cells, manufactured at its Sparks, Nevada facility and integrated at the California Megapack factory — described by Tesla as a milestone for domestic manufacturing and energy security. A host notes this supports Section 45X-style manufacturing credits and reduces supply-chain risk, though the facility's capacity is described as limited (roughly 10 GWh/year cited).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck brake control system issue resolved with reimbursement:** Tesla announced the pre-2026 Cybertruck brake control system (BCS/PCS) failure issue — a known but intermittent defect — has been resolved via design changes in 2026 model-year trucks, and affected owners who previously paid out of pocket will be reimbursed. Tesla also extended the Foundation Series warranty to 8 years/150,000 miles, versus roughly 70,000 miles cited for post-2026 trucks.
2. **Rivian R2 vs. Model Y Performance range test:** A third-party range test at highway speed found the Rivian R2 fell short of its EPA-rated range by a wider margin than the Tesla Model Y Performance, which came in over its EPA rating; hosts note the test was run near 80 mph and attribute the R2's shortfall to its boxy, high-drag form factor rather than a fixable software issue.
3. **Volvo drops LiDAR for vision-only approach:** Volvo will no longer use its LiDAR sensor, moving toward a vision-only approach for driving automation — a shift hosts frame as validating Tesla's long-held camera-only strategy.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Successful Starship flight and days-long ocean recovery:** SpaceX's most recent Starship test flight was described by hosts as executing a clean descent and landing, with the booster/stage reportedly floating intact in the ocean for several days afterward; only one of the two stages is being targeted for catch-tower recovery currently due to limited catch tower infrastructure.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **FAA proposing streamlined licensing for rocket launches:** Per Sawyer Merritt, the FAA is proposing changes to streamline commercial launch licensing to support higher launch cadence, which hosts say is overdue given current systems were designed for a much lower volume of annual launches.
2. **Jared Isaacman comments on SpaceX and space data centers:** Referencing a recent interview, Jared Isaacman said that when SpaceX states something can be done, it should not be doubted, specifically regarding building data centers in space; hosts add commentary that terrestrial data center siting is facing growing local resistance (citing an Amazon facility controversy in Gilroy, CA), which could favor orbital data center concepts.

## Market & Competition
1. **Costco supercharger gap noted:** Hosts observe Costco has not broadly partnered with Tesla for supercharging (with at least one location using Electrify America at higher prices) despite Costco's long dwell times being well suited to Supercharger use, contrasting with a new program allowing third parties to own and operate Superchargers.
