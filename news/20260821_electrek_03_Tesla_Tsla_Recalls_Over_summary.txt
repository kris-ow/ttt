Tesla is recalling 2,740,642 domestically built Model 3 and Model Y vehicles in China, produced between March 4, 2019 and December 7, 2025, to strengthen driver-attention monitoring on its assisted-driving system via an over-the-air software update.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Regulatory basis for the recall:** China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) found that when vehicles use Autosteer within "combination driving assistance" (China's term for Level 2 systems), Tesla's existing attention-monitoring mechanism is insufficient to alert drivers when their gaze leaves the road, increasing crash risk. The fix adds cabin-camera monitoring on top of existing steering-wheel torque monitoring, effective immediately via OTA update.

2. **Camera monitoring had been circumvented:** Tesla began using cabin-camera monitoring in China this year alongside its supervised "Tesla Assisted Driving" rollout (previously marketed as Full Self-Driving), but the article reports Chinese drivers found ways to defeat it, including mounting cheap plastic doll heads near the rearview mirror to fool the camera — one Model 3 owner reportedly drove 30 minutes eating sunflower seeds behind a figurine. Tesla had previously resisted activating in-vehicle cameras in China, stating in 2021 that China-market vehicles had not turned on their in-vehicle cameras, citing privacy and Chinese law.

3. **Parallel to a US precedent:** The article notes this mirrors NHTSA's December 2023 Autopilot recall of roughly 2 million US vehicles, which forced Tesla to add stronger camera-based driver-engagement safeguards in North America.

## Market & Competition
4. **Scale of China recalls:** Tesla filed two recalls in China the same day — this driver-monitoring recall (2,740,642 vehicles) and a separate, larger recall of nearly 3 million vehicles over an interior emergency door release regulators say is too hard to find after a crash. Combined, the two recalls total 5,716,552 vehicles, described by the article as Tesla's biggest recall total ever in its second-largest market.
