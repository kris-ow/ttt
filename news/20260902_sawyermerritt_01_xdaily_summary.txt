## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla pushes for EU-wide FSD approval with open-sourced safety data:** Tesla disclosed that its engineering test fleet has driven roughly 1 million cumulative miles on FSD (Supervised) across 22 European countries (UK, France, Sweden, Germany, Slovakia, Hungary, Spain, Italy, Belgium, Czechia, Austria, Switzerland, Croatia, Finland, Ireland, and others listed but cut off). A country-by-country mileage breakdown was shared: Germany 193,805 miles, Spain 166,936 miles, Netherlands 157,723 miles, France 89,444 miles, Italy 69,586 miles, UK 50,001 miles, Switzerland ~44,000+ miles (cut off). Separately, Tesla open-sourced key evidence — including data from over 230,000 structured tests (figure cut off) — used to support the Netherlands' approval of FSD (Supervised), arguing the safety benefit remains "locked behind bureaucracy" as it pushes for EU-wide approval.

2. **Waymo expands robotaxi footprint to three more US cities:** Starting September 1, Waymo began offering its first paid robotaxi rides to the general public in Denver, San Diego, and Tampa, bringing its total US coverage to 14 cities offering fully autonomous (driverless) paid rides — a notable competitive expansion relevant to Tesla's robotaxi ambitions.

3. **Model Y / Model Y L EPA range and demand updates:** The Model Y L Premium AWD received official EPA range ratings: 332 miles on base 19" wheels (up from an originally listed 325) and 328 miles on 20" wheels (up from an originally listed 320) — only a 4-mile difference between wheel sizes. Comparative EPA figures across variants: Model Y Premium AWD 19" — 327 miles; Model Y L Premium AWD 19" — 332 miles; Model Y Premium AWD 20" — 303 miles; Model Y L Premium AWD 20" — 328 miles, with the Y L's slightly larger battery narrowing the wheel-size range penalty. Separately, the Model Y L Launch Series is reported nearly sold out for 2026, with estimated delivery dates now pushing into early 2027 across all configurations — a bullish demand signal.

4. **Narrative shift on Cybercab/robotaxi criticism:** A widely engaged post (9.4K likes) argued that public criticism of Tesla's autonomy program has repeatedly moved goalposts — from "can't drive themselves" to "has a supervisor" to "has steering wheels" to now "only a few dozen Cybercabs" — framed as evidence that skepticism keeps shifting rather than being substantively resolved. A separate viral post/video (14.6K likes) commented on Cybercabs "taking over Austin," though no new fleet figures were given (per RobotaxiTracker.com, Austin's active fleet remains at a stale count of 135 as of 2026-08-07).

5. **Elon Musk comments on AI power crisis (G20 interview):** In a new G20 interview, Musk stated there is "a crisis of power" and warned of a "significant power shortfall next year," saying he tracks the AI topic via the X platform. This is tangential to Tesla/SpaceX equity theses but relevant context for Tesla's Energy/AI compute ambitions (Dojo, data centers) and broader AI infrastructure demand.
