## Executive Summary
Today's digest is dominated by the Cybercab's public launch in Austin — Tesla's first vehicle with no steering wheel or pedals to offer paid public rides — with @SawyerMerritt providing extensive first-hand ride footage, cost comparisons, and hardware/software details. Secondary items include a Model Y financing rate increase and a new SpaceX/Grok bot marketplace, but the Cybercab launch is by far the highest-signal news of the day for the robotaxi thesis.

---

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Cybercab opens to the public in Austin — a milestone launch:** Tesla began offering Cybercab rides to the general public starting at 5 PM CT on Friday (Sept 4), roughly 23 months after the vehicle's unveil, per Merritt's congratulatory post to Elon Musk, Ashok Elluswamy, and the Tesla AI/Robotaxi teams. Merritt says he took one of the world's first public rides, calling the cabin "comfortable and spacious," the suspension "soft," and FSD "VERY smooth." Within about 20 minutes of public rides going live, wait times jumped from 5-10 minutes to over an hour and prices increased, which Merritt frames as a demand signal ("Tons of people wanna try Cybercab!"). The launch event itself drew roughly 400 attendees, about half of them Tesla employees, including a keynote, demo displays, and rides.

2. **Extended ride experience and cost comparisons:** Merritt logged multiple rides totaling about 3 hours in the Cybercab and spent $92.51 total, which he says would have cost roughly $200 via Uber for the same travel time — his longest single ride was about 40 minutes (downtown Austin to North Austin), with FSD "handling everything perfectly" including navigation through a busy downtown area with hundreds of pedestrians. In a specific side-by-side comparison for one 15-minute ride: Uber cost $21 (with tip), a Tesla Model Y ride cost $16.99, and the Cybercab cost $9.65 — Merritt calculates this as 54% cheaper than the Uber fare. He states he has no major complaints about FSD V15 based on his experience so far.

3. **Cybercab hardware and engineering details:** Tesla disclosed several new Cybercab-specific engineering elements at the launch event: it's the first Tesla to use the "Supermanifold V3" thermal system, described as removing unnecessary refrigerant valves/lines and consolidating high- and low-voltage controllers, with a modular design enabling production that is 80% automated. The drive unit uses a simplified bar-wound stator and lubrication system enabling sub-10-second automated production cycle times, and is claimed to be 18% smaller and 25% lighter than other top-performing EV drive units. The Cybercab is also the first Tesla to use brake-by-wire with no brake fluid. Other features shown include a 22-inch entertainment screen (used for Netflix/music with beat-synced lighting), reclining seats, and a dedicated in-app arrival UI.

4. **Milestone: 1 million unsupervised robotaxi rider miles:** Tesla has officially crossed 1 million Unsupervised robotaxi rider miles, per Merritt — a cumulative fleet milestone rather than a single-city or single-vehicle figure.

5. **24/7 robotaxi network timing — Ashok Elluswamy comment:** Merritt posted a clip of Ashok Elluswamy discussing when Tesla's robotaxi network will be available 24/7, though no specific timeline was given in the post text itself.

6. **Model Y financing rate increase:** Tesla raised U.S. financing APR for Model Y RWD, AWD, and Premium trims from 0.99% to 1.49%; the Performance trim financing rate remains unchanged at 3.99%.

7. **SpaceX/Grok bot marketplace:** "SpaceXAI" (per the post's framing) launched a new Grok Bot template marketplace with categories spanning engineering, sales, product, operations, finance, and design — a minor corporate/AI tooling item with limited direct investor signal.
