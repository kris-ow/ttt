Hosts Herbert and Cern Basher take one of the first public Cybercab rides in Austin on the morning it opened to the general public, filming the in-cabin experience (interior lighting, seat reclining, entertainment apps, Grok assistant) and relaying details from conversations with Tesla engineers and executives — including Ashok Elluswamy — at the previous night's launch event.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab opens to the public in Austin:** The hosts hail one of the first Cybercabs available to the general public on a Friday morning, noting that as of filming, rides were still restricted to a private/early-access window with full public availability starting at 5:00pm Central that day.
2. **Software version and rollout plans:** A Tesla executive confirmed the Cybercab was running version 15 of the software (declining to specify further detail) and said, per Ashok Elluswamy, that this version would be distributed to the entire public "very soon."
3. **No steering wheel, no pedals, no rearview mirror:** The vehicle has no steering wheel or pedals, no rear window (replaced by carpeted storage space), and no rearview mirror; riders are told they are not supervising the drive at all, unlike supervised FSD in a personally-owned Tesla.
4. **Design trade-offs explained by Tesla engineer "Eric" (Robotaxi lead):** The touchscreen isn't wider or full-windshield-sized due to airbag deployment space requirements for safety certification; Tesla reportedly designed the Cybercab to stay close to a "normal car" configuration initially to ease self-certification, with a full-windshield display floated as a possible future direction.
5. **Reclining "lounge" seating and bench mechanics:** Seats can recline significantly (described by Herbert as being able to "lie down"), but the two front seats function as a connected bench — reclining one seat beyond a certain point forces the other to move as well.
6. **In-cabin comfort vs. suspension:** Cern Basher noted the ride was very quiet, aided by active noise cancellation, but said Tesla still needs to improve suspension to fully eliminate road feel, joking about Ashok Elluswamy's self-described "coffee cup test" — Ashok's stated goal that a coffee cup placed on the edge of the cabin should not spill.
7. **Automated interior cleaning "coming soon":** Engineers reportedly confirmed automated cabin cleaning is real and in development but gave no further detail or timeline; the single interior camera's ability to detect objects left in blind spots (e.g., the rear cargo area or seat edges) is untested — the hosts left cash in the cabin as an informal test.
8. **In-app/support features:** Riders can change interior ambient lighting color via the app, access movies/games/music (eight games available at time of filming), adjust cabin temperature (range cited as 66–81°F), and use a support-call button that accesses the cabin camera/microphone to connect with a Tesla support agent.
9. **Personalization and memory features:** Tesla plans (or already supports) personalized settings — seat position, temperature, music — that follow a rider across Cybercab trips, plus FSD's existing arrival-reminder feature, which Cern Basher said he uses to leave voice messages for his children and wife.
10. **Comparison to Waymo and Zoox:** The hosts and Cern Basher argued Cybercab's lack of a steering wheel differentiates it from Waymo and Zoox, both of which retain conventional driving controls; Basher noted Zoox operates only a small fleet (guessed at around 400 vehicles) in a limited, loop-style service area, while an unnamed Tesla engineer told him Zoox's maximum production capacity is 10,000 vehicles per year versus a Tesla target of 10,000 Cybercabs per week (an internal target relayed secondhand, not independently confirmed).
11. **Basher's fleet-scale vision:** Cern Basher said replacing vehicle ownership at mass scale would require roughly 30–50 million robotaxi-type vehicles nationally, calling current Waymo/Uber/Lyft competition "just the appetizer" compared to the goal of replacing car ownership outright.
12. **Waymo fleet size in Texas:** The hosts cited a figure of roughly 800–850 Waymo vehicles operating in Texas, attributed to something said at the previous night's event (unconfirmed independent figure, not from RobotaxiTracker.com).
13. **Starlink hardware confirmed, but no rider Wi-Fi access yet:** Grok (the in-car assistant) confirmed the Cybercab has Starlink hardware installed, but said it had no information on rider access to Starlink Wi-Fi, and the hosts were unable to connect their phones to it.
14. **Launch event described as employee-focused, light on new business details:** Cern Basher said the prior night's launch event felt more oriented toward celebrating Tesla employees than revealing new information about Cybercab economics or robotaxi fleet business plans, and said he'd have liked more detail on the business model; Elon Musk was reportedly expected to possibly attend but did not.
