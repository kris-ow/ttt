## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD 14.3 Early Rollout — Mixed Initial Reports:** FSD 14.3 is in very early rollout phases, with fewer than approximately 50 vehicles on the software based on trackers at the time of the video. Elon Musk described the update as "a banger" and stated it "nags less," with some users reporting going up to a minute without a nag prompt. However, early user feedback is mixed: Chris's wife reported phantom braking getting "out of hand" on 14.3, and DJ reported a critical disengagement within the first 30 seconds of the update (though he later noted performance improved after the rough start). Devon Olsen shared a video showing the car backing up unusually far during a destination park and hitting a curb, raising concerns about parking regressions — a concern echoed by Dirty Tesla's video. Mike P also reported continued left-lane camping in standard mode on 14.3.

2. **UI Changes in FSD 14.3:** The intervention feedback screen has been updated and now categorizes disengagements as navigation, parking, critical, or other. Users can reportedly dismiss this screen by double-clicking the voice activation button on the steering wheel. A new streak feature has been added to the UI, tracking how long a driver goes without disengaging FSD. Dillon Loomis expressed a preference for a total-miles-on-FSD leaderboard instead, arguing it would encourage safe engagement without penalizing necessary disengagements.

3. **Smart Summon Speed Increase:** The actually smart summon speed has been increased to 8 mph, up from 6 mph previously.

4. **FSD Regulatory Expansion — Israel:** In an interview at the Samson Smart Mobility Summit, Elon Musk said he is hoping FSD will receive regulatory approval in Israel sometime in 2026, "hopefully soon."

## Robotaxi
1. **Elon Musk on Robotaxi Deployment Timeline:** At the Samson Smart Mobility Summit, Elon Musk stated he expects widespread deployment of robotaxis in the US by the end of 2026. Dillon Loomis was notably skeptical, pointing out that Musk has been "a literal yo-yo" in his public statements about robotaxi rollout timelines, and cautioned viewers against putting too much stock in any specific prediction.

2. **Cybercab Spotted with License Plate in Houston:** A user named Sloak reported spotting a production-version Cybercab in Houston that now has a license plate. Dillon Loomis noted that the Texas Transportation Code requires all vehicles driven on public roads to have a license plate, framing it as a required — but not conclusive — step toward eventual Cybercab deployment. He cautioned against reading too much into the sighting.

3. **India Factory Plans Reportedly Abandoned — Implications for Cybercab Global Rollout:** A Chinese financial outlet reported that Tesla has decided to abandon plans to build a factory in India, citing shortcomings in India's domestic supply chain, a long-running negotiation deadlock over tariff sequencing, insufficient infrastructure, a mismatch between Tesla's high-end positioning and India's auto market buying power, and policy uncertainty. Dillon Loomis suggested this could be a negotiating tactic and speculated that once the Cybercab is in production and globally deployable, India could re-enter the conversation.

## Energy
1. **Tesla Solar Panel Factory in Houston (Brookshire):** Electric reported exclusively that Tesla is building a solar panel factory in Houston, specifically in Brookshire, co-located with the Megafactory Tesla is constructing at the same site. The Brookshire site consists of two buildings Tesla leases totaling 1.65 million square feet, with plans for an additional 600,000 square foot building, bringing the total to over 2 million square feet. The site is 100% leased to Tesla under a long-term agreement. The factory is described as a fully integrated facility, taking raw silicon inputs through ingots, wafers, and PV cells all the way to final panel assembly. Tesla is reportedly building "highly complex clean room-grade manufacturing environments" with more than $250 million in capex across multiple simultaneous facility builds. Chinese solar manufacturing equipment was reported to be shipping to Texas.

2. **Scale of Tesla's Solar Ambitions:** Tesla's existing solar capacity at Giga New York (Buffalo) is approximately 300 megawatts per year. Elon Musk has stated that both Tesla and SpaceX will each build out 100 gigawatts of solar manufacturing capacity in the years ahead — representing more than a 300x increase from the Buffalo facility. For context, First Solar, currently the largest domestic solar manufacturer in the US, is expecting under 18 gigawatts of capacity by 2027, meaning Tesla's target would be roughly six times that. The US solar industry installed 43 gigawatts in 2025, a 14% decrease from 2024; Tesla's 100-gigawatt target would be more than double the entire US installation capacity added in 2025.

3. **Capex Context and Solar Equipment Orders:** On the Q1 2026 earnings call, Tesla CFO Vaibhav Taneja stated Tesla's capex expectation for 2026 is over $25 billion, up from a prior $20 billion figure. He cited solar manufacturing equipment as part of the incremental $5 billion increase. Tesla was also reported earlier in 2026 to be in talks to buy $2.9 billion of Chinese solar manufacturing equipment. Dillon Loomis suggested the extra $5 billion in capex likely includes the Houston facility, though he noted it was not officially confirmed. Tesla also bundles Megapacks with utility-scale solar and Powerwalls with rooftop solar, making co-location of manufacturing operationally logical. Houston's access to a major port for equipment imports was cited as an advantage over Austin.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi Demand and Early Performance:** The New York Times published a positive article on the Tesla Semi, describing early reviews as suggesting it "could be a much-needed hit for Tesla." The owner of King Theot Trucking in California, which has ordered 20 Tesla Semis, described it as addressing key problems with current electric trucks — limited range, heavy weight, and high cost. California trucking firms have applied for state subsidies to purchase more than 1,200 Tesla Semis — more than all applications for other electric trucks combined since the incentive program began in 2019. A truck driver quoted noted the Semi is quieter than diesel rigs and allows air conditioning while waiting to unload, which is restricted for diesel trucks in California due to idling regulations. Diesel prices have reportedly risen approximately 50% since what the transcript refers to as "the war with Iran," making electric alternatives more attractive.

2. **Tesla Semi Operational Metrics:** In 2025, Tesla's early Semi fleet achieved over 95% uptime, and 75% of service visits were completed in under 24 hours.

3. **Competitor Responses to Tesla Semi:** The president of Volvo Trucks North America stated he does not think the Semi is disruptive and cited Volvo's planned longer-range model for next year and its service reliability track record. The president of Daimler Truck North America cited insufficient high-powered charging infrastructure as a barrier to electric truck practicality for long trips.

4. **Proposed EV Road Fee Legislation:** House lawmakers proposed bipartisan legislation requiring EV owners to pay a $130 annual fee for road maintenance (in lieu of fuel taxes), and $35 for plug-in hybrid owners. Starting in 2029, fees would rise $5 per year, up to a maximum of $150 for EVs and $50 for plug-in hybrids. These fees would be in addition to existing state registration fees. Dillon Loomis noted he personally pays $250 per EV in state fees where he lives, and flagged the fairness concern that low-mileage EV drivers would pay the same flat fee as high-mileage drivers.

He stated Cursor is an important piece of making Grok better. Dillon Loomis noted that Grok is already integrated into Tesla vehicles and will play a significant role in Optimus development, framing AI ecosystem developments as directly material to Tesla's future.
