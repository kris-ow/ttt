Tesla has launched a new Model 3 Premium RWD trim in Canada at $39,490 CAD (~$29,000 USD), a record-low price for the sedan in that market. The pricing is enabled by sourcing the vehicle from Giga Shanghai following a January 2026 tariff deal that reduced Canada's duty on Chinese-made EVs from 100% to 6.1%.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Canadian Model 3 lineup and pricing:** The Model 3 Premium RWD starts at $39,490 CAD, offering 463 km of range and a 4.2-second 0-100 km/h time. Tesla simultaneously cut the Model 3 Performance by 17% to $74,990 CAD (from $89,990), with 478 km of range and a 3.1-second sprint. The $35,500 gap between the two trims is nearly double the base price of the Premium RWD. Tesla removed the Model 3 Long Range AWD from the Canadian lineup entirely, which had been priced at $79,990 CAD. First deliveries are expected as early as May or June 2026.

2. **Supply chain background:** Canada previously received Shanghai-built Model 3s before imposing a 100% surtax on Chinese EVs in late 2024, forcing Tesla to source from Fremont. Canada's subsequent 25% counter-tariff on US-made vehicles pushed the Long Range AWD to $79,990 CAD, prompting Tesla to ship remaining US-built Canadian inventory back to the United States. The January 2026 Carney-Beijing tariff deal — reducing the Chinese EV duty to 6.1% under a 49,000-unit annual quota — cleared the way for the Shanghai return.

3. **Federal rebate exclusion:** Shanghai-built vehicles do not qualify for Canada's $5,000 EVAP rebate, which requires manufacturing in a free-trade-agreement country. Competing EVs like the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Chevrolet Equinox EV do qualify, narrowing the effective price advantage for the Tesla.

## Market & Competition
4. **US vs. Canada price gap confirms sourcing split:** At today's exchange rate of 1.357 CAD/USD, the Premium RWD is 31% cheaper in Canada than in the US ($42,490 USD), consistent with lower Giga Shanghai production costs and a minimal 6.1% tariff. The Performance is priced nearly identically in both markets when converted, strongly suggesting it remains Fremont-sourced with the 25% US counter-tariff offsetting potential savings. Tesla is the first automaker to bring product to Canada under the new tariff deal.

5. **Competitive landscape widening:** BYD is planning 20 Canadian dealerships within a year, with Toronto as its first market. The 49,000-unit annual import quota grows to 70,000 by 2030, with a requirement that half of imported vehicles cost under $35,000 CAD by that year. Tesla holds a first-mover advantage via existing retail, service, and Supercharger infrastructure in Canada.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert views the launch as genuinely significant, calling the $39,490 price competitive with mainstream ICE sedans and noting the irony that Canadian buyers are now getting a materially better deal on a Tesla than American consumers. He questions who would pay $35,500 more for the Performance when the Premium RWD offers nearly identical range.
