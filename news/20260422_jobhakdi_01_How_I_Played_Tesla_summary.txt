This video, published on Tesla earnings day (April 22, 2026) by Jo Bhakdi, lays out a pre-earnings analytical framework and options trading strategy for Tesla. Rather than focusing on granular EPS or delivery estimates, Jo Bhakdi argues that robotaxi trajectory and guidance are far more material to Tesla's valuation than near-term earnings beats or misses. He then explains why he is selling short puts into earnings, using Polymarket data and options pricing to argue that downside risk is already largely priced in.

## Financials
1. **Consensus EPS estimates and net income range:** The analyst consensus heading into earnings is 33 cents adjusted EPS (based on 20 analysts), with a broader Refinitiv/FactSet range of 36–37 cents. In absolute dollar terms, Jo Bhakdi translates this to a low-end estimate of approximately $1 billion in net profit for the quarter and a high-end estimate of approximately $1.3 billion. He notes that a miss of roughly 10% would represent a swing of approximately 4 cents per share — a number he argues is dwarfed in significance by robotaxi trajectory.

2. **Why EPS misses matter less than robotaxi math:** Jo Bhakdi presents a simplified framework to contextualize the earnings numbers relative to robotaxi economics. Assuming $60,000 in margin per robotaxi per year, a $1 billion quarterly net income figure is equivalent to the annualized earnings contribution of approximately 66,000 robotaxis. A 10% earnings miss — roughly $100 million — would be fully offset by just 1,600 robotaxis on a quarterly basis, or approximately 6,000 on an annualized basis. He uses this to argue that any upward revision to robotaxi fleet guidance would easily override even a meaningful EPS miss.

3. **Expectations for what Tesla will say on the call:** Jo Bhakdi anticipates Tesla will offer cautious, non-committal language on the robotaxi rollout — affirming that expansion into Dallas and Houston is underway, citing regulatory progress in the Netherlands as an important unlock for broader European deployment, and emphasizing a safety-first approach. He expects no concrete fleet size targets or timelines to be disclosed, consistent with Tesla's pattern over the prior four earnings calls.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi guidance as the dominant stock catalyst:** Jo Bhakdi argues that the market's reaction to earnings will be driven primarily by any changes in robotaxi deployment guidance rather than by the headline EPS number. He outlines a spectrum of hypothetical fleet ramp scenarios — from 6,000 to 12,000 to 40,000 to 200,000 vehicles — and asserts that any upward revision in these numbers would be a far more powerful stock catalyst than a beat on quarterly income. He characterizes the current rollout as slow but continuous, with recent "step changes" on unsupervised operations occurring on a low base.

2. **Known rollout milestones heading into earnings:** As of the video date, Jo Bhakdi references that unsupervised robotaxi operations have been added in Dallas and Houston, and that Tesla has received regulatory approval in the Netherlands, which he frames as a key domino for unlocking broader European deployment. He acknowledges the Cybercab is scaling but emphasizes the base remains small.

## Market & Competition
1. **Pre-earnings stock behavior and Polymarket signal:** Jo Bhakdi highlights that Tesla's stock underperformed its expected beta (approximately 2x the NASDAQ) in the day leading up to earnings, falling roughly $10 from around $400 on April 21, 2026, while the NASDAQ itself was not weak. He correlates this with a sharp drop on Polymarket's "Will Tesla beat quarterly earnings?" contract, which fell from 33% probability to approximately 18–20% in the same window. He interprets this as evidence of a likely information leak and treats the low beat-probability reading as a bullish setup for options positioning.

2. **Options strategy — short puts into earnings:** Based on his analysis, Jo Bhakdi describes selling short puts with a Friday expiration at strikes of 390, 385, 380, or as conservatively as 375, noting that the 375 strike was offering approximately $3–4 in premium, implying a breakeven near $371. He reasons that with only ~18% probability of a beat priced in, the market has already discounted a significant portion of any potential downside move. His base case is a flat-to-slightly-positive stock reaction (±2% or less), with tail risk skewed to the upside rather than the downside. He explicitly cautions beginners against excessive position sizing and emphasizes survivability if the stock were to fall to $350.
