Citing a Wall Street Journal report, the article says Tesla is exploring cutting loose its China business — via spinoff, sale, or wind-down — to remove an obstacle to a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger, though nothing is finalized and the timeline is unclear.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **China divestiture floated to clear merger path:** Per the WSJ, citing a person familiar with the talks, Tesla advisers have discussed options ranging from spinning off to selling or winding down Tesla's China operations. The article frames this as an attempt to remove a national-security obstacle to combining Tesla with SpaceX, since SpaceX is a major US defense/space contractor and Chinese ownership stakes in it are already under Washington scrutiny (senators have asked the Pentagon to review this). Tesla and SpaceX did not respond to requests for comment.

2. **Musk's mixed signals on the merger:** Musk has been raising the idea of a Tesla-SpaceX merger for months, citing "more and more overlap" between his companies, including a reference to "Terafab" on Tesla's Q2 earnings call last week. When pressed further on that call, he said combining companies "has got to be done with the appropriate process" and deferred the question to Tesla general counsel Brandon Ehrhart. The article also notes Tesla avoided shareholder questions about a potential SpaceX merger during that same call.

3. **Scale of Giga Shanghai:** The article notes Giga Shanghai produced Tesla's 4 millionth China-built car in December, and Tesla's China chief has said the plant accounts for more than half of Tesla's global deliveries; it also exports to Europe and much of Asia.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert is skeptical a sale would actually happen given Giga Shanghai's efficiency and profitability, but argues that if real, it would represent Musk fully pivoting Tesla away from being a car company toward an AI/robotics-and-SpaceX-xAI holding structure. He calls the prospective merger Musk's "fourth billion-dollar self-deal" with Musk on both sides of the transaction, and argues shareholders should question whether restructuring Tesla's manufacturing base to enable this deal serves their interests, and at what price.
