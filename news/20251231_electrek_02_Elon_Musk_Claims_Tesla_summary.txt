Electrek disputes Elon Musk's claim that the Tesla Model Y was the world's best-selling car for a third consecutive year in 2025, citing independent tracking data suggesting Toyota has reclaimed the top spot.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Musk's claim and its shaky basis:** Musk posted on X congratulating the Tesla team for the Model Y allegedly being the "world's best-selling car" for a third straight year. The article says this was clearly true in 2023 and debatably true in 2024 (a near-tie with the Toyota RAV4, decided by about 2,000 units), but argues it is likely false for 2025.

2. **Independent estimates for 2025:** Based on tracking data from multiple independent analysts covering Q1–Q3 2025 plus Q4 projections, the article reports estimated annual volumes of: Toyota RAV4 ~1.2 million units (+0.6% YoY), Toyota Corolla ~1.08 million units (-8.1% YoY), and Tesla Model Y ~1.03 million units (-12.7% YoY) — placing Model Y third. The article notes full validated global registration data won't be available for months.

3. **Tesla's reporting opacity:** The article criticizes Tesla for not breaking out Model Y deliveries separately from Model 3, reporting only a combined "Model 3/Y" figure each quarter, unlike other major automakers that report individual model sales. This opacity, the article argues, lets Musk make unverifiable claims that take analysts months to check.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's own take, plus an included reader comment, argue Musk is being misleading by making the claim while Tesla withholds the data that could prove or disprove it; the author adds that Model Y remaining competitive for a podium spot is still an impressive achievement, but accuracy matters and the evidence suggests Toyota has retaken the top spot.

Notable quotes and data:
- Toyota sold almost 500,000 RAV4 units in the US alone in 2025, per the article.
- Reader comment (top-voted, 35 likes): questions whether Musk is "delusional about the state of the car markets or knowingly lying to shareholders."
- Electrek's closing line: "the crown has passed back to Toyota for now."
