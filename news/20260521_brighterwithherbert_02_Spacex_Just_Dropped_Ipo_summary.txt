This video covers the SpaceX S1 IPO filing dropped on May 20, 2026, with Alexandra Merz (also known as "Tesla Boomer Mama") walking host Herbert and guest Jeff through her deep-dive analysis of the 308-page prospectus and linked documents. Her core thesis is that multiple structural elements embedded in the filing — from voting rights architecture to board composition to lockup schedules — appear deliberately designed to facilitate a future merger of equals between SpaceX and Tesla, with Elon Musk retaining majority voting control of the combined entity.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX Texas Reincorporation as the Legal Foundation:** SpaceX originally incorporated in Delaware in 2002 but reincorporated in Texas on February 14, 2024, effectively resetting its legal foundation. Alexandra Merz notes that Tesla similarly reincorporated in Texas in the summer of 2024, shortly after its shareholder meeting. She argues the Texas certificate of formation and its subsequent amendments — not the original Delaware documents — are the critical documents for understanding SpaceX's current governance structure.

2. **Dual-Class Share Structure and Elon's 85% Voting Control:** SpaceX has Class A shares (one vote per share, available to public investors) and Class B shares (10 votes per share, held by Elon Musk and a small group). This gives Elon approximately 85.1% of total voting rights. This structure has existed since at least February 2024 and likely throughout SpaceX's Delaware history, predating the IPO. Alexandra notes that early investors who were surprised by this figure should have known, as it was embedded in founding documents accessible to accredited investors.

3. **Merger Veto Clause — A Key Structural Breadcrumb:** The certificate of formation includes a dual-condition requirement for any merger to pass: first, a combined vote of all share classes must approve it; second, the Class B shares alone must independently approve it as a separate condition. Alexandra argues this is a deliberate protection ensuring Elon retains a decisive "thumb on the scale" even if future dilution brings his combined-entity voting share below 50%. She calculates that a merger of equals between SpaceX (Elon at 85% voting) and Tesla (Elon at approximately 20.3% voting) would yield roughly 52.5% combined voting control for Elon — still a majority but much closer to the threshold where dilution becomes a risk.

4. **Controlled Company Status — When the Language First Appeared:** SpaceX qualifies as a "controlled company" under NASDAQ rules because a single person (Elon Musk) holds more than 50% of voting power. This status exempts SpaceX from requirements such as a majority of independent directors or a fully independent nominating/governance committee. Crucially, Alexandra notes this "controlled company" language did not appear in any 2024 or early 2026 filings, suggesting the IPO decision crystallized in January or February 2026. She compares this to Meta (Zuckerberg at 57–60%), Alphabet (Page and Brin at 51–53%), and Snap (Spiegel and Murphy at ~60%), all of which established dual-class structures at their IPOs — something Tesla did not do at its 2010 IPO, which Alexandra characterizes as a missed opportunity.

5. **Board Composition and the Ira Ehrenpreis Signal:** SpaceX had six board members as of February 2024 and has since expanded to eight, with a ninth position to be filled within one year. The only board member overlapping between Tesla and SpaceX (other than Elon) is Ira Ehrenpreis, who chairs SpaceX's Compensation and Nominating Committee — the same role he played in structuring Tesla's performance awards for Elon. Alexandra interprets his presence on both boards as a meaningful breadcrumb suggesting he may be positioned to draft a compensation plan for a combined entity. Gwen Shotwell is also on the SpaceX board. Antonio Garcia (of Valor Partners) is the only non-employee, non-Elon board member classified as non-independent, due to his firm's significant SpaceX shareholding.

6. **Share Class Amendment History as an IPO Timeline Indicator:** Alexandra traced all amendments to SpaceX's Texas certificate of formation from February 2024 through May 2026. Key milestones: (1) Initial Texas filing, February 14, 2024 — legal move only; (2) First amendment — authorized Class B shares increased from 1 billion to 1.2 billion; (3) A new Class D common stock was introduced, likely for employees; (4) On May 4, 2026 (morning) — preferred shareholders given the option to convert to common stock ahead of IPO; (5) On May 4, 2026 (evening) — a 5-for-1 stock split of Classes A, B, and C, with Class C subsequently closed. SpaceX also filed a DBA under the name "Adustra." Elon is known to have purchased secondary shares from employees and early investors over this period, which Alexandra argues makes sense only if he anticipated dilution from a future merger.

7. **Shareholder Litigation Rights Severely Restricted in New Bylaws:** The IPO bylaws (linked within the S1 and considered filed) include three provisions that Alexandra says make suing the company extremely difficult: (1) all disputes must first go to private arbitration in Houston; (2) all shareholders irrevocably waive jury trials; and (3) class actions and collective lawsuits are explicitly banned. Combined with a Texas law requiring 3% of outstanding shares to bring a derivative suit — and only 4.3% of shares being floated — Alexandra notes that organizing any meaningful legal challenge would be nearly impossible. She acknowledges that a lawyer contact (Warren) believes some of these provisions may not survive legal challenge.

8. **Amended and Restated Investor Rights Agreement (IRA) — No Preferential Exits:** The S1 links to an amended and restated investor rights agreement covering early investors (series up through Series N, last updated August 2020). Alexandra reviewed it and concluded that no early investor class has preferential or accelerated lockup rights. All large investors are subject to the same lockup schedule. Elon himself, when approached by investors wanting to exit early, has historically purchased those secondary shares himself rather than letting them hit the open market.

## Financials

1. **IPO Valuation, Float Size, and Offering Amount:** The IPO is rumored to be priced at a total market cap of approximately $1.75 trillion (with some rumors suggesting $2.25 trillion). The offering raises approximately $75 billion, representing only 4.3% of the total market cap — an extremely small public float. Alexandra notes $75 billion would be more than double the size of previous record IPOs. If the market cap is actually $2.25 trillion, the float percentage would be even smaller than 4.3%.

2. **Lockup Period Structure — Staggered Selling Windows:** Directors and executive officers face a 180-day lockup. Elon Musk faces a stricter 366-day lockup from the date of the prospectus, meaning he cannot sell until approximately June or July 2027. For all other major shareholders (e.g., ARK Invest, Valor Partners), a staggered selling schedule applies: after Q2 earnings (approximately July 20–25, 2026), they may sell up to 20% of holdings (or 30% if the stock is 30% above IPO price); on approximately August 21 (day 70), another 7%; approximately September 10, another 7%; approximately September 25, another 7%; after Q3 earnings, another 28%; in October, another 7%; with all remaining shares unlockable by December 9, 2026. Cathie Wood of ARK Invest has publicly stated ARK intends to sell a portion of its SpaceX holdings given the scale of expected profits. Goldman Sachs is the lead manager overseeing lockup compliance.

3. **Index Inclusion Timeline and Buying Pressure:** Within the first 15 trading days after the June 12, 2026 IPO (approximately by July 6–7, 2026), SpaceX is expected to qualify for inclusion in the NASDAQ 100, FTSE Russell, and CRSP indices under their respective rules. All three are float-adjusted, meaning their initial purchases will be limited by the 4.3% float — Alexandra estimates these three indices will each need to purchase approximately $10 billion worth of shares (roughly $30 billion combined), but the constrained float means they may not be able to acquire their full target allocation immediately, creating upward price pressure. These indices will conduct quarterly rebalancing in September 2026, at which point the expanded float (from staggered seller windows in July–September) will allow larger purchases. The S&P 500 currently requires 12 months post-IPO for inclusion, but the S&P is conducting a consultation on new rules that would reduce the waiting period to 6 months, eliminate the 10% minimum float requirement, and remove the profitability requirement. If approved (a decision expected on June 8, 2026 — four days before the IPO), SpaceX could be eligible for S&P 500 inclusion as early as December 2026. Alexandra's S&P inclusion purchase estimates by float size: at 5% float, ~$19 billion; at 10% float, ~$39 billion; at 20% float, ~$77 billion; at 40–50% float, proportionally higher. She describes a potential S&P inclusion as "the tightest S&P inclusion ever."

4. **Merger of Equals Scenario — Potential Impact on Tesla:** Alexandra's working hypothesis is a "merger of equals" in which SpaceX and Tesla combine at equivalent market caps. Under this scenario, Tesla shareholders would see the Tesla ticker eventually replaced by SpaceX's ticker, and the S&P 500 would replace Tesla's weighting with SpaceX rather than executing a new inclusion. Alexandra estimates Tesla is currently trading around $420 and suggests a merger announcement could push it to approximately $650 in a matter of days. She models the Tesla shareholder vote as requiring approval from 50% of approximately 3.75 billion outstanding shares, which she characterizes as "an uphill battle." On timing, she speculates the merger announcement could come before SpaceX's Q2 2026 earnings call (around July 2026), with the actual merger closing most likely in Q2 2027 (with Q1 2027 being ambitious and end of 2026 being unlikely).

5. **IPO Access for Retail Investors:** Five lead underwriters are named: Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citigroup, and JP Morgan. Retail access is being provided through Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, and E*Trade (by Morgan Stanley). Some platforms require minimum account balances (e.g., $100,000 or $500,000 depending on the institution). The IPO is reported to be multiple times oversubscribed. Alexandra notes a critical window in early June: money must be deposited by approximately June 4, and final allocation requests must be submitted by approximately June 10.
