## Robotaxi

- **Texas Level 4 self-certification now official.** Effective May 28, Texas's new driverless vehicle law authorizes Tesla to operate commercially; 42 VINs are listed on the Texas Motor Carrier registry. Waymo has 619 VINs listed in the same registry; AVID has 317.
- **First Cybercab autonomously exits Giga Texas.** Elon Musk posted video of the first Cybercab driving itself out of the factory and parking unassisted. Ashok Elluswami stated "soon it will drive itself into Austin and report for duty." Joe Tegtmeyer documented Cybercabs making autonomous two-mile trips within the Giga Texas lot; dozens are visible staged with hatch covers open.
- **Cybercab testing reaches Pennsylvania.** Three Cybercabs were spotted at the Bridgeville Tesla showroom south of Pittsburgh — the first confirmed testing in the state — expanding the pre-deployment validation footprint beyond prior known locations.
- **Houston and Dallas fleets contracting.** On-the-ground reporters note Houston's operational fleet has dropped from six vehicles to two and has held there for several weeks. In Dallas, riders reported "no rides available nearby" on two consecutive days. Both Bhakdi and Dillon Loomis tie the unsupervised fleet plateau — no new driverless additions since May 9 per community trackers — to an anticipated software release.
- **Tesla hiring fleet supervisors in six cities.** New postings for a robotaxi fleet support supervisor role cover Palo Alto, Tampa, San Antonio, Orlando, San Francisco, and Miami. Three of those cities — Miami, Orlando, Tampa — align with Tesla's reported target list for service launch by end of June 2026.
- **Dallas Grand Prairie facility confirmed for autonomous vehicle ops.** A court document reveals a 51,000 sq ft Tesla facility under construction in Grand Prairie, TX (between Dallas and Fort Worth) with an operational plan explicitly citing charging, cleaning, repair, and maintenance of Tesla-owned autonomous vehicles. It will not be ready for the World Cup.
- **H1 2026 multi-city deadline: ~30 days remaining.** Per Bhakdi, Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings deck committed — including from the CFO — to unlocking multiple cities before H2 2026. Bhakdi predicts "multiple dozens" of unsupervised Cybercabs across multiple cities in June, citing Cybercab sightings in Dallas and the World Cup opener in Dallas on June 14 as a logical deployment window.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD daily miles: 26.7M/day, up from 18.5M in April.** Morgan Stanley's new research note cites this figure and flags FSD's ~90% gross margins as a meaningful profitability lever. The note also references normalized NHTSA data implying improvement in robotaxi miles per accident and increased AI safety operator hiring in northern metro areas.
- **World's first autonomous coast-to-coast drive across Canada completed.** David Moss, Devin Olsen, and Scott drove 3,700 miles from Horseshoe Bay, BC to Nova Scotia over 4 days and 21 hours on FSD v14.3 with zero disengagements, including parking. This is the fourth coast-to-coast autonomous drive in five months, totaling 12,427 miles across all four trips in unmodified production vehicles.
- **48-state FSD drive completed.** Joe Anderson drove all 48 contiguous US states plus DC on FSD v14.3.2 — over 7,200 miles across 9 days — with FSD driving every mile. Tesla's official account congratulated him.
- **Estonia approves supervised FSD — third EU country.** Approval is effective May 29, following the Netherlands and Lithuania. Belgium's Minister of Transport stated approval is expected "in the coming weeks" after completing the required 5,000 km of testing. Tesla is hiring vehicle operators in Slovenia and Austria, signaling those countries may be next. Jeff Lutz characterizes the EU regulatory process as largely a "paperwork loop" now that the Netherlands approval provides a template.
- **FSD v14 complacency risk flagged by Electrek.** Fred Lambert reports traveling hundreds of miles without needing to intervene after weeks of daily v14 use, and invokes the "vigilance decrement" effect — research showing drivers need 5–8 seconds to mentally reengage after a handover request. He cites the crash of Raffi Krikorian (former head of Uber's self-driving division) as a real-world consequence. He also criticizes Tesla marketing calling FSD "your personal chauffeur" and Musk's confirmation that texting while using FSD is acceptable "depending on context" — both of which Lambert argues actively worsen the complacency problem.
- **FSD supervised miles surpass 10 billion.** The fleet is now accumulating approximately 30 million miles per day.
- **Tesla Semi described as "robo trucking ready."** Dan Priestley (cited by Cern Basher) describes the Semi as autonomous-ready. Supervised platooning — a human-led convoy reducing driver headcount — is discussed as a near-term bridge before full autonomy validation is complete.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Terafab land acquisition: 3,100+ acres in Grimes County, Texas.** SE Robinson reported that Witt LLC — sharing the physical address of xAI in Palo Alto, with xAI general counsel James Burnham signing — is acquiring the land near Gibbons Creek Reservoir, roughly two hours from Giga Texas. SpaceX is listed as the property owner for a tax abatement session; a public hearing is scheduled June 3. The project is described as a "multi-phase next-gen vertically integrated semiconductor manufacturing and advanced computing fab facility" with $55B initial phase capex and up to $119B total investment if all phases are built.
- **Figure AI's live warehouse robots cited as the sector's inflection point.** Figure's three Figure 3 robots (Bob, Frank, and Gary) have been running a publicly live-streamed, continuous warehouse mission in California since May 14 — sorting packages at human-parity speed, autonomously swapping for battery charges, and operating end-to-end via neural network without teleoperation. The runtime counter had surpassed 60 hours at time of recording. Per Brett Adcock, Figure scaled from one robot per day to one robot per hour in approximately four months.
- **Actuator cost collapse enabling humanoid economics.** High-torque actuator costs have fallen from $2,000–$5,000 each in 2020 to $500–$2,000 today at low volume. Morgan Stanley projects costs of $100–$300 each at Tesla's target scale of one million units per year. Tesla is targeting a $20,000 Optimus price point at that volume, with one million units per year as the goal around 2030.
- **Tesla Optimus: production recorded in March; 3x target by May.** Tesla stated a goal to triple March production by May 2026. Internal pilots continue at Fremont and Giga Texas.

## Electric Vehicles

- **European Tesla sales surge in May.** France +655%, Spain +113%, Denmark +136% (Model Y was Denmark's best-selling vehicle in May), Sweden +71%, Norway +29%, Portugal +350% YoY. Jeff Lutz attributes the spike to new Model Y inventory availability, easing brand headwinds, high European gasoline prices, and strong net promoter score word-of-mouth. Giga Berlin is expanding output capacity.
- **Tesla Semi: first production unit off line in April 2026.** Nine years after the 2017 announcement, the first Semi came off the production line. The hub-and-spoke charging model — where fleet operators charge at their own depots overnight — reduces Tesla's near-term infrastructure capex. Cost savings are cited at approximately 35 cents per mile versus diesel. One company reportedly ordered 340 Semis since production began. The Covenant logistics two-week real-world test on California's Grapevine route drew a "amazed" driver assessment.
- **Camouflaged Model Y L spotted testing at Fremont.** Jeff Lutz, drawing on supply chain expertise, describes US launch as "imminent" in H2 2026, noting that even though the vehicle is already built elsewhere, domestic production requires supplier requalification.
- **Tesla Canada Chinese-EV quota: ~12% used.** Approximately 2,900 Chinese-built EVs (nearly all Model 3 from Giga Shanghai) have been imported under the new Canadian quota framework; the current window allows up to 24,500 units through August 31, 2026.

## Market & Competition

- **BYD assumes full, uncapped liability for God's Eye crashes.** Announced May 28, BYD will cover all direct economic losses — repairs, third-party property damage, and personal injury — when its urban navigate-on-autopilot is active and used in compliance with regulations. No payout cap; claims will not raise commercial insurance premiums. Coverage applies to God's Eye A and B systems for one year from delivery; existing owners receive it via OTA update to God's Eye 5.0. God's Eye B is a one-time 12,000 yuan (~$1,770) option versus Tesla Assisted Driving in China at 64,000 yuan (~$9,400). BYD's fleet stands at 3.15 million assisted-driving vehicles generating up to 200 million km of driving data per day. A comparable parking guarantee in July 2025 drove feature usage from 21% to 93%.
- **SpaceX S-1 amendment hints at strategic acquisitions.** New language added ahead of the planned June 12 IPO states SpaceX "may issue a significant amount of equity in connection with future transactions" (plural). Bert and Jeff Lutz read this as a potential signal of a Tesla acquisition or merger, since the Cursor deal was already public when the original S-1 was filed. Elon Musk agreed to a 366-day lockup on all SpaceX shares; 5% of IPO shares are reserved for employees, friends, and family of executive officers with no lockup.
- **xAI proprietary training stack claimed to be ~10x faster than Google's Jax.** SpaceX is nearly finished with an in-house AI training stack written in C, designed for a cluster of ~220,000 Nvidia GB300s. The claimed performance advantage for large training runs is approximately one order of magnitude versus Google's Jax. The stack is confirmed for use in Grok v5. Colossus 2 currently has seven models in simultaneous training: two at 1T parameters, two at 1.5T, one at 6T, and one at 10T.
- **Anthropic–SpaceX Colossus lease: 180-day term, mutual 90-day cancellation.** Musk clarified the structure is short-term at SpaceX's own request. "If compute gets super tight, we might need it back at some point."
- **Waymo Zeekr robotaxi economics challenged.** The host of FutureAzA estimates the Zeekr base vehicle at ~$35,000 becomes ~$70,000 after the 100% import tariff, arguing the Cybercab's cost structure and lower cleaning/energy requirements make it structurally superior on unit economics.
