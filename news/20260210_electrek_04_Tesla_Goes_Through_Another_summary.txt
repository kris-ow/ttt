Electrek reports that Tesla VP Raj Jegannathan, who took over leadership of North American sales after Troy Jones was dismissed, is departing the company after 13 years, marking the second North American sales leadership change in under a year.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Jegannathan's departure and background:** Jegannathan announced his exit via a LinkedIn post, citing 13 years at Tesla spanning IT, AI infrastructure, security, sales, and service. His official title was VP of Information Technology, AI Infrastructure, Business Apps, and Information Security; he was given additional responsibility for North American sales after Troy Jones was dismissed in July 2025.

2. **Pattern of sales/service leadership exits:** The article lists recent departures: Troy Jones (Head of Sales, North America, July 2025), Omead Afshar (VP/Head of Sales and Manufacturing, North America and Europe, June 2025), and Tesla's head of service in North America (August 2025). The author frames this as part of a broader talent exodus since Tesla's April 2024 layoffs, which cut over 10% of the workforce.

3. **Revenue and demand context:** Tesla's annual revenue declined 3% in 2025, described as the first year-over-year drop on record, which the article attributes to an aging vehicle lineup and, per the author, consumer backlash tied to Elon Musk's political activities.

4. **Recent uptick in departures:** The author states they have noticed an increase in departures at Tesla in the past week, following revelations about Elon Musk and Kimball Musk's relationship with Jeffrey Epstein — though no specific names or numbers beyond Jegannathan are tied to this claim.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the revolving door in NA sales leadership is a bad sign given Tesla's demand problems. He speculates that appointing an IT/AI-infrastructure executive (Jegannathan) to lead sales suggests either a lack of qualified internal candidates or a belief that sales issues are operational rather than strategic. He also recalls believing Musk was expected to personally take over sales leadership after Afshar's departure but thought it unrealistic given Musk's schedule, and speculates Jegannathan may have been filling in temporarily before finding the role too demanding alongside his other duties.
