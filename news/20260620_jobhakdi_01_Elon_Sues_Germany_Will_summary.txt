This video, published by Jo Bhakdi on 2026-06-20, covers Elon Musk's lawsuit against the German public broadcaster ZDF (Zweites Deutsches Fernsehen) for alleged defamation. The core argument is that ZDF falsely and directly stated that Elon Musk called upon a mob to hunt down foreigners in Ireland, and that this lawsuit is both legally justified and symbolically important as a challenge to what Bhakdi characterizes as a state-controlled propaganda media infrastructure in Germany.

## Market & Competition

1. **The ZDF Defamation Claim:** Bhakdi describes a ZDF broadcast in which a presenter, discussing violent unrest in Ireland following an attack by a migrant on an Irish man, stated that Elon Musk "called upon" a mob to hunt down foreigners. Bhakdi argues this was not imprecise language or a misunderstanding — as ZDF later claimed in a public statement — but a direct, fabricated lie constituting defamation. Bhakdi states he reposted the clip on X, tagged Elon Musk, and that Musk responded within approximately an hour or a few hours, indicating he would take action.

2. **Elon Musk's Legal Response and Initial Victory:** Musk hired a German lawyer named Joachim Steinhöwel (referred to by Bhakdi as "Steinhov" or "Steinhur"), described as experienced with this type of case. Bhakdi reports that the lawyer has already achieved a first legal victory, specifically a successful cease and desist undertaking, which the lawyer publicly labeled a "great victory." The lawyer is quoted as saying the defamation was "journalistically completely unjustifiable" and that he intends to investigate every instance of alleged defamation of Elon Musk across German media — including both public broadcasters ARD and ZDF and private outlets — over the past approximately three years, subject to statute of limitations.

3. **Scale and Funding of German Public Broadcasting:** Bhakdi contextualizes the lawsuit by describing the scale of Germany's state-linked public broadcasting system. ZDF holds approximately 15.3% of the total German television market share, while ARD holds approximately 13%, giving the two government-affiliated broadcasters a combined roughly 28% — nearly a third — of the entire German TV media market. Together, they are funded by a mandatory fee of approximately €18 per month per household, generating over €10 billion per year, which citizens are compelled to pay under penalty of law. Bhakdi argues this creates a structurally uncompetitive media environment that distorts the broader media market.

4. **Political Bias in Public Broadcasting Talk Shows:** Bhakdi presents a chart showing the share of political talk show appearances on ARD and ZDF by party affiliation in 2025. The CDU received approximately 40% of appearances despite holding 33% of Bundestag seats. The AFD, which holds 24% of Bundestag seats and leads in current polling at approximately 30%, received only 2.8% of talk show appearances. Bhakdi argues this disproportionality demonstrates explicit political bias in the state-funded broadcasters, whose governance boards he says are controlled by CDU and SPD representatives.

5. **Public Trust in Government Broadcasters:** Bhakdi cites a survey from the Mediathek Future Index Germany conducted in August (year unspecified in transcript) of 3,250 representative respondents aged 16 and older. Only 31% overall expressed trust in the public broadcasting programs. Among those aged 16–29, only approximately 25% (one in four) said they trust these outlets. Trust levels by party affiliation were: SPD voters 49%, CDU voters 40%, Greens voters 38%, and AFD voters 15%.

6. **Broader Political and Cultural Context:** Bhakdi frames the lawsuit as significant beyond its legal outcome, arguing it represents an external challenge to a media system that he believes suppresses political opposition and manufactures public opinion. He expresses particular confidence in East German populations as being more resistant to propaganda given their lived experience under the communist GDR regime, and attributes the AFD's stronger polling in eastern Germany partly to this historical awareness. He also credits X (formerly Twitter) with enabling broader alternative political discourse in Germany, noting a growing trend of German-speaking influencers posting in English to reach international audiences.
