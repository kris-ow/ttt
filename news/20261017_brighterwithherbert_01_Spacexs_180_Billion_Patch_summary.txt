Host Herbert and guest Cern Basher (certified financial analyst, Brilliant Advice) discuss SpaceX's newly confirmed data center site near Brownsville, Texas — a 444-acre plot recently removed from Brownsville city limits (avoiding the city's proposed moratorium on data centers) — and Basher's projections for its revenue potential, framing SpaceX's "mini-hard" data center design as a repeatable, high-margin product with major implications for the Tesla/SpaceX investment thesis given reported merger speculation.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **New Texas data center site confirmed:** SpaceX purchased a 444-acre plot outside Brownsville, Texas in late August, which was removed from the city's jurisdiction — timing that Basher and Herbert say let SpaceX sidestep a data-center moratorium Brownsville was considering. Basher refers to this site informally as "Colossus 3" (not confirmed as an official name), distinct from existing Colossus 1/2 locations in the Memphis, Tennessee and northern Mississippi area.
2. **Construction evidence points to "Mini Hard"-style buildings:** Aerial imagery from RGV Aerial Photography (cited by Basher) shows one building already under construction and a second foundation being laid, both resembling the "Mini Hard" data center design used at the Colossus 2 site (alongside "Macro Hard" and "Macro Harder" buildings). Evidence cited for the data center identification: a windowless, dockless two-story structure (compute on top, cooling below), roofing materials stockpiled for multiple buildings, and roughly 400 MW of power already installed on site (which Basher says has likely risen above 500 MW since the photos were taken) — power levels Basher says would be unnecessary for a warehouse.
3. **Power infrastructure detailed:** The site has Mitsubishi Power FT8 mobile gas turbine units and GE Vernova TM2500 aviation gas turbines (each turbine ~37 MW, installable in about 14 days, water-free operation) plus Megapacks visible nearby. A new natural gas pipeline is being laid specifically to this 444-acre site, separate from the existing pipeline serving Starbase. Basher notes SpaceX (not Elon Musk personally, despite media reports) acquired a Jacksonville, Florida-based power systems company for roughly $1 billion earlier this year; equipment from that company is visible on site.
4. **Revenue projections (explicitly framed as theoretical):** Basher estimates each "Mini Hard"-style building can house about 220,000 Nvidia GPUs, which at current premium AI compute pricing could generate roughly $22 billion/year in revenue using Grace Blackwell-class chips, or potentially up to ~$30 billion/year if upgraded to Nvidia Vera Rubin systems (per SemiAnalysis estimates cited by Basher, which project ~40% more revenue from Vera Rubin-equipped centers). Vera Rubin systems are expected to arrive around the first quarter of 2027. A conservative estimate of six such buildings fitting the 444-acre site could generate $130–180 billion/year in aggregate; Basher says the site could potentially fit up to ten buildings, and that SpaceX owns additional adjacent land (including acreage north of the Brownsville port) that could host considerably more — he floated a figure as high as 20+ data centers across the broader landholding, but stressed this is speculative.
5. **Build timeline and cadence:** Basher estimates each Mini Hard-style building takes roughly 5–6 months to construct; with construction starting around September, he estimates the first Texas building could be ready around March, with a second following roughly two months later. He is skeptical that the October/November/December-timed launches Musk has mentioned apply to this specific Texas site, believing those likely refer to additional Memphis-area buildings, with this Texas building more likely to go live in 2027.
6. **GDP growth projections:** Basher cites a recent ARK Invest forecast (to which Musk reportedly responded he'd be "disappointed" if that were the ceiling) estimating SpaceX's combined ground and orbital compute buildout. Applying economics Musk and Nvidia's Jensen Huang discussed at a Washington D.C. AI summit, Basher calculates SpaceX's data center buildout alone could add roughly 40 basis points to GDP this year (at ~2.1 gigawatts of compute), rising to an estimated 1.4% of GDP next year if SpaceX reaches roughly 7.5–10 gigawatts, and an estimated 2.5% by 2028 at ~13 gigawatts — figures Basher describes as his own projections based on ARK's numbers, not confirmed SpaceX guidance.

## Financials
1. **Comparison to Tesla's scale:** Basher notes that three "Mini Hard"-style data centers generating roughly $30 billion/year each would together approximate $100 billion — comparable to Tesla's current total annual revenue — while Herbert notes a single 444-acre data center cluster could out-earn Tesla's entire global manufacturing and sales operation.

2. **$100 trillion SpaceX valuation reference:** Basher notes that Musk recently discussed a $100 trillion valuation for SpaceX, framed as a multiple of GDP contribution, in the context of this data center buildout discussion.
