This live panel discussion hosted by "brighterwithherbert" features James from Invest Answers, Cern Basher, and Jeff Lutz covering a wide range of Tesla news and thesis items, including stock technicals, Elon Musk's China visit, Cybercab production sightings, a Las Vegas robotaxi maintenance facility permit, Tesla's evolving profit mix across business lines, the SpaceX IPO implications, a Piper Sandler analyst report, and the state of Optimus and humanoid robot competition. The core argument is that Tesla is transitioning from a car company into a multi-line technology business, and that analysts and investors are only beginning to price in the non-automotive revenue streams. The panel is broadly bullish and believes multiple catalysts — FSD approval in China, robotaxi expansion, Cybercab production ramp, and eventual Optimus deployment — are converging in the near term.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Stock Surge and Technical Setup:** James described Tesla climbing $105 in 30 days, reaching near the $450 resistance level before pulling back. The 200-day moving average sits around $410 and the stock is cleanly above it. The long-term support/resistance line around $330 provided a clean bounce. James predicted a new all-time high within 3 to 6 months, while Jeff and Cern Basher suggested it could happen more quickly depending on news catalysts, particularly out of China.

2. **FSD and Word-of-Mouth Driving Car Sales:** Jeff argued that FSD is increasingly driving car sales through word of mouth in the US, and that rising oil prices (with California at $7 per gallon cited) are pushing consumers toward EVs. He also noted that Panasonic has signaled a capacity increase specifically naming Tesla as the demand source — a meaningful signal given that Tesla has been stagnant or slightly down on total units for the past couple of years, meaning any net demand increase is significant.

3. **Cybercab Sightings at Giga Texas:** Approximately 70 Cybercabs were spotted in the outbound lot at Giga Texas, up from previous sightings of 30–40 units. Joe Tegtmeyer confirmed that most appear to be the same units observed the prior day, meaning they sit for multiple days before being moved — so the 70 figure does not necessarily represent a single day's output. The panel interpreted this as evidence of a ramping production line. Large numbers of Model Ys and Cybertrucks were also visible in the same lot.

4. **Cybercab Production Pacing and Deployment Strategy:** Jeff explained his view that Tesla is calibrating Cybercab production relative to robotaxi deployment readiness rather than rushing to peak production. He suggested Tesla may be building and staging Cybercabs across its 39 tested cities to conduct vehicle-level testing on a new form factor, and that the fleet may need substantial supervised FSD miles before unsupervised deployment scales. The panel noted that there is no point ramping Cybercab production sharply if the software isn't ready to deploy them commercially at scale.

## Financials

1. **Q1 2026 Profit Breakdown — Tesla as a Multi-Line Business:** James presented a profit attribution chart showing Tesla generated approximately $63 million in profit per day in Q1 2026. Of that, roughly 62.8% came from automotive (car sales), 17.5% from energy storage, approximately 6.9% from FSD subscriptions (coincidentally equal to regulatory credit revenue), and a small but growing slice from robotaxi/internet/services/charging. The panel emphasized this is a profit chart, not revenue, explaining why high-margin lines like FSD subscriptions and regulatory credits punch above their weight.

2. **FSD Subscriptions as a Recurring High-Margin Revenue Stream:** The panel highlighted that FSD subscriptions representing close to 7% of Q1 profit was higher than expected. Cern Basher noted that some one-time FSD sales and deferred FSD revenue recognition may have contributed, but argued the more important story is the expanding base of recurring FSD subscriptions. The flywheel effect was discussed: FSD drives car sales, which drives charging revenue, which feeds the broader ecosystem.

3. **Revenue Timeline for New Business Lines:** James presented a slide estimating when each new Tesla business line becomes "significant" (defined as roughly 3–5% of revenue, or approximately $3–5 billion). His sandbagged timeline: Tesla Semi sales already ramping with close to 1,000 orders at approximately $300,000 per unit (with some states offering up to $150,000 in tax credits); FSD significant by early 2027; robotaxi significant toward end of 2028; humanoid robots significant by late 2028; software and services/Digital Optimus by late 2029; in-car inference around 2030. Cern Basher pushed back, suggesting robotaxi could move sooner given Cybercab production ramp, and that Digital Optimus (described as a combination of Tesla FSD and Grok) could be massive by 2027 since it has no manufacturing constraints.

4. **Piper Sandler / Alex Potter Report:** Alex Potter of Piper Sandler released a detailed note arguing that at $400/share (the price at time of writing), Tesla shareholders are effectively getting Optimus for free — it is not priced in at all. His price target is $500. His model includes FSD licensing, automaker hardware purchases, and robotaxi revenue, but with dates pushed to 2030 and beyond. Jeff flagged several specifics from the report: FSD subscription revenue modeled at $900 million in 2027, growing to $1.8 billion in 2028, $4.1 billion shortly after, and more than doubling repeatedly through 2031. Megapack revenue was modeled at $9.7 billion this year, not exceeding $12 billion until 2030, which the panel found inconsistent with company guidance. Tesla insurance was modeled growing to $26 billion by 2035 and nearly $300 billion by 2045. Optimus was excluded entirely from the model. The panel noted that Potter will be joining the show within approximately two weeks.

## Robotaxi

1. **Current Fleet Size and Unsupervised Expansion:** The panel referenced a fleet of approximately 39 unsupervised (driverless) robotaxis currently operating, describing this as a figure they're watching closely. Jeff argued the current fleet has likely hit a performance ceiling under the existing FSD release and is effectively waiting for the next software update before expanding. He noted a coincidence that Tesla is testing in approximately 39 cities and has approximately 39 supervised unsupervised cars. James mentioned that Elon stated on a prior earnings call that there were over 500 robotaxis operating in the Bay Area, and that he personally observes them operating even outside mapped areas.

2. **Las Vegas Cybercab Car Wash / Maintenance Facility:** Tesla filed a permit with Clark County in Las Vegas for what is described as a "Tesla Center Cybercab Phase 2 car wash" — a 36,000 square foot facility involving interior and exterior improvements to an existing structure, including a car wash enclosure, relocation of tire service equipment, and installation of power raceways. The panel interpreted this as a full-service autonomous fleet maintenance hub. AI was cited as estimating the space could hold approximately 60 cars at once, though panelists were uncertain of that figure. Jeff highlighted that this is the kind of ancillary infrastructure required to run an autonomous fleet at scale, and that competitors like Waymo are still largely in manual mode for such operations. The panel speculated that cleaning cadence may coincide with charging cadence to maximize efficiency.

3. **City and State Expansion Strategy:** The panel noted a predominance of politically red states (Texas, Florida, Nevada, Arizona) among Tesla's approximately 39 tested cities, attributing this to more favorable autonomous vehicle legislation already on the books. Cern Basher noted Tesla can follow Waymo's regulatory footprint in many cases. Federal AV legislation was mentioned as a future tailwind. The panel agreed that Florida, Texas, Arizona, and Nevada alone provide massive fleet scaling capacity. James noted Sparks, Nevada and Las Vegas are both on the list.

4. **FSD Approval in China and Robotaxi Launch Path:** Tesla's job postings in China have had the word "urgent" added recently, which the panel interpreted as a strong signal that FSD approval in China is imminent. Separately, a robotaxi-specific job role was posted in China — the first time the word "robotaxi" (rather than just FSD) appeared in a Chinese job posting. The panel's view is that the logical sequence is: FSD approval in China → robotaxi launch in China → Cybercab production in China (with Giga Shanghai or a new facility, since importing from the US is impractical). Cern Basher noted Elon already announced Cybercab and 4680 production at Giga Berlin, suggesting a similar announcement for China is plausible. Jeff also noted that Cybercab production in China would open export markets in South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, and Japan.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Elon Musk's China Visit and FSD Approval Expectations:** Elon Musk traveled to China with President Trump and approximately 16 other CEOs, with Musk and Jensen Huang (Nvidia) reported to have flown together on Air Force One. The panel's consensus view is that deals are largely pre-negotiated before such high-level visits, and that the trip is partly ceremonial. The expectation is that FSD approval in China is very close and that the urgency language in Tesla's China job postings supports this. Jeff explained that China wants to learn from Tesla's vision-only approach as the economically viable path to broad global expansion, and that getting Tesla fully operational in China will catalyze a broader autonomy boom there — similar to what Tesla's 2019 Giga Shanghai entry did for EVs.

2. **FSD Software Release Gating Unsupervised Fleet Growth:** Jeff theorized that Tesla operates robotaxi expansion against a mathematical performance model, and that the unsupervised fleet size is held at a ceiling until a new FSD release is validated to meet performance thresholds. He suggested this is why the approximately 39-vehicle unsupervised fleet has plateaued, and that the next meaningful expansion will coincide with the next validated FSD release. He also raised a concern about wireless charging infrastructure — arguing that Tesla needs to demonstrate autonomous wireless charging at scale (e.g., in outbound lots) before the fleet can grow without manual charging intervention.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Factory and Hiring Activity:** The panel noted that Tesla has stopped production on the Model S/X line and is replacing it with an Optimus production line at Giga Texas, targeting capacity for one million robots. Ground has been broken and the foundation is being laid for the Optimus factory at Giga Texas. Tesla has also been hiring data collection operators for Optimus across approximately 12 Tesla offices and factories, consistent with Elon's stated goal of having approximately 5,000 Optimus units training in Tesla facilities. Reports indicate these robots are being stress-tested carrying 30 pounds continuously — a load the panel noted is beyond what a human can sustain for a full workday.

2. **Wall Street Reluctance to Model Optimus:** Despite concrete manufacturing steps, no major Wall Street analysts — including Alex Potter's Piper Sandler report — have yet included Optimus revenue in their models. The panel debated what inflection point would cause analysts to add even a small Optimus estimate. Cern Basher suggested ARK Invest confirmed they are working on Optimus research papers and will add it to their model eventually, possibly waiting for a robotaxi inflection point first. The panel argued analysts should take Tesla's manufacturing announcements more seriously than those of any other company given Tesla's execution track record.

3. **Figure Robotics 50+ Hour Live Stress Test:** Figure ran a live-streamed autonomous robot test that was originally planned for 8 hours but extended past 50 hours, with a team of four robots sorting over 63,000 packages. Brett Adcock stated the test will run until a point of failure. The panel gave Figure credit for the transparency and endurance of the demo, but noted technical limitations — Figure's robots still use a claw-style hand without forearm actuators, limiting them to domestic/sorting use cases rather than the heavy industrial use case Optimus is targeting. Cern Basher cited Figure's progress in making approximately 50 robots per day (Jeff was uncertain of the exact number), and argued this makes Tesla's automated manufacturing capability even more impressive by comparison.

4. **Scale as the Decisive Factor in Humanoid Robots:** The panel repeatedly emphasized that the decisive factors in humanoid robotics are: (1) the right AI brain, (2) the right physical product, and (3) manufacturing capability and supply chain at scale — and that very few companies will check all three. Herbert drew an analogy to EV announcements: many companies announced EV ambitions and are now quietly canceling them, and the same pattern is expected in humanoid robots. The panel agreed that fleet scale creates a compounding training advantage — more robots doing more diverse tasks accelerates AI capability across the entire fleet simultaneously — and that this favors whoever can scale manufacturing fastest.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO and Tesla Halo Effect:** The SpaceX IPO prospectus is expected as soon as the following week, with a road show slated for approximately June 8 and the IPO expected around mid-June, which would make it the largest IPO of all time. Ron Baron reportedly stated that SpaceX will be added to three index funds within 15 days of the IPO (one every 5 days), which is unprecedented and would create sustained institutional buying pressure. James argued that the IPO proceeds will fund Terafab, from which Tesla (via Optimus and xAI) would be a long-term beneficiary. The panel's consensus was that Tesla shareholders are unlikely to sell Tesla stock to rotate into SpaceX in any significant way — due to tax consequences, the differing time horizons (Tesla's robotaxi story is closer-term), and the fact that many SpaceX holders are already in via Echelon/SBVS and may actually trim on the IPO pop. If anything, the panel agreed with Larry Goldberg's view that the SpaceX IPO would create a halo effect lifting Tesla stock by approximately 10–15%.

2. **Waymo vs. Uber and Competitive Dynamics:** James briefly noted that Waymo and Uber leadership have begun publicly attacking each other, which he characterized as a sign of weakness ("zombies fighting"). He contrasted this with the collaborative and mutually respectful relationship between Elon Musk and Jensen Huang, calling it a masterclass in CEO partnerships built on shared AI vision and cooperation.

3. **Nvidia and Terafab Partnership Speculation:** Cern Basher raised the question of whether Nvidia might become a partner in the Terafab venture. Jeff responded that Nvidia is not a fabricator — they have no process capability — so any partnership would only make sense if Tesla anticipated demand- or funding-gap issues. Jeff noted Nvidia partners broadly across the industry ("they sleep around with anything that moves"), so a partnership is conceivable but not necessarily meaningful. The panel noted there were rumors of H200 chips becoming more accessible in China, which could have implications for Tesla's FSD and Optimus programs there.
