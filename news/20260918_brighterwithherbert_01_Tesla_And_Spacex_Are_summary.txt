A panel of Tesla/SpaceX bulls discusses a busy news week: rumors of an imminent Tesla-SpaceX merger, progress on FSD version 15 ahead of robotaxi scaling, the Terafab chip venture, the upcoming Roadster unveil, and a slate of new SpaceX developments including Starship's next launch, Starlink V3 satellite economics, and a new multibillion-dollar AI compute deal.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Merger speculation intensifies but timing uncertain:** One panelist (Alexandra Merz) argues that for a Tesla-SpaceX merger vote to be included in this year's Tesla shareholder meeting, the companies would need to move before October 15, since that meeting must also reconfirm three directors (Elon Musk, Robin Denholm, JB Straubel). M&A contacts she's spoken with believe a merger proxy is too large to fold into a general proxy and expect a separate meeting a few months later, though she still holds out hope Elon pushes to combine them. She estimates it's "imminent," possibly within weeks to a couple of months, but stresses there's no confirmed leak or announcement yet.
2. **Elon's All-In podcast comments read as confirmation of merger direction by panel:** Panelists characterize Elon's response when asked why Tesla and SpaceX remain separate companies — citing deep collaboration and management overlap — as strongly suggestive a merger is coming, though he did not explicitly confirm one; one panelist put unofficial odds at "99.999%."
3. **Terafab structure unresolved pending merger:** A panelist notes Terafab AI LLC (the chip fab entity) is currently registered in Texas and appears to be 100% SpaceX-owned, while Tesla has separately filed a trademark for "Terafab" and funded a ~$3 billion pilot R&D site at Giga Texas; both companies are hiring for it. She argues a merger would resolve the ambiguity by putting it under one roof; absent that, she wants clarity on how the joint venture will be formalized.
4. **New SpaceX AI compute deal:** SpaceX CFO Brett Johnson reportedly confirmed a new AI hosting/compute deal worth $1.1 billion per month (about $13.3 billion annualized) starting December 1, with the company still on track for roughly $100 billion in annualized recurring revenue by December, per the panel.
5. **SpaceX stock stability noted:** One panelist observed SPCX trading in roughly a 5% range over the prior 30 days, calling it a sign of stabilizing valuation ahead of potential merger catalysts.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship launch date shifts:** The next Starship launch, initially expected around September 22, was described as moved to September 28 or 29 per an update noted during recording.
2. **Starship 13 booster recovery aids tile fixes:** SpaceX recovered a Starship booster (referred to as "Starship 13") from the ocean, which the panel says gave engineers a roughly three-month head start on diagnosing and fixing heat-shield tile issues.
3. **Elon "highly confident" on orbital AI data centers next year:** Elon Musk reportedly stated he is highly confident SpaceX will launch NVIDIA-based AI compute hardware into space next year; panelists view this as plausible given overlapping technology between Starlink V3 satellites and planned space-based data centers, and parallel work on terrestrial data centers (Colossus/"Beer Rubin," per the transcript).
4. **Starship 14 to carry Starlink V3 satellites, boosting capacity ~10% per launch:** The panel states Starship's next mission after 13 (referred to as "Starship 14") will carry an initial batch of Starlink V3 satellites (described as 26 units), adding about 3% to Starlink's network capacity; a full-capacity Starship launch of 60 V3 satellites could eventually add roughly 10% capacity per launch. The current constellation was built via 422 Falcon 9 launches reaching just under 10,000 active Starlinks (over 11,000 launched total, accounting for de-orbits).
5. **Kathy Wood's Starship revenue scenario, per Elon's response:** Cathie Wood was cited projecting each Starship launch could generate roughly $1 billion in revenue, potentially reaching about $10 trillion annually by 2030 if SpaceX hits a target of roughly 10,000 flights per year; Elon Musk's reported response was that it's "not impossible." A $1.75 trillion valuation figure was also referenced by the panel in connection with this scenario.
6. **Fuel logistics economics:** Each Starship launch reportedly burns about 5,250 tons of liquid oxygen and methane, requiring 389 tanker truck deliveries costing an estimated $1.8–2.7 million; a third-party estimate (attributed to "Aaron Bernett at Mark 33") suggests an on-site production plant near the launchpad could cut this to roughly $200,000, a roughly 90% cost reduction — cited as the economic rationale for SpaceX building local fuel production.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD version 15 framed as key near-term catalyst:** Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy has called version 15 "ridiculously safe" and said it brings earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction times, and better collision avoidance; the panel views this as the major unlock for robotaxi scaling, though no firm release date is confirmed. One panelist noted a Tesla owner (Steve) reported FSD helping him avoid a collision when a car pulled out from a parking lot.
2. **Unsupervised miles growth accelerating:** The panel cites a reported ~17% week-over-week growth rate in unsupervised paid miles, implying roughly 1.4 million cumulative unsupervised miles at the time of the discussion, and notes a jump attributed to roughly 130% growth in the unsupervised fleet from August to September. One panelist estimates this pace would require on the order of 180-190 vehicles currently in service to produce those miles, and suggests the current fleet may be sufficient for the near term before more vehicles need to be added.
3. **Reduced "looping" and edge-case issues:** Panelists say reports of robotaxi vehicles looping or exhibiting convenience/edge-case glitches, which were fairly common in August, have become rare, alongside NHTSA data showing only very low-speed contact incidents (1-2 mph) rather than real collisions.
4. **Cybercab fleet size remains small ahead of Tesla fan event:** The panel notes the active Cybercab fleet is around 49 vehicles, which they say will likely be insufficient to meet demand from roughly 2,000 attendees expected at an upcoming Tesla fan takeover event on October 10.
5. **China Optimus supply-chain rumors treated skeptically for FSD/robotaxi context:** Not directly FSD-related, but panelists (particularly one with supply-chain experience) cautioned that rumors about supplier "orders" for Optimus (citing figures like 5,000 or 50,000 units) are often mischaracterized using contract-manufacturing terminology that doesn't match how Tesla vertically manufactures, and that factory audits described in Chinese reports resemble routine supplier certification processes seen roughly six months earlier, not confirmed new mass-production events.
6. **Regional robotaxi rollout seen as lagging:** Panelists flagged that expansion of robotaxi service to Las Vegas and Phoenix has been quieter than expected, along with a lack of recent updates on FSD approval progress in China and Israel; a vote on FSD approval in Europe is expected around October 6.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus demo timing still unknown:** No formal Optimus demo 3 or product event has occurred yet; panelists expect Tesla to reveal capabilities only when ready, possibly timed near start of production, and speculate this could occur within the next month or be held back until larger-scale production next year.
2. **Terafab positioned as long-term enabler for Optimus and robotaxi:** Panelists describe Terafab as critical future chip supply infrastructure for scaling Optimus and Cybercab production, distinct from a traditional chip fab due to its multi-chip-type development environment and scale (described as potentially many times larger than existing output at comparable U.S. facilities).

## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Roadster unveil set for October 1st in Texas:** Tesla's Roadster event will take place October 1st in the McGregor, Texas area (near Waco), roughly 90 minutes north of Austin; invites have reportedly gone out to reservation holders and select others. Marketing imagery referenced "no roads," alluding to a hover/flight-assist capability described by panelists as likely limited to short bursts rather than sustained flight, using thrusters partly for cornering/stability assistance rather than true flying.
2. **Q3 delivery expectations:** Panelists expect a potentially record Tesla delivery quarter, speculating deliveries could exceed the 400,000-unit range ("a five in front of the delivery number"), while noting a difficult year-over-year comparison because the prior-year quarter benefited from an expiring U.S. EV tax credit.
