This video from Investing Against the Grain mixes trading-course promotion and macro market commentary (Iran de-escalation, a hedge-fund blowup tied to rate-hike rumors) with a substantive discussion of the Wall Street Journal report on a possible Tesla–SpaceX merger involving a separation of Tesla's China business, and what to watch for in SpaceX's upcoming earnings. The host's core thesis is that hyperscaler capex trends support continued AI/Tesla/SpaceX upside, and that a Tesla-SpaceX merger remains plausible but complicated by China's approval and SpaceX's current (declining) valuation.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **WSJ report on Tesla-SpaceX merger and China separation:** The Wall Street Journal reported that Tesla is weighing separating its China business to pave the way for a potential merger with SpaceX; Elon Musk publicly called the report fake news, saying it had never been discussed. The host and a co-host (Alexandra Merz) believe full denial is unlikely to be entirely accurate, arguing it would be poor business practice for Tesla/SpaceX not to have at least modeled contingency plans for China-related risk.
2. **Reported SpaceX supply-chain move on Chinese nationals:** A post from an account called "Whale Wisdom Insider" (per the host) claimed SpaceX is moving to keep Chinese nationals out of its supply chain to insulate the US space program from Chinese influence; the host presents this as unconfirmed but as one more signal something is happening.
3. **Why China is the key obstacle:** The host argues that because SpaceX is a US military contractor (Starlink is used in military operations), China is likely to resist any structure that lets Tesla's China operations (factory, data, rare earth sourcing) feed into a military-linked company; the US government could have parallel concerns about a defense contractor being exposed to Chinese leverage. The host thinks an outright sale or spin-off of Tesla China is unlikely, expecting instead a joint venture or "ring-fencing" arrangement that satisfies Chinese regulators.
4. **Valuation dynamics of a merger:** The host argues that a lower SpaceX valuation makes it harder to offer Tesla shareholders a premium without diluting SpaceX investors, whereas a rising SpaceX valuation would make a merger-of-equals or premium offer easier; Tesla shareholders (needing 50%+ approval) are seen as the easier hurdle to clear via incentives, versus Elon Musk's outsized influence on the SpaceX shareholder vote.
5. **Stock reaction to merger rumors:** Following the initial merger/China-separation rumors (before Musk's denial), TSLA reportedly moved from around $303 up to roughly $323–324, which the host cites as evidence the market would welcome such a deal.
6. **What to watch on SpaceX's earnings call:** The host flags SpaceX's earnings (Tuesday) as pivotal, specifically hoping for detail on a "NeoCloud" leasing/data-center business, profitability signals, and guidance — contrasting this with Meta's earnings call, which the host says disappointed the market by declining to give guidance on leasing out data center capacity.

## Market & Competition / Financials
7. **Hyperscaler capex trends:** The host recaps recent earnings: Google raised capex guidance to $195–205 billion; Microsoft's capex stayed roughly flat near $175 billion; Meta narrowed guidance to $130–145 billion (raising the low end, partly due to legal costs); Amazon raised capex to $220 billion from $200 billion, citing demand visibility out to 2028. A chart (credited to Ryan Detrick) shows hyperscaler AI capex (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) projected to exceed $1 trillion in 2027, nearing 3% of GDP — a figure the host notes excludes Tesla, SpaceX, Palantir, Nvidia, and Micron.
8. **Cloud revenue growth as ROI evidence:** Microsoft Azure revenue surpassed $100 billion for the fiscal year for the first time; Google Cloud grew 82% year-over-year to $24.8 billion; Amazon AWS grew 37% year-over-year but on a much larger base (~$42.2 billion year-over-year growth cited), which the host says represents accelerating absolute growth despite the lower percentage.
9. **Meta's earnings miss explained:** Meta missed earnings largely due to a roughly $2 billion legal expense tied to a lawsuit over child safety/platform addictiveness plus a one-time severance charge from layoffs; excluding these one-time items, the host says Meta actually beat earnings, and argues Wall Street underappreciates that Meta's AI-driven ad-targeting business is itself a meaningful AI monetization story.
10. **Ken Griffin/Citadel and a levered fund's collapse:** The host relays a report/rumor that Citadel's Ken Griffin was linked to circulating rumors ahead of a Fed decision (attributed to "Kevin Walsh"/Fed official) about a surprise rate hike, which the host had also flagged to his own subscribers as unconfirmed chatter; this contributed to an AI-stock selloff that forced a heavily leveraged fund called "Situational Awareness" (run by a young manager described as ex-Anthropic) to unload assets, which Citadel reportedly then bought at depressed prices. The host frames this as a cautionary tale about excessive leverage rather than a confirmed, sourced fact.
