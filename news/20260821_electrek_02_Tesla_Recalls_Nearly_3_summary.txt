Tesla is recalling nearly 3 million vehicles in China after regulators found the emergency door release too hard to find and operate in a crash, marking Tesla's largest-ever recall in China.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Recall scope and models affected:** China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) filing covers 2,975,910 vehicles: 973,156 domestically built Model 3s, 1,956,713 domestically built Model Ys, 35,590 imported Model 3s, 8,328 imported Model Xs, and 2,123 imported Model Ss.

2. **Nature of the defect and remedy:** The issue is the interior mechanical emergency door release, which SAMR says is difficult to identify and operate because its color closely matches surrounding interior trim, making it hard to use if a crash disables the car's electrical system. Tesla's remedy is not a hardware fix but added warning labels plus an over-the-air software update changing how windows lower to aid escape. The recall takes effect September 25, 2026.

## Market & Competition
1. **Coordinated industry-wide action:** Eight other automakers — Xiaomi, Leapmotor, Xpeng, Geely, Chery, Dongfeng, BAIC BluePark, and FAW — filed similar recalls with SAMR on the same day for the same interior-release issue, making this one of the broadest coordinated safety actions by China's regulator on this issue, though Tesla's recall is by far the largest of the group.

2. **Regulatory trend in China:** In February 2026, China finalized a ban on Tesla-style hidden door handles on safety grounds, following a broader crackdown on sleek EV door handle designs that began the prior year.

3. **Parallel US regulatory and legal pressure:** Tesla faces multiple wrongful-death lawsuits in the US alleging its door handles trapped occupants after crashes. NHTSA widened its probe into the design last year and, in July 2026, denied a defect petition on the door releases but opened new rulemaking that could reshape the safety standard. Members of Congress have introduced the "SAFE Exit Act" targeting the handle design.
