This video features Cern Basher and a co-host discussing how SpaceX's S-1 IPO prospectus reveals that 80% of SpaceX's total addressable market (TAM) — estimated at $20.5–22.7 trillion — depends on technologies that Tesla owns or is developing, specifically "Digital Optimus" and "Macrohard." The core thesis is that Tesla investors are underappreciating how deeply SpaceX's growth trajectory is intertwined with Tesla's AI and autonomy stack, and that this interdependency either implies a merger is coming or — at minimum — that Tesla's Digital Optimus opportunity has been dramatically undermodeled by investors. The hosts also walk through hypothetical Tesla-SpaceX merger scenarios, stressing they are not advocating for a merger but analyzing what conditions would make one fair for Tesla shareholders.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD as the Foundation for Digital Optimus:** The hosts note that the vision technology underlying Full Self-Driving is described in SpaceX's prospectus as the same technology powering "Digital Optimus" — an AI agent system. Instead of a camera feed from around a car, it uses a video feed from a computer desktop to perceive, plan, and act. Elon Musk has described this capability as an "intuition beyond deep thinking AI agents," and the hosts emphasize that FSD development at Tesla is therefore directly relevant to SpaceX's largest TAM segment.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Digital Optimus as Tesla's Fastest-Scaling Business:** The hosts argue that Digital Optimus — Tesla's vision-based AI agent product — could potentially be the first of Tesla's major business lines to scale significantly, because it is a purely digital service that can be "turned on overnight." Unlike robotaxi (which requires physical car production) or physical Optimus robots (which require manufacturing ramp), Digital Optimus has no hardware constraint. Elon Musk reportedly suggested that some form of Digital Optimus could be in operation by the end of 2026, though the hosts are cautious about that timeline given xAI's current development pace.

2. **Digital Optimus vs. Macrohard — Complementary Roles:** The post being discussed describes Digital Optimus as Tesla's side of the enterprise AI agent opportunity, while "Macrohard" is on the xAI side. The hosts explain that the vision component (Tesla's contribution) is necessary to augment pure compute-based LLMs, giving the combined offering a potential advantage over competitors like Anthropic. However, the hosts acknowledge that very few Tesla investors have modeled out the Digital Optimus market, and little has been publicly disclosed about how it will work in practice.

3. **Optimus Robot Connectivity via Starlink:** The hosts suggest that Elon Musk would likely prefer to connect both Optimus robots and Tesla robotaxi vehicles to his own Starlink network rather than third-party connectivity. They speculate that Starlink broadband antennas on building rooftops could enable Optimus connectivity in indoor environments, though they acknowledge this is speculative.

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Network as a Starlink Customer:** The hosts note that Tesla's future robotaxi network represents a massive potential customer base for Starlink Mobile, providing always-on connectivity for a large vehicle fleet. They also float the idea of in-ride entertainment revenue streams — such as multiplayer gaming experiences during robotaxi rides — enabled by persistent high-quality connectivity, likening it to "playing Mario Kart in real time."

2. **Tesla Market Cap and Robotaxi Upside:** During the merger scenario discussion, the hosts observe that Tesla's current market cap is approximately $1.4 trillion, but argue this is an unfair baseline for evaluating any merger ratio because robotaxi has not yet scaled. They suggest Tesla's market cap could double within 6 months to a year if robotaxi scales as expected, making the timing of any merger deal critically important for Tesla shareholders.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX TAM and Tesla's Strategic Leverage:** According to SpaceX's S-1 prospectus, the total addressable market is $20.5 trillion (also cited as $22.7 trillion at one point in the discussion), with enterprise AI applications ("Digital Optimus" + "Macrohard") representing approximately 80% of that figure — making it larger than Starlink and orbital launch combined. AI infrastructure (Terafab, a joint venture between Tesla and SpaceX using the Dojo 3 / AI6 chip) represents approximately 8% of the TAM. The hosts argue this gives Tesla significant leverage in any merger negotiation, since SpaceX cannot access its two largest TAM segments without Tesla.

2. **Infrastructure Build Speed as Competitive Moat:** The hosts reference SpaceX's prospectus claim that Colossus 1 was built in 122 days and Colossus 2 in 91 days, compared to roughly two years for competitors. They frame this execution speed as a key differentiator for the Elon ecosystem and draw parallels to the rapid scaling seen at Nvidia and Anthropic, arguing the combined Tesla-SpaceX AI infrastructure effort could produce a similarly fast revenue ramp.

3. **Merger Scenario Analysis — Conditions for Fairness to Tesla Shareholders:** The hosts walk through the conceptual framework for evaluating a Tesla-SpaceX merger, emphasizing that the ratio and timing of any deal matters enormously. They note that Elon Musk expressed frustration on Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call about the burden of having independent directors evaluate inter-company transactions, which the hosts interpret as a signal that he may be motivated to streamline governance through a merger. They are explicit that they are not recommending a merger, but are stress-testing scenarios for what terms would be acceptable to long-term Tesla shareholders.
