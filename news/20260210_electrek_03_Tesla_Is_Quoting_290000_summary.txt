Electrek reports it obtained a customer quote showing Tesla is pricing the 500-mile Long Range Tesla Semi at $290,000 before destination fees and taxes, the first clear pricing data since the truck's 2017 unveiling at an advertised $180,000.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New Semi pricing disclosed:** A customer quote obtained by Electrek shows the 500-mile Long Range Tesla Semi priced at $290,000 before fees and taxes, consistent with California CARB/HVIP program documents listing Tesla Semi pricing around $260,000 for the Standard Range (325 miles) and $300,000 for the Long Range (500 miles). This compares to the original 2017 advertised prices of $150,000 (300-mile) and $180,000 (500-mile) — a roughly 60% increase for the Long Range version, which the article notes is still above the roughly $240,000 figure the original price would equal after inflation since 2017.
2. **Finalized specs:** Tesla recently revealed final specs for both trims: Standard Range at 325 miles, 82,000 lbs gross weight, curb weight under 20,000 lbs; Long Range at 500 miles, curb weight 23,000 lbs, with 1.2 MW peak charging. Both share 1.7 kWh/mile efficiency, three motors producing 800 kW, and are described as "designed for autonomy."
3. **Competitive positioning:** CARB data cited in the article shows the average cost of a zero-emission Class 8 truck was $435,000 in 2024, meaning Tesla's $290,000 quote undercuts the industry average by about $145,000, though the article notes some competitors have raised prices to better capture incentives.
4. **Incentives and partnerships:** California has reserved nearly $165 million in HVIP vouchers specifically for Tesla Semi purchases — roughly 992 vouchers worth between $84,000 and $351,000 each — which competitors have criticized as preferential treatment for a truck not yet widely available. Tesla has also partnered with Uber Freight on a fleet accelerator program offering additional discounts to qualifying operators.
5. **Production timeline:** The Tesla Semi is expected to enter volume production this year, after roughly six years of delays from its original timeline.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that despite the ~60% price increase since 2017, the $290,000 Long Range Semi is priced aggressively and competitively given its range, charging speed, and efficiency, and that Tesla has "a compelling offering" that could pay back its premium over diesel trucks within a few years for local distribution use. A highlighted top comment pushes back, noting the payback estimate excludes charging infrastructure capital costs and that Tesla lacks the nationwide service/parts network of established truck makers like Mercedes and Volvo.
