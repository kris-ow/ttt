Jo Bhakdi reviews Tesla's earnings week performance and lays out his near-term stock thesis, arguing that the market's post-earnings sell-off is irrational and that Tesla is positioned for a rapid recovery to $400 and potentially beyond. He identifies multiple bullish signals from the earnings call — including a strong auto gross margin beat, a capex increase, and an Optimus production announcement — and draws a parallel to Amazon's post-earnings drop on capex news, which ultimately resolved to all-time highs. His core argument is that the market is misreading high capex as a negative when it is, in fact, a signal of aggressive investment during the most transformative technological period in history.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Auto Gross Margin Beat in a Chaotic Industry:** Tesla reported an auto gross margin of 19.2% versus an expected 17.5%, which Jo Bhakdi highlights as the single most important earnings beat. He contextualizes this against a backdrop of severe stress across the global auto industry — including struggles at Ford, GM, Volkswagen, Toyota peers, and Chinese EV makers including BYD — arguing that Tesla's ability to expand margins while competitors are being "slaughtered" demonstrates exceptional manufacturing execution.

2. **Cybercab Production Underway at Giga Texas:** Jo Bhakdi shares visuals of what he describes as the production-version Cybercab rolling off the line at Gigafactory Austin, Texas, citing coverage by Joe Tegtmeyer and Tech Optimus. He notes the production version is glossy rather than matte — which he uses as a distinguishing signal that this is the real production unit and not a prototype — and states that "dozens and dozens" are being produced every day.

## Financials
1. **Capex Increase from $20B to $25B Seen as Bullish:** Tesla raised its capital expenditure guidance from $20 billion to $25 billion. Jo Bhakdi argues this is "incredibly bullish" and compares it to Amazon's and Meta's large capex announcements, which were also initially punished by the market before those stocks recovered to all-time highs. He contends that for a frontier AGI company, not spending aggressively would be the real mistake, and that Tesla's $25 billion is actually modest — roughly 15% of what he characterizes as competitor-level spending.

2. **Stock Positioning and Near-Term Price Target:** Jo Bhakdi describes repositioning his portfolio ahead of earnings using short puts at the $375–$385 range and states he has largely exited those positions with slight to moderate gains. He is now targeting $400 as his near-term price target (one to two weeks), using a strategy of in-the-money short puts stacked at $380, $385, $390, and $400. He also mentions a potential run beyond all-time highs to $500 on a longer horizon, contingent on the Optimus catalyst.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Production Start Announced for June–July 2026:** Jo Bhakdi highlights what he calls the "Optimus surprise" from the earnings call — an announcement that Optimus will go into live production as early as June, with July being his working estimate ("let's say July"), which he characterizes as approximately 90 days away. He describes this as Tesla likely seizing the "pole position in the world" in humanoid robotics.

2. **Optimus Gen 3 Described as Revolutionary:** In addition to the near-term production announcement, Jo Bhakdi references Optimus version 3 as a "revolutionary humanoid robot" and plans a dedicated video on the topic. He frames this as a separate, longer-term catalyst beyond the initial production ramp.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi Scaling Vagueness Viewed as Bullish Signal:** Jo Bhakdi notes that the earnings call lacked clear milestones or delineated scaling plans for the robotaxi business, which he says was exactly what he predicted and interprets positively. His reasoning is that Elon Musk becoming "less bombastic" and more conservative in public statements typically precedes execution, not failure — summarized by his analogy: "dogs that bark don't bite and dogs that don't bark bite."

## Market & Competition
1. **Amazon Parallel as Template for Tesla Recovery:** Jo Bhakdi draws a direct analogy between Tesla's current post-earnings drop and Amazon's prior earnings sell-off driven by high capex announcements, which he estimates was a drop of roughly 10–20%. Amazon subsequently recovered to all-time highs, and he argues Tesla will follow the same pattern. He attributes the market's behavior to irrationality about capex spending at frontier AI companies.

2. **AI Singularity Framing as Macro Context:** Jo Bhakdi argues that the broader market context — including the recent launch of ChatGPT 5.5, DeepSeek version 4, and Anthropic model updates — confirms that the world is "living through the singularity," making high-capex AI and robotics companies the primary beneficiaries. He explicitly rejects the "AI bubble" narrative he says he has argued against publicly since approximately a year prior.
