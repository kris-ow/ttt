This episode features host Brian interviewing drone photographer Joe Tegtmeyer about recent aerial footage of Giga Texas, covering NVH/test track activity, Cybercab staging in the outbound lot, the new Optimus "Cortex 2" AI training data center, a next-generation Optimus factory under construction, 4680 battery production scale-up, and site infrastructure (retention pond, transformers, superchargers) tied to the planned Tesla/SpaceX chip fab.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercabs staging and testing at Giga Texas:** Drone footage from July 8 shows multiple Cybercabs intermixed with Model Ys and Cybertrucks in the outbound lot, being loaded onto trucks almost as fast as they accumulate; Tegtmeyer says they appear to be prepositioned "all over the place," with sightings reported in nearly every major U.S. market, though neither speaker can say when they'll enter the robotaxi fleet.
2. **Test track NVH activity:** Vehicles are being run continuously around Giga Texas's (still-incomplete) test track over rumble strips and a newly added "cobblestone"-style rough-road section, reportedly for noise/vibration/harshness testing and active noise cancellation intended for Cybercab passenger experience; Tegtmeyer notes Lars Moravy recently described using acoustic sensors during autonomous drives to the outbound lot to flag rattle issues back to the factory floor.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **"Cortex 2" Optimus training data center coming online:** A new Giga Texas facility dedicated to training Optimus's neural network is roughly 50% operational, with about half its Megapacks installed and progress visible via decommissioned temporary chillers; it began coming online in April (on schedule) and is expected to be fully operational around fall 2026, with roughly double the cooling capacity of the existing "Cortex 1" facility used for FSD training. Once complete, Cortex 2 is expected to have roughly 260 Megapacks installed.
2. **Next-generation Optimus factory under construction:** Land reclamation and steel/footing work are underway at Giga Texas for a new Optimus factory, described by Tegtmeyer as roughly an order of magnitude larger than the Fremont pilot line (which he says has been advertised at up to ~1 million robots/year); this new facility is targeted at a stated ~10 million robots/year and could ultimately stretch roughly 4,000 feet long, built using the same "minimum essential capability" phased-construction approach used at Giga Texas and Berlin. Tegtmeyer estimates equipment installation could begin as early as next spring, with meaningful production likely not until 2028.
3. **Semi crash testing observed:** A refreshed Tesla Semi was seen inside the Giga Texas crash test facility; while semis aren't legally required to undergo crash testing, Tegtmeyer notes Tesla has crash-tested semis before and speculates this unit may be undergoing data verification or collection.

## Energy
1. **Transformer stockpiling for grid and data-center buildout:** Tesla has been accumulating large numbers of transformers at Giga Texas over the past couple of years due to multi-year lead times, storing them until needed for Megapack installations and other projects; most are later shipped offsite once needed.
2. **Megapack cost advantage for internal data centers:** The host argues Tesla's roughly 30% margin on Megapacks (per the host) means Tesla effectively obtains them cheaper than external buyers, giving it a structural cost advantage in building data center power infrastructure; a new Megapack factory is expected online later this year in Houston.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **4680 battery production scale-up:** Tegtmeyer describes signs of expanded 4680 cell production at Giga Texas — new cabinetry, concrete slab reconfiguration, increased recycling volume suggesting trial line setups — noting original space existed for eight lines versus roughly 1.4 lines previously in use. He says Tesla has reportedly gotten its dry cathode and calendaring/rolling processes under control, and that Giga Berlin is now adding 4680 production using lessons learned at Giga Texas, leading him to believe Tesla has "a good handle" on 4680 supply for Cybercab, Cybertruck, and (believed) Semi production.
2. **Model Y production cadence at outbound lot:** Recent footage shows large volumes of Model Y variants (rear-wheel drive, Performance, and the newer Model Y L) accumulating in the outbound lot alongside Cybertrucks and Cybercabs.

## Market & Competition
1. **Site context — Tesla/SpaceX chip fab and Musk-company cluster:** A large water retention pond is being built at Giga Texas's south end ahead of a planned joint venture with SpaceX for an "advanced technology chip fab," described by a Tesla executive (per Tegtmeyer) as smaller than the main factory, with a separate, much larger "Terafab" facility (reportedly ~10x the main factory's size) planned elsewhere. Nearby facilities from other Musk-linked and partner companies — Samsung, Starlink, an orbiting data center factory, satellite solar panel production, Boring Company, and Neuralink — are also cited as clustering around the site.
