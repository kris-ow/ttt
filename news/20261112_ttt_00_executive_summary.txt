## Financials
- **SpaceX-Tesla merger could land as early as December 3**, Bloomberg reports via Electrek, with talks now on price and control; an early-2027 announcement is the fallback scenario. SpaceX's valuation tops $1.9 trillion versus Tesla's roughly $1.5 trillion, and Musk holds ~20% of Tesla but ~85% of SpaceX's voting power per its S-1. Sticking points include ring-fencing Tesla's China business (Giga Shanghai shipped 67,829 vehicles in October alone) against SpaceX's defense-contract sensitivities; a deal valuing Tesla at $2 trillion would instantly clear the first tranche of Musk's 2025 CEO award. TSLA rose up to 1.9% on the report. Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the independent board committees "not credible" and frames the deal as folding an underperforming Tesla into a premium-valued SpaceX.

## Robotaxi
- **Tesla's registered Robotaxi fleet in Texas overtook Waymo's** for the first time, per DMV data cited on brighterwithherbert — roughly five vehicles ahead after Tesla added 58 vehicles in a week versus Waymo's 38.
- **24/7 Robotaxi operation launched** across every active city, tied to a new V15 tech-stack capability referenced by Ashok Elluswamy (per electrified).
- **Fleet expanded to eight cities** with the addition of Fort Worth and San Antonio; unsupervised miles have surpassed 5 million, up more than 13x since late July, under ten months after Austin's unsupervised launch (per electrified).
- **Robotaxi-specific hiring now spans 42–44 US cities**, plus ~39 cities with precursor test-operator roles, per tracking cited on brighterwithherbert; charging-attendant and service-tech postings suggest a rental-style fleet model is coming. Las Vegas is floated as the likely next launch city ahead of a mid-December permit deadline.

## Autonomous Driving
- **Finland approved Tesla's supervised driving system** ("Tesla Assisted Driving"), the 11th EU country, adding ~5.6 million people to coverage (~15.5% of EU population versus the 65%+ threshold needed for an EU-wide vote). Approval came with a tightened speed-overshoot allowance, cut from 50% to 10% above the limit.
- **Another sleeping-driver incident in Canada:** Ontario Provincial Police charged a Tesla driver with careless driving after finding them asleep on Highway 417 ($490 fine, six demerit points). Electrek's Fred Lambert catalogs a string of similar Canadian and U.S. incidents this year, including a Congressional inquiry citing 43 NBC-sourced videos of apparently sleeping Tesla drivers, and argues Tesla's camera-based monitoring and strike system are inadequate versus GM's infrared eye-tracking.
- Lars Moravy, in a Jay Leno interview, said liability for supervised FSD rests with the owner but shifts to Tesla for unsupervised/Robotaxi driving: "If we wrote the software, we're the driver."

## Electric Vehicles
- **Cybertruck production extended through end of summer 2027** to work through backlog before the line shuts down; Musk cited the opportunity cost of Cybertruck's complexity versus redirecting capacity to Cybercab. Commentary (futureaza) frames this alongside the end of Model S/X production as Tesla closing out its legacy human-driven lineup in favor of a Cybercab-platform-centric roadmap.
- Model Y L is seen by one commentator as under-marketed relative to its sales potential, with strong China volume expected if pricing lands near the Model Y Performance tier.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- **SpaceX confirmed two new supercomputer facilities** — Cochise County, Arizona (~$50B) and Brownsville, Texas (~$24B) — totaling over $74 billion in planned investment; the Brownsville site will generate its own power rather than draw from the grid.
- **Musk detailed fab inefficiency rationale** at the Baron Investment Conference: wafers spend over half their cycle time waiting rather than being processed, driving Tesla/SpaceX's push to build and run their own "Terafab" in Grimes County, Texas rather than rely on TSMC, Samsung, or Intel. TSMC's CEO has said TSMC would need to run the fab if involved; Musk has said Tesla/SpaceX "will build and run the fab."
- Outside estimates diverge sharply from Musk's timeline: Seaport's Jay Goldberg expects a first small Terafab batch in 2028 and meaningful output nearer 2030, versus Musk's Q3 2028 volume-production goal; Bernstein pegs a literal terawatt of annual silicon at $5–13 trillion in cost.
- Anthropic may end its SpaceX compute-leasing deal by mid-next year per unconfirmed rumors; Musk said he'd be "happy to take that compute back" but expects Anthropic to want more, not less.

## SpaceX — Starship

## SpaceX — Starlink
