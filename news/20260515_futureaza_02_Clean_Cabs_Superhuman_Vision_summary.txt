This episode of the Tesla Beat podcast (episode 146) covers a wide range of Tesla news including FSD safety improvements, the Cannonball Run cross-country FSD record, Model S/X end of production, Giga Berlin milestones, Cybercab robotaxi infrastructure developments, and Tesla Semi updates. The hosts — Joe, Brian, and the main presenter — also promote their upcoming "X Takeover" community event at Giga Texas on October 10th, featuring Maye Musk as keynote speaker.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Tesla Vision Superhuman Capability Highlighted:** Elon Musk posted a comparison between a human-perceived RGB image and Tesla AI's photon count reconstruction image, illustrating why FSD can see well at night and through extreme glare. The hosts noted that FSD V14 represents a major improvement over V12 and V13 in handling glare and adverse weather conditions, with one host recounting a rainy night drive where the car autonomously reduced its maximum available speed in response to road conditions without driver input.

2. **FSD Cannonball Run Record Set:** A team including David, Zach (Zack Black Model 3), and Dan completed a coast-to-coast Cannonball Run from California to New York using FSD version 143.2, covering 2,833 miles in 49 hours and 55 minutes with zero disengagements or human interventions. This beat the previous FSD Cannonball record by 8.5 hours. The hosts emphasized this as a demonstration that a Tesla can now effectively drive cross-country autonomously, needing only human assistance for charging.

3. **Early Airbag Deployment via Camera Detection:** Tesla Vision enables airbags to be deployed up to 70 milliseconds earlier than traditional sensor-triggered systems by detecting an unavoidable collision via cameras before physical impact occurs. The hosts noted that a prior episode reported FSD now reacts approximately 20% faster than a previous version, and that FSD is statistically six to seven times safer per mile than human drivers.

4. **FSD Expansion into EU Underway:** Ireland is in active talks with Tesla regarding FSD approval, with plans to adopt EU-level regulations permitting FSD rather than create independent rules. The hosts noted that Tesla is proactively engaging individual EU member states — telling each country "tell us what you need to see" — to ensure affirmative votes when EU-wide FSD policy comes up. The Netherlands has already approved FSD, and Belgium was cited as likely next.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab Car Wash Facility Planned for Las Vegas:** Tesla reportedly filed a permit with Clark County on May 12th describing a "Cybercab Phase 2 car wash" at an existing facility in Las Vegas. The project involves interior and exterior improvements, construction of a car wash enclosure, and relocation of tire service equipment. The hosts noted this signals Tesla is planning robotaxi infrastructure in Las Vegas even before public robotaxi service has been announced there, and contrasted Tesla's likely automated approach with Waymo's heavily manual cleaning operation.

2. **Cybercab Spotted with Starlink Mini:** A Cybercab was observed with a Starlink Mini mounted on the back. The hosts speculated that Starlink connectivity will eventually be integrated directly into Tesla vehicles, making the robotaxi network more resilient — referencing the San Francisco power outage that caused Waymo vehicles to fail while Tesla's vision-based system was unaffected. The hosts also noted that the Fremont factory already has numerous large Starlink installations visible on the test track, suggesting deep existing integration between Tesla and SpaceX connectivity infrastructure.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model S and Model X Production Ends at Fremont:** The last Model S and Model X vehicles have been produced at the Fremont factory, ending 14 years of Model S production and 11 years of Model X production. The hosts reflected on the Model S's legacy as the first genuinely desirable mass-market EV and noted that the final Model S is expected to go to Steve Jurvetson, who received the first one. The hosts framed the end of production as a deliberate pivot toward Optimus and autonomous vehicles.

2. **Giga Berlin Reaches 750,000 Model Y Milestone:** Giga Berlin has produced 750,000 Model Y vehicles, and separately announced a million drive units produced. The hosts noted that 4680 cells are being or will soon be manufactured on the Giga Berlin campus, which should drive down vehicle costs. Tesla is also routing Model Y production from Berlin to Canada to optimize around tariff structures, and shipping Model 3s to Canada from Giga Shanghai.

3. **$250 Million Investment in Giga Berlin Cell Factory:** Tesla announced a $250 million investment in its Giga Berlin 4680 cell factory, targeting 18 gigawatt-hours of annual cell production capacity and creating 1,500 new jobs. Brian noted the factory footprint is far smaller than Giga Nevada (which has 35 GWh capacity), demonstrating dramatically improved energy density per square foot of manufacturing space.

4. **Model Y L Expanding to Singapore and Southeast Asia:** Kyle Connor (6'1") tested the Model Y L at the Beijing Auto Show and confirmed adequate knee clearance in the third row at that height. The vehicle is being delivered in Singapore and spreading across Oceania and Southeast Asia. Test models have been spotted in both Texas and Germany. The hosts expressed confidence it will arrive in the US before the end of 2026, citing a brief appearance on Tesla's website (quickly removed) and Bay Area sightings as signals.

## Energy
1. **Tesla Semi E-PTO Powers Refrigerated Trailers:** Tesla Semi's Electric Power Take-Off (E-PTO) now delivers up to 25 kW to power trailer refrigeration units, eliminating the need for diesel auxiliary motors. The hosts noted this is a significant efficiency gain for truckers — particularly those running refrigerated loads — and referenced a 50% cost-per-mile savings in California versus diesel. They also discussed how semi drivers may increasingly use the power capability for in-cab living amenities.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo's LiDAR Limitations Contrasted with Tesla Vision:** The hosts compared Tesla's camera-only FSD approach favorably to Waymo's LiDAR-dependent system, noting that Waymo vehicles are regularly observed getting stuck in challenging conditions (fog, glare, unusual scenarios) while Tesla's photon-count reconstruction vision system handles those scenarios by seeing through noise that would confuse both LiDAR and human eyes.

2. **Chinese EV Competition in Europe:** Despite strong Chinese EV presence in Europe and Germany's competitive domestic auto market (BMW, Mercedes, Porsche, Volkswagen), Tesla's Model Y continues to outsell most Chinese competitors. Brian attributed part of this to Tesla's superior ride quality and suspension tuning, areas where even premium Chinese brands like Xiaomi and BYD still lag. The hosts also noted that the Model Y is the best-selling car in the world.
