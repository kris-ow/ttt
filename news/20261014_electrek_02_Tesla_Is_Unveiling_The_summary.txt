Electrek recaps Tesla's repeatedly delayed Roadster, reporting that the company has now rescheduled its "unveil" event to October 15 at 7:55pm Central after postponing the October 1 date, citing weather since the event must be held outdoors.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Nine years of delays:** The Roadster was first revealed in November 2017 with production promised for 2020; the article lists at least six subsequent production delays (to 2021, 2022, 2023, "hopefully" 2024, 2025, and most recently November 2025's push to 2027 or 2028), and notes Tesla has never delivered a single unit.
2. **Unveil event itself kept slipping:** A demo planned for the end of 2025 moved to April 1, 2026, then late April, then "maybe in a month or so" per the Q1 earnings call, then late May/June, then August or later, then October 1, and now October 15 — at least seven different unveil dates by the article's count.
3. **Expected demo content:** Based on Tesla/Musk teasers, the article expects the demo to center on SpaceX-derived cold gas thrusters (Musk has claimed sub-one-second 0-60 mph), scaled back from an earlier "flying" tease to a short hover; the demo car is reportedly remote-operated and not street-legal. The article also notes a full Cyber-style redesign and a recent fan-car patent filing that could point to an upside-down driving demonstration.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert is skeptical and unenthusiastic despite holding two Roadster reservations, arguing the event looks built around a thruster gimmick ("a few bursts per tank") rather than a real product, and says what he actually wants Thursday is a production car, real price, real start-of-production date, and clarity on the fate of 2017 reservations — none of which he expects.

Notable quotes and data:
- Original 2017 reservation terms: $50,000 reservation / $250,000 "Founders Series," promising a four-seat, 620-mile range, 0-60 in 1.9 seconds roadster for $200,000.
- Musk (May 2023): production would "hopefully" start in 2024, "this is not a commitment."
- Musk (mid-August 2026): teased a "flying" demo with no driver.
- Musk on the April 1, 2026 date: said it gave him "deniability."
