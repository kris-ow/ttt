The article reports that on Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call, Elon Musk failed to give a concrete plan for upgrading Hardware 3 (HW3) vehicles to run future FSD versions, despite years of promises that these cars had all the hardware needed for self-driving.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Musk's noncommittal answer on HW3 upgrades:** Asked by Truist analyst Will Stein whether Tesla still plans hardware upgrades so HW3 cars can run FSD v15 and beyond, Musk said it would "make sense" and be "financially sensible at some point" to upgrade "all cars that have less than Hardware 4" — without committing to timing, method, or cost.
2. **Next-gen chip, not HW4, floated as the fix:** Musk said Tesla would rather upgrade HW3 cars to a future AI chip generation rather than Hardware 4 — either a "moderate improvement over AI4" targeted for production "around the middle of next year," or the more powerful AI5, which he said would "hopefully" reach volume production later next year. Musk said AI5 "will initially go into Optimus," meaning HW3 car owners would not be first in line even if that chip becomes the fix.
3. **History of the hardware promise and walkback:** Tesla stated as far back as 2016 that "all Tesla cars being produced now have full self-driving hardware" and sold FSD for as much as $15,000 on that basis. Musk has since acknowledged HW3 "simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD," citing "1/8th of the memory bandwidth of Hardware 4." Tesla quietly altered its "all cars have self-driving hardware" language in its Q3 2025 shareholder letter amid class-action lawsuits in the US, China, and Australia. The author (per Electrek's own prior reporting) states Tesla may ultimately need to replace the computer in roughly 4 million cars or compensate owners. Tesla has begun shipping a stripped-down FSD v14 "Lite" version to HW3 vehicles as a stopgap.
4. **No update on retrofit logistics:** Musk earlier floated building dedicated "micro-factories" to retrofit HW3 cars, acknowledging that service centers would be too slow to handle the volume; on this call he gave no update on that plan or any alternative.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Musk's answer confirms Tesla has no real plan and is simply stalling — every retrofit path (service centers or micro-factories) is costly and impractical for Tesla, so the company keeps pushing the timeline further out while HW3 owners wait indefinitely; the author contends Tesla oversold hardware capability and should compensate affected customers rather than continue deferring.
