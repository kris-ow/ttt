Electrek reports that Tesla's estimated US sales fell 17% year-over-year in January 2026, based on Motor Intelligence registration data, continuing a broader pattern of domestic demand decline.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **January US sales drop:** Tesla is estimated to have sold 40,100 vehicles in the US in January 2026, down from 48,500 in January 2025, a 17% year-over-year decline, per Motor Intelligence registration data. Domestic registrations have declined year-over-year in nine of the past twelve months; the only growth period (July-September 2025) was driven by buyers rushing to purchase before the $7,500 federal EV tax credit expired on September 30, 2025.
2. **2025 full-year US and global totals:** Motor Intelligence estimates full-year 2025 US registrations at 568,454 units, down 10% from 625,712 in 2024. Globally, Tesla delivered 1.636 million vehicles in 2025, an 8.6% decline and its second consecutive year of falling deliveries. November 2025 US sales fell below 40,000 units (lowest since January 2022); December recovered to 48,300 units but remained below year-ago levels.
3. **Pricing moves and affordability:** Tesla launched the lower-priced Model Y Standard ($39,990) and Model 3 Standard ($36,990) in Q4 2025 to address affordability concerns, but the article states these price cuts alone have not reversed the sales decline, arguing brand perception — not price — is now the core problem.
4. **California market share erosion:** In California, Tesla's most important US market, Experian data shows market share fell from 11.6% of registrations in 2024 to 9.9% in 2025, even as the broader state EV market grew.
5. **Europe collapse:** Tesla's European sales fell 27.8% for full-year 2025. In January 2026, registrations across five major European markets were down 44% year-over-year, with the UK down 57% and Norway down 88%. In the UK, BYD sold 1,326 battery-electric vehicles versus Tesla's 647.

## Market & Competition
1. **EV market contraction and Tesla's shrinking share:** US EV market share fell to 6.6% of retail sales in January 2026, down from 9.5% a year earlier (Kelley Blue Book estimates total US EV sales fell nearly 30% year-over-year in January). Tesla's share of the US EV market fell to 46% (from 49% in 2024 and 75% in early 2022). Total 2025 US EV sales were about 1.28 million units, down roughly 2% from 2024, per Cox Automotive.
2. **Competitor gains and losses:** GM's US EV sales rose 48% in 2025 to nearly 170,000 units, raising its share from 8.8% to 13.2%; the Chevrolet Equinox EV alone sold nearly 58,000 units, becoming the third best-selling EV in the US behind the Model Y and Model 3. Ford's EV sales fell 14% to about 84,000 units, and Hyundai-Kia's fell from 124,000 to fewer than 104,000.
3. **Global EV leadership shift:** BYD overtook Tesla as the world's largest EV seller in 2025, selling 2.26 million battery-electric vehicles versus Tesla's 1.64 million, a gap the article says is widening.
4. **Brand value decline:** According to Brand Finance, Tesla's brand value fell 36% in 2025 to $27.6 billion, less than half its 2023 peak of $66.2 billion. The article attributes this partly to brand damage from Elon Musk's political activities and his links to Epstein.
5. **Rising EV prices post-tax-credit:** Cox Automotive data shows the average EV transaction price rose 18.1% year-over-year to $51,981 in January 2026, following expiration of the federal tax credit.

## Financials
1. **Capex pivot toward AI/autonomy:** Following the discontinuation of the Model S and Model X announced on Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings call, Tesla plans to more than double capital expenditures to $20 billion in 2026, directed almost entirely at AI and autonomous driving infrastructure rather than new vehicle models; executives have directed investor focus toward robotaxis and Optimus ("transportation as a service") rather than vehicle deliveries.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the January decline is not alarming in isolation given the post-tax-credit environment, but is serious because it affects Tesla's last major stronghold and follows nine months of decline out of the past twelve, with the only growth period attributable to tax-credit pull-forward. He characterizes Tesla's pivot away from car sales toward robotaxis/Optimus as "automotive suicide" while the core revenue-generating business shrinks in every major market, and expresses skepticism about Musk's timeline track record for delivering replacement revenue streams; he also speculates Tesla might stop reporting delivery figures this year. A quoted top reader comment argues Tesla's "not a car company" messaging risks alienating remaining buyers and that the company needs a way to offset weakening auto sales, such as a possible merger involving Starlink or increased government contracts.
