This is Tesla's Q1 2025 earnings call, featuring opening remarks from Elon Musk and CFO Vaibhav Taneja (referred to as "Bev" in the transcript), followed by an analyst and retail investor Q&A. The core narrative: Q1 was disrupted by a simultaneous global changeover to the refreshed Model Y and by brand-related headwinds, but management frames near-term "bumps" as noise against a long-term thesis centered on robotaxi (Cybercab) deployment starting in Austin in June, rapid Optimus scaling, and continued vertical integration to blunt tariff exposure.

## Financials
1. **Auto margins declined sequentially:** Taneja attributed this to lower delivery volumes, reduced fixed-cost absorption from the factory changeover, and lower regulatory credit revenue, partially offset by slightly higher Model Y pricing (despite incentives used to clear legacy inventory).
2. **Other income hit by Bitcoin mark-to-market loss:** Other income fell sharply sequentially, primarily due to a $472 million swing from a Bitcoin mark-to-market loss in Q1 versus a gain in the prior quarter, plus FX remeasurement effects; Tesla expects more volatility in this line going forward under the new mark-to-market accounting standard.
3. **Capex guidance still above $10 billion for the year:** Even after attempting to optimize spending in light of tariffs, Taneja said 2025 capex is forecast to still exceed $10 billion, with the company still evaluating further adjustments.
4. **Tariff impact on capex:** Taneja noted the counterintuitive dynamic that expanding U.S. manufacturing capacity requires importing equipment (often from China) that is itself subject to tariffs, since domestic equipment-manufacturing capacity is limited.
5. **Services and other margins slightly down:** Attributed to pressure on the used car and insurance businesses; Tesla says it continues working to improve profitability in services/collision via productivity gains.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Simultaneous global Model Y changeover:** Tesla updated all four factories to the refreshed Model Y within the same quarter — described by management as unprecedented in the auto industry — while still selling out legacy Model Y inventory in the US, China and other markets before the switch (legacy production ran through mid-to-late February).
2. **Q1 delivery decline drivers:** Management attributed the drop to lost production during the changeover, insufficient new Model Y availability in most markets until late in the quarter, and what Taneja called "vandalism and unwarranted hostility" toward the brand in certain markets.
3. **Cheaper models still targeted for June production start:** Musk and Lars Moravy said new, more affordable models remain on track for production starting in the timeline given in prior calls (previously stated as June), though the ramp may be "a little slower" than hoped; the strategy is to build them on existing lines to maximize factory utilization rather than launch new dedicated lines.
4. **No meaningful order-inflow damage from brand controversy in Q1:** Per Tesla, despite economic strain and negative press, Tesla remained the best-selling car (not just EV) in Q1 and had a record number of global test drives; Musk added that Tesla isn't immune to macro demand pauses for big-ticket purchases but sees no demand reduction absent macro issues.
5. **India market still on hold over tariffs:** Musk said Tesla wants to enter India (a market with a large middle class) but current tariffs (70%, plus a 30% luxury tax) roughly double the cost of imported vehicles, making the timing a careful decision.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab vs. robotaxi terminology clarified:** Musk distinguished the "Cybercab" as a specific forthcoming product from "robotaxi," which refers to any autonomous Tesla (S3XY); the June Austin launch will use existing Model Y vehicles, not the Cybercab.
2. **Austin launch mechanics:** Management said the initial fleet on day one would be small — Musk suggested "maybe 10 or 20 vehicles" — scaled up carefully and rapidly thereafter; the public should be able to visit Austin in late June or July and hail a ride.
3. **Cybercab "unboxed" manufacturing process progressing:** Executives said the unboxed production method — enabling extreme automation and low cost — is progressing, with early builds expected by end of Q2 and large-scale equipment installation starting in Giga Texas in coming months; production remains on schedule for next year. Early crash testing and corrosion/sealing derisking on uncoated aluminum structures have reportedly been completed.
4. **Target cycle time of 5 seconds per unit:** Musk said the new architecture is designed to ultimately support a vehicle every 5 seconds on a single line (versus Tesla's current fastest line, in Shanghai, at 33 seconds), though the line will run slower initially.
5. **Competitive comparison to Waymo:** Musk argued Waymo's sensor-heavy vehicles cost far more to build (he estimated Tesla's cost at roughly a quarter to 20% of Waymo's) and are made in low volume, while Tesla's camera/AI-based approach is manufactured at high volume; he predicted Tesla could reach "99%" or "90-something percent" market share in autonomy, adding uncertainty ("something might change") and citing regulatory approval as the main gating factor.
6. **Financial materiality timeline:** Musk reiterated that autonomy is expected to become materially impactful to Tesla's financials around the middle to second half of next year, at which point he expects millions of Teslas operating fully autonomously.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Personal-use unsupervised FSD targeted this year:** Musk said he expects unsupervised FSD available for personal use in many U.S. cities by the end of this year, but the rollout will be cautious, emphasizing that autonomy must be meaningfully safer than manual driving, not just equally safe.
2. **Validation is the key bottleneck to robotaxi readiness:** Ashok Elluswamy described self-driving as a "long-tail problem" where critical interventions are extremely rare (Musk cited roughly one every 10,000+ miles), making it hard to measure safety improvement without extensive simulation, including neural-network-based video generation, and large-scale real-world testing in Austin via a QA fleet.
3. **Generalized AI approach validated by China rollout:** Elluswamy said FSD supervised in China, achieved with minimal China-specific data, generalized well to different driving styles, supporting Tesla's camera/AI-first approach over rule-based or HD-map solutions.
4. **Vision-only approach addresses glare/fog concerns:** Musk said Tesla's cameras use direct photon counting (bypassing the image signal processor), allowing the system to see through direct sun glare, in near-total darkness, and in fog — claimed to perform at least as well as, and possibly slightly better than, an average human driver.
5. **Localized driving parameters possible but not required:** Musk said region-specific parameter tuning (e.g., for snowy climates) is likely valuable ("nice to have," not required), analogous to a mixture-of-experts AI approach.
6. **Remote support available but not safety-critical:** Elluswamy confirmed remote operators may occasionally assist if a car gets stuck, but said this is for availability, not required for safe operation.
7. **Tiered FSD pricing under consideration:** Asked about potential pricing tiers between supervised and unsupervised FSD, Musk said this is being considered; he argued current $99/month pricing is already undervalued relative to the utility once attention-monitoring restrictions are gradually relaxed over time.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production target reiterated, still in development phase:** Musk said Optimus remains a development program, with most of the "thousands" of units planned for this year concentrated toward year-end; he reiterated a goal of reaching a million Optimus units per year within four to five years (potentially by 2029).
2. **Rare-earth magnet export licensing is a current constraint:** Musk said Optimus arm actuators use permanent magnets sourced from China, and China's new export licensing requirement for rare-earth magnets has affected supply; Tesla is working with China to obtain a license, noting China wants assurance the magnets aren't for military use.
3. **Extreme component complexity limits ramp predictability:** Musk said Optimus involves roughly 10,000 largely new, unique components with no existing supply chain (motors, gearboxes, actuators, electronics), making precise production ramp forecasts effectively impossible; the ramp rate is dictated by whichever component is the current bottleneck.

## Energy
1. **Record energy storage gross profit despite lower deployments:** Tesla achieved record gross profit in its energy storage business in Q1 despite a sequential decline in deployments, per Taneja.
2. **Powerwall 3 demand outstripping supply:** Taneja said Powerwall 3 has received strong customer reception and Tesla is currently supply-constrained on it.
3. **Tariff exposure concentrated in energy business:** Taneja said tariffs will have an outsized impact on the energy business because Tesla sources LFP battery cells from China; Tesla is commissioning U.S. LFP cell manufacturing equipment, though current capacity covers only a fraction of total installed capacity, and is also pursuing non-China supply sources. A new Megafactory in China (started operations in Q1) is expected to serve non-U.S. energy business.
4. **Long-term ambition to scale storage to terawatt levels:** Musk said the stationary energy storage business is expected to scale ultimately into the terawatt range, driven by utilities' growing recognition that storage effectively increases total usable grid output.

## Market & Competition
1. **Vertical integration as tariff defense:** Musk and executives emphasized Tesla's regionalized supply chains (roughly 85% USMCA-compliant weighted average in the US; over 95% local content in Shanghai vehicles) and heavy vertical integration — including a lithium refinery in South Texas (described as potentially the largest outside China once fully ramped) and a cathode refinery in Austin — as competitive advantages against tariffs relative to other automakers.
2. **Section 232 auto tariffs create incremental cost pressure:** Taneja said upcoming Section 232 tariffs affecting Canada and Mexico (effective May) are expected to raise per-vehicle costs by roughly a couple thousand dollars, in line with prior modeling, despite Tesla's regionalization efforts.
3. **China dominance in drone manufacturing:** Musk said China manufactures about 70% of drones globally and that nearly all drones have some China supply-chain dependency, calling this a strategic vulnerability for the US, which currently cannot manufacture its own drones independently.
4. **Musk's competitive assessment on humanoid robots:** Musk said he doesn't believe any company or country can currently match Tesla in humanoid robotics, ranking Tesla and SpaceX as "number one," while expressing concern that ranks two through ten on a global leaderboard could be occupied by Chinese companies.
