The article reports BYD's Q2 net profit rose 29.8% to 8.2 billion yuan ($1.22 billion), ending four straight quarters of profit declines, as record vehicle exports offset weak domestic Chinese sales.

## Market & Competition
1. **First-half results still down despite Q2 rebound:** BYD's first-half revenue fell 7.13% to 344.82 billion yuan ($50.9 billion), and net profit attributable to shareholders dropped 20.54% to 12.33 billion yuan. The company attributed the decline to weakness in its new energy vehicle (NEV) business and foreign-exchange losses from overseas expansion. Automotive and related products revenue fell 8.98% to 275.34 billion yuan (79.85% of group revenue), while electronics rose 0.96% to 69.41 billion yuan.

2. **Exports drive the turnaround:** BYD sold 1,808,511 NEVs in H1, down 15.72% year-over-year, but the decline narrowed from a 30.01% Q1 plunge to just 3.24% in Q2 (1,108,048 units). Overseas exports totaled roughly 792,000 vehicles in H1 (up 67.8%), about 44% of total sales; Q2 exports alone hit 471,091 units, up 82.46% year-over-year. Gross margin improved to 18.85% from 18.01% a year earlier, which the article attributes to more lucrative export sales. Premium brands Denza, Fang Cheng Bao, and Yangwang saw sales climb 61%, reaching 12.8% of passenger-vehicle sales.

3. **China price war backdrop:** BYD's domestic weakness reflects intense price competition in China's auto market, which Beijing has reportedly begun pressuring automakers to curb. BYD's R&D spend in H1 reached about 28.9 billion yuan (roughly 2.3x its net profit for the period), with cumulative R&D spending exceeding 270 billion yuan. Operating cash flow rose 17.3% to 37.34 billion yuan, while inventory turnover stretched to 109 days from 79 a year earlier due to longer ocean shipping for export cars.

4. **Momentum and export target:** July sales rose 21.76% to 419,211 units, a third consecutive month of year-over-year growth, with overseas passenger vehicle and pickup sales hitting a record 179,841 units (up 124.3%, ~43% of the month's total). BYD has told analysts it is targeting 1.5 million vehicle exports for all of 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
5. **Comparison to Tesla:** BYD delivered 557,090 fully electric vehicles in Q2 (excluding plug-in hybrids), ahead of Tesla's 480,126 vehicle deliveries in the same quarter. The article notes Tesla's Q2 was also a rebound quarter with record revenue but a large profit miss, and that both companies are relying on overseas markets while facing margin pressure.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues BYD's domestic performance was rough (H1 deliveries down nearly 16%, profit down over 20%), but overseas sales at higher margins saved the quarter — a dynamic paralleling BYD's growing overseas presence versus Tesla. He views rising inventory days, tariff uncertainty, and currency exposure as new risks to the export-driven model, but believes BYD's trajectory is improving and that it is well-positioned to win as China's price war eventually cools. He also states BYD earned more profit than Tesla last quarter despite a market cap roughly one-tenth of Tesla's, calling this hard to justify given his view that BYD's product roadmap, driver-assist progress, and battery technology are more advanced.
