Kazakhstan's Ministry of Emergency Situations has confirmed plans to purchase additional Tesla Cybertrucks following successful deployment in rescue operations around Almaty, as Tesla continues searching for buyers amid collapsing domestic demand for the electric pickup.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Kazakhstan emergency services deployment:** Vice Minister Yerbolat Sadyrbayev confirmed the expansion after the first Cybertruck deployed in Almaty demonstrated effectiveness in mountainous terrain rescue operations. Officials cited high off-road mobility, vehicle-to-load power output for communications and rescue equipment, quiet operation, and extended power delivery as key advantages. The Cybertruck is positioned as a supplementary rapid-response vehicle, not a replacement for traditional fire engines, ambulances, or rescue trucks.

2. **Government and security use:** Kazakhstan's State Guard Service also deployed Cybertrucks as mobile command-and-control vehicles at the Summit of the Organization of Turkic States in Turkistan on May 15. One unit served the State Guard Service for command and coordination; a second was added to the Ministry of Emergency Situations' rapid response reserve. The State Guard cited power supply and communications deployment capabilities as the selection rationale.

## Market & Competition
3. **Cybertruck demand collapse:** Cybertruck sales fell 48% in 2025 to approximately 20,000 units — far below Tesla's original 250,000 annual production target. In Q1 2026, Tesla sold just 3,519 Cybertrucks, with registration data showing SpaceX purchased 1,279 units over the past year, artificially inflating figures. Tesla launched the Cybertruck in the Middle East in January 2026, delivering roughly 63 units in the UAE at prices starting around $110,000, and introduced a new $60,000 AWD trim in February 2026. The truck remains ineligible for export to Europe due to EU regulatory non-compliance.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert views Kazakhstan's purchase as emblematic of Tesla's desperation to find any buyer for the Cybertruck and does not expect small government contracts to reverse the demand trajectory. He identifies the $60,000 AWD trim as the vehicle's "last hope," warning that if it fails to move the needle, the Cybertruck program may follow the path of the Model S/X in terms of volume.
