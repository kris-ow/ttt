This video argues that the merger of xAI and SpaceX into a publicly traded entity (SPCX) is the first two-thirds of an eventual three-way combination with Tesla, and that Elon Musk's recent moves to boost his Tesla voting power are the opening steps of that process. The host frames Tesla and SpaceX/xAI as two halves of a single "inference" value chain — one produces AI intelligence, the other converts it into physical action — and argues legal and structural friction, not economics, explains why Tesla is being left for last.

## Market & Competition
1. **xAI-SpaceX merger and IPO:** In February 2026, xAI and SpaceX merged in an all-stock deal valued at about $1.25 trillion. The combined company went public on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX on June 12, 2026 at roughly $1.7 trillion — called the largest IPO in history — and surged to about $2.4 trillion within a week.
2. **Musk's Tesla voting stake jump:** On June 17, 2026, Musk exercised options from his 2018 CEO award (~304 million shares at $23/share), converting them into restricted stock with immediate voting rights, raising his Tesla voting power from ~13% to ~20% (roughly 700 million shares). He sold 17 million shares (~$7 billion) to cover the strike price and taxes; the converted shares remain locked up until January 2028. Motley Fool ran a headline linking the move to a possible SpaceX merger.
3. **2025 pay package and 25% voting target:** In November 2025, Tesla shareholders approved a new pay package for Musk with over 75% support — 12 tranches, ~424 million shares, worth up to roughly $1 trillion over 10 years, tied to milestones including an $8.5 trillion market cap, 20 million vehicles sold, and a million Optimus robots deployed. As tranches vest, his voting control moves toward ~25%, a figure he stated as a goal back in January 2024 ("enough to be influential, but not so much that I can't be overturned").
4. **Why Tesla is last — legal structure:** Musk controls roughly 82–85% of SpaceX's vote via super-voting shares versus approaching 25% at Tesla, meaning he effectively negotiates with himself in any merger. The host contrasts this with the 2016 Tesla-SolarCity deal (~$2.6 billion), which survived a Delaware trial (ruled entirely fair in 2022, affirmed in 2023) only due to an independent board, minority shareholder vote, and defensible price. The xAI-SpaceX merger avoided this by staying private and incorporating in Nevada, where the standard ("known misconduct") is far friendlier to insiders than Delaware's "entirely fair" standard — making a public, minority-shareholder-heavy Tesla merger much harder to execute cleanly. Tesla shareholders are already in litigation over Tesla's $2 billion investment into xAI from January 2026.
5. **SpaceX bond and cash position:** In June 2026, SpaceX priced its first investment-grade bond, initially targeting $20 billion, upsized to $25 billion, with an order book of about $90 billion (~3.5x oversubscribed). Five tranches carried coupons from 5.35% to 6.65%, maturities out to 2056, costing about $1.5 billion/year in interest; lead banks included Bank of America, Citi, Goldman, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley. Proceeds refinance debt from the 2022 Twitter acquisition and 2025 xAI loans. SpaceX also disclosed roughly $100 billion in cash.
6. **SpaceX's compute-as-a-service business:** SpaceX signed startup Reflection AI (an open-source, Nvidia-backed AI company) to rent compute for $150 million/month (~$6.3 billion through 2029). This sits alongside Google (~$920 million/month) and Anthropic (~$45 billion over multiple years) deals on the Colossus supercomputer (formerly xAI's Grok-training cluster, now repositioned as a multi-tenant "neo cloud"). Combined committed outside-customer compute revenue tops $80 billion through 2029, supporting SpaceX's roughly $1.8 trillion valuation and new bond debt.
7. **Terafab chip megafab:** Announced March 2026 near Austin as a three-way Tesla/SpaceX/xAI joint venture to produce custom chips for FSD, Cybercab, and Optimus plus radiation-hardened space chips. Cost estimates rose from ~$25 billion in March to as much as $120 billion all-in by May 2026 per SpaceX estimates. Tesla targets small-batch AI5 chips by late 2026 and volume production in 2027, but Morgan Stanley projects meaningful output not until roughly 2028–2030, with some commentary describing the project as reflecting "desperation."
8. **AI5 chip and Dojo history:** Tesla's AI5 next-gen inference chip for FSD/Optimus had its design finalized (taped out) in April 2026, with Tesla claiming roughly 10x cheaper inference cost than Nvidia hardware. This follows Tesla killing its in-house Dojo supercomputer program in August 2025 (Musk said "all paths converge on AI6," calling Dojo 2 "an evolutionary dead end"), after which the program lead and about 20 staff departed to start their own companies — before the program was later revived.
9. **Cross-company AI/hardware integration:** Tesla trademarked "Megapod," a turnkey container-style AI data center reportedly powered by Megapack batteries, in June 2026. Grok has shipped inside Tesla vehicles since July 2025 and powers conversational ability in Optimus Gen 3. SpaceX trademarked "Star Mind," its orbital AI inference layer, unveiling its first satellite in June 2026 — illustrating how Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI hardware/software are already interlinked ahead of any formal merger.

## Financials
1. **Valuation tension in a potential merger:** The host estimates a combined Tesla-SpaceX-xAI entity would be worth roughly $4 trillion, but argues the combined GAAP profit is small relative to that scale, requiring a large valuation multiple to justify it. This creates tension between Tesla and SpaceX investors over relative exchange-ratio pricing, which the host expects Musk's voting power and investor trust will ultimately determine — with subsequent litigation risk regardless of outcome.
