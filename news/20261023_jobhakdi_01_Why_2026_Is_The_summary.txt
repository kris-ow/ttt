## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab ramp and pricing:** James cites Cybercab rides reaching roughly 250–300 paid miles per vehicle per day at about $250/mile revenue (James's figures, not attributed to the canonical tracker), growing about 10% week-over-week, which he projects could reach a million cumulative miles by year-end. James initially estimates 300,000–400,000 Cybercabs per gigawatt (before depreciation) and settles with Joe on a figure "in the realm of the possible" around 500,000 Cybercabs per gigawatt of compute, after disputing Joe's compute revenue assumption (Joe argues $11B/gigawatt revenue cited elsewhere is too low, suggesting more like $40B based on SpaceX reportedly making $26B on a roughly half-gigawatt deployment).
3. **Cybercab vs. Waymo paid miles:** Joe says he read (unverified, could not locate the source again) that Robotaxi/Cybercab is now doing more paid miles than Waymo in Austin, which he calls an early but significant tipping point.
4. **Safety milestone:** Joe cites 3.3 million Robotaxi/Cybercab miles with zero accidents as a safety benchmark, though he frames broader safety validation as still concentrated in the Austin market.
5. **Wall Street "wake-up" threshold:** Both hosts converge on roughly 2,000 active Cybercabs as the tipping point where they expect Wall Street to re-rate the stock, which they think could plausibly be reached by year-end, though they stress this is uncertain.

## SpaceX — Starship / AI & Compute
1. **Orbital compute timeline:** Joe projects Starship will be largely ready for deployment by the end of next year, with SpaceX and Nvidia's orbital compute rack (described as scheduled for Q4 2027) enabling at-scale orbital compute deployment starting in 2028, and the first full gigawatt of compute in space achievable by 2029 (best case) or 2030 (conservative case). He and James broadly align on this 2029–2030 timeframe after stress-testing flight-cadence assumptions (roughly 15,000–20,000 tons of material needed per gigawatt, requiring dozens of Starship flights).
2. **Terrestrial compute bottleneck shifting to space:** Joe argues Tesla/SpaceX-linked terrestrial compute buildout faces intensifying regulatory and political resistance ("the walls are coming in") that will make large-scale terrestrial data centers increasingly difficult to permit by 2028–2029, pushing the strategic need for orbital compute as an "escape valve."
3. **Compute buildout bottlenecks and moat:** The hosts describe Elon Musk as having solved chip supply (reportedly securing roughly 40% of Nvidia's Vera Rubin chip supply, per a rumor James heard from an unnamed source in July, unconfirmed), memory, and cooling (said to be securing around 20 gigawatts of cooling capacity for next year) for his compute buildout, with turbine blade manufacturing now also handled in-house using Tesla's Giga Press casting technology. Permitting is described as the one remaining bottleneck, which Musk reportedly claims to have a "hack" for, though neither host knows what it is.
4. **Distributed/inference compute via Megapack and Cybercab fleets:** Joe raises Musk's mention of "distributed compute" as a possible hack, speculating that Tesla's Megapack fleet and Cybercab onboard chips could be networked for inference workloads using the Supercharger/Starlink network, given that inference compute can tolerate the latency of distributed nodes (unlike training clusters). Joe calculates roughly 4,000 quarter-megawatt satellites would be needed per orbital gigawatt.
5. **AI5 chip timing:** Both hosts note Tesla's AI5 chip is expected to be delivered around Q2 next year (April), which they view as relevant to future inference/compute capacity, including possibly retrofitting existing Cybercabs.

## Energy
1. **Megapack business valuation:** James says Tesla's Megapack business alone could be worth roughly $1.1 trillion in market cap based on data from the recent earnings call showing 130–140 gigawatt-hours of capacity, about $33 billion in revenue at roughly 33% margin (around $11 billion profit), capitalized at a 100 P/E — a business he argues the market largely ignores within Tesla's roughly $1.5 trillion valuation.

## Market & Competition
