This episode is a guest-hosted conversation (Larry, sitting in for the usual host, and returning guest Jo Bhakdi) about what could justify SpaceX's roughly $2 trillion valuation ahead of upcoming quarterly results. The two debate whether Starlink growth, AI/orbital compute, enterprise AI (Cursor), or terrestrial AI compute buildout is the most likely near-term catalyst, with Tesla robotaxi invoked only as a brief comparison point.

## Market & Competition
1. **Starlink called insufficient alone to justify valuation:** Bhakdi argues Starlink could become a trillion-dollar revenue business but its growth (he cites roughly 80%) won't generate enough near-term earnings to support a ~$2 trillion valuation on its own; he expects Starlink V3 satellites to deliver strong results but not enough to move the stock materially in the short term.
2. **Terrestrial AI compute flagged as the strongest near-term catalyst:** Bhakdi argues SpaceX's terrestrial compute buildout (echoing a thesis he says Morgan Stanley also raised) could generate far more near-term earnings than Starlink — citing Anthropic/Google-related compute deals as a precedent worth roughly $25 billion/year at ~80% margin — and estimates terrestrial compute could bring in $50–60 billion in earnings over the next 12 months if capacity (e.g., Blackwell-generation buildout) scales as expected.
3. **Cursor/enterprise AI seen as upside but too small and unproven:** Larry notes Cursor's revenue reportedly grew from roughly $1 billion to $5 billion in a quarter; Bhakdi is bullish long-term but argues enterprise AI spend is too narrow and risks a pullback (e.g., enterprises cutting token spend), and that even strong growth there won't reach the tens of billions needed to move a $2 trillion valuation in six months.
4. **Starship as a longer-horizon catalyst, contested on timing:** Larry argues a successful Starship orbital launch (which he estimates at better than 50%, "in the 60s, maybe even 70s" probability within roughly 1–3 months, with an actual orbital satellite deployment closer to 30% probability) would be a major unlock for Starlink bandwidth and revenue growth. Bhakdi counters that even a successful Starship launch won't be priced in by the market until subsequent quarters demonstrate satellite production, deployment, and actual sales growth — arguing the market's bar for a $2T+ stock is proof of realized earnings, not technical milestones.
5. **Micron and Nvidia cited as comparably mispriced value plays:** Both speakers note broader market skepticism toward AI-capex-heavy names — citing Micron's low forward P/E (Bhakdi mentions roughly 8.5x) despite strong order books — as evidence the market is currently unwilling to price in future AI compute growth; Larry says he's a buyer of SpaceX shares up to a private-market price around $160 and separately considers Nvidia and Micron undervalued (explicitly not investment advice).
6. **Tesla robotaxi cited as an easier market narrative than Starlink:** Bhakdi suggests investors will more readily believe Tesla's robotaxi can scale from thousands to millions of units once proven in one market, versus Starlink, which he says requires separately "unlocking" each market segment (aviation, consumer, enterprise, international) before the market credits it with broader scalability.
