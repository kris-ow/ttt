## Robotaxi
- Texas DMV data shows registered Tesla AV fleet jumped to 476-478 vehicles (409 Model Y, 67-69 Cybercab), up 285 in 30 days, per multiple sources citing Tesla's own Robotaxi account and independent trackers.
- Despite the registration surge, Electrek reports Austin's active 7-day robotaxi count (unique vehicles carrying riders) fell back to 8 as of September 22 — matching June 2025 launch levels — after briefly spiking past 100 following the September 3 Cybercab event. Only 265 of the 476 registered VINs have been matched to spotted license plates, and just 179 were seen on the road at all in the last 30 days.
- Consumer-facing Cybercab software (standard Tesla touchscreen UI, seat controls, "Tesla Theater") was spotted on a touring Australian unit and in a Tesla showroom, fueling speculation Tesla plans eventual direct-to-consumer Cybercab sales — though Bhakdi argues fleet ownership remains more profitable for Tesla in major metros.
- A rider comparing Cybercab to their personal Model Y on FSD said the robotaxi "does behave really safely, even more safely than Model Y," describing the overall experience as nearly identical to personal FSD aside from the cabin design.
- Jo Bhakdi predicts "multiple thousands" of Cybercabs by year-end, contingent on Tesla clearing NHTSA's brake-pedal audit — he's ~90% confident Tesla can't pass under current rules but ~90% confident a regulatory amendment passes first.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA's audit into Tesla's Cybercab self-certification (whether FMVSS 135 brake-pedal and US Code 30122 requirements apply) has 8-9 days left before Tesla's under-oath response is due September 30. Estimated per-vehicle fines (~$27,000) are seen as immaterial to Tesla even at scale.
- Czech Republic became the seventh EU country to approve supervised FSD, bringing the total to 15 countries globally; an EU-wide TCMV vote could come as early as October 6 but needs a large-population country (Germany, France, Italy) to hit the 65% threshold.
- FSD v14.3.9 falsely flagged clean cameras as dirty, prompting unnecessary service visits; Tesla is canceling those appointments and refunding owners, with a fix expected in v14.3.10.
- Elon Musk reiterated Tesla has "openly invited" automakers to license FSD for years with no takers; Bhakdi argues cross-brand licensing is regulatorily complex since NHTSA approves car-maker combinations, not software alone.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus training expanded to Giga Berlin, where employees began wearing camera-equipped backpacks/helmets in mid-August to record movement data, per factory management confirmation.
- Chinese state media (The Paper) reported Tesla's robotics team began supplier audits at Tupa Group, Sanhua Intelligent Controls, and Joyce Electronics, who reportedly already hold Optimus orders — unverified but described as more than rumor given specificity across outlets.
- Oppenheimer maintained a "perform" rating, praising Cybercab manufacturing progress but expecting further Optimus ramp delays past Musk's prior June/July/August targets.

## Market & Competition
- Mercedes-Benz announced a global partnership integrating Waymo's autonomy stack into future vehicles within two years, running Nvidia's Alpamayo in parallel for other models — bypassing Tesla FSD entirely.
- Samsung reportedly began trial production of Tesla's AI5 chip on a 2nm line in Taylor, Texas, targeting volume production in 2027.
- California's discrimination trial against Tesla (Fremont factory, uncapped damages under state law) began, running through October 30.

## Financials
- Tesla named primary OEM for a 2,500-truck Class 8 order from newly formed ZET SCALE alliance (Microsoft, PepsiCo, Red Bull among shippers), five times larger than Einride's prior 500-truck order — though Tesla is lead, not exclusive, supplier alongside Kenworth, Volvo, and RIDE. Separately, IMC Logistics ordered 50 Semis, roughly doubling its zero-emission fleet.
- Nevada Semi factory (50,000 trucks/year nameplate capacity) opened the same week; analysts still expect only 5,000-15,000 Semi deliveries in 2026.

## Energy

- Nvidia's new "DSX Ready" data-center qualification program certified Tesla Megapack alongside Hitachi Energy and LG Energy for AI factory deployments.
