Tesla told the DC City Council that it is developing a wheelchair-accessible autonomous vehicle in Texas, but the author notes this claim came with no timeline, vehicle name, or specifics, and questions whether it's anything more than the previously unveiled Robovan.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla policy advisor's claim to DC lawmakers:** During a Monday hearing on a bill that could allow robotaxi services in DC, Tesla senior policy advisor India Herdman told council members that Tesla is developing a purpose-built, wheelchair-accessible autonomous vehicle, calling it "an active product being built by Tesla in Texas" and citing difficulties with paratransit access, per WIRED's reporting (first to report the comments). Tesla did not respond to WIRED's request for comment, and Herdman gave no timeline.

2. **No current Tesla vehicle is wheelchair accessible:** Tesla's driverless fleet in Austin, Dallas, Houston, and (as of this month) Miami uses the Model Y, which is not wheelchair accessible. The two-seat Cybercab, which Tesla has started building, has accessibility features Tesla highlighted in an X post this month (braille lettering on controls, wheelchair-height seating for easier transfers), but the article argues a two-seater a passenger must climb into isn't meaningfully wheelchair-accessible.

3. **Prior accessibility signals:** Tesla added an accessibility tab to its Robotaxi app last fall stating "We are working on accessible rides," though it currently directs users to third-party wheelchair-accessible providers rather than Tesla's own service. When an X user asked about it, Elon Musk replied "Absolutely."

4. **Robovan as the likely candidate:** At the "We, Robot" event in October 2024, Tesla unveiled the Robovan, a bus-sized, steering-wheel-free vehicle Musk said could carry up to 20 people or haul cargo. The article notes Tesla never gave it a price or launch date, and trademark filings suggest it may be renamed "Robobus." The author speculates Herdman's "active product" reference could describe this vehicle rather than something new, since a flat-floored van platform would be far better suited to wheelchair accessibility than the Cybercab.

## Editorial Stance
The author is skeptical that Herdman's statement represents a genuine new product commitment, framing it as a convenient claim to make before lawmakers concerned about robotaxi accessibility that "costs Tesla nothing to say." The author leans toward believing the referenced vehicle is the Robovan rather than a new design, noting nobody at Tesla has publicly connected the Robovan to accessibility plans or given it a timeline since 2024, and concludes that without a specific vehicle, spec, or date, the claim is not yet a real product — and that it remains dependent on Tesla solving unsupervised autonomy at scale.

Notable quotes and data:
- Herdman: "We are in development for a purpose-built, wheelchair-accessible autonomous vehicle."
- Herdman: "We know that paratransit can be very difficult, and people who are confined to wheelchairs permanently should still be able to move around freely, so that is an active product being built by Tesla in Texas."
- Musk's reply to an X user asking about accessible rides: "Absolutely."
