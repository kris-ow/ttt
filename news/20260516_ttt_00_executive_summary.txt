## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.3 is the operative deployment version.** Elon characterized 14.3 as a "major architectural update" sufficient for unsupervised FSD, with reasoning capabilities introduced in 14.3.1 and expected to extend to more edge cases (railroad crossings, prolonged red-light stops) in subsequent point releases. Unsupervised FSD is targeted to be available in certain regions by end of 2026.
- **Version 15 is a longer-horizon leap, not a near-term gating item.** Elon described v15 as a "complete overhaul of the software architecture" running on AI 4 hardware, arriving "hopefully later this year, certainly by early next year" (early 2027). It is framed as a further safety magnitude improvement beyond what 14.3 already delivers, not a prerequisite for deployment.
- **Elon: FSD v14 is "significantly safer than human" driving.** Tesla has not publicly quantified a specific safety multiple but has confirmed it is above human-level safety; v15 is expected to raise that bar further.
- **Unsupervised FSD global ambition stated.** Elon said Tesla has "a whole pipeline of major improvements" targeting unsupervised FSD availability "anywhere in the world that it is legal to do so."
- **FSD China approval flagged as the single most important near-term catalyst.** During the May 13 Beijing trip — where Elon accompanied President Trump alongside Tim Cook, Jensen Huang, and senior executives from Boeing, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, and others — Elon's stated priority was reportedly Chinese regulatory approval for FSD. Per brighterwithherbert, this would open Tesla's software stack to its largest single market.

## Robotaxi
- **Zero incidents across full deployment history.** Both Elon and Ashok confirmed Tesla's robotaxi program has had no accidental injuries to date. Elon stated explicitly: "A lot of what limits wider deployment of robotaxi are actually not safety issues, but convenience issues where the car basically gets paranoid and gets stuck" — including infinite routing loops and excessive caution near construction zones.
- **~14 production Cybercabs spotted leaving Giga Texas.** Per cernbasher, Joe Tegtmeyer drone footage confirms Cybercab production has begun and newly built units are moving off the lot rather than accumulating. Hosts argue Cybercabs without steering wheels will flow directly into robotaxi expansion in Austin, Dallas, and Houston rather than QA fleet duty.
- **Las Vegas permitted a 36,000 sq ft automated Cybercab car wash facility.** Per brighterwithherbert, the robotic facility requires minimal human staff and is presented as evidence Tesla is building physical operations infrastructure ahead of volume deployment — with Herbert suggesting every major city will eventually require a similar facility.
- **Elon: robotaxi revenue "not super material this year," but "material in a significant way" in 2027.** Tesla has not provided a fleet size target; Vibob confirmed the fleet grew quarter over quarter and Tesla expects continued ramping into new geographies. A speculative 10,000-unit year-end estimate was floated on cernbasher but explicitly flagged as unconfirmed.
- **QA fleet expanding broadly across U.S. cities and rural areas.** Ashok stated Tesla is increasing its QA fleet to validate across many geographies, while simultaneously growing the customer fleet to generate "useful metrics for safe scaling." Cybercab engineering prototypes have been spotted in cities and rural areas nationwide.
- **Waymo framed as structurally unscalable at ~$100/ride loss.** Per brighterwithherbert, Waymo is estimated to lose approximately $100 per ride based on a claimed $5–6 billion annual loss across roughly 10 cities, with ~1,000 incidents per month and frequent remote operator interventions cited.

## Energy
- **Multiple large Megapack contracts confirmed as signed.** Per brighterwithherbert: $2.7 billion with Georgia Power (3+ gigawatts), 1 gigawatt-hour project in Scotland, 240 megawatt project in France (described as one of the country's largest), and $556 million grid battery project in China. These are characterized as executed purchase orders, not projections.
- **Megablock product ramping later in 2026.** A new Megablock product is scheduled to ramp in the second half of 2026, per brighterwithherbert. No capacity or pricing figures provided.
- **Tesla Energy backlog flagged as underappreciated.** The host specifically calls out Megapack backlog as a metric to monitor at the next earnings call, arguing national-scale grid contracts represent a bull case "nobody talks about enough."

## Electric Vehicles
- **Model Y prices raised up to $1,000 in the U.S. — first increase in two years.** Per Electrek, the Premium RWD rises to $45,990 (+$1,000), Premium AWD to $49,990 (+$1,000), and Performance AWD to $57,990 (+$500). Base RWD ($39,990) and base AWD ($41,990) are unchanged. All increases are under 3% on affected trims.
- **Prior pricing context:** Tesla cut Model Y prices by up to $2,000 in April 2024 and had removed as much as $13,000 from sticker since early 2023. Automotive gross margins fell from above 25% (early 2023) to below 18% (mid-2025). Electrek's Fred Lambert reads the selective trim increases as a margin-capture move on high-profitability configurations, not a broad demand signal.
- **Tesla poised to dominate Canada's Chinese EV import quota.** Canada established a 49,000-vehicle/year low-tariff quota for Chinese-made EVs through 2030 (import price threshold: CAD $35,000). Per FutureAzA, Tesla is expected to claim a disproportionate share via Model 3 exports from Giga Shanghai, given its existing Canadian showroom, service, and brand infrastructure. The Canadian government is reportedly debating individual manufacturer allocations, concerned Tesla will take the majority.
- **Model Y was the top-selling vehicle overall in British Columbia for March and April 2026**, with over 4,000 units sold in the first four months of the year — not just top EV, but top vehicle in the province across two consecutive months.
- **Tesla China denied raising domestic Model Y prices** in early May 2026, issuing statements to Chinese media disputing the characterization. Relevant for investors monitoring Chinese demand and competitive pricing dynamics.
- **Newport, KY police adding four Model Y vehicles**, estimating $148,311 in five-year savings versus conventional alternatives. Police Chief cited both cost and product superiority.
- **Tesla Semi first production-line vehicle rolled off Giga Nevada in April 2026**, roughly nine years after the December 2017 announcement. Estimated annual fuel savings of ~$60,000–$80,000 per truck vs. diesel (~35 cents/mile), implying a 3–5 year payback period. The truck is described as "autonomy ready" and ~1,000 lbs lighter than originally designed.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus not yet doing autonomous production work.** Per brighterwithherbert, Optimus is currently being tested inside Tesla factories but has not begun independent production activity. No official 2026 production target has been issued by Tesla.
- **Analyst models: ~50,000 units in 2027, ~500,000 in 2028.** These are third-party analyst projections, not Tesla guidance. Elon's stated long-term vision remains 1 million units per year per production line.
- **Enabling technology convergence cited as the key differentiator vs. earlier humanoid robots.** FSD-derived motion intelligence, AI maturity, and Tesla's mass-manufacturing expertise are identified as what separates Optimus from prior efforts like Honda's ASIMO, which demonstrated capability without a path to scale.

## Financials
- **Automotive gross margins context:** Fell from above 25% (early 2023) to below 18% (mid-2025) through sustained price cuts. Q1 2026 saw a ~50,000-vehicle inventory build and missed delivery expectations. Full-year 2025 deliveries were 1.636 million, down from the 2023 peak.

## Market & Competition
- **Three distinct Tesla valuation frameworks in circulation.** Per brighterwithherbert: (1) car company with normal auto multiple; (2) car + major energy business with multi-billion grid contracts; (3) strategic infrastructure across energy, defense, AI, robotics, and space. The host argues the market is pricing between (1) and (2), making the (3) framing "currently free." His summary: "Tesla still trades like a car company with a little software upside. That's the gap and that's the trade."
- **Anthropic signed to use full compute of SpaceX's Colossus 1** (Memphis: 220,000+ Nvidia GPUs, 300MW of new capacity), per brighterwithherbert. This occurred on May 6, 2026, despite Elon having called Anthropic "evil" approximately three months prior. Google is separately reported (WSJ, May 12) to be in talks with SpaceX to launch orbital data centers.
- **BYD planning 20 Canadian sites in 2026**, with the Dolphin and Seal as likely initial models. A potential Canadian manufacturing joint-venture arrangement is under government discussion that would allow Chinese automakers to sidestep import quotas via domestic production.
