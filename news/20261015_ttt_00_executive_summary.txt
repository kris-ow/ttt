## Robotaxi
- Cybercab registrations in Texas hit 349 (223 added in October alone), with 319 of Tesla's 739 Texas robotaxis now Cybercabs — about 43% of the fleet and roughly 7x the count at launch, per Texas DMV data. The Model Y robotaxi count has held flat at 420 since late September, meaning all recent fleet growth is Cybercab.
- Cybercab logged zero accidents in its first two weeks of Austin operation; Tesla's broader safety data through mid-September showed nine new incidents (seven Tesla-at-fault, two teleoperator-at-fault), including the first-ever Cybercab incident — driving into a water-filled pothole.
- Growth pace: Cybercab is adding ~50 cars/week in Austin, more than double the ~22 cars/week pace of the earlier Model Y ramp, though Waymo still leads Texas with 1,185 registered vehicles versus Tesla's 739.
- Tesla extended Cybercab operating hours to 6 a.m.–11 p.m., cut wait times (8–11 minutes observed in Austin), added trunk automation and in-car Grok access.
- Jody Kerns, Tesla's Senior Manager of Cybercab General Assembly, is leaving for Rivian to lead R2 assembly — the second Cybercab launch leader to depart this year after Michael So left for a nuclear-reactor startup in April/May.
- Ride pricing shows wide variance: Cybercab trips in Austin averaging roughly $4-5/mile, with surge pricing reportedly as high as ~$23/mile.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's own NHTSA crash filing confirms Autopilot/FSD was "Verified Engaged" in an August 2026 New Jersey crash that killed 68-year-old Phillip Eastman — a fact never disclosed in police reports. Tesla redacted the crash narrative and software version, per Electrek, which argues Tesla should release unredacted details rather than treat them as trade secrets.
- NHTSA's latest Standing General Order data shows Tesla robotaxi incident reports up to 36 (from 22 in July). Two new filings in Dallas and Austin bring to five the total number of known remote-operator-controlled crashes, all of which ended in contact; Waymo says it has never used remote "tele-operation" of the driving task.
- Musk says Tesla's FSD stack is now fully neural-network based, with hand-written driving code entirely phased out.
- Europe renamed Autopilot/FSD to "Tesla Assisted Driving," cutting the speed-limit overage allowance from 50% to 10% over the limit ahead of a December adoption vote.
- GM CEO Mary Barra reiterated a 2028 target for hands-free, eyes-off driving but said GM won't enter the robotaxi business.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk reportedly said China's magnet export restrictions have delayed Optimus by two to three months due to missing components, per a secondhand account from a Musk-hosted X event.
- Optimus's production scale — not dexterity — is framed as Tesla's core bet, with Fremont tracking toward 1 million units/year and eventually 10 million/year; the last publicly shown version was "Optimus 2.5," and hand dexterity specifics remain undisclosed.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

## SpaceX — AI & Compute

TSMC says it's open to working with Terafab but insists on controlling the plants — contradicting Musk's stated plan for Tesla/SpaceX to run it themselves.
