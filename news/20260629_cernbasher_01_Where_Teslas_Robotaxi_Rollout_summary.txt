This video from the Cern Basher channel covers the current state of Tesla's robotaxi rollout, with particular focus on new FSD capability announcements from Elon Musk, Cybercab production and staging activity at Giga Texas, and what various field observations suggest about the pace of autonomous deployment. The hosts analyze a cluster of signals — fleet movements, decal applications, overseas testing — to argue that Tesla is methodically preparing for a broader robotaxi launch rather than experiencing delays. The core thesis is that while there is no single dramatic announcement, the accumulating evidence points toward an imminent expansion of robotaxi service to new cities.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Four-Month Safety Milestone and Parking as the Last Friction Point:** The hosts note that FSD has now gone approximately four months since February without any at-fault incidents, and that global regulatory approvals continue to expand. Elon Musk responded to a user post celebrating a 13-day, 96% autonomous driving streak by stating that upcoming FSD releases will remember individual parking preferences — home, office, school drop-off — calling destination parking "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD." The hosts interpret this as Elon confirming that critical safety is no longer the primary challenge; the remaining friction is convenience and personalization.

2. **Grok Voice Control of FSD Coming in ~3 Months:** In a separate exchange on X, Elon replied to a user requesting the ability to give conversational commands to the car — such as directing drop-off location or instructing the vehicle to park itself remotely — saying this functionality would arrive "in about 3 months or so." The hosts characterize this as robotaxi-level capability: not just a question-and-answer interface like ChatGPT, but direct natural-language control of vehicle behavior while FSD is active.

3. **Persistent Memory as a Platform Shift for FSD:** The hosts discuss at length what it means for FSD to gain persistent memory. Currently, they note, FSD reacts purely to real-time conditions, which is why it can behave differently in identical-looking situations. With memory, the system could learn preferred routes, preferred parking lots at specific locations (not just at home), and even user-specific behavioral patterns. One host raises the possibility that memory could extend to things like known speed enforcement zones. Another host flags a hardware constraint: existing vehicles were not designed to store large amounts of persistent data, though upgrading the onboard storage stick to 5 GB or 10 GB is described as technically trivial and inexpensive. The hosts also note that the upcoming AI5 and AI6 chips will carry significantly larger memory capacity, though they clarify that chip memory is volatile rather than persistent storage.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Staging at Giga Texas Reaching Record Levels:** The hosts report that 102 Cybercabs were spotted in the open lot at Giga Texas — described as a record — up from earlier observations of 20, then 30, then 50 units. One host, drawing on manufacturing experience, interprets the outward movement of vehicles from the factory as a positive signal: companies typically keep brand-new products close to the factory during early reliability validation, and the decision to stage cars at distributed sites suggests Tesla has passed a meaningful maturity threshold. He also notes that available parking near the factory is not the constraint, meaning Tesla is actively choosing to move the vehicles — suggesting something is "forcing their hand" toward deployment.

2. **Cybercabs Now Appearing with Service Decals:** The hosts observe that Cybercabs coming out of the factory are now being fitted with Tesla robotaxi decals on the sides and back, which one host interprets as preparation for vehicles that will actually be put into revenue service rather than held for internal testing. He notes the timeline remains uncertain — Tesla could stage cars in multiple cities before any formal unveil — but this cosmetic preparation is a meaningful signal of forward progress.

3. **Cybercabs Spotted in New Zealand for Testing:** Several Cybercabs were reportedly spotted in New Zealand, which the hosts attribute to a combination of cold-weather testing and left-hand-traffic testing for FSD. One host notes Tesla has used New Zealand as a testing ground for vehicles before, calling it unsurprising. The hosts also note the reaction of local observers who had no idea what the vehicle was.

4. **Q2 Robotaxi City Expansion Deadline Approaching:** The hosts reference a prior Tesla statement that robotaxi service would roll out to additional cities before the end of Q2 2026. Given the video's publish date of June 29, 2026, they note the deadline is essentially upon them and that if Tesla is still on track, an announcement should be imminent. The combination of decal application, large-scale staging, and distributed vehicle movement is presented as circumstantial evidence that Tesla remains on that timeline.

5. **Cybercab Ramp-Up Cadence:** One host shares his expectation that Cybercab production would ramp from dozens of units in April to hundreds in May, reaching thousands of units per week by the end of June. He frames this as his personal projection rather than confirmed data, and notes that without continuous drone coverage it is difficult to track actual output. He also raises the open question of whether Tesla is intentionally pacing Cybercab scale relative to the autonomous network rollout rather than pushing maximum production volume.
