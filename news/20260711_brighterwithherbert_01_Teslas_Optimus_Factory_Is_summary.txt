This episode of the show (hosted by guest Cern Basher filling in, with regular guest Phil) covers humanoid robot competition from Neo and Figure, the scale of Tesla's Optimus robot factory near Giga Texas, an extended discussion of Elon Musk's posts about Anthropic and SpaceX AI compute economics, Starlink speed milestones, FSD's rollout to hardware 3 vehicles in Korea, Grok integration in Japan-market EVs, and a detailed guest presentation on an AI compute demand/supply framework ("the Beisel curve") arguing that only Terafab-scale chip fabrication plus space-based solar/Starlink infrastructure can meet future AI compute demand.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Neo and Figure show off new capabilities:** Neo (a competitor) demoed a humanoid robot with tendon-driven hands rated IP68 (waterproof/food-safe), fingers moving up to 8 meters per second, 25 degrees of freedom (22 actively controlled joints in the hand plus three at the wrist), performing tasks like screwing in a light bulb, sorting objects, gripping a 20-lb weight, and self-charging; the company has manufactured hundreds of hands and targets production capacity of 10,000 hands/year (5,000 robots). Figure also released an update showing robot progress and cited having the first humanoid robot in the White House.
2. **Tesla's Optimus factory scale:** Hosts discuss the Tesla robot factory (documented via aerial footage by Joe Tegtmeyer) being built near Giga Texas, reportedly targeting production capacity of 10 million robots/year, with a footprint just slightly smaller than the Giga Texas car factory; construction is actively expanding southward with steel erection and dirt-moving visible.
3. **Economic impact math (host estimate, not Tesla-stated):** A co-host calculated that at a hypothetical $25,000/unit, 10 million robots/year would generate roughly $250 billion — comparable to the ~$268 billion GDP of the Austin area; at a hypothetical $100,000/unit, output would approach roughly four times Austin's GDP. This is explicitly framed as back-of-envelope host speculation, not a company figure, and reflects only the impact of building the robots, not ongoing economic activity from operating them.
4. **Competitive thesis — manufacturing scale as differentiator:** Phil argues Tesla's edge over Neo/Figure won't be raw dexterity (where competitors may reach comparable capability) but manufacturability at scale; he frames this as mirroring the Waymo-vs-Robotaxi dynamic and stresses that composable, generalizable AI skills plus at-scale hand durability/production are the hard problems Tesla must solve simultaneously.
5. **China humanoid dominance flagged:** Peter Diamandis noted China is shipping ~90% of the world's humanoid robots; Musk responded "For now."

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD v14 "light" rolling out in Korea:** Tesla is rolling out a lighter FSD version to early access owners in Korea for hardware 3 vehicles, with further rollout planned; Phil frames Tesla's willingness to backport advanced FSD to seven-year-old hardware as evidence of a performance-engineering-driven culture.
2. **Grok conversational AI in Japan vehicles:** Tesla is rolling out conversational Grok AI for in-vehicle search in Japan, demonstrated fluidly switching between Japanese and English mid-sentence.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab visibility and production:** Cybercabs are reportedly being spotted publicly (e.g., a Palo Alto sighting) and observed loading onto trucks at Giga Texas alongside Model Y, Model Y L, and Cybertruck units in what hosts described as a high-output period.
2. **FSD subscription/margin thesis:** Phil argues Tesla's shift toward FSD subscriptions (rather than one-time car sales) could dramatically expand margins, analogous to Apple's hardware-to-services transition, with a compounding effect on both revenue and margin.

## Market & Competition
1. **Musk's Anthropic reversal:** Musk posted that he was "clearly wrong about Anthropic," calling them the current AI leader with no released model as good as their latest, and stated he wouldn't cut off their compute access even as a competitor, citing Tesla's open-sourced patents and Supercharger network as precedent for non-walled-garden behavior.
2. **SpaceX AI compute deal scrutiny:** A third-party poster questioned whether the reported SpaceX AI compute deal with Anthropic (cited figures spanning single-digit to ~$40 billion) was a strategic error, suggesting users might shift to SpaceX's own model and complicate Anthropic's IPO; Musk responded that "SpaceX will be worth more than the rest of Earth if we accomplish our goals."
3. **Grok 4.5 efficiency claims:** Musk stated Grok 4.5 isn't yet using SpaceX's internally developed C++ inference software matched to GB300 hardware, and that doubling or more of current speed is "probably achievable."

## Energy
1. **AI compute demand-supply framework ("Beisel curve"):** Phil presented an analysis (partly AI-generated with Grok) modeling AI compute demand growing at roughly 350% year-over-year on a logarithmic scale, arguing that even after performance-engineering optimizations, projected demand vastly outstrips global chip fabrication supply. He argues only large-scale "Terafab" facilities (each producing on the order of a terawatt of annualized compute) combined with space-based solar/compute infrastructure ("Starmind") can close the gap, since terrestrial power grids (citing roughly half a terawatt total US daily consumption) cannot supply the power needed.
2. **Starlink speed milestone:** Musk announced Starlink can now provide reliable 10 gigabits/second symmetric connectivity anywhere in the world, illustrated by a deployment in a remote northern Alaska city (~320 miles above the Arctic Circle) delivering up to 20 gigabits/second via bonded gateways.
