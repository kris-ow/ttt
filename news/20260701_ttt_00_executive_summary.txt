## Robotaxi

- **Production Cybercabs now testing on Austin public roads without steering wheel or pedals.** Tesla officially confirmed engineering validation of production-spec Cybercabs on open public roads in Austin — a regulatory and engineering threshold that directly precedes commercial deployment, distinct from the supervised pilot fleet already operating there. Joe Tegtmeyer separately reported extensive pickup and drop-off testing around Giga Texas, simulating passenger activities. A Cybercab with decals, no steering wheel, and a safety monitor on the passenger side was also spotted in Austin.

- **Cybercab first responder guide published — confirms fleet-wide coordination capabilities.** Tesla's official safety manual for emergency personnel reveals that responders can call Tesla's robotaxi support team to impose a temporary geofence blocking Cybercab traffic within ~1,000 feet of a location, rerouting the entire fleet before any vehicle arrives. The guide also confirms: the Cybercab uses cameras and dedicated sound detection to recognize emergency vehicles, pulls over, disengages autonomy, unlocks doors, and opens a two-way audio line to Tesla support. Post-crash, the car parks itself, flashes hazards, unlocks doors, rolls down windows, and auto-connects to Tesla support.

- **Confirmed Cybercab interior details from the guide and production video.** The first responder guide confirms electrically adjustable seats via touchscreen, exterior B-pillar microphones, power windows, and a two-seat cabin with a single landscape touchscreen — no glove box, no steering wheel, no pedals. Production video released this week revealed notably thick A-pillars, consistent with Tesla's crash-integrity engineering on other models.

- **Cybercab manufacturing line is ~90% automated; "full self-hearing" NVH testing deployed.** Per Lars Moravy (Tesla VP Vehicle Engineering) in an interview with content creators Herbert and Jeff, the Cybercab line automation already exceeds 90%. Tesla has also implemented what it calls "full self-hearing" — onboard microphones autonomously listen for creaks and rattles during NVH testing, with AI identifying noise sources and flagging required fixes. Moravy characterized production as on track with internal expectations, with no major issues found during ramp-up.

- **Staged Cybercabs across Texas cities signal rapid deployment once go-decision is made.** More than 100 Cybercabs are described as staged at Giga Texas, with close to 100 in Dallas and growing activity in Houston. Jo Bhakdi, who has publicly missed prior deployment timelines (April/May 2025, June 2026), maintains July 2026 conviction — pointing to first responder documentation filed on an official Texas state government website (with Arizona addendums legally required for commercial operation) as a signal that deployment is legally imminent rather than distant.

- **Coral Gables, Florida fire personnel trained with Tesla engineers on the robotaxi platform**, with Tesla indicating potential Miami-area expansion in the future — the first public signal of a Florida market entry.

- **Jo Bhakdi's Cybercab scaling conditional:** if Tesla reaches 1,000 deployed Cybercabs by August 2026, he believes 10,000+ by year-end is achievable, and expects a stock repricing to $700–$1,000 once the fleet visibly compounds and Wall Street recognizes the constraint is pure manufacturing, not regulatory or technical.

## Humanoid Bots

- **First Optimus manufacturing line has physically arrived at Fremont.** Lars Moravy confirmed in the Herbert/Jeff interview that the Model S/X lines at Fremont have been largely cleared and the first Optimus manufacturing line has been delivered to the U.S. Installation has already begun. Validation of machines happens at Tesla's German automation facility (formerly Kuka/Grohmann Engineering), after which lines ship to the U.S. and can be powered on in 2–3 days and fully proved out in about a week.

- **~40 additional Optimus lines still to come; production not expected until Q4 2026 at earliest.** Moravy indicated roughly 40 more lines are required, covering actuators, each limb and limb subcomponents, the torso, and battery pack. Despite fast individual setup times, the sheer number of lines pushes production start to Q4 2026 at the earliest.

- **Elon Musk directly pushed back on speculation of an imminent large-scale Optimus demo.** Responding to social media speculation (from a user called Dr. Jack) that engineers were ahead of schedule and a major production reveal was imminent, Musk stated: *"Optimus production will be extremely slow at first as everything is new. This is not like making a car."* Musk separately shared a photo captioned as him walking the Optimus production line at Fremont.

- **Jo Bhakdi on Optimus competitive positioning:** Bhakdi argues Tesla will achieve "total domination" in humanoid robotics, with the decisive variable being manufacturing scale — "the machine that builds the machine" — rather than robot capability alone. He plans to cover the thesis in depth in a future video.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD pickup/drop-off location memory identified as the key remaining gap for commercial service.** The host of the Herbert channel, who has taken Tesla's robotaxi service in California multiple times, cites one recurring issue as the primary reason full commercial launch has not yet occurred: the vehicle routes passengers to suboptimal pickup locations (e.g., across a parking lot rather than to the nearest lobby). He connects this to Elon Musk's prior statement that upcoming FSD releases would incorporate memory of preferred locations at recurring destinations, and believes that feature is in testing with a 1–2 month solidification timeline.

- **Tesla Semi fatal crash raises AEB and drowsiness detection questions for the platform.** A Tesla Semi rear-ended two stopped passenger vehicles on June 28 in Dayton, Nevada; the driver reportedly fell asleep. Tesla does not offer FSD on the Semi, and this was a fully manual-control incident. The open question per Electrek is whether the Semi's automatic emergency braking engaged — Tesla has never published a Semi-specific AEB specification. Tesla has also not confirmed whether the Semi has a cabin-facing camera or drowsiness detection equivalent to the system introduced for passenger cars in 2023.

## Electric Vehicles

- **BYD Q2 2026 BEV deliveries: 557,090 — more than 160,000 units ahead of Tesla's expected ~396,500.** Tesla had narrowly led BYD in Q1 2026 (358,023 deliveries), but BYD's domestic dip from China's eliminated EV purchase-tax exemption was temporary. Tesla's official Q2 figures are due July 2; Wall Street consensus is ~406,000, with tracker forecasts ranging from Gary Black at 420,000 to James Stevenson at 482,000.

- **Cybertruck Power Conversion System failures are widespread and Tesla is not issuing a recall.** A self-selected poll on Cybertruck Owners Club (223 respondents) found 40.8% reporting confirmed PCS replacements under warranty. Failures create an "AC Charging Unavailable" fault, cutting Level 2 charging and throwing MOSFET-related codes; most retain DC fast-charging. Out-of-warranty repair costs ran $5,000–$7,200 before Tesla reduced them to ~$1,000 as a "goodwill" gesture. Tesla quietly added 7-year/70,000-mile propulsion coverage (explicitly including the PCS) for 2026 Cybertrucks — not retroactive to the Foundation Series trucks most exposed to the defect. Replacement units are on national backorder with 14+ day lead times reported.

- **Tesla Semi effective price: ~$50,000 after California incentive stacking.** Qualifying California fleet operators can combine the HVIP clean truck voucher (up to $120,000) with the California Clean Fuel Reward (up to $120,000) for a maximum $240,000 reduction on a truck estimated at ~$290,000. Washington and Oregon offer additional incentives.

- **BMW iX5 unveiled at $81,250** with 435-mile EPA range, 144 kWh battery, 800V architecture, 460 kW peak charging, and bidirectional charging — the most directly spec-competitive premium EV to Tesla's lineup disclosed this week.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO — largest in history at ~$1.77 trillion; valuation debate is wide open.** SpaceX priced at $135/share on Nasdaq (SPCX), raising ~$85 billion — more than 2.5× the prior record (Saudi Aramco, ~$29B in 2019). Morningstar's DCF arrives at ~$780 billion (~half the IPO price); Oppenheimer issued an outperform at $190/share (~$2.5 trillion implied). At ~$2 trillion, SpaceX trades at approximately 107× annual revenue vs. 10–30× typical for high-growth tech. Q1 2026 SpaceX revenue was ~$4.69 billion, with ~69% from Starlink connectivity; Starlink EBITDA margin is ~63%.

- **Tesla–SpaceX merger speculation is active; Jordan Giesige (The Limiting Factor) opposes near-term terms.** Prediction markets have bets placed on a merger before spring 2027. Giesige argues a merger now would dilute Tesla shareholders by blending Tesla's ~1–2 year robotaxi revaluation with SpaceX's longer-dated 2–4 year timeline, delaying gains at a 15–20% annual opportunity cost. His minimum acceptable terms: $800/share for Tesla or Tesla comprising ≥60% of the combined entity. He estimates the most likely deal is a merger of equals at roughly $500–$600/share for Tesla (30–50% premium).

- **Terafab lands first major semiconductor hire.** Tesla hired Gary Jiang — nearly 18 years at Intel, most recently overseeing construction of Intel's high-volume Arizona fab and transfer of Intel's 18A (1.8nm) process — as Director of Tera Fab. Terafab is set to license Intel's next-generation 14A (1.4nm) process, with Tesla reportedly Intel's first 14A customer. The pilot line is planned near Giga Texas; the full facility (estimated ~5× larger) at a separate already-selected site. Realistic pilot line timeline per community analysis: proof chips around 2030; full-scale production 2032–2033. SpaceX and Tesla are each reportedly planning to invest $20 billion in the joint venture; compute allocation is ~20% Earth-based, ~80% orbital.

- **Ford CEO Jim Farley stated Chinese automakers are "completely dominating the EV landscape globally" and that "there's no real competition from Tesla, GM or Ford."** Sawyer Merritt noted the Tesla Model Y is one of the best-selling cars in China, implying Farley's framing may not apply uniformly to Tesla.

- **Grok 4.5 — 1.5 trillion parameter model — is in private beta at SpaceX and Tesla.** Elon Musk posted that early evaluations show performance "close to, perhaps exceeding" Anthropic's Opus. A 2 trillion parameter model began training "a few weeks ago" (relative to early July 2026) with an August 2026 release target. Musk projects "truly massive gains" in approximately three months when the training and inference stack is rewritten in C/C++ and mapped precisely to Nvidia's GB300 architecture. Jo Bhakdi frames the $60 billion Cursor acquisition as strategically decisive, adding enterprise distribution, elite AI talent, and "harness" layer expertise (memory, reasoning loops, meta-thinking scaffolding) that Grok previously lacked.
