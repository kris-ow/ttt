This episode of Electrified covers a proposed FAA rule to waive environmental-statute requirements for commercial space launch licensing (a potential Starship cadence unlock), a detailed recap of Starship Flight 13's mostly successful test (with a booster landing-burn failure and a floating, recoverable ship in the Indian Ocean), a new SpaceX Space Force launch contract, and several Tesla-related items including a Model Y vs. Rivian R2 real-world range test, an FSD safety incident report, and Waymo platform updates, closing with a market wrap noting TSLA's decline after the Fed's rate statement.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **FAA proposes waiving 13 environmental statutes for launch licensing:** Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced an FAA proposal to waive requirements under 13 laws (including NEPA, the Endangered Species Act, Clean Water/Air Acts, and others) for commercial space launch and re-entry licenses, provided they aren't necessary for public safety, property safety, or national security. The host notes this targets the exact environmental review bottlenecks that have historically slowed Starbase cadence increases, though a 30-day comment period and likely environmental-group litigation remain ahead; safety and national-security reviews stay intact.
2. **SpaceX wins $1.6 billion Space Force contract:** SpaceX secured a new $1.6 billion task order from the US Space Force covering 18 Falcon 9 launches from Vandenberg through the end of 2027, supporting the Space-Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio (missile detection, airborne target tracking, battlefield data relay). It is described as the largest publicly announced task order so far under the National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane One program.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 13 achieves key milestones despite booster landing-burn failure:** Flight 13's booster completed its full boost-back burn with all 33 engines (a first for V3), but the landing burn only relit 8 of 13 engines (three failed within seconds), causing a harder-than-planned splashdown. The ship, however, made a very soft splashdown in the Indian Ocean, kept transmitting telemetry, deployed 20 functional next-gen Starlink V3 satellites, and completed an in-space engine relight roughly 40 minutes into flight — a test that had been aborted on Flight 12. Elon Musk said the flight intentionally used higher acceleration to test heat-shield tile retention at high dynamic pressure, calling the test successful.
2. **Flight cadence data and Flight 14 catch attempt planned:** Across 13 Starship launches over 39 months since the April 2023 maiden flight, the average turnaround has been 99 days (fastest: 37 days between Flights 5–6; slowest: 221 days between Flights 11–12 during the V2-to-V3 transition; 63 days between Flights 12–13). Musk said SpaceX will attempt to catch the ship with the tower on Flight 14 (barring issues found in data review), while the booster will again attempt a water landing given Flight 13's landing-burn issues. Flight 14 is expected to attempt an orbital insertion, a shift from the roughly 90-minute suborbital flights 1–12.
3. **S40 recovery expected, precise "landing" confirmed by Musk:** Musk confirmed SpaceX is sending a ship to recover the floating S40 booster/ship from the Indian Ocean, and said the splashdown was precise enough that it would have been caught by the tower arms had this been an actual catch attempt. The host speculates (his own conjecture, not confirmed by SpaceX) that S40 will likely be towed to a Western Australian port for inspection and partial component salvage before being scrapped, rather than fully recovered and shipped back to the US.
4. **Third-party analysis: heat shield reuse could cut launch costs ~45-58%:** An analyst referred to as Brett (from an outlet the host calls "Mark") argued that even a single reuse of the Flight 13 ship before eventual re-entry burn-up would cut cost-per-kilogram-to-orbit by roughly 45% versus an expendable Starship upper stage and 58% versus Falcon 9's estimated cost. The same analysis projects that mastering Starship reusability this year could raise the firm's 2031 SpaceX revenue/EBIT estimates roughly three-fold and 2036 estimates 6-7x, with reusable vs. disposable upper-stage costs pegged at $100/kg versus $570/kg to orbit.
5. **Rapid tile replacement capability cited:** A commentator referred to as Aaron Bernett stated SpaceX can already place all heat-shield tiles on a new ship in under a month (potentially a couple of weeks), replacing rather than individually testing each tile, with burn-through to the underlying steel being the main risk — which Flight 13 data suggests is not occurring.
6. **Musk reiterates Mars timeline:** Musk said people could be on Mars in roughly 5 to 7 years, with an uncrewed Mars lander arriving a few years sooner.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **First functional V3 Starlink satellites deployed and tested on Flight 13:** The 20 V3 satellites carried on Flight 13 were fully functional units (not mass simulators); SpaceX confirmed two-way radio-frequency and laser communication with the full batch and downloaded telemetry, with six satellites carrying cameras to image the ship's heat shield (some tiles were deliberately painted white as imaging targets). The host clarifies that despite some outlet reports, SpaceX's own summary confirmed only engineer-to-satellite communication and telemetry — not full integration into the operational Starlink constellation — before the satellites burned up on their suborbital trajectory after about 20 minutes.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Unverified report of an FSD-related highway incident:** The host flagged a Reddit post (unverified, origin unclear) captioned "FSD14 light hits a wall," showing a vehicle apparently drifting and hitting a concrete barrier, with the original poster claiming responsibility for looking away and noting prior near-misses on right curves. The host stressed this is unconfirmed and urged viewers to remain vigilant with FSD regardless.
2. **Musk comments on lidar vs. vision approach:** Responding to a post about Volvo discontinuing lidar sensors, Musk said he "did try to warn them," reiterating that humans and robotic cars alike should rely on neural nets and optical sensors.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y outperforms EPA estimate; Rivian R2 underperforms in real-world range test:** In a 70 mph highway range test by Kyle and the Out of Spec team, the Rivian R2's EPA estimate of 330 miles came in at an actual 253 miles (using ~89 kWh), a significant underperformance. Tesla's Model Y, with an EPA estimate of 306 miles, achieved 310 miles in the same test using about 83 kWh — outperforming its rated range.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo relaunches Ohio UI with Gemini integration and resumes freeway routes:** Waymo announced a revamped in-car UI for a new Ohio vehicle platform featuring Google Gemini integration, with early access live and wider rollout planned. Separately, after a multi-week shutdown, Waymo began gradually resuming freeway routes starting in Phoenix; the earlier freeway pause had been due to vehicles driving into active construction zones (a separate, coincidentally simultaneous issue involved vehicles struggling in flooded areas).
