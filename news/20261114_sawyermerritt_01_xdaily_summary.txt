## Executive Summary
The biggest news is Tesla officially opening personal-use Cybercab orders to select Austin customers at $29,990 plus a required $299/month autonomy subscription, with deliveries starting December 2026 — a major robotaxi-thesis milestone. Simultaneously, SpaceX attempted Starship flight 15 with FAA approval for the first full booster-and-ship catch attempt, while Tesla also quietly raised Model Y RWD range and tightened Model Y trim availability/lead times.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Cybercab personal-use ordering officially begins:** Tesla has started inviting select customers in Austin to order the Cybercab for personal use, per Sawyer Merritt. Key terms: price $29,990, a $1,000 non-refundable order fee, and a mandatory $299/month Autonomy Subscription (one month included with purchase), with deliveries starting December 2026. The subscription agreement reportedly restricts the vehicle to personal (non-commercial) use for now, prohibits modifications (wraps, window tint, etc.), and includes maintenance items such as front/rear tire replacement, tire rotations, pressure/wear/damage inspections, flat tire replacement, and windshield/wiper replacement. This is a concrete, near-term catalyst for the robotaxi/Cybercab thesis — moving from pilot fleet to individual consumer ownership with a subscription-based autonomy model.

2. **Starship flight 15 — first full reusable-system catch attempt:** SpaceX received FAA approval and launched Starship flight 15, described by Merritt as the first attempt to recover both the booster and the ship back at the launch site via catch. He characterized full reusable recovery of an orbital launch system as potentially "one of the most important moments in human history" if all mission objectives are met — a framing of significance rather than a confirmed outcome. The liftoff was confirmed successful; the digest does not confirm landing/catch results. SpaceX also teased next-gen V4 Starlink satellites (focused on a larger power system and better cooling) and showed manufacturing of V3 Starlink satellites underway.

3. **Starlink competitive validation from United Airlines CEO:** In a new interview, United's CEO said in-flight WiFi is the "No. 1 reason" people choose an airline and called it the biggest near-term competitive advantage in United's history, implicitly tied to Starlink's onboard WiFi deal. He also commented on Delta choosing Amazon's LEO satellite service instead of Starlink, calling it "a mistake" and saying he disagrees with that decision — a competitor executive's opinion, not new data on Starlink's actual market share or financials.

4. **Tesla Model Y pricing/range/availability shifts:** Tesla raised the Model Y RWD's U.S. range rating by 8.5% to 348 miles (from 321), while keeping the price at $39,990. Separately, Tesla removed the $41,990 Model Y AWD trim from the U.S. order page, and all Model Y Premium trims are now sold out for 2026, with new-order estimated deliveries pushed to Jan–March 2027 (from the prior Dec 2026–Jan 2027 window). Model Y L delivery estimates were also mentioned as shifting, though specific new dates weren't given in the post.
