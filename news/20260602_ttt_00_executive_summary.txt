## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Europe May 2026 surge:** France posted +655% YoY registrations — described as the best-selling May ever for Tesla in that market. Denmark (+136%), Spain (+113%), Sweden (+71%), and Norway (+29%) also recovered strongly, with the Model Y ranking as Denmark's best-selling vehicle overall across all powertrains and the Model 3 placing fifth.
- **Denmark BEV record:** 15,020 BEVs out of 19,092 new passenger car registrations in May, pushing BEV share to a record high. The breadth of gains across five markets is a positive signal for Q2 2026 delivery expectations.
- **Covenant Logistics Tesla Semi evaluation:** The publicly traded trucking operator (2,600+ tractors, $1.16B revenue) completed two weeks of testing including a loaded run over Tejon Pass (4,160 ft, 6% grade for 5 miles). The driver was "amazed at the performance" and said it offered "a level of confidence that was hard to match in a diesel truck." Regenerative braking on the steep southbound descent was flagged as a structural advantage over diesel.
- **Growing fleet trial pattern:** ArcBest logged 4,494 miles at 1.55 kWh/mile; DHL completed 3,000 miles at 1.72 kWh/mile and has taken delivery of its first Semi; CEVA avoided 4.38 metric tons of CO₂. Tesla holds a 60-truck port drayage order. Volume production at Giga Nevada began April 29.
- **4680 energy density globally competitive, not class-leading:** Tesla's current 4680 achieves ~270 Wh/kg, placing it between last-gen (250–270 Wh/kg) and newest-gen (280–290+ Wh/kg) Panasonic/LG cells. The 4680-equipped new Model Y in Germany posts ~5% less range than vehicles with the newest competing packs — framed as a value-for-money trade-off given lower price.
- **2170 cells to continue for existing models:** Tesla's working plan keeps 2170 cells supplying current passenger platforms; an upgrade reportedly expected within ~18 months would improve charging speed and match or slightly improve energy density. Panasonic's new Kansas City 2170 facility reinforces this supply path.

## Robotaxi
- **Fourth Tesla fleet support facility confirmed in Texas:** A 51,000 sq ft service and delivery center is under construction in Grand Prairie (between Dallas and Fort Worth, near I-20). City documents confirm approved uses include charging, cleaning, repair, and maintenance of Tesla-owned autonomous vehicles. The four known facilities now span Grand Prairie, a separate Dallas-area site (36,000 sq ft), Austin, and Las Vegas.
- **Camera hardware gap between Robotaxi fleet and consumer vehicles:** Tesla's Austin Robotaxi Model Y fleet uses external camera washer jets on fender repeater and B-pillar cameras — the repeater cameras have been redesigned with the turn signal removed in favor of a washer nozzle. No consumer Model Y includes this hardware, and it is not retrofittable. Tesla has separately added a new front-facing camera cleaning guide in service mode. Electrek notes this contradicts Tesla's claim that its Robotaxi service uses "the same Model Y vehicles it sells to consumers."
- **New camera wiper patent granted:** Patent US 12,636,684 B1 ("Lens Cleaning System," granted May 26) integrates a fluid dispenser and curved mechanical wiper directly into the camera assembly, activating automatically when image quality degrades rather than on a fixed schedule. The system supports water, alcohol-based solutions, washer fluid, or lubricants. With FSD running vision-only, a blocked camera is effectively a blind spot — and the Cybercab, now in production, must operate without human intervention.
- **World Cup Cybercab launch theory assessed skeptically:** A theory that Tesla will time a Dallas Cybercab commercial launch to coincide with FIFA World Cup games at Cowboys Stadium in Arlington was discussed. The Grand Prairie facility will not be complete in time, and the consensus view is that FSD still requires additional real-world data before large-scale unsupervised commercial deployment — though hardware is assessed as ready given Tesla's commitment to start of production.
- **Bhakdi's 15,000 unsupervised Cybercab target for end of 2026:** Bhakdi reiterates his projection of 15,000 unsupervised Cybercabs operating by year-end 2026, framing it as the key valuation catalyst that would drive Tesla toward ~$800/share (~$3 trillion market cap).
- **Cybercab battery pack prototyping confirmed at Giga Nevada:** During a Giga Nevada facility tour, a guide cited the manual pack assembly line's ability to produce 23 Cybercab battery packs on demand — confirming small-batch custom pack work for programs still in development.

## Autonomous Driving
- **Geofenced unsupervised Robotaxi areas as the logical bridge to personal unsupervised FSD:** If Tesla is already operating driverless robotaxis within specific geofences in Austin, Dallas, and Houston, extending unsupervised personal FSD to those same areas is described as the natural next step — no new safety threshold required, just a policy decision.
- **Tesla insurance on unsupervised FSD framed as structurally profitable:** Jeff Lus argues that Tesla's actuarial data already demonstrates the system is not at fault in accidents, making an insurance product for unsupervised FSD essentially a premium revenue stream with minimal payout risk. Tesla already sells insurance in multiple regions and has reportedly established a licensed entity in China.
- **FSD nag removed in testing:** Personal testing of a newer FSD version shows the driver monitoring nag has been removed, allowing increasingly long unsupervised intervals — though the system remains officially labeled as supervised FSD.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus facility under construction at Giga Texas north campus:** A dedicated Optimus building is reportedly under construction on the north campus of Giga Texas, confirmed in the context of assessing whether 4680 production capacity at the site is sufficient to supply both vehicle and robot needs.
- **Tesla building toward 1 million Optimus units; longer-term target 10 million:** Per FutureAzA, Tesla is already constructing capacity for 1 million units, with a stated longer-term target of 10 million. The ability to build factories rapidly is characterized as a structural advantage over any new entrant.
- **Hyundai/Boston Dynamics named most credible second competitor:** Bhakdi identifies Hyundai — citing its Boston Dynamics acquisition, industrial deployment capability, and South Korea's manufacturing strength — as the second most formidable robotics player after Tesla. He dismisses Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI as near-term threats due to software-first DNA and zero factory experience.
- **OpenAI robotics push met with skepticism:** OpenAI posted job listings for a humanoid robotics division. Both Bhakdi and Herbert predict OpenAI will ultimately need to acquire an existing robotics company, drawing a parallel to their predicted Waymo/automaker acquisition thesis.

## Energy
- **Q2 2026 Megapack deployment rebound expected:** Q1 2026 energy deployments came in at 8.8 GWh — described as a "low lump" — against current Megapack capacity of 17 GWh. Q2 2026 projections range from a floor of 16–17 GWh to 21–23 GWh or higher if deferred prior-quarter deployments are counted. Revenue is recognized at electrification/substantial completion, not at shipment.
- **Battery constraint is at the pack level, not cells:** Tesla confirmed on a recent earnings call that the bottleneck is pack assembly, not cell supply. The constraint stems from transitioning previously outsourced pack production in-house and upgrading older Giga Nevada lines — analogous to the production dip during the Model Y to Juniper transition.
- **Tesla still importing most Megapack cells; internal LFP capacity at ~10 GWh:** Tesla's internal LFP capacity is approximately 10 GWh while at least one external supplier produces 30–40 GWh/year — confirming material external dependency for energy storage. Megapack packs use LFP chemistry.

## Market & Competition
- **Waymo loses ~$100/ride on a fleet of ~3,700 vehicles:** Total estimated losses of $5–6 billion are cited, with ~$100 lost per ride. Waymo also issued a voluntary software recall covering ~3,800 fifth- and sixth-gen robotaxis for a flood/standing water navigation failure (resolved via software update) and temporarily paused freeway rides over construction zone issues — encountering ~10,000 construction zones daily.
- **Waymo's new "Oho" van (Zeekr-based) now carrying public passengers:** Sixth-gen hardware features 13 cameras, 4 lidars, and 6 radars — 42% fewer sensors than prior gen — with 17-megapixel cameras. Projected cost: $75,000–$100,000 per vehicle after tariffs vs. ~$200,000 for the Jaguar I-Pace fleet. Jeff Lus cautions the estimate assumes Zeekr hits sufficient production volumes; landed cost could be materially higher otherwise. Selected riders in San Francisco, Phoenix, and Los Angeles have access, with expansion planned to Denver, Las Vegas, and San Diego.
- **BYD offers industry-first full damage coverage for its Level 2 "God's Eye" system:** One free year of 100% damage coverage — vehicle repairs, third-party property damage, and personal injury with no payout cap — for China owners using the system in compliance with traffic rules. The move comes as BYD sales reportedly dropped 36% amid user complaints of unintended acceleration, erratic steering, and navigation failures, and as Chinese regulators banned "autonomous driving" and "smart driving" marketing terms and drafted stricter L2 safety rules. Tesla can no longer use the "Full Self-Driving" label in China.
- **SpaceX S1 authorizes 36 billion shares — Bhakdi reads it as Tesla acquisition signal:** SpaceX's amended S1 (IPO date: June 12, 2026) has ~12.86 billion shares currently outstanding against 36 billion authorized — roughly 3x current issuance. Bhakdi argues this is far too large for capital raises or normal acquisitions, and can only be intended for a major acquisition of a company valued two to three times SpaceX — i.e., Tesla. His central scenario: Tesla reaches ~$3 trillion (~$800/share) in early 2027 while SpaceX deflates ~25% from its IPO valuation to ~$1.5 trillion, setting up a 1-to-2 share exchange. Legal disclosure constraints prevent any merger announcement immediately post-IPO, placing earliest realistic initiation in 2027. Bhakdi is not participating in the SpaceX IPO and views Tesla as the superior vehicle for capturing combined upside.
- **Alphabet raising $80 billion for AI compute:** $30B underwritten offering + $40B ATM + $10B Berkshire Hathaway investment, earmarked for AI infrastructure. Google Q1 revenue: $110B (+22% YoY); Cloud backlog: $46B; capex plan: $180–$190B.
- **Anthropic IPO filing and $65B Series H:** Confidential S-1 filed with the SEC; $65B raised at ~$965B valuation, led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Green Oaks, and Sequoia. Reported ARR of ~$47B. Bhakdi flagged model-update fragility as a risk for enterprise customers.
- **Legacy automakers pitched as Tesla alternatives — rejected:** A 24/7 Wall Street article recommends GM, Ford, and Stellantis for retirees citing Tesla's 373x trailing P/E. FutureAzA hosts argue ICE dependence makes all three poor long-term bets. Stellantis is trading at $7.35 (–32% YTD, price-to-book 0.32). Ford posts 11.4% margins and 30% YoY software subscription growth but carries ~2.4x Tesla's recall rate.

## Financials
- **IRA credits: $35/kWh for domestic cell manufacturing, $10/kWh for pack assembly (~$700/vehicle):** By manufacturing 4680 cells domestically, Tesla qualifies for the $35/kWh production tax credit. Shipping cells to Germany for pack assembly forfeits the $700-per-vehicle pack credit, implying the current arrangement must offer at least equivalent cost savings to justify it.
- **Tesla Q1 2026: automotive revenue +16% YoY, deliveries +6%, inventory recovering from historic lows.** Revenue was still 19% below the same period in 2023. Inventory rose from what is characterized as unsustainably low levels to a still industry-low days-of-supply figure.
- **Cortex compute capacity may be offered for rent:** Tesla's Cortex facility — designed for real-world AI training rather than standard LLM workloads — is described as likely having surplus capacity. Randy Kirk raises the possibility Tesla could monetize this externally, drawing an analogy to AWS.
