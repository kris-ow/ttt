## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Europe May 2026 surge:** France posted +655% YoY registrations — its best-selling May ever. Denmark (+136%), Spain (+113%), Sweden (+71%), and Norway (+29%) also gained. The Model Y was Denmark's best-selling vehicle overall across all powertrains; the Model 3 placed fifth. Denmark also set a new national BEV record with 15,020 BEVs out of 19,092 registrations (79% share). Breadth across five markets is a positive signal for Q2 2026 deliveries.

- **Tesla Semi commercial validation deepens:** Covenant Logistics (2,600+ tractors, $1.16B 2025 revenue) completed a two-week evaluation including a loaded run over Tejon Pass (4,160 ft, 6% grade) — one of the most demanding US freight corridors. Covenant's VP of Sustainability called driver confidence "hard to match in a diesel truck" and cited regenerative braking as a structural advantage on the descent. Covenant joins ArcBest (4,494 miles, 1.55 kWh/mi), DHL (3,000 miles, 1.72 kWh/mi at full load), and CEVA as fleet operators completing structured trials since volume production began at Giga Nevada on April 29.

- **4680 cells competitive but not best-in-class:** Current 4680 energy density is approximately 270 Wh/kg, per The Limiting Factor's Jordan — between last-gen (250–270 Wh/kg) and newest Panasonic/LG cells (280–290+ Wh/kg). The 4680-equipped new Model Y in Germany delivers roughly 5% less range than vehicles using the newest competing packs, though at a lower price point. Giga Berlin is still importing cells and assembling modules locally; local cell production is targeted for 2027.

## Robotaxi

- **Fourth Tesla fleet support facility identified:** A 51,000-square-foot service and delivery center is under construction in Grand Prairie, Texas (between Dallas and Fort Worth, near I-20). City documents confirm approved use includes charging, cleaning, repair, and maintenance of Tesla-owned autonomous vehicles. The four known facilities are now: Grand Prairie (51K sq ft), a second Dallas-area location (36K sq ft), Austin, and Las Vegas.

- **Cybercab battery packs in small-batch production at Giga Nevada:** During a factory tour, a guide cited producing 23 Cybercab battery packs on demand as an example use case for Giga Nevada's fully manual prototyping line — indicating active small-batch pack work for the program.

- **Bhakdi reiterates 15,000 unsupervised Cybercabs by end of 2026:** Bhakdi stands behind this projection as the valuation catalyst that would drive Tesla to ~$800/share (~$3 trillion market cap), acknowledging the target draws criticism. No independent corroboration appeared in today's sources.

- **Waymo's Oho van now in public service:** Waymo launched its new Zeekr-based "Oho" van with sixth-generation hardware (13 cameras, 4 lidars, 6 radars — 42% fewer sensors than prior gen, with upgraded 17-megapixel cameras) in San Francisco, Phoenix, and Los Angeles. Target: 1,000 vehicles on the road by year-end 2026. Industry analyst Barely AI projects vehicle cost at $75,000–$100,000 after tariffs, versus ~$200,000 for the current Jaguar I-Pace fleet. Waymo also issued a voluntary software recall covering ~3,800 vehicles for a flood-navigation issue and temporarily paused freeway rides near construction zones. Estimated losses: ~$100 per ride.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Tesla camera-wiper patent granted:** Patent US 12,636,684 B1 (granted May 26) covers a "Lens Cleaning System" integrating a fluid dispenser and curved mechanical wiper directly into the camera assembly, activating automatically when image degradation is detected. FSD v14 introduced targeted front-camera cleaning on the new Model Y, but side and rear cameras on consumer vehicles remain unaddressed.

- **Camera hardware gap between Robotaxi fleet and consumer vehicles:** Austin Robotaxi Model Ys already carry external washer jets on fender repeater and B-pillar cameras — hardware not present on any consumer Model Y and not retrofittable. Electrek notes this contradicts Tesla's repeated claim that its Robotaxi fleet uses "the same Model Y vehicles it sells to consumers." The patent may be critical for Cybercab, which must operate without human intervention.

- **Geofenced unsupervised Robotaxi zones as the logical first step for consumer FSD:** Multiple commentators framed Tesla's path to unsupervised personal FSD as an extension of the geofences already active for Robotaxi operations in Austin, Dallas, and Houston — if Tesla is comfortable running driverless vehicles in those corridors, unlocking personal FSD in the same areas requires no new geographic validation.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Dedicated Optimus facility under construction at Giga Texas north campus:** Jordan (The Limiting Factor) confirmed a new Optimus-specific building is being constructed on the Giga Texas north campus, consistent with Tesla building capacity toward 1 million units with a longer-term target of 10 million per year.

- **OpenAI enters robotics; analysts skeptical on execution:** OpenAI posted job listings for a robotics division, signaling a renewed push into humanoid robots. Bhakdi and Herbert both predicted OpenAI will ultimately need to acquire an existing humanoid company, citing zero factory experience and no manufacturing infrastructure. Bhakdi named Hyundai/Boston Dynamics as the most credible second-place competitor to Optimus, citing Hyundai's industrial scale and South Korea's manufacturing ecosystem.

## Energy

- **Q2 2026 Megapack deployment expected to rebound sharply:** Q1 2026 came in at 8.8 GWh — described as a "low lump" tied to revenue recognition timing (units recognized only when electrified and fully paid). Randy Kirk (FutureAzA) expects Q2 to rebound to a minimum of 16–17 GWh, with a potential range of 21–23 GWh or higher if deferred Q1 deployments are counted. Current Megapack nameplate capacity is 17 GWh.

- **Battery constraint is at the pack level, not cells:** Jordan confirmed Tesla's stated battery constraint is a pack assembly bottleneck, not a cell supply issue. The cause: Tesla is transitioning previously outsourced pack production in-house and upgrading older lines at Giga Nevada — a process analogous to the production dip during the Model Y-to-Juniper transition in 2025.

- **Megapack still largely dependent on imported LFP cells:** Jordan stated Tesla is still importing the majority of Megapack cells. Tesla's internal LFP capacity is approximately 10 GWh annually; at least one external supplier produces 30–40 GWh/year, indicating material external dependency for energy storage.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX IPO on June 12; Bhakdi argues 36B authorized shares signal a Tesla merger:** SpaceX's amended S-1 reveals ~12.86 billion shares currently issued but 36 billion authorized — roughly 3x the current count. Bhakdi argues this excess authorization is structurally inexplicable by capital raises or conventional acquisitions, and is sized for a 1-to-2 share exchange to acquire Tesla. His scenario: Tesla reaches ~$3 trillion (~$800/share) by early 2027 while SpaceX deflates ~25% post-IPO to ~$1.5 trillion, making a 1-to-2 merger the logical structure. He notes legal disclosure constraints prevent any merger announcement until well after the June 12 IPO, placing earliest initiation in 2027. Bhakdi is not participating in the SpaceX IPO and holds Tesla as his preferred vehicle.

- **BYD offers industry-first full liability coverage for L2 system — amid product problems:** BYD announced one free year of 100% damage coverage (vehicle repairs, third-party property, personal injury, no payout cap) for its "God's Eye" Level 2 system in China — the first such coverage by any automaker. The announcement came amid user reports of unintended acceleration, erratic steering, and navigation failures, with BYD sales reportedly down 36%. Chinese regulators responded by banning marketing terms like "autonomous driving" and drafting stricter L2 safety rules, affecting BYD, Tesla, Xiaomi, Xpeng, and NIO. Tesla can no longer use the "Full Self-Driving" label in China.

- **AI infrastructure spending accelerates across major players:** Anthropic raised $65 billion (Series H, ~$965 billion valuation) and filed a confidential S-1. Alphabet announced an $80 billion equity raise ($30B underwritten, $40B ATM, $10B from Berkshire Hathaway) earmarked for AI compute, alongside Q1 revenue of $110 billion (+22% YoY) and a $180–190 billion capex plan. Nvidia's RTX Spark Superchip (Blackwell architecture, ~20–24 CPU cores, ~60 GPU cores) debuted at Computex for Dell and Lenovo; shares rose ~6%.
