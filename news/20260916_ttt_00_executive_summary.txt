## Brief

- Starship Flight 14 targeted Sept 22, carrying Starlink V3 satellites
- Ship catch attempt pushed from Flight 14 to Flight 15
- Tesla Roadster event set for October 1 in Waco

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab fleet estimated at 42 deployed, 49 registered in Texas DMV, per Joe Bhakdi/Pioneer Lens — a gap the analyst reads as vehicles awaiting deployment. Bhakdi predicts the deployment bottleneck could be "more or less solved" by year-end, with fleet scaling into the thousands.
- Tesla brought Cybercab to China for a static showcase opening September 17 (Beijing through Sept 27, Shanghai through Sept 21), explicitly ruling out sales or driverless ride-hailing there; Electrek's Fred Lambert calls it mainly a showroom-traffic tactic given China's regulatory barriers and lack of driverless approval.
- Tesla released its first extended "Unboxed" manufacturing footage from Giga Texas, showing parallel section-building rather than a single moving line; Tesla claims (from its March 2023 investor day) roughly 50% lower production cost and 40% smaller footprint, with Musk expecting Cybercab to become Tesla's highest-volume vehicle.
- A Curbed article claiming Cybercabs have no brakes or mirrors was debunked by futureaza hosts, who noted the article's own quoted city DOT spokesperson describes the test vehicles as having a steering wheel and pedals.
- Host-modeled unit economics (illustrative, not Tesla-reported) suggest a $30,000 Cybercab over 300,000 paid miles implies roughly 10 cents/mile in purchase-cost allocation before opex.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 14 officially targeted for September 22, 8:15 AM ET — SpaceX's first fully orbital Starship flight and first payload deployment, carrying 26 Starlink V3 satellites (each adding 1 Tbps, ~26 Tbps total). Heatshield upgrades add tile-retention mechanisms in high-risk zones after new flow-pattern issues were identified.
- Tower catch attempt deferred from Flight 14 to Flight 15; Musk puts catch odds at 50–60%, citing flight 13's precise vertical splashdown as evidence a catch would have worked with a tower present.
- A separate recovered Starship ("Ship 40") survived re-entry and splashdown intact in the Indian Ocean, was towed ~600 miles by a SpaceX team through rough seas, and yielded landing-guidance data validating tower-catch precision for the first time during an ocean splashdown.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Grok 4.7 (2.1T parameters) graded by Musk as merely on par with Anthropic's Opus 5.0 — a step down from his August claim it would "exceed all current models" — and shipped late against his own 10-day target, which he attributed to RL reward-shaping issues.
- Grok 4.8 (2.5T parameters) is finishing base training this week on a rebuilt C++ stack; Grok 5 (6–10T parameters) is positioned as Tesla's AGI attempt, trained partly on SpaceX engineering records, per Musk.
- Musk publicly endorsed Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call to "pace the frontier" AI development and backed peer review among AI labs; TSLA and chip stocks reportedly dipped ~2% the following Monday amid "AI moving too fast" headlines.
- SpaceX reportedly earns roughly $1.25 billion/month (~$15 billion/year) renting compute to Anthropic, notable given Musk's past public criticism of the company.
- Terafab, a planned $17 billion chipmaking complex in Grimes County, Texas, is teased as eventually becoming the largest building on the planet (100M+ sq ft), with an unspecified "ring" technology said to enable much higher chip output — details remain sparse.

## Energy
- Tesla's new "Accordion" Supercharger is a factory-built, pre-assembled 16-stall unit that installs ~20% cheaper and commissions without a technician; Tesla says the goal is to pre-assemble all Superchargers in a factory.
- Connector growth has slowed to ~17% annually (82,357 connectors, 8,704 stations as of Q2 2026) from 35–45% in 2020–2021, a trend Electrek ties to the 2024 Supercharger team layoff; BYD is now deploying more charging power per month than Tesla via its flash-charging rollout.
- 4680 cell production is running at a 20 GWh/year rate with Berlin targeting ~18 GWh, per Joe Bhakdi's analysis; Giga Texas reportedly has 40 GWh of unstalled 4680 equipment, framed as the key near-term bottleneck.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla Roadster event confirmed for October 1, 5:30pm Pacific in Waco, Texas, with a render showing four rear plumes from a SpaceX-supplied cold gas thruster package.
- Tesla Semi's European rollout event is set for September 24, with a version showing camera-mirror "blades" and 1.6 kWh/mile efficiency versus 1.8 in the US (possibly reflecting a newer "Atlas" design); over 100 Megacharger stalls (up to 1.2 MW) are already live across Europe.
- Tesla is hiring heavily at Giga Nevada for Semi production (471 full-time roles cited), though one analyst argues Tesla's battery constraints mean Semi output will remain capped in favor of higher-margin Cybercab production.
- Model 3 received a five-star crash safety rating, sweeping all individual test categories.
- BYD, CATL, and Mercedes-Benz (via a 50+ truck Amazon order in Germany) are advancing competing electric commercial trucks with strong range and fast-charging specs.

## Market & Competition
- BYD deployed 10,000 flash-charging stations (1.5 MW peak) across China in five months, targeting 20,000 by year-end — a charging-power buildout Electrek says now exceeds Tesla's monthly pace, a gap that didn't exist two years ago.
- Chinese robotaxi operators (Baidu's Apollo Go, Pony.ai, WeRide) already run paid, no-safety-driver services across multiple cities, contrasting with Tesla's static-only Cybercab display in China.
