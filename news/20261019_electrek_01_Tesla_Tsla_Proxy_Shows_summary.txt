Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that details in Tesla's newly filed 2026 proxy statement — board overlaps with SpaceX, Musk's pay package structure, and a new retail auto-voting program — collectively resemble preparation for a Tesla-SpaceX merger vote, though no merger is confirmed or formally proposed.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Board overlap deepens:** Tesla director Ira Ehrenpreis, who chairs Tesla's compensation and governance committees, joined SpaceX's board in February 2026 while remaining classified as an "independent" Tesla director. Kimbal Musk, another Tesla director, has also served on SpaceX's board, meaning a third of Tesla's nine-member board has SpaceX ties. Tesla's governance committee chair's duties include reviewing potential director conflicts of interest.
2. **Cross-shareholding and revenue ties:** Tesla's $2 billion xAI investment converted into SpaceX Class A stock after SpaceX acquired xAI Holdings in February 2026, closing March 12; per SpaceX's S-1, this stake is 18,990,195 shares, under 1% of SpaceX. Tesla's proxy separately discloses $430.1 million in 2025 revenue from xAI (mostly Megapacks) and $143.3 million from SpaceX (mostly vehicles).
3. **Musk's comment on a potential combination:** On Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call in July, Musk said a combination would have to be done with the appropriate process, per the article's account.

## Financials
1. **2026 annual meeting ballot:** Tesla's meeting is set for December 3 at Gigafactory Texas, for shareholders of record as of October 13, with three items: re-electing Elon Musk, Robyn Denholm, and JB Straubel to three-year terms; a non-binding say-on-pay vote; and ratifying PwC as auditor (a role it's held since 2005). No shareholder proposals are on the ballot this year, unlike 2025. The board states it won't revive 2025 proposals to declassify the board or drop supermajority voting requirements, which failed to reach the required support threshold (39.4% of outstanding shares versus a higher bar needed, from 73.6% participation).
2. **Musk's reported 2025 pay — $158.36 billion on paper:** The proxy reports Musk's 2025 compensation at $158,359,009,867, implying a CEO-to-median-employee pay ratio of 2,522,203 to 1 (median employee pay: $62,786). This combines a $132.3 billion valuation of the 2025 CEO Performance Award (assuming all milestones hit) and $26.1 billion for an August 2025 "interim award" that Musk forfeited in April 2026 after the Delaware Supreme Court reinstated his 2018 package. Tesla's own "realized compensation" table shows Musk received $0 in 2025.
3. **2018 option exercise:** The proxy discloses that on June 16, 2026, Musk exercised all 303,960,630 of his 2018 stock options at a $23.34 exercise price, covering the cost via Tesla withholding 17,531,857 shares — implying (per the article's calculation) a share price of about $405 and an exercise value of roughly $116 billion.
4. **Stock-based compensation expense booked so far:** The 2025 CEO award has generated $689 million in stock-based compensation expense to date ($162 million in 2025, $527 million in first half 2026), since Tesla only expenses milestones it deems "probable" — currently just one of twelve (20 million cumulative vehicle deliveries). FSD subscriptions, Optimus, robotaxi, and EBITDA milestones remain in the "not probable" bucket, representing $105.8–$120.4 billion in unrecognized potential expense.
5. **2025 financial backdrop:** Tesla's net income fell 46% to $3.86 billion in 2025, and revenue declined for a second straight year to $94.8 billion.
6. **Merger-triggered vesting mechanics:** Under the 2025 CEO award, a "change in control" drops the operational-milestone requirement for each of 12 tranches, leaving only a market-cap test (shares outstanding times the higher of last closing price or per-share deal value), with earned tranches vesting immediately instead of over 7.5–10 years. With 3.95 billion shares outstanding, the article calculates the first $2 trillion tranche implies about $506/share, and a hypothetical $3 trillion valuation (~$759/share) would trigger three tranches (105.9 million shares) with no operational milestones met. Separately, the April 2026 Implementation Agreement for Musk's 2018 package states the 286.4 million restricted shares from his June option exercise, which don't otherwise vest until January 19, 2028, would vest immediately upon a change in control while he remains CEO or in a senior product/operations role.
7. **New retail auto-voting program:** Tesla is launching a "Voluntary Retail Voting Program" starting with the next shareholder meeting after December 3 (not this one). Retail shareholders voting via ProxyVote or Robinhood can enroll in a standing instruction to vote all future meetings with the board, either on everything, or on everything except a "Special Matter" (defined as a contested director election or a merger/acquisition/divestiture requiring shareholder approval). There is no option to vote against the board, and enrolled shareholders choosing "all matters" would have shares counted for the board's position on a merger unless they log in to override it; Tesla's only stated commitment is a reminder before that meeting.

## Editorial Stance
Lambert argues these elements together — Musk's pay packages paying out quickly on a deal price, the conflicts-review director sitting on SpaceX's board, a board locked in through 2029, and the new auto-vote default — read like a checklist preparing for a merger vote rather than independent coincidences. He contends Musk benefits from both sides of any exchange ratio given his roughly 20% Tesla ownership and about 85% of SpaceX's voting power, and argues Musk's call for an "appropriate process" is undermined by his having shaped Tesla's board and shareholder base to answer to him.
