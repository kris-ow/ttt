## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Merger vote math is much tighter than Elon's comp-package vote:** Tesla and SpaceX have each formed committees (Elon has recused himself) to negotiate merger terms; Elon has said on SpaceX's earnings call that he supports a merger. Unlike the comp-package vote, where non-voted shares simply didn't count, the merger requires 50%+1 of all 3.325 billion outstanding Tesla shares — meaning non-votes count as "no." Applying the comp package's 1.892 billion yes votes to the merger threshold would yield about 56.9% (a pass, but down from the 77% headline approval on the comp vote), illustrating the host's point that the merger vote is "a lot tighter than you would think."
2. **Expected premium and timeline:** The host says a merger committee member (named as sitting on one of the committees) has floated roughly a 30% premium, with an expected merger price landing between $500 and $550 per Tesla share. The host (speculatively) expects a merger announcement within roughly 2–3 months, possibly by January or February, and believes institutional holders (Fidelity, BlackRock, Blackstone) will need to be incentivized given they'd cede effective voting control to Elon similar to Mark Zuckerberg's position at Meta.
3. **Host's own merger-arbitrage options trade:** The host disclosed selling an at-the-money short put LEAP on Tesla expiring December 2027 (illustrated with 10 contracts near a $370 strike, collecting roughly $50,000–$60,000 in premium), betting that a merger price announcement will push the stock up and let him close the trade profitably regardless of whether the merger vote ultimately passes.
4. **AI capex self-funding comparison includes Tesla and SpaceX:** The host cites last-quarter operating cash flow versus AI/data-center buildout spend across companies, including Tesla (~$6.3 billion cash flow vs. ~$4.7 billion buildout spend) and SpaceX (~$13.1 billion cash flow vs. ~$19.6 billion buildout spend), noting both have had to raise debt but have other profitable businesses (vehicles/energy for Tesla; rockets/Starlink for SpaceX) funding the gap. SpaceX is also cited as having raised $40 billion in debt to build more data centers, with Elon saying SpaceX can't build fast enough and is charging rising spot prices for GPU compute every 90 days due to demand.
