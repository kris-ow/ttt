This Electrified episode argues Tesla's robotaxi service isn't scaling because it isn't operationally ready yet — not due to regulation or deliberate slow-walking by Elon Musk — citing recurring anecdotal reports of long wait times and pickup/drop-off looping failures. It also covers a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger's implications for Elon Musk's pay package, a hands-on look at Grok's agentic "Grokbot" as a precursor to Digital Optimus, and updates on SpaceX AI (Grok 4.6/4.7) and Starlink capacity targets.

## Robotaxi
1. **Persistent pickup/drop-off failures suggest the service isn't ready to scale:** Multiple riders in Austin (Abhi Manu, Ethan McConnell, and others in the comments) report cars looping repeatedly at pickup or drop-off, sometimes requiring a call to support; one rider said pickup/drop-off performance has "significantly regressed" versus three months ago, and Abhi Manu reported wait times of 15–25 minutes stretching to as long as 55 minutes despite the badged fleet growing to roughly 90 vehicles. The host concludes even full Cybercab validation wouldn't immediately mean mass fleet deployment given these reliability issues.
2. **Possible new city launch, likely San Antonio:** The host expects another city launch this month or next, possibly San Antonio, but expects only a handful of vehicles initially, consistent with past rollout patterns.
3. **Virtual booking queue may be coming to the Tesla app:** Due to high demand, a future update may introduce a virtual queue for robotaxi bookings, but the host views this as only a short-term band-aid given users won't tolerate 30–60 minute queue waits long-term.
4. **Waymo Arizona factory lot image:** A widely circulated image shows Waymo's Arizona factory with just under 1,000 new vehicles in the lot — potentially around 25% of Waymo's deployed fleet — though it's unclear whether these will replace existing Jaguar I-Pace vehicles or be added on top.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Mixed real-world FSD anecdotes:** A shared clip showed a Tesla on FSD backing directly into a shopping cart corral despite clear visual cues, illustrating ongoing edge-case weaknesses even as the system improves overall; separately, another clip showed FSD proactively moving the vehicle to avoid a BMW that had bounced off a nearby truck, illustrating a positive real-world reactive save.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Grokbot as a preview of Digital Optimus:** The host tested Grok's new agentic "Grokbot" product (via a $200/month Cursor Ultra subscription) and describes each bot as running on a dedicated persistent virtual machine that can log into websites without APIs, collaborate with other bots, and learn recorded browser routines. Elon Musk stated Digital Optimus is distinct from Grokbot, calling it "real-time video human emulation" rather than screenshot-based cloud reasoning; the host expects Digital Optimus to run primarily on local Tesla inference hardware (optimizing for cost, latency, and scale) with Grok serving as a high-level cloud orchestrator, and speculates a beta of Digital Optimus could arrive later this year.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok 4.6 performance and Grok 4.7 timeline:** Investor Gavin Baker praised Grok 4.6 and Cursor for strong price-performance versus rivals, citing an ~85% cost discount at similar performance to a competing model, though the host criticized a SpaceX-shared comparison chart for appearing to overstate Grok 4.6's lead in a category where a competing model actually scored higher. Elon Musk said Grok 4.7 should be ready in three to four weeks, with initial training complete and SpaceX proprietary data now being added; Musk claimed Grok 4.7 will exceed all current models, citing the uniqueness of SpaceX's training corpus, while acknowledging Anthropic will likely release improved models too.
2. **SpaceX all-hands meeting made public:** SpaceX held an all-hands meeting that was later published — something Tesla has done only once in its history, per the host — which he found light on new information but interprets as consistent with a narrative that AI will drive the vast majority of SpaceX's value over the next five years.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation and Elon Musk's compensation plan mechanics:** The host declines to speculate on Musk's intent behind merger-related commentary but notes that if a change-of-control event under Musk's new Tesla compensation plan is triggered, operational milestones (FSD, robotaxi, bots, deliveries, adjusted EBITDA) would be replaced by 12 market-cap-based tranches tied only to Tesla's implied valuation in the transaction — not SpaceX's value. The host pushes back on claims that Musk engineered this outcome, noting boards would likely renegotiate the awards and that SpaceX is not going to bid at a valuation (67–8 trillion, as referenced) that would automatically vest all tranches.
2. **Starlink surpasses 13 million broadband customers:** Per the SpaceX all-hands meeting, Starlink now has over 13 million broadband customers, with SpaceX reiterating a target of 10 gigawatts of nameplate capacity by the end of next year; at a stated $30–$50 per watt rate, the host calculates this could translate into $300–500 billion in revenue, while questioning whether SpaceX can scale compute nearly tenfold in 18 months.
3. **Ron DeSantis press conference references Waymo and Cybercab:** At a press event about upcoming eVTOL testing at Sunrise (Florida) testing grounds later this year, Ron DeSantis spoke positively about both Waymo and Tesla's Cybercab, though the host notes the event's primary focus was eVTOL testing, not autonomous vehicles.
4. **Musk teases flying cars and a Roadster demo:** Elon Musk said "you will get flying cars" and affirmed excitement around a prior Roadster demo; Franz von Holzhausen reportedly said the Roadster is "coming soon," though the host remains skeptical until it materializes, speculating a debut could coincide with merger-related messaging.

## Financials
1. **Terafab phase 1 jobs and capex clarification:** Musk clarified that the widely cited 3,000 high-quality jobs figure for the Terafab project in Texas refers only to phase 1 of 10 planned phases, which is set to cost just under $17 billion; the host reiterates his view that the full project will exceed $100 billion in capex, noting not all phases need cost the same.
2. **Daily stock move framed as part of "merger playbook":** TSLA closed at $327.51, down 1.59% on the day, while SpaceX shares closed up 9.65% at $146.15, a divergence the host frames as fitting a "merger playbook" narrative.
