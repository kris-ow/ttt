This episode of Electrified covers an FCC proposal favorable to Starlink's direct-to-cell ambitions, continued Starship test progress ahead of a planned dual tower-catch attempt, a Tesla Semi fire under investigation, Tesla robotaxi fleet growth and market expansion news, FSD regulatory momentum in Europe, a South Korean court ruling in Tesla's favor, and a Waymo quarterly loss update that the host frames as supporting his long-held bearish view on Waymo's sustainability.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **FCC proposes major spectrum expansion for direct-to-cell:** The FCC proposed modernizing direct-to-device (DTOD) rules, increasing the number of bands available for Starlink's supplemental coverage from space (SCS) service from five to 13, potentially making an additional 482 MHz of spectrum available. This doesn't automatically grant Starlink free access but makes the bands available via leases or ownership. The proposal would also eliminate burdensome geographic-area and lease requirements, replacing formal leases with self-certification, and would codify protections for Starlink's existing mobile satellite service (MSS) spectrum. Crucially, the FCC rejected a push by the "big three" telecom carriers to strip the 2.5–3.5 GHz bands from the SCS framework entirely. The comment period could run into early 2027, with final rules potentially not effective until the second half of 2027.
2. **SpaceX pushes back on T-Mobile and AFTRCC globally:** SpaceX filed comments accusing T-Mobile and the AFTRCC (Aerospace and Flight Test Radio Coordinating Council) of pursuing rules to harm a competitor, calling AFTRCC's commentary non-independent lobbying. SpaceX argued T-Mobile's proposed base-station protection limits would create buffer zones of more than 400 km, disenfranchising millions from direct-to-cell service, and is pushing for consistent, favorable rules globally, not just in the US.
3. **SpaceX in talks for land near Starbase, Louisiana:** Thousands of acres (about 4,000 acres across six parcels) owned by the Vermillion Parish School District could be acquired by SpaceX for uses like employee housing, staging, warehousing, and access roads adjacent to the Starbase, Louisiana spaceport site. The land still needs appraisal before negotiations begin.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship 42 completes static fire tests ahead of planned tower catch:** SpaceX confirmed Ship 42's static fire is complete, having performed a full-duration six-engine static fire plus a single-Raptor burn simulating the de-orbit maneuver needed for a tower catch. Both vehicles for the upcoming flight have now completed major engine tests, with a dual catch attempt hoped for before Thanksgiving, pending regulatory clearance.

## Robotaxi
1. **Houston robotaxi geofence rollout shows early rough edges:** Tesla's Houston robotaxi service has portions of its geofence still inaccessible for pickup/drop-off, and early riders reported inconsistent, "squirrelly" navigation issues in the downtown area, possibly related to train tracks. The Houston Police Officers Union publicly welcomed the expansion. Tesla is reportedly offering free first rides in Dallas and Houston.
2. **Fleet reaches 1,080 vehicles with 573 in Texas:** Tesla added 29 Cybercabs to the fleet in one day, bringing the Texas total to 573 and the overall fleet count to 1,080, per the host.
3. **Tesla hiring for charging "market attendant" roles across eight cities:** Tesla listed market attendant lead roles for robotaxi charging in Las Vegas, Phoenix, Orlando, Miami, San Antonio, Dallas, Houston, and Austin, overseeing staff who manually plug in robotaxis at Supercharger sites — a sign the host says points toward eventual 24/7 operations and suggests launches in Las Vegas, Phoenix, and San Antonio are approaching.
4. **Robotaxi team held an accessibility day:** Robotaxi said it hosted an accessibility day with members of the disabled community, with feedback feeding into Cybercab and future product design.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Germany and Italy signal support for FSD approval, moving population threshold to 46%:** Italy's transport minister described a "positive and constructive" exchange with Elon Musk on assisted driving and EU approval, following similarly positive comments from Germany. The host estimates this brings Tesla to 12 of 15 states needed for EU approval, with the tougher population threshold now at roughly 46% (vs. a required 65%) once Germany (18.5%) and Italy (~13 points) are counted alongside the 10 states already approved (~15%), leaving about 19% of the population still needed.
2. **South Korean court rules in Tesla's favor on FSD lawsuit:** A South Korean court rejected a lawsuit from owners who purchased FSD between 2019–2022 and sought refunds, filed in December 2024, alleging Tesla failed to deliver on its FSD obligations. The court found Tesla's FSD purchase option is an indefinite contract tied to future regulatory approval and technological progress, and that Tesla's delivery of other features like Enhanced Autopilot meant it wasn't liable for defects.
3. **Host disputes GM product chief's criticism of Tesla's vision-only approach:** Sterling Anderson, now GM's chief product officer and a former year-long head of Tesla's Autopilot program (2016), publicly argued that dropping radar for camera-only self-driving "ignores physics" and that cameras can't match radar in fog or velocity measurement. The host calls these claims largely unfounded and notes Tesla sued Anderson after he left in 2017 (settled with no admission of wrongdoing), alleging he took proprietary data and tried to recruit Tesla engineers for his new company, Aurora — suggesting personal motive behind the criticism.

## Electrical Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Tesla begins hiring in Vietnam ahead of market entry:** Tesla is now hiring for sales, service, and charging roles in Vietnam, though no order or launch timeline has been shared. The host notes Vietnam's EV sales were about 40% of new car sales as of 2025, with annual sales around 550,000, but import duties (potentially over 50%, since Tesla won't manufacture locally) could limit early volume to a few hundred-to-thousand units annually, growing to an estimated 10,000–20,000 units/year by the 2030s if things go well (host's own guesstimate).
2. **New EV tax credit bill unlikely to pass before 2029:** A new EV tax credit bill sponsored by Democrats has "basically a 0% chance," per the host, of becoming law before 2029 while Trump remains in office, regardless of midterm outcomes.
3. **South Korean reporting highlights fast execution pace at Terafab:** Tesla and SpaceX representatives attending Semicon West reportedly described Terafab's response speed as unusually fast compared to conventional semiconductor customers, with one unnamed executive saying they'd never seen this level of execution speed in the industry.

## Financials
1. **Tesla Semi catches fire in Hayward, California:** A pre-production (circa 2022) Tesla Semi caught fire with no known cause and no crash involved; the driver was unharmed. Root cause remains unknown. The host notes the current production Semi differs significantly — using 4680 cells instead of 2170s, a different drivetrain, and a "mega manifold" heat pump thermal system — making this incident less representative of today's trucks. All highway lanes reopened about 10 hours after the incident.
2. **Waymo's "Other Bets" losses widen sharply in Q3:** Waymo (under Alphabet's Other Bets segment) generated $372 million in revenue in Q3 2026, up only about $26 million year-over-year, while losses grew to $2.05 billion, up roughly $621 million from Q3 2025. Alphabet's CFO reportedly linked the Other Bets operating loss directly to Waymo's expansion. The host argues this trajectory is unsustainable and reiterates his view that this marks "the beginning of the end" for Waymo, though he acknowledges Alphabet can continue subsidizing it for some time.
3. **Tesla and SpaceX shares pull back amid broader tech weakness:** Tesla closed down about 2–3% on the day near the mid-$400s (exact figure garbled in transcript), and SpaceX closed down under 1%, while the Nasdaq-100 fell 1.38%. The host characterizes this as a normal breather after a recent run-up, with no change to his prior stock outlook.
