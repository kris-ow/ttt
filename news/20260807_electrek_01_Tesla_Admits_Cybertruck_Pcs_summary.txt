Tesla has acknowledged that the power conversion system (PCS) in pre-2026 Cybertrucks is defective and is extending its warranty coverage on the part to 8 years or 150,000 miles, after months of owners paying out of pocket for replacements.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla admits PCS defect, extends warranty:** In a statement on its official Cybertruck account, Tesla said it identified PCS electronics in pre-2026 Cybertrucks that "do not meet our reliability standards" and made design changes for newer vehicles. Pre-2026 trucks (2024 and 2025 Foundation Series and standard builds) get PCS coverage extended to 8 years/150,000 miles, effectively moving it into the powertrain warranty. The 2026 Cybertruck, which ships with a redesigned PCS, carries a 7-year/70,000-mile warranty on the part.

2. **Reimbursements and software fix:** Owners who already paid for PCS replacements will be reimbursed in the coming weeks. Tesla also issued a software update allowing Supercharging even when the PCS has failed, so a dead unit no longer strands the vehicle, and says it will cover Supercharging costs for affected owners until a parts backlog clears.

3. **Scale of the problem:** Electrek reported in July that a self-selected owner poll found 91 of 223 Cybertrucks (nearly 41%) had needed a PCS replacement, with new failures logged weekly. The PCS combines the onboard charger and DC-DC converter; when it fails, home charging drops from 48 amps to 24 before cutting out entirely. Pre-2026 trucks originally shipped with only a 4-year/50,000-mile basic warranty, leaving owners with repair bills of $5,000–$7,200 outside that window; Tesla had been quietly discounting repairs to around $1,000 as a "goodwill" gesture before this formal admission.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert credits Tesla for the warranty extension, reimbursements, and software fix as the right outcome, but criticizes the company for only acting after owner forums, press reporting, and public pressure forced its hand, rather than admitting the defect proactively. He notes Tesla previously extended warranties on eMMC touchscreen failures in 2020 only after an NHTSA investigation, and flags a pattern of letting manufacturing defects run past short warranties at customers' expense; he says he'll watch whether reimbursements actually happen and whether future defects require high failure rates or regulatory pressure to prompt a fix.
