During Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call, CEO Elon Musk confirmed that Optimus robot production will begin at Tesla's Fremont factory in late July or August, following conversion of the former Model S/X assembly line. Musk cautioned that initial output will be "quite slow" and declined to provide any volume target for 2026.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Fremont line conversion timeline:** The last Model S and X vehicles will roll off the line in early May, ending a 14-year and 11-year production run respectively, with over 610,000 combined units produced. Tesla will then dismantle the entire line — starting with smaller parts equipment and ending with final assembly, which Musk said will be torn down next month — before installing entirely new equipment for Optimus, including wiring, communications, and testing infrastructure. Musk called the four-month conversion "insanely fast" and said no other company on Earth has done anything comparable.

2. **Production complexity and no volume target:** Optimus has 10,000 unique parts, none of which have been through mass production before. Musk said output "will move as fast as the least lucky, slowest, dumbest part in the entire 10,000" and called volume predictions "literally impossible." He provided no production target for 2026, saying initial robots will perform only "simple skills in the factory." This is a significant walk-back from his January 2025 prediction of roughly 10,000 Optimus robots being built that year — a target Tesla missed entirely, with Musk admitting in January 2026 that zero Optimus robots were doing "useful work."

3. **Second factory and Gen 3 delay:** Musk confirmed a second Optimus factory is under construction at Giga Texas, with production expected around summer 2027, targeting the higher-volume Gen 4 variant. The Gen 3 reveal, previously expected in Q1 2026, has been pushed again to "probably middle of this year" (mid-2026), with Tesla citing competitive intelligence concerns — specifically that rivals conduct frame-by-frame analysis of Tesla releases.

## Market & Competition

4. **Competitors advancing in parallel:** Boston Dynamics is shipping its electric Atlas humanoid to Hyundai factories in 2026, targeting a production facility capable of 30,000 units annually. Figure AI, valued at $39 billion, has an active pilot deployment at BMW. Agility Robotics' Digit is already commercially deployed in customer warehouses, including at a Toyota plant in Ontario.

## Editorial Stance
The author characterizes Musk's Optimus promises as following a recurring pattern of ambitious timelines followed by missed targets, and credits Musk for being more candid than usual on this call about production uncertainties. The editorial argues that "starting production" and "producing meaningful quantities" are fundamentally different milestones, pointing to the Cybertruck ramp as a cautionary parallel.
