## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14.3.2 broad rollout with new disengagement taxonomy.** Version 2026.2.9.9 is being pushed widely; the notable change is a new "Navigation" disengagement reason category, which may signal Tesla is refining its intervention taxonomy ahead of unsupervised deployment requirements.

- **FSD Supervised earns positive first impressions in South Korea.** A South Korean reporter described the system as flawlessly handling traffic signals, lane keeping, speed limits, merging, and direction changes — meaningful for international expansion momentum in a new market.

- **FSD subscription growth cited as a revenue catalyst.** CIC (described as the "Goldman Sachs of China") highlighted rising FSD subscriptions as actively driving a sales rebound and framed the autonomous roadmap as a massive value driver.

- **AI5 chip tape-out anchors institutional bull thesis.** Cantor Fitzgerald specifically cited Tesla's AI5 tape-out as a central pillar of their overweight rating, alongside the autonomous roadmap.

- **Cortex compute moat detailed by automotive engineer "Genma_JP."** Per Genma, Tesla trains with ~40,000 AI4 (in-car) chips running inference during every training epoch, making the deployed model bit-exact with what was trained — a quantization advantage he describes as the least-discussed but most significant technical moat. Tesla's Q1 call referenced 150,000 H100 GPUs in training infrastructure; Cortex was built for ~$2B versus an estimated $5.5–6B/year to rent equivalent compute on AWS.

- **Tesla's data flywheel described as structurally unreplicable.** Genma argues Tesla's ~10 billion miles of real-world data, gathered with 1M+ supervised FSD users, cannot be matched by simulation; replicating it in two years would require ~333,333 vehicles driving daily for two years.

- **Tesla promised FSD V14 Lite to international HW3 owners — with no timeline.** Tesla posted on X that V14 Lite will expand internationally after the U.S. HW3 rollout, subject to "completion of technical verification, regional adaptation & relevant regulatory approvals." The U.S. HW3 rollout is targeted for end of June 2026, meaning international owners face a wait well into H2 2026 at the earliest.

- **Musk's HW3 micro-factory retrofit plan raises new questions.** Tesla plans dedicated micro-factories to retrofit ~4 million HW3 vehicles worldwide with AI4 computers, new cameras, and new wiring harnesses. One day after the earnings call, Tesla announced HW4 Plus with doubled memory, prompting questions about whether HW4 owners face a similar obsolescence cycle.

## Robotaxi

- **Active Cybercab fleet stands at 643 vehicles as of late April.** Bhakdi acknowledges missing his ~1,800 target by roughly two to three months, but cites monthly growth of ~50–100% and projects ~800–900 vehicles by end of May 2026.

- **Bhakdi maintains 10,000–20,000 Cybercabs deployed by year-end.** He states nothing from the Q1 earnings call changed his prior estimate, citing visible production increments of 10–20 units at a time and a current rate of ~300/month; month-over-month doubling would exceed 10,000 by December. The host's personal estimate is ~15,000.

- **Cybercab logistics activity accelerating.** Two separate semi trucks loaded with Cybercabs were spotted on April 28, with frequency suggesting active fleet-staging logistics consistent with pre-launch deployment.

- **Cybercab and Optimus featured at Miami F1 Fan Fest.** Tesla's Autonomy Pop-Up at Lummus Park, co-located with the Crypto.com Miami Grand Prix Fan Fest starting April 29, puts both products in front of a large, affluent, high-visibility international audience.

- **Waymo structural scaling ceiling detailed.** Per Genma, Waymo's HD-map dependency requires constant human maintenance and collapses in construction zones, power outages, and repaved roads — a fatal flaw at scale. Waymo's Mesa facility produces vehicles in "low single digits per day" with a plan for just over 2,000 additional Jaguar I-Pace units by end of 2026. Waymo's estimated per-vehicle cost is ~$150,000 vs. Cybercab's target ~$25,000; Waymo prices at ~$4/mile vs. Tesla's current Austin pricing of ~$1.50/mile.

- **Cybercab first-principles design gives ~$0.67/mile vehicle-cost advantage.** Per Brian (Futurezza), the Cybercab's ~4,000 parts (vs. 10,000 for a Model Y), three pieces of glass, no paint shop, and giga-cast sub-assemblies underpin a sub-$30,000 build cost and a 500,000–1,000,000-mile target lifespan versus ~150,000 miles for a Jaguar iPace.

- **Current operational issues are infrastructure-related, not FSD-related.** Per Bhakdi, citing Dillon Loomis, the known service issues — connectivity drops, screens going black, vehicles stopping — are tied to AWS/server connections, not FSD software, and are characterized as expected optimization-phase growing pains.

- **5,000 Cybercab units flagged as Q2 earnings validation threshold.** The host (brighterwithherbert) states that a figure above 5,000 units at the Q2 earnings call (expected late July 2026) would validate the manufacturing moat thesis.

## Electric Vehicles

- **First Tesla Semi off high-volume production line at Giga Nevada.** The new 1.7-million-square-foot dedicated factory is designed for 50,000 trucks/year. Two trims: Standard Range (~$260,000, 325 miles) and Long Range ($290,000, 500 miles at 82,000-lb GCW) — both below competitor pricing. Analysts project 5,000–15,000 deliveries in 2026; Electrek's Lambert considers that too optimistic.

- **MDB Transportation launches three-week drayage pilot at Ports of LA and Long Beach.** The Compton-based operator is tracking energy efficiency, cycle time, and driver experience — the second port trucking company to deploy the Semi commercially. Electrek notes MDB is a commercially driven carrier, not a sustainability showcase.

- **California voucher demand: Tesla Semi took 965 of 1,067 applications.** In California's Clean Truck & Bus Voucher program (January 2025–February 2026), Tesla's Semi accounted for 90% of applications versus fewer than 100 combined for Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo.

- **"Semi as a Service" model to debut at ACT Expo May 4.** Alyath will unveil a bundled monthly payment model covering vehicle, charging infrastructure, and energy supply — eliminating fleet capex barriers.

- **Megacharger buildout: 66 locations mapped across 15 states.** First Pilot truck stop Megacharger locations expected by summer 2026; first station opened in Ontario, California.

- **Model Y L unveiled in India; deliveries starting this summer.** The Model Y L Premium AWD is priced at ₹61,99,000 (~$66,600 USD) — approximately 9% cheaper than the smaller Model Y Premium RWD in that market.

- **NHTSA closes Model Y steering wheel probe without action.** The preliminary evaluation covering 120,089 2023 Model Y vehicles was closed with no action, removing a regulatory overhang.

- **Rising U.S. gas prices strengthen EV cost case.** Regular gasoline hit $4.18/gallon nationally (California near $6/gallon); Model Y Premium operating cost cited at ~$0.04/mile vs. ~$0.104/mile for a Toyota RAV4 Hybrid — a 61% per-mile advantage.

## Market & Competition

- **Institutional analysts hold $465–$522 price targets post-earnings.** Cantor Fitzgerald: $510 overweight; CIC: $500 outperform; RBC Capital: $465 buy; Baird: $522 outperform (lowered from $538); Roth Capital (Craig Irwin): buy (target cited as $55, likely a transcript error for $550).

- **Xpeng identified as sole credible global challenger.** Per Genma, Xpeng rebuilt its entire stack after FSD v12, going full vision-only end-to-end; its Turing chip is rated 3,000 TOPS with 200,000+ units shipped; VLA 2.0 is offered free to all Turing owners. Projected ~500,000 VLA 2.0 vehicles by end of 2026. Key remaining gaps: ~30,000 GPUs hosted by Alibaba (roughly 5x less compute than Cortex) and no Turing-chip-in-loop training. Genma predicts unsupervised release from Xpeng in 2028.

- **SpaceX IPO reportedly roadshowing at $1.75T; June 2026 target.** Bhakdi and the host discussed a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger mechanics requiring a 20–30% premium above Tesla's market cap; Chamath Palihapitiya is cited as "99% certain" a reverse merger will happen. Banks lined up: Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley.

- **Waymo's Zeekr pivot undermined by 100% Chinese vehicle tariff.** Waymo's planned adoption of the Zeekr as a next-generation platform loses its cost advantage entirely under the current tariff structure, per Brian (Futurezza).

- **HW3 collective claim: ~3,000 owners, 29 countries, €6.5M.** A Dutch Model 3 owner launched a claim site following Tesla's first European FSD approval (Netherlands, HW4-only), attracting owners who paid up to €6,400 for FSD as far back as 2019.

- **Tesla Safety Score V3.0 launches in Florida and Maryland.** New policyholders gain access now; existing policyholders transition July 12. V3.0 adds night driving as a scored category — relevant to both insurance pricing and FSD training data.
