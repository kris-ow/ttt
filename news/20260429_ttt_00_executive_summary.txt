## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD V14.3.2 broad rollout with new disengagement taxonomy.** Software version 2026.2.9.9 is pushing to a significantly larger owner group. A new "Navigation" disengagement reason was added to the FSD popup — a data collection refinement that may inform unsupervised deployment readiness.
- **Tesla Safety Score V3.0 launches in Florida and Maryland.** New policy holders can enroll now; existing holders transition July 12. Night driving is added as a new scored behavior, expanding Tesla's real-world nighttime data flywheel for both insurance pricing and FSD training.
- **AI5 chip tape-out flagged as key structural catalyst by Cantor Fitzgerald.** The milestone anchors Cantor's $510 overweight thesis alongside the autonomous roadmap, cited as central to long-term FSD scalability.
- **Tesla's integer-based training architecture identified as a hidden technical moat.** Per automotive engineer Genma_JP (15 years ADAS experience, viral post after Q1 earnings), Tesla's Cortex cluster runs inference on actual AI4 chips *during every training epoch* — making the deployed model bit-exact with the trained model. All competitors training on floating-point Nvidia GPUs face a quantization gap on deployment; Genma calls this "hope is not a strategy when safety is involved."
- **Tesla's data flywheel: 10 billion miles, 1M+ active supervised FSD users.** Genma estimates a competitor would need ~333,333 vehicles driving every day for two years to replicate this dataset. Tesla can cover equivalent volume in under a year with its current fleet.
- **Cortex compute: ~150,000 H100 GPUs per Q1 2026 call; built for ~$2B, owned outright.** Equivalent AWS rental cost estimated at $5.5–$6B/year at market rates. Cortex 2.0 already coming online.
- **FSD earns strong first-impression review in South Korea.** A Korean reporter described flawless traffic signal compliance, lane keeping, merging, and direction changes — qualitative signal for continued international expansion momentum.
- **Tesla promises FSD V14 Lite to international HW3 vehicles — no timeline given.** Delivery is conditioned on completion of U.S. rollout (targeted end of June 2026), technical verification, regional adaptation, and regulatory approvals. Tesla confirmed at Q1 2026 earnings that HW3 "simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD," meaning V14 Lite remains a supervised Level 2 system for these owners.
- **HW4 Plus (doubled memory) announced one day after Q1 earnings**, raising concerns about a recurring upgrade treadmill. Tesla plans dedicated micro-factories to retrofit approximately 4 million HW3 vehicles worldwide with AI4 computers, new cameras, and new wiring harnesses — work deemed too complex for service centers.
- **~3,000 HW3 owners from 29 countries filed a collective claim representing €6.5M in FSD purchases.** Triggered by Tesla's first European FSD approval (Netherlands) being limited to HW4 only; some owners paid up to €6,400 as far back as 2019.

## Robotaxi
- **Active Robotaxi fleet: 649 per RobotaxiTracker.com** (Bay Area 542, Austin 101, Dallas 3, Houston 3). Unsupervised subset: 25 (Austin 19, Dallas 3, Houston 3).
- **Cybercab logistics activity accelerating.** Two separate semi trucks loaded with Cybercabs were spotted on April 28 — two sightings in a single day — consistent with active fleet staging ahead of broader deployment.
- **Tesla Autonomy Pop-Up at Miami F1 Grand Prix Fan Fest (starting April 29).** Located at Lummus Park alongside the Crypto.com Miami Grand Prix Fan Fest; attendees can view the Cybercab and meet Optimus. High-visibility, affluent audience for Tesla's autonomous and robotics thesis.
- **Jo Bhakdi maintains 10,000–20,000 Cybercab deployment target for end-2026**, stating nothing from the Q1 earnings call changed his view. Current production rate referenced at approximately 300/month with expected month-over-month doubling. Host flagged a threshold: any Cybercab production figure above 5,000 units at the Q2 earnings call (expected late July 2026) would validate the manufacturing moat thesis.
- **Cybercab cost and design advantages detailed.** Brian (Futurezza) estimates ~$25,000 production cost and ~4,000 total parts — versus ~10,000 for a Model Y, ~$150,000 per vehicle for Waymo's upfitted iPace, and ~$250,000 for Zoox's custom platform. Cybercab uses only three pieces of glass, no paint shop, giga-cast sub-assemblies, and is engineered for 500,000–1,000,000-mile lifespan with replaceable panels.
- **Waymo structural ceiling identified: the HD map.** Genma_JP argues centimeter-precise HD maps require continuous human maintenance for every construction zone, repaved road, or power outage — and at thousands of vehicles produce gridlock and blocked intersections. Real failure cases cited in Nashville, Phoenix, Houston, and LA. "The HD map is not a feature. It is proof that the intelligence is missing."
- **Waymo ~$4/mile pricing, not yet profitable; Tesla Austin pricing ~$1.50/mile.** Cybercab projected unit economics: ~$1/mile with ~$0.50 profit per mile. Waymo's planned Zeekr platform undercut by 100% U.S. tariff on Chinese-made vehicles.
- **Jo Bhakdi acknowledges fleet size miss but defends trajectory.** His April/May 2026 target of ~1,800 unsupervised vehicles was missed; current fleet is 649 per RobotaxiTracker.com. He frames the shortfall as 2–3 months behind schedule, citing ~50–100% monthly growth and projecting ~800–900 vehicles by end of May.
- **Known operational issues are infrastructure, not FSD.** Bhakdi (citing Dillon Loomis content) identifies current service disruptions as AWS/server connectivity drops and screen blackouts — explicitly not FSD software failures. Framed as expected growing pains in the optimization phase.
- **Xpeng identified as only credible global challenger; unsupervised release predicted 2028.** Genma's analysis: Xpeng rebuilt its entire stack after FSD v12 in July 2024, went full vision-only end-to-end, built the Turing chip (3,000 TOPS, 200,000+ units shipped), and offers VLA 2.0 free to Turing owners. Remaining gaps: ~30,000 GPUs (5x less compute than Cortex) and no chip-in-loop training. Projected ~500,000 vehicles on VLA 2.0 by end of 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
- **First Tesla Semi rolls off high-volume production line at Gigafactory Nevada.** The new 1.7M sq-ft dedicated factory is designed for 50,000 trucks/year; Tesla will ramp gradually. Analyst projections: 5,000–15,000 deliveries in 2026. Two trims: Standard Range (~$260,000, 325 mi) and Long Range ($290,000, 500 mi at 82,000-lb GCW) — both positioned as the lowest-priced Class 8 BEV on the market.
- **MDB Transportation launches 3-week Tesla Semi freight pilot at Ports of LA and Long Beach** — the second port drayage operator to deploy the truck in commercial service after Hight Logistics. MDB is tracking energy efficiency, cycle time, and driver experience.
- **California voucher data shows Tesla Semi dominance: 965 of 1,067 applications** (Jan 2025–Feb 2026) vs. fewer than 100 combined for Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo.
- **"Tesla Semi as a Service" model to debut at ACT Expo, May 4, 2026.** Alyath will unveil a bundled monthly payment covering vehicle, charging, and energy supply — eliminating fleet capex barriers.
- **66 Megacharger locations mapped across 15 states; first Pilot truck stop locations expected summer 2026.** First station already open in Ontario, California.
- **Model Y L unveiled in India; deliveries starting this summer.** Premium AWD priced at ₹61,99,000 (~$66,600 USD) — approximately 9% cheaper than the smaller Model Y Premium RWD in that market.
- **NHTSA closes Model Y steering wheel probe without action**, removing a regulatory overhang on 120,089 Model Year 2023 vehicles.
- **U.S. gas prices hit multi-year high at $4.18/gallon** (California ~$6/gallon). Model Y cost per mile: ~$0.04 vs. ~$0.104 for a Toyota RAV4 Hybrid — a 61% operating cost advantage that structurally strengthens EV demand.
