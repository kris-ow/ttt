This live stream discussion on the eve of the SpaceX IPO (June 11, 2026) features host Herbert alongside panelists James (Invest Answers), Jeff Lutz (Rational Bull), and Alexandra Merz debating the Tesla and SpaceX investment outlook. The core thesis is that Tesla stock was artificially depressed by retail investors liquidating positions to fund SpaceX IPO allocations, and that this capital will rotate back into Tesla while a cascade of near-term catalysts — robotaxi expansion, FSD adoption, a potential Tesla/SpaceX merger announcement, and Macrohard — could drive Tesla toward a $587 end-of-year price target. The panel disagrees mainly on the timeline of a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger announcement, with Alexandra Merz predicting it between July 7 and August 15, 2026, and James believing it won't be announced until 2027.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla stock dip blamed on SpaceX IPO liquidity drain:** James explains that Tesla's share price dropped approximately $40 in the week prior to the discussion — from roughly $420–$430 down to $380 — because retail investors were selling Tesla to raise cash for SpaceX IPO allocations. He describes $380 as a "perfect kill zone" and states he bought aggressively every day since the prior Friday. He predicts the capital will rotate straight back into Tesla, pushing the price back toward $420–$440 in the coming days, noting Tesla had already recovered $18 on the day of the stream.

2. **SpaceX IPO demand described as massively oversubscribed:** The panel reports the SpaceX IPO is described as at least four times oversubscribed, with some suggesting demand is far higher. Specific figures cited include BlackRock committing $5 billion, Ron Baron seeking $1 billion, and Saudi sovereign wealth funds seeking large allotments. Retail allocation was initially targeted at 30% but is reported to have been reduced to approximately 20% due to institutional demand pressure. Anecdotes from attendees include one person at Merrill Lynch who requested 3,700 shares and received 700 (roughly 19%), and a UBS client with $100 million on account who was offered only 100 shares.

3. **SpaceX IPO mechanics and post-IPO volatility:** Alexandra Merz explains that 555 million shares will be tradable at IPO, while no pre-IPO SpaceX shareholders can sell. She identifies the period between IPO open and index fund inclusion (estimated around June 19, potentially pushed to June 22 due to a bank holiday, or Monday June 22) as a window of high volatility. James sets his opening price target at $159 (vs. the $135 IPO price) with potential for higher. Index inclusion is expected to create a sustained buying floor. Alexandra also notes a second wave of index buying in September and a third in December as the float grows.

4. **Waymo versus Tesla robotaxi unit economics:** Jeff argues that mainstream media consistently fails to engage with the unit economics and scalability differences between Waymo and Tesla. He contends that while Waymo is an acceptable competitor, its model will never be economically feasible at the scale Tesla is pursuing, and that this distinction is almost never covered in mainstream financial media, which focuses only on revenue, EPS, and gross margins.

5. **Combined Tesla/SpaceX market weight cited as geopolitical force:** James notes that Tesla and SpaceX together represent approximately 8% of the S&P 500, arguing that traditional Wall Street and financial media can no longer afford to be adversarial toward Elon Musk's companies given their systemic weight. He also observes that Japanese, Korean, and Middle Eastern investors do not exhibit Elon Derangement Syndrome, and that those regions' sovereign wealth funds and institutional investors are among the most enthusiastic SpaceX IPO participants.

## Robotaxi

1. **Nevada robotaxi permit application for 5,000 vehicles:** James highlights a major development from the prior Monday (June 9, 2026) in which Tesla officially applied for an autonomous vehicle network company permit in Nevada, requesting authorization for up to 5,000 robotaxis during the first 12 months after the permit is granted. James and Jeff argue this number is not arbitrary — regulators would not be given an inflated figure since doing so could cause permitting delays — making it a credible signal of Tesla's near-term deployment intentions. James notes this would be approximately 1.5 times the size of Waymo's entire nationwide fleet.

2. **Reno, Nevada robotaxi permit also filed:** Jeff adds that Tesla also applied for a robotaxi permit in Reno, Nevada (near the Giga Nevada facilities), in addition to Las Vegas. A specific vehicle count for the Reno application was not mentioned.

3. **Texas DMV registrations of unsupervised robotaxis:** The panel notes that in a single day, 10 additional robotaxis were registered by the Texas DMV, described as unsupervised units. Jeff references observing a parking lot in Houston containing 133 cars, of which over 20 were Cybercabs, expressing confidence Tesla will not leave them sitting idle in summer heat for 30–60 days.

4. **Active robotaxi fleet and deployment timeline:** The panel references an active fleet of approximately 69 unsupervised Cybercabs currently operating, characterizing this as still in a "prototypy" phase. Jeff predicts major deployments within the next two to six weeks barring unforeseen problems. He frames the robotaxi city rollouts as analogous to factory ramp-ups — you wouldn't pre-position inventory in those "factories" (cities) unless you intended to forward-deploy. He also speculates Tesla may time a high-profile robotaxi launch around FIFA World Cup events happening in U.S. cities including Houston.

5. **FSD Version 15 not a prerequisite for near-term expansion:** Jeff states he does not believe Tesla is waiting for FSD version 15 before expanding robotaxi deployments. He draws a distinction between current small-scale prototype-phase operations and Elon's ultimate vision of hundreds of thousands to millions of vehicles, arguing version 15 is targeted at the latter scale threshold.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD approvals accelerating across Europe:** The panel notes that FSD has now been approved by the Ministry of Transport in Belgium, following approval in Denmark just days earlier. Countries now approved include Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, and Estonia. The pace of approvals is described as accelerating.

2. **Personal FSD experience cited as proof of maturity:** Alexandra Merz states she has been over a year without a disengagement using FSD, describing it as working reliably anywhere she drives. She mentions Tesla recently extended or reduced the frequency of the nag prompt, affecting many Tesla owners, and notes that despite this, no significant uptick in accidents has been observed — citing data showing supervised FSD remains safer than driving without it.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus reveal expected as a potential stock catalyst:** Jeff argues that unlike most Tesla demos (which he says typically cause only a 24-hour stock pop before fading), an Optimus reveal tied to a concrete production and deployment announcement — such as "3,000 units built and deploying into Tesla factories next week" — could have a lasting effect on Tesla's stock price. He frames Optimus as part of Tesla's emerging "physical real-world AI" revenue line that will distinguish it from all other AI companies.

2. **Optimus described as joint Tesla/SpaceX project:** Jeff notes that Optimus is increasingly being described as a convergence project between Tesla and SpaceX engineering, alongside Terafab and the solar 100 gigawatt initiative, suggesting these product reveals will serve as breadcrumbs pointing toward deeper integration between the two companies.

## Financials

1. **End-of-year Tesla price target of $587:** James presents a base-case Tesla price target of $587 by end of 2026, predicated on the Cybercab beginning to scale. His underlying logic is a market cap convergence thesis: if SpaceX reaches a $2.2 trillion market cap (which he suggests could happen by the close of business the following day), Tesla at ~$1.5 trillion represents a ~$200/share discount to a logical parity level, creating an arbitrage opportunity for investors wanting exposure to Elon Musk's portfolio of companies.

2. **Q2 earnings expected to be strong:** Jeff predicts Q2 2026 earnings will be better than Q1 2026 and better than the prior year, driven by strong auto sales and energy. He identifies Q2 earnings as one of the three primary catalysts that could produce a lasting re-rating of Tesla stock, particularly if analysts begin categorizing Tesla's FSD/robotaxi revenue as "physical real-world AI" revenue — distinct from traditional automotive revenue.

3. **Tesla semi demand cited as a near-term revenue contributor:** The panel notes Tesla Semi is beginning to be sold across Europe, with strong demand. In California, state rebates cover more than half the purchase price, and over 1,000 rebate applications have already been submitted. Jeff argues that once Semi production volumes appear in delivery reports alongside cars and energy, analysts will begin to appreciate Tesla's multi-segment revenue profile.

4. **Alexandra Merz's merger timeline and stock option thesis:** Alexandra Merz presents a detailed argument that Elon Musk has a personal financial incentive to announce a Tesla/SpaceX merger before August 15, 2026, the deadline by which he must exercise approximately 303 million Tesla stock options (being converted from options to restricted stock). She argues a higher Tesla stock price at the time of exercise reduces his tax burden due in January 2028, giving him a direct incentive to drive the stock price up — most effectively through a merger announcement. Her predicted announcement window is July 7 to August 15, 2026, with consummation expected in Q1 or Q2 2027 following a shareholder vote (estimated October/November 2026) and regulatory approvals.

5. **SpaceX authorized share structure cited as merger evidence:** Alexandra notes that after the IPO there will be approximately 13 billion SpaceX shares outstanding out of 36 billion authorized. She argues amendments to the S-1 filing were specifically structured to make clear that the delta between outstanding and authorized shares is reserved for a large post-IPO acquisition, which she interprets as strong evidence of an imminent Tesla acquisition.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Roadster reveal expected but not a major stock mover:** Jeff and James agree the Roadster reveal is likely coming in the near term and will help reinforce the Tesla/SpaceX engineering convergence narrative, but that on its own it will not produce a lasting stock re-rating. The panel treats it as a marketing and narrative event rather than a financial catalyst, consistent with their view that demos without accompanying production numbers are discounted by the market.
