A Belgian pedestrian/cyclist safety nonprofit, Johanna.be, tested Tesla's "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" in Brussels and found it exceeded the 30 km/h speed limit in 55% of tested zones, averaging 44 km/h, just weeks before an EU-wide vote on approving FSD.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Johanna.be's FSD30 study findings:** Over three days in July and roughly 400 km of testing, mostly on low-speed streets, FSD exceeded the 30 km/h limit in 55% of zones tested (averaging 44 km/h) and exceeded the 20 km/h limit in 42% of "woonerven" (residential shared streets) tested. The group says the car's dashboard displayed a 50 km/h limit in the majority of the 30 km/h zones it actually drove through, suggesting a map/sign-reading failure rather than deliberate speeding. Johanna cites WHO data that a pedestrian struck at 44 km/h is nearly six times more likely to die than one struck at 30 km/h. The group also documented FSD repeatedly attempting to overtake cyclists on "fietsstraten" (cycle streets), where passing is not legally allowed, though it credited FSD for yielding to pedestrians and cyclists, often preemptively. Johanna sent its findings to the Dutch RDW and Flemish mobility ministry on September 7, urging reconsideration before the EU vote.

2. **Belgium's existing approval and government response:** Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder approved FSD (Supervised) on June 10 after Tesla completed 5,000 km of supervised testing on Flemish roads, making Belgium an early adopter of the Dutch RDW's provisional approval. Asked by Reuters about the study, a Flemish government spokesperson said the system is "learning" and that drivers remain "100% responsible." Tesla did not respond to the outlet.

3. **EU-wide approval process and speeding as the central obstacle:** The Netherlands' RDW gave FSD (Supervised) provisional approval under UN Regulation 171 in April; Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania, Slovenia, and (as of this week) Czechia have since recognized it nationally. The EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles is scheduled to vote on October 6 on extending the RDW approval bloc-wide, requiring a qualified majority of at least 15 countries representing 65% of the EU's population. Sweden told the EU in June to reject FSD unless Tesla removes its ability to exceed speed limits, with Norway and Finland raising similar concerns; France refused to recognize the Dutch approval in July, with Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot citing speeding and driver attention in cities. Tesla has countered with its own study claiming FSD vehicles "do not speed excessively" and match surrounding traffic speed — the article notes this argument is undercut in a city that lowered speed limits specifically because human drivers were speeding, and recalls Electrek's earlier reporting that Tesla's safety data presented to Dutch and Swedish regulators relied on what it called an apples-to-oranges comparison inflating FSD's advantage.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that reading a speed limit sign is a basic task rather than an edge case, and that regular FSD users know the system performs poorly at it; he cites his own experience of receiving a speeding ticket after FSD drove 78 km/h in a 50 zone after Tesla removed a setting that would have prevented it. He calls the Flemish government's "driver is 100% responsible" stance a "cop-out," arguing regulators are approving the software's behavior, not just licensing a human driver.
