The panel (host Herbert with Alexandra Merz, Jeff Lutz and Cern Basher) reviews Elon Musk's DealBook Summit interview with the New York Times, in which he said Tesla and SpaceX are "probably" better together while stressing that shareholder votes may or may not succeed. They also cover the December 3 Tesla annual meeting and proxy-advisor fight, Musk's comments on an autonomous Model Y, the Cybercab launch in Dallas, and the Nvidia leadership turmoil as a succession-risk lesson for Tesla and SpaceX.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Musk's merger stance:** Musk said the companies do not need to be together but would "probably" be better together, and that this is not a merger at any cost. Votes are required, which "may or may not succeed, at least in the case of Tesla." He said he must represent other SpaceX shareholders and avoid a non-economic deal because he would be sued. The test is whether the two companies create more value together, and shareholders decide. Merz reads him as increasingly affirmative, with the question shifting from whether it happens to whether it succeeds. Lutz also thinks the detailed public Q&A suggests the two sides are converging, and Basher says Musk cannot say much while the process is under way.
2. **Lawsuit risk is overstated (Merz):** She argues that suing Musk would be very hard. Per her reading of the SpaceX S-1, a derivative suit needs 3% of shares, cases are confined to the Texas business court without a jury, directors' monetary liability is limited, and Texas's business-judgment presumption means a plaintiff must prove fraud, intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law. She also notes a 2025 Texas provision that lets special committees obtain an independence determination before a merger announcement, and that SpaceX is a controlled company with Musk's class B shares carrying 10 votes each. She says she is surveying the courts on whether that provision is being used.

## Financials
1. **Vote math on the Tesla side (Merz):** Musk will vote about 20% in favor. About 500 million shares follow the proxy advisers ISS and Glass Lewis, and roughly 30% of shares do not vote and count as no. She says that to reach 50% of outstanding shares, seven of ten remaining voters must say yes, and nine of ten to reach 60%. A generous premium is therefore needed, and she doubts a proposal will come unless it is compelling. Lutz agrees the offer must be very compelling.
2. **Compensation and milestones:** Musk said he should not be compensated for merely merging, and that Tesla stock options contingent on Tesla execution should not vest because of a merger. Merz says the 2025 plan's market-cap milestones, the first at $2 trillion for Tesla, would be taken over while operational ones vanish on a merger. She wants operational milestones carried over and market-cap thresholds reset for the combined entity, which she thinks would be at least $3.5 trillion. She also wants succession-plan, class B share and other provisions cleared up in the merger proposal. Whether a change-in-control clause is triggered depends on the ratio and is a key definitional question. The panel sees Musk getting ahead of the issue.
3. **December 3 annual meeting and proxy advisers:** Votes include re-electing Elon Musk, JB Straubel and Robyn Denholm. ISS and Glass Lewis recommended against Denholm, as they did in 2023. Tesla answered with a five-page letter, arguing the firms have no financial exposure and previously opposed pay awards that shareholders approved with 75% (the 2025 award) and 77% (2018) of votes cast. Merz says Denholm will be re-elected and that Tesla is really fighting to break the roughly 500 million-share block before the merger vote, which counts outstanding shares. She also says Texas law now limits advisers to recommendations on stock they hold, and ISS and Glass Lewis are suing Texas lawmakers.

## Robotaxi
1. **Autonomous Model Y:** Musk said Cybercab's two seats cover about 90% of passenger miles (average occupancy about 1.2 people), but for families Tesla plans an autonomous Model Y without steering wheel or pedals carrying six or seven people. Lutz sees a gradual transition and Tesla pivoting to robots and high-volume designs. Basher likes that it can use existing Model Y capacity on three continents, but is wary of anything requiring new factories.
2. **Fleet growth math (Basher):** Tesla added about 200 Cybercabs to its Texas fleet in one day this week. To hold 17% week-over-week growth it needs about 200 vehicles a week now and about 400 a week by year-end, which he says would hit stated Cybercab capacity by March or April. He thinks sales of Cybercabs to consumers will be minimal because fleet use is more profitable.
3. **Dallas launch with Cybercab:** Tesla's robotaxi account announced Cybercab in Dallas, its second Cybercab city after Austin. Ashok Elluswamy said it came at almost no extra engineering effort, which Lutz attributes to automating new-city bring-up and reducing labor. Eric Early called it version 1.5. Observers noted a rear trunk camera and a darker integrated Starlink antenna spot.
4. **Consumer Cybercab specs:** Personal-use Cybercabs (not for commercial use, geofenced to robotaxi areas) have 279 miles of range, 20.1 cubic feet of storage, are 176 inches long, and add 123 miles in 50 minutes at a Supercharger. Warranty is 4 years or 50,000 miles, with drive units covered 8 years or 100,000 miles. Features include 100W USB power delivery and a 21-inch touchscreen. Basher sees this mainly as a test of integrating owner vehicles into the fleet.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia and key-man risk:** The panel debates Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia as a governance cautionary tale. Musk called Huang the "Michael Jordan" of the industry and said removing him did not seem good for shareholders. Lutz criticizes the speed of the board's action, and Basher faults the board for lacking succession planning, noting Tesla and SpaceX show more of their bench. Merz criticizes the interim leadership and Nvidia's Delaware incorporation and annual board elections, and contrasts Tesla's staggered three-year terms. She says she is Tesla-focused and not organizing an Nvidia retail campaign.
