This video is an interview with venture capitalist Larry Goldberg (identified as running the site lumiscenti.com) laying out his case for why Tesla stock could reach $1,500 by 2027 — a number he says the host of another show, Randy Kirk, first proposed, with Goldberg only calling it "not impossible." His argument centers on Tesla's multi-year transition from a car company to an "embodied AI" company, with robotaxi expansion, FSD regulatory change, Semi production, and Optimus pilot programs as the main catalysts for a stock revaluation he expects to play out through 2027.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **NHTSA rule change expected by end of 2027:** Goldberg predicts NHTSA will set a formal standard permitting unsupervised FSD by the end of next year (before the next election cycle), after which he believes Tesla will declare FSD a driverless system and drop current geofencing restrictions as a "containment strategy" rather than a technical necessity.
2. **Personal experience, not fleet data:** Goldberg says he has used FSD for about a year and a half without needing to intervene except for personal preference, but cautions this is a single data point and that only Tesla has the data on how close the system truly is to full autonomy at scale.
3. **Automatic collision avoidance cited as a safety signal:** He notes Tesla's FSD-equipped cars now have automatic collision avoidance, which he argues strengthens the case for unsupervised driving even though a driver may still need to remain in the seat as a fallback under near-term rules.

## Robotaxi
1. **Geofencing seen as temporary:** Goldberg expects geofencing to effectively disappear by mid-to-late next year, with robotaxi expansion accelerating in the first half of the year at the rideshare level before removing boundaries by around June.
2. **Sub-$1/mile pricing as the key threshold:** He argues Tesla needs to get robotaxi pricing below roughly $1 per mile (and ideally toward 70 cents/mile) to go beyond replacing ride-share trips and start displacing personal car ownership, since about 70% of trips involve a single driver; he believes Tesla can hit this pricing within the next year, achievable he says specifically in the Cybercab rather than necessarily the Model Y.
3. **Robotaxi economics already close:** He states current robotaxi rides are already under $2 per mile and "not that far off" the $1/mile target.
4. **Robotaxi modeled as a business by mid-2027:** Per the host's framing, Goldberg expects robotaxi to become a business Wall Street can realistically model by mid-2027 as regulatory clarity and fleet scale increase.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus framed as proof-of-concept, not mass sales:** Goldberg clarifies he does not expect large external customer deliveries of Optimus next year — he estimates roughly 5,000–10,000 units distributed across perhaps five to ten pilot customers as a plausible (and possibly even lower) outcome, with the real catalyst being visible use-case validation rather than volume.
2. **Internal Optimus deployment described:** He describes (unverified, via plans or rumor) roughly 20,000 initial units going to Tesla for internal training, with further units potentially going to factories and suppliers; he relays a claim — which he says is "probably not just a rumor" — that Giga Berlin employees report teleoperation staff already training Optimus units on factory tasks there.
3. **Scaling humanoid robots seen as harder than scaling pure AI:** Goldberg argues embodied AI is substantially more complex than software AI, so scaling robot production to tens of thousands of units will take significantly more effort than comparable past AI scaling milestones.

## Financials
1. **Capital raise of $30–50 billion expected:** Goldberg states Tesla will need to raise on the order of $30 billion to $50 billion (he's not certain of the exact figure) in capital markets to fund its AI/robotics transition, which he says is part of why the stock has faced pressure.
2. **Semi and energy businesses as overhead offset:** He argues that because Tesla has built up overhead to support robotaxi and robot development, incremental profit from Semi, energy, and core auto operations now flows straight to the bottom line; he cites a potential 50,000 Semi trucks per year production level as something that could meaningfully cover overhead and boost gross profit, though he says this narrative matters less than FSD/robotaxi catalysts in the near term.
3. **Terafab and AI data center rental pushed to later years:** Goldberg says Tesla's Terafab chip factory story belongs to around 2030, and that Tesla hasn't launched an AI data-center rental business (unlike xAI) because its own data centers are fully consumed by Optimus and FSD compute needs — he says Tesla needs its next-generation AI5 chip before it would have spare capacity to rent out.
4. **Stock already reflects partial robotaxi expectations:** Goldberg acknowledges Tesla's elevated P/E (cited as roughly 300x) already partially prices in robotaxi launch expectations, but argues the leap toward $1,500 is comparable in scale to a prior roughly 20-fold increase in the stock over a year and a half when Tesla was "just a car company," which he cites as precedent for large repricing moves.
