Electrek's coverage today centers entirely on Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call and 10-Q filing, scrutinizing capex guidance, free cash flow quality, Optimus delivery timelines, a reversal on self-driving mapping strategy, and an unanswered shareholder question about Hardware 3 owners.

## Financials
1. **Tesla quietly cuts 2026 capex guidance to $20B:** Fred Lambert reports that Tesla's Q3 10-Q now guides 2026 capex to "in excess of $20 billion," down from the "more than $25 billion" guided since April. Tesla has spent $13.01 billion through Q3 ($2.49B, $5.79B, $4.73B by quarter); hitting $25B would have needed roughly $12 billion in Q4 alone versus about $7 billion under the new floor. The article notes $3.67 billion of equipment/construction spending was booked in liabilities but unpaid at quarter-end, up from $2.63B in June and $1.63B a year ago, meaning actual spending outpaces reported cash capex. Tesla reported $6.26 billion operating cash flow and $1.53 billion free cash flow; Lambert's analysis finds working capital items — $1.92B higher accounts payable, $1.95B higher accrued liabilities, and $1.26B from deferred revenue (including $588 million billed to SpaceX for undelivered Megapacks, still unpaid at quarter-end) — drove the positive figure, and without the $2.59B working-capital boost, free cash flow would have been about negative $1.06 billion; counting unpaid capex, free cash flow on an "as spent" basis was closer to $500 million. Tesla also signed $30 billion in new credit facilities (a $20B delayed-draw term loan maturing 2029 and $10B revolving credit) and held $45.4 billion in cash and investments, undrawn. Lambert's take: the capex cut, revealed quietly in the 10-Q rather than the shareholder letter, signals either project slippage into 2027 or deliberate cash preservation, and argues Tesla should clarify which. ## Humanoid Bots
2. **Tesla pushes Optimus deliveries to late 2027, says hands still a problem:** On the Q3 2026 earnings call, Elon Musk said Tesla now targets external Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027, citing unresolved issues with the robot's hands, durability, and AI. Musk said Tesla has begun early production on a high-volume Fremont line designed for roughly a million robots a year (about 20,000 units/week), though reaching that rate will take at least a year or more since the design is still changing; a Tesla engineer flagged the hand and custom gears as the hardest areas. Musk said Tesla plans to build 10,000–20,000 robots for an "Optimus Academy" to generate training data, and aims to put its AI5 chip in Optimus around April 2027; he also promised a video within a month or two showing unscripted task performance. Tesla's shareholder letter called the new bots "the first production-quality bots that we have manufactured." Musk claimed no one is close to Tesla on general-purpose humanoid robots. Fred Lambert's take disputes this, citing XPeng's automated IRON production line (targeting mass production by end of 2026, deliveries in H1 2027) and Boston Dynamics' Atlas, already deploying at Hyundai's Metaplant with committed 2026 customers, arguing Tesla faces the same unsolved generalization problem as competitors and that timelines have slipped every year historically. ## Autonomous Driving
3. **Tesla now says it needs its own maps for FSD after years of dismissing maps:** Responding to a shareholder question on the Q3 2026 call, Tesla VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy said "we shouldn't be depending on external people's maps" and described a new AI-based mapping and navigation architecture built from fleet data, which will handle trip start/end with FSD v15 and later take over navigation, parking and destination handling. Fred Lambert contrasts this with Musk's 2019 Autonomy Day comments calling reliance on high-precision maps "a huge mistake" and "extremely brittle." Lambert's take argues this brings Tesla's approach closer to Waymo's mapping-based model, citing a Belgian road safety group's finding (reported last month) that FSD sped in 55% of tested 30 km/h zones in Brussels, averaging 44 km/h, and NHTSA's consideration of an investigation into Tesla Robotaxis speeding through school zones covering roughly 1,000 Model Y and Cybercab vehicles, as evidence that vision-only perception has struggled with basic environmental understanding like speed limit signs. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/22/tesla-building-own-maps-fsd-after-mocking-maps/)

4. **Tesla skipped the most important shareholder question on its earnings call:** Fred Lambert reports Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call skipped a top-5-ranked shareholder question (557 votes, 652,900 shares on the Say platform) asking when Hardware 3 owners will learn if they'll get a free computer upgrade for unsupervised FSD, while answering lower-ranked questions on Optimus revenue and FSD mapping. On the second question Tesla did answer, Ashok Elluswamy said FSD v15 software would bring customer cars into the Robotaxi fleet "somewhere around Q2 next year" — but this applies only to HW4, not HW3, after Musk said on the Q1 2026 call that "Hardware 3 simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD." The article notes Tesla faces class actions in the US, China, and Australia plus a Dutch collective claim now covering nearly 7,000 HW3 owners, and that HW3 owners received only a stripped-down FSD v14 "Lite" this year. Lambert's take: Tesla likely avoided the question deliberately because any answer carries a large potential cost and litigation risk, and he predicts no real HW3 plan will emerge without a court forcing one. [Read on Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/22/tesla-skipped-hw3-shareholder-question-earnings-call/)

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert takes a consistently skeptical line across all four pieces: arguing Tesla's headline free-cash-flow and capex figures obscure a weaker underlying cash position, that Optimus and FSD timelines keep slipping while Musk overstates Tesla's competitive lead, that Tesla's pivot to proprietary mapping contradicts years of dismissing map-based self-driving, and that Tesla avoided its most consequential shareholder question to limit legal and financial exposure on Hardware 3.
