This video features host Herbert interviewing Cern Basher, a chartered financial analyst and founder of Brilliant Advice, a wealth management firm. The conversation addresses a core challenge facing successful Tesla, SpaceX, and other concentrated-position investors: how to generate cash flow from highly appreciated stock holdings without selling shares, triggering capital gains taxes, or sacrificing long-term upside. Eight potential strategies are presented and evaluated, with particular depth on options strategies and a margin-plus-income-securities approach using preferred stocks.

## Financials

1. **The Concentrated Position Problem:** The central dilemma is that investors with large, appreciated stock positions — the video uses Tesla and SpaceX as primary examples — see their net worth rise but not their cash flow. Selling triggers capital gains taxes, while simply holding provides no income. The video frames the challenge as having three dimensions: generating cash flow while keeping upside, generating cash flow while reducing downside risk, and generating cash flow without triggering capital gains events.

2. **Income Needs vs. Portfolio Size:** Cern Basher presents a framework matching annual income needs to portfolio size. On a $5 million portfolio, $100,000/year represents 2% (described as doable), $200,000 is 4% (potentially achievable), and pushing into the 6–8% range starts to carry meaningful risk. On a $25 million portfolio, $1 million/year is only 4%, making it far more achievable with conservative strategies. The duration of the income need matters as much as the amount — drawing $1 million/year from a $5 million portfolio over five years requires the portfolio to double just to preserve capital.

3. **SpaceX IPO Context and Liquidity Warning:** Cern notes that SpaceX has recently gone public at an IPO price of $135, rising to a high of approximately $225 before closing at $185 on the Thursday prior to the video. He cautions that only approximately 4% of total SpaceX shares are freely tradable at this time, making it an extremely illiquid stock prone to large, rapid price swings. Lock-up periods are expected to expire beginning in August, with full lock-up release extending potentially into next year. He flags that most of the strategies discussed require shares to be post-lock-up, so SpaceX holders should treat this as preparation for future action rather than immediate opportunity.

4. **SpaceX Valuation History Cited:** Cern presents a SpaceX valuation chart showing the company started at approximately $27 million in late 2002, reached roughly $1 billion around the time of the first Falcon 9 flight (2010–2011 timeframe per the speaker), hit $10 billion when Google and Fidelity invested approximately $1 billion, and most recently was valued at less than $400 billion "last summer" (implying summer 2025, per the 2026-06-20 publish date), with the current public market valuation described as "around a couple of trillion dollars."

## Market & Competition

1. **Eight Strategies Overview:** The video outlines eight potential income strategies for concentrated-position holders: (1) option strategies (covered calls, collars, strangles, etc.); (2) securities lending (renting shares to short sellers); (3) securities-backed line of credit (pledged asset line); (4) margin loans; (5) prepaid variable forward contracts; (6) exchange funds; (7) charitable vehicles (charitable remainder trusts, donor advised funds, gift annuities, private foundations); and (8) margin combined with income-producing securities. Each carries distinct trade-offs around upside sacrifice, leverage risk, tax complexity, counterparty risk, and loss of flexibility.

2. **Options Strategies — Covered Calls and Variations:** Selling covered calls generates premium income by granting someone the right to buy your shares at a set price. The risk is "upside regret" — if the stock surges past the strike price, shares get called away and the investor misses significant appreciation. Cern recommends selling calls on only a portion of the position (he uses 30% as an illustrative example, not a recommendation) to preserve some upside. Selling shorter-duration calls (e.g., weekly) reduces the probability of large moves but also reduces premium income. Tesla's high volatility makes call premium attractive but inconsistent week-to-week, and Cern warns against selling covered calls around Tesla earnings periods due to elevated move risk. More complex structures — covered strangles, protective collars, put spreads, ratio collars — can customize the payoff profile but add significant complexity and monitoring requirements.

3. **Buying Put Options as Downside Insurance:** Purchasing put options caps downside losses but costs premium, reducing net returns. Cern frames this as analogous to home or auto insurance — you pay a recurring cost for protection. He notes this approach is typically very expensive for stocks and would be difficult to sustain long-term for risks like "keyman risk" (citing Elon Musk as an example for SpaceX and Tesla). A short-duration put might be appropriate around a specific event (e.g., a high-risk international trip), but long-term put buying is described as prohibitively costly.

4. **Securities-Backed Line of Credit vs. Margin Loan:** A pledged asset line (securities-backed line of credit, offered through brokerage-affiliated banks such as Schwab Bank or Morgan Stanley) allows borrowing against securities at typically lower rates than standard margin, but proceeds cannot be used to purchase additional securities. Margin loans allow borrowed funds to be reinvested in securities, enabling leverage, but carry the same margin call and forced liquidation risk. In both cases, the lender can change collateral requirements — Cern gives the example of a broker reducing the allowable loan-to-value ratio from 50% to 20% during a market decline, potentially forcing liquidation even if the investor thought they had a buffer.

5. **Prepaid Variable Forward Contracts:** A large broker-dealer pays cash upfront to the investor today; in exchange, the investor contractually agrees to deliver shares at a specified future date (e.g., five years). The number of shares delivered may vary based on where the stock price is at the delivery date. Cern notes this becomes more attractive on very large positions (e.g., $50 million or more) where favorable financing rates are available, and that tax and legal complexity is significant.

6. **Margin Plus Income Securities — STRC (Strategy Preferred Stock):** The most detailed strategy discussed involves borrowing on margin and investing the proceeds in high-yielding preferred stocks to capture a net spread. The example used is STRC, a preferred stock issued by Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy), the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder. STRC currently pays an 11.5% annualized dividend, paid monthly and moving to semi-monthly (every two weeks) starting in July, with the first record date at end of June and first payment July 15th. On a $5 million stock portfolio, Cern illustrates borrowing $3.25 million on margin at a negotiated rate of 5.5% and investing it in STRC, generating a 6% net spread and approximately $195,000/year in annual income — roughly 4% yield on the original $5 million portfolio. At the $25 million portfolio level (with a modeled margin rate of 5%), the strategy could generate approximately $1 million/year. He notes that default margin rates at major brokers like Schwab are approximately 11.83% and Fidelity approximately 10%+, but large accounts can negotiate materially lower rates.

7. **STRC Tax Treatment — Return of Capital:** A key differentiator for STRC is that its dividends may qualify for return-of-capital tax treatment, meaning each dividend payment reduces the investor's cost basis rather than being taxed as current income. At 11.5%/year, it would take over nine years before the cost basis reaches zero, at which point dividends revert to qualified dividend (long-term capital gain) treatment. Cern illustrates that a nominal 11.5% dividend with 0% current tax is economically equivalent to roughly 13%+ on an after-tax basis compared to ordinary income alternatives. Upon sale, the reduced cost basis creates a larger capital gain — described as tax deferral, not tax elimination.

8. **STRC Price Mechanics and Recent Volatility:** Strategy actively manages STRC's price by selling new shares when STRC trades above $100 (capping upside), and adjusting the dividend upward if the volume-weighted average price falls below certain thresholds: +25 basis points if VWAP is between $95–$99, and +50 basis points if VWAP falls below $95. This means the dividend for the current month could rise from 11.5% to approximately 12% given recent price weakness. Cern reports that STRC hit a low of approximately $83 during the week of the video and closed around $88, attributed to cascading margin-call liquidations from investors who over-leveraged their STRC positions or had other portfolio assets decline simultaneously. He believes STRC will recover based on fundamentals.

9. **Strategy vs. Strive Fundamental Comparison:** Cern compares Strategy (MSTR/STRC) and its competitor Strive (preferred stock: SATA, branded as "the daily dividend company" paying every business day — approximately 250 times/year at 13%). Strategy holds approximately 846,000 Bitcoin ($53 billion reserve at $63,000/Bitcoin), $1.1 billion cash, $6.7 billion debt, net reserve approximately $47–48 billion, annual dividend commitment of $1.7 billion, implying approximately 28 years of dividend coverage. Strive holds 19,150 Bitcoin ($1.2 billion), $141 million cash, approximately $44–45 million in STRC reserves, no debt, total reserve approximately $1.4 billion, annual dividend commitment under $100 million, implying approximately 14 years of coverage. Despite Strive's stronger recent price performance (down only ~2.3% from par vs. STRC down ~11%), Cern argues Strategy's fundamentals are approximately 34–39 times stronger on a reserve basis, and expects STRC to recover. He also notes STRC was the largest preferred stock IPO of 2025, raising approximately $2.4–2.5 billion, subsequently eclipsed by the SpaceX IPO in 2026.

10. **Estate Tax Threshold Mentioned:** Cern briefly flags that holding highly appreciated stock until death may trigger estate tax issues. He estimates the per-person estate tax exemption at approximately $15 million (noted with some uncertainty — he says "15 or 16 million"), above which the estate becomes subject to estate taxes.
