Tesla has launched "Tesla Home," a consumer-facing home energy management platform built on its existing Opticaster AI engine, though Electrek notes this is largely a rebrand and app consolidation rather than a genuinely new product.

## Energy
1. **Tesla Home ties together existing hardware:** Tesla Home controls a home's battery, solar, Wall Connector, and smart breakers to optimize electricity use, coming standard on every Powerwall with no new hardware required. Users pick a goal — "Savings" (minimize bill vs. utility rates) or "Self-Powered" (run on stored solar after dark) — and the app shows each decision the system makes and why.

2. **Opticaster is years-old, proven software:** The underlying AI, Opticaster, has run on every Powerwall for years and is part of Tesla's "Autonomous Control" suite that also powers Autobidder trading software. Tesla says Opticaster has accumulated over a hundred million hours of operational experience and delivered tens of millions of dollars in value from opex savings and grid service revenue across thousands of customers globally; it also underlies Tesla's utility-scale deployments and virtual power plants, which paid Powerwall owners nearly $10 million in 2024.

3. **What's actually new is an app update:** Tesla app version 4.58.6, rolling out this week, consolidates scattered energy toggles into a single "Home Controls" menu, adds smart breaker circuit control, and introduces a "Home Status" AI summary feature. Rate Plan Charging (timing EV charging to cheapest hours) works when Powerwall is paired with a Gen 3 or Universal Wall Connector. None of this requires new hardware for existing customers.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls Tesla Home a smart consolidation and rebrand with a useful UI cleanup, praising Opticaster as genuinely good software, but stresses it is not really a new product — and questions whether Opticaster can deliver for residential owners the same benefits it provides Tesla's commercial energy storage fleet.
