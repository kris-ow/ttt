This video from brighterwithherbert features host Herbert and guest Brian White (of the FutureAzA YouTube channel) reviewing a series of competitive moves in the autonomous driving and EV space, including updates on Stellantis, Mercedes, Xiaomi, Waymo, and Pony AI. The central thesis is that Tesla's vision-only, neural-net-first approach is increasingly being validated as competitors either pivot toward it (Xiaomi/Xpeng) or continue struggling with lidar-dependent systems. The discussion also covers Tesla's European sales recovery and the unit economics challenges facing robotaxi competitors.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Europe Sales Rebound Continues:** New car registrations for Tesla vehicles across the European Union, UK, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland jumped 46.5% year-over-year last month (April 2026), totaling 10,654 units. In the EU alone, sales grew over 67% from a year earlier. This marks the third consecutive month of growth, following an improvement of over 84% in March 2026 and nearly 12% in February 2026. Brian White contextualizes this as a return to pre-refresh levels rather than a new high, noting Tesla is holding steady with the same core lineup despite intensifying competition and lower-priced alternatives.

2. **FSD Europe Approval Has Not Yet Impacted Sales:** Brian White argues that FSD approval in Lithuania and the Netherlands has had essentially no measurable effect on sales yet — perhaps five units out of roughly 10,000. He reasons that awareness is low, the product is only in very limited hands, and customers won't purchase until FSD is approved in their specific country and clearly available today.

3. **Xiaomi YU7 Standard Edition Launches at $33,000:** Xiaomi released a new standard trim of the YU7 — a vehicle frequently positioned as a Model Y competitor — priced at approximately $33,000. Brian White notes Xiaomi is losing roughly $5,000 per car, which structurally limits their ability to scale since selling more vehicles only deepens losses. He explains Xiaomi did not build its first car (the SU7) in-house but contracted a state-run manufacturer, then subsequently purchased that factory. He views it as a strong first effort but one still exhibiting early-production quirks.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Xiaomi/Xpeng Vision-Only Pivot Validates Tesla's Approach:** Xpeng CEO He Xiaopeng publicly stated the company is "absolutely adamant" they no longer need lidar, even though he acknowledged it is good technology. Brian White notes this declaration was first made in July 2024, after which lidar on existing Xpeng vehicles was deprecated and is now purely ornamental. He positions Xpeng as the current leader in end-to-end neural net autonomous driving in China, ahead of competitors still relying on Huawei's lidar-based platform. He argues that Huawei functions as the "Intel Inside" of FSD in China but has not yet fully adopted neural net approaches due to AI chip export restrictions.

2. **Stellantis Announces $70 Billion EV and Autonomy Plan with Wave AI:** Stellantis announced a €60 billion strategic plan to build 29 new EVs by 2030, with nine priced under €30,000, on a dedicated EV architecture. Their partner Wave AI announced it will bring the Wave AI driver to Stellantis's auto-drive platform, promising hands-free, door-to-door supervised automated driving across urban streets and highways, launching in 2028. Brian White acknowledges the plan has real strategic necessity — EU regulations require all-electric vehicles by 2035 — but remains skeptical Wave AI is the best partner and notes the system is still lidar-based.

3. **Mercedes Plans Automated Driving Rollout in Germany by End of 2026:** Mercedes announced plans to deploy a point-to-point automated driving system (not highway-only) in selected German cities by the end of 2026, with nationwide rollout planned for 2027. The hosts speculate it may involve Nvidia's Alpamayo platform but note that platform was only recently announced and could not realistically be integrated into production vehicles in under a year. Brian White gives roughly 10% odds this timeline is met, calling it structurally implausible for a company as large as Mercedes to execute that quickly, and notes Mercedes previously abandoned its Level 3 highway system after minimal consumer adoption.

4. **Waymo Pauses Atlanta Service Over Flooded Road Incidents:** A gold-painted Waymo driverless vehicle was spotted in Los Angeles, which the hosts note visually echoes Tesla's Cybercab. More significantly, Waymo paused service in Atlanta after vehicles repeatedly drove into flooded areas — a software recall was issued. Brian White provides context: Waymo first over-corrected by stopping for any puddle, then pushed an update to drive through them, leading to the current problem. He contrasts this with Tesla's FSD, which he says uses neural net reasoning to assess whether another vehicle's behavior in similar conditions makes the path safe.

## Robotaxi

1. **Pony AI Raises Fleet Target to 3,500 Vehicles by End of 2026:** Pony AI announced it is raising its robotaxi fleet target to 3,500 vehicles by the end of 2026 — an increase of 500 — after reporting stronger-than-expected Q1 2026 revenue of $34 million against an average estimate of $21 million. The company now expects full-year robotaxi revenue to grow 3.5 times its 2025 level. However, Pony AI reported a net loss of $58 million against that $34 million revenue, meaning they are losing nearly twice what they earn. The hosts note their fares would need to be roughly 300% higher to break even, though some costs won't scale linearly.

2. **Robotaxi Unit Economics Remain Deeply Challenged Across the Industry:** The hosts highlight systemic profitability problems across robotaxi operators. Waymo is losing approximately $5 billion per year and roughly $100 per ride. Pony AI's losses suggest fares are underpriced by approximately 70% relative to break-even. Brian White argues these are structural problems requiring fundamental fixes, not incremental fare increases, and that scale will only partially help since some costs (compute, vehicles) don't fall proportionally.

## Market & Competition

1. **Lidar vs. Vision-Only Debate Continues to Favor Vision:** The hosts argue that every lidar-first company is underperforming Tesla in autonomous driving capability. Waymo's Atlanta flood incident is cited as an example of lidar's inability to substitute for genuine intelligence and neural net reasoning. Brian White contends that FSD supervised today is "vastly safer than a human driving," citing distractions like phone use as a persistent human problem, and argues the industry is moving toward vision-centric end-to-end systems pioneered by Tesla and now being adopted by Xpeng in China.

2. **China AV Market Faces Regulatory Headwinds:** The hosts reference a prior incident in China where widespread outages of BYD robotaxis caused major traffic disruptions, prompting regulators to freeze new permits for high-level self-driving vehicles. This backdrop makes Pony AI's fleet expansion ambitions more challenging even as their revenues improve.
