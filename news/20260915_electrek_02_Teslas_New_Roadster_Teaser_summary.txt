Electrek's Fred Lambert writes that Tesla will unveil the long-delayed Roadster on October 1, and a new teaser suggests the production version will adopt Tesla's angular "Cyber" design language rather than the original 2017 design; the author argues this is an unnecessary redesign when the car simply needs to ship.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster reveal set for October 1:** Tesla will unveil the Roadster at an event near Waco, Texas, more than eight years after first showing the car in 2017. A weekend teaser shows SpaceX-derived cold gas thrusters firing from the rear and a full-width light bar resembling the robotaxi's styling, and Elon Musk has promised "radical design changes."

2. **Cold gas thruster performance claims:** Musk says the Roadster will do 0-60 mph in under two seconds without the thrusters and under one second with them. The article reports the demo has been scaled back from Musk's earlier claim that the car could "fly" to a brief hover, that the demo car reportedly won't be street-legal, and that Tesla plans to run it remotely with no driver inside because the thrusters pose a noise hazard. Musk himself reportedly called the sub-one-second 0-60 figure "the least interesting part."

3. **Original 2017 promises and repeated delays:** At the 2017 reveal, Tesla promised a 620-mile range, 0-60 in 1.9 seconds, a top speed above 250 mph, four-seat capacity, and a base price of $200,000; a $250,000 Founders Series edition sold out on $50,000 deposits. By the author's count, the reveal has slipped at least eight times since a promised 2020 production start, and reservation holders including Marques Brownlee and Sam Altman have canceled their orders.

4. **Merger speculation on delay motive:** The article notes that a former Tesla president has suggested the repeated delays are intended to tie the Roadster's narrative to SpaceX ahead of a possible merger — described in the article as speculation, not a confirmed plan.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla should simply produce the 2017-design Roadster updated with modern range/performance and FSD, at a price near original reservations, without thrusters or a "cyber" makeover, and criticizes Tesla for conflating "new" with "better" and for repeatedly re-launching old products for stock-price effect.
