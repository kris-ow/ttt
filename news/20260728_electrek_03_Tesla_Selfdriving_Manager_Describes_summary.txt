The article reports on a wrongful-termination lawsuit filed by Javier Medrano, former manager of Tesla's Houston "Full Self-Driving" test fleet, alleging chronic understaffing led to unsafe conditions, including a crash involving a member of the public, before he was fired.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Staffing ratio allegedly doubled past Tesla's own safety baseline:** Medrano says he became sole operational manager of Tesla's Houston FSD test fleet by mid-2024, and Tesla then increased his workload from 15 vehicles/operators to 38 across three 24-hour shifts — a "38:1 operator-to-manager ratio" the complaint says exceeded Tesla's own "mandated 15:1 safety baseline." Other test cities reportedly had multiple safety leads while Houston had only Medrano; his duties included auditing camera footage, weekly ride-alongs, and incident investigations.

2. **Test drivers worked extreme hours; manager describes exhaustion:** The filing states test drivers averaged 60–80 hours per week and remained on-call through weekends. Medrano describes his own condition as trying not to lose his absolute mind from lack of sleep and food, and says he sent an S.O.S. requesting time off in March 2025.

3. **Crash incident and manager's alleged impaired response:** On March 30, 2025, at 2:05 a.m., one of the autonomous test vehicles struck a member of the public. Medrano says he was so sleep-deprived when he received the emergency call that he processed it while physically asleep and gave unsafe guidance, later reporting no recollection of the call; the operator reportedly stayed at the scene for about an hour.

4. **Concerns raised, then termination:** Medrano says he raised staffing concerns in February 2025 and was given a performance ultimatum instead of additional hires, with an HR representative allegedly telling him to put his phone on "Do Not Disturb" rather than address understaffing. Tesla fired him on May 1, 2025, with an Autopilot director reportedly blaming him for not delegating; a stock award due to vest days later was stripped. After his firing, Tesla promoted one of his subordinates and brought in two additional team leads from Dallas to fill the role. Medrano, who filed the complaint himself and now works for a rival EV company, is seeking reinstatement, back pay, and damages; Tesla did not respond to a request for comment.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert cautions the complaint is one-sided, unproven, and from a self-represented plaintiff, with Tesla declining comment, so details could change once Tesla responds. Still, he argues the allegations are believable and consistent with Tesla's "move fast, break things" approach, and that if accurate, they support his view that safety validation — not convenience — is the real bottleneck for Tesla's robotaxi plans.
