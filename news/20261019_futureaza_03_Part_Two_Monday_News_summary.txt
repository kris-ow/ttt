## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Roadster draws skeptical praise from a rival EV CEO:** The Bugatti Rimac CEO, reacting to the Tesla Roadster, said his company considered adding cold-gas thrusters back in 2020 but decided against it due to cost and complexity, noting that without SpaceX-level expertise in cold gas thrusters it would be prohibitively expensive to develop. The host frames the Roadster as Tesla's equivalent of a Formula 1 program — a halo/tech-demonstrator project whose real value is the technology (e.g., 4680 cells capable of higher charge rates) that could filter down to production vehicles, rather than a mass-market product.
2. **Host addresses Roadster "fake" skepticism:** Responding to viewer comments, the host says some people believe the Roadster preview was staged; he notes Elon Musk said the large invite list was meant to make the vehicle's capabilities credible ("seeing is believing"), and compares it to Tesla inviting a skeptical Freight Waves editor to the Semi unveiling, who reportedly changed his view after driving it. The host argues the Roadster's unconventional capabilities will naturally look "fake" to some, but that skeptics are unlikely to be buyers regardless given the expected high price.
3. **Model Y / Model Y L sold out, delivery delays:** A viewer comment states Model Y L is sold out through April and most other Model Y configurations are sold out through year-end; the host speculates this reflects strong demand (possibly underestimated gas-price sensitivity at the start of the year) rather than lack of interest, and notes a separate claim that Model Y L delivery dates have been pushed back to a January–March window. The host says he saw Model Y L units during a factory tour but is barred by NDA from discussing specifics.
4. **China EV sales chart — Model Y leads amid stiff competition:** Citing a sales chart (not differentiated fully by pure-EV vs. plug-in hybrid in its first section), the host notes Tesla Model Y is reportedly selling over 10% ahead of the next-closest model (a Geely vehicle) in China, with BYD's Song also shown; the host pushes back on narratives that Tesla is "doing badly" in China, noting Tesla ranked No. 1 on the metric shown despite being down modestly year-over-year, which he calls normal fluctuation. BYD still outsells Tesla in China only when aggregating across BYD's full model lineup.
5. **Volkswagen to import Chinese-made EVs into Europe:** Volkswagen is reportedly evaluating bringing China-only EV models (cited: an ID Unix model) to Europe as lineup additions rather than replacements; these vehicles use Chinese batteries, motors, software, and manufacturing. The host frames this as Chinese-made cars entering the European market via a legacy Western brand, undercutting locally-built models on price.

## Market & Competition
1. **Toyota threatens to scale back UK sales over EV targets:** Toyota has reportedly warned it may reduce UK vehicle sales due to EV mandate targets it calls unrealistic; Renault's CEO separately called for the UK to align with EU net-zero targets and Chinese-car tariffs. The host is skeptical, arguing automakers had a decade of notice and pursued alternatives (hydrogen, stalled solid-state battery efforts) instead of EV readiness.
2. **Cadillac Escalade IQ fire and factory idling:** A fire involving Cadillac Escalade IQ EVs reportedly burned for about 18 hours at a dealership, requiring crews to work in two-hour shifts to prevent reignition. Separately, GM's Lansing Grand River plant (retooled since a prior commitment of over a billion dollars for EV production) may not build cars for up to 18 months amid slowing EV demand, per the segment discussed.
3. **Canadian EV market sentiment favors Europe over the US:** An opinion piece cited says Canadians, per a survey, favor a more European-influenced vehicle market more than twice as much as a US-influenced one, amid Canada negotiating closer EU ties; the piece reportedly notes Nissan Leaf sales in Canada exceed total US Leaf sales.
4. **Faraday Future unveils humanoid robot and mini companion product:** Faraday Future is launching a roughly $2,490 humanoid robot aimed at developers and schools alongside a $299 "Nova mini" for children, with the first 100 Nova units going only to invited buyers. The host is highly skeptical given Faraday's history of unfulfilled products and rebadged Chinese vehicles, and says employees he's spoken with indicate the company spent heavily with little actual production progress.
5. **Austrian Tesla range-extender firm goes bankrupt:** An Austrian engineering firm known for modifying Teslas with gas-powered range extenders ("hyper hybrid" tech) has filed for bankruptcy with about $37 million in debt after over a decade pursuing the technology.
6. **Volvo to use Nvidia Hyperion platform for hands-free driving:** Volvo will use Nvidia's Hyperion platform (chips plus sensors) for hands-free, point-to-point driving on local roads, positioned as similar in concept to FSD. The host is skeptical of reported timelines suggesting availability by 2030, arguing Nvidia provides hardware/tools rather than a finished self-driving system, and that vehicle design lead times (around three years) and unresolved sensor specs make an aggressive 2030 target unrealistic.
