Tesla's own promotional videos are actively undermining its legal defense in FSD and Autopilot crash litigation, according to Electrek. Two recent official videos — one showing a driver making espresso while FSD drives, another showing FSD committing multiple traffic violations in Denmark — directly contradict Tesla's core courtroom argument that drivers bear full responsibility for supervising the system.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Espresso video invites driver inattention:** On May 26, Tesla's official X account posted a video of a driver using a portable espresso machine hands-free while FSD Supervised drives, captioned "With FSD Supervised, your Tesla can drive you anywhere you want. Try it yourself." The video, credited to creator @lucapasturini, has 5.6 million views. A fine-print disclaimer states active driver supervision is required, but the article notes the disclaimer directly contradicts the video's content.

2. **Denmark promotional video shows FSD breaking traffic laws:** On June 9, Tesla Europe posted a video celebrating FSD's approval in Denmark — the fourth European country to approve the system — and Elon Musk personally shared it. Danish newspaper Politiken analyzed the footage, shot near Rådhuspladsen in Copenhagen, and identified FSD driving in a bus-only lane, making an illegal right turn, ignoring a "no entry" sign, driving on a street closed to car traffic, and driving on a bicycle path. Denmark's FDM (equivalent to AAA) called the findings "worrying" and "quite critical." Tesla told Politiken it had no comment.

3. **Legal exposure context:** Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits across at least 21 litigation tracks, including crash lawsuits tied to 50–60 fatal Autopilot/FSD incidents, a certified class action over FSD false advertising, a China fraud lawsuit, and securities fraud claims. A jury awarded $243 million to victims in the Benavides Autopilot crash case — a verdict a federal judge upheld in February 2026 after Tesla attempted to overturn it; Tesla had previously rejected a $60 million settlement. Since that verdict, Tesla has quietly settled at least four additional Autopilot crash lawsuits. NHTSA has an open Engineering Analysis covering 3.2 million vehicles with over 80 documented FSD traffic violations already on file. Tesla has also been caught retroactively modifying FSD purchase contracts to insert "supervised" language not present in original agreements.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla is knowingly pursuing incompatible strategies — using near-autonomous marketing to sell FSD subscriptions while claiming in court that drivers are fully responsible — and that these two videos hand plaintiff attorneys direct evidence of that contradiction, potentially making the $243 million Benavides verdict "just the beginning."
