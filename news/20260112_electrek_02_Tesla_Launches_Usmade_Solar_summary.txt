Tesla has added a new solar panel, listed as the TSP-420, to its online configurator, marking what the author calls a rare sign of life for a solar business it has largely been winding down over the past two years.

## Energy
1. **New panel assembled in Buffalo:** The TSP-420 datasheet states it is "Proudly made on Earth by humans and assembled in Buffalo, New York, in the United States of America." The article notes "assembled" implies solar cells are produced elsewhere and assembled at Gigafactory New York, but calls this still significant since the factory — originally built for SolarCity to be the largest solar factory in the Western Hemisphere, then operated by Panasonic until its 2020 exit, and since used mainly for Supercharger components and Autopilot data labeling — is now making solar panels again after years of Tesla white-labeling panels from manufacturers like Q Cells.

2. **Specs and "Power Zones" technology:** The panel comes in 415W and 420W versions, in line with current market standards (Tesla previously adopted 420W panels in 2021). The datasheet claims "18 Power Zones" versus 6 in a standard panel; the article speculates this likely reflects a shingled cell design or custom string layout to better maintain output under partial shading, though this is the author's inference rather than a confirmed technical detail. The panel also integrates Zep-derived rail-less mounting hardware, acquired via the SolarCity acquisition, which Tesla says lets panels sit closer to the roof for a "minimalist aesthetic."

3. **Context of Tesla's declining solar business:** Tesla's solar deployments have never matched SolarCity's prior highs since the 2016 acquisition; Tesla stopped scheduling projects in many markets and stopped reporting solar deployment numbers in 2024, shifting instead to a strategy of supplying hardware to third-party certified installers via an "Installer Day" program. Tesla had announced late last year it would restart solar panel production in Buffalo, of which this launch is the fuller realization.

## Editorial Stance
The author is encouraged that Gigafactory New York is being used for its original purpose and finds the Power Zones shading technology potentially genuinely useful and competitive if manufacturing costs can be controlled. However, the author questions what this means strategically: Tesla has spent two years dismantling its solar scheduling and installation teams and pivoting to third-party installers, so it's unclear whether this premium US-made panel is meant for Tesla's own (now skeletal) crews or for sale to third-party installers, where it would compete with the commodity panels those installers currently favor. The author concludes Tesla's solar strategy still looks inconsistent ("a bit of a yo-yo") but credits the business with at least showing a pulse. A top user comment is also noted, skeptical that developing a panel is hard and cautioning the listing means little until production units actually ship.
