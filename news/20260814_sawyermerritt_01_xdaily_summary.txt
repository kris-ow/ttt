## Executive Summary
Today's digest is Tesla-heavy with one major SpaceX crossover item: the Terafab compute project officially breaking ground with a combined $16.8B capital commitment from Tesla and SpaceX. On the Tesla side, the standout news is a new Nevada Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit (capped at 10 vehicles), the resolution of a nearly three-year labor dispute with Sweden's IF Metall union, and incremental Energy/Supercharger infrastructure expansion. Competitor EV launches (Lucid, Rivian) round out the market-context coverage.

---

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Terafab construction begins — $16.8B joint Tesla/SpaceX capex:** The Terafab project, a joint Tesla-SpaceX initiative, has officially started construction. The initial phase alone requires $16.8 billion in capital investment split between the two companies, with future expansion phases described as pushing total investment "much higher" (no specific figure given for later phases). This is a significant compute/infrastructure buildout tying the two companies' theses together and represents a material capex commitment worth tracking in future guidance.

2. **Tesla gets Nevada Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit:** The Nevada Transportation Authority has granted Tesla an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit, but operations are explicitly capped at a maximum fleet of 10 fully autonomous vehicles, confined to an Authority-approved Operational Design Domain. This marks a new state added to Tesla's regulatory footprint for autonomous/robotaxi operations, though the initial scale is very limited.

3. **Tesla wins three-year labor dispute with Swedish union IF Metall:** After nearly three years of conflict, IF Metall has announced it is ending all strike actions against Tesla in Sweden, stating there are no remaining strikers. This closes out a prolonged labor/PR overhang in the European market.

4. **Energy — Powerwall leasing option launches in Texas:** Tesla introduced a Powerwall lease option bundled with the Tesla Electric plan in Texas, offering whole-home backup for as low as $35/month when leasing two Powerwall units, with a one-time $100 order fee and no installation cost (conditional on Tesla Electric enrollment). This is a new financing/adoption mechanism aimed at lowering the barrier to home battery storage.

5. **Infrastructure — large new San Francisco Supercharger station:** Tesla is building a new 124-stall Supercharger station in San Francisco, described as one of the largest charging stations in the world, with standard parking stalls only (no pull-through/trailer stalls).

6. **Competitive EV landscape — Lucid and Rivian new model unveils:** Lucid unveiled the Gravity GT-S, priced from $126,000, with 373 miles range, 1,070 hp, 0-60 mph in 3.1s, seating for 7, and a "Mojave PurLuxe Premium" interior package. Rivian unveiled the 2027 model year R1S and R1T with new features including optional second-row captain's chairs (heated, fold-flat) for the R1S. These are relevant as competitive context for Tesla's EV market share but are not Tesla-specific developments.
