Financial analyst Cern Basher joins host Herbert to argue that Tesla's stock chart can support wildly different narratives depending on the arbitrary start/end dates chosen, using this to caution against reading too much into short-term price patterns. The conversation then shifts to concrete Robotaxi growth math from Tesla's paid autonomous mile data, a comparison of Tesla's market cap trajectory versus SpaceX's, and a speculative case for a Tesla–Boring Company tie-up.

## Market & Competition
1. **Same Tesla chart, different narratives depending on window:** Basher showed that overlaying Tesla's YTD performance with 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021, and 2020 produces contradictory conclusions — three consecutive years (2024–2026) appear to support a "sell in January, buy in July" seasonal pattern, but adding 2023 (up ~100%) or 2020 (up ~8-8.5x) completely breaks that narrative, illustrating that apparent patterns are artifacts of the chosen time frame rather than reliable signals.
2. **Multi-year "flat" periods are common and not unique to Tesla:** Basher noted Tesla has traded roughly flat over various four-to-five-year lookback windows depending on the exact dates chosen, and drew a parallel to Amazon and Microsoft, each of which had roughly 14-year periods without hitting a new stock high before going on to massive gains, arguing a multi-year flat stretch is not unusual for transformative tech companies.
3. **Most stock gains occur after-hours, on very few days:** Basher cited research (including on Micron) showing the vast majority of stock returns accrue overnight/after-hours rather than during the trading day, and noted that across Tesla's public history, nearly all of its cumulative percentage gains came from a small handful of specific days — meaning traders who are out of the stock on those days, or who don't hold overnight, miss most of the return.
4. **Step-changes in valuation, not gradual moves, drive re-ratings:** Basher framed Tesla's (and Nvidia's, Micron's) historical stock jumps as resulting from discrete "step changes" — turning cash-flow positive, launching a dominant product (Model Y), or unlocking a fundamentally more profitable business model (robotaxi, Optimus, or Nvidia's AI data center pivot from gaming) — rather than smooth appreciation.

## Robotaxi
1. **Paid autonomous miles scenario modeling from a single data point:** Ashok Elluswamy disclosed 380,000 paid autonomous miles as of the Q2 earnings call (around July 22) and guided to "double digit" weekly growth without specifying the rate. Basher modeled three scenarios (10%, 14%, 17% weekly growth) from that base: at 10% growth, miles would reach roughly 633,000 currently and about 1.54 million by the next earnings call (~October 22) and roughly 3.7 million by year-end; at 14%, roughly 743,000 now, ~2.44–2.45 million by the next call, and ~8 million by year-end; at 17%, roughly 840,000 now, ~3.5 million by the next call, and ~14 million by year-end. Basher stressed these are illustrative scenarios, not confirmed figures, since only one prior data point exists.
2. **Next earnings call is the key data point to watch:** Basher said a second paid-autonomous-miles disclosure at the Q3 call (~October 22) would let analysts back out the actual weekly growth rate, which he considers highly significant regardless of near-term revenue, since revenue itself is described as a "lagging indicator" in this buildout phase.
3. **Cybercab registration and fleet ramp still nascent:** Texas had roughly 270 registered Robotaxi vehicles (all Model Ys, no Cybercabs yet) with about a 40% one-day jump reported recently; Herbert estimated roughly 9-10 Cybercabs will eventually exist for every Model Y in the fleet. Of the roughly 2,000+ Cybercabs believed to exist today, Herbert estimated (attributing the reasoning to a third party) that perhaps 1,000+ will remain supervised (steering-wheel) vehicles used for FSD data collection and new-market mapping rather than entering robotaxi service, tempering earlier expectations of thousands of robotaxi-ready Cybercabs by year-end.
4. **Cybercab utilization advantage over Waymo:** Herbert stated each Cybercab currently generates roughly three times the paid-mile utilization of a Waymo vehicle due to battery size/charging and cleaning downtime differences, and separately claimed Cybercab costs roughly a third (or less) of Waymo's vehicles — meaning Tesla doesn't need fleet parity with Waymo's reported ~3,000 vehicles or its cumulative ~200 million paid autonomous miles to be competitive on a miles-driven basis.
5. **September 3rd robotaxi/Cybercab framing:** The hosts referenced a stated plan for Cybercabs to be part of the robotaxi service around September 3rd, describing the rollout to date as slower and smaller in scale (fewer cities, more Model Ys) than earlier expectations.

## Financials
1. **Per-vehicle robotaxi economics vs. car sales:** Herbert cited a "conservative/realistic" estimate that each robotaxi could generate roughly $30,000 per year (versus roughly $5,000–$7,000 profit per car sold today), calling this a potential 6–10x step-change in per-unit economics; some unnamed estimates go as high as $50,000–$100,000/year per car with full 24-hour utilization, though the speakers flagged these higher figures as more speculative.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX data center revenue could dwarf Tesla's near-term robotaxi revenue:** Basher argued SpaceX doesn't need Starship to succeed for near-term growth, citing a plan to add roughly 8-10 gigawatts of compute capacity next year; if SpaceX is on a roughly $100 billion run rate at 2 gigawatts by year-end, scaling to 5-10 gigawatts of rentable compute could translate to roughly $300-500 billion of revenue in a single year — a figure Basher called comparable to Nvidia's current ~$400 billion annual revenue.
2. **Nvidia–SpaceX chip partnership deepening:** Herbert noted a partnership announcement between SpaceX and Nvidia roughly two weeks prior, followed by Nvidia announcing its next-generation ("Reuben") chip design roughly a week later, framed as supporting SpaceX's orbital data center ambitions; Basher suggested Nvidia is highly motivated to prioritize SpaceX with its latest chips since SpaceX can deploy and monetize compute unusually fast.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Whether Tesla's market cap could exceed SpaceX's:** Basher believes Tesla's market cap could eventually surpass SpaceX's on the strength of robotaxi and Optimus growth, but cautioned that most people underestimate SpaceX's near-term (pre-Starship) revenue growth potential from its data center/compute business, and that SpaceX could plausibly sustain a larger market cap than Tesla for the foreseeable future given the scale of that opportunity.
2. **Speculative Tesla–Boring Company merger case:** Basher proposed (as speculation, not a prediction) that the Boring Company is a more natural near-term merger fit for Tesla than SpaceX, since tunnel networks could relieve robotaxi-induced surface congestion, let Tesla vertically integrate underground infrastructure, and benefit from network effects as station count grows (e.g., his stated station-pair combinatorics: 10 stations ≈ 90 pairs, 50 stations ≈ 2,450 pairs, 100 stations ≈ 9,900 pairs, 1,000 stations ≈ nearly a million pairs). He noted Boring Company has already partnered with apartment buildings and Las Vegas hotels for tunnel access points.
