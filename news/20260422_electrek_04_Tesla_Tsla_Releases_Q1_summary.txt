Tesla released Q1 2026 financial results after market close on April 22, 2026, posting a slight beat on earnings while meeting revenue expectations, though several underlying metrics were weaker than anticipated.

## Financials
1. **Earnings beat, revenue in line:** Tesla reported non-GAAP EPS of $0.41, beating the Wall Street consensus of $0.37. Revenue came in at $22.38 billion, roughly in line with the $22.3 billion consensus. This represents approximately 14% revenue growth and 33% EPS growth year-over-year versus Q1 2025's $19.335 billion revenue and $0.27 EPS.

2. **Gross margin strongest in recent quarters:** Gross margin jumped to 21.1%, up 478 basis points year-over-year from 16.3% and above Q4 2025's 20.1%. Tesla cited "increase in automotive one-time benefits related to warranty and tariffs" as a key profit driver — suggesting a possible early tariff refund contributed to the result.

3. **FSD subscriber figure questioned:** Tesla reported 1.28 million "FSD subscribers," but the article notes this figure includes all package purchasers, not only active subscribers, complicating the metric — particularly since Tesla recently shifted to a subscription-only model that may have pushed one-time purchases last quarter.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Deliveries missed and inventory built up:** Tesla delivered 358,023 vehicles in Q1 2026, missing expectations by roughly 7,600 units. The company also produced over 50,000 more vehicles than it sold, signaling a significant inventory buildup.

## Energy
1. **Energy storage deployment dropped sharply:** Energy storage deployment fell 38% sequentially to 8.8 GWh, well below the analyst consensus range of 12–14 GWh.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi launch reportedly delayed:** A linked follow-up article referenced in the piece indicates Tesla signaled the robotaxi launch will be pushed back across 5 U.S. cities, though no further detail is provided within this article.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert flags that Tesla "pulled questionable levers" to improve Q1 financials, pointing to one-time warranty and tariff benefits as factors that flatter the headline results rather than reflecting core operational improvement.
