This video recaps a Jay Leno segment featuring Tesla's Lars Moravy (VP Vehicle Engineering) and Franz von Holzhausen (Chief Designer) demonstrating the 2027 Model Y L, a longer-wheelbase, three-row variant of the Model Y, covering its engineering tradeoffs, aerodynamics, safety features, interior packaging, and current supervised FSD capability, with the host (futureaza) adding corrections and context throughout.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y L dimensions and powertrain:** The Model Y L has a wheelbase about 150mm (6 inches) longer and is roughly 7 inches longer overall than the standard Model Y, about 4cm taller, on 20-inch wheels, with dual-motor all-wheel drive producing 496 horsepower. Moravy stated it performs between the standard and performance Model Y trims.
2. **Range, acceleration, and efficiency:** Tesla confirmed via EPA a 332-mile range rating and a 4.4-second 0-60 time. The host notes the battery is only about 7% larger than a standard Model Y's yet yields this range, crediting a drag coefficient of 0.216 — lower than even the base Model Y — achieved through the vehicle's added length and height improving frontal-area aerodynamics, plus a rear spoiler designed to extend the car's effective aerodynamic length and reduce wake turbulence. The host cites a third-party test (Kyle Connor) showing the car achieved over 300 miles of real-world highway range.
3. **Third-row and interior upgrades:** Unlike the standard Model Y's three-row option (described by the host as an afterthought lacking vents or climate control), the Model Y L's third row has heated seats, USB-C ports, UV-rejecting glass, and dedicated climate vents. Moravy noted an upgraded heat pump system with roughly 15% more cooling capacity and a "triple silver" reflective roof layer to manage cabin heat. All three rows have independent climate control, which Moravy says improves per-passenger range efficiency since the car isn't conditioning the whole cabin for a single occupant.
4. **Safety features:** Moravy and the host state the Model Y L has 10 airbags, including coverage extending into the third row (absent on the current seven-seat Model Y), and uses in-cabin radar plus cameras (rather than just seat sensors or bumper intrusion sensors) to detect occupants and determine airbag deployment timing — potentially deploying airbags before an unavoidable collision impact. The host adds that four of the top five safest cars by probability-of-injury ranking are Teslas (unsourced in the segment) and clarifies that Automatic Collision Evasion (ACE) requires purchasing FSD rather than being standard-equipped.
5. **Design and manufacturing details:** Von Holzhausen highlighted a hidden cut line concealed under the rear spoiler trim (avoiding a visible stamped-metal seam, easing manufacturing tolerances and quality control), antennas integrated into the glass rather than roof-mounted, and a center high-mount stop light relocated into the spoiler to gain extra third-row headroom. Interior storage includes seats that fold flat in a choreographed sequence (front seats auto-adjust out of the way) creating roughly 9 feet of cargo space, plus a magnetic flip-up storage panel behind the third row.
6. **Pricing:** The host notes the Model Y L is currently at the end of a "launch series" pricing period at $61,000, with price expected to come down once that period ends.
7. **Comfort tuning options:** Moravy described an adaptive damping mode (standard on performance trims, also available here) that can shift the vehicle's pitch center toward the second row for rear-passenger comfort — framed as more relevant to chauffeured use (noted as more common in China) than typical US buyers.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Supervised FSD demonstrated and current camera-based attention monitoring:** During the drive, the car operated on supervised FSD, correctly yielding to an oncoming ambulance and handling a construction zone; Moravy confirmed a driver-facing camera monitors attention periodically. The host emphasizes the current system still requires a seat-occupied driver and would not operate with an inanimate weight on the seat, since Tesla uses seat sensors, cameras, and in-cabin radar together to distinguish real occupants (including child/adult differentiation) — the host notes people have previously been temporarily suspended from FSD for attempting to defeat attention-monitoring with steering wheel weights.
2. **Robotaxi service referenced as unsupervised:** Moravy stated that the Cybercab and robotaxi service currently operating in Texas and Florida runs unsupervised with nobody driving, and that Tesla plans to eventually extend unsupervised capability to personally owned vehicles.
