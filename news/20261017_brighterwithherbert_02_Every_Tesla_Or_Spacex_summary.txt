This is a weekly Tesla/SpaceX news roundup covering the joint Tesla-SpaceX Roadster unveiling at SpaceX's McGregor test site, fresh Barclays commentary on a possible Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement, Cybercab registration growth and NHTSA safety data, an Optimus V3 supplier claim, Tesla EV sales and energy news, and a cluster of SpaceX items (upcoming Q3 earnings date, Nvidia/Jensen Huang comments, Starshield in Ukraine, Starship regulatory filings and Starlink adoption stats).

## Electric Vehicles / Robotaxi
1. **Roadster unveiled with flying capability at SpaceX site:** Tesla showed the new Roadster at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas test site, demonstrating it driving up an inverted ramp held by suction fans, flipping, firing cold-gas thrusters, and flying over a row of Tesla vehicles before landing — all unmanned, with Starman in the seat. Tesla detailed three trims: Performance (2-seat, sub-1.6s 0-60), Thrust (2-seat, cold-gas jets, sub-0.9s 0-60), and Rocket (1-seat, perimeter thrusters, sub-0.59s 0-60, 230+ mph top speed, 300+ mile range for all versions); the host says he heard only 1,000 Rocket units will be made. Live 0-60 testing for the Rocket produced readings of 0.81 seconds (later revised by Tesla to 0.67 seconds accounting for rollout); Elon Musk said he wants to improve this toward Mach 0.5. Lars Moravy said a full tank of the ~600-liter, 10,000 PSI cold-gas system gives about 5 seconds of acceleration, with current refueling taking 2–6 hours (target: 30 minutes); Tesla confirmed the rear wing actuator and track-mode systems share components with Optimus actuators. Deposits: $50,000 for Performance, $250,000 for Thrust; Rocket is invitation-only and reportedly not street-legal. Production target stated by Elon Musk is 2028, with four to five prototypes already built and larger-scale production planned for early next year.
2. **Cybercab fleet and NHTSA safety data:** Texas Cybercab registrations rose from 45 (September 1) to 349 (per Sawyer Merritt), plus 420 Model Y robotaxis, totaling 769 Tesla robotaxis registered in the state. NHTSA's latest incident report (through September 15) covers the first two weeks of paid Cybercab trips and recorded zero accidents or injuries involving Cybercab robotaxis in that window, alongside one logged incident of a Cybercab driving into a water-filled pothole at low speed with a safety supervisor aboard; a separate analysis of nine total robotaxi incidents found all were property-damage-only, with at-fault contacts occurring at 5 mph or less. NHTSA also opened an October 9 defect petition (not yet a full investigation) covering roughly 1,000 Model Y and Cybercab robotaxis over alleged school-zone speeding, based on three social-media videos and four incidents with no accidents or injuries; Tesla must respond to NHTSA by October 30 on Cybercab safety standards.
3. **Robotaxi expansion and service improvements:** Tesla is hiring "SI vehicle operator and security" roles in 34 U.S. cities across 19 states plus D.C., and robotaxi market leader roles in Austin, Dallas, Las Vegas, Miami, Phoenix, and Palo Alto — with no launch dates confirmed. Two Cybercabs were photographed at a Dallas-area Supercharger; Model Y robotaxis already offer driverless rides in Dallas, while paid Cybercab rides remain Austin-only. Austin service hours expanded to 6am–11pm with shorter wait times, automatic trunk opening, and an in-car Grok bot. Per a Texas-wide tracker cited by the host, Waymo had about 1,185 vehicles versus Tesla's 739 at the time, though Tesla added roughly 300 vehicles in the trailing 30 days versus Waymo's roughly 200.
4. **US and China EV sales:** Cox Automotive estimates Tesla sold about 99,000 Model Ys in the US in Q3, 37% of the entire EV market. In China, the Model Y was the best-selling car of any type in September (near 50,000 units), lifting Tesla's China market share to about 6% (fourth place). Canada's import quota for the Model 3 premium trim is exhausted, pausing orders until new quotas expected in March. Jefferies raised its Tesla price target from $350 to $400; RBC maintained $480.
5. **FSD rollout and supplier/factory updates:** Tesla began deploying a lightweight FSD Supervised version for older Intel-based Model S/X, and Slovakia became the ninth European country to approve FSD Supervised (branded "Tesla assisted driving" in Europe). A Tesla supplier's IPO filing claimed the Optimus V3 hardware design is essentially complete and slated for mass production by the end of 2026 (supplier's claim, not confirmed by Tesla); Tesla separately posted new job listings for an Optimus assembly-line engineer in Austin and an Optimus fleet coordinator. Giga Berlin's planned new building spans about 536,000 sq m, more than double the existing plant, with construction starting in 2027.

## Energy
1. **Megapack order and energy exec departure:** Taiwan's Apollo Power is purchasing 260 Megapacks (about 1 GWh). The Wall Street Journal reported Tesla's chief energy officer Mike Snyder is stepping down as the company expands its battery business and shifts focus toward AI and robotics; the host notes no confirmed reason was given.

## Market & Competition
1. **Global EV sales threshold:** A Polymarket data point cited by the host shows gasoline-only cars fell below 50% of global new car sales for the first time; Elon Musk called it an "important threshold."

## Financials / SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation builds ahead of shareholder meeting:** Barclays analyst Dan Levy suggested a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement is possible by December, tied to Tesla's annual shareholder meeting due by December 6, with a shareholder vote expected within 6–12 months of announcement; Barclays' base case is an all-stock deal where Tesla acquires SpaceX with Elon Musk retaining voting control. Kalshi reportedly priced the probability of a merger within the next year at 60% (a new high, per the host); JPMorgan said the deal became "more real" after SpaceX's public listing with regulatory (not financial) hurdles as the main obstacle; Dan Ives put the probability above 80%; Oppenheimer argued the companies are better off remaining separate. Elon Musk has not commented on the merger on X this week.
2. **Upcoming back-to-back earnings:** SpaceX will report Q3 results Tuesday, October 20 at 3:30pm Central via audio webcast — the day before Tesla's Q3 earnings call on Wednesday at 2:30pm Pacific / 4:30pm Central. The host speculates (without confirmation) that the scheduling proximity could be more than coincidence.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Nvidia partnership and compute targets:** Jensen Huang said on a podcast that Nvidia and SpaceX engineers are closely collaborating on new server/data-center architecture for space, and that a jointly developed Starlink computer will enter mass production next year. Elon Musk, reacting to a post about wanting 10 gigawatts of compute by the end of 2027, said the figure could be "more than 10 gigawatts," an increase from his prior earnings-call framing of 5–10 gigawatts "closer to 10 than 5."
2. **Terafab chip factory dispute:** TSMC CEO reportedly said TSMC is in talks with Terafab but that any factory should be run by TSMC — which the host says contradicts Elon Musk's October 7 statement (made directly to the host) that Tesla would build and operate the factory itself.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starshield disruption in Ukraine:** A Ukrainian military unit reported Starshield terminals shutting off above roughly 160 km/h, disrupting drone operations; Elon Musk responded to a former Ukrainian defense minister that Starshield is owned and controlled by the US government, with speed/usage restrictions set by the US military and ultimately the president — not by him. Regular Starlink service was unaffected and normal Starshield operation reportedly resumed by Thursday.
2. **Starlink adoption milestones:** Jefferies projects Starlink could be installed on nearly a quarter of the world's aircraft by 2028, up from about 9% today. United said about 5.4 million passengers used Starlink on flights in September; Southwest is installing it fleet-wide. In Japan, 10 million people have connected to Starlink directly via phone through Docomo. Time named Starlink V5 one of its best inventions of 2026.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **FAA review for expanded Starbase launch cadence:** The FAA opened an environmental review for Starbase covering an increase from 25 launches per year currently to 100 in a first phase and eventually up to 456 launches per year; public comment runs through November 30. Ship 40 returned to Starbase after 75 days at sea for heat-shield inspection. Elon Musk said at a company event that Flight 15 would occur "a little before Thanksgiving" (his own estimate) and that SpaceX will attempt its first tower catch of the ship on that flight.
