## Financials
1. **Unexplained ~7% stock drop post-earnings:** Tesla dropped sharply despite reporting its best-ever Q2 vehicle deliveries (roughly 481,000 vs. ~460,000 expected), which the host called a "total déjà vu" of a similar unexplained crash the prior week. He argues no credible fundamental explanation exists (dismissing takes from other analysts as insufficient) and believes it was a tactical, possibly coordinated "rug pull" or short-term market cornering move rather than a reaction to news.
2. **Host's near-term price view:** The host believes Tesla is now oversold, citing that the stock is still up roughly 6% over the prior week despite the crash, and floats $410 as a "fair" level with potential to reach $490, while cautioning that near-term moves remain unpredictable and "something weird is going on."
3. **SpaceX short position and merger skepticism:** The host disclosed a short options position (short calls) against SpaceX, betting it will struggle to reach $180, citing large amounts of stock float coming to market by November as a source of selling pressure. He is skeptical that a Tesla-SpaceX merger would happen as early as suggested by some (referencing an analyst named Alexandra), instead expecting it more likely next year.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi launches unsupervised service in Miami:** Tesla officially launched unsupervised Robotaxi rides (no safety driver) in a new market — Miami, including Miami Beach and areas around the airport — expanding beyond existing cities. The host called this modestly positive news, not a major catalyst on its own.
2. **Texas DMV registrations climb; Cybercab enters production without controls:** A commenter cited Texas DMV data showing unsupervised vehicle registrations now over 100. Separately, the host noted Tesla has begun production of Cybercabs without steering wheels or pedals, which he characterizes as a meaningful signal of the vehicle moving toward real scaling rather than pure engineering testing.
3. **Anticipated Tuesday reveal in Austin tied to "scaling":** The host referenced comments from Tesla VP Lars Moravy (in an interview with Jeff Lutz on the Herbert channel) indicating some form of reveal or announcement in Austin, Texas on the following Tuesday related to "scaling," though details are unknown; the host does not expect major news from it.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Potential FSD approval in Germany within weeks:** Citing a response from a German parliamentary state secretary (Christian Hutton) regarding easier approval rules and pilot regions for autonomous transport, the host says Full Self-Driving could see a surprise approval in Germany "this month" — a development he did not expect and considers a potential stock-moving catalyst given Germany's large but underpenetrated car market.
2. **Swedish authorities recommend EU block FSD rollout:** A commenter noted Swedish authorities have recommended the EU block FSD rollout over concerns it can be set to speed; the host acknowledged hearing this and used it as a jumping-off point for broader (largely non-Tesla) commentary on Swedish politics.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Host argues Optimus faces a lower bar than FSD:** Unlike FSD, which the host describes as requiring near-perfection for a singular driving objective, he argues Optimus can be commercially deployed profitably long before reaching perfection, since industrial use cases (warehousing, agriculture, mining, hospitals, grocery stocking) tolerate imperfection and carry far lower safety risk than autonomous driving.
2. **Optimus scale timeline expectation:** The host's personal expectation is that Optimus could be sold in the range of thousands next year, then scaling to hundreds of thousands and eventually a million units in subsequent years, even if the product isn't "perfect" until around 2030 — driven by business demand for early pilots. He separately said he expects Optimus to become financially material in 2027.
