This "Future Aza" roundup ("smart news" edition) covers a batch of positive Tesla and broader EV industry developments: a Tesla Semi fleet order, a Model 3 performance refresh, European sales recovery, German registration records, and discount/incentive news, framed by the hosts as a wave of good news for the EV transition.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi lands 50-truck order from IMC Logistics:** Tesla signed a deal to supply 50 Semi trucks to logistics firm IMC Logistics, announced Monday morning. The host assumes the margin on these trucks today is close to zero since the plant just started production (heavy depreciation and overhead costs with no ramped-up pace yet), but expects Tesla to eventually reach its typical ~20% margin per truck, which at an assumed $50,000 margin per unit would mean roughly $2.5 million in margin on this order alone. The host also expects additional revenue from custom truck upgrades (e.g., sleeper cabs), noting Munro's Kevin asked a Tesla executive (referred to as Dan Priestley in the transcript) why there's no sleeper-cab option; the response was that there aren't yet enough charging stations for serious intercity trucking, but a sleeper design has already been engineered and could be added later. The host cites his own research that 54% of the target market uses day cabs, meaning Tesla's current day-cab-focused lineup could already address over half the addressable market.
2. **Model 3 gets performance and feature refresh:** For its anniversary, Tesla increased Model 3 horsepower from 510 to 528 (the host compares this to a 1999 Dodge Viper's 500 hp/490 lb-ft), increased range from 309 to 316 miles, and added a larger 16-inch center screen (up from 15.4 inches, matching the Model Y) along with a black headliner option — all at no extra charge, which the host finds notable.
3. **Tesla breaks 13-month European sales slump:** Tesla sold 13,740 units in the EU, UK, and EFTA last month, up 29.1% year-over-year, ending a prolonged decline the hosts attribute partly to the Model Y update cycle. Separately, Taiwan reportedly sold around 4,800 Tesla units in a month (host notes this is from memory and uncertain). The broader European car market grew registrations just 1.7%, while EVs (190,000 units) and plug-in hybrids (96,000 units, up roughly a third) outperformed; hybrids remain the best-selling powertrain type overall in the region.
4. **German Tesla registrations more than triple in September:** Tesla registrations in Germany more than tripled year-over-year in September, the best monthly figure since December 2022 (when the Giga Berlin plant was newly ramping capacity and Tesla pricing became more competitive in Germany).
5. **Discounts and incentives active in multiple markets:** Tesla is offering a discount of almost $3,000 on in-stock Model Y units in the US. In Australia, a $3,000 discount plus a $2,000 trade-in bonus (in Australian dollars) can combine for up to roughly $5,000 off a new Model Y. The host also mentions state-level Semi incentives of $175,000 in Washington and $120,000 in Oregon.
