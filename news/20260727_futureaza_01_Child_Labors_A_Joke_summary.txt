This Monday news roundup from futureaza covers a fast autonomous cross-country "Cannonball" run, upcoming FSD personalization and semi-truck rollout, the Uber–Waymo exclusivity change, a novelty Tesla kids' balance bike, Cybertruck's sales struggles, GM and BYD competitive moves, and a sizable segment on SpaceX's post-IPO stock decline and merger skepticism, plus lighter EV/energy industry items.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Record-setting driverless Cannonball run:** David Moss's team completed a fully autonomous, zero-disengagement cross-country Cannonball run of roughly 2,800 miles in 47 hours, 15 minutes, 39 seconds, beating the prior record by about 2 hours 40 minutes.
2. **FSD to gain personalization and memory:** Tesla has confirmed FSD is moving from a one-size-fits-all model to a personalized one that can remember driver habits and specific interventions (e.g., preferred parking/charging spots), rather than behaving identically across all cars.
3. **FSD confirmed coming to Semi:** Tesla confirmed during its Q2 earnings call that FSD is expanding to the Class 8 Semi truck, following visible testing equipment (including lidar/ground-truthing gear) on refreshed Semi units unveiled the prior fall with an "autonomy ready" designation; the host cites this as relevant given past fatal Semi driver-fatigue crashes.
4. **FSD 14 Light restarted for Hardware 3:** Tesla restarted rollout of FSD 14 Light for Hardware 3 vehicles with new features including optional brake confirmation, destination parking changes, UI updates, FSD "streaks," and mobile app improvements; the host, who tested it, calls it not yet good but expected to improve, and notes it's a distilled version of the same build underlying Cybercab software.

## Robotaxi
1. **Uber ends exclusivity with Waymo in two markets:** Uber and Waymo (Alphabet) are ending their multi-year exclusivity arrangement in Atlanta and Austin, which the host frames as reflecting Waymo's growing robotaxi traction and Uber's structural weakness (it owns neither the vehicles nor loyal drivers/riders).

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla launches a kids' balance bike:** Tesla released a non-motorized balance bike for children aged 2–5 with a magnesium frame, adjustable seat, and a weight limit, priced at the higher end of the category and restricted to existing Tesla owners; one was spotted resold on eBay for a premium.
2. **Cybertruck sales narrative persists:** A Bloomberg piece (relayed by the host) argues Cybertruck could be among the biggest sales flops in automotive history, comparing it to the Ford Edsel (which sold 200,000 units over two years, a bar Cybertruck hasn't cleared); the host is skeptical of the "biggest flop" framing but agrees sales have been weak, and reiterates his view that a Cybertruck-based SUV could help by increasing production line utilization and lowering costs.
3. **Tesla rises to #9 in Consumer Reports rankings:** Tesla broke into the top 10 of a Consumer Reports reliability ranking (per an older article the host is covering late), which he attributes to Model S/X exiting the lineup and improving Cybertruck/Model Y quality, calling it Tesla's best ranking in a long time.
4. **Ford's smaller EV pickup tested at Tesla Superchargers:** Ford's upcoming smaller EV pickup, featuring a native NACS port and LFP battery, was spotted charging at a Tesla Supercharger; the host is skeptical Ford will actually sell the truck at its advertised low starting price point, drawing a parallel to the F-150 Lightning's early low-volume base trim.
5. **BYD ramps recruitment on demand recovery:** BYD is hiring heavily as international demand surges; the host notes BYD's earlier supplier-payment accounting issues were unwound roughly a year prior, sales stopped declining in May (after eight consecutive months of year-over-year declines), and grew about 5% year-over-year in June.
6. **BYD unveils new supercar:** BYD's Denza brand showed a new supercar (nearly 2,000 horsepower) lapping the Nürburgring at the Goodwood Festival of Speed, part of BYD's two flagship performance models alongside its existing Roadster-rival car.

## Market & Competition
1. **GM hires new marketing chief from Nissan:** GM hired a marketing executive from Nissan to lead GMC and Buick marketing; the host is skeptical, citing Nissan's declining global sales figures during and after her tenure there as evidence against the move.
2. **GM lands ~$1B Army vehicle deal:** GM is pursuing a roughly $1 billion Army contract for about 10,000 Colorado-based tactical vehicles, pending Congressional approval, projected to generate about $700 million in revenue this year with rising profit margins in subsequent years; the host notes GM beat Oshkosh and Polaris as the only other bidders, and pushes back on framing such contracts as "subsidies."
3. **OpenAI reportedly missing sales targets:** The host relays reporting that OpenAI is missing its sales goals by a wide margin, with its own forecasts reportedly requiring advertising to reach about 36% of total revenue by 2030; he calls the broader AI investment boom (with AI companies said to have burned over $1.6 trillion so far) a bubble, noting AI-related stocks reportedly made up 37% of the stock market as of the prior week.
4. **Intel plans further data center layoffs:** Intel is reportedly planning a new round of layoffs in its data center group, following prior downsizing; the host expresses hope that Intel's manufacturing partnership with Tesla can help stabilize the company.
5. **Sodium-ion battery storage deployment in California:** ESS Tech signed a preliminary agreement with Juniper Energy to potentially deploy at least 500 megawatt-hours of sodium-ion battery storage, expected within about the next year, which the host frames as healthy diversification of the battery supply chain away from lithium.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX stock down sharply from its post-IPO peak:** SpaceX shares are down roughly 34–35% over the past month and about 49% from their peak, per figures cited in the video; Elon Musk reportedly said this decline is expected as SpaceX prioritizes funding Moon and Mars programs over near-term profitability, and may miss earnings quarters as a result.
2. **Host remains skeptical of a Tesla–SpaceX merger:** The host argues he has not seen a compelling financial rationale for merging Tesla and SpaceX, noting Tesla's shareholder base is now largely institutional and may resist a merger given a combined entity would leave Elon Musk with such control that shareholder votes would become merely advisory; he suggests some investors might prefer to wait years to buy into SpaceX once its heavier spending phase has passed.
