This episode of Futureaza features host Brian and guest Randy Kirk discussing a report on record global EV sales despite a decline in overall auto sales, then broadening into a wide-ranging conversation about oil demand, boom-bust cycles in EV adoption, and the potential shift from EVs to autonomous vehicles (AVs) as the bigger disruptive force. Tesla is referenced mainly as an example within this broader macro discussion rather than as a dedicated topic.

## Market & Competition
1. **Record EV sales globally amid oil-price volatility:** A report cited by the hosts found that while total global auto sales fell 5%, EV sales rose 35% quarter-over-quarter, with second-quarter sales hitting records in 50 countries (Australia, Brazil, India, Korea, Vietnam roughly doubling year-over-year) and more than 90 countries showing year-on-year growth in the first half of the year; China's overall auto sales declined even as EVs held share.
2. **Oil demand impact from China's EV boom:** The hosts cited a claim that China's EV boom is cutting global oil demand by 1.7 million barrels a day, which Brian likened to roughly one Panamax-sized oil tanker's worth of demand removed daily; both speakers agreed oil will remain necessary for petrochemicals, aviation, and off-grid applications (e.g., mining) for the foreseeable future.
3. **US automakers seen as retreating from EVs, ceding ground to AVs:** Randy Kirk argued the real disruption won't be EVs but autonomous vehicles (AVs), and that this shift would primarily benefit Tesla rather than Ford, GM, Stellantis, Rivian, or Lucid — asserting Rivian and Lucid's claims of being roughly a year from full autonomy show they underestimate the data and compute gap versus Tesla. Brian noted the "Big Three" pulled back on EVs after ZEV credits and CAFE standards were rolled back.
4. **Oil/gasoline price dynamics and consumer amnesia:** Kirk suggested that if the Middle East conflict resolves, an oil glut could push prices back toward roughly $60/barrel and $2.50/gallon gasoline, reducing incentive for consumers to buy EVs — describing a repeating "boom-bust" pattern also seen in the bicycle industry during past gas-price spikes.

## Robotaxi
1. **AV fleet math favors Tesla, per Kirk's speculative estimate:** Kirk speculated that each autonomous vehicle deployed into robotaxi fleets could replace five to seven traditional gas vehicles, and — using rough, self-acknowledged simplified math — suggested Tesla could meet total US demand by selling far fewer vehicles than the ~14–18 million currently sold annually, with a global figure around 90 million scaled down similarly; he characterized these as illustrative, not precise, projections and noted this dynamic would benefit Tesla shareholders but "do nothing to protect" the broader US auto industry.
