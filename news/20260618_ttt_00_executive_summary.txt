## Robotaxi

- **Record 120 Cybercabs spotted at Giga Texas on June 17**, the highest single-day count reported to date per Sawyer Merritt, reinforcing production ramp acceleration. Over 100 units were also visible in the outbound staging lot, with 55 additional units spotted in Dallas. Brian White estimates total Cybercabs produced is in the "high hundreds," likely exceeding 1,000.

- **EPA Certificate of Conformity secured — the critical commercial unlock.** Both FutureAzA and brighterwithherbert identify this filing as the primary regulatory gate that had been preventing paid Cybercab rides; with it cleared, no physical or regulatory barriers to deployment remain. Brian White estimates commercial-scale rollout is roughly one to two months out, contingent only on software readiness.

- **Infrastructure buildout confirmed in multiple markets.** A ~51,000 sq ft combined service, repair, and charging/cleaning facility is under construction in Grand Prairie, Texas (Dallas metro). A permitted equivalent facility has been identified in Las Vegas. These hubs are purpose-built for fleet operations and only need to exist in each active deployment market.

- **Texas DOT Executive Director rode a production Cybercab and issued a formal endorsement**, stating: *"The complete absence of traditional driver controls underscores a significant shift in mobility and vehicle design. No steering wheel, no accelerator, no brake."* A senior state transportation official publicly validating the vehicle design is a meaningful near-term signal for launch readiness.

- **Four consecutive months of NHTSA reporting with zero Tesla robotaxi at-fault incidents.** The most recent report shows one new event: a stationary robotaxi rear-ended by a truck — Tesla not at fault. All recent incidents in the Austin fleet have involved human drivers striking stopped Teslas. Elon Musk affirmed Sawyer Merritt's observation of this pattern.

- **Robert Scoble's "storm the parking lot" thesis:** Scoble argues Tesla should stage roughly 2,000 Cybercabs before simultaneously activating the service, creating an immediate visible scale that functions as its own news cycle. He projects 10,000–20,000 Cybercabs on the road by end of the current ramp year. Herbert's independent model targets 10,000–15,000 active robotaxis across multiple cities by end of 2026, at roughly 1,000 units per city.

- **Owner-vehicle network inclusion increasingly unlikely near-term.** Brian White identifies camera washers and potentially Starlink terminals as hardware retrofits that cannot feasibly be added aftermarket, making personal vehicle inclusion improbable in the first or second deployment rounds — a tension with prior Tesla executive promises.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD v14.3.4 is the current release and represents a material step-change** over prior 14.x builds per both FutureAzA and brighterwithherbert. Brian White's first drives showed correct detection and appropriate slowing for a small animal crossing the road; Herbert reports the brake-stabbing behavior present in v14.3 is resolved. Both hosts describe v14.x broadly as ready to demonstrate to skeptical passengers.

- **Navigation remains FSD's most persistent unresolved weakness.** Brian White reports the car missed his street's turn on v14.3.4; the system is described as stitching four separate navigation sources. Both hosts treat this as a genuine engineering gap not yet addressed.

- **FSD now approved or available in 12 countries and regions:** US, Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium. Japan is conducting road tests in Tokyo and Yokohama. Taiwan has formally submitted FSD Supervised for regulatory review, with a technical committee expected to convene within 2–4 weeks.

- **Taiwan moving to FSD subscription-only starting July 1**, discontinuing the one-time purchase option — interpreted by brighterwithherbert as a signal that FSD launch in Taiwan is imminent. The same shift has already occurred in New Zealand.

- **Dutch RDW formally defended its FSD approval** following a Reuters article alleging Tesla used misleading US safety statistics in its European regulatory submissions. The RDW stated its approval followed an independent 18-month review including 1.6 million km of road tests, 4,500 track tests, and 13,000 customer ride-alongs. Post-approval, Tesla recorded zero highway collisions across 23.6 million km of FSD driving in the Netherlands.

- **New Jersey legislation would effectively prohibit driverless deployment.** Tesla emailed New Jersey owners directly urging them to contact legislators opposing the bill, citing 578 traffic fatalities in the state in 2025 with 94%+ attributed to human error. Brian White argues the bill appears designed to target Tesla's FSD while leaving other ADAS systems unaffected, and suspects transportation lobby involvement.

- **FSD intervention data from Sawyer Merritt's feed:** destination parking is the dominant reason users take over; critical safety interventions are described as "extremely rare" — consistent with the thesis that remaining barriers to unsupervised deployment are operational, not safety-fundamental.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Industry timeline for Optimus achieving 20% of factory tasks: 2–7 years**, per Scoble's conversations with people building humanoid training hardware (egocentric camera rigs, force-sensing gloves). Tesla employees give the more optimistic end of that range — approximately 18 months. Scoble's own view is "a couple of years" before Optimus can reliably walk factory floors, take instructions, and perform varied tasks.

- **SNX line closure at Fremont linked to Optimus co-location strategy.** Scoble argues Elon closed the Model S/X production line specifically to place the Optimus development team adjacent to an active car production line, enabling the rapid deploy-fail-iterate loop he identifies as the key accelerant for commercial readiness.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab EPA specifications confirmed in full:** 3,113 lbs curb weight (lightest EV on US roads, ~700 lbs lighter than Model 3), ~418-mile estimated range (well above the ~300-mile figure cited at reveal), 165 Wh/mile efficiency (best in North America per Brian White), 219 hp single front-mounted permanent magnet motor, front-wheel drive, custom 326V electrical architecture. Gross vehicle weight allows approximately 700 lbs of payload, opening potential delivery use cases.

- **48 kWh battery sized for fleet longevity, not just range.** Operating in a 20–70% state-of-charge window in daily fleet use could extend battery life to 800,000 miles versus ~300,000 miles at full cycling, per Brian White. The oversized range figure gives Tesla headroom to manage degradation across the vehicle's commercial life.

- **Custom 326V architecture is intentional, not off-the-shelf.** Neither the industry-standard 400V nor 800V, this voltage was tuned specifically for the Cybercab's commercial use case — consistent with Tesla's broader vertical integration philosophy. Because Tesla owns the entire fleet, any early compatibility issues can be resolved without public recalls.

## Financials

- **Elon Musk exercised 304 million Tesla stock options from his 2018 compensation package.** Tesla withheld 17.53 million shares at $404.66/share via net share settlement — no open-market sale. The received shares are restricted and do not vest until January 19, 2028, followed by a 5-year lockup, meaning no sell pressure from this block until 2033.

- **FSD subscription revenue trajectory:** With one-time FSD purchase discontinued in Taiwan (effective July 1) and previously in New Zealand, all new international FSD users will be on recurring subscriptions. Brian White references 10 million FSD subscribers as Elon Musk's next compensation plan target, implying a potential ~$1B/month revenue run rate at that scale.

## Market & Competition

- **Waymo efficiency disadvantage estimated at ~3x energy per mile** versus the Cybercab's 165 Wh/mile, per FutureAzA. Herbert's fleet math: Tesla needs roughly 1,000 Cybercabs to match Waymo's current ~3,500-vehicle fleet on service miles delivered — a structural per-mile cost advantage compounded by Tesla's ownership of Supercharger infrastructure.

- **Fewer than 10 million people globally have ever ridden in an autonomous vehicle**, per Scoble's estimate aggregating Tesla, Waymo, Zoox, Nuro, and Chinese AV operators. Against a global population of 8 billion, this frames the market as genuinely nascent.

- **Chinese competitors identified as the primary long-term structural threat**, not European or Japanese OEMs. Scoble names Xpeng, Huawei, Xiaomi, DJI, and Unitree as building original brands — not copies — with manufacturing iteration speeds Tesla has consciously tried to replicate through production-adjacent engineering.
