## Robotaxi

- **EPA Certificate of Conformity clears the final regulatory hurdle for commerce.** Per Brian White (FutureAzA), this certificate is a legal prerequisite before any Cybercab can generate commercial revenue — not a data filing. With it approved, no physical or regulatory barriers remain; software readiness is now the sole gating factor. Brian estimates commercial-scale rollout is one to two months away as Tesla continues accumulating FSD training data on the new platform.

- **Record 120 Cybercabs spotted at Giga Texas on June 17.** Additional units: 55 confirmed in Dallas, with further sightings at robotaxi hubs across Texas and one unit spotted at an event in Austria. Brian White estimates total production is in the "high hundreds," likely exceeding 1,000. The outbound lot — the same staging area used for finished consumer vehicles before truck transport — is confirmed as the accumulation point, per Joe Tegtmeyer's reporting.

- **Infrastructure buildout accelerating in parallel with production.** A ~51,000 sq ft combined service, repair, and charging/cleaning facility is under construction in Grand Prairie, Texas (Dallas metro). A similarly permitted facility has been identified in Las Vegas. Brian White frames these hubs as proof of fleet-owned deployment logic: they only need to exist in markets Tesla intends to serve.

- **Autonomous Cybercab filmed backing into parking space without a steering wheel.** Joe Tegtmeyer captured footage of a no-steering-wheel Cybercab arriving and reversing smoothly at Giga Texas. Other units on the lot retain removable steering wheels. Brian White notes the absence of wheel covers on many units reflects standard Tesla shipping procedure.

- **Texas DOT executive director rides production Cybercab, issues enthusiastic statement.** The senior state transportation official stated: *"The complete absence of traditional driver controls underscores a significant shift in mobility and vehicle design. No steering wheel, no accelerator, no brake."* A senior regulatory stakeholder publicly validating the vehicle design is a meaningful near-term signal.

- **Four consecutive months of zero at-fault robotaxi incidents per NHTSA data.** The most recent report shows one new event: a stationary Tesla robotaxi rear-ended by a truck — Tesla not at fault, consistent with the two prior incidents in March. Sawyer Merritt noted the irony that every robotaxi crash in recent months was caused by a human driver in another vehicle; Elon Musk affirmed the observation.

- **Herbert's end-of-2026 fleet forecast: 10,000–15,000 active robotaxis.** His model assumes roughly 1,000 units per city with a ~90/10 Cybercab-to-Model Y split, reflecting his estimate that ~90% of trips involve two or fewer passengers. Production rate is estimated at 50–100 units per day, though external verification is difficult.

- **Owner-vehicle robotaxi inclusion increasingly unlikely near-term.** Brian White identifies camera washers and potentially Starlink terminals as hardware retrofits that cannot be added aftermarket, making personal vehicle inclusion in the first or second deployment wave deeply unlikely. He acknowledges this creates legal exposure given prior executive commitments.

- **Robert Scoble's "storm the parking lot" deployment thesis.** Scoble argues Tesla should wait until roughly 2,000 Cybercabs are staged, then activate the service simultaneously for maximum visibility and news impact, rather than deploying piecemeal. He notes the FSD software may require one or two more updates before legal sign-off.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD v14.3.4 described as a larger step than a typical point release.** Both Brian White and Herbert have driven it and describe it as meaningfully improved over v14.3. Specific fixes include resolution of minor brake-stabbing behavior when exiting driveways. Brian's early drives showed correct detection of and response to a small animal crossing the road.

- **FSD now approved or available in 12 countries and regions.** Markets confirmed: US, Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium. Japan is conducting road tests in Tokyo and Yokohama. Brian White notes that as European and Australian approvals accumulate, other Asian regulators are likely to defer rather than conduct independent full reviews.

- **Taiwan regulatory submission filed; one-time FSD purchase discontinued there effective July 1.** Tesla submitted FSD Supervised to Taiwan's Vehicle Safety Inspection Center. The technical committee is expected to convene within 2–4 weeks; approval then moves to the Ministry of Transport. Brian White interprets the simultaneous shift to subscription-only as a signal FSD launch is imminent in that market.

- **Dutch RDW publicly defends FSD approval against Reuters criticism.** The authority issued a formal statement confirming its approval was based on an independent 18-month review covering 1.6 million km of road tests, 4,500 track tests, and 13,000 customer ride-alongs. Post-approval data: zero highway collisions across 23.6 million km of FSD driving in the Netherlands.

- **Reuters' safety methodology criticism disputed.** Reuters alleged Tesla used misleading US crash statistics in European regulatory submissions — specifically that Tesla counts only serious crashes for its own vehicles and compares to all US crashes. Brian White argues the article was technically defensible but intentionally incomplete, omitting the context that no other manufacturer provides equivalent telematics data for a fair comparison.

- **New Jersey legislation would effectively prohibit driverless deployment.** Tesla emailed New Jersey owners directly urging them to contact legislators opposing a bill moving through Trenton that would impose restrictions sufficient to block true driverless service. The email cited 578 New Jersey traffic deaths in 2025, with 94%+ attributed to human error. Brian White and Herbert believe taxi or transportation lobbying interests are driving the legislation; they note the bill appears to target Tesla's FSD while leaving Blue Cruise, Super Cruise, and ProPilot unaffected.

- **FSD intervention data: destination parking is the leading friction point; critical safety events rare.** Community FSD feedback data indicates destination parking is by far the most common reason for user intervention, while critical safety interventions are described as "extremely rare." This is consistent with Tesla's thesis that remaining barriers to unsupervised deployment are operational, not safety-fundamental.

- **Navigation remains a persistent unresolved weakness.** Brian White reports the car missed the turn for his street on a v14.3.4 drive. Both he and Herbert describe FSD's navigation as stitching four different data sources — a genuine and unresolved problem.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Industry insiders put Optimus general capability at 2–7 years; Tesla employees say ~18 months.** Robert Scoble reports that people building humanoid training hardware (sensor gloves, egocentric camera rigs, force-sensing devices) estimate 2–7 years before a humanoid can perform 20% of Tesla factory tasks. Those working at Tesla give approximately 18 months. Scoble's own view is "a couple of years" before Optimus can safely walk, listen to instructions, and perform varied factory work.

- **SNX line closure at Fremont linked to Optimus co-location thesis.** Scoble argues the closure of the Model S/X line was a deliberate move to place the Optimus development team physically adjacent to an active production line — enabling the same rapid deploy-fail-iterate loop that accelerates hardware development in Chinese manufacturing. He frames this as Tesla engineering the feedback cycle the program needs.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybercab EPA specs confirmed in detail.** Curb weight: 3,113 lbs (lightest EV on US roads; ~700 lbs lighter than the lightest current Model 3). Estimated range: ~400–418 miles (well above the ~300-mile figure from initial reveal). Efficiency: 165 Wh/mile (best in North America per EPA data). Motor: 219 hp, single front-mounted permanent magnet. Drivetrain: front-wheel drive. Electrical architecture: custom 326V (not standard 400V or 800V). Gross vehicle weight allows ~700 lbs of payload.

- **48 kWh battery pack sized for fleet longevity, not just range.** Operating in a narrow state-of-charge window (e.g., 20–70%) could extend battery life to ~800,000 miles versus ~300,000 miles at full cycling. The 400+ mile rated range gives Tesla headroom to maintain that window throughout the vehicle's commercial life.

- **3x energy efficiency advantage over Waymo quantified.** Herbert estimates Waymo vehicles consume roughly three times more energy per mile than the Cybercab's 165 Wh/mile, partly due to sensor compute stack power draw. Combined with Tesla's ownership of the Supercharger network, Brian White and Herbert argue Tesla's per-mile operating cost will be structurally lower than Waymo's at scale.

## Financials

- **Elon Musk exercises 304 million Tesla stock options from 2018 compensation package.** Tesla withheld 17.53 million shares at $404.66/share average as net share settlement — no open-market sale occurred. Received shares are restricted and do not vest until January 19, 2028, after which a 5-year lockup begins. Shares cannot be sold until 2033. No near-term supply overhang or sell pressure from this transaction.

- **FSD subscription revenue runway cited at 10 million subscriber target.** Brian White references Elon Musk's next compensation plan milestone of 10 million FSD subscribers, estimating revenue potential at roughly $100/month per subscriber across that base. The shift to subscription-only (one-time purchase discontinued in Taiwan and New Zealand effective July 1) means all new international FSD users enter as recurring revenue.

## Market & Competition

- **Waymo fleet of ~3,500 vehicles cited; Herbert estimates Tesla needs only ~1,000 Cybercabs for equivalent service coverage** due to the Cybercab's 3x energy efficiency and 400+ mile range advantage.

- **Global AV ridership still in its infancy.** Scoble estimates that aggregating all customers of Tesla, Waymo, Zoox, Nuro, BYD, and Chinese AV operators, fewer than 10 million out of 8 billion people have ever ridden in an autonomous vehicle — framing the current moment as the very start of adoption, not approaching saturation.

- **Chinese brands identified as the primary long-term competitive threat.** Scoble specifically names DJI, Insta360, Xiaomi, Huawei, Xpeng, and Unitree as original-brand builders — not copycats — with a structural manufacturing iteration speed advantage. He notes Xpeng's investor presentation cited Tesla as the only American competitor worth naming.

- **2025 global energy capacity data: 814 GW renewables vs. 158 GW fossil fuel additions.** Renewables overtook coal globally in new capacity additions in 2025, providing macro tailwind for Tesla Energy's Megapack and solar businesses.
