## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Merger timing debated: for a vote at this year's Tesla shareholder meeting, action would be needed before October 15 (that meeting must also reconfirm directors Musk, Denholm, Straubel); M&A contacts say a merger proxy is likely too large to fold in, suggesting a separate meeting months later. One panelist (Alexandra Merz, per brighterwithherbert) pegs odds as "imminent" but unconfirmed.
- Panelists read Elon's comments on the All-In podcast about Tesla/SpaceX overlap as directional confirmation of a coming merger, though he did not explicitly confirm one.
- Terafab's ownership structure is unresolved: the entity is Texas-registered and appears SpaceX-owned, while Tesla has separately trademarked "Terafab" and funded a ~$3B pilot R&D site at Giga Texas; a panelist argues a merger would resolve the ambiguity.
- SPCX has traded in roughly a 5% range over the past 30 days, read by one panelist as stabilizing ahead of merger catalysts.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship flight 14 now targets Monday, September 28 at 8:15 AM ET, and per Sawyer Merritt would be the first Starship flight to reach orbit and the first to carry a paying payload.
- SpaceX recovered the Starship 13 booster from the ocean, giving engineers what the brighterwithherbert panel says is a roughly three-month head start on heat-shield tile fixes.
- Elon Musk said he is "highly confident" SpaceX will launch Nvidia-based AI compute hardware into space next year, per the panel.
- Starship 14 is expected to carry an initial batch of Starlink V3 satellites, adding roughly 3% network capacity; a future full-capacity launch of 60 V3 satellites could add ~10% per launch, per the panel. Cathie Wood's projection that each launch could generate ~$1B in revenue, scaling to ~$10 trillion/year by 2030 at ~10,000 flights/year, drew an Elon response of "not impossible."
- A third-party estimate cited by the panel pegs on-site fuel production near the launchpad as cutting logistics costs from $1.8–2.7 million to roughly $200,000 per launch, a ~90% reduction.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX CFO Brett Johnson reportedly confirmed a new AI compute/hosting deal worth $1.1 billion/month (~$13.3B annualized) starting December 1, with SpaceX still tracking toward ~$100 billion in annualized recurring revenue by December, per the brighterwithherbert panel.
- A separate commentary (farzad-fm) argues full Starship reusability drives cost-per-kilogram-to-orbit down ~100x, creating a flywheel where satellite compute demand raises launch cadence and lowers costs further, with a space-optimized chipmaker referred to as "Terra" supplying chips for the loop.
- Jo Bhakdi's video presents Terafab as his own theory of a shared "civilizational" mega-site where Nvidia, AMD, Micron, SK Hynix, TSMC, Intel, and Samsung could co-locate, with chip output ultimately feeding SpaceX-launched AI satellites — explicitly framed by the host as speculation, not confirmed plan.
- In a separate clip, Bhakdi argues SpaceX's real risk isn't technical execution but whether AI compute demand materializes, hinging on AI actually delivering on practical tasks like tax automation and finance workflows.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- FCC has granted SpaceX international telecommunications authority supporting Starlink Mobile, allowing U.S.–international direct-to-cell service — another regulatory step in the rollout, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Robotaxi
- Unsupervised paid miles are growing roughly 17% week-over-week, implying about 1.4 million cumulative unsupervised miles, with the unsupervised fleet reportedly up ~130% from August to September, per the brighterwithherbert panel.
- Reports of robotaxi "looping" or edge-case glitches, common in August, have become rare; NHTSA data reportedly shows only low-speed (1-2 mph) contact incidents rather than real collisions.
- The active Cybercab fleet remains around 49 vehicles, which panelists say may be insufficient for the roughly 2,000 attendees expected at Tesla's October 10 fan event.
- A farzad-fm analysis models a mature Cybercab system at under 40 cents/mile operating cost, implying a 15-mile trip (10 useful + 5 empty repositioning) costs about $6 — roughly a fifth of a comparable Uber fare in the host's example — with Tesla targeting sub-$20,000 manufacturing cost per vehicle via its "unboxed" process and eventually at least 2 million units/year.
- Tesla has opened an interest form for Cybercab fleet purchases and infrastructure, read by the host as signaling a model where third parties buy and operate vehicles while Tesla supplies software and the ride network.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla VP Ashok Elluswamy has called upcoming FSD version 15 "ridiculously safe," citing earlier hazard prediction and faster reaction times; panelists view it as the key unlock for robotaxi scaling, though no release date is confirmed.
- Regional rollout to Las Vegas and Phoenix has been quieter than expected, with no recent update on FSD regulatory approval progress in China or Israel; a European FSD approval vote is expected around October 6.

## Humanoid Bots
- Figure unveiled a new neural network, Helix 2.5, which the company claims lets its humanoid robot enter unseen homes and perform complex tasks with zero additional training, tested across roughly 30 unseen environments — a competitive data point for the Optimus thesis, per Sawyer Merritt (claims unverified by third parties).
- Panelists caution that Chinese supply-chain rumors about Optimus orders (figures like 5,000 or 50,000 units) may be mischaracterized using contract-manufacturing terms that don't match Tesla's vertical manufacturing approach.
- No formal Optimus demo or product event has occurred yet; panelists expect Tesla to reveal capabilities only near production start, possibly within a month or held until larger-scale production next year.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's Roadster unveil is set for October 1 in the McGregor, Texas area; marketing imagery referencing "no roads" points to a short-burst hover/thruster-assist feature rather than sustained flight, per panelists.
- Panelists expect a potentially record Q3 delivery quarter, speculating deliveries could exceed 400,000 units, against a tough year-over-year comparison from the prior year's expiring EV tax credit.

## Energy
- Tesla partnered with Forum Mobility to operate public Megacharging sites at three new electric-truck depots in California, adding 30 MW of charging capacity; Forum Mobility has contracted fleet offtake for over 330 Tesla Semis.
