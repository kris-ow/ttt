Wisconsin-based carrier Paper Transport (PTI) is evaluating the Tesla Semi Long Range for dedicated Chicago-market operations, marking another fleet testing Tesla's electric Class 8 truck; the article frames this as an evaluation/pilot, not a confirmed fleet order.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **PTI evaluation details:** PTI, which has logged more than 87 million miles on compressed and renewable natural gas and has run a sustainability program for over 15 years, is testing the Tesla Semi Long Range within its dedicated operating model (fixed routes, consistent mileage). CEO Tyler Ellison said the Tesla partnership expands the company's portfolio alongside renewable natural gas and intermodal options for reducing Scope 3 emissions; VP of Maintenance Bryan Ellen said PTI is bullish on the parallels between its dedicated model and the Semi's efficiency.

2. **Production ramp context:** Tesla rolled the first Semi off its new high-volume production line at Gigafactory Nevada on April 29, less than three months before PTI's announcement. The dedicated 1.7-million-square-foot facility is designed for 50,000 trucks a year, though Tesla is ramping gradually; the article notes this shift from low-volume builds to a real production line is what has enabled fleets to actually order the truck after nine years of delays.

3. **Specs and pricing:** Tesla finalized specs in February for two trims: Standard Range (325 miles) and Long Range (500 miles, the version PTI is testing), both using an 800-kW tri-motor drivetrain rated at 1,072 hp and supporting 1.2-MW Megacharger speeds that restore 60% of range in about 30 minutes. Tesla was earlier quoting $290,000 for the Long Range and about $260,000 for the Standard Range; the article cites CARB data putting the average zero-emission Class 8 truck price at $435,000 in 2024, implying Tesla undercuts the field by roughly $145,000.

## Market & Competition
4. **Demand signals since production start:** Between January 2025 and February 2026, the Tesla Semi accounted for 965 of 1,067 applications to California's Clean Truck & Bus Voucher program, versus fewer than 100 combined for Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo. In June, ArcBest bought Tesla Semis for its ABF Freight fleet following a 2025 pilot in which the truck averaged 1.55 kWh per mile — about 9% better than figures earlier reported by DHL and Saia. Drayage operator MDB ran a freight pilot moving containers at Southern California ports, and PepsiCo — the original Semi customer — now runs close to 100 trucks out of depots in Modesto, Sacramento, and Fresno. Tesla also holds reservations from Walmart, Sysco, Anheuser-Busch, UPS, DHL, and J.B. Hunt, with Walmart Canada alone reserving 130 units.

5. **Charging infrastructure as bottleneck:** Tesla opened its first Megacharger station in Ontario, California, and has mapped 66 Megacharger locations across 15 states; the article states that charging infrastructure, not demand, is now the limiting factor, and that a Chicago-based operation like PTI's would need the network to expand eastward before an evaluation could become a full fleet deployment.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert (Electrek) says he is curious whether Tesla will start releasing Semi production and delivery figures, and predicts demand could surge as more fleet operators calculate that electric semi total cost of ownership is already competitive with diesel on many routes. He forecasts a couple more years of validation followed by a major adoption shift in North America, comparing it to trends he says are already underway in Europe and Asia.
