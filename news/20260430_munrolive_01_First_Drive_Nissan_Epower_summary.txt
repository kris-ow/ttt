This video from Munro Live features host Paul Turnbull test-driving the Nissan e-POWER hybrid system in Farmington Hills, Michigan at Nissan's North American headquarters. The segment covers the technical architecture of Nissan's third-generation series hybrid system and previews its upcoming introduction in the US-market Nissan Rogue later in 2026. The video has no direct connection to Tesla, autonomous driving, or the core TTT investment thesis.

## Market & Competition

1. **Nissan e-POWER Series Hybrid Architecture:** Nissan's e-POWER system is a series hybrid, meaning the wheels are driven purely by an electric motor with no mechanical connection between the gasoline engine and the drivetrain. The engine functions solely as a generator to charge the battery, which then powers the electric motor. This delivers an EV-like driving feel without requiring plug-in charging, while retaining the convenience of gasoline refueling. The system debuted in Japan in 2016 on the Nissan Note.

2. **Third-Generation 5-in-1 Modular Unit:** The third-generation e-POWER system features a 5-in-1 modular unit that integrates components to reduce mass, improve modularity, and increase efficiency. This was described as essential for the US market, which demands higher driving speeds, higher payloads, and longer road-trip capability that earlier-generation systems could not support. Paul Turnbull noted that the integrated design aligns with Munro's philosophy of eliminating excess cables, brackets, and connectors to improve quality and reduce failure points.

3. **US Rogue Launch with All-Wheel Drive:** The European version of the Rogue tested in the video is front-wheel-drive only, with the front motor integrated into the 5-in-1 unit. The US-market Nissan Rogue e-POWER will be offered with all-wheel drive, adding a second electric motor at the rear axle for additional power. Launch is expected later in 2026, described as "later this summer or fall."

4. **NVH Tuning and Engine Behavior:** A known challenge with series hybrids is acoustic disconnection — the engine can run at full power while the vehicle is stationary, which feels unnatural to drivers. Nissan engineers targeted this specifically, tuning the system so the engine shuts off at idle, runs quietly at low speeds, and ramps up in proportion to vehicle power demand. Turnbull noted the engine startup produced virtually no torque bump and minimal noise, calling the transition "seamless."

5. **Powertrain Specs and Competitive Positioning:** The system uses a 1.5-liter turbocharged engine as the generator and an electric motor shared with the Nissan Leaf, producing 210 horsepower. Turnbull argued that because the vehicle does not carry a large battery pack, it avoids excessive weight, allowing the 210 hp to feel adequate. He suggested the 1.5L turbo offers strong thermodynamic efficiency at highway speeds, enabling competitive fuel economy. Turnbull concluded that Toyota and Honda will have difficulty matching the smoothness of this system and that the average buyer could save hundreds of dollars annually on fuel versus a comparable ICE vehicle.
