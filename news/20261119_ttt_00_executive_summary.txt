## Brief

- Tesla launches Cybercab rides in Dallas
- Musk plans no-wheel Model Y for groups of 6 or 7
- Tesla added 205 robotaxis to Texas registry in a week
- Anthropic discloses up to $120.5B SpaceX compute commitment
- Musk says merger would be a shareholder-vote question
- Optimus demo possibly "in a month or two"

## Robotaxi
- Tesla notified Dallas residents that Cybercab rides are arriving, per Farzad, with driverless Cybercabs spotted on city streets hours earlier. It is the first Cybercab expansion outside Austin. Service area, pricing, hours and fleet size are unpublished, and Tesla confirmed rides are about to launch, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Texas DMV registry: Tesla added 205 robotaxis in the past week against 24 for Waymo, per Herbert and guest Brian White. That puts Tesla at about 1,500 registered versus roughly 1,300 for Waymo. A record 200 Cybercabs were registered in one day, bringing the total to 773. A registration is legal authorization, not proof of passenger service.
- Musk said at the DealBook Summit that Tesla plans "an autonomous version of the Model Y that won't have steering wheels or pedals," pitched for groups of 6 or 7 and as the answer to the two-seat Cybercab. Per Electrek, no timeline, price or build location was given. Electrek calls it "yet another vehicle nobody asked for" and argues that dropping the wheel adds a federal compliance fight for little gain.
- Musk said the two-seat Cybercab covers about 90% of passenger miles, since average occupancy is about 1.2 people. Farzad cites the National Household Travel Survey at about 1.5 people per mile overall, so the two sources diverge on occupancy.
- California's DMV called Waymo's driverless freeway rides an imminent hazard, and Waymo paused freeway rides in every city it serves, its second such pause this year. Waymo still gives over half a million rides a week. Photos show a Cybercab testing on a freeway in Chandler, Arizona.
- Federal crash logs through mid-November list 36 Tesla robotaxi incidents since June 2025, including two injury reports and no deaths, per Farzad. Eight involve Cybercabs. NHTSA's audit query into Tesla's self-certification of the Cybercab remains open with no published finding. Zoox took the Part 555 exemption route, capped at 2,500 vehicles a year.
- Personal Cybercab orders opened at $29,990 with an estimated December 2026 Austin delivery and a $299 monthly autonomy subscription. Musk posted that the fee is too high and should be just over $100 a month. Customer-car participation in the robotaxi network may start trials next month, with broader onboarding pointed to Q2 2027.
- Sawyer Merritt reported that Dallas Cybercabs have Starlink installed on the back from the factory. Tesla has not confirmed whether the terminals are active.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk said "Optimus is going well" and that Tesla will hold a demo "when we feel it's an authentic demo," possibly "in a month or two." The hosts expect third-generation robots built in Fremont. Per Farzad, Tesla's Q3 letter confirms early Optimus production at Fremont with no unit counts, outside deliveries or price. Deliveries outside Tesla are targeted around end of 2027.
- Per Herbert, Musk indicated actual sales will not begin until late next year, with another three to six months after that. He also relayed Musk's estimate that 10,000–20,000 robots in an "Optimus Academy" would need months of real-world training. Herbert expects industrial tasks such as plugging chargers into Cybercabs first, and home robots five or more years out.
- Farzad speculates that Optimus could be a fit for Travis Kalanick's Adams, which raised nearly $2B in July. The host stresses that no partnership, contract or talks with Tesla have been announced.

## Autonomous Driving
- Brian White said Ashok Elluswamy named three requirements for FSD v15: 24/7 operation, different weather and higher speeds. Herbert says 24/7 operation is now running in all cities. Brian also says FSD v15 is in the Cybercabs and Texas robotaxis.
- Tesla has more than 1.5 million active FSD subscribers paying $99 a month, per Farzad.

## Electric Vehicles
- Musk called the Model Y L "certainly our most popular car" and said Tesla should probably switch all Model Y production to it. He also said the Cybertruck is being sunset to make room for Cybercab production.
- First 50 Tesla Semis were delivered to HMD Trucking in Illinois. Tesla opened its first Texas Megacharger station with 8 stalls and up to 1.2MW peak charging, the seventh Megacharger station across seven states.
- The new Tesla Roadster goes on display at the LA Auto Show starting November 20.

## Energy
- A filing shows Tesla planning a $3.1B polysilicon refinery in Nueces County, Texas, for high-efficiency solar cells as part of a vertically integrated solar supply chain. Sawyer's roundup also lists a $10.1B Texas solar manufacturing facility.
- Tesla deployed nearly 14 GWh of storage in Q3, and per Farzad the Megapack line is nearly sold out for 2027.

## Financials
- Tesla sent shareholders a 5-page letter pushing back on ISS and Glass Lewis, both of which recommended voting against re-electing Board Chair Robyn Denholm.
- Per Tesla's October proxy, none of the 12 tranches of the 2025 CEO performance award has been earned. Market cap goals climb to $8.5 trillion.
- Musk said he should not receive any award for doing a SpaceX merger. He argued that stock options contingent on Tesla execution should be earned only through Tesla performance.

## Market & Competition
- Farzad frames Cybercab economics as a threat to Uber. Human ride-hailing carries a cost floor of about $2 a mile against Tesla's long-term target of about 30 cents, which is not yet reached. Early Austin Cybercab trips priced roughly 40% below Uber, with longer waits.
- Alphabet reported more than 500,000 paid Waymo rides a week and 300 million autonomous miles across 15 US cities, per Farzad. Brian White plans to watch Rivian's autonomy day on December 9.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Per Cern Basher's reporting as cited by Jo Bhakdi, Anthropic's IPO filing discloses it expects to spend up to $120.5B on SpaceX compute through 2029. That implies about $3.1B a month at full capacity, versus $1.25B a month previously disclosed. After initial non-cancelable periods expire in early 2027, either party can terminate on 90 days' notice.
- Musk said Anthropic told him it is "extremely likely" to keep renting SpaceX compute and wants significantly more, with its highest-probability outcome an incremental 10 gigawatts. He described termination notice as 3 to 6 months depending on the contract, which differs from the 90-day figure Bhakdi cites from the filing.
- Bhakdi cites Cern Basher's projection of about $8.3B a month in SpaceX compute rental revenue, a $100B annualized run rate by the end of December. It includes Alphabet, Reflection AI and an undisclosed customer.
- Musk predicted orbital compute will "round up to 100%" of superintelligence compute over time.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX will begin launching its direct-to-cell version 2 constellation next year, with about 40 times the bandwidth of version 1 per Musk. A version 3 would connect ordinary unmodified phones directly from space.
- SpaceX plans micro ground stations using the 800 MHz band it bought, installable on a roof like a dish, to reach people indoors. Production starts next year. Musk does not expect AT&T or Verizon to go out of business.
- Musk said Starlink is civilian infrastructure not for offensive military use. He said offensive actions belong on Starshield, where the kill decision rests with the US government.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk said a Tesla–SpaceX merger is possible either way and is not a "merge no matter what" situation. He cited AI and robotics convergence, said the companies would probably be better together but do not need to be, and said the outcome depends on shareholder votes. He gave no terms or timing.
- Bhakdi argues SpaceX at a roughly $2 trillion market cap looks undervalued against a projected $100B run rate that he expects to grow three- to fourfold next year.
