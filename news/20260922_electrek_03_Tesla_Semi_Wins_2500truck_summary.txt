The article reports that Tesla was named primary truck supplier for a 2,500-truck electric Class 8 order placed through a new shipper alliance called ZET SCALE, alongside a separate 50-truck order from drayage operator IMC Logistics.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **ZET SCALE order structure:** ZET SCALE (Zero-Emission Truck Shipper-Carrier Alliance Leading Electrification), launched by Catalyst Mobility (formerly CALSTART) and the Smart Freight Centre, pools freight demand from shippers including Microsoft and PepsiCo to buy electric trucks in bulk. Tesla won the primary OEM slot via competitive RFP; Kenworth, Volvo, and RIDE are named secondary suppliers. The article notes the 2,500-truck figure covers all four truckmakers combined, with Tesla as lead but not exclusive supplier, meaning not all 2,500 will be Semis — despite Tesla's own framing of "2,500 Semis on order."
2. **Deployment plan and financing:** Trucks will be deployed across 10 freight hubs over the next few years: Los Angeles, Stockton, Bakersfield, Seattle/Tacoma, Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, Chicago, Atlanta, and Newark/New York. A financing arm called ZET Financial issues purchase orders and runs a "ZET Lease" program intended to shift residual-value risk off fleets' books. Tesla Semi program director Dan Priestley and Catalyst Mobility CEO Michael Berube commented on the initiative's market-building goal.
3. **Comparison to prior orders:** The 2,500-truck deal is five times larger than Einride's 500-truck order from a month earlier, which had been Tesla's largest Semi order to date.
4. **Nevada Semi factory timing:** The order lands the same week Tesla inaugurates its new Nevada Semi factory, which has nameplate capacity of 50,000 trucks/year. The article states analysts expect Tesla to deliver only 5,000–15,000 Semis in 2026, with the Long Range model priced around $290,000, suggesting production rather than demand is the current constraint.
5. **IMC Logistics order:** IMC Logistics, a large North American drayage operator, is adding 50 Tesla Semis (a mix of 325-mile Standard Range and 500-mile Long Range trucks) to its California fleet — roughly doubling its existing 56-vehicle zero-emission fleet. IMC also operates hydrogen fuel-cell and renewable-diesel trucks, describing its approach as a "blended" strategy per Jim Gillis, IMC's Pacific Region President.
