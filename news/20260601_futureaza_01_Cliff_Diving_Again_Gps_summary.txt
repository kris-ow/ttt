## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Model 3 survives 300-ft cliff plunge in Malibu:** A Model 3 plunged roughly 300 feet off a cliff on Mulholland Highway in Malibu on a Friday morning, with occupants surviving. The host attributes this to the Model 3's NHTSA five-star overall rating in every category and subcategory, and its record as having the lowest probability of injury of any car ever evaluated by the US New Car Assessment Program. The absence of a traditional engine provides additional crumple zone, and the rigid occupant cell structure is credited for the outcome.

2. **87-year-old driver dies after Tesla enters pond — Autopilot misreported as FSD:** In Tampa, an 87-year-old man driving a Model Y veered off the road, struck an electrical box, and drove into a pond while Autopilot was engaged. The host emphasizes that Autopilot (lane-keep assistance) is categorically different from Full Self-Driving, and that the vehicle's speed — sufficient to break through an electrical pole and still reach the pond in what he describes as a 30–35 mph zone — points strongly to human error rather than a system failure. The host criticizes media coverage that uses incidents like this to discourage adoption of what he calls demonstrably the safest driving system in the world.

3. **Reuters "fake FSD" story dismissed as misinformation:** A Reuters report claimed Tesla staff admitted to selling fake self-driving with fake numbers, citing workers at a Utah office who label video footage of crashes, near-misses with children, and animal strikes. The host argues these data labelers are deliberately shown only the most traumatic edge-case footage — perhaps 10 miles of bad driving per day — while Tesla's fleet is accumulating roughly 30 million miles of good driving daily. He notes that at 10 billion supervised FSD miles driven, the catastrophic death toll that critics imply would exist has simply not materialized, and he calls the Reuters framing deliberately misleading.

4. **Tesla owner repeatedly uses Autopilot from back seat:** A news story profiled a man who has been arrested multiple times for riding in the back seat while Autopilot is engaged. The host strongly discourages this behavior. He also notes the article falsely cited two men killed in Texas while sitting in back and passenger seats of an Autopilot-engaged Tesla — which he identifies as being the unrelated story of intoxicated occupants who crashed into a tree, calling the article "pure slop."

5. **Estonia becomes third European country to clear self-driving on public roads:** Estonia has approved self-driving vehicle operation on its roads, joining the Netherlands and Lithuania. The host notes that while this is technically classified as Level 2 driver assistance under SAE levels, he argues the SAE framework is essentially meaningless — designed before the systems existed, it lumps mild lane-keep through Full Self-Driving into the same band. Drivers remain legally responsible. Other European countries are expected to follow.

6. **Supervised FSD surpasses 10 billion miles:** The host references that Tesla's supervised Full Self-Driving has surpassed the 10 billion mile mark, and that the fleet is now accumulating approximately 30 million miles per day.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **72% of new EV buyers traded in a gas car despite losing the $7,500 tax credit:** EV trade-ins as a share of new EV purchases rose from 67% in January 2026 to 72% by April 2026. The host interprets this as evidence that EV adoption is expanding beyond early adopters into the broader market. Analysts cited high interest rates and more expensive gasoline as contextual factors.

2. **Model 3 reaches 360,000 miles — battery replaced at 321,000 miles for $5,000:** A Model 3 owner hit 360,000 miles, replacing the battery at 321,000 miles. A reconditioned pack cost approximately $5,000, well below the $15,000–$22,000 range for a new battery replacement. The host notes this is exceptional longevity compared to traditional vehicles, and that the original motors remain functional.

3. **Model Y with 111,000 miles retains 90–92% battery capacity despite near-total DC fast charging:** A pre-refresh Model Y at 111,000 miles had received only 36 kWh of charging from AC sources — virtually all charging was DC fast charging — yet retains 90–92% of original range capacity. The host calls the persistent narrative about fast charging destroying EV batteries "ludicrous."

4. **FSD praised by mainstream automotive press:** The host cites a piece describing Tesla's supervised Full Self-Driving as technology that "felt like sci-fi 10 years ago," with a new owner who bought a Model Y after his son recommended it, calling it "the most impressively engineered consumer product since forever." Features highlighted include voice control, Grok integration (available with the spring update), self-parking, summon, collision avoidance, and home charging convenience.

5. **Ford Escape to return as all-electric vehicle in 2029:** Ford is reportedly planning to revive the Escape nameplate as a fully electric vehicle after ending production following the 2026 model year. The host notes a parallel with the Mustang Mach-E strategy of using established nameplates to introduce new technology, though he expresses skepticism about Ford's EV execution. He also notes a Ford Explorer electric exists in Europe but is built by Volkswagen.

6. **AC Cobra electric supercar — host correction:** The host initially reported on an electric AC Cobra supercar before discovering mid-segment that the vehicle may not actually have an electric version, acknowledging the error on air.

## Market & Competition

1. **BYD charges to 97% in 12 minutes even in freezing cold:** BYD's Denza Z9 GT, powered by the second-generation Blade battery, can charge from 10% to 70% in 5 minutes and 10% to 97% in under 10 minutes in ideal conditions — now demonstrated at 12 minutes in freezing temperatures. The host notes these batteries are not available in the US due to import tariffs (cited at approximately 30% for batteries) and export restrictions on equipment to manufacture cutting-edge LFP cells, meaning US LFP factories are using older designs.

2. **Toyota cancels next-generation EV development:** Toyota announced it will cancel development of the LFZ electric sedan under the Lexus brand, reallocating management resources to SUVs. The host frames this as consistent with Toyota's stated "multi-pathway" strategy — gas, hybrid, EV, and hydrogen — which he argues amounts to committing fully to nothing.

3. **Ferrari's new Purosangue-successor criticized by chairman and Italy's deputy PM:** Ferrari's new electric vehicle (referred to as the "luche") drew criticism from Ferrari's former chairman and Italy's deputy prime minister, sending the stock down 8%. The host notes it has a larger battery than a Cybertruck but shorter range than a Cybertruck, and appears designed to compete with Chinese EVs rather than as a traditional Ferrari performance product.

4. **Xiaomi losing $5,600 per car sold; Chinese EV gross margins declining:** Xiaomi (called "Shia" in the transcript), the phone-maker-turned-EV-manufacturer praised by Ford CEO Jim Farley, is losing $5,600 per vehicle. Gross margins for EVs in the broader Chinese market fell from 23.2% to 20.1%. The host pushes back on the narrative that China is simply dumping subsidized cars, noting that a subsidy of $10,000 per car across ~33 million annual sales would amount to over three years of a trillion dollars — a figure he calls implausible.

5. **Vinfast's US factory promise collapses:** Vinfast made promises about building a US factory, went through embassy vetting, but never completed the facility, instead building plants in Indonesia and India. The host expects efforts to claw back committed funds will fail.

6. **GM wins NASA contract to build battery for Moon-bound vehicle:** GM has been awarded a NASA contract to build a battery for a lunar vehicle. The host notes GM is not building the lunar buggy itself — that contract went to Astrolab and Lunar Outpost at $220 million each — and jokes about GM's track record with battery fires.

7. **DC converting streetlights into EV chargers using Dieselgate settlement funds:** Washington DC is using existing light poles — which have surplus electrical capacity after switching from high-wattage sodium lamps to low-wattage LEDs — to install EV charging equipment funded by Dieselgate penalty money. Companies like Shell Recharge and BP have equipment that fits inside or onto the poles. The host estimates these would deliver charging speeds roughly between Level 1 and Level 2, suitable for street parking in dense urban areas.

8. **Tesla stock snapshot (as of June 1, 2026):** Down approximately 3% on the day, down approximately 2.5% over five days, up approximately 7% for the month, down slightly over six months, down slightly year-to-date, significantly up on one-year and five-year timeframes.
