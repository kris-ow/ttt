Tesla will hold a launch event in Casablanca, Morocco on February 6, 2026, introducing the Model 3 and Model Y to the country, with its Morocco website now live for event registration though online configurators are not yet active.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Morocco launch event details:** Tesla will host its official launch at AnfaPlace Mall in Casablanca on February 6, 2026, from 8-10 PM local time, letting attendees meet the Tesla team and see the Model 3 and Model Y; the Morocco website is live for registrations but vehicle configurators are not yet active.
2. **Tesla has operated Superchargers in Morocco pre-sales:** The article notes Tesla has been operating Supercharger stations in Morocco for years prior to beginning vehicle sales there, ahead of its typical pattern of deploying chargers alongside market entry.

## Market & Competition
1. **Morocco's auto market growth:** Morocco's vehicle market hit a decade-high of 179,013 units in 2024 (up 12.8% year-over-year), with H1 2025 sales of 103,391 units, up 22.4% year-over-year. The market is dominated by budget brands: Dacia (22.8% share), Renault (16.8%), Hyundai (8.7%), Peugeot (6.5%), and Volkswagen (6.1%). Morocco's GDP growth is projected at 3.8% for 2025 and 2026, with inflation near 2%.
2. **Morocco as Africa's leading EV market, led by BYD:** EV sales in Morocco surged 176.5% in H1 2025, reaching 3% of total vehicle sales. BYD leads the EV market after a 407% increase in H1 2025, followed by Citroën and Dacia. Morocco's EV fleet was estimated at roughly 11,000 units in 2025, projected to exceed 18,000 in 2026, with about 1,000 public charging points as of 2024. The Moroccan government has said it intends to double EV production capacity to strengthen the country's export-oriented automotive sector.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert notes that Tesla is now entering markets where BYD is already established and selling more affordable vehicles, a reversal from Tesla's earlier role as an EV market pioneer; he says BYD's lineup suits Morocco better but expects Tesla can still capture some share at the premium end. A featured top comment from a reader (Leonard Bates) argues more critically that Tesla's expansion into "marginal markets with marginal products" (citing the Cybertruck launch in the UAE) raises administrative costs for little revenue gain and likens it to a company retreating to the fringes after losing ground in core markets — this is a reader comment, not Electrek's own view.
