This Tesla Beat Podcast episode dissects SpaceX's latest earnings report (revenue, losses, Starlink growth) and the newly announced Terafab chip plant location, alongside a wide-ranging Tesla news roundup covering FSD-driven demand, Megapack 3 production ramp, a California EV rebate, robotaxi competitive dynamics, and consumer-product updates like X Money and a Model Y hardware failure.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **SpaceX earnings beat expectations across segments:** SpaceX reported total revenue of $7.8 billion, up 92% year-over-year and 66% quarter-over-quarter. Net loss was about $540 million (vs. $467 million reported the prior year), operating loss improved to $143 million from $970 million year-over-year, EBITDA was $3.5 billion (up 191%), and capex rose to $18.3 billion from $10.1 billion. A co-host noted every major line item — connectivity, launch, and compute revenue — came in above expectations, with the main risk being uncertainty around how AI-compute valuation assumptions hold up as compute costs and models evolve.
2. **Terafab location and scale confirmed:** SpaceX/Tesla's new Terafab chip facility will be built in Grimes County, Texas, near Giga Texas, with civil work starting almost immediately and construction expected within months; hosts noted Tesla typically uses in-house construction crews rather than contractors. The stated target output is one terawatt per year of chips for FSD and Optimus.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Connectivity (Starlink) revenue drives growth:** SpaceX's connectivity revenue reached $4.3 billion, up 66% year-over-year — roughly half of total company revenue. Hosts noted Starlink now operates across roughly 160 markets and has not reached saturation in most regions, while legacy satellite competitor HughesNet has fallen to about half a million subscribers.
2. **Trillion-dollar revenue goal pulled forward to 2030:** A host stated Elon Musk has pulled forward SpaceX's target of $1 trillion in annual revenue from 2031 to 2030, implying continued ~75–80% annual revenue growth if current trends hold.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **AI compute treated as a distinct, fast-growing revenue pillar:** The earnings report broke out a third segment, described as "AI," which hosts believe includes compute-leasing revenue (e.g., deals referenced with Anthropic and Google). Hosts speculated but could not confirm whether large compute-leasing deals are booked under connectivity, space, or the AI segment.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9 upper stage impacts the Moon:** A recorded, reportedly world-first observation captured a SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage impacting the lunar surface; hosts described this as expected given the Moon's uneven gravity field and orbital mechanics, with no concerns raised about the milestone itself.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Viral "FSD actually works" trend and Tesla's marketing gap:** A host described a growing trend of previously skeptical influencers posting viral videos praising Full Self-Driving after test drives, including a case where a prominent truck reviewer critical of the Cybertruck was reportedly impressed by FSD in a loaner vehicle. Hosts argued Tesla should expand advertising and social presence (e.g., TikTok, where Tesla currently has no official account) to capture this demand, contrasting it with GM's Super Cruise, which one host said remains far behind FSD in capability after a personal test drive experience.
2. **Tesla FSD take rate cited at 55%:** A host cited a 55% FSD take rate on new vehicle purchases, framing last quarter as an inflection point where more customers are buying Tesla vehicles specifically for Full Self-Driving, and expects the take rate to keep climbing before eventually plateauing.

## Robotaxi / Market & Competition
1. **Move raises $250M to build robotaxi infrastructure:** Autonomous ride-hailing infrastructure company Move raised $250 million, which hosts framed as a bet on backing the broader robotaxi industry alongside Tesla and Waymo rather than picking one winner.
2. **Tesla's robotaxi market-entry strategy compared to Uber's early expansion playbook:** A host described Tesla launching robotaxi service in markets where Waymo has already engaged local regulators, similar to how Uber historically let Lyft absorb regulatory pushback before entering a market — a strategy one co-host called "dirty" but effective.
3. **Ford's Fathom EV project carries acknowledged execution risk:** Ford CEO Jim Farley reportedly said there are "no guarantees" the new Fathom EV project will succeed and that Ford aims to become profitable on new EV models with margins, quality, and volumes comparable to competitors including Tesla within a year; a host was openly skeptical, citing Ford's history of unmet EV promises. A release/order timeline of roughly late 2027–2028 was mentioned.
4. **Lucid delays its ~$50,000 "Cosmos" model to late 2027:** Lucid pushed back its planned lower-priced EV (positioned against the Model Y/Rivian R2) to late 2027, with the CEO citing a desire to avoid repeating build-quality and software issues seen in the Air and Gravity. A host also relayed a personal account of a Lucid Air rental car experiencing a dead 12-volt battery and software issues, and recommended leasing rather than buying non-Tesla EVs given uncertain long-term software/support commitments.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **California launches new $3,500 EV rebate with limited, matched funding:** California introduced a $3,500 rebate for new EVs and $1,750–ish (approximate, per speaker) for used EVs, with manufacturers required to match state funding; Tesla's allocated pool is proportionally smaller than in some prior state programs, leading hosts to expect the funds to run out within days to weeks. Rivian and Lucid reportedly have no sales-price cap for eligibility, while other manufacturers (including Tesla) face a price cap around $50,000, effectively excluding higher-priced Cybertruck purchases.
2. **Pre-assembled Supercharger V4 units deploying in Europe:** Tesla has begun deploying pre-assembled, EU-approved folding Supercharger V4 station units, which hosts said should significantly speed up permitting and installation since much of the complex wiring/transformer work is now pre-built into the unit.
3. **New Santana Row showroom emphasizes FSD and Cybercab:** Tesla's showroom in Santana Row, San Jose, was described as showcasing a Cybercab alongside a Model Y L or performance Model Y, with interactive screens and advisor-guided questionnaires focused on matching customers to Full Self-Driving use cases, reflecting the broader shift toward marketing Tesla as an AI/software company.
4. **Possible hardware/software issue on aging Model Y computers:** A co-host detailed a personal experience where, after a low-voltage battery replacement and a Full Self-Driving software update on an out-of-warranty Model Y, the car's main computer failed within roughly 24 hours, knocking out Autopilot, FSD, navigation, and GPS; Tesla told him the failure was unrelated to the software update. He noted another user's thread reporting similar overheating/failure issues across multiple vehicles and speculated Tesla may need to address this via warranty extension or recall, given the company's stated plan to eventually replace older computers with the newer AI4 hardware.

## Energy
1. **Megapack 3 factory reaches production in 16 months:** Tesla's new Megapack manufacturing facility in Brookshire, Texas went from groundbreaking to production in about 16 months and is designed to produce 50 gigawatt-hours of Megapack 3 annually.
2. **Tesla battery recycling framed as a "gold mine":** A Tesla video highlighted battery recycling/refining, with hosts (who have toured Redwood Materials and Giga Nevada) noting refined battery material can end up higher quality than the original and that Tesla aims to send no batteries to landfill; hosts speculated an informal partnership with Redwood Materials (founded by former Tesla exec JB Straubel) given his board position.
