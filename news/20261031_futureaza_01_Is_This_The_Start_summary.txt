This futureaza panel discussion argues that Tesla's Cybercab/robotaxi rollout is entering an exponential ("hockey stick") growth phase, contrasting Tesla's rapid fleet scaling and unboxed manufacturing cost advantage against Waymo's slower, more constrained expansion model. Hosts cite recent earnings-call commentary from Elon Musk, Ashok Elluswamy, and Vaibhav Taneja, plus a Lars Moravy comment from a Munro Live interview, as evidence the company sees regulation — not technology or production — as the binding constraint.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet growth rate and 2026 trajectory:** The hosts say week-over-week robotaxi fleet growth, initially described by Ashok Elluswamy as "double-digit," has reportedly accelerated from an assumed ~10% to ~15% and now appears closer to ~20% per week based on recent data points. They state the fleet has surpassed 1,000 vehicles and, at this pace, is "very likely" to reach 2,000 robotaxis by the end of the year, with the hosts speculating whether 5,000 is reachable; one host relays a view attributed to Brian Wang that a large addition of Cybercabs over the December holidays is unlikely given staffing, with growth possibly resuming in January or February.
2. **Geofence and city expansion in Texas:** The hosts describe the Houston geofence as having expanded roughly 10x in a single day, and show Dallas preparing additional Cybercabs in a parking lot. They claim Tesla's Austin-area geofence is now roughly five times larger than Waymo's despite Waymo having operated there much longer, and assert Tesla has now passed Waymo in total vehicle count in Texas.
3. **Waymo's structural cost disadvantage:** Citing analysis from an institutional analyst the hosts identify as Pierre Ferragu, they argue Waymo's higher per-vehicle cost and operating losses limit it to roughly 500 cars in Austin (covering only base demand of an estimated 300–500), forcing it to expand into new cities rather than add density to existing ones — unlike Tesla's Cybercab, whose lower cost and automation reportedly let it stay idle more cheaply during off-peak periods.
4. **Tesla's service/charging network advantage over Waymo:** The hosts argue Waymo would face major logistical barriers deploying 500 cars to a new market in a week, lacking local service centers and charging infrastructure, whereas Tesla already has dense nationwide coverage of service centers and charging stations supporting Cybercab deployment.
5. **Production reportedly outpacing deployment:** One host relays a comment from Lars Moravy (via a recent Munro Live interview) that Tesla is deliberately throttling Cybercab production — turning the line on and off — because output is currently ahead of the company's ability to deploy vehicles, a reversal of Elon Musk's earlier warnings that the new unboxed manufacturing process and supply chain would be the harder challenge.
6. **Rider pickup/drop-off experience improving but inconsistent:** One host reports that during an October 10 Austin visit, every Cybercab ride dropped off and picked up at the exact requested location, an improvement from prior visits where pickups were offsite from the actual destination; however, after surveying other riders at the same October 10 event, some still reported being picked up or dropped off in inconvenient spots, so the host does not consider the location-accuracy issue fully resolved.
7. **Visible fleet density increase in Austin:** Hosts describe a sharp increase in visible Cybercabs and Model Y robotaxis around Austin compared to a prior visit when the fleet was roughly half its current size, including a surge of "hundreds and hundreds" of additional cars added for an F1 event the week after October 10, alongside shorter rider wait times.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla "flying past" Waymo on cost curve, per Musk:** The hosts note Elon Musk recently stated the robotaxi race with Waymo "wasn't even a competition," and the hosts frame Tesla's cost structure as likely to make Waymo's current approach look outdated within roughly a year and a half to two years, drawing an analogy to steamships and incandescent bulbs being displaced by cheaper alternatives.
2. **Second-quarter plan for third-party-owned robotaxi fleet:** The hosts reference an upcoming plan (described as coming "in the second quarter," timeframe not otherwise specified) under which Tesla would allow customers to supply their own vehicles to the robotaxi network, intended to address demand spikes during special events the way Uber does by pulling in more driver-supplied cars.
