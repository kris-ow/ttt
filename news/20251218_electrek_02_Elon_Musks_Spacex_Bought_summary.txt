Electrek reports that SpaceX has purchased over 1,000 Cybertrucks from Tesla, with potential to ramp toward about 2,000, according to a source familiar with the matter — a move the article frames as Elon Musk using his privately owned company to absorb unsold Cybertruck inventory.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck demand far below planned capacity:** The article estimates Tesla is struggling to sell even 20,000 Cybertrucks per year, less than 10% of the up-to-250,000 annual production capacity Tesla had planned for Gigafactory Texas, and far below Musk's stated expectation of selling up to 500,000 per year. Tesla does not break out Cybertruck sales separately, bundling it with Model S/X, so the figure is the author's estimate based on registration data and historical S/X sales splits. The article states Tesla had over 1 million reservations before production but estimates only about 60,000 converted to orders in the roughly two-plus years since production began, attributing the shortfall mainly to the vehicle's higher-than-announced price and reduced range/features versus the 2019 prototype.

2. **SpaceX purchase and its scale:** A source familiar with the matter told Electrek that SpaceX bought over 1,000 Cybertrucks, potentially ramping to about 2,000, with hundreds visible parked at SpaceX's Southern Texas lots. At a base price of $80,000, the article calculates this would represent between $80 million and $160 million in sales. This follows an earlier Electrek report that Tesla had begun delivering truckloads of Cybertrucks to SpaceX and xAI.

## Financials
1. **Potential Q4 impact for Tesla:** The article says this purchase could meaningfully help Tesla's fourth-quarter results, which are otherwise pressured by the expiration of US EV incentives at the end of Q3, in Tesla's most important market.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the purchase is legal but questions whether it is a smart capital allocation for SpaceX, noting the company has also reportedly committed $2 billion to xAI, and raises concern about the eventual utilization rate of the purchased Cybertrucks, suggesting SpaceX investors may have reason for concern.
