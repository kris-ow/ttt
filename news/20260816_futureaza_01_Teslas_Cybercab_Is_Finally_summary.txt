This video from futureaza, featuring guest Dr. Know-It-All, examines leaked footage and images of Tesla's Cybercab as the vehicle moves toward wider distribution, covering its operator interface, the "Project Rodeo" test-driver program, and open questions about regulatory and technical barriers to full driverless scale.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab distribution ramping, but customer rides not yet live:** The host reports thousands of Cybercabs now visible in various locations, with VINs spotted as high as roughly 2,300 and an estimate that perhaps 1,500 have been sent out to await activation and undergo testing. He notes customer availability may be imminent but was not confirmed as live at time of filming.
2. **Leaked footage reveals removable driving controls ("Project Rodeo"):** Footage showed a Cybercab's dashboard opened to reveal a pop-in accelerator pedal, brake pedal, and a removable steering wheel/paddle used by Tesla's test drivers. The guest linked this to a Business Insider report (dated by the host to around Halloween 2024) describing Tesla's "Project Rodeo" test-driver program, historically run on Model Y/3 and now extended to Cybercab, where drivers are instructed to intervene only when absolutely necessary.
3. **New operator interface buttons detailed:** The interface shown includes four controls — "short oops" (minor issue), "long oops" (critical intervention, sent to top of a review queue), "win" (positive feedback), and a voice note function to annotate events. The guest noted one screen element remains unexplained ("nobody knows what's inside" a labeled robotaxi tab) and speculated, without confirmation, that seat/dynamics profile settings likely live in a separate rider-facing interface rather than the technical operator screen.
4. **Seamless cross-vehicle personalization already exists in Model Y robotaxi rides:** The guest described personal experience from limited robotaxi testing where the vehicle recognized his profile (e.g., automatically setting temperature to Celsius) and resumed media playback across different cars; he expects the Cybercab to extend this further since its screen-controlled seats allow full-position adjustment, unlike the Model Y's back seats.
5. **Cybercab's distinct gold color seen as a deliberate attention-drawing/safety choice:** Both speakers characterized the unusual gold color as intentional — comparable to a "yellow cab" safety color — to make the robotaxi immediately recognizable to non-Tesla-owning riders, distinguishing it from the Model Y robotaxi fleet, which the guest said many people don't realize is already in service.
6. **Guest expects most future Cybercab riders to be non-Tesla owners:** Both speakers speculated (without hard data) that as the Cybercab scales, over half of riders may have no prior Tesla experience, driven by the vehicle's distinctive appearance attracting curiosity and word-of-mouth rather than by riders already being familiar with Tesla software.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Regulatory and data bottlenecks flagged for Cybercab's driverless rollout:** The guest said it remains unclear which bottleneck dominates, citing a likely regulatory issue around vehicles with no steering wheel or pedals (even in Texas) and citing Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's head of Full Self-Driving, who reportedly stated on X that Cybercab's different form factor from the Model 3/Y requires additional data collection to fine-tune the driving model — paralleling the extended timeline needed for Cybertruck FSD, which the guest attributed to Cybertruck's rear-steering and variable front-steering complexity. The guest added, based on conversations with Cybercab engineers, that they expect the Cybercab's FSD challenge to be less significant than Cybertruck's since it is closer in size to a Model 3.
