Tesla's California vehicle registrations fell 24.3% year-over-year in Q1 2026, dropping from 42,211 to 31,958 units — a loss of over 10,000 vehicles — according to the CNCDA's Q1 2026 Auto Outlook report. The decline occurred against an already weak Q1 2025 baseline, when Tesla was production-constrained due to the Model Y changeover.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla California registrations collapse:** Tesla registered 31,958 vehicles in Q1 2026, down from 42,211 in Q1 2025 (-10,253 units, -24.3%). Overall California market share fell from 9.2% to 7.7%. The Model Y led with 22,907 registrations and 53.3% of the luxury compact SUV segment; the Model 3 registered 5,688 units.

## Market & Competition
2. **California ZEV market hits four-year low:** Total ZEV registrations in California dropped 40.2% YoY, from 95,520 to 57,111 units. ZEV market share fell from ~21% in 2025 to 13.7% — the lowest since Q4 2021. California accounted for 29.6% of all US ZEV registrations. Declines were widespread: Mercedes-Benz ZEV registrations fell 81.9%, Chevrolet -59.6%, BMW -58.9%, Ford -58.8%, Kia -48.2%, Rivian -35.9%, Hyundai -30.4%. Meanwhile, hybrids surged to 20.9% market share and gas vehicles climbed to 61.1%, up from 54% in 2025.

3. **Tax credit expiration identified as primary driver:** The expiration of the $7,500 federal EV tax credit on September 30, 2025, is cited as the primary cause. National new EV sales dropped 28% in Q1 2026 as a result.

4. **Lucid and a handful of brands buck the trend:** Lucid was the top-performing brand by percentage growth, with California registrations rising 37.1% (959 to 1,315 units), driven by the Gravity SUV. Only six other brands grew: Mitsubishi (+22.6%), Genesis (+10.2%), Lexus (+7.3%), Volvo (+7.1%), Chrysler (+4.1%), and Toyota (+3.5%) — all predominantly hybrid or ICE sellers.

5. **Tesla's ZEV share within California rose despite volume decline:** As competitors fell harder, Tesla's share of California's ZEV market increased from 44.2% to 56.0%.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert acknowledges Tesla's relative outperformance within the ZEV segment but frames it as cold comfort, arguing that Tesla registering 10,000+ fewer vehicles in a quarter when it was already capacity-constrained represents genuine damage, and that California's retreat from 21% to 13.7% ZEV share — with gas vehicles back above 61% — represents a serious setback for the EV transition driven by removal of the federal tax credit.
