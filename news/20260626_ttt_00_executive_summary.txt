## Autonomous Driving

- **NHTSA removes manual brake pedal mandate for full-autonomy vehicles.** NHTSA officially eliminated the requirement for hand- or foot-operated brake controls on vehicles designed exclusively for automated driving system operation, explicitly framing the move as enabling new vehicle form factors — a direct regulatory unlock for the Cybercab. Equivalent stopping-distance performance must still be demonstrated via alternative testing procedures (FMVSS Order 135).

- **UNECE adopts first global autonomous vehicle technical regulation.** The United Nations Economic Commission for Europe voted in the world's first cross-border regulatory framework permitting fully autonomous vehicles, with officials stating such technology "can be approved and certified and will come to the roads." The dual NHTSA and UNECE announcements on the same day represent the most significant single-day regulatory advancement for autonomous vehicles to date.

- **Texas crash falsely attributed to Autopilot; Tesla and Elon Musk push back hard.** Tesla VP of Autopilot Ashok Elluswamy clarified that the driver had manually overridden FSD by pressing the accelerator to 100%, reaching 73–75 mph in a residential area, with the pedal still depressed after impact. Elon Musk called the media coverage "blatantly irresponsible," citing over 10 billion miles of FSD data showing it is far safer than manual driving. NTSB and NHTSA have both opened probes; the panel on brighterwithherbert noted that factual corrections receive a fraction of the amplification of the original misleading headlines.

- **Q1 2026 earnings deck confirms autonomy architecture progress.** Tesla's Q1 2026 deck states that architectural advances in AI software "will help accelerate our efforts to eventually deploy unsupervised autonomy on both a robotaxi fleet and customer-owned vehicles," with AI inference compute improvements cited as the key enabler, per Jo Bhakdi's live readthrough.

## Robotaxi

- **150+ Cybercabs at Giga Texas — new record.** Joe Tegtmeyer reported observing more than 150 Cybercabs driving or parked across the Giga Texas site, surpassing prior milestones of 30, 50, and 100. The brighterwithherbert panel views the accumulation as evidence Tesla is staging units for a coordinated multi-city launch rather than a gradual trickle.

- **Cybercabs now shipping away from Giga Texas.** Per brighterwithherbert, Tesla is actively shipping Cybercabs to remote locations — a meaningful signal because factory rework is cheapest at the source, and outbound shipping implies confidence in hardware quality. Tesla shipping to itself also allows tighter control over early service experience.

- **Cybercabs spotted near Washington D.C. and Nashville.** A content creator identified as "DJ" shared footage of Cybercabs arriving in the Washington D.C. area roughly four hours before Jo Bhakdi's Friday broadcast. Separately, Sawyer Merritt noted plans to attempt a robotaxi ride in Nashville, implying some service availability there. Neither city appears in RobotaxiTracker.com's current coverage; both sightings are unverified and should be treated as anecdotal pending confirmation.

- **Q2 city-expansion commitment slipping — catalyst window closes July 2.** Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings deck listed Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas as targets for robotaxi launch within six months of the Q4 2024 call — a window closing around June 30. The Q1 deck replaced date-specific language with "preparations underway." Jo Bhakdi identifies an "elevated local probability maximum" for an announcement before Wednesday, July 2, and states explicitly that if nothing materializes by then, he will shift to a more patient H2 2026 posture — warning the stock would remain under pressure without a near-term catalyst.

- **Austin unsupervised geofence expanded to cover entire city.** Per cernbasher, the unsupervised robotaxi geography in Austin now covers the whole city, framed as a precursor to eventually allowing customer-owned vehicles into the same operational zone.

- **Tesla hiring for a full robotaxi operating company.** Cernbasher catalogued at least 11 distinct robotaxi operational functions in open job listings: fleet support, incident response, business development, market analysis, real estate, charging optimization, app development, maps and navigation, pricing, code hardening, and North America logistics. The hosts' conclusion: "You don't need somebody to keep building these things if you don't expect to have millions of users."

- **Customer-owned Cybercab network integration still more than a year away.** Cernbasher found no job listings specifically targeting owner-vehicle onboarding, and the hosts estimate integration into the robotaxi network will not happen in Q4 2026. At scale (10–20 million vehicles), they view it as operationally necessary — but note Ashok Elluswamy has flagged that each vehicle would require a service-center inspection before eligibility, adding significant complexity.

- **Host projects 10,000–15,000 robotaxis live across seven cities by year-end.** The brighterwithherbert host modeled each city eventually reaching ~900 Cybercabs and ~100 Model Y robotaxis, with a seven-city target achieved by end of July in small numbers before a meaningful ramp. Jeff Lutz proposed ~300–500 vehicles as the threshold for a city to feel like a real service competitive with Waymo.

## Humanoid Bots

- **208 total Optimus job listings found — manufacturing leads.** Cernbasher identified approximately 208 Optimus-related listings (202 in North America, 6 in Greece), with Palo Alto hosting roughly half (~111) and Fremont second at 44. Manufacturing, quality, and industrialization roles lead all functional categories at 65 listings, followed by data collection and training (21), AI/ML and Digital Optimus (17), hardware/robotics (17), validation/test (17), and actuators/motors (17).

- **Digital Optimus flagged as the most underappreciated near-term opportunity.** Six of 17 AI/ML Optimus job titles explicitly include "Digital Optimus" — a software-based agentic counterpart to the physical robot capable of reasoning, operating computers, and supporting physical robot training. Cernbasher argues Digital Optimus could reach market before the physical robot given its lack of hardware dependencies, and notes Ashok Elluswamy featured it on a slide in a presentation earlier this week. The hosts compare it to computer-use agents from Anthropic and OpenAI and frame 2026–2027 as the key market window.

- **Fremont is the planned start for Optimus production; first ~5,000 units stay in California and Las Vegas.** Per cernbasher, the initial Optimus production line will be at Fremont, with the first approximately 5,000 units deployed across five to eight Tesla-owned facilities in California and Las Vegas for controlled testing.

- **Langley, BC cluster linked to actuator partnership.** A concentration of Optimus job listings in Langley, British Columbia is attributed by cernbasher (with some uncertainty) to a partnership with a local actuator-focused manufacturer — tentatively identified as Sumitomo or a similar firm.

## Energy

- **$6B+ in new Megapack projects announced in recent weeks.** Sawyer Merritt reported that aggregate new Megapack project announcements over the past several weeks have exceeded $6 billion, with no single project broken out. Separately, brighterwithherbert's Jeff Lutz noted that individual Megapack deals are now being signed at 25 GWh sizes — more than half the Lathrop factory's ~40 GWh annual capacity in a single contract — with recent examples in Italy, one additional European country, and Australia.

- **Megapod confirmed: distributed AI inference co-located at Supercharger stations.** Elon Musk confirmed "Megapod" as the name for Tesla's distributed AI inference data centers, to be co-located at Supercharger sites that already carry grid connections and, in many cases, Megapacks. Musk described eventual scale as millions of units.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Giga Berlin targeting 7,500 units/week starting October 2026.** Tesla announced a second consecutive 20% production increase at Giga Berlin, layered on top of a prior ramp announced earlier this year. The October ramp will require hiring 1,000+ additional workers and has direct upside implications for Q4 2026 deliveries and European revenue.

- **Slate truck launches at $24,950 — lowest-cost BEV in the U.S.** Slate officially revealed its truck at $24,950 with 205 miles of range, 1,550 lbs payload, 2,000 lbs towing, and a convertible pickup-to-SUV modular system. While not a Tesla product, Munro Live — which played a foundational consulting role in the program — covered the launch extensively; Sandy Munro personally ordered two units.

- **Tesla Semi VDC system demonstrated on ice.** Tesla Semi program lead Dan Priestley posted video of the truck recovering from a trailer slide on ice with a loaded trailer (concrete blocks and steel bars). The 800 kW tri-motor drivetrain's independently controllable rear motors allow microsecond-level torque modulation per wheel — a structural advantage over hydraulic traction control on diesel Class 8 trucks. Electrek's Fred Lambert affirms the underlying physics but notes Tesla's "impossible to jackknife" claim from 2017 still lacks independent third-party validation from real-world fleet miles.

- **Tesla Semi Nevada plant began high-volume production in April 2026; capacity up to 50,000 trucks/year.** Confirmed battery configurations: 822 kWh (500-mile) and 548 kWh (shorter-range). The Standard Range variant (325-mile, under 20,000 lbs) carries the same turning radius as a Model S, positioning it for city delivery use cases distinct from the long-haul variant.

- **Polestar banned from U.S. sales after 2027 model year.** The U.S. Department of Commerce denied Polestar the clearance required to continue selling vehicles in the United States, citing Connected Vehicle regulations. The exit removes a competitor in the premium EV segment and reinforces the tightening regulatory environment around Chinese-linked automakers in the U.S.

- **Q2 deliveries data expected the week of June 30.** Jo Bhakdi flagged production and delivery figures as due next week and characterized them as a likely positive catalyst.

## Market & Competition

- **Elon restructured from stock options to restricted stock ~June 17, with a five-year lock-up.** Per Alexandra Merz on brighterwithherbert, Musk's Tesla compensation was changed from options to restricted stock approximately eight days before the recording, giving him voting rights but locking him in as a key person at Tesla for five years — framed as a strong signal of long-term commitment over SpaceX.

- **SpaceX IPO completed in June 2026 at over $2 trillion valuation.** SpaceX went public in what the farzad-fm host describes as the largest IPO in human history, with the company valued at over $2 trillion post-listing. SpaceX's 2025 total revenue per its S-1 was $18.67 billion; Starlink was the only profitable segment at ~$11.4B revenue and ~$4.4B operating profit. Musk has publicly stated SpaceX may reach ~$1 trillion in revenue by 2030–2031 — implying ~123% annual growth for five consecutive years. Goldman Sachs's most bullish 2030 forecast is ~$470B; Morgan Stanley sits at ~$330B.

- **Tesla–SpaceX–XAI–Intel chip JV (Terafab) estimated at $55–$120B.** Musk's stated rationale: "We either build the Terafab or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the Terafab." The orbital compute vision (AI 1 satellite) faces cited engineering barriers: ~10:1 radiator-to-compute mass ratio per IEEE Spectrum, and space radiation degrading standard AI chips within weeks to months.

- **OpenAI restricted new model rollout under U.S. government request.** OpenAI launched three new models (GPT-5.6, Soul, and Luna) but limited initial access to trusted partners following a Trump executive order requesting voluntary government capability assessment before frontier model release. Jo Bhakdi frames this as the opening of structural government oversight of AGI-capable systems, with no obvious technical resolution.
