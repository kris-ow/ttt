FutureAzA hosts Brian and Randy Kirk analyze Tesla's newly released unredacted robotaxi crash reports from Austin, Texas, arguing that the 17 documented incidents are largely minor, non-fault, or unavoidable events that media outlets are sensationalizing. The video's core thesis is that the crash reports, when read carefully, actually reflect well on Tesla's autonomous driving system — and that critics like Dan Oda are misrepresenting the data. The hosts also discuss Waymo's ongoing software and operational problems as a contrasting example of the limitations of rule-based autonomous driving code.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Waymo's standing water recall backfires:** The hosts describe a Waymo software recall triggered after a vehicle was swept away by Texas floodwaters. The original software caused Waymo vehicles to stop and refuse to drive through standing water, so Waymo issued an update to allow vehicles to proceed through standing water regardless — which then led to a vehicle being swept away. Brian frames this as symptomatic of the fundamental problem with hard-coded, rule-based autonomous systems: every new edge case (standing water, snow, ice, fog, sandstorms) requires its own explicit rule, leading to a never-ending and error-prone expansion of code. He contrasts this with neural network-based approaches, which he likens to intuition rather than rules.

2. **Waymo runs a red light in Dallas:** The hosts describe and replay a video clip showing a Waymo vehicle running a red light in Dallas. Brian notes that LiDAR is colorblind as a possible contributing factor. The vehicle made it through the intersection without a collision, though a trailing car had to slow down. Both hosts characterize this as a serious and genuinely dangerous failure — one that stands in stark contrast to the minor incidents documented in Tesla's crash reports.

3. **Waymo vehicles flood a residential neighborhood:** The hosts describe an incident in which empty Waymo vehicles — sometimes 30, 40, or 50 cars at a time — repeatedly circled and filled a residential neighborhood in the mornings. When a resident placed a barricade at the neighborhood entrance to stop them, all the vehicles became stuck and jammed at the entry point, unable to resolve the blockage.

4. **Dan Oda's Model Y school bus test:** The hosts note that Tesla critic Dan Oda posted results showing the Tesla Model Y scored 50 out of 50 on his school bus test — but then undercut the positive result by calling for a recall because the vehicle still runs on Hardware 3. Brian and Randy mock this as an example of motivated reasoning, with Randy crediting his own trolling of Oda as partially responsible for eliciting the half-positive review.

## Robotaxi

1. **Tesla's 17 unredacted robotaxi crash reports reviewed in full:** Tesla released unredacted crash report data for its Austin robotaxi fleet, documenting 17 incidents total. Brian goes through all 17 chronologically, with annotations. The vast majority involve other vehicles or objects making contact with a stationary or lawfully operating Tesla robotaxi: rear-end collisions at stop signs and red lights, a metro bus's bike rack scraping a stopped Tesla, a scooter rider colliding with a parked Tesla, and an SUV creeping forward and tapping a stopped Tesla at a red light. Several incidents involve incidental contact with inanimate objects during low-speed maneuvers: a mirror brushing a parked tow truck, reversing into a wooden electrical pole, a tire touching a curb while parking, and a mirror grazing a gooseneck hitch in a residential driveway.

2. **Two incidents flagged as legitimately worth addressing:** Of all 17 incidents, Brian identifies only two that he considers genuinely noteworthy: (1) the vehicle driving into a chain stretched across a parking lot entrance, which he suggests could be addressed via simulation and targeted test drives; and (2) a vehicle scraping a construction site barricade — though notably, that incident occurred after a remote teleoperator took over control from the ADS, not while the ADS was driving autonomously.

3. **Remote teleoperators implicated in at least two incidents:** Two of the 17 crash reports involve a safety monitor requesting remote operator support, with the teleoperator then taking over and causing or contributing to the incident. In one case, the teleoperator gradually increased vehicle speed and drove it up a curb into a metal fence. Brian and Randy joke that the teleoperators may be the bigger problem and suggest the ADS should perhaps never hand off control.

4. **One injury reported across all 17 incidents:** A safety monitor reported a minor complaint of pain after a Tesla robotaxi was rear-ended by an SUV in a right-turn-only slip lane with a yield sign. No passengers were inside the vehicle at the time of at least one incident. Brian and Randy treat the injury report sardonically, noting it resulted from another driver hitting the Tesla, not from any ADS error.

5. **One animal interaction documented:** A dog wandered into the road, causing the Tesla to slow. The dog ran toward the vehicle and made contact with the front bumper before moving on. Brian and Randy treat this as clearly unavoidable and note that the dog was seen leaving under its own power in the camera footage.
