This video from "brighterwithherbert" features host Herbert and guest Brian White (of the FutureAzA YouTube channel) breaking down five major Tesla-related developments from the week: the SpaceX Terafab chip fabrication filing, the Boring Company's Nashville residential loop agreement, a rumored FSD version rollout in China, new Roadster trademark filings, and a new tiered Model Y lease structure. The core thesis is that Tesla and its ecosystem companies are accelerating on multiple fronts simultaneously, reinforcing a long-term bull case centered on autonomous driving, infrastructure, and vehicle affordability.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Rumored FSD Version 14.3.2 Push to Employees in China:** According to a rumor circulating online, Tesla may have pushed FSD version 14.3.2 — described as "full functionality" — to its employees in China. Brian White notes that FSD rollout in China has been delayed from an expected March launch due to unresolved government regulatory hurdles. He explains that China must train on locally sourced and processed data, and that the driving style there differs significantly from the US. White recounts that Xiaomi engineers who conducted head-to-head comparisons told him that Chinese FSD (running version 13) is not on par with US FSD, which they described as "just about flawless." He suggests that if the rumor is true and employees are receiving the latest version, it signals the Chinese government may be preparing to approve a wider rollout — which would unlock a significant new recurring revenue stream for Tesla.

2. **Boring Company Nashville Loop to Use FSD:** The Boring Company has signed its first residential loop station agreement in Nashville, covering luxury towers at Prime Al Cove and Paramount Towers in downtown Nashville. Residents will take an elevator directly to an underground loop station, board a Tesla, and reach the airport in 9 minutes or Lower Broadway in 2 minutes. Herbert notes that the Boring Company has already begun testing Tesla Full Self-Driving inside its Las Vegas loop (as of October), with the car running robotaxi software on version 14. The Nashville project is described as privately funded, with first segments targeted for late 2026. Brian White emphasizes the economic model: the Boring Company asks only for permits and land at terminal endpoints, then recoups its investment through fares — a model made viable because its tunneling costs (targeting ~$50 million per mile) are a fraction of traditional subway construction ($500 million to $2 billion per mile).

3. **FSD Subscriber Growth and Version 14 Momentum:** Herbert mentions that Tesla gained another 170,000 FSD subscribers, describing this as a small but persistent and growing revenue stream. He and Brian White both express enthusiasm for FSD version 14, with White suggesting it may be time for Tesla to offer another free one-month trial given how good the software has become.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **New Five-Tier Model Y Lease Structure:** Tesla has rolled out a new flexible five-tier Model Y lease. The entry-level standard rear-wheel drive starts at $459/month, the dual motor all-wheel drive at $499/month, a premium tier at $569/month, premium all-wheel drive at $669/month, and the performance variant at $799/month — all requiring just over $4,000 at signing. Brian White highlights the competitiveness of these numbers by citing a comparison in which a Model 3 standard lease on a 48-month, 10,000-mile-per-year, zero-down basis came in at $299/month — cheaper than a comparable Toyota Camry or Honda Civic lease — while also saving nearly as much in fuel costs as the monthly payment itself.

2. **Leasing as a Robotaxi Pipeline Strategy:** Both hosts discuss the strategic logic of Tesla's lease pricing: by offering attractive terms, Tesla gets cars into consumers' hands with low upfront commitments, then reclaims those vehicles at lease-end (typically 3–4 years, ~30,000–40,000 miles on the odometer) and redeploys them into the robotaxi fleet. White notes this strategy dates to 2018 Model 3 leases, which contractually prevented buyers from keeping the cars. At that time, the robotaxi fleet wasn't ready, so Tesla sold the returned cars as near-new, effectively collecting margin twice. White frames this as a plan that works whether or not the robotaxi deployment is ready at lease-end.

3. **Canada's Affordable Model 3 and Model Y L Spotted in the US:** Brian White highlights a newly introduced Canadian-market rear-wheel drive Model 3 Premium priced at CAD $39,000 (approximately USD $29,000), calling it cheaper than anything "remotely comparable" even before factoring in fuel savings. Separately, White mentions that a camouflaged Model Y L (long version) was spotted in the United States, which he views as a positive signal that the vehicle may arrive sooner than Elon Musk's stated "end of year" timeline — White had publicly predicted a first-half 2026 arrival.

4. **Model Y Five-Star ANCAP Safety Rating:** The refreshed Model Y received a five-star safety rating from ANCAP (the Australian equivalent of Euro NCAP). Brian White notes this result was expected given the Model X's strong safety record and the Model Y's structural continuity, and describes it as confirmatory rather than surprising.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX Terafab — Largest Chip Fab Filing:** SpaceX filed documents with Grimes County, Texas proposing construction of what Elon Musk described as "the largest and most advanced chip fabrication facility in the world." The filing cites a $55 billion initial capital investment and up to $119 billion total. Musk confirmed the filing is real but noted Grimes County is one of several locations under consideration. A public hearing is scheduled for early June 2026. Brian White notes that Tesla has pledged $20 billion and SpaceX has pledged $20 billion toward the project, covering most of the initial $55 billion phase. He pushes back on the "most advanced" claim, suggesting the Fab Lab adjacent to Giga Texas would hold that title. He also speculates that Intel could be a funding partner, and that operational income from both Tesla and SpaceX could cover the remaining capital, potentially supplemented by debt.

2. **Next-Gen Roadster Trademarks and Hover Capability Tease:** Tesla filed three new trademarks for the next-generation Roadster. Sawyer Merritt noted the trademark filing describes lines depicting "speed, propulsion, heat or wind." In a clip from an interview at a moonshot event, Elon Musk confirmed that hover time using cold gas engines is "classified" but acknowledged there will be a hover capability and called the reveal "a cool demo." Musk also notably characterized the Roadster as "the best of the last human-driven cars," suggesting it may be one of the final vehicles designed primarily for driver enjoyment rather than autonomy.
