This episode of Electrified covers Tesla's new company-wide AI token spending cap, a detailed breakdown (via a Lars Moravy interview) of how Tesla uses AI internally across manufacturing and service, the rapid rise of xAI's Grok 4.5 model and its ties to the Cursor acquisition, and several Tesla-specific updates including the Miami robotaxi launch, Cybercab hardware details, Semi order growth, and NHTSA's openness to dropping steering-wheel requirements for driverless vehicles.

## Financials
1. **Tesla caps AI token spend at $200/week per engineer:** Tesla has implemented a companywide cap of $200 per week per engineer on AI token usage, a reversal from a previous "free-for-all" era with a leaderboard tracking top token users. Exceptions require higher-up approval, and usage of beta Grok products (free) doesn't count toward the cap.
2. **Stock closing prices:** Tesla closed at $406.55, up 3.17% on the day; SpaceX closed at $152.16, up 2.6%, versus the Nasdaq-100 (NDX) up 1.62%.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Lars Moravy details Tesla's internal AI use (Brighter with Herbert podcast):** Moravy described four categories: (1) a searchable "lessons learned" database for issues like battery/inverter failures, benefiting supply chain teams too; (2) refined leaderboards/KPI tracking that spot subtle equipment drift; (3) cross-system "reasoning" links where downstream issues trigger automatic upstream corrections (e.g., end-of-line vehicle scans notifying the stamping team); (4) a shared service-center knowledge database logging fixes and customer complaints across locations, which Moravy said is reducing service wait times.
2. **NHTSA signals openness to dropping steering wheel requirement:** NHTSA's head said the agency will "absolutely consider" ending requirements that driverless vehicles include steering wheels, calling manual controls unnecessary for vehicles never intended to be human-driven. No legislation yet exists on this specific point (unlike the brake pedal requirement, which already has legislation under review).
3. **FSD natural-language destination requests floated by Ashok Elluswamy:** Responding to a user request that FSD understand conversational directions ("it's the White House on the left, just past the SUV") and remember them, Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is "working on it." The host expressed skepticism given Tesla's past removal of speed control features for model simplicity, questioning how guardrails would prevent misuse of open-ended voice input.

## Robotaxi
1. **Miami robotaxi launch used a smaller safety-driver team than Austin:** According to The Information, Tesla's Miami robotaxi rollout used a smaller pre-launch test-driver team compared to the over 300 drivers used ahead of the Austin launch roughly a year earlier. The exact Miami driver count wasn't disclosed, but the host called it a step toward Musk's stated goal of deploying FSD/robotaxi without exhaustive pre-mapping of every city. The Miami geofence is estimated at roughly 15 square miles with likely under 10 vehicles deployed.
2. **Cybercab reportedly getting upgraded dual-GPS hardware:** Per exclusive reporting from a Tesla-focused outlet citing an unnamed source, production Cybercab units feature a more powerful dual-GPS configuration (unclear if dual-band or dual physical units) for significantly improved positioning accuracy versus current consumer vehicles — distinct from the "black box" telematics hardware in currently deployed Model Y robotaxis (Project Halo). The host speculated (explicitly labeled as speculation) this could relate to prior incidents where remote operators moved a vehicle into an object, possibly due to lag or GPS inaccuracy.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y tops Netherlands H1 sales:** Tesla stated the Model Y was the Netherlands' best-selling vehicle across all fuel types in the first half of the year.

## Market & Competition
1. **QX Logistics orders 20 Tesla Semis via Forum Mobility:** QX Logistics, which runs four warehousing/fulfillment sites in the LA basin, ordered 20 Tesla Semis through fleet enabler Forum Mobility, expecting delivery in 2027. This brings total publicly confirmed Semi orders to 666. Lars Moravy previously said most of this year's Semi allocation is already accounted for.
2. **Grok 4.5 launch and SpaceX AI's competitive leap:** xAI released Grok 4.5, built on a reported 1.5 trillion parameter foundation (up from ~500 billion for Grok 4.3), emphasizing token efficiency and lower cost over raw benchmark supremacy. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Grok 4.5 scored 54 versus Grok 4.3's 38, ranking fourth behind GPT-5.5, Opus 4.8, and another top model. Pricing is $2/$6 per million input/output tokens (under 200k context) versus Claude's $10/$50. Cursor's blog showed Grok 4.5's cost-per-task at roughly $1.51 versus $17.32 for a competing model. A cursor-bench result favoring Grok 4.5 was flagged by Cursor itself as potentially skewed by inadvertent inclusion of cursor codebase data in training, a caveat the host noted most commentators are ignoring.
3. **Cursor acquisition deepens SpaceX AI's vertical integration:** The pending Cursor acquisition (expected to close Q3) gives SpaceX access to Cursor's "Origin" code-repository product (a GitHub competitor built for AI-agent-driven commits) and real developer workflow data, which the host argues is a uniquely valuable training data source. Elon Musk indicated a new two-trillion-parameter foundation model is expected to finish training soon and reach customers shortly after, with SpaceX AI reportedly planning to launch a new model monthly and developing custom inference software mapped to Nvidia GB300 hardware.
