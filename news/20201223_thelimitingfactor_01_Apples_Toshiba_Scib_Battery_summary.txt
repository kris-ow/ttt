This livestream by thelimitingfactor reacts to breaking news (relayed via Galileo Russell) that Apple is reportedly developing a car battery pack using lithium-titanate cells made by Toshiba. The host, drawing on general battery knowledge rather than new research, explains the chemistry's characteristics and argues it represents a different strategic tradeoff than Tesla's battery choices rather than a superior or inferior one.

## Market & Competition
1. **Apple reportedly pursuing Toshiba lithium-titanate battery for a car:** The host relays a report (via Galileo Russell) that Apple is working with Toshiba on a lithium-titanate battery pack, with an unconfirmed detail that the pack may use around 326 cells to reach an 800V architecture; the host stresses he has no direct knowledge of Toshiba beyond it being a large battery manufacturer, and this is presented as breaking, not yet verified, news.
2. **Lithium-titanate characteristics — durability and fast charging over range:** The host describes lithium-titanate as decades-old, low-fire-risk chemistry with strong cold-temperature performance (down to -30°C), a cycle life around 20,000 cycles (citing unspecified data from Jeff Dahn's lab suggesting roughly 8 years to degrade one cell), and rapid charging (80% capacity in about 6 minutes) with no need to avoid full charge/discharge cycles. He speculates (explicitly "shooting from the hip") that a resulting vehicle might target around 200 miles of range, given the chemistry's low energy density, but says he has no real basis for that number.
3. **800V architecture not seen as an advantage over Tesla:** The host argues Apple's rumored 800V pack architecture would not make it superior to Tesla's lower-voltage approach; he believes (while noting he's "not an electrical engineer") Tesla chose lower voltage/higher current deliberately to reduce corrosive stress on components, offsetting the higher current/heat via battery management, tabless electrode design, and thermal management.
4. **Different battery chemistries suit different products, not a hierarchy:** The host frames lithium-titanate as filling a different use case than Tesla's cells (similar to how lithium iron phosphate was once dismissed before Tesla adopted it) — trading energy density and cost for durability, safety, and charge speed — and expects this suits an "Apple experience" oriented toward low-maintenance, fast-charging vehicles rather than maximum range. He says Tesla could technically build a similar pack in its 4680 format but hasn't because it doesn't fit Tesla's strategy, and separately speculates a resulting Apple vehicle could appear before 2025.

Notable quotes and data:
- "Lithium-titanate batteries have been around for decades... super safe, they charge up fast... but they don't have high energy density and they tend to be more expensive."
- Cycle life cited: ~20,000 cycles; fast charging: 80% capacity in about 6 minutes.
- Cold-temperature performance cited down to -30°C.
- Unconfirmed report detail: ~326 cells in the pack, targeting roughly 800V.
- "I would like to think that they're going to shoot for at least 200 miles of range" (host's own speculation, not sourced).
