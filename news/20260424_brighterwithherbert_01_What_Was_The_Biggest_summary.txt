This video is a post-earnings roundtable discussion hosted by Herbert, featuring Tesla retail analyst Matt Smith ("Nerd Alert"), executive commentator Jeff Lutz, and financial analyst Alexandra Merz, reviewing Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call. The panel dissects what they consider the most important news — good and bad — covering Cybercab production, robotaxi deployment pace, FSD progress, Optimus production timelines, energy segment headwinds, and significant corporate governance developments around Elon Musk's compensation plan. The overarching tension throughout is between Tesla's long-term transformational potential and the near-term pace of execution falling short of prior guidance commitments.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14 Cleared for Unsupervised Operation:** Elon Musk stated on the earnings call that FSD version 14 is safe enough for unsupervised driving, and that a series of incremental upgrades within the version 14 series will bring the system to full unsupervised capability by the end of 2026. Herbert emphasized this was not a delay from his own expectations — he had anticipated unsupervised capability arriving in 2026 or later. The panel noted that FSD 14.3 resolves known safety issues but that operational edge cases (such as vehicles getting stuck in loops or blocking traffic during left turns) remain the primary barrier to scaled deployment, not safety per se.

2. **Hardware 3 vs. Hardware 4 Clarification:** Alexandra Merz highlighted that a key underappreciated statement from the call was Elon's confirmation that Hardware 4 is sufficient for Full Self-Driving unsupervised operation. For Hardware 3 owners who purchased FSD outright, Tesla indicated there will be trade-in vouchers as compensation, though this applies specifically to those who purchased FSD outright — not every Hardware 3 vehicle owner. Alexandra noted this was important news that was not communicated with the weight it deserved during the call.

3. **Software Rearchitecting Concern:** Jeff Lutz flagged that Elon's discussion of rearchitecting software components during the call was an example of over-communicating engineering details without attaching a clear business timeline. Jeff argued that without a specific deadline or milestone tied to these software changes, such commentary creates unnecessary ambiguity for investors and analysts trying to model the business.

## Robotaxi

1. **Guidance Pulled on Fleet Doubling Commitment:** A key negative flagged by the panel was that Tesla effectively pulled its prior commitment to double robotaxi vehicle deployments monthly, a target that had been set approximately 90 days before the earnings call. Jeff noted that as recently as early 2026, the company had stated it was doubling vehicle deployments monthly — implying a target of roughly 2,000 vehicles in April 2026 — but that commitment was not reaffirmed. Herbert countered that the failure to hit the doubling target had been visible in the data for months and was not a surprise announcement from the call itself.

2. **12-State Expansion Target Reiterated:** Elon confirmed on the call that Tesla's robotaxi service is on track to expand to 12 states by the end of 2026, with Cybercabs replacing Model Y vehicles as the primary robotaxi fleet vehicle over time. Elon also stated that approximately 90% of rides are in two-seater vehicles, which he cited as justification for prioritizing Cybercab production. However, the panel debated what "12 states" means in practical terms — whether it implies 50 cars per city, 500 per city, or something else — and concluded that without normalized metrics (e.g., miles per city, wait times, ride completion rates), the target is nearly impossible to model or verify.

3. **Meaningful Revenue Pushed to 2027:** Elon stated on the call that robotaxi will not generate meaningful earnings until 2027, not 2026. Matt noted that Elon had previously suggested robotaxi would begin to have a "somewhat meaningful impact" in the second half of 2026 and a big impact in 2027, but those goalposts now appear shifted — with meaningful impact expected in 2027 and significant earnings growth perhaps not until 2028. Matt's financial model places robotaxi gross margin crossing $1 billion per quarter sometime in Q3 2027, which he defines as the threshold for materiality.

4. **Operational Challenges Are the Binding Constraint:** Multiple panelists agreed with Elon's framing that the primary barrier to scaling robotaxi is not safety — FSD is already statistically safer than a human driver — but operational challenges such as edge cases causing vehicles to block traffic, get stuck in loops, or create logistical bottlenecks. Matt argued these are solvable but that their complexity was underestimated in prior models, including his own. Jeff noted that without clear exit criteria for a "launched" city (e.g., wait time thresholds, ride completion percentages), it is impossible to objectively assess progress.

5. **Cybercab Fleet Accounting Treatment:** Herbert raised and the panel briefly explored the question of how adding Cybercabs to Tesla's own robotaxi fleet would be treated on the balance sheet. Matt and Jeff agreed the most logical accounting treatment would be to capitalize the vehicles as assets with an assumed useful life (Matt suggested roughly one year as a cash-on-cash payback period; a five-year useful life was floated as a GAAP assumption), rather than expensing them. This would make reported GAAP gross margins look stronger once safety monitors are removed and scale is achieved.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Production Start Targeted for July–August 2026:** Jeff Lutz noted that one of the genuinely positive surprises from the earnings call was Elon's indication that Optimus production is expected to begin in July or August 2026. Jeff speculated this likely represents a smaller pilot production line — capable of producing a production-quality robot but at limited throughput — running in parallel with a larger planned million-unit-per-year line. He further speculated this early line may be producing what could become Optimus version 4.

2. **2026 Production Volume Impossible to Quantify:** Elon declined to give a specific Optimus production volume for 2026, stating it was impossible to know, which contrasted with a prior statement of 5,000 humanoid robots for 2025. Matt's financial model projects approximately 3,000 units for 2026, which he described as a reasonable estimate that he has not changed. Jeff referenced plans for both a million-unit-per-year line and a separate ten-million-unit-per-year line in the longer term. Alexandra humorously noted her primary interest is receiving an Optimus unit to handle household tasks.

3. **2025 Compensation Plan Provisioning Signals Optimus Milestone Confidence:** Alexandra flagged that Tesla has begun provisioning approximately $1 billion toward the 2025 executive compensation plan — which was voted on in November 2025 and includes milestones tied to market cap, vehicle production volumes (including 1 million Optimus units), FSD licenses (10 million), and other operational targets. Under accounting rules, a company only begins provisioning for a milestone-based award when it reasonably anticipates the milestone will be achieved. Alexandra interpreted this provisioning as a signal that Tesla's board believes at least one of the milestones is within reach.

## Energy

1. **Energy Segment Faces Compression from Tariffs and Competition:** Herbert flagged a concerning statement from the call: Elon indicated that on a normalized basis, Tesla expects continued energy revenue compression going forward due to increasing competition and tariff impacts. Tesla explicitly noted that most battery cells for the Megapack are procured from China, making tariffs particularly impactful. Matt confirmed this was a disappointment — deployments declined significantly sequentially, and energy revenue declined almost in lockstep with deployments rather than being buffered by a deferred revenue pipeline as he had hoped. However, Matt noted that Tesla achieved nearly 40% gross margins in the energy segment this quarter, though he acknowledged some of that was attributable to one-time items.

2. **Pack Assembly Is the Current Bottleneck:** Jeff noted that Tesla is not constrained by battery cells but specifically by module and pack assembly capacity. He found this surprising, since pack assembly carries the shortest capital expenditure lead time in the battery supply chain and should be relatively quick to bring online. He speculated that Tesla may have been hit by unexpectedly strong demand rather than a foreseeable supply gap. Tesla reiterated that its order backlog for the energy business remains robust.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Auto Sales and Backlog at a Two-Year High:** Jeff noted that Tesla's auto order backlog reached its highest level in more than two years during Q1 2026 — returning to levels not seen since 2023. He cited the Model Y as a continued strong seller and mentioned inventory standing at approximately 25 days, suggesting it would be worked down relatively quickly into the backlog. He also noted that oil prices around $93 per barrel may be serving as an incremental tailwind for EV demand.

2. **Cybercab Enters Production, Aesthetic Finalized:** Herbert highlighted that Cybercab has officially started production and is on track for volume production by the end of 2026. A video of the Cybercab driving autonomously outside Giga Texas — with no steering wheel and no occupant — was released in conjunction with the earnings call. Jeff also noted that Tesla revealed the final exterior finish on the Cybercab: a high-gloss finish rather than the previously expected matte finish, which Jeff argued is more appropriate for a lower-price-point product and will make the vehicle appear more premium and iconic. Herbert estimated production of roughly 15,000 units by year-end as a working assumption, though he acknowledged uncertainty.

3. **Giga Berlin Hiring Increase:** Herbert noted that Tesla is hiring additional workers at Giga Berlin to support increased vehicle production, which he cited as evidence that Tesla expects to resolve near-term supply and demand challenges across its factory network.

## Financials

1. **Q1 2026 Earnings Beat Expectations with Strong Quality:** Matt described Q1 2026 earnings as a "significant beat" relative to analyst expectations, with him having been on the high end of analyst EPS estimates — and Tesla still beating his number. Jeff highlighted that gross margin performance and free cash flow were the two biggest positive surprises. Automotive gross margin came in at approximately 21% (with a lower figure cited on an adjusted basis excluding certain items). Jeff specifically called out a $230 million warranty reserve adjustment — Tesla revised downward its expected warranty costs because product failure rates and repair costs are coming in better than anticipated, out of an approximately $8 billion total warranty reserve built up over time.

2. **ZEV Credits Remain a Meaningful Revenue Source:** Jeff noted that regulatory credit (ZEV credit) revenue came in at $380 million for the quarter, despite prior bear arguments that these credits would disappear. He attributed the continued contribution to participation from other countries in similar programs and some residual U.S. activity.

3. **FSD Subscription Surge Was Partially a One-Time Event:** Matt provided a detailed analysis suggesting that a significant portion of the quarter-over-quarter surge in FSD subscriptions (which Tesla records as subscriptions, including outright purchases) was driven by customers rushing to buy FSD outright before the option was eliminated going forward. He estimated that approximately 38,000 outright FSD purchases — roughly a 10% take rate on deliveries — contributed approximately $300 million in one-time revenue benefit. He warned this will not recur and will create a meaningful but not catastrophic headwind to automotive gross margins in subsequent quarters. To generate an equivalent ongoing benefit from subscriptions alone would require approximately 10x the current subscriber base, or roughly 1 million subscribers.

4. **Capex Raised to $25 Billion for 2026, Free Cash Flow Expected Negative:** Tesla raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to approximately $25 billion, up from a prior figure of approximately $20 billion. In Q1 2026, Tesla spent approximately $2.5 billion in capex against an expected $4 billion, resulting in a free cash flow of approximately $1.4 billion — a positive surprise. However, Alexandra calculated that spending the remaining roughly $22.5 billion across the remaining three quarters implies approximately $7.5 billion per quarter in capex going forward, which would push free cash flow deeply negative — potentially $3.5 to $4 billion negative per quarter. Tesla holds approximately $44 billion in cash with minimal operating debt (approximately $7 billion in asset-backed structured debt), a $5 billion undrawn credit facility, and no need to raise external capital under this scenario. Alexandra also noted that production exceeded deliveries by approximately 50,000 vehicles in Q1 2026, representing approximately $500 million in negative free cash flow impact that should normalize in future quarters. Additionally, a $2 billion acquisition was announced around the time of the earnings call, consuming a portion of the raised capex budget.

5. **Q1 2026 Capex Breakdown from 10-Q:** Alexandra read out capex details from page 16 of the 10-Q: approximately $1 billion in machinery, equipment, vehicles, and office furniture; approximately $750 million in land and buildings; approximately $900 million in AI infrastructure; approximately $200 million in leasehold improvements; approximately $200 million in computer equipment, hardware, and software; and approximately $1 billion in property, plant, and equipment, for a total of approximately $7.7 billion in cumulative AI infrastructure noted in the quarter's filings.

6. **Elon's 2018 Compensation Package Resolution:** Alexandra provided an extensive breakdown of a significant corporate governance development disclosed in the Q1 2026 10-Q. A special committee of the Tesla board — including Katharine Wilson Thompson and Robin Denholm — convened without Elon Musk or his brother Kimbal to resolve the status of the 2018 compensation package following the final resolution of the Delaware legal dispute. Key outcomes: (1) The 2018 package was formally reinstated in its original format, with stock options exercisable by January 2028. (2) Tesla will assist Elon in exercising those options, but instead of receiving freely tradable shares, he will receive restricted stock that must be held for five years — meaning the bulk cannot be sold until 2033. (3) Tesla agreed to compensate Elon for any required tax payments arising from the exercise, reducing the risk of a large forced stock sale. (4) The interim 2025 award of 96 million shares (which had been created as a contingency if the Delaware case was lost) was returned and canceled, as confirmed by a Form 4 SEC filing. Alexandra interpreted this as eliminating a major bear argument — that Musk would be forced to dump shares to cover taxes — and noted that the resolution is compatible with a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger since restricted stock could convert to the new entity's stock at the applicable conversion rate.

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo Perceived as "At Scale" Despite Limited Fleet:** Jeff pushed back on the media narrative that Waymo is operating at scale, pointing out that Waymo has deployed approximately 3,000 vehicles. He argued this creates a "reality distortion field" in which Tesla is portrayed as chasing a competitor that has achieved relatively modest deployment. He suggested that Elon's definition of "volume scale" for robotaxi likely requires numbers with a million in front of them, and that the current debate about whether Tesla is "behind" Waymo is based on a misleading framing.

2. **Tesla's Unique Multi-Product Portfolio:** Jeff repeatedly emphasized that no other company simultaneously produces the world's best-selling EV, a leading Full Self-Driving autonomous system, the Cybercab, a fully functional electric semi-truck, and what may become the world's highest-volume humanoid robot in Optimus. He argued this portfolio uniqueness is being undervalued by investors focused narrowly on near-term quarterly guidance misses.

3. **Terafab / SpaceX Governance Complexity:** Alexandra discussed Elon's on-call reaction to a Goldman Sachs question about Terafab — a chip manufacturing initiative involving multiple corporate entities — as evidence of his frustration with managing Tesla and SpaceX as separate public and private entities. Elon expressed exasperation at having to navigate two boards, conflicts of interest, and committee approvals. Alexandra speculated, based on this and other signals, that a Tesla-SpaceX merger of equals could occur, with her personal timeline estimate being July 2026, though she acknowledged this is speculative. Matt said he also believes the merger will happen but on a longer timeline, perhaps in 2027, citing the complexity of combining equity structures, compensation plans, and board governance. A Reuters report was mentioned — though sourced reluctantly — indicating that SpaceX's S1 filing includes language stating Tesla's board does not need to maintain a majority of independent directors, giving it "controlled company" status.
