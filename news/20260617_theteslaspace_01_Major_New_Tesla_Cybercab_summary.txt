This represents the first official, third-party-verified technical portrait of the vehicle ahead of commercial launch.

2. **Powertrain and Battery Details:** The Cybercab is powered by a single front-wheel drive motor producing 219 horsepower. It is paired with a battery pack of roughly 48 kWh. For comparison, these figures are described as slightly smaller, lighter, and less powerful than the base Model 3 rear-wheel drive standard trim.

3. **Range Figures Exceed Expectations:** Despite the modest battery size, the EPA certification documents list an equivalent all-electric range of 418 miles and a highway range of 375 miles — notably higher than the base Model 3. The video notes that official EPA ratings typically come in significantly lower than pre-certification figures, but suggests Tesla is on track to meet its own stated target of 300 miles or more.

4. **Vehicle Weight and Payload Capacity:** The Cybercab weighs 3,113 lbs at curb weight, with a gross vehicle weight of 3,730 lbs, yielding just over 600 lbs of payload capacity. The video frames this practically: enough for two 200-lb adults and approximately 200 lbs of luggage — sufficient for airport travel with multiple large suitcases.

5. **Interior Design Optimized for Actual Trip Patterns:** Cybercab lead engineer Eric E, speaking at a Tesla enthusiast meetup in December 2024, explained that roughly 80% of vehicle trips involve two passengers or fewer. Tesla designed the Cybercab around that reality rather than the occasional edge-case family road trip. With no second row, interior legroom is described as exceeding many traditional sedans despite the compact exterior. Engineers also stated the vehicle can accommodate large suitcases, golf bags, bicycles, and certain foldable wheelchairs.

6. **Testing Mileage and Certified Useful Life:** The vehicle Tesla submitted for EPA testing had already accumulated more than 2,100 miles on its odometer at the time of submission. The EPA certified it for a useful life of 150,000 miles — treating it not as an experimental prototype but as a production vehicle expected to spend years transporting passengers on public roads.

7. **Unboxed Manufacturing Process as a Structural Advantage:** The Cybercab is described as the first vehicle in Tesla's lineup built using the "unboxed" process, which assembles major vehicle sections in parallel before joining them at the end. The video draws a parallel to the Ford Model T, arguing that the Cybercab's potential historical significance lies not just in the vehicle itself but in pairing a purpose-built design with a manufacturing system capable of producing it at unprecedented scale and low cost.
