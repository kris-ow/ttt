The video reports on a leak and job-posting analysis suggesting Tesla's Optimus training program is larger and more established than previously disclosed, with camera-suited data collectors operating in Palo Alto, Fremont, Austin, and Giga Berlin. The host builds a theory that Tesla's hiring pattern — 15 cities, many overlapping with robotaxi markets — points to service centers and robotaxi depots as the next training ground for Optimus, while cautioning that this link is unconfirmed.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Palo Alto "robot university" leak:** Industry commentator Robert Scoble said (per his own account, not confirmed by Tesla) that Tesla employees at its Palo Alto R&D center have for months worn roughly 24-pound suits with 40 cameras and 3D sensor grids, working in shifts around the clock, with similar efforts reportedly seen at a Texas plant; teams reportedly collect both home data (e.g., making a sandwich) and shop-floor data (e.g., installing HVAC systems in cars). Scoble did not disclose his source, and the specific numbers (40 cameras, 24 pounds) are his figures, not Tesla's. The underlying hiring program itself predates this leak — Tesla job postings for this role date back to at least 2024, and Business Insider reported on a Palo Alto lab with a five-camera helmet and backpack setup roughly a year earlier.
2. **Giga Berlin data collection confirmed:** Handelsblatt reported in July that an internal Tesla memo described selected Giga Berlin employees wearing shoulder-mounted cameras during assembly work; the plant confirmed in September (via a statement obtained by a Tesla-focused tracker) that from mid-August, selected Berlin-Brandenburg teams joined "global training for Optimus," wearing backpacks and helmets with cameras, and that an Optimus prototype was seen behind frosted glass inside the factory.
3. **Worker pushback and compliance questions:** The host notes reports that some Fremont and Austin workers refused to wear the data-collection suits, reportedly objecting to training a robot that could replace them; in Germany, Handelsblatt reported the works council (an employee body with a say on workplace monitoring) had not yet been consulted on the camera program, raising potential privacy/labor-law friction that could slow data collection.
4. **Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy on the data strategy:** Across the Lex Friedman podcast (circa August of a prior year), the Cheeky Pints podcast with John Collison and Dwarkesh Patel, and Tesla's October 21 earnings call, Musk described building an "Optimus Academy" of roughly 10,000–20,000 self-learning robots that practice real-world tasks, combined with millions of simulated robots to close the sim-to-real gap. Ashok Elluswamy, who leads Tesla's AI effort including Optimus work since the prior year, explained that unlike FSD — where customer driving data directly trains the car's "incarnation" — Optimus has no equivalent fleet, so it must first learn by watching humans perform tasks, then refine through what he called a large reinforcement learning loop combining simulation and real-world independent practice; he said Tesla is "very close to getting great results" in this loop.
5. **Elluswamy skeptical of rival robot demos:** In September, after a viral video of a Chinese robot dancing, Elluswamy posted on X that such robots' movements are essentially remote-controlled and performed "pretty blindly" while maintaining balance — a comment the host frames as showing the bar Tesla holds itself to for credible demos.
6. **Job board shows ~45 data-collection roles across 15 cities:** The host's review of Tesla's careers site found about 360 open Optimus-related positions overall, with roughly 45 specifically for data-collection operators and managers across 15 US cities — most without a Tesla factory, including Dallas, San Antonio, Tampa, Orlando, Doral/Miami, Tempe/Phoenix, Henderson/Las Vegas, Draper, Littleton/Denver, San Diego, and Marina del Rey, plus Fremont, Palo Alto, and Austin. Listings specify operators wear motion-capture gear, walk 7+ hours daily carrying up to 30 pounds, and must be 5'7"–6'3" tall (Optimus is about 5'8"). Separate listings for an "Implementation Program Manager" (scouting new data-collection sites and external partners) and an "Optimus fleet coordinator" suggest Tesla is planning additional, undisclosed external collection sites.
7. **Host's theory — robotaxi depots as next training ground (unconfirmed):** The host argues that since the data-collection cities overlap heavily with robotaxi markets (and with six newly posted "lead robotaxi charging specialist" roles in Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Miami, and Orlando), Optimus's next practical job may be connecting robotaxis to chargers at depots overnight. This reading stems from a Munro Live interview this week in which Tesla's Lars Moravy, discussing Cybertruck/Cybercab charging, said Tesla uses a charging port because it needs existing infrastructure and that "hopefully Optimus will help us with that in the near future," possibly servicing cars or doing delivery tasks — framed by Moravy as "maybe," not a confirmed plan. The host stresses this entire depot-service connection is his own speculation, not something Tesla has stated.
8. **Fremont as near-term Academy production source:** Tesla's Q2 report said first Optimus units would go to the "Optimus Academy" for training and data collection, and the Q3 presentation (October 21) called Fremont-line robots the first production-quality units on a high-volume line; Musk said Tesla has "the vast majority" of a planned million-unit-a-year line running in Fremont now, but reaching 20,000 units a week will take at least a year (possibly longer) due to roughly 10,000 manufacturing process steps needing to work reliably.
9. **Giga Texas Optimus factory progress:** A drone-footage-focused observer (commonly covering Giga Texas construction) reported the new Optimus factory there, described as over 7 million square feet, is about 40% complete, with small-scale production targeted for late 2027 and scale-up in 2028, per Tesla. The host speculates (without confirmation from Tesla) that the Academy itself may eventually relocate to this Texas facility.
10. **Compute and chip infrastructure:** Tesla's Q3 presentation set a goal of reaching 400 MW of "SI" (superintelligence) training capacity by year-end to support both autonomy and humanoid robot software, up from a combined roughly 225 MW (Cortex 1 at just over 105 MW, Cortex 2 at just over 120 MW) — meaning roughly 175 MW of additional capacity needs to be added in one quarter. Musk said the AI5 chip, offering an order-of-magnitude more compute than AI4, is expected in Optimus around April of next year, and that he halved AI5's memory spec to enable sufficient production volume; he called an analysis questioning robots' future memory needs "ridiculous," saying robot memory needs will exceed anything else on Earth. He also called the Terafab chip facility in Austin — installing equipment in the first half of next year, with a first test chip expected around May/June and production chips around Q3 2028 — an "incredibly important," possibly essential, asset for scaling, especially for Optimus.
11. **Bearish counterpoints and realism checks:** John Carmack argued this week that no robot in the world can currently do a task as simple as changing batteries in a jackhammer; Musk responded by describing a much higher bar (a robot assembling a watch with one hand while catching a fast-pitched baseball with the other) that he acknowledged no robot, including Optimus, has approached. Musk also said the robot's arm remains the hardest unsolved engineering problem, and it has been reported that a significant portion of early V3 Optimus units required rework off the line. The host notes Optimus release timelines have slipped multiple times before and that external customer sales are unlikely for at least a year (around late 2027, per Musk's own estimate), with the first 10,000–20,000 units going mostly to internal use — meaning near-term ramp won't show up in Tesla's profits. Tesla's October 19 filing reportedly ties stock awards for key employees to Optimus production and deployment milestones.
