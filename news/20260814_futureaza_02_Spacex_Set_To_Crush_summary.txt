This episode of the weekly "Beat Podcast" mixes promotion for the hosts' own "Starships Were Meant to Fly" (XT Takeover) event with a roundup of Tesla and SpaceX news: a resolved multi-year Swedish union strike against Tesla, a Cybertruck power-conversion-system (PCS) warranty extension and reimbursement program, falling Tesla battery-replacement costs, rising FSD take rates, and continued Cybercab/robotaxi expansion in Houston, Las Vegas, and other markets, plus a discussion of SpaceX Starlink's V3 satellite bandwidth gains and integration into Tesla vehicles.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck PCS warranty extended after reliability issue:** A Tesla executive statement (read by a co-host) said Tesla identified that PCS electronics in pre-2026 Cybertrucks did not meet reliability standards and have been redesigned; the 2026 model's PCS warranty is now 7 years/70,000 miles (up from 50,000 miles), and 2024–2025 models get 8 years/150,000 miles. Owners who previously paid out of pocket for PCS failures are reportedly being contacted for reimbursement checks; one co-host said he personally paid for a PCS failure at about 90,000 miles and was told by the team he spoke with that refunds are being issued directly to affected owners.
2. **Cybertruck resale values holding up:** A co-host cited observations that early "foundation series" Cybertrucks are showing trade-in values reportedly over $70,000 more than two and a half years after launch, which the hosts argue undercuts predictions of steep depreciation; they also discussed that Tesla's overall used-vehicle values are rising, partly attributed to the end of federal EV tax credits pushing up new (and thus used) vehicle pricing.
3. **Model 3 long-range battery replacement price cut:** Tesla quietly lowered the listed price for a Model 3 long-range replacement battery pack from about $14,500 to about $8,000, per the hosts, who cited a Tesla-tracking analysis of roughly 31,000 used Tesla listings finding only about 0.17% had lost more than 25% of original range, with the average used Tesla retaining over 90% of original battery capacity.
4. **Sweden Tesla strike ends:** A roughly three-year strike by a small number of unionized Tesla service employees in Sweden (organized via the union commonly called Mattal by the hosts) has ended after Tesla reportedly bought out the contracts of the remaining approximately 80 striking employees; sympathy actions during the strike had blocked mail delivery (license plates), supercharger inspections/activation, and janitorial services at Tesla facilities. Despite the disruption, the Model Y remained the number three best-selling vehicle in Sweden this year per one co-host.
5. **FSD take rate rising:** Hosts cited take-rate figures showing an increase from 11% in 2024 to 15.2% currently (implying new-vehicle take rate is higher than the blended figure), framed as a recurring-revenue trend valuable to Wall Street; one co-host argued FSD margin could let Tesla exceed the total per-unit profit margin of some legacy automakers within about a year of ownership.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Bus lane detection added to FSD:** An FSD veteran observed that the system can now visually recognize and display bus lanes, a new UI/detection capability the hosts had not previously seen.
2. **Anecdotal safety and adoption commentary:** A co-host relayed a story from a comedy-podcast producer (a long-time tech early-adopter, unnamed public figure per transcript) who claims FSD drives better than most humans and feels safer than being driven by an Uber driver or his wife; hosts also noted a personal example of helping a friend add FSD to a used 2020 Model 3 after seeing viral videos.
3. **Suggestions for future FSD trial structure:** Hosts speculated Tesla could improve trial engagement by charging a small fee (e.g., under $10/month) rather than offering free trials, or by triggering situational trial offers (e.g., during unexpectedly long commutes) to demonstrate value — these are the hosts' proposals, not announced Tesla plans.

## Robotaxi
1. **Houston robotaxi lot expansion:** A large lot in Houston reportedly holds around 200 Model Ys and Cybercabs combined, per a figure the hosts attributed to a query to Grok; testing is also expanding in Sacramento and the Seattle region.
2. **Las Vegas robotaxi approval:** Las Vegas/Clark County has approved deployment of 10 robotaxis to start, restricted initially to rides to/from the Las Vegas Strip; Waymo has also applied for a permit in the same market. Hosts noted this could showcase Tesla autonomy to the large volume of international tourists concentrated on the Strip, distinct from the existing Vegas Loop tunnel system.
3. **Zoox pricing observed in Vegas:** Hosts reported personally taking a roughly 30-minute Zoox ride around the Las Vegas Strip during the week Zoox began charging $15–$25 per ride.
4. **San Francisco supercharger site:** A new 124-stall supercharger site in San Francisco has only two bathrooms, leading hosts to speculate the site may be intended for mixed use or robotaxi support rather than public use only.

## Energy
1. **Home solar/Powerwall economics anecdote:** A tech-industry figure (a comedy-podcast producer, unnamed) described earning about $500/month from selling solar electricity back to the grid in Texas after installing Tesla solar roof tiles and multiple Powerwalls, alongside owning three Teslas and a Hummer EV.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **V3 satellite bandwidth increase:** Hosts discussed that the jump from Starlink V2 to V3 satellites could yield roughly 100x more total bandwidth — described as a combination of about a 10x increase in usable spectrum (from acquiring Hughesnet's larger spectrum allotment after Hughesnet's bankruptcy) and about a 10x per-satellite throughput increase (V3 satellites can simulate multiple satellites via directional beam-pointing). A figure attributed to Sawyer Merritt/Elon Musk was cited: Starship could put up to roughly 100,000 V3 Starlink satellites into orbit, up from about 10,000 V2 satellites currently, with Musk reportedly expecting the total constellation (including direct-to-cell) to exceed 100,000 satellites.
2. **Starlink integrated into production Cybercabs:** Starlink connectivity is now reportedly fully integrated into production Cybercabs, enabling high-bandwidth features (e.g., streaming/recording up to 4K) and maintaining connectivity during power outages; described as an interim solution ahead of future direct-to-cell capability.
3. **Speaker's own estimate on Falcon-delivered Starlink's addressable market:** One co-host relayed a claim from unnamed former SpaceX engineers that a Falcon-delivered Starlink network alone (excluding V3 or Starship-launched capacity) could become the world's largest ISP outside China, requiring roughly 340 million subscribers per the host's figure, and that with Starship-scale capacity (~100x more), the network could serve roughly half the world's population — this is the host's own framing of a secondhand estimate, not a confirmed SpaceX figure.
