This is a recap of Tesla's Q3 earnings call, with Elon Musk, CFO Vaibhav Taneja, Lars Moravy, and Ashok Elluswamy covering robotaxi scaling in Austin, early Optimus production in Fremont, FSD attach rates, energy margins, and updated capex and financing guidance. The core thesis is that Tesla is entering a new growth phase after a flat period, driven by autonomy (Cybercab, FSD) and robotics (Optimus), while management stresses many execution hurdles remain before these businesses scale meaningfully.

## Robotaxi
1. **Austin Cybercab fleet up 10x since launch:** Musk said the Cybercab fleet in Austin has grown by a factor of 10 since its launch about a month prior, with cumulative autonomous miles growing exponentially week-over-week; he described the weekly mileage chart as looking "like a rocket going to orbit."
2. **Cumulative unsupervised miles up 8x in the quarter:** Taneja said cumulative unsupervised miles grew 8x in Q3 with weekly double-digit growth throughout the quarter; Alex (Bank of America) cited unsupervised miles growing at a high-teens weekly rate since late July, which management confirmed they expect to sustain for the foreseeable future.
3. **Dallas launch next month; expansion to six cities:** Elluswamy said Tesla will launch Cybercab robotaxi service in Dallas within a month, using the same software stack scaled to additional cities alongside Model Y robotaxis; the service currently operates in six cities, expanding operating hours (currently expanding toward roughly 2 a.m.) toward eventual 24/7 operation and higher-speed roads by year-end.
4. **Customer FSD unsupervised robotaxi participation targeted ~Q2 next year:** Elluswamy said Tesla expects to turn on the first customer-owned vehicles as part of the robotaxi fleet around Q2 of next year (2027), pending the same V15 software release and geofence/fleet-management integration.
5. **Cybercab for customer ownership targeted by end of 2026:** Moravy said Tesla is trying to get Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, though currently all units produced in Giga Texas are going to Tesla's own robotaxi fleet; Cybercab is expected to remain primarily a Tesla-owned "transport as service" product rather than one sold to individual owners, per Musk.
6. **Unit economics targeting 30 cents per mile, not yet reached:** Taneja said Tesla is not yet at its target cost of 30 cents per paid robotaxi mile but sees "a path" to get there through fleet scaling and operational learning (pickup/drop-off, cleaning, etc.).
7. **Cybercab line has 50% fewer parts; listed capacity understates true potential:** Moravy said Cybercab has 50% fewer parts than any other Tesla vehicle built, calling it one of the easiest products to scale manufacturing; the publicly listed production capacity (over 125,000 units/year) reflects the current running rate, not the line's true maximum capacity, which he said is significantly higher.
8. **Revenue/profitability from robotaxi expected to become material in 2027:** Management said robotaxi likely won't move the needle this quarter but should become "noticeable" around Q2 2027 and "a big deal" by Q3 or Q4 2027, based on sustained exponential growth in unsupervised miles.
9. **Pricing strategy aims to undercut ride-share and take majority of passenger miles:** Musk said Tesla's robotaxi pricing is structurally lower than ride-share competitors due to not needing a driver and manufacturing its own vehicles, and said he is confident Tesla will "end up doing a majority of all passenger miles on Earth" in markets where it's allowed to operate freely, barring government restrictions protecting domestic industry.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Early production started on high-volume Optimus line in Fremont:** Musk said Tesla has started early production on a high-volume Optimus line in Fremont, with most of the equipment for a million-unit-per-line production line already installed, but expects it will take at least a year, possibly longer, to reach an annualized rate of 20,000 units a week.
2. **External shipments targeted around end of next year:** Musk said he expects AI, electromechanical design, and reliability/duration issues to be resolved around the end of next year (2027), which is when Tesla would begin delivering Optimus units to companies outside Tesla; initial customers are expected to be industrial applications with dangerous or repetitive 24/7 tasks.
3. **Training data bootstrapped via "Optimus Academy" self-play, not crowdsourcing:** Musk and Elluswamy said Optimus lacks a large fleet to source training data (unlike cars), so Tesla plans to build roughly 10,000–20,000 bots to perform self-play in an "Optimus Academy," combining human-demonstration-based initial training with reinforcement learning in simulation and the real world.
4. **New non-scripted demo video planned within a month or two:** Musk said Tesla plans to release an Optimus video showing non-pre-programmed, AI-driven task performance (as opposed to canned, deterministic demos) within about a month or two.
5. **AI5 chip integration targeted around April 2027:** Musk said integrating Tesla's AI5 chip into Optimus — offering roughly an order of magnitude more compute than AI4 — is targeted for around April of next year, calling it a major capability improvement given the far greater degrees of freedom a humanoid robot must handle versus a car.
6. **Hand design remains the primary hardware challenge:** An Optimus engineer (referred to as "Costas" in the call) and Musk said most of the electromechanical system has converged to a workable design, but the hand remains the most difficult area due to bespoke gears and the challenge of balancing durability with the delicate, sensor-laden dexterity needed.
7. **Second Optimus factory under construction in Austin:** Taneja said Tesla has started constructing an additional Optimus factory in Austin, intended to have greater capacity than the Fremont facility, alongside continuing parallel work on hardware design, software, and supply chain.
8. **Optimus unit costs trending toward Cybercab cost levels:** Taneja said Optimus costs are trending well internally and are approaching levels similar to what Tesla expects Cybercab unit costs to be, though it's too early to finalize given ongoing design iteration.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD paid attach rate over 75% in North America:** Taneja said over 75% of North American deliveries in Q3 included a paid FSD subscription sign-up at delivery, up about 20% from Q2; globally, FSD has surpassed 1.7 million paid customers, with 50% now on subscriptions for the first time.
2. **Europe approval push and incentive sales ahead of full approval:** Musk said FSD is approved in many European countries with hope for EU-wide approval possibly within a few months; Tesla is offering incentives on certain European car sales to build fleet presence ahead of broader approval.
3. **New AI-based mapping/navigation system in development:** Elluswamy said Tesla is building its own AI-based mapping and navigation architecture (rather than relying on third-party maps), integrated with voice control and personalized/fleet-based route learning, with early elements rolling out as part of the V15 release.
4. **FSD economics framed as nearly $1B annualized revenue (unconfirmed by Tesla):** An analyst (Pierre, New Street) estimated FSD's annual recurring revenue at roughly $1 billion based on ~$1,000+/year per subscriber; Taneja confirmed the analyst's figures were "correct" but did not independently disclose exact FSD revenue, saying Tesla will share more detailed metrics (including churn/retention) as the business scales.

## Energy
1. **Megapack nearly sold out through 2027; margins to improve:** Musk said Tesla is almost sold out of Megapack capacity for 2027 and is ramping production to meet demand, expecting gross margins to increase meaningfully.
2. **Q3 energy storage deployments of 13.7 GWh; margins jumped to 33.1%:** Taneja said Tesla had its second-best quarter of deployments at 13.7 GWh, with energy gross margins improving from a prior level to 33.1%, driven partly by tariff-related fund recognition and warranty true-downs from a vendor cost reimbursement.
3. **Strong data-center-driven demand; "megapod" concept still early:** Management said the energy business is seeing extreme demand from data centers; a potential "megapod" compute/power product mentioned previously remains early-stage, with more clarity expected around Q1–Q2.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record delivery backlog; Model Y leads in key markets:** Taneja said Tesla exited Q3 with its highest backlog in four years, constrained by production rather than demand; Model Y was preliminarily the top-selling BEV in Europe and top-selling passenger car in South Korea, with records also set in Australia, Japan, and Taiwan.
2. **Automotive gross margin (ex-credits) improved to 18.1%:** Margins rose sequentially from 16.3% to 18.1%, attributed to a higher delivery mix in APAC, despite headwinds from commodity price increases and interest rates.
3. **5 million vehicles/year target by approximately 2030:** Musk reiterated Tesla's aim to build capacity for roughly 5 million vehicles annually by around 2030, citing ongoing manufacturing capacity investments including new factories (e.g., the new semi-truck factory in Reno) and Berlin expansion permits.

## Financials
1. **Capex guidance revised to ~$20 billion for the year; new $30B credit facility secured:** Taneja said Tesla cut its 2026 capex guidance to approximately $20 billion due to efficient capital deployment, while securing capacity to borrow up to $30 billion to support future growth; Tesla does not plan to draw on the new facilities this calendar year.
2. **Free cash flow positive at over $1.5 billion; cash balance over $45 billion:** Tesla ended the quarter with free cash flow above $1.5 billion and a total cash and investments balance exceeding $45 billion.
3. **Net income affected by SpaceX mark-to-market loss and Bitcoin gain:** Taneja said net income was impacted by a $142 million mark-to-market reduction on Tesla's SpaceX holding, partially offset by an approximately $290 million mark-to-market gain on Bitcoin holdings.
4. **Vehicle deliveries topped 480K for two consecutive quarters:** Taneja highlighted this as a first for Tesla, alongside the start of Megapack deliveries using in-house LFP battery cells.
5. **Capex expected to rise into the tens of billions by 2028:** Management indicated capex related to AI compute, semiconductor fab build-out, and robotaxi/Optimus capacity expansion would become more meaningful next year and reach a noticeable tens-of-billions level by 2028, with semicap supply constraints not yet having a major impact on the current budget.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Terafab research fab progressing; capital needs in the tens of billions, rising above $100B long term:** Musk said the Austin-based Terafab research fab will begin commissioning machinery in the first half of next year, targeting a first test chip around May or June, with aspirational volume production of a useful chip around Q3 2028; capital investment will be in the tens of billions through early 2028 and likely exceed $100 billion long term, with the split between SpaceX and Tesla funding still to be determined.
