This is a used-EV dealership review video from the "futureaza" channel's Plat Auto series, featuring a test drive of a used Cadillac Lyriq with dealer host Jack. The bulk of the content covers the Lyriq's features, pricing, an Oregon state EV rebate program, and Nepal's EV market — with Tesla only entering via brief comfort comparisons and no discussion of Tesla's or SpaceX's core investment thesis.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cadillac Lyriq comfort compared favorably to Tesla Model Y:** The hosts repeatedly state the used 2024 Cadillac Lyriq (priced around $35,000, ~21,000 miles) is far more comfortable than a 2023 Tesla Model Y, describing the Lyriq's ride as "couch comfort" versus the Model Y's "office chair comfort," and noting the Model Y felt "bouncy and bumpy" by comparison; one host says he hasn't spent much time in newer Model Ys so couldn't compare those directly.
2. **Tesla seats sold secondhand as office chairs:** One host mentions owning a genuine Tesla seat as an office chair, sourced from a seller near Reading who reportedly bought factory Tesla seats and resold them until Tesla shut the operation down at one location, after which the seller allegedly continued at another location.
3. **Nepal's EV market is almost entirely Chinese-brand, no Tesla presence:** The host discusses an upcoming trip to Nepal's NADA auto show, noting Nepal's new-car market shifted from roughly single-digit percent EV to about 87% EV share within five years, driven by import tax policy (140% tax on gas cars vs. 15–30% on EVs) and cheap hydroelectric power (~$0.56/kWh cited). The market is described as almost entirely Chinese EV brands plus two Indian brands, with no U.S., European, Japanese, or Korean EV brands currently sold there (Korea reportedly has a knockdown plant planned but not yet operating). A Fremont-built Model Y Performance is noted as the first Model Y in Nepal, imported at high cost and modified with larger, more road-appropriate wheels and a lift due to poor road conditions.
