## Brief

- Tesla Q2 deliveries beat: 481,000 vs. ~460,000 expected
- UBS raises Tesla PT to $442 on physical AI thesis
- RBC raises Tesla PT to $500 on SpaceX-merger scenario
- NHTSA chief open to dropping steering-wheel mandate
- Megapack bookings top $9B, 43 GWh in six weeks
- Giga Texas drone footage shows 50+ Cybercabs shipped

## Financials
- Q2 deliveries came in at 481,000, above the ~460,000 consensus estimate — Tesla's best-ever second quarter and fourth-best quarter overall (per futureaza).
- EPS estimates for the quarter range from ~$0.63 (flat auto margins) to ~$0.90 (strong FSD uptake), versus $0.40 last quarter; each 1-point margin gain adds roughly six cents per share.
- Inventory overhang fell from ~50,000 vehicles entering the quarter to ~20,000 exiting it, converting stored production cost directly into free cash flow.
- Tesla's SpaceX stake, bought under $100/share and now marked near $160/share, is estimated to add ~$0.35/share to EPS this quarter, partly offset by a small Bitcoin markdown.
- RBC raised its Tesla price target to $500 (from $475), explicitly baking in a 25–30% premium for a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger — a rationale both hosts on brighterwithherbert called into question.

## Autonomous Driving
- Paid FSD subscriptions rose ~170,000 quarter-over-quarter, a high-margin revenue driver at near-zero marginal cost (per futureaza).
- NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison said the agency will "absolutely" consider dropping steering-wheel requirements for driverless vehicles, calling manual controls senseless for vehicles never meant to be human-driven — a direct regulatory tailwind for the wheel-less Cybercab.

## Robotaxi
- Driverless Cybercabs spotted in Austin navigating apartment/parking-lot entrances; separate test-track footage showed vehicles running self-diagnostic NVH checks that auto-report defects back to the assembly line.
- Giga Texas drone footage showed strong Model Y output plus more than 50 additional Cybercabs shipped out (unofficial, via Airwave Dynamics).
- UBS's Joseph Spak raised his Tesla PT to $442 (from $364), citing the robotaxi/Optimus "physical AI" thesis as a core long-term driver.
- A Tesla impact report details Cybercab's cost-cutting design: two-seat layout (justified by >85% of ride-hail trips carrying two or fewer riders), no steering wheel/pedals, parallel "unboxed" manufacturing, injection-molded panels replacing the paint shop (cutting paint cycle time from hours to minutes, 35% lower related emissions, zero VOCs), and a new drive unit reducing reliance on Chinese-sourced critical minerals.

## Energy
- Megapack bookings surged: more than $9 billion and 43+ GWh of new projects announced over six weeks — roughly matching a full year's prior deployment pace (49 GWh trailing-12-month).

## Electric Vehicles
- LG Energy is expanding cylindrical battery capacity at its Nanjing factory citing rising Tesla Model Y demand, with existing lines reportedly running at full utilization.
- QX Logistix, a Southern California logistics provider, is adding 20 Tesla Semis to its fleet.

## Market & Competition
- xAI-SpaceX (merged Feb 2026, ~$1.25T deal) went public as SPCX on June 12 at ~$1.7T, surging to ~$2.4T within a week; Wall Street targets now cluster $200–225 (Goldman, JPMorgan) with an outlier $800 (Raymond James) — a valuation debate the hosts flagged as unusually wide.
- Musk exercised ~304M Tesla options on June 17 at $23/share, lifting his voting power from ~13% to ~20% (toward a stated ~25% goal), fueling speculation (Motley Fool, farzad-fm) about an eventual Tesla-SpaceX-xAI combination — though legal structure (Nevada vs. Delaware incorporation, minority-shareholder protections) is argued to make such a merger far harder to execute than the 2016 SolarCity deal.
- Terafab, the Tesla/SpaceX/xAI chip megafab near Austin, has seen cost estimates balloon from ~$25B (March 2026) to as much as $120B all-in (May 2026), with Morgan Stanley projecting meaningful output not until 2028–2030 versus Tesla's own 2027 volume-production target.
- Competitive signals: Mercedes unveiled an electric CLA AMG with simulated engine sounds/gear shifts (up to 416 miles WLTP range); 1X unveiled new tendon-driven humanoid hands (25 DOF, IP68, made in USA) — a direct Optimus hand-development benchmark. Lucid's ~4,000-unit quarterly deliveries were flagged as too low for meaningful parts pricing leverage.
