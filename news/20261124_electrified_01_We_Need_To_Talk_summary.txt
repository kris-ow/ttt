Host Dillon Loomis reviews early-access reports on FSD V15, weighing real-world driving behavior against the hype around the new visualization. His thesis is that V15 is one of the most polished first releases yet, with parking still the main weakness. He argues the real story is the new architecture and the in-house generated visualization, and he also covers the AI4/AI4.5/AI5 hardware roadmap, Cybercab ride economics, and EU approval progress.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **V15 driving behavior is mostly positive, with parking letdowns:** Early testers report V15 is somewhat more cautious around pedestrians. Pothole avoidance is getting strong reviews, and the turn-signal sound and a reduced nag at faster profiles (Hurry, Mad Max) are popular, though the host calls the nag change subjective. Complaints include stop-and-go creeping, skittishness at red lights, some phantom braking, and minor navigation issues. The host expects the navigation issues to persist until Tesla builds its own in-house map system. He says regressions have been few compared with the V14 launch.
2. **Parking features fall short of Elon Musk's claims:** You cannot pick a specific spot while FSD is engaged, only when driving manually. FSD can offer different lots or garages, sometimes including street options. There is no back-in versus head-first option, and V15 still parks in handicap spots at times. It does not always stop on the visualized chevron (in one video it parked six spots away), and it does not read overhead signs. Banish is not yet available. Home parking preferences are being remembered well. Mike P reported it backing into cone-marked spots, getting stuck in reverse, and looping in a garage exit and a blocked lot.
3. **Chuck Cook's chain test:** V15 failed, performing the same as V14 despite release notes saying chain handling would improve. School-zone behavior is untested so far.
4. **Visualization limitations and fixes:** Text in the visualization is blurry and largely illegible. There are occasional false renders, such as a window reflection drawn as a vehicle. Some testers already prefer night mode because the new view is distracting. The visualization goes away in Smart Summon, which Dirty Tesla thinks may be slightly worse on V15. The "reason for disengagement" popup is gone. Tesla has said it will fix the purple haze at night and add tap-to-direct control. Musk said latency is high due to compute constraints, and interleaving universal vision encoder features along the diffusion transformer layers should cut roughly 35% of it. The view currently lags reality by about half a second.
5. **Rollout status:** V15 appears limited to Model Y vehicles with the front bumper camera. The host has not seen it on Model 3, S/X, or Cybertruck, or on vehicles without that camera. Dirty Tesla covered the bumper camera and both FSD and the visualization still worked. Tesla Scope believes that if there is no further wave before Thanksgiving, nothing will move until December, with a goal of reaching over 70% of eligible vehicles before Christmas. The host hopes wide release lands in the holiday update. Grok voice integration only sets or adjusts the destination pin and cannot pick parking spots or change lanes.
6. **In-house reality view architecture:** The host says Tesla's reality view is generated by the same neural network that drives the car rather than Unreal Engine. It uses about 8% of AI4's neural accelerator capacity: half of one of six trip cores, or 1/12. Tesla also removed several separate models that existed only for visualization.
7. **EU approval progress:** Italy became country number 12 to approve FSD, the first major EU country, bringing population coverage to 28.7% against the 65% needed. The host relays a rumor that the early-December EU TCMV vote has been delayed with no new date set.

## Hardware and Humanoid Bots
1. **AI5 and AI4.5 roadmap:** Musk said AI5 is going to Optimus, an order-of-magnitude hardware performance improvement, while cars stay on AI4, which he says is enough for superhuman driving. AI4.5 (also called 4.1 or 4+) is a relatively small improvement driven by a RAM upgrade to LPDDR5X, expected around mid-next-year (2027). Musk said RAM is expensive, so AI4.5 was cut from 32 GB to 24 GB, and that memory bandwidth, not capacity, is AI4's real constraint. He said it is hard to tell whether RAM supply will catch up soon.
2. **Cost headwind:** Musk said AI4 now costs twice as much due to memory and storage price hikes. In March he put AI4 at about $650, so the host estimates it could now be well over $1,200 per chip, a margin headwind. The host's rough estimate is that the 24 GB cut saves Tesla about $500 million given over a million Model Y sold per year.
3. **Optimus and radar comments:** Musk said Optimus will be Tesla's finest product ever and the best product of all time. The host views it as an economic needle-mover unlike the Cybertruck. Musk said adding radar reduces effective signal-to-noise, that Tesla built its own radar that is on some Model X and S units but turned off, and that real-world AI intelligence will grow roughly an order of magnitude per year.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab ride economics:** Robin took 18 Cybercab rides in Austin totaling 484 minutes for $265. That works out to just under $33 per hour, or about $72,000 gross per vehicle per year at roughly 25% utilization (6 hours a day). At 12 hours a day it would be about $145,000. The host says Tesla sometimes charges $3-$4 per mile at high-demand times on Cybercab and some Model Y rides, and questions how sustainable the premium is once the novelty fades and supply grows. The Model Y robotaxi app also gained seat-fold and movement buttons and new climate controls.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Native Starlink in Teslas:** Musk said native Starlink integration in all Tesla models should hopefully be available toward the end of next year (2027), to give unsupervised FSD passengers high bandwidth for work and entertainment. It will not come to China because Starlink is illegal there. The host notes it could slip into early 2028.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Reported internal AI pressure at SpaceX:** Per reporting the host relays, Musk told SpaceX employees the company is falling behind in AI development, temporarily renamed an internal chat "sell compute" in August, and now requires engineers to justify GPU use. The host dismisses it as nothing new, since Musk has said publicly that they are at least a few months behind the frontier.
