This episode of The Tesla Space covers three main topics: Tesla Semi's apparent preparation for Full Self-Driving capability (evidenced by a recent sensor-rig sighting), the upcoming North American launch of the Model YL, and a triple update from The Boring Company across its tunneling projects. The video's core thesis is that Tesla has been quietly building toward autonomous trucking since the Semi's 2017 unveiling, and that recent hardware and data-collection activity suggests that long-term plan is now accelerating.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla Semi spotted with ground-truthing equipment:** A Tesla Semi was recently spotted in Sunnyvale, California, carrying a tall sensor rig above the cab typically associated with ground truthing — a process that creates highly accurate environmental reference data to validate what an autonomous vehicle perceives versus what is actually present. The host frames this as evidence that Tesla is actively collecting the kind of training data needed to bring autonomous capability to the Semi, though notes that the sighting alone does not confirm a rollout timeline.

2. **Semi's 2025 redesign was built with autonomy in mind:** When Tesla rolled out the Semi's updated design in late 2025, it included a revised camera layout with 10 external cameras and an interior driver monitoring camera, plus new side-mounted camera pods above the front wheel arches to reduce blind spots. The host argues these changes, taken together, indicate Tesla was deliberately building a platform suited to a modern vision-based autonomous driving system rather than simply refreshing an existing product.

3. **Highway freight as an early autonomy use case:** The video argues that long-haul trucking is, in several respects, an easier environment for autonomous driving than urban passenger vehicles, because trucks spend most of their operating life on highways with relatively predictable routes. The economic case is amplified by utilization: a long-haul truck can exceed 100,000 miles per year versus roughly 12,000 miles for a typical passenger car, meaning efficiency and safety improvements compound dramatically across a fleet.

4. **FSD safety figures cited for the Semi opportunity:** Tesla's own safety data is cited: vehicles operating with Full Self-Driving supervised engage reportedly experience one serious collision approximately every 5.3 million miles, compared to the U.S. average of one every 660,000 miles for human drivers. The host acknowledges uncertainty about whether these numbers hold at scale but notes they illustrate why Tesla is motivated to bring its autonomy stack to the Semi, given that large trucks are involved in roughly 5,000–6,000 fatal crashes annually in the U.S., with the majority of fatalities occurring in the other vehicle.

5. **Convoy mode ambition traces back to 2017:** At the Semi's original 2017 unveiling, Tesla introduced the concept of "convoy mode," in which a lead truck driven by a human would be followed autonomously by additional Semis drafting closely to reduce wind resistance. Elon Musk claimed at the time that a three-truck convoy could bring freight costs down to approximately $0.85 per mile versus roughly $1.25 for diesel trucking, and argued the system could operate 10 times more safely than a human driver. The host positions the current ground-truthing activity as a continuation of that vision rather than a new initiative.

6. **FSD fleet milestone referenced:** Tesla's Full Self-Driving supervised fleet has now accumulated more than 11.3 billion miles of real-world experience, which the host cites as context for how far the underlying technology has matured since the Semi was first unveiled.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model YL targeting August–September North American launch:** According to a report from Not a Tesla App, Tesla is targeting an August or September 2026 launch for the Model YL in North America — a longer-wheelbase version of the Model Y already available in China, Australia, and New Zealand. The vehicle is expected to be built at Giga Texas, where tooling changes are reportedly underway. Tesla has not officially confirmed the timeline.

2. **Physical dimensions and seating configuration:** The Model YL features a wheelbase approximately 6 inches longer and an overall length roughly 7 inches greater than the standard Model Y. This extra space enables a 2-2-2 seating configuration — two captain's chairs (heated and ventilated) in the middle row and two seats in the third row — creating a genuine six-seat family vehicle rather than the cramped third-row arrangement of the standard Model Y.

3. **Market positioning and expected pricing:** The Model YL is positioned to bridge the gap between the standard Model Y and the significantly more expensive Model X, offering real third-row usability at a more accessible price point. Based on overseas market pricing, the North American version is expected to carry roughly a $4,000 premium over the current all-wheel drive Model Y, placing it around $54,000 before incentives — above the premium all-wheel drive trim but below the Model Y Performance. North American pricing has not been officially announced.

4. **Pre-launch evidence accumulating:** Earlier in spring 2026, observers spotted a Model YL body-in-white at Giga Texas; more recently, camouflage prototypes have been seen testing on public roads in both California and Florida, suggesting the program is progressing toward production readiness.

## Market & Competition *(The Boring Company)*

1. **Vegas Loop expands to UNLV campus:** The Nevada System of Higher Education Board of Regents approved an agreement for a Vegas Loop station on the UNLV campus, with The Boring Company covering construction costs and the university owning the completed station. The plan includes a 25% student discount on fares.

2. **Emergency preparedness milestone in Las Vegas:** The Boring Company completed a large-scale tunnel rescue exercise with the Clark County Fire Department inside a Vegas Loop tunnel. More than 200 firefighters and first responders have participated in over 150 hours of tunnel-specific training, which the host frames as a critical but underappreciated step in gaining regulatory and public confidence for long-term operations.

3. **Nashville Music City Loop machinery commissioned and scaling:** The Boring Company's second Prufrock boring machine (MB2) is now fully commissioned and ready to begin tunneling for the Music City Loop in Nashville. A third machine (MB3) is already scheduled to ship in August 2026. The host interprets this as a sign the company has moved past proving faster tunneling is possible and is now focused on the harder challenge of scaling across multiple cities simultaneously.
