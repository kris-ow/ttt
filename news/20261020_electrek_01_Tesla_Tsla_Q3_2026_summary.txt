This Electrek article previews Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings (reporting October 21, after market close, with a 5:30 p.m. ET call), laying out already-known delivery and deployment figures alongside Wall Street's revenue and EPS expectations, and argues Tesla's core auto business is stagnant while spending on AI/robotics bets accelerates.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Deliveries beat consensus but fell year-over-year:** Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3 2026, down 2.1% from the Q3 2025 record of 497,099, but above the 461,974 analyst consensus (compiled from 24 analysts) by more than 24,000 units. Tesla built only 464,391 vehicles, delivering 22,141 more than it produced — the second straight quarter of working down inventory after roughly 50,000 excess vehicles built in Q1. Model 3 and Model Y accounted for 478,237 deliveries, while "other models" (Cybertruck, Semi, remaining Model S/X) fell 48% year-over-year to 8,295 units. Year-to-date deliveries are up 8.8% at 1,324,681.

## Energy
2. **Storage deployments missed estimates:** Tesla deployed 13.7 GWh of energy storage in Q3, up 9.6% from 12.5 GWh a year earlier, but well short of the roughly 15.9 GWh analysts expected and below the 14.2 GWh record set in Q4 2025. Energy storage ran a 20.4% gross margin in Q2 2026, with Tesla guiding to a low-to-mid 20% range long term; the article notes the Q3 shortfall means energy won't meaningfully offset auto weakness.

## Financials
3. **Revenue expected flat despite higher volumes:** Wall Street consensus calls for $28.06 billion in Q3 revenue (Estimize: $28.36 billion), both roughly flat versus Q3 2025's $28.1 billion — an outcome the article calls decent given that 2025 figure was inflated by buyers rushing to claim the $7,500 federal EV tax credit before its September 30, 2025 expiration. Sequentially, Tesla reported $28.24 billion in Q2 2026 revenue on 480,126 deliveries and 13.5 GWh storage; with Q3 higher on both metrics, flat-to-slightly-higher revenue estimates imply lower average selling prices.
4. **EPS expected down double digits:** Analysts expect non-GAAP EPS of $0.45, down 10% from $0.50 a year ago (Estimize: $0.48; Wall Street range $0.30–$0.70). The article flags unreliability in these estimates, noting Tesla missed Q2 2026 consensus badly ($0.33 actual vs. $0.53 expected) as operating margin collapsed to 1.4%, with operating income down 57% to $398 million and operating expenses up 47% to $4.35 billion. Tesla has missed Street EPS estimates in two of the last four quarters and seen EPS decline year-over-year in five of the last seven.
5. **Margin and cash flow watch items:** Automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits fell to 16.3% in Q2 from 19.2% in Q1, which Tesla attributed to a $230 million warranty true-down and non-repeating tariff relief; the article flags this as the first number to watch in Q3. Regulatory credit revenue, pure profit, fell to just $146 million in Q2 (down 67% year-over-year) and is shrinking as automakers no longer face federal CAFE penalties. Tesla burned $1.09 billion in free cash flow in Q2 as capex more than doubled to $5.8 billion; management guided to over $25 billion in 2026 capex weighted toward the second half, leading the article to say another negative free-cash-flow quarter "wouldn't be a surprise."

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving / Humanoid Bots
6. **Top shareholder questions submitted via Say Technologies:** The Q&A drew 832 questions from about 2,860 participants representing 3.85 million shares (more participants but fewer shares than Q2's roughly 1,710 participants / 8.6 million shares). Leading questions by shares represented included: Cybercab fleet scaling and regulatory path given an open NHTSA safety review (1.2 million shares); timing for unsupervised FSD in customer cars (1.1 million shares); Cybercab customer availability (952,900 shares); unit economics per paid robotaxi mile versus a thirty-cent target (694,900 shares); whether HW3 owners get a free hardware upgrade for unsupervised FSD (652,900 shares); and Optimus per-unit cost and its first paying customer (643,600 shares).

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Tesla's core auto business is flat and overly dependent on two models, and that the quarter's outcome hinges on whether margins stabilize and opex growth slows; if not, he contends Tesla is funding expensive bets on AI, Optimus, Cybercab and chips out of a shrinking profit pool. He is critical of shareholders for focusing questions on Cybercab, FSD, Optimus and HW3 rather than the core business, calling this "delusional" and asserting the stagnation was avoidable had Musk not "bet the whole company on autonomy too early." The article also contains a sponsored solar-financing mention (EnergySage) unrelated to Tesla earnings.
