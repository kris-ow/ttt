## Robotaxi
- Tesla filed for a Nevada robotaxi permit on June 5, covering Clark County for up to 5,000 vehicles in year one, with Reid Airport service written into the application; a depot has been built on Mohawk Street and gold-liveried robotaxis have been spotted around Las Vegas, though the permit remains under review with no rides yet.
- Cybercab ramp is now expected to begin in earnest around August–September rather than earlier this year, per investor Larry Goldberg, citing regulatory, infrastructure, production-quality, and safety/PR hurdles.
- Per Tesla VP Lars Moravy (relayed by brighterwithherbert), roughly 90% of Cybercab automation is already in place, with fewer problems than anticipated; the biggest remaining training gap is pickup/drop-off logistics rather than vehicle dynamics.
- Tesla missed its stated goal of expanding robotaxi service to seven cities by June 30, currently operating in three.
- Jo Bhakdi models Tesla's 2027-28 Robotaxi EBIT potential at $5B (100K Cybercabs) to $50B (1M Cybercabs) against a ~$3.45B 2025 company EBIT baseline, arguing deployment scale-up — not manufacturing — is the key uncertainty.
- X Money (launched June 26, 6% APY, 3% cash back) is speculated as a future in-house payment rail for robotaxi fares, potentially capturing processing fees currently going to Apple Pay/Google Pay — unconfirmed by Tesla.
- Vegas Loop (Boring Company) has carried 3M+ passengers since 2021 using Tesla FSD software with a safety driver still present; fleet set to grow to 160 Teslas, with a new 5-year, $25M Las Vegas tourism authority operating deal cited as future Robovan/Cybercab demand.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus production is slated to begin at Fremont this summer on former Model S/X lines; Musk posted about the line conversion with new stations being installed.
- Per Moravy, one automated production line from Tesla's German automation subsidiary took about two weeks to set up, with 40+ additional sublines still planned — suggesting full line automation will take longer than expected. Texas is described as the real production factory, roughly a year from fully operational.
- Neuralink announced June 30 it threaded electrode implants through the dura without cutting it, in a live clinical trial; Musk called it a "very big deal" for brain-interface safety. Neuralink's head of surgery said connecting the implant to Optimus "will happen very soon," though commentators frame this as a 5-10+ year thread, not a near-term catalyst.
- SoftBank sold its ~10% Boston Dynamics stake back to Hyundai for ~$300M, implying a ~$3B valuation; futureaza hosts argue a spun-out Optimus would exceed that given Tesla's AI stack advantage.

## Financials
- FSD subscription revenue seen as a potential upside surprise on growing public FSD acceptance, per Goldberg.
- AI5 estimated more than a year away; Terafab estimated two-to-three years away, per Goldberg.
- Macrohard (Tesla/SpaceX joint project) flagged as a "dark horse" but not expected to contribute meaningful revenue in the next 12 months.
- Bhakdi calls Tesla's recent selloff to the $390s "overdone," holding a ~$410 fair-value view (down from ~$490) and disclosing a call-option purchase during the dip.

## Market & Competition
- SpaceX's Colossus 1 Memphis data-center lease to Anthropic (~$1.25B/month) implies ~$10-11B EBIT — nearly double Tesla's entire company EBIT — per Bhakdi's model; Colossus 2's partial Google lease adds ~$2.4B more.
- Bhakdi argues grid/electricity capacity, not chips or capital, is the binding constraint on further Colossus scaling, revising bull-case SpaceX EBIT upside down to ~$40B (from $100B+ claims) pending lawsuit resolution.
- Figure's robot reportedly ran ~200 continuous hours of package-sorting and was deployed at BMW per a company exec, cited as evidence the humanoid robotics category is broadly validating industry-wide.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla Japan posted its best-ever sales month in June 2026: 3,997 registrations, up 183% year-over-year.
- Goldberg expects a new Model Y variant ("Y L") to expand Tesla's addressable market by roughly a third or more, driving a significant US sales jump.
- Tesla delivered over 480,000 vehicles last quarter, called a "blowout" driven by Europe strength and steady China demand.
