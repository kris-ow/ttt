Electrek reports that Tesla has cut roughly 1,700 jobs at Gigafactory Berlin over the past year, according to internal workers' council documents cited by German newspaper Handelsblatt, contradicting repeated denials from the plant's manager.

## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Workforce decline confirmed by works council data:** Internal documents from the Tesla works council election committee, cited by Handelsblatt, show headcount at Gigafactory Berlin has fallen to 10,703, down from 12,415 at the time of the last works council election in 2024 — a drop of about 1,700 workers, or nearly 14%.
2. **Plant manager's denials contradicted:** André Thierig, the Gigafactory Berlin plant manager, reportedly denied staff reductions were occurring, including claiming as recently as last month that there were "no plans" for cuts, even as production volumes stayed flat or declined, according to the article.
3. **Unclear mechanism for cuts:** The article states it is unclear whether the reduction came through layoffs, voluntary buyouts, or non-renewal of temporary contracts — a method the article says Tesla commonly uses to shrink headcount without triggering formal mass-layoff notifications. Some attrition may stem from a global Tesla workforce reduction in 2024 (reported elsewhere as a 10% cut), but the decline reportedly continued afterward.
4. **Context — European demand and capacity:** The article ties the cuts to previously reported declining Tesla sales in Europe amid rising competition from Chinese EV makers and legacy automakers, leaving the plant (said to have capacity for 375,000+ vehicles annually) with more capacity than current regional demand requires. The article also references prior reporting that Tesla's Berlin expansion is on hold and that management has threatened to halt investment if a union (IG Metall) gains control of the workers' council.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the cuts validate concerns that Giga Berlin is overbuilt relative to demand and is "burning cash," calls the plant manager's denial despite contradicting data "classic Tesla transparency, or lack thereof," and speculates Tesla could use a potential IG Metall takeover of the workers' council as a pretext to divest from the factory. He frames this as evidence that a "unlimited demand" narrative for Europe is "officially dead" and questions whether further cuts or a slow shutdown may follow.

Notable quotes and data:
- Headcount dropped from 12,415 (2024 works council election) to 10,703 — about 1,700 fewer employees, ~14% decline.
- Thierig reportedly said there were "no plans" for staff reductions as recently as last month.
- Factory capacity cited by the author as 375,000+ vehicles/year, against sales Electrek describes as "significantly fewer" in the region.
