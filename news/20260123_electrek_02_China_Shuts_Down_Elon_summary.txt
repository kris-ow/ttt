Chinese state media outlet China Daily reports that Elon Musk's claim that Tesla FSD could be approved in China "next month" (February 2026) is "not true," citing an unnamed source described as close to the government.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Musk's Davos claim disputed:** At the World Economic Forum in Davos, Musk said he hoped FSD could be approved in Europe in February 2026, "and then maybe a similar timing for China." Tesla stock rose on the comment. Less than 24 hours later, China Daily cited a "reliable source" close to the government stating the February timeline for China approval does not align with current regulatory reality.
2. **Prior delays:** In late 2025, Musk told shareholders FSD approval in China would likely come in early 2026, itself a delay from an earlier prediction of approval "by the end of the year" (2025).
3. **Progress so far:** Tesla has reportedly cleared some data security hurdles in China, notably following Musk's visit to Beijing in April 2024, and partnered with Baidu in 2024 for lane-level mapping and navigation required under China's surveying laws. Full regulatory approval for supervised autonomous driving on public roads has not been granted.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitive pressure in China:** The article notes that Chinese competitors such as XPeng already offer capable city-driving ADAS without Tesla's data-gathering restrictions, and that rivals are bundling similar autonomous features into vehicle prices that are already lower than Tesla's.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert characterizes Musk's "next month"/"hopefully"/"maybe" framing as pattern of overly optimistic timelines that move Tesla's stock before reality (often delays of months or years) sets in. The author is skeptical Tesla can successfully sell supervised FSD in China given lower-priced competitors offering similar features, and is "not as optimistic" as shareholders about the rollout.
