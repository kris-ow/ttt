Tesla has launched a "Cell Giga Challenge" inviting outside startups to pilot technologies on its live 4680 battery cell production line at Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg, run in partnership with JUNI, a Berlin-Brandenburg startup platform backed by Germany's federal economics ministry.

## Financials
1. **Program structure and timeline:** Tesla is seeking startups working on materials, equipment, operations, automation, and AI to improve cell manufacturing "quality, speed, cost, safety, or scalability." Applications are open via Submittable through July 24, 2026, with the program starting in August 2026 and running through five phases (application, screening, technical interview, pitch day, pilot discussions); top teams may reach a paid pilot with Tesla's Grünheide cell team.
2. **Cell ramp and investment figures:** Tesla is ramping 4680 output at Grünheide toward a planned 18 GWh annual capacity. As previously reported in May, Tesla committed an additional $250 million to more than double its cell target at the site from 8 GWh to 18 GWh, pushing cumulative investment in the cell unit toward €1 billion. JUNI's materials cite "$350 million in investments announced over the last six months" — a figure the article notes is higher than Tesla's confirmed $250 million and likely bundles earlier commitments; Tesla has not broken out a single $350 million announcement.
3. **Staffing and output scale:** Tesla expects to need more than 1,500 employees for cell production alone, with output scaling through the first half of 2027. At 18 GWh, Grünheide would produce enough cells for roughly 250,000 to 350,000 vehicles per year, with plans to build both cells and complete vehicles at the site.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert offers two readings: optimistically, this is a smart open-innovation play letting Tesla crowdsource improvements from startups it might not otherwise access; skeptically, it signals that Tesla's in-house 4680 effort — long troubled by cost and yield issues, relying on suppliers like LG Energy Solution and Panasonic — still has real gaps, especially since Tesla doubled its capacity target before fully proving cost-competitive 4680 production. The author says the real test is whether pilots make it into production by 2027 or whether the challenge is mainly a talent/PR funnel.
