This is a recap of SpaceX's Q3 earnings call, featuring Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Brett (last name not given in transcript), covering Starship flight progress, Starlink and connectivity growth, the buildout of SpaceX's "super intelligence" (SI) compute business, Q3 financials, and analyst Q&A touching on chip fabrication (Terafab), compute funding, Starlink Mobile strategy, and a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 reached orbit, flight 15 targets full reuse:** Musk said Starship's flight 14 reached orbit for the first time and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites. Flight 15, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and ship — which Musk called a potentially historic first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle, though he cautioned success isn't guaranteed and framed it as "more likely than not."
2. **Cadence ramp and Louisiana spaceport:** Musk said Starship is expected to fly weekly by the first half of next year and twice weekly or more by the end of next year, as reusability improves. SpaceX is also developing a new Louisiana spaceport that Musk said could eventually have on the order of 20+ launch towers; Shotwell separately said SpaceX's internal goal is a first Starship launch from Louisiana in 2 years (around 2028), with Musk saying full-scale launching there by 2029 is "highly likely."
3. **Q3 launch cadence and milestones:** SpaceX flew 35 launches in Q3, including 21 Starlink Falcon 9 missions, two Starship flights, a Falcon Heavy (carrying NASA's Roman Space Telescope), and 12 other high-profile customer missions, including six national security launches. On October 1st, SpaceX flew three Falcon missions across three launch sites within 13 hours (Crew 13 to the ISS, a Falcon Heavy NRO mission, and a rideshare mission with 130 payloads).
4. **New contracts secured:** SpaceX won three additional Dragon human spaceflight missions (through 2028), a Starlink communications contract on NASA's Artemis 3 mission, 18 new National Security Space Launch missions, a lunar payload delivery contract via Starship, and three new Starfall (microgravity research capsule) missions on Starship.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Bandwidth and satellite scaling ambitions:** Musk said flight 14 deployed enough Starlink V3 bandwidth (26 terabits/second, roughly equivalent to 10 Falcon 9 launches) despite the ship being only about half-loaded. He projected roughly 100x the current constellation's total bandwidth within about three years via higher-bandwidth satellites and a constellation scaling toward the order of 100,000 satellites, with demand for bandwidth potentially growing a thousand-fold in 3–5 years driven by AI/SI agents.
2. **Subscriber and market growth:** Starlink ended Q3 active in 174 markets (adding Vietnam and Uganda), reaching just under 14 million global broadband consumer subscribers (13.6 million per CFO figures) after adding 1.6 million in the quarter. Starlink Mobile surpassed 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter.
3. **Aviation and enterprise growth:** Starlink is now contracted for roughly 12,000 planes (about a quarter of available aircraft), with agreements across three of four major US carriers and several international airlines (Lufthansa, Swiss, Copa, Korean Air, Austrian, Asiana flew first Starlink-equipped aircraft in Q3). Aviation production rate has quadrupled since the start of the year, with a further doubling expected by year-end.
4. **Connectivity financials:** Connectivity revenue was $5.2 billion, up 69% year-over-year and 22% sequentially; gross margin was 55%, including a one-time ~3% benefit from tariff refunds. Enterprise/government revenue grew 91% year-over-year. Consumer ARPU rose 5% sequentially to $69/month. SpaceX received FCC approval to launch 15,000 mobile V2 satellites and announced a low-band spectrum acquisition to support a push into becoming a major US mobile carrier, with direct-to-cell service targeted for early 2028.
5. **Disaster response:** Starlink supported response across 25 crises in Q3, including earthquakes in Venezuela and western Colombia and a hurricane in Hawaii; in Venezuela it donated over 1,400 kits, reaching over 250,000 people.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute buildout targets:** Musk said SpaceX is tracking to bring roughly 200,000 GB300 GPUs online per month for the rest of this year (CFO cited a more precise 220,000/month figure), reaching roughly 2.5–3 gigawatts of compute by year-end. He said he's "highly confident" of adding 10 incremental gigawatts in 2027 (totaling ~13 GW) and sees a "non-zero," rising-probability chance of over 20 GW of incremental power/compute online next year, contingent on an aggressive power/cooling buildout plan he declined to detail publicly.
2. **Casting capability as a bottleneck advantage:** Musk said SpaceX's advanced rocket-engine casting capability is being applied to manufacture turbine blades and vanes — a bottleneck he said is otherwise 12–18 months behind the rest of turbine production — giving SpaceX a power-deployment speed advantage he believes is hard for competitors to replicate.
3. **Largest training cluster and Grok:** SpaceX plans to have its most powerful training cluster online by the end of Q2, consisting of 440,000 Vera Rubin compute units, intended to make Grok "potentially the best" or at least highly competitive AI model.
4. **Grokbot adoption:** Musk described Grokbot's growth as the fastest he's personally seen for any product, with usage per user increasing over time rather than declining; wide enterprise deployment is expected within a few weeks. The CFO said Grokbot had 800,000 paid weekly users as of earlier this month, more than doubling in the prior month, with enterprise customers above $1M run-rate revenue (including Cursor) up 50% quarter-over-quarter on a pro forma basis.
5. **SI revenue and capacity in Q3:** The SI segment generated $7.6 billion in revenue (up $5 billion sequentially, $6.7 billion year-over-year), with SI solutions and infrastructure revenue of $7 billion (including $68 million from the Cursor acquisition). SpaceX ended Q3 with 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate compute capacity and expects nearly 2.2 gigawatts by the end of October, with a goal of 2.5–3 GW by end of Q4.
6. **Compute economics:** Compute pricing is tracking slightly above the high end of a previously discussed $30–$50 per watt range, with new deployments generating payback in under a year; Musk said a six-month payback period next year is "not impossible." He described compute demand as effectively unlimited (every hyperscaler and dozens of smaller firms seeking more), with an internal target of roughly a 50/50 split between external rental and internal use of compute, done via short-term/spot-rate contracts rather than long-term deals, on the thesis that useful intelligence per watt — and thus compute value — will keep rising.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Elon's SpaceX ARR prediction:** Musk said the implication of SpaceX's compute, Starlink, and launch trajectory is annual recurring revenue by the end of 2027 somewhere between $500 billion and $1 trillion.
2. **Q3 headline financials:** Total company revenue grew 161% year-over-year to $14.6 billion; adjusted EBITDA grew 306% year-over-year to $8.8 billion. Space segment revenue was $1.8 billion (up 84% sequentially, 6% year-over-year). Total capex in Q3 was $19.6 billion. Backlog totaled $63 billion as of September 30, with $17.5 billion recognized as deferred revenue and about $42 billion expected to be recognized within the next year.
3. **Forward guidance:** The CFO reaffirmed a goal of $100 billion in annualized cloud services ARR (based on December run-rate) by year-end. Q4 capex guidance was raised to $35–40 billion due to additional compute capacity coming online. The company issued new Q1 2027 total revenue guidance of $33–37 billion.
4. **SI segment profitability detail:** SI segment adjusted EBITDA was $5 billion (up $3.8 billion sequentially, $5.4 billion year-over-year). SI segment operating income was $431 million, but excluding $1.2 billion of stock-based compensation tied to the Cursor acquisition, the segment had $1.6 billion of operating profit in Q3. Of the $60 billion Cursor acquisition equity value, $23 billion was attributed to employee retention and revesting agreements, to be recognized as stock-based compensation over roughly three years.
5. **Terafab chip ambitions:** In response to an analyst question, Musk said SpaceX's internal (highly uncertain) target is a functional first test chip (a SRAM-type test chip at its Austin research lab) around May/June next year, with an aspirational goal of reaching volume production at the Terafab facility at Given Creek around Q3 2028 (possibly slipping to Q4 2028). He said SpaceX has unspecified potential breakthroughs in chip fabrication he declined to discuss, and reiterated the facility's ultimate goal of producing a terawatt of SI chips per year.
6. **SpaceX–Tesla merger commentary:** Asked directly about a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger, Musk said both companies have formed special committees of independent directors to discuss the idea at shareholders' request, that he is a conflicted party excluded from those discussions, and that the committees have not reached agreement on terms with no guarantee they will. He said he personally believes a merger "would be a good idea" given the overlap in AI/software, chip design (Terafab), robotics, and Starlink/connectivity, but cautioned against assuming it is a certainty, noting Tesla's side would require a 50% vote of all shareholders (not just those voting) — which he called a high hurdle.
7. **Funding for compute buildout:** The CFO said SpaceX's investment-grade rating and strong balance sheet give it access to multiple debt capital pools to fund compute growth, alongside strong operational cash flow and potential vendor/customer financing partnerships, while aiming to preserve its investment-grade status.
