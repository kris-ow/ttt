This is SpaceX's Q2 2026 earnings webcast featuring Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Bret Johnsen, covering quarterly financials, Starship flight progress, Starlink subscriber and satellite growth, and the company's rapidly scaling AI compute business (including Grok and the pending Cursor acquisition). The core thesis presented: SpaceX is pulling forward its revenue trajectory dramatically, driven by Starship's V3 Starlink satellite ramp and an AI/compute business that management says could make SpaceX exceed $100 billion in annualized revenue run rate by December 2026.

## Financials
1. **Q2 revenue and profitability:** Revenue was $7.8 billion, up 92% year-over-year from $4.1 billion; net loss was $541 million (an improvement of $467 million); adjusted EBITDA was $3.5 billion, up 191% from $1.2 billion.
2. **Segment breakdown:** Space segment revenue grew 29% YoY to $962 million with adjusted EBITDA loss of $205 million (due to Starship R&D); Connectivity segment revenue rose 66% YoY to $4.3 billion with adjusted EBITDA up 64% YoY to $2.6 billion; AI segment revenue was $2.6 billion (up 247% YoY), turning adjusted EBITDA positive at $1.1 billion for the first time, narrowing AI segment net operating loss to $1.3 billion.
3. **Capital raises and balance sheet:** SpaceX completed its IPO raising approximately $85.7 billion in net proceeds, followed by a $25 billion inaugural investment-grade senior notes offering (five tranches, weighted average interest rate 5.855%, average maturity 11.7 years) partly used to repay a $20 billion bridge loan. The company ended Q2 with $100 billion in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities, and $47.5 billion in backlog.
4. **Capex and compute economics:** Total Q2 capex was approximately $18.4 billion, of which roughly $15.8 billion funded AI compute infrastructure; capex is expected to remain similar over the next two quarters. New AI compute capital deployments are generating less than a one-year payback, per CFO commentary.
5. **Revenue trajectory pulled forward:** Management now expects to exceed $100 billion in annualized revenue run rate (ARR) by December 2026, including contribution from the pending Cursor acquisition; an additional $6.7 billion in cloud services revenue was contracted in the first weeks of Q3, ramping from October 2026. Musk also said internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "nonzero chance" of 2029.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 13 success and heat shield:** Starship Flight 13 (Starship V3) demonstrated core orbital-mission capabilities; the heat shield performed robustly, and the ship was recovered floating in the ocean for analysis. Musk said he now considers the heat shield problem "solved," calling it the single biggest technical obstacle to full rapid reusability, though further heat-shield refinements will continue.
2. **Flight 14 and catch ambitions:** Flight 14 will be the first to carry V3 Starlink satellites to operational orbit; SpaceX aims to catch both the booster and ship this year, with a ship catch attempt possible as soon as the next flight (tentatively scheduled for the end of the month), pending regulatory approval.
3. **Cadence targets:** Management expects Starship flight cadence to increase rapidly, projecting at least one flight per day (possibly more) roughly a year from the call.
4. **Starship vs. Falcon scale ambition:** SpaceX currently delivers roughly 2,500 tons/year to orbit via Falcon (said to be 80–90% of total Earth mass to orbit), versus the rest of the world combined at around 300 tons/year; Starship's aspiration is to deliver "well over" a million tons/year and ultimately up to 10 million tons/year. Starship is designed to quadruple payload capacity and cut launch costs tenfold versus Falcon 9.
5. **HLS and Artemis timeline:** Orbital propellant transfer is described as critical to both SpaceX's internal plans and NASA's Human Landing System program; Artemis 3 (docking with Orion) is slated for next year, followed by a direct-to-lunar cargo mission, with a goal of "boots on the moon" in 2028.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and satellite growth:** Starlink added more than 1.7 million net new consumer subscribers in Q2 (its best quarter to date), up from 1.4 million in Q1, while holding ARPU stable at $66/month. The constellation reached roughly 10,200 operational satellites as of June 30 (about 9,600 broadband satellites delivering ~800 terabits/second of total downlink capacity), with service in 167 markets.
2. **V3 satellite step-change:** The new Starlink V3 satellite is described as roughly an order of magnitude more capable than V2, and SpaceX plans to launch roughly an order of magnitude more of them — implying a roughly two-order-of-magnitude increase in delivered bandwidth and, even with a 10x drop in monetization per bit, a potential 10x revenue increase for Starlink. Management expects a critical mass of about 1,000 V3 satellites (targeted around Q2 2027) before service improvements become broadly noticeable.
3. **Enterprise and government momentum:** Enterprise and government revenue grew 108% YoY; SpaceX won more than $6 billion in U.S. government contracts in Q2. Aviation remains under 10% penetrated according to management, with Q2 wins including American Airlines and new activations with Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus. Shotwell said SpaceX has never lost an enterprise customer and expects enterprise revenue to substantially exceed consumer revenue over time.
4. **Starlink Mobile and EchoStar spectrum:** SpaceX plans to launch next-generation Starlink Mobile (V2) satellites next year and begin providing mobile service by the end of next year, unlocking the newly FCC-approved 65 MHz of EchoStar spectrum (versus roughly 5 MHz currently used) — described as up to roughly 100x better capability than the current mobile system. The company intends to build out a terrestrial component using low-cost small cell stations co-located with Starlink dish hardware rather than traditional expensive cellular towers, which management says will be capital-efficient; SpaceX paid $856 million in Q2 under its spectrum credit agreement tied to the pending EchoStar transaction.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute capacity scaling:** SpaceX ended Q2 with 1.4 gigawatts of nameplate compute, up from 1 gigawatt in Q1 and 400 megawatts a year earlier, and expects to exceed 2 gigawatts by year-end. Musk said cumulative compute could reach somewhere between 5 and 10 gigawatts (closer to 10) by the end of next year, with a more aggressive internal target of around 20 gigawatts of power/cooling/electrical capacity online (with roughly 15 gigawatts considered a more realistic outcome allowing for project slippage).
2. **Exclusive Nvidia partnership:** SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture going forward, citing deep partnership and the belief it is the best available AI computer platform; the company expects to receive a significant share of Nvidia's GPU supply next year.
3. **Grok development roadmap and training data:** Grok 4.5 launched last month with strong enterprise reception (token consumption tripled after release); Grok 4.6 is expected roughly next week and Grok 4.7 about three to four weeks out; Grok 5 is expected before year-end and will incorporate the full corpus of SpaceX's historical data (roughly a quarter century's worth) to make Grok, in Musk's words, "by far the best engineer." Roughly 10% of SpaceX's compute is expected to be allocated to Grok training over time, with the rest shifting toward inference and third-party compute leasing.
4. **Cursor acquisition nearing close:** The Cursor acquisition is said to be nearly through regulatory hurdles and expected to close soon; it is expected to contribute to the AI segment's revenue trajectory, though management declined to detail the combined product roadmap before closing to avoid pre-empting regulators.
5. **Starmind AI satellite concept:** SpaceX plans to deploy a space-based AI compute satellite (an optimized Nvidia NVL72-class computer), framed as a radical simplification versus standard rack designs, with deployment potentially starting as soon as next year both on the ground and in orbit.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **H1 2026 launch cadence:** SpaceX delivered 78 total launches and 1,041 tons of mass to orbit in the first half of 2026, primarily Starlink missions, while operating at its highest-ever Falcon cadence and robust commercial and U.S. government demand.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Capital allocation priorities:** Management reiterated three priority investment areas — Starship development/production scale, next-generation Starlink broadband and mobile constellations, and AI compute infrastructure — while emphasizing capital efficiency and maintaining an investment-grade balance sheet.
