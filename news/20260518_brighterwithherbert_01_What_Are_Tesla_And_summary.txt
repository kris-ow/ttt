This video features host "brighterwithherbert" in conversation with Larry Goldberg, a serial entrepreneur and active venture capital investor, discussing the relative valuations of Tesla and SpaceX ahead of a potential merger announcement. The core thesis is that a Tesla-SpaceX merger of equals is likely but premature to debate now, that current share prices are not the right basis for evaluating merger fairness, and that SpaceX's Starlink business is a direct parallel to Tesla's robotaxi opportunity — already profitable, growing faster, and widely underappreciated by Tesla retail shareholders.

## Market & Competition

1. **Current Valuations and Information Asymmetry:** As of the video's recording, Tesla's market cap stood at approximately $1.32 trillion, while SpaceX's private market valuation was cited at approximately $2 trillion — having roughly doubled in a short period. Goldberg notes that Tesla suffers a "specific disadvantage" of being well-studied by public markets, while SpaceX benefits from being a highly sought-after, information-scarce private name. The last official SpaceX funding round — which included the acquisition of xAI and X — was valued at approximately $1.25 trillion, but excitement around the IPO has pushed the implied valuation to around $2 trillion.

2. **SpaceX IPO Set for June 12 on NASDAQ:** The SpaceX IPO is described as already announced and "set in stone" for June 12, listing on NASDAQ. The float — shares issued at IPO — is described as only approximately $75 billion against a total valuation of $1.4–$2 trillion, meaning a very small proportion of total stock will be publicly available. Index fund inclusion is expected in waves: one group 5 days after IPO, a second 5 days after that, and smaller index funds 15 days later, with the S&P 500 representing a much larger and slower potential inclusion. Goldberg expects the IPO to be oversubscribed by approximately 12 times.

3. **Merger of Equals: Timing and Process:** Goldberg argues that a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement is unlikely before the end of 2025 (i.e., late 2026 by the video's calendar context) due to the mechanics of IPO lockup periods, which typically run 3–6 months. He describes the merger process as beginning with boards agreeing to "explore" a merger — without any deal terms disclosed — followed by independent directors and external advisors forming a committee to determine exchange ratios. He states it would be inappropriate to announce a merger before lockups expire and index funds have completed their buying, since no stable market price for SpaceX shares would yet exist. Another guest perspective mentioned is that the announcement could come before November, with a Tesla shareholder vote in November, but Goldberg sees practical obstacles.

4. **Tesla Stock Price Forecast and Merger Fairness Debate:** Goldberg predicts Tesla's stock will reach approximately $500 by year-end 2025 (i.e., December 2026 per video context), which at that price would put Tesla's market cap at just over $2 trillion — roughly comparable to his expected stabilized SpaceX valuation of $1.8–$2.2 trillion. He argues that Tesla investors claiming the merger is unfair are making the error of comparing today's Tesla price to SpaceX's hyped IPO valuation, rather than waiting to compare both stable post-lockup prices. He also argues Tesla bulls underestimate SpaceX just as Tesla bears underestimate Tesla.

5. **Starlink vs. Robotaxi — Parallel Businesses, Equal Scale:** Goldberg's central comparative argument is that Starlink is approximately 1–2 years ahead of Tesla's robotaxi business in its development curve. Starlink is described as already profitable, already generating strong revenue, and currently operating at approximately 60–70% margins, compared to Tesla's automotive margins of approximately 14–18%. SpaceX's total annual revenue for last year was cited at approximately $18.5 billion, while Tesla's combined auto, energy, and services revenue ran at approximately $25–26 billion per quarter (roughly $100 billion annualized). Goldberg argues that because Starlink's margins are so much higher, revenue alone is a misleading comparison — Starlink's earnings quality is superior.

6. **Starship as the Growth Catalyst for Starlink:** Goldberg argues that Starlink is currently at roughly 10,000 satellites and generating strong revenue, but the introduction of Starlink Generation 3 satellites launched via Starship will be transformational. He states that a single Starship launch carries 60 satellites, each equivalent in bandwidth capacity to an entire Falcon 9 launch. At a launch cadence scaling from once a week to once a day to multiple times a day, the equivalent of the entire five-year Starlink build-out could be replicated every single month. He describes SpaceX as holding a Starlink monopoly in low-earth orbit broadband, analogous to Tesla's current lead in FSD/robotaxi — with no "Waymo in the sky" as a near-term competitor. Additional revenue opportunities cited include Starlink direct-to-cell, a recently announced deal with Anthropic valued at $5–6 billion (described as an "opening bid"), and discussions with Google for space-based data centers.

7. **Strategic Case for Merger — Four Interlocking Product Lines:** Goldberg frames the strategic rationale for a merger around four product lines — FSD/robotaxi (Tesla), Optimus (Tesla), Starlink (SpaceX), and AI data centers in the sky (SpaceX) — all of which require Terafab chip manufacturing and advanced AI capabilities. He argues these four lines are not truly separate businesses; they feed into one another, and Terafab is described as critical to maintaining competitive advantage across all four. He draws explicit parallels: SpaceX's dominance in space-based connectivity equals or exceeds Tesla's dominance in FSD, and SpaceX's data center opportunity equals or exceeds Optimus's opportunity. He concludes that viewed strategically, these are already one company.

## Financials

1. **Revenue and Margin Comparison:** Tesla's Q1 revenue was cited at approximately $22 billion with 21% margins. SpaceX's full-year prior revenue was cited at approximately $18–18.5 billion. Starlink's margins are described at approximately 60–70%, versus Tesla's automotive margins of approximately 14–18%. The host notes Tesla's combined revenue across auto, energy, and services runs approximately $25–26 billion per quarter, or roughly $100 billion annualized.

2. **Valuation Methodology — DCF Over PE:** Both the host and Goldberg agree that PE ratios are not the appropriate valuation tool for either Tesla or SpaceX. The recommended approach is a sum-of-the-parts discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis, projecting forward 5–10 years, applying probability discounts (e.g., 50% likelihood of achieving a market outcome, or 10% market share capture), and discounting back to present value. Goldberg acknowledges that the total addressable markets for all four product lines are so large as to be "almost inconceivable."

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Robotaxi Expansion Expected by Year-End:** Goldberg expects significant robotaxi expansion between now and the end of 2025 (December 2026 per video context), citing this as a key reason Tesla's stock should rise before any merger announcement. He notes that robotaxi is not yet covering its overhead — it has not reached the scale needed to cover base operating costs and begin generating profit — in contrast to Starlink, which he says has already crossed that threshold.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Factory in California:** Goldberg mentions that the first humanoid robots (Optimus) will be coming out of an existing factory in California, expected by December of the current year. He frames Optimus as one of Tesla's four major future product lines and draws a direct size comparison to SpaceX's AI data center opportunity, saying he cannot determine which is larger — both are described as "gigantic."
