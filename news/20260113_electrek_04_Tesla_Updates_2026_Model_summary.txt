Tesla has launched a third-row, 7-seat option for the US Model Y and rolled out several minor 2026 updates to the Premium trim.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Third-row seating returns in the US:** Tesla's 7-seater option for the Model Y is now live on the US configurator, costing an extra $2,500 and available only with the Premium All-Wheel-Drive Long Range trim. Based on the configurator image, the article notes the third row doesn't appear to have grown much and remains suited mainly for children due to limited leg and head space. This differs from the "Model YL," a 6-seater launched in China in August 2025 with an extended wheelbase (roughly 6 inches) for genuinely extra legroom; CEO Elon Musk has said the Model YL isn't due in the US until late 2026, or "might never come."

2. **Minor 2026 Model Y Premium updates:** Changes apply only to the Premium trim (not the Standard version launched last year) and include a dark grey color for the 20" Helix wheel option (previously lighter grey/silver), a black headliner replacing the lighter material used across Model Y and Model 3 trims, a new 16" higher-resolution screen (previously seen in the Model Y Performance and China's Model YL) now extended to all Premium Model Y, and black badging replacing chrome.

3. **Third-row history and past take-rates:** The third-row option was originally introduced about a year after Model Y production began in 2019 at $3,000 in the US. It was briefly offered in Europe in 2024 and removed during the Model Y redesign in early 2025. In China, the option launched in 2021 but was discontinued due to low take-rate; it was also not popular in the US. The redesigned Model Y launched in January 2025 initially omitted the third-row option, which Tesla teased returning in a marketing email in June 2025.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert welcomes the third row's return but questions its practical utility, expecting legroom to remain "a major compromise" since Tesla apparently did not extend the wheelbase as it did for China's Model YL. He singles out the new black headliner as the most impactful update, saying it makes the interior look more cohesive.
