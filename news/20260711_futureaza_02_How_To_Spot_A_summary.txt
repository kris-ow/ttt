This video is an interview with John, founder of the used-Tesla valuation tool Boltwise, discussing how buyers and sellers can identify hardware/software specs (FSD status, Hardware 3 vs. Hardware 4, chip type) on used Teslas that dealers often fail to disclose or price in. The core thesis is that mispriced used Teslas — especially from the 2022-2024 changeover years — represent a "hidden gem" opportunity for informed buyers, and that FSD/hardware capability will command a growing premium as autonomy improves.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Used Tesla mispricing opportunity:** John described buying a 2021 Model Y for his wife for $16,000 after a $4,000 used-EV credit discount, having found it listed with FSD included at $20,000 — an example of dealers underpricing cars with valuable options because they don't recognize what's under the hood.
2. **Hardware/software changeover ambiguity:** 2022–2024 model years saw multiple mid-cycle changeovers (Hardware 3 to AI4/Hardware 4, Intel to AMD Ryzen processor) that are not disclosed in standard listings; Boltwise infers likely hardware version from VIN manufacture date combined with dealer-submitted screen photos, and is progressively narrowing the changeover window (e.g., Model Y Hardware 3→4 transition pinpointed to roughly May–June of a prior year) as more VINs are confirmed.
3. **Valuation deltas by trim:** In a demo, a 2023 Model Y with Hardware 4 and FSD included was valued about $5,000 above the "blended" base price of $28,000 (blending Hardware 3/4 and FSD/no-FSD cars), while a base Hardware 3, no-FSD version would sit roughly $1,500 below that blended figure — implying real-world spreads that can approach $10,000 for savvy buyers who negotiate off list price.
4. **FSD's used-market discount vs. new price:** John noted buyers originally paid roughly $8,000–$15,000 for FSD when new, but the feature currently resells for only about $2,500–$4,000 in the used market — a gap he expects to narrow as Tesla's unsupervised autonomy progresses and used FSD value rises.
5. **Boltwise search/report tool mechanics:** The "buyer pass" product lets users photograph a Tesla's software display and "Additional Vehicle Information" screen to auto-fill hardware/FSD details, or search up to a 100-mile radius by zip code for qualifying used listings (e.g., year, mileage, drivetrain); reports designate top-spec cars (Hardware 4 + FSD) as "platinum" — the best candidates for potential robotaxi network participation. Subscriptions run 60 days (starting from first assessment, not purchase), with an option to extend for $10/month, and unlimited searches are included.

## Market & Competition
1. **Dealer disclosure gaps:** Both John and the host noted that dealer listings and screen photos typically show cosmetic details (e.g., radio station displays) rather than substantive specs like hardware version or FSD inclusion, leaving value hidden from both dealers and buyers; one commenter noted a used-lot salesman falsely claimed only refreshed "Juniper" Model Ys had Hardware 4, apparently to push a Hardware 3 car.
2. **Private-sale and title-check features in development:** Boltwise is adding private-sale (non-dealer) listings, expected to run a couple thousand dollars cheaper than dealer prices, and already runs a federal database check to flag salvage/rebuilt titles (reporting "no issue found" rather than certifying a clean title).
