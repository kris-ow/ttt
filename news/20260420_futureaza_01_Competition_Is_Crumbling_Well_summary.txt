This Monday news roundup from the "futureaza" channel covers a wide range of Tesla and EV industry topics, recorded while the host is traveling through Sacramento. The video touches on competitor struggles (Ford, Volkswagen), Tesla's robotaxi expansion into Dallas and Houston, Cybercab development updates, Optimus V3 patent details, battery technology news, and broader EV market trends. The host's core thesis is that Tesla's autonomous driving and robotaxi rollout are progressing steadily while legacy automakers and competitors continue to stumble.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Ford's Canceled Moonshot EV and Affordable EV Pivot:** Ford had a secretive "skunk works" EV project that was canceled, and apparently CEO Jim Farley is not even permitted in the building where it was being developed — a detail the host sees as a sign of dysfunction. Ford is now pivoting to doubling down on affordable EVs, driven by currently high oil prices. The host warns this commitment may prove fleeting if oil prices drop, as Ford has historically suffered from strategic amnesia on electrification. The broader point is that EVs should get progressively cheaper to produce, but Ford's approach lacks long-term conviction.

2. **Volkswagen's $600 Million Write-Down and Vehicle-to-Grid Move:** Volkswagen is taking a $600 million hit — a 60–75% write-down of its original investment — after ending production of its only US-made EV, due to shifting political and market conditions (removal of EV incentives). Simultaneously, Volkswagen's "Ellie" is launching a vehicle-to-grid offering, which the host endorses as a promising technology that allows cars to both receive and supply power to the grid, potentially benefiting consumers financially.

3. **Tesla's Model Y Battery Durability Data:** A real-world example shared in the video shows a Tesla Model Y with 111,000 miles that was almost exclusively fast-charged (only 36 kWh lifetime on non-Superchargers) still retaining 92% state of health. The host uses this to push back on the common fear that fast charging degrades EV batteries significantly, calling out the durability advantage over comparable gas vehicles at the same mileage.

4. **Tesla Preps Affordable Model Y Trim for India:** Tesla originally launched in India in 2025 with a quiet rollout targeting the luxury segment with the standard Model Y, but it proved too expensive for the market and gained limited traction. Tesla is now preparing a new, lower-priced Model Y trim (referred to as the "Y L") potentially launching as early as the following week, demonstrating the company's adaptability in a challenging but important market.

5. **SpaceX Buys Nearly 20% of Cybertruck Sales in Q4:** SpaceX purchased nearly 20% of all Cybertrucks sold in Q4, representing a significant fleet buy. The host notes that while this raises questions about nepotism between Elon Musk's companies, SpaceX is a private company and the Cybertruck is a genuinely practical choice for their use case — large battery, onboard 240V generator output, and sufficient range for operations within a roughly 230-mile radius of SpaceX facilities.

6. **UK EV Price Parity Reached:** The average new electric car price in the UK has fallen below the average new petrol car price. The host frames this as a major tipping point — even if electricity costs were equivalent to fuel costs, the lower purchase price plus cheaper maintenance makes EVs financially superior from day one in the UK market.

7. **Global EV Sales Momentum vs. US Slowdown:** March was a record month for EV sales in Europe, with both pure EVs and hybrids rising. Sales are also described as booming in Asia and Australia. In contrast, the overall US car market was down 7% in Q1 2026, and EV interest in the US is rising but hampered by automakers pulling back from electric plans. High gas prices are boosting EV interest globally, though Tesla's limited inventory constrains its ability to capitalize quickly in the US.

8. **Canada Courts BYD for EV Investment:** Canada's trade minister met with BYD during a China visit to pitch EV investment, as US partners have been pulling back from Canadian manufacturing commitments. The host notes this is a predictable geopolitical consequence of US trade policy shifts.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Samsung Texas Foundry Begins Equipment Installation for Tesla Chips:** Samsung's Texas foundry has begun equipment installation for Tesla's chips — an important milestone distinct from simply completing the building. The host clarifies that while the building construction was an earlier step, the actual installation and commissioning of foundry equipment is the harder and more critical phase, and this news confirms that process has now started.

2. **No New Robotaxi Crashes Reported in Austin:** Elon Musk touted a report claiming Tesla's robotaxi service in Austin has recorded no new crashes. The host notes this is the kind of safety milestone that was hoped for earlier in the program's rollout, and its arrival is a meaningful positive signal for the supervised FSD and early robotaxi deployment narrative.

3. **FSD Hardware Eligibility Guidance:** In the Q&A, the host shares his view that Hardware 4 is sufficient for FSD robotaxi eligibility, and that Hardware 3 may become sufficient but remains uncertain. He advises viewers looking for used vehicles to find a pre-refresh Model 3 or Model Y with Hardware 4 and FSD already purchased, noting FSD doesn't yet add its full retail value to used car prices.

4. **Autonomous Vehicle Regulation Update:** A viewer question prompted discussion of national AV regulation. The host notes that new rules have recently been passed that improve the regulatory environment, and that many AV regulations don't require Congressional action — they can be enacted or reversed by executive pen. He also noted that Waymo already has over 2,500 vehicles deployed, suggesting current regulations are not the binding constraint on robotaxi expansion.

## Robotaxi
1. **Driverless Robotaxi Expands to Dallas and Houston:** Tesla's robotaxi service has expanded with driverless operation now rolling out in Dallas and Houston, beyond its original Austin base. The host treats this as a significant milestone and directly addresses skeptics who had predicted it would never go driverless or would never leave Austin, calling out the "moving goalposts" from critics. The stock was down 2% on the Monday following the announcement, which the host uses to challenge the narrative that the expansion was purely a stock pump.

2. **Cybercab Validation Miles Underway:** In response to a Q&A question about why no Cybercabs have been deployed publicly yet, the host explains that Tesla is currently accumulating validation miles — running them empty to gather data before commercial deployment. He confirms that Cybercabs are actively being built, which he views as a credible indicator of genuine progress, contrasting it with cases like Lucid, where production guidance was later revealed to be dishonest based on supplier order data.

3. **Cybercab Consumer Sales Uncertainty:** The host expresses hope that consumers will eventually be able to purchase Cybercabs but acknowledges genuine uncertainty, noting that Tesla may choose to keep the fleet in-house if the robotaxi economics are strong enough. No timeline was given for consumer availability.

4. **Tesla Supercharger Diner Expansion Confirmed:** Elon Musk hinted at upcoming new Tesla diner locations, with Palo Alto and somewhere in Austin/Texas mentioned as potential sites. The host confirmed from personal visits that the existing diner location is very busy, including lines outside on Friday nights, pushing back on reports that the diner concept is dead.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus V3 Hand and Arm Patent Details Revealed:** New patents related to Optimus V3 have surfaced, revealing design details about the hand and arm. The key engineering approach involves moving heavier components away from the hand and into the forearm, while preserving wrist dexterity and maintaining control systems. The host declines to go into full technical depth but notes that the fact Tesla is patenting these designs signals meaningful progress, and plans to find an engineer to explain the specifics more thoroughly.

2. **Optimus Not Present at Tesla Diner Event:** Despite being asked and then "begging," Optimus was not present at the Tesla Owners Club of LA event at the Tesla diner, which the host attended over the weekend.

## Market & Competition
1. **Jim Farley's BYD Comments Draw Pushback:** Ford CEO Jim Farley stated in an interview that if Americans want to beat China in the car business, they should be looking at BYD — not Tesla — as the best in class for cost competitiveness, supply chain, and manufacturing. The host pushes back, noting that Farley conspicuously failed to mention that the Model Y remains one of the best-selling vehicles globally, and quips that Tesla should not be looked at directly.

2. **Donut Labs Battery Lawsuit:** A partner involved in Donut Labs has filed a criminal complaint, alleging the company overpromised capabilities of its battery technology. The company's CEO disputes these claims. The host frames this as a cautionary tale about miracle battery marketing.

3. **China Electrifying Heavy Trucking:** China is pushing toward near-100% electrification of its truck fleet, framed as a national energy security initiative as well as an emissions reduction effort. The host notes that while trucks are a small percentage of the total vehicle fleet, they account for a disproportionately large share of emissions and pollution.

4. **Journalistic Integrity Highlighted:** The host praised "Not a Tesla App" contributor Karan Singh for transparently correcting a story that incorrectly reported Tesla was in talks with Michelin to test airless tires. Michelin denied the report, and the outlet left the original article up with a clear correction at the top — which the host holds up as a model for how corrections should be handled.
