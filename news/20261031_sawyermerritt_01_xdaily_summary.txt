## Executive Summary
Today's digest is Tesla-heavy, with the most notable items being Tesla's new FSD referral program, an extension of robotaxi service hours toward 24/7 operation, a report that Optimus reservations may open in early December, and Tesla pushing Australia to accelerate autonomous vehicle regulation. ## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla launches first-ever FSD (Supervised) referral program:** Tesla officially introduced a referral program where existing FSD (Supervised) subscribers can refer other Tesla owners; a successful referral earns one month of free FSD (Supervised) or a $99 Tesla Credit. Unlike a prior, unofficial/limited referral setup that reportedly only applied to first-time Tesla buyers, this new program allows existing Tesla owners to be referred, which Sawyer Merritt argues broadens the usable audience and should accelerate FSD trial conversions. Merritt noted hitting his personal annual referral limit (12 referrals) within about an hour of the program going live, with Tesla's app indicating referrals reset January 1st ("Refer again on January 1st. Come back in 62 days"), and reported multiple pending referrals within 15 minutes — anecdotal evidence of strong initial demand, though not a volume figure for the broader user base.

2. **Robotaxi service hours extended, new app features added:** Tesla's robotaxi service now operates until 2 AM, up from 1 AM previously, which Merritt frames as movement toward 24-hour operation. Additional app updates include the ability to share trip details with friends, a rear-view camera feed during rides, and a new "Calm Mode" that dims the screen (described as a comfort/UX feature).

3. **Optimus reservations reportedly opening early December:** A San Francisco Standard report claims Tesla will open reservations for its Optimus humanoid robot in early December; Elon Musk reportedly responded to this report, per Merritt's post, though the specific content of Musk's response was not detailed in the text provided.

4. **Tesla pushes Australia on autonomous vehicle regulatory timeline:** Tesla is urging the Australian government to move up autonomous vehicle trial approvals on public roads to 2027 (from a planned 2029 start), arguing the acceleration could save hundreds of lives, prevent thousands of injuries, and unlock more than $10 billion in economic value — figures attributed to Tesla's advocacy/argument rather than independently verified.

5. **FSD (Supervised) mainstream visibility — Turo and celebrity endorsement:** Car rental marketplace Turo sent a promotional email highlighting FSD (Supervised) availability on rental Teslas, encouraging renters to "go beyond the showroom lot." Separately, 2016 F1 World Champion Nico Rosberg posted that trying FSD (Supervised) for the first time was "life-changing," citing time savings — a notable third-party celebrity endorsement with significant reach (3.4K likes on the relaying post).

6. **Waymo partners with Toyota for bZ4X robotaxi fleet (competitive context):** Waymo announced a partnership with Toyota to add bZ4X EVs to its robotaxi fleet, with initial test/validation operations planned in Japan starting 2027, and deployment later planned for the UK and other Waymo-served markets. This is relevant competitive context for Tesla's robotaxi thesis, showing Waymo expanding its vehicle sourcing and geographic footprint.

7. **SpaceX introduces dynamic pricing for Starlink Residential:** SpaceX announced dynamic pricing for Starlink Residential plans in select areas, with pricing to vary based on network capacity and local supply/demand. SpaceX stated that "as our constellation grows and network capacity changes, service pricing may change from time to time" — a shift toward more sophisticated, utilization-based monetization of the Starlink network.
