This is Tesla's official Q1 2026 earnings call and Q&A webcast, hosted by Head of Investor Relations Travis Axarrod and featuring Elon Musk, CFO Vaibhav Taneja, and other executives. The call covers Q1 financial results, strategic priorities for 2026, and deep dives into FSD progress, robotaxi expansion, Optimus production timelines, energy storage, chip development, and the Terafab semiconductor initiative. The overarching thesis is that Tesla is entering a major multi-year capital investment cycle — with over $25 billion in capex planned for 2026 — to lay the groundwork for autonomous driving at scale, Optimus commercialization, and next-generation AI chips.

## Financials

1. **Record Q1 Order Backlog and Demand Recovery:** CFO Vaibhav Taneja noted that Tesla ended Q1 2026 with its highest Q1 order backlog in over two years. Demand recovery was attributed both to rising gas prices and to Tesla's work bringing more affordable and compelling vehicles to market. He highlighted that the Model 3's current starting price is well below its inflation-adjusted launch price of approximately $48,000 in today's dollars, even as the product has improved dramatically.

2. **Auto Margins Improve Sequentially with Caveats:** Automotive gross margins excluding credits improved from 17.9% to 19.2% sequentially. However, Taneja flagged that this included approximately $230 million in one-time warranty-related benefits and some tariff relief. Tesla has not yet realized any benefit from the recent Supreme Court ruling on IPA tariffs due to ongoing uncertainty about the final outcome. Sustained high interest rates continue to pressure margins because interest rate subvention costs are recognized upfront.

3. **Energy Storage Gross Margins Hit Record on One-Time Items:** The energy storage segment posted a gross margin of over 39.5% in Q1, driven partly by over $250 million in one-time benefits from recognition of tariffs paid in prior quarters. Taneja warned that on a normalized basis, energy margins are expected to compress going forward due to increasing competition and tariff impacts, particularly since most battery cells are procured from China.

4. **Energy Deployments Down Sequentially but Full-Year Outlook Positive:** Tesla deployed 6.8 gigawatt-hours of energy storage in Q1 2026, a 38% sequential decline. Despite the quarterly drop, management maintained that full-year 2026 deployments are still expected to exceed 2025 levels. The business was described as "inherently lumpy" due to customer deployment timing.

5. **Services and Other Segment Margins Improve:** Services and other gross margins improved from 8.8% to 9.2% sequentially. This segment includes service centers, used cars, paid Supercharging, parts, insurance, and the robotaxi business. Taneja noted that Tesla is making deliberate infrastructure investments to support robotaxi growth, and the robotaxi fleet grew quarter over quarter.

6. **Free Cash Flow and Massive Capex Ramp:** Tesla ended Q1 with just over $1.4 billion in free cash flow. Taneja confirmed that Tesla is entering a major capital investment phase expected to last a couple of years, with capex guidance of over $25 billion for 2026. This covers six factories going into operation (some already active, some later in the year), AI infrastructure for robotaxi, Optimus production launch, and the research semiconductor fab in Austin. Negative free cash flow is expected for the rest of the year. Net income was also impacted by mark-to-market charges on Bitcoin holdings (which depreciated 22% quarter over quarter) and unfavorable FX impacts.

7. **FSD Paid Customers Reach Nearly 1.3 Million Globally:** FSD adoption reached nearly 1.3 million paid customers globally. Most growth came from subscriptions, while upfront purchases increased only 7%, partly because Tesla removed the purchase option in some markets during Q1. Subscriber churn is declining, and users are driving longer distances on FSD — both interpreted as signals of improving product quality. Tesla described its evolving sales strategy as treating FSD as the primary product and the vehicle as merely the delivery mechanism.

8. **Operating Expenses Rising:** OpEx increased sequentially due to a full quarter of stock-based compensation expense for the 2025 compensation plan (one milestone still deemed probable), plus elevated spending on AI initiatives including AI5 chip development, Cybercab, Semi, Optimus, and Megapack development. This elevated spending level is expected to continue throughout 2026.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 14.3 Described as Last Architectural Piece for Unsupervised FSD:** Elon Musk stated that FSD v14.3 represents the last major architectural piece needed to enable unsupervised Full Self-Driving. However, he clarified that further known architectural improvements are in the pipeline that would significantly improve safety probability, and Tesla does not plan to scale to very large unsupervised deployments until those improvements are validated and released.

2. **FSD Version 15 Planned as Complete Software Architecture Overhaul:** Musk announced that FSD version 15 is targeted for release by end of 2026 or early 2027 at the latest. V15 will be a complete overhaul of the software architecture and will run on AI4 hardware. The goal is to push FSD safety significantly above already-above-human levels. A robotaxi executive confirmed that current deployments in Austin, Dallas, and Houston are running on v14.3 variants, and that the V14.3 base will continue to be used for expansion until V15 is ready.

3. **FSD Approved in Netherlands; EU-Wide Approval Expected in Q2 2026:** Tesla received regulatory approval for supervised FSD in the Netherlands. The broader EU approval process is expected to go to Brussels for review in May 2026, with EU-wide approval anticipated later in Q2. FSD approval in China has also been partially received, with broader approval targeted for Q3 2026. These approvals are expected to drive adoption across the existing European and Chinese fleet and incremental vehicle demand.

4. **Hardware 3 Cannot Achieve Unsupervised FSD:** Musk confirmed that Hardware 3 vehicles are fundamentally incapable of running unsupervised FSD due to having only 1/8 the memory bandwidth of Hardware 4 — identified as the key bottleneck for transformer-based AI inference. Tesla is offering Hardware 3 owners a discounted trade-in toward Hardware 4 vehicles, as well as a hardware upgrade path involving replacement of both the compute unit and cameras. To do this at scale efficiently, Tesla plans to set up dedicated micro-factories or small-scale production lines in major metropolitan areas rather than relying on service centers alone. A distilled version of V14 software for Hardware 3 is expected to launch by end of June 2026, enabling park-to-destination driving and all major V14 features.

5. **Unsupervised FSD Rollout to Customer Cars Expected in Q4 2026:** Musk estimated, with stated uncertainty, that unsupervised FSD would begin rolling out to the customer fleet in Q4 2026. The rollout will be geographically gradual, starting with areas confirmed as safe based on fleet data before expanding to new geographies.

6. **Tesla FSD Fleet Approaching 10 Billion Miles:** A Tesla executive noted that the combined FSD customer fleet is approaching 10 billion miles driven, expected to be reached within the next few weeks. This data is used to monitor safety metrics and validate unsupervised deployment readiness.

7. **Camera Visibility Enforcement Added to Recent Software Builds:** Tesla confirmed it has updated camera hardware (photon-counting cameras) some months ago and has also implemented software-level enforcement in recent builds: if a camera cannot see clearly due to residue buildup or obstruction, FSD will be disabled for that vehicle. This was noted in the context of an outstanding NHTSA inquiry related to older vehicles.

8. **AI4.1 Upgrade Planned for Mid-2027:** Musk announced that a hardware upgrade to the AI4 chip — designated AI4.1 or "AI4 plus" — is planned for mid-2027, dependent on Samsung completing the modifications. The upgrade will increase RAM from 16 GB to 32 GB per SOC (64 GB total), improve memory bandwidth, and deliver approximately a 10% increase in compute (measured in trillions of operations per second).

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Now Operating in Austin, Dallas, and Houston:** Musk confirmed that Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi service has expanded to Dallas and Houston using the same software base as the Bay Area deployment. The primary limiting factor for further expansion is described as rigorous safety validation. Tesla reported zero accidents or injuries in the unsupervised robotaxi program to date, which was corroborated by the referenced NHTSA filings.

2. **Target of Approximately a Dozen States by End of 2026:** Musk stated that Tesla hopes to have unsupervised FSD or robotaxi operating in approximately a dozen or more states by end of 2026, though he emphasized the cautious, safety-first approach to expansion.

3. **Robotaxi Revenue Material but Not Massive in 2026; Significant in 2027:** Musk said robotaxi revenue is unlikely to be "super material" in 2026 but will be material. He expects it to be material in a significant way in 2027. The robotaxi fleet grew quarter over quarter in Q1 2026, with continued ramp planned as geographic expansion accelerates.

4. **Key Operational Challenges Are Convenience, Not Safety:** Musk described the most common issues limiting robotaxi scale as operational/convenience problems rather than safety failures — including vehicles getting "scared" to cross railroad tracks, getting stuck at malfunctioning traffic lights, looping infinitely due to construction blockages, and a specific incident where multiple Tesla robotaxis were blocked in a left-turn lane in Austin because a Waymo had crashed into a bus. He noted that the QA fleet is spread across the US and uses both real-world data and high-fidelity neural-network-based simulators to validate expansion decisions.

5. **Europe Robotaxi Timeline Uncertain and Regulator-Dependent:** Musk acknowledged that getting supervised FSD approved in Europe took an "immense amount of time," and that the timeline for unsupervised FSD or robotaxi approval in Europe is not known and largely dependent on EU regulators. The technology used for European deployment is the same architecture as the US, supplemented with European training data.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus Production Start Targeted for Late July–August 2026 at Fremont:** Musk confirmed that Fremont is being prepared for Optimus startup production, with the target being somewhere in the late July to August 2026 timeframe. The final Model S/X assembly line will be dismantled next month (May 2026) after the last S/X vehicles are completed, followed by installation of the new Optimus production line — a process Musk described as "insanely fast" by industry standards given the full line swap in roughly four months.

2. **Production Rate Impossible to Predict; Expected to Be Very Slow Initially:** Musk explicitly stated it is impossible to predict the Optimus production rate for 2026, noting that with 10,000-plus unique components in an entirely new supply chain, the ramp speed is determined by the "least lucky, slowest, dumbest part." Initial production will be very slow, with exponential ramp expected toward end of year and into 2027. Initial skills will be simple factory tasks, building up from there.

3. **Second Optimus Factory Under Construction at Giga Texas:** Tesla is constructing a second Optimus production facility at Giga Texas, expected to begin production around summer 2027.

4. **Optimus V3 Design Nearly Ready; Reveal Deferred to Near Production Start:** The Optimus Gen 3 design is described as functionally ready but pending aesthetic finalization. Musk said the reveal is now being deliberately delayed closer to production start — tentatively around mid-2026 — partly to limit competitor frame-by-frame analysis and copying of design elements.

5. **Optimus Intelligence Architecture: Local + Grok Orchestration:** Musk explained that Optimus is designed to carry sufficient local on-board intelligence to continue operating safely even without connectivity (analogous to how FSD works without cellular connection). For higher-level task orchestration and voice interaction, Grok AI is expected to serve as the "manager" model — with Musk estimating Optimus could work for several hours without requiring AI management oversight. External data center inference will be needed primarily for complex queries and voice interaction.

6. **Optimus Described as Potentially the Biggest Product in History:** Musk reiterated his view that Optimus will be not only Tesla's biggest product ever but potentially the biggest product in history. He expects Optimus to eventually be deployed outside Tesla — likely sometime in 2027.

## Energy

1. **Megapack 3 Production Starting Later in 2026 Near Houston:** Musk announced that production of Megapack 3 will begin later in 2026 at a new world-class factory outside Houston, addressing strong ongoing demand for grid-scale energy storage.

2. **Solar Business Navigating Market Correction; New Lease Product Launched:** Tesla's residential solar business is impacted by the US market correction following the loss of the homeowner tax credit. Tesla introduced a new solar lease product that allows Tesla itself to capture the tax credit and offer competitive pricing to homeowners. Tesla also launched its own solar panel and a proprietary mounting system, completing a fully integrated home energy ecosystem. Strong demand is expected to return in the second half of 2026.

3. **Battery Pack Capacity Is the Primary Production Constraint:** Taneja and Lars (battery executive) confirmed that the key constraint on vehicle production volume is battery pack capacity rather than cell supply per se. Actions underway include: ramping in-house 4680 cells in the Model Y battery pack at Giga Berlin (launched a few months ago), retooling the Reno facility for more efficient pack production, and growing in-house LFP module and pack production in China.

4. **Giga Berlin Sets Record Output:** Giga Berlin achieved a record output of over 61,000 units in Q1 2026, driven partly by the new in-house 4680 battery pack integration and demand recovery in Europe — with France and Germany showing over 150% quarter-over-quarter delivery growth.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Cybercab Production Has Begun; Ramp Will Follow S-Curve:** Musk confirmed that Cybercab production has just started, with Semi production also beginning soon. Both programs involve entirely new supply chains and production lines, so initial output will be very slow, with ramp accelerating toward the end of 2026 and more significantly into 2027. Musk characterized the 90% of miles driven by one or two people as justification for Cybercab eventually comprising the majority of Tesla's long-term production volume.

2. **Tesla Roadster Debut Possibly Within a Month:** Musk mentioned that the new Tesla Roadster may be able to be debuted within approximately a month of the call, pending testing and validation. He described it as potentially "one of the most spectacular demos ever" but acknowledged it is not a major revenue driver. He also noted that long-term, the Roadster will be the only manually driven vehicle in Tesla's lineup — all other models are expected to be autonomous.

3. **Demand Recovery Across Multiple Geographies:** Beyond Europe, Tesla reported delivery growth in South Korea and Japan in the Asia-Pacific region, and slight quarter-over-quarter growth in the US. The order backlog improvement preceded the recent uptick in gas prices, suggesting it was driven by product improvements and pricing rather than solely macro tailwinds.

4. **Model S/X Production Ending in Early May 2026:** The last Model S/X vehicles will be produced in early May 2026, with the final assembly line to be dismantled that month to make way for Optimus production at Fremont.

## Market & Competition

1. **AI5 Chip Taped Out Early; Road Map Updated:** Tesla's AI5 chip taped out ahead of schedule following six months of continuous weekend work by the chip team. Musk clarified that AI5 is now targeted primarily for Optimus and data center use rather than vehicles, because AI4 is proving sufficient for unsupervised FSD at far greater than human safety levels. A hardware refresh — AI4.1 — will extend the AI4 platform's life in vehicles until a transition to AI5 in cars makes practical sense. Work on AI6 design has begun, and preliminary discussions on Dojo 3 are underway.

2. **Terafab: Tesla Research Fab at Giga Texas; SpaceX Scaling; Intel Partnership:** Tesla is building a research semiconductor fab on the Giga Texas campus — a roughly $3 billion initiative capable of producing a few thousand wafer starts per month, intended for rapid iteration on novel chip physics and process validation. SpaceX will handle the initial phase of the larger-scale Terafab buildout. Tesla plans to use Intel's 14A process node for Terafab, which Musk described as state-of-the-art and not yet fully mature but expected to be production-ready by the time Terafab scales. The research fab is unique in planning to integrate lithography mask creation, logic, memory, and packaging under one roof for rapid recursive R&D. Construction orders have already been placed.

3. **Tariffs Creating Material Cost Headwinds:** Both auto and energy businesses face tariff-related cost pressures. Auto margins face ongoing tariff costs (with no benefit yet from Supreme Court IPA ruling), and the energy business faces outsized tariff impacts since most battery cells are sourced from China. Management is monitoring tariff outcomes but building production plans based on both existing and expected demand.
