Tesla goes to trial today in California state court over allegations of systemic racial discrimination against Black workers at its Fremont factory, in a case brought by the state's Civil Rights Department covering more than 6,000 workers with no cap on damages.

## Financials
1. **Uncapped state lawsuit begins trial:** The California Civil Rights Department (CRD) filed suit in February 2022 after a nearly three-year investigation, alleging supervisors and managers used racial slurs, workers called the factory a "plantation," and Black employees were called "slaves," with swastikas and racist graffiti in common areas. The CRD's pay analysis found Black workers at Fremont earned about $1,533 a month less than white workers between June 2018 and June 2024, and alleges Black employees were funneled into harder, lower-paid jobs, disciplined more harshly, and passed over for promotion. The complaint also cites Tesla's HR staffing ratio of roughly one HR person per 604 to 740 employees.
2. **Bench trial with no jury or arbitration shield:** The trial, a bench trial before Judge Peter Borkon (no jury), opened in Alameda County Superior Court in Oakland and is scheduled to run through October 30. Because California itself is the plaintiff, there is no class to decertify and no arbitration clause applies — both of which Tesla has used to limit exposure in prior cases. Tesla says it does not tolerate discrimination and fires workers for misconduct; the lawsuit alleges some fired offenders were later rehired through contract work. Tesla declined to comment on the trial.
3. **Broader litigation pattern:** This follows Tesla's 2021 Owen Diaz case (a $137 million jury verdict, cut to $15 million on appeal, then to $3.2 million after a second jury trial), a partial settlement in July of the Vaughn v. Tesla class action (with roughly 580 Black workers still pursuing individual claims), and a separate 2023 EEOC lawsuit over alleged "systematic racial harassment and retaliation," now in mediation. The article references an earlier Electrek estimate (from April) that Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in combined lawsuits across autonomy, securities, and discrimination claims, with discrimination not the largest component.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla has spent nearly a decade fighting racism allegations at one factory via settlements, class decertification, and arbitration, but those escape routes are unavailable in this case; he characterizes the scale of alleged misconduct as systemic rather than isolated incidents, and expresses hope the trial addresses it.

Notable quotes and data:
- CRD Director Kevin Kish (at filing): "Tesla operates a racially segregated workplace where Black workers are subjected to racial slurs and discriminated against in job assignments, discipline, pay, and promotion."

- HR staffing ratio alleged: ~1 HR staffer per 604–740 employees.
