This episode of Futureaza is a rapid-fire news roundup covering a Tesla energy leadership change, global EV market share shifts, a flying-car demonstration tied to the Roadster, a SpaceX launch scrub, and competitive EV/robotaxi developments in Asia and Europe. The host frames most items with skepticism toward clickbait framing while affirming Tesla's underlying competitive position.

## Energy
1. **Tesla energy exec Snider departs:** A Tesla energy executive named Snider (who joined in 2014) is stepping down, as first reported by the Wall Street Journal; AJ Booth, senior director of energy product engineering, will take over the role. The host argues this doesn't threaten the energy business, calling it a "money printer," and notes Tesla's energy business grew 9.6% in Q3 measured by energy deployed.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y has no real equivalents, per host:** A roundup piece looking for Model Y "equivalents" (Ioniq 5, Kia EV6, Chevy Equinox) is dismissed by the host as not finding genuine competitors given the Model Y's efficiency, comfort, features, and self-driving capability.
2. **US EV sales plateau post-tax-credit:** One year after the federal EV tax credit ended, EV sales have dropped from a roughly 11% share in 2025 (pulled forward by credit expiration) back down to a stable 5–6% of new car sales, per Cox Automotive and Edmunds data cited in the segment. The host calls this inconsistent with energy-security goals, noting Tesla continues to lead in many markets where overall EV sales aren't declining.
3. **India EV sales exceed US for sixth month:** India's battery electric vehicle sales share has topped the US for six straight months since April, one of 15 countries now surpassing the US in BEV sales in 2026 (others cited include Brazil, Italy, Malaysia, New Zealand). The host notes this is despite very low Tesla penetration in India, attributing it to Tesla's high market entry pricing there.
4. **BYD vs Tesla price comparison called misleading:** A widely shared claim that "BYD beats Tesla by $5,500 in Europe" is criticized by the host as clickbait, since the underlying comparison (sourced via Supercar Blondie) actually pits the Model 3 against the BYD Dolphin rather than comparable vehicles like the Model Y vs. Seal Lion or Model 3 vs. Seal.
5. **European automakers push back at Paris Auto Show:** European OEMs (including references to BYD, Geely, and Xiaomi-adjacent Chinese brands) are reportedly escalating competitive responses to Chinese EV makers at the Paris Auto Show, with some looking to build factories within the EU. The host is skeptical this response is timely, saying it's "five years" overdue.

## Market & Competition
1. **First federally funded Arizona EV chargers open, mostly Tesla:** The first three federally funded EV charging stations in Arizona have opened, two of which are Tesla sites; the host notes Tesla received the majority of this program's funding despite claims the government favored it less.
2. **BP Pulse opens Australia's largest EV charging hub, soon to be topped by Tesla:** BP Pulse opened a 24-stall charging hub in Australia (150–300kW, with some solar canopy coverage), but a Tesla Supercharger site with 37 stalls is reportedly coming soon to surpass it.
3. **Faraday Future unveils low-cost humanoid robot:** Faraday Future unveiled a humanoid robot priced at $2,500 aimed at developers and schools, mentioned briefly as a competitive data point without further analysis before the stream cut out.

## Robotaxi
1. **Amoda and Waymo-adjacent purpose-built robotaxi push:** A report describes a purpose-built Level 4 robotaxi being showcased for global markets (referenced alongside Waymo), which the host flags as evidence that competition in autonomous ride-hailing, particularly from Eastern players, is intensifying.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Bandwagon-5 launch scrubbed for weather:** Poor weather forced a scrub of the Bandwagon-5 launch, which was carrying the Starfall re-entry capsule; SpaceX is now targeting a launch the following day (Tuesday). The host frames this as a routine delay given SpaceX's launch track record.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Flying car demo tied to Roadster draws tempered expectations:** Elon Musk showcased a flying-car demonstration (linked to the Roadster) at a rocket testing facility, with the vehicle shown hanging upside down on a track and performing aerial stunts. The host and cited AP reporting stress that consumer production isn't expected until 2028, pricing and road-legal flight approval remain unknown, and that the technology shown is a capability showcase rather than something for public-road use; stock reaction was muted, described as "back to where it was before the event."
