This is live-streamed coverage of SpaceX's first-ever public quarterly earnings report and call, hosted by futureaza with co-host John Twig and later guest Nick Gibbs (host of Investing Against the Grain). The video covers pre-earnings speculation, a full readout of the earnings call (with Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Brett), and post-call reaction, with the hosts broadly bullish on the results.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **First public earnings beat expectations:** SpaceX reported Q2 revenue of $7.88 billion, up 92% year-over-year from $4.1 billion, beating Wall Street's roughly $7 billion estimate; net loss was $541 million (an improvement of $467 million year-over-year), and adjusted EBITDA was $3.5 billion, up 191% from $1.2 billion.
2. **Balance sheet and capital raises:** SpaceX ended Q2 with $100 billion in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities and $47.5 billion in backlog (signed but unfulfilled contracts). The quarter included an IPO raising ~$85.7 billion in net proceeds and a $25 billion inaugural investment-grade bond offering (five tranches, weighted average interest rate 5.855%, average maturity 11.7 years), partly used to repay a $20 billion bridge loan.
3. **Revenue run-rate guidance raised:** Management said the company is on pace to reach $100 billion in annualized revenue run rate (ARR) by December, driven substantially by AI cloud services; CFO Brett called this a "not a question mark" baseline that could go higher. Elon Musk also said internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029.
4. **Stock reaction was volatile and directionally negative despite strong results:** TSLA-adjacent hosts noted SPCX shares initially popped ~1% after-hours before reversing to down roughly 7%, which the hosts (including guest Nick Gibbs) characterized as an oddly negative reaction to what they viewed as an excellent first earnings report; Gibbs argued the market doesn't yet appreciate the scale of the $100B ARR and sub-one-year capex payback figures.
5. **Share lockup dynamics discussed:** The first post-IPO lockup unlock occurs two days after this earnings call (allowing roughly 20% of restricted shares to be sold). Nick Gibbs argued he does not expect this to meaningfully pressure the stock, noting institutional holders have had prior liquidity opportunities and markets are forward-looking; the host speculated some employees may sell modest portions for personal liquidity without dumping large stakes.
6. **Merger-with-Tesla speculation remains unresolved:** Hosts discussed a betting-market (Kalshi) probability of roughly 40% that a Tesla–SpaceX merger would be "mentioned" (not announced) on the call; the host's own view is that a near-term merger is unlikely because institutional Tesla shareholders (now the majority) would likely resist losing shareholder voting power. No SpaceX–Tesla merger was announced or substantively addressed on the earnings call itself.
7. **Nvidia exclusivity partnership:** Elon Musk stated SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's "Bar Rubin" (Rubin) architecture going forward, calling it the best available AI compute architecture and citing close cooperation with Nvidia; he also said SpaceX expects to receive "a very significant percentage" of Nvidia's GPUs next year. Hosts framed this as confirming Musk/SpaceX gets first access to Nvidia's latest chips.
8. **Cursor acquisition nearing close:** Musk said the ~$60 billion Cursor acquisition (announced previously) is through "almost all" regulatory hurdles and should close soon; management declined to detail combined product roadmap plans until after closing to avoid complicating the regulatory process.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **AI segment posted triple-digit growth and turned EBITDA-positive:** AI segment revenue was $2.6 billion, up 213% sequentially and 247% year-over-year; segment net operating loss narrowed to $1.3 billion while adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $1.1 billion for the quarter, driven by new cloud services agreements at Colossus and Colossus 2 (which contributed $1.6 billion of incremental revenue in the quarter).
2. **Massive compute buildout planned:** SpaceX ended Q2 with 1.4 gigawatts of nameplate compute (up from 1 GW in Q1 and 400 MW a year earlier) and expects to exceed 2 gigawatts by year-end; Musk said the company is targeting a tentative 20 gigawatts of power/cooling capacity online by the end of next year (acknowledging full execution is unlikely, but expecting ~15 GW realistically), with cumulative compute potentially reaching "closer to 10 than 5" gigawatts.
3. **Star Mind satellite compute partnership with Nvidia:** SpaceX announced a partnership with Nvidia to design the "Star Mind" AI satellite compute payload, based on an optimized NVL72 Rubin architecture; management said the same design will also be deployed on the ground (with liquid/closed-loop cooling instead of solar/radiator), calling it a potential "radical simplification" versus standard rack designs, with launches expected to begin next year.
4. **Grock roadmap and compute allocation:** Grock 4.5 was released last month with token consumption tripling after release; Grock 4.6 is expected within about a week and Grock 4.7 in three to four weeks; Grock 5 is expected before year-end and will incorporate SpaceX's full historical data corpus. Musk said he expects only about 10% of compute to be used for Grock training over time, with the majority allocated to inference/rented compute for third parties like Google and Anthropic.
5. **Compute monetization economics:** Musk estimated (explicitly as a rough guess) monetization of roughly $30–$50 per watt for Rubin-based compute, and management said new AI capital deployments are seeing less-than-one-year payback periods, with $6.7 billion of additional cloud services revenue already contracted in the first weeks of Q3.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Heat shield problem considered largely solved:** Musk said based on data and visual inspection of the recovered Starship from flight 13, the team believes the heat shield problem is solved, though continued improvements will be made; he characterized it as the single biggest remaining technical obstacle to full and rapid reusability.
2. **Two successful V3 test flights completed:** SpaceX completed two Starship V3 flights in the past 90 days; flight 13 demonstrated core capabilities for an orbital mission and return-to-Starbase catch, and flight 14 is planned to deliver operational V3 Starlink satellites to orbit. A tower catch attempt of the ship is tentatively planned for the next flight, targeted for the end of this month, pending regulatory approval.
3. **Rapid cadence targeted:** Management expects at least one Starship flight per day roughly a year from now, and expects to catch both the booster and ship (potentially at different sites) within this year or next.
4. **Massive orbital mass ambitions:** SpaceX currently delivers roughly 2,500 tons/year to orbit via Falcon (versus ~300 tons for the rest of the world combined); Starship's stated aspiration is to eventually deliver "well over a million tons" per year, ultimately potentially 10 million tons/year.
5. **Starship segment financials:** Space segment revenue grew 55% sequentially and 29% year-over-year to $962 million; segment adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $205 million, reflecting elevated Starship R&D spending; total capex in Q2 was approximately $18.4 billion, of which $15.8 billion went to AI compute infrastructure with the remainder to Starship, launch infrastructure, satellites, and ground stations.
6. **Lunar program timeline:** Musk stated the Artemis 3 mission (docking with Orion) is planned for next year, followed by an uncrewed direct-to-lunar cargo mission, with a goal of "boots on the moon" in 2028; the host expressed skepticism that NASA's broader Artemis timeline is achievable given dependencies on multiple contractors, while suggesting SpaceX may be quietly preparing to execute lunar landing capability independently.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Record subscriber growth and stable ARPU:** Starlink added a net 1.7 million subscribers in Q2 (up from 1.4 million in Q1), the best quarter to date, while holding average revenue per user (ARPU) stable at $66/month. The constellation reached roughly 10,200 operational satellites (about 9,600 broadband) delivering roughly 800 terabits/second of downlink capacity, with service available in 167 markets.
2. **Connectivity segment financials:** Connectivity segment revenue rose 32% sequentially and 66% year-over-year to $4.3 billion; segment operating income rose 79% year-over-year to $1.7 billion; segment adjusted EBITDA rose 64% year-over-year to $2.6 billion. Enterprise and government revenue grew 108% year-over-year.
3. **V3 satellites framed as a step-change:** Musk said Starlink V3 satellites are roughly an order of magnitude more capable than V2, with roughly 10x as many planned for launch, implying a potential ~100x increase in delivered bandwidth even if per-bit monetization falls by 10x. Management said Starlink could eventually deliver a majority of the world's internet within under 10 years.
4. **Enterprise/aviation expansion:** SpaceX signed a major agreement with American Airlines and activated service with Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus; management said Starlink remains under 10% penetrated in aviation, one of its largest addressable enterprise segments, and has never lost an enterprise customer.
5. **Government contracts and mobile spectrum:** SpaceX won more than $6 billion in U.S. government contracts in Q2, largely supporting Space Force programs. It also launched new Starlink mobile partnerships with SoftBank, NTT Docomo, and Spark New Zealand, and received FCC approval for a transfer of 65 MHz of EchoStar spectrum, up from the roughly 5 MHz currently used, which management said could make Starlink Mobile roughly 100x more capable; mobile V2 satellites are planned to begin flying next year with commercial service expected by the end of next year.
6. **Terrestrial mobile buildout strategy:** Rather than large traditional cell towers, SpaceX plans to deploy small terrestrial base stations attached to existing Starlink dish hardware (on rooftops), which management said would be significantly more capex-efficient than a traditional buildout; management declined to give specific capex figures for this initiative.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon cadence remains dominant:** SpaceX reported 78 total launches and 1,041 tons of mass delivered to orbit in the first half of the year, with the commercial and government manifest robust and Falcon manifest reportedly filled out through 2028–2029.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus development kept confidential ahead of commercialization:** The hosts noted that people close to Tesla's Optimus program are described as very tight-lipped about progress; they emphasized that the publicly known capabilities of Optimus 2/2.5 likely understate roughly two years of undisclosed hardware and design improvements (new motors, ground-up redesign) ahead of a targeted commercialization push by year-end.

## Energy
1. **Tesla's in-house LFP Megapack cells and China export restrictions:** The hosts noted Tesla's in-house-manufactured LFP battery cells (used in Megapacks, as distinct from previously supplier-sourced cells) are not the latest generation, because China has restricted export of the newest LFP cell technology and manufacturing equipment, meaning Tesla would need to develop its own next-generation process without infringing existing patents.
2. **New flash-charging battery technology discussed generally:** The hosts discussed a next-generation "Blade 2" flash-charging battery reportedly capable of 0–97% charge in about 8 minutes at roughly a megawatt (up to ~1.2 MW) of charging power, already being deployed by BYD in China alongside on-site energy storage buffers, with a stated warranty of a million kilometers for qualifying manufacturer/vehicle combinations.

## Market & Competition
1. **Lucid reported an EPS miss:** The host noted Lucid reported an earnings-per-share miss on the same day, referencing his own past skepticism about the company's trajectory.
