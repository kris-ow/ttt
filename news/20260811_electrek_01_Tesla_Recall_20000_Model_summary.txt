Tesla is recalling over 20,349 Model 3 and Model Y vehicles because their low-beam headlights exceed the maximum allowed brightness, and unlike most Tesla recalls, this one likely requires a physical hardware fix rather than a software update.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Recall scope and cause:** The recall covers 18,735 Model Y vehicles (2020-2023) and 1,614 Model 3 vehicles (2017-2023) equipped with headlamp assemblies supplied by Marelli Automotive Lighting. The spec allows a maximum of 125 candela, but Tesla's testing found brightness as high as 230.1 candela. Most affected vehicles were manufactured before June 2, 2023, though 432 additional vehicles with the same parts were built between then and May 3, 2024.

2. **Recall history and NHTSA petition denial:** A Canadian lab first found the headlights out of spec in 2023, which Tesla confirmed internally. Tesla proposed recalling only a small subset of vehicles and petitioned NHTSA to avoid a full recall, arguing the headlights hadn't caused accidents or injuries (though the article notes 3 warranty claims and 1 field report tied to the issue). NHTSA denied that petition in July, making the full recall inevitable.

3. **No software fix indicated:** Unlike many Tesla recalls that are resolved via over-the-air updates (sometimes already applied before the recall notice is even sent), this recall's paperwork contains no mention of a software remedy, suggesting Tesla will need to physically replace headlight sets and cover installation labor. Tesla sales locations were notified last week, and affected owners will receive mailed notifications by September 15.

## Editorial Stance
The author speculates, without direct evidence, that Tesla likely explored a software-based brightness fix and found it wasn't possible, given that the company fought the recall and now faces the costlier path of physical part replacement.
