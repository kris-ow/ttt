A host and a guest (identified on-screen as "Joe," an economist/investor running a Tesla-and-AGI-focused YouTube channel) discuss why SpaceX stock appears under-owned despite bullish sell-side commentary, centering on Morgan Stanley's "cheap" call, leaked compute-capacity figures, ARK Invest's Starship revenue model, and Starlink's airline wins. The throughline is that SpaceX's valuation is hard for generalist analysts to model because it spans Starlink, Starship, and — increasingly the biggest driver — AI compute sold "as a service," which the guest argues most institutional investors are failing to price in.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Morgan Stanley reiterates "cheap" call, near-zero institutional ownership:** Analyst Adam Jonas keeps an "outperform" rating and $300 price target (shares around $174), saying SpaceX trades roughly 40% below mega-cap AI peers on a growth-adjusted basis. Jonas says that in a recent room of 40 clients, not a single one owned SpaceX stock, and attributes the gap to the difficulty of valuing a conglomerate spanning telecom, aerospace, and AI simultaneously rather than in isolation.
2. **Guest's long-term valuation thesis:** The guest argues SpaceX, currently valued around $2.2 trillion per the discussion, could grow roughly 100x to around $200 trillion based on infinite AI compute demand, Starship reliability by 2028, and mass AI-satellite deployment also beginning in 2028 (a timeline he attributes partly to Elon Musk's own public comments). He frames SpaceX's current price as effectively a price-to-earnings ratio of about 10 if profit reaches roughly $200 billion, calling that "crazy" cheap, though this rests on his own projections rather than confirmed company guidance.
3. **Older shareholder liquidity as a share-price headwind:** The guest notes that early SpaceX shareholders whose lock-up-like terms are expiring are now able to sell, which he says could be holding back the stock price.
4. **Share class note:** The guest says he personally remains more bullish on Tesla than SpaceX specifically because of the prospect of a Tesla-SpaceX merger, from which he believes Tesla stands to gain more value than SpaceX.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Leaked compute roadmap:** According to leaked conference notes cited in the video, SpaceX compute capacity is projected to reach 2.3 gigawatts by December (2026) and 10+ gigawatts by the end of 2027, with a new unnamed customer to be announced in November. The guest says he personally estimates the 2027 build-out will land closer to the high end — "closer to 10 than to five" gigawatts — rather than taking the 10GW figure as certain.
2. **~60% of compute reserved internally:** Per a cited leaked Q&A (source described as an administrative/approval process, not Elon Musk), roughly 60% of the planned 10 gigawatts of compute is reserved for internal use by xAI, SpaceX, and Tesla, with the remaining 40% serving external customers including Anthropic and Google.
3. **Grok's rapid performance gains:** The guest describes Grok as unexpectedly fast and responsive in his own daily use, and notes Elon Musk replied "It's over. We are back" to a comment about Grok's speed — which the guest interprets (his own inference, not confirmed) as a signal that Musk has seen strong internal performance data rather than making a typical promotional claim.
4. **Compute economics and demand framing:** The guest cites Gavin Baker's estimate that Anthropic currently has about $70 billion in annualized revenue but could reach roughly $140 billion if it had unconstrained compute at current prices — used to argue compute, not model quality, is the binding constraint across the industry. He also cites a figure that only about 2% of Americans currently pay for AI as evidence of large remaining growth headroom.
5. **Competitive landscape — Meta's Muse and OpenAI's "dots":** The guest flags Meta's Muse (tied to Alexandra Wang and the Cursor team, per the guest) as an underrated competitor due to Meta's existing Instagram/Facebook distribution and compute strength, while describing OpenAI's "dots" agent as similar to Grok but potentially derivative; he says Grok's advantage increasingly lies in product execution and speed rather than raw intelligence.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **ARK Invest's per-launch revenue model:** ARK's Brett Winton estimates each Starship launch could add about $700 million in incremental annual revenue initially (modeled for 2027), rising toward $2.6 billion per launch by 2031 as AI software and "intelligence as a service" monetization ramp; Elon Musk responded "I agree" to this analysis. The guest adds his own unconfirmed estimate that by Q3 2028 SpaceX could be launching the equivalent of one "Colossus" computing cluster every quarter, and separately speculates that as launches scale, per-launch annual revenue could reach roughly $1 billion once AI/intelligence services are included — his own extrapolation, not a cited source figure.
2. **Starship 15 flight as near-term catalyst:** Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas flagged the launch of Starship (referred to as "Starship 15") this month as a potential major catalyst, calling a successful landing possibly the biggest catalyst since the IPO; the guest separately called the most recent Starship flight "extremely significant."
3. **Launch cadence ambitions:** The guest says Elon Musk responded to his post stating that in 2-3 years SpaceX aims to be able to launch roughly once per hour; the guest contrasts this with a more near-term goal he's heard of roughly one launch per day.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **United and Delta fleet-wide rollout plans:** United is cited as planning Starlink on 880 aircraft by the end of 2026 and across its full fleet by 2027. Delta, by contrast, has committed to Amazon's Kuiper satellite service, which per the discussion won't be available until 2028–2029, which the guest frames as a major competitive disadvantage for Delta.
