This futureaza news roundup covers Tesla's Roadster event delay speculation, a temporary Chinese sales discount, European FSD regulatory developments, ongoing donut-battery skepticism, EV market/demographic trends, Tesla Q3 delivery speculation, and a brief note on SpaceX's stock reaction to a recent Starship test flight, interspersed with a live Q&A.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **EU delays Tesla FSD vote to December:** The European Union has pushed back its vote on Tesla's FSD approval from October 6th to December, per an agenda published on the EU's website, to gather more information. The host notes Ireland is separately in talks for FSD approval, and pushes back on the idea that a Belgian research group's finding that FSD "can't read signs" dooms approval, arguing other systems (including LiDAR-based ones) share similar sign/traffic-light limitations.
2. **VLA 2.0 testing in Europe:** An analyst named Larry Evans, who has previously tested Full Self-Driving-adjacent autonomy systems in China and elsewhere, is testing a VLA-based autonomy stack (not FSD) in Europe; he could not drive it himself for insurance reasons but experienced it as a passenger, running the same route also tested in Tesla FSD. Only part one of his review had been published at time of recording. The host frames wider multi-company autonomy testing in Europe as making eventual regulatory approval harder for the EU to keep blocking.
3. **UK plug-in hybrids largely lack DC fast-charging:** The host cites a report that roughly three-quarters of UK plug-in hybrid models cannot use DC fast chargers above 50kW, limiting them to home charging or gasoline for real-world range use.

## Robotaxi
1. **Personal Cybercab timeline uncertain:** Responding to a viewer question, the host notes Elon Musk has repeatedly said personal Cybercab purchases could happen by end of this year, but the host is skeptical, guessing it's "at least a year, but potentially three or never." He mentions working with a person from "Boltwise" on a resource for people building Cybercab/robotaxi fleets.
2. **Viewer field reports on Cybercab service:** A live-chat viewer described taking a first Cybercab ride, calling the car itself "lovely" but citing high price, an odd pickup location, and an indirect route; another viewer reported seeing increasing numbers of Cybercabs in Houston, which the host called "bullish." The host pushed back on a suggestion that churches/senior centers buy dedicated Cybercabs, arguing it's more efficient to rent from the shared network only when needed rather than owning idle vehicles for weekday lulls.
3. **Optimus personal ordering timeline:** The host estimates a personal (non-business) Optimus could be orderable by someone as early as next year (2027), but believes he personally won't be able to get one until at least 2028.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla China price cut:** Tesla is offering up to $1,000 off vehicles in China as a short-term sales push, timing uncertain (may end this month or extend longer); the host argues temporary discounts, unlike permanent price cuts, shouldn't hurt resale values as much.
2. **Tesla financing promo in the US:** The host received a message from a Tesla representative about financing as low as 1.49%, with the offer's expiration date unclear (the rep said "tomorrow," but the host's own research points to it changing around the 30th).
3. **European Tesla sales up year-to-date:** Despite a Norway sales dip (Tesla fell to seventh place in that market last month with 627 units sold, per a cited chart, even as Norway's overall EV share was about 98.7% of sales), the host says European Tesla sales are up 43% year-to-date versus the same period last year, and delivery patterns are back-loaded within the quarter as usual.
4. **Tesla Q3 US delivery uncertainty:** The host addresses viewer concern about weak Q3 US sales, noting analyst Randy Kirk (to be interviewed later that day) projects a high delivery number (roughly 400,000+, exact figure unclear to the host) that the host says he has reviewed but does not agree with. The host separately notes Cybertruck has sold poorly, Model S/X sales have largely stopped, and Berlin/Texas gigafactories still have unused capacity despite delays, with idle time during the quarter; a viewer also reported a Tesla parking lot full of cars awaiting delivery, suggesting a end-of-quarter delivery push.
5. **EV adoption skews by age, not politics:** Citing a new industry-group survey, the host says age is now a bigger predictor of EV purchase intent than political affiliation, noting 42% of respondents said they were unlikely to buy an EV, with older buyers ("boomers") showing the most resistance.

## Market & Competition
1. **BYD expanding EU manufacturing footprint:** In response to EU tariffs, BYD is building factories in Hungary and planning three more in Europe to produce vehicles locally; the host notes BYD doesn't get the same cost efficiencies there as in China (citing roughly $300/car in relevant subsidies, which he says is smaller than comparable US EV subsidies) but argues it's still worthwhile if cheaper than paying the tariffs.
2. **GM rare-earth magnet sourcing claim:** A report describes GM as nearing payoff on a long-term investment (a magnet factory called "Independence") to source rare earths domestically rather than from China; the host is skeptical of the claim's completeness, noting China controls roughly 90% of rare earth mining relevant to EV motor magnets, about 95% of refining, and about 99% of finished magnet production — meaning even non-Chinese-mined material is usually sent to China for refining/finishing. He notes Tesla's Cybercab motor design avoids rare earth magnets entirely.
3. **Donut battery claims remain unresolved:** The host revisits ongoing back-and-forth between a critic ("Zeroth") and a proponent (Christopher Arcus) over Donut Labs' battery claims. A UK lab reportedly found only about 3.4 parts per million of lithium or sodium in a tested sample (rounding to effectively zero), but the host notes the sample arrived pre-disassembled and there's no confirmation it came from a real production vehicle, so he considers both sides' claims inconclusive — with Arcus's rebuttals, in his view, weaker than Zeroth's original critique.
4. **Stellantis pauses white-collar hiring; battery-ejection patent:** Stellantis has paused white-collar hiring, which the host says he predicted. Separately, Stellantis has patented a system to physically eject an overheating battery pack from a vehicle before thermal runaway; the report notes this comes with a disclaimer that it does not indicate the feature will go into production.

## Financials

SpaceX's stock fell despite what the host called a very good Starship test flight, which he attributes to "buy the rumor, sell the news" — success already being priced in.
