Electrek reports that a Tesla Model Y's driver-assist system was "Verified Engaged" during a fatal March 25, 2026 crash in Batavia, Illinois that killed passenger Maggie Espinosa, a 37-year-old teacher and mother of five, based on Tesla's own filing to NHTSA — a fact not disclosed in police or media reports of the crash.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Crash details:** Around 4:19 p.m. on March 25, 2026, a Tesla Model Y heading south on Randall Road attempted a left turn onto McKee Street and struck a Dodge pickup traveling straight with the right of way, per Batavia police; a Nissan SUV was also involved. Espinosa died from injuries; the Tesla's driver and two others were injured. No mention of Autopilot or FSD appeared in the public police account.

2. **Tesla's NHTSA filing:** Under NHTSA's Standing General Order, Tesla reported the crash and marked automation "Engagement Status" as "Verified Engaged," listing the 2026 Model Y's pre-crash movement as "Making Left Turn" at 24 mph at an intersection, striking the front of the pickup. Tesla confirmed it holds the event-data recorder, telematics, and video, but redacted the crash narrative, software version, and whether the road was within the system's approved operating area as "confidential business information."

3. **FSD vs. Autopilot inference:** Electrek argues that an unprotected left turn across oncoming traffic is a City-Streets maneuver that basic Autopilot (a highway lane-keeping system) cannot perform, making it likely — though not confirmed by Tesla — that "Full Self-Driving" was the active system, not basic Autopilot. The article does not claim FSD was at fault, only that Tesla is withholding data needed to determine responsibility.

4. **Matching methodology and caveat:** Electrek matched the NHTSA report to this crash via unredacted details — location (Batavia), vehicle (2026 Model Y), left-turn-into-pickup mechanics, fatality, and month. It flags one discrepancy: Tesla's logged incident time (12:54 UTC) doesn't cleanly convert to the ~4:19 p.m. local time police reported, unlike other crashes previously matched in this investigation series, though all other identifying details align.

5. **Investigation status:** Batavia PD told Electrek the crash "remains under investigation and is currently under review by the Kane County State's Attorney's Office." No charges have been filed.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla is effectively testing "beta" software on public roads without accountability, criticizing the company for releasing favorable data while hiding details of fatal crashes, and contends that a company marketing "self-driving" and encouraging phone use while driving should not be permitted to withhold data when its system is involved in a fatal crash.
