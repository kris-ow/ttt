Episode 145 of "The Tesla Beat," hosted by John, Joe, Brian, and Matthew, covers a broad range of Tesla and tech news heading into the weekend, including Tesla Semi updates, FSD milestones, new vehicle launches, Model S/X end-of-life, and market dynamics. The panel is broadly bullish on Tesla's product pipeline and the company's competitive positioning across EVs, autonomous driving, and energy.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi at ACT Expo:** Tesla's Dan Priestley presented the Tesla Semi at the ACT Expo in Las Vegas in a roughly 43-minute on-stage appearance. The panel praised his transparency and engineering philosophy. Key highlights included the Semi's one-speed direct drive transmission — contrasted with competitors (Cascadia, Kenworth) who use two- to four-speed transmissions, adding 500 lb to over 2,000 lb of weight. The front end was redesigned from a single replaceable piece into three sections to reduce repair costs and improve total cost of ownership. Competitor electric semis were described as electrified versions of existing diesel platforms, while Tesla's is a purpose-built vehicle priced approximately 25% below competing trucks (which are in the $400,000–$500,000 range). NA Container Freight Line ordered 20 units, with the first truck slated to operate next year. Watt EV was noted as having placed an order of approximately 370 units. In California, the cost per mile for the Semi is reportedly 50% cheaper than diesel; outside California, approximately 20% cheaper.

2. **Model Y L Incoming:** A covered Model Y L was spotted this week, consistent with earlier reports. Matthew shared that Giga Texas is reportedly preparing for two large SUV variants — one likely being the Model Y L and potentially a Cyber SUV built on the Cybertruck platform. Elon had previously responded (approximately six weeks prior to the episode) to a comment about needing larger family vehicles. The Model Y L is expected to be built on the same production line as the Model Y in Austin and potentially Fremont, requiring no new line build-out. The panel estimated the Model Y L could arrive at approximately $55,000–$60,000 at launch, with an unveil possible within 60 days and deliveries potentially beginning around October. The panel dismissed Osborne Effect concerns, noting that Model Y L availability in other markets has not hindered standard Model Y sales.

3. **Model S and Model X End of Production:** The last Model S and Model X units rolled off the production line, with Tesla employees holding a signing event for the final vehicles. The panel acknowledged the Model S as historically significant for kicking off EV mass production. The Model Y L and potential Cyber SUV were cited as likely volume successors, particularly given the Model X's relatively low production volumes.

4. **Model 3 Now Available in Canada from Giga Shanghai:** Tesla began selling the Giga Shanghai-manufactured Model 3 in Canada at approximately $30,000 USD — described as the cheapest Tesla has ever been offered anywhere in the world and roughly 35% below the average new vehicle price in Canada. The panel noted the import cap is 49,000 units for this year (rising through 2030), and that Tesla is positioned to take the lion's share of that quota. Model Y is not expected to be imported from China to Canada as it already arrives from Giga Berlin under a preferred trade agreement.

5. **Model Y: Top-Selling Vehicle Globally and in Norway:** The Model Y was confirmed as the bestselling EV in the world in March. In Norway, BEVs represent nearly 98% of the total market, with the Model Y selling approximately 5,400 units and Model 3 approximately 2,000 units. BYD was noted to have posted its 10th consecutive month of sales decline, and Tesla passed BYD in Q1 2026 — though the panel noted the media will prominently report if BYD reclaims the lead.

6. **New Colors for Model 3 and Model Y:** Tesla released two new colors — Frost Blue and Marine Blue — for the Model 3 and Model Y in the US. Frost Blue was confirmed available on the Model 3 Performance trim. The panel noted this is one of the first recent color launches to debut in the US rather than Giga Berlin or Giga Shanghai.

7. **Tesla Brand Loyalty:** Tesla was cited as leading all automotive brands in owner return-to-purchase loyalty, ahead of Subaru and Toyota. The panel attributed this to software updates, FSD capability, safety ratings, low maintenance costs, and the seamless ownership experience.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Hits 10 Billion Miles:** Tesla's Full Self-Driving cumulative miles reached 10 billion, a milestone the panel noted as significant for unsupervised autonomy validation. As of the recording, the counter had already moved to 10.133 billion miles on Tesla's live website tracker, illustrating the pace of accumulation. The panel noted 42% (described loosely as "4% of vehicles FSD-based") are now running Tesla self-driving — though the phrasing in the transcript was ambiguous. FSD versions 13 and 14 were discussed qualitatively, with version 14 described as so capable that users don't go back after experiencing it.

2. **FSD Ethics and Pricing Debate:** The panel debated whether Tesla has an ethical responsibility to make FSD more widely accessible given its demonstrated safety benefits. Brian argued that paywalling supervised FSD at $100/month is arguably too restrictive given lives that could be saved, and proposed splitting supervised (potentially free or cheaper) from unsupervised (premium, akin to a chauffeur service). Matthew noted Tesla does zero advertising for FSD, calling it a missed opportunity. The panel also discussed frustration that FSD subscriptions do not follow the person across vehicles (e.g., on Turo rentals), with Matthew recounting paying a Turo owner separately for a one-month FSD subscription in Hawaii.

## Robotaxi

1. **Robotaxi Loyalty and Consumer Experience:** The panel discussed the expectation that once consumers use a Tesla robotaxi, they will not return to traditional ride-share platforms. The key value drivers cited were: faster arrival than Uber, lower cost, no tipping, and consistent vehicle/experience quality regardless of which unit arrives. No specific fleet counts or fare data were mentioned.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus 3 in the Pipeline:** Optimus 3 was briefly mentioned by John as part of a broader observation about the unprecedented number of simultaneous product releases Tesla is managing — alongside the Model Y L, Roadster, Semi, and potential Cyber SUV. No technical details about Optimus 3 were provided.

## Energy

1. **Solar and Battery Storage Cost Decline:** The panel discussed an IRENA report (cited via Sawyer Merritt) showing the cost of solar and wind with battery storage has fallen from $100/MWh in 2020 to $54–$82/MWh today. New coal capacity in China was cited at $70–$85/MWh, making renewable-plus-storage cost-competitive with new coal. The panel connected this to Tesla's Megapack business and broader energy transition momentum.

2. **EV Charging Lounges:** Tesla is building a 28-charger station designed as an experiential lounge, competing with convenience-store-style charging stations from other networks. The panel noted constraints on upgrading existing supercharger sites (insufficient grid capacity, leased parking lots) but praised the lounge concept where feasible.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla Stock Up 11% on the Week:** Tesla's stock rose approximately 11% over the past week, attributed partly to broader market optimism around trade war resolution and potentially to positive momentum from the OpenAI trial (where Elon Musk's odds of winning on Prediction Market platform Poly Market also improved). The panel warned against selling covered calls on Tesla given the potential for 10%+ weekly moves.

2. **XAI / SpaceX Restructuring:** XAI, previously a subsidiary of SpaceX, is being dissolved as a separate entity and folded into SpaceX as a division. The panel viewed this as a defensive antitrust posture — Elon's practice of opening up patents, offering FSD licensing to other OEMs, adopting the North American charging standard, and partnering with Anthropic (via the assistant integration) was cited as evidence of intentional competitive friendliness to reduce breakup risk. Historical analogies to Standard Oil and AT&T breakups were discussed, with Brian noting breakups often increase total enterprise value.

3. **Neuralink Surgical Robot:** Neuralink announced a new surgical robot capable of reaching any region of the brain, described as enabling a generalized neural interface to address any neurological condition originating in the brain. No financial or timeline details were provided.
