This video is a conversation between the host and a UK-based Tesla YouTuber ("Tesla Jigsaw") about why FSD has rolled out across several EU countries but not yet the UK, covering the RDW's approval process, French regulatory pushback over speeding, UK-specific restrictions on driver-assist maneuvers, and speculation on timing and cultural acceptance for both the UK and EU.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **RDW approval and EU rollout:** The guest states the Dutch regulator RDW gave FSD approval around April after roughly 18 months of testing and collecting around 100 million miles of data, and that FSD has since been approved in about eight (mostly smaller) EU countries, with the guest noting it has been "confirmed" to work well even in Amsterdam, which he calls one of Europe's most chaotic cities to drive (cyclists, pedestrians, trams).
2. **UK testing and shadow-mode data collection:** Tesla has reportedly tested FSD in London and at a complex roundabout ("magic ring") in Swindon, which the guest says FSD handled well; he also believes Tesla or UK owners may be collecting shadow-mode data at home via their cars, though he is not certain, speculating this data could be used to support a future UK regulatory push.
3. **France's speeding dispute:** The guest describes the French Minister of Transport's refusal, which centered on Teslas on FSD being flagged for speeding in 30 km/h zones; data cited by the guest claims human drivers speed in such zones about 82% of the time versus roughly 50% for Teslas on FSD. He also says Tesla has provided the French government with data from the "last TCMV meeting" and supplied vehicles for the minister to test personally. The host raises, with uncertainty ("I'm not sure... maybe I'm wrong"), a claim that Elon Musk said he had spoken with the French Minister of Transport, asking viewers to clarify.
4. **UK's "system-initiated maneuver" restrictions:** The guest criticizes an EU-derived UK regulation that restricts Autopilot/Advanced Autopilot lane-change behavior — requiring eye-tracking confirmation and manual turn-signal/steering-wheel contact, and aborting a lane change mid-maneuver if those conditions aren't met — calling it a safety downgrade he experienced while driving in Austria.
5. **EU-wide approval timeline and risk of a watered-down product:** The guest says EU rules allow a single member state's approval to extend FSD across the bloc, with a target of January 2027 for all EU countries. He worries regulators may still impose restrictions on system-initiated maneuvers, resulting in a "fully supervised" but functionally limited version of FSD compared to the US experience.
6. **UK approval outlook:** The guest estimates UK approval of FSD is possible within 12 months and would find it "embarrassing" if the rest of Europe gets approval by around January while the UK lags. He also notes the UK's Autonomous Vehicles Act technically permits robotaxis in London now, raising the possibility that robotaxis could legally arrive in the UK before supervised FSD/Autopilot does.

## Market & Competition
1. **Minneapolis driverless taxi conditions:** The host cites a report that Minneapolis's city council approved driverless taxis only on condition they include a safety driver able to take full manual control at any moment, which he criticizes as contradictory to the concept of driverless operation.
2. **In-car Grok voice assistant:** The host mentions that phone use is blocked while FSD is engaged, and describes using Grok's in-car voice integration (e.g., to find restaurants) as a highly effective alternative, contrasting it favorably with using Google Maps/search.
