## Autonomous Driving

- **Denmark approves FSD Supervised**, becoming the fourth European country to do so in approximately eight weeks, following the Netherlands, Lithuania, and Estonia. Denmark's Færdselsstyrelsen accepted the Dutch RDW provisional type approval after conducting its own independent technical review — notable because Denmark had previously raised concerns at the EU level about FSD's speed limit compliance and icy-road performance.

- **Netherlands FSD safety data (April 10 – June 5, 2026):** Over 23.6 million km driven, Tesla reported FSD Supervised was 3.5x safer than manual driving overall — 3.4x safer on highways across 16.6M km with zero collisions, and 1.6x safer on non-highway roads across 7M km with three collisions. Additional metrics: nearly 15x fewer automatic emergency braking events, 8.8x less harsh acceleration, 7.3x less harsh braking.

- **EU-wide approval delayed:** The June 30 TCMV meeting agenda shows only a continuation of discussions — no vote. The next realistic window is October 2026, with actual software rollout across the EU potentially slipping into early 2027. Critically, if the EU Commission formally rejects the system, the Dutch provisional approval becomes invalid after six months, pulling down all four national approvals with it.

- **German OEMs cede the field:** Mercedes-Benz paused Drive Pilot from upcoming S-Class and EQS facelifts; BMW discontinued Personal Pilot L3 entirely. Both are retreating to Level 2+ systems, leaving Tesla's FSD Supervised as the most capable driver-assistance system widely available in Europe. Belgium and Greece are reportedly moving quickly toward national approvals.

## Robotaxi

- **Cathie Wood** took an unsupervised Tesla Robotaxi ride in Austin and publicly endorsed the experience: "The fact that I was talking to you the whole time and didn't pay any attention to the ride itself means that I think it's completely safe; I'm excited for Tesla. I'm excited for Tesla shareholders." The post drew 6,500 likes and 829 reposts.

- **Texas fleet registrations at 59 VINs**, up from just over 40 approximately two weeks ago per the Texas Motor Carrier website. All 59 are currently Model Ys. This represents Tesla's maximum theoretical deployable capacity in Texas, not confirmed active vehicles.

- **Cybercab staging visible in Houston and Dallas:** A flyover of the Houston lot showed 21 Cybercabs staged alongside Model Ys. The Dallas lot showed dozens of additional Cybercabs lined up. No steering-wheel-free Cybercab has yet been confirmed on-site, which analysts flag as the bar for near-term commercial deployment expectations.

- **Cybercab testing now spans approximately 20 US states**, indicating broad geographic preparation ahead of commercial launch.

- **Arizona SAE Level 4 operating domain confirmed:** A Tesla first responder interaction plan for Arizona, dated April 24, 2026, lists the robotaxi service's design domain as SAE Level 4 across Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Mesa, Paradise Valley, Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tempe, and Maricopa County — a combined population of approximately 4.8 million.

- **Q1 city launch timeline under pressure:** Tesla's Q1 slide deck indicated launches in five cities within a specific window. With approximately 20 days remaining on that timeline, analysts assess the target as unlikely to be met.

- **Bhakdi on scale threshold:** If Tesla reaches 15,000 deployed Robotaxis, Bhakdi stated the share price will go "way above $700." He described himself as "pretty bullish, not very bullish" on the current June 2026 Robotaxi situation.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Model 3 RWD leads Edmunds real-world efficiency rankings:** The tested vehicle achieved 393 miles on a full charge versus its 363-mile EPA estimate — 30 miles (8.3%) beyond the official figure — and returned 21.7 kWh per 100 miles versus the EPA's 25 kWh rating, making it 13.2% more efficient than the EPA estimate and the most efficient EV currently in production in Edmunds' testing.

- **Tesla durability ranks 6th overall, 3rd among luxury brands** in an iSeeCars study of 174 million vehicles, with a 4.6% probability of reaching 250,000 miles. Tesla outranked Cadillac, Mazda, Subaru, Volvo, Mercedes, BMW, and Porsche. The luxury segment average was 3.2%. For the Robotaxi thesis, vehicle longevity directly improves fleet economics and useful-life DCF assumptions.

- **Tesla Semi European commercial buildout underway:** Tesla posted a Business Development Manager role for the Semi, based in Parsdorf near Munich, focused on B2B fleet sales and charging infrastructure. Commercial deployment in Europe is not expected before 2027.
