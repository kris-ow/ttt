The video argues that Tesla's robotaxi lead is widening as California's DMV forces Waymo off freeways and Tesla adds Cybercabs to the Texas registry at a record pace. Host Herbert and guest Brian White (FutureAzA) credit Tesla's FSD training data and manufacturing capacity, while warning that regulators could curb any operator after a single bad incident.

## Robotaxi
1. **Waymo freeway pullback:** California's DMV called Waymo's driverless freeway rides an imminent hazard to public safety. The cars kept driving into active construction zones, and one tried to get around highway patrol cars holding traffic back. Waymo then paused freeway rides in every city it serves, the second such pause this year. Waymo remains the larger service, at over half a million rides a week, and none of the freeway incidents hurt anyone.
2. **Texas registrations:** Tesla added 205 robotaxis to the Texas DMV registry in the past week against 24 for Waymo (136 over 30 days). That puts Tesla at about 1,500 registered and Waymo at about 1,300. Tesla also registered a record 200 Cybercabs in one day, bringing registered Cybercabs to 773. Herbert says Tesla had been registering Model Ys since October 28, which undercuts a theory that NHTSA's review of Tesla's Cybercab self-certification was holding up registrations. Brian expects Dallas Cybercab rides soon and thinks a few thousand Cybercabs could be serving paid customers by quarter-end. He also expects Las Vegas, with about 20 days left on that permit, and Phoenix.
3. **Cybercab on Waymo's home turf:** Photos show a Cybercab testing on the freeway in Chandler, Arizona, on freeways Waymo already serves. Brian says Tesla has been spotted with 50 mph highway behavior, and that Ashok Elluswamy named three things needed for FSD v15: 24/7 operation, different weather conditions and higher speeds. Herbert says the first two are done, with 24/7 now running in all cities. Brian does not expect Tesla to hold back on highways, since most of its FSD training miles are highway miles.
4. **Scaling gap:** Brian argues Waymo is constrained by upfit labor and donor-vehicle supply and cannot build 100,000 cars. Tesla has its own production capacity and can fall back on Model Y if a Cybercab part is wrong. Herbert shows a chart that he says tracks how long Waymo took to reach 46,000 driverless miles per week in Austin, which he says Tesla has already surpassed. Brian says the Austin network held up during a crowded weekend of Tesla fans, though prices were higher.
5. **Regulatory risk and strategy:** Brian says Tesla is not approved in California because it has not applied, and that once it does the process should be the quickest ever. Tesla has explicit permission in many states, and in most others there is no approval but also no denial. The fear is a state later imposing prohibitive rules such as $3 billion of insurance per vehicle. Brian reads the Texas and Florida first rollout as deliberate, with hostile states left for later. He says "we know that Tesla can operate robotaxis in five cities", while the Tesla Robotaxi account lists eight cities, running 24/7 since about November 12. He is less confident in NHTSA after the FAA grounding. He also asks why Tesla does not run more cars for more hours, which he reads as hardware or software caution rather than regulation.
6. **Backlash risk:** Herbert relays Jason Calacanis's post predicting that regulators will attack autonomy violently once job losses compound in Q1 and Q2 next year, and that one mistake risks an entire state. Brian counters that Waymo is not banned and has only reverted to earlier operations. He sees significant job losses arriving in 2028 or 2029, not 2027. He expects firsthand rides to blunt opposition, citing CES in January, where he says permits suggest Cybercabs may give driverless rides in the Boring Company tunnel. Herbert adds that lower robotaxi prices would help public acceptance.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD training data versus Waymo's approach:** Brian says Waymo's perception is fine but its approach hits edge cases, such as a Waymo reversing through a San Diego railroad crossing barrier with no passengers aboard. He believes Waymo is quietly building a new stack. He says Tesla's supervised FSD fleet has accumulated far more miles, mostly highway, so it has already met the highway edge cases Waymo has not. He adds that he and others have seen FSD v14 (not v15) handle comparable situations without such mistakes, and that FSD v15 is in the Cybercabs and Texas robotaxis. Herbert says neither Waymo nor Tesla can be assumed to have avoided every edge case.
2. **Phoenix crash:** A four-vehicle crash in Phoenix involving two Waymos was not Waymo's fault; the hosts attribute it to a human driver, with speed possibly a factor. They think a Tesla robotaxi probably could not have avoided it, and Brian still says he cheers for Waymo and other AV companies.

## Market & Competition
1. **Competitor outlook:** Brian is deeply skeptical of Rivian's autonomy approach and plans to watch its autonomy day on December 9. He says Waymo has to move to a neural-net approach, and it could do so given its Google compute resources.
