SpaceX's Q3 2026 earnings webcast covers record financial results driven by launch, Starlink connectivity, and a rapidly scaling "Super Intelligence" (SI) compute business, alongside Starship Flight 14 reaching orbit, plans for Flight 15's full recovery attempt, and a notably candid discussion of the status of SpaceX–Tesla merger talks.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Q3 results post major inflection:** Revenue was $14.6 billion, up 161% year-over-year from $5.6 billion; net income was $4.4 billion versus a prior-year net loss of $1.1 billion; adjusted EBITDA was $8.8 billion, up 306% year-over-year from $2.2 billion.
2. **Segment breakdown:** Space revenue was $1.8 billion (up 6% YoY, up 84% sequentially); Connectivity revenue was $5.2 billion (up 69% YoY, up 22% sequentially); SI segment revenue was $7.6 billion, up $6.7 billion YoY and $5 billion sequentially, including $7 billion from SI Solutions and Infrastructure ($608 million of which came from the Cursor acquisition, closed roughly mid-quarter).
3. **Capex and guidance:** Q3 total company capex was $19.6 billion; Q4 capex is now guided to $35–40 billion given accelerating compute buildout. Q1 2027 total company revenue is guided to $33–37 billion. Management reiterated a prior goal of $100 billion in annualized run-rate revenue by the end of 2026.
4. **Backlog and balance sheet:** Backlog totaled $63 billion as of September 30, with $17.5 billion already recognized as deferred revenue and ~$42 billion expected to be recognized within the next year. CFO Bret Johnsen emphasized SpaceX's investment-grade status, strong balance sheet, short compute payback periods, and multiple available debt capital pools to fund growth.
5. **Cursor stock-compensation detail:** Of the $60 billion equity value attributed to the Cursor acquisition, $23 billion was allocated to retaining key employees/replacing equity awards, to be recognized as stock-based compensation over roughly a 3-year vesting period. Q3 carried $1.2 billion of that expense, leaving SI segment operating income at $431 million, versus $1.6 billion excluding it.
6. **Tesla–SpaceX merger status, directly from Musk:** Musk said both companies have formed special committees of independent directors to evaluate a potential merger at shareholders' request, from which he is excluded as a conflicted party. He stated the special committees have not reached agreement on terms, cautioned against assuming a merger is a "certainty," and noted any Tesla-side approval would require a vote of 50% of all shares outstanding (not just those voted) — a "high hurdle." He said he personally believes combining the companies would be a good idea given overlapping opportunities in AI software, chip design, Terafab, robotics, and connectivity, but stressed the outcome rests entirely with the special committees and shareholders.

## SpaceX — Starship
7. **Flight 14 orbital milestone:** Starship's Flight 14 reached orbit for the first time and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites, delivering 26 terabits per second of Starlink bandwidth — equivalent to 10 Falcon 9 launches — from a ship that was only about half loaded.
8. **Flight 15 recovery attempt:** The next Starship flight, expected next month, will attempt to catch both the booster and the ship; if successful, it would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle. Musk called this "more likely than not" to succeed and said SpaceX expects continued flight attempts through year-end and an accelerating cadence next year. Gwynne Shotwell said SpaceX expects an on-orbit refueling attempt early next year and to begin flying Mobile V2 and Starmind satellites in 2027, with constructive FAA engagement expected to yield a Flight 15 license covering return-to-launch-site and tower catch for both stages.
9. **Cadence targets:** Musk said he expects Starship to fly weekly in the first half of next year, and twice weekly or more by the end of next year, as rapid/complete reusability is achieved.
10. **Louisiana spaceport:** SpaceX announced development of a new Starship spaceport in Louisiana, envisioned eventually with on the order of 20+ launch towers. Internal goal is a first Louisiana launch in two years (2028), with Musk calling 2029 "highly likely" for launching from the site at scale.

## SpaceX — Starlink
11. **Subscriber and market growth:** Starlink added 1.6 million consumer subscribers in Q3, reaching 13.6 million global broadband consumer subscribers (ending the quarter active in 174 markets, including new launches in Vietnam and Uganda). Monthly ARPU rose 5% sequentially to $69.
12. **Aviation momentum:** Starlink is now contracted for roughly 12,000 planes (nearly a quarter of available aircraft), with agreements with three of four major U.S. carriers and new international carriers (Lufthansa, Swiss, Copa, Korean Air, Austrian, Asiana) flying first Starlink-equipped aircraft in the quarter; aviation production rate has quadrupled since the start of the year and is expected to double again by year-end.
13. **Mobile expansion:** Starlink Mobile surpassed 22 million monthly users, up 37% quarter-over-quarter. SpaceX received FCC approval to launch 15,000 Mobile V2 satellites and announced a low-band spectrum acquisition intended to support Starlink becoming a major U.S. mobile carrier; near-term priorities include flying the Mobile V2 constellation through next year and integrating spectrum to turn on service in early 2028.
14. **Connectivity margins and roadmap:** Connectivity gross margin was 55%, including a one-time ~3-point benefit mainly from tariff refunds; excluding one-time items, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded roughly 2 points year-over-year. Each future Starship-launched V3 batch is expected to deliver over 20x the downlink capacity of current Falcon 9-launched satellites. Gwynne Shotwell said a few hundred V3 satellites in orbit (expected around mid-next year) should be needed before users notice meaningfully improved network performance.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
15. **Compute scale targets:** SpaceX ended Q3 with 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate compute capacity, expects to reach ~2.2 gigawatts by the end of October and 2.5–3 gigawatts by end of this quarter. Musk said he is "fairly confident" of bringing an incremental 10 gigawatts of compute online in 2027 (roughly 13 gigawatts total), and said there is a "non-zero," rising-probability chance of over 20 gigawatts online next year, with an internal plan targeting 20 gigawatts of incremental power/cooling infrastructure. He said SpaceX is tracking to bring roughly 200,000 GB300s online per month for the remainder of this year.
16. **Casting as a competitive moat:** Musk attributed SpaceX's compute buildout speed advantage partly to its advanced turbine-blade and vane casting capability (originally developed for rocket engines), saying turbine suppliers report blades/vanes are 12–18 months behind the rest of turbine production — a bottleneck SpaceX says it can bypass, giving it a lead difficult for competitors to replicate.
17. **Grok/Cursor traction:** Grok bot reportedly reached 800,000 paid weekly users earlier this month, more than doubling in the prior month; Grok enterprise customers with over $1 million in run-rate revenue (including Cursor) were up 50% quarter-over-quarter on a pro forma basis. A new bundled plan combining Grok, Grok bot, Cursor, and X Premium was announced. Musk said compute allocation between internal use and third-party rental is targeted at roughly a 50-50 split, flexible month to month. CFO Bret Johnsen said new compute deployments are generating a payback of under a year at current pricing, which is tracking slightly above the high end of the $30–50 per watt range cited last quarter; Musk added that a six-month payback period next year is "not impossible."
18. **Terafab chip ambitions:** Musk outlined internal (non-binding) targets of a first functional test chip (a SRAM-type test-line chip) at the Austin research fab around May/June next year, and an aspiration for volume production at the main Terafab site in Gibbons Creek around Q3 2028 (with Q4 2028 also within the realistic range). He called these "insanely fast" and said SpaceX is counting on continued acceleration from suppliers including ASML.
