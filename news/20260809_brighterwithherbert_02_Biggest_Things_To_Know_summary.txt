This video builds a 2027 SpaceX revenue forecast off the company's first-ever earnings call, using SpaceX's own guidance, Brian Wang's (Next Big Future) detailed model, Wall Street bank estimates, and the host's own back-of-envelope math, then walks through the four business lines (AI compute leasing, Starlink, Starlink mobile, Starship) driving that number, before closing with a bear case and a Tesla-investor framing.

## Financials
1. **2027 revenue range spans a signed floor to a speculative ceiling:** Tesla's SpaceX did about $19 billion in all of 2025 (referenced as prior-year context). SpaceX CFO Brent (per transcript, "Bret Johnson") guided to a $100 billion annualized run rate by December of this year, which Elon Musk said represents contracted business already signed and called "not a question mark," adding it would likely be higher. Goldman Sachs models the AI compute piece at roughly $34.5 billion for 2027; Morgan Stanley models the whole company at $160 billion but not until 2028. Brian Wang's base case projects $163 billion of 2027 revenue exiting the year at a $212 billion run rate, with a bull case (holding today's compute pricing) of $256 billion exiting at a $390 billion run rate. The host's own rough math, using Musk's cited $30–50 per watt per year monetization figure and a conservative 4 gigawatts leased on average, lands near $120 billion from compute alone. The host's working range is $130–250 billion, with $100 billion as the contractual floor.
2. **Q2 earnings beat and CapEx scale:** SpaceX reported Q2 revenue of $7.81 billion versus $6.81 billion expected (per Sawyer Merritt), up 92% year-over-year. Quarterly CapEx was cited at roughly $18–18.4 billion (per an App Economy Insights chart), with Brian Wang's model requiring about $138 billion of CapEx in 2027; the host notes ~$100 billion of cash on the balance sheet post-IPO and bond raise, but flags depreciation and a still-present AI segment operating loss ($1.3 billion in Q2) as real drags, with Wang's model showing cash flow trailing new CapEx until the second half of 2027.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Cloud/compute deals ramping fast, segment turns EBITDA-positive:** SpaceX signed cloud services agreements (Colossus/Colossus 2 compute rented by customers including Google and Anthropic) worth $14.1 billion in Q2, with $1.6 billion already recognized as revenue that quarter; an additional $6.7 billion of cloud deals were signed in the first few weeks of July. The AI/compute segment swung to roughly $1.1 billion EBITDA-positive this quarter from a $600 million loss the prior quarter. Wang's model has AI leasing revenue at roughly $5.5 billion this quarter, $16 billion in Q4, and around $30 billion in Q1 of next year.
2. **Compute capacity buildout and Nvidia exclusivity:** SpaceX ended June with 1.4 gigawatts of compute, expects to exceed 2 gigawatts by year-end, and per Musk is targeting closer to 10 gigawatts by the end of next year, with power/cooling infrastructure already lined up for 20 gigawatts. The company said it has gone exclusive with Nvidia, securing what it describes as a very significant share of Nvidia's GPU output next year. New compute capital is guided by the CFO to pay back in under a year — a figure Brian Wang notes only holds if compute pricing stays near today's ~$35 billion per gigawatt per year; if pricing decays, payback stretches toward 1.5 years, implying the bull-case revenue numbers require sustained high pricing.
3. **xAI-related products:** Grok 4.5 tripled token consumption in July; the Cursor coding-tool acquisition (cited at $60 billion) is expected to close within weeks, possibly as soon as next week per a report from The Information.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Consumer Starlink and V3 satellite step-change:** Starlink had 12 million subscribers at the end of June (double a year prior), adding 1.7 million in the quarter (a record), with pricing holding at $66/month and connectivity generating $1.7 billion of operating income in the quarter (39% operating margin per an App Economy Insights chart). The new V3 satellite is roughly 10 times more capable than V2 per Musk, and roughly 10 times as many will be launched, which a third-party analysis (Cern Basher) calculated as over 20x bandwidth increase per launch, implying roughly 100x total constellation bandwidth. Musk said even a 90% drop in monetization per bit would still yield a 10x revenue increase. A critical mass of roughly 1,000 V3 satellites is needed before the service change appears, targeted by Musk for around Q2 of next year; the first V3 is expected on the next Starship flight, tentatively end of this month.
2. **Enterprise/government growth and mobile spectrum expansion:** Enterprise and government Starlink revenue grew 108% year-over-year, with Gwynne Shotwell stating the company has never lost an enterprise customer. Aviation wins this quarter include American Airlines, Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus going live, with under 10% market penetration; over $6 billion in U.S. government contracts (mostly Space Force/Starshield) were signed in Q2 alone. Separately, the FCC approved transfer of EchoStar spectrum to SpaceX (65 MHz, 13x current spectrum), and next-generation mobile satellites — roughly 10x as many — start flying next year per Shotwell, with full EchoStar-integrated service switching on at the end of next year; the addressable market cited is the roughly $600 billion U.S. carrier business (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile), and SpaceX plans to deploy small cellular base stations on existing rooftop dish mounts rather than build towers.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Heat shield problem declared solved after Flight 13:** Flight 13 in July deployed 20 production V3 Starlink satellites, re-lit a Raptor engine in space, and achieved the softest splashdown yet with the ship still intact. Musk said he considers the heat shield problem solved and sees no remaining technical obstacles to full and rapid reusability, while hedging that he doesn't want to jinx it. The next flight is tentatively planned for the end of this month, targeting a tower catch pending regulatory approval. Starship's stated targets are roughly four times Falcon 9's payload at about a tenth of the cost, with infrastructure (Gigabay factories, accelerating Raptor production, new pads at Starbase, pad 39A, and pad 37) being built toward a stated goal of thousands of launches per year.
2. **Aspirational roadmap flagged as high-uncertainty:** Additional goals mentioned — a flight-a-day cadence within a year, robotic lunar factories, a mass accelerator, boots on the moon around 2028, an Artemis 3 docking next year, and the Starmine orbital AI data center satellite starting to launch next year — are characterized by the host as real engineering intent but likely to slip, consistent with Musk's historical date record; the 2027 revenue floor is not dependent on these.

## Market & Competition
1. **Bear case centers on compute pricing and execution risk:** The host frames compute pricing durability as the single biggest swing factor between bank estimates (~$34.5B) and bullish models (~$232B) for AI leasing revenue, and personally believes pricing holds or even rises next year. Other risks flagged: Musk's history of slipping timelines (cited alongside past robotaxi date delays), skepticism on achieving a flight-a-day Starship cadence, high CapEx needs relative to cash generation, and competition from Amazon's Kuiper constellation plus resistance from incumbent telecom carriers.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
