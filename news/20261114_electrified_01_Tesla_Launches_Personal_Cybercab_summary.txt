The host (Dillon Loomis) breaks down the surprise news that Tesla has begun inviting select customers — reportedly starting with Austin, Texas residents — to purchase a Cybercab for personal, non-commercial use, priced at $29,990 plus a required $299/month autonomy subscription. He walks through leaked screenshots, order confirmations, and terms-and-conditions details, while flagging many unresolved questions about geofence limits, insurance, charging, and how this interacts with Tesla's broader robotaxi fleet strategy.

## Robotaxi
1. **Personal Cybercab ownership program launches:** Tesla sent invite-only emails (shared by Austin real estate leader and Tesla club president Matt Holm) offering a Cybercab for personal use at $29,990 plus a $299/month autonomy subscription (one month included with purchase), with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit due immediately for eligible invitees. Tesla's official Robotaxi account confirmed select customers are being invited, with estimated delivery in December 2026, initially limited to Austin.
2. **Vehicle has no wheel or pedals, limited to one per customer:** Official images confirmed the version being sold has no steering wheel or pedals (not the test-vehicle "wheels and pedals" variant). Tesla limits orders to one Cybercab per customer unless Tesla agrees otherwise, and vehicles arrive with roughly 250 miles already on the odometer from testing/delivery transport, which count toward warranty limits.
3. **Operation strictly tied to Tesla-controlled geofence, non-transferable:** The Cybercab only operates within Tesla-enabled robotaxi geofences, which Tesla can expand, shrink, or move at any time; coverage may not match the broader robotaxi service area. Resale, transfer, or encumbering the vehicle requires Tesla's prior written consent, and Tesla may repurchase the Cybercab at its own determined fair market value at any time. Strictly non-commercial use is specified — no third-party ride-hailing, delivery, or rental use unless Tesla authorizes it.
4. **Subscription covers connectivity/support but not insurance, maintenance largely included:** The $300/month covers autonomous driving, vehicle connectivity, remote support, roadside assistance (including towing up to roughly 50 miles), and other Tesla-determined covered services — but explicitly excludes insurance, registration, parking, charging, and general cleaning, which the owner must pay. The host pushed back on commentators (naming Whole Mars and Alexandra Merz) who claimed the subscription includes insurance, calling this false per the written terms. Routine maintenance — tire/wiper/cabin filter replacement, windshield replacement, camera lens cleaning, inspections, water-repellent coating reapplication — appears to be covered under the subscription or purchase.
5. **Charging limited to DC fast charging only:** Per the agreement, Cybercab is only compatible with DC fast charging (including Tesla Superchargers), raising the question of whether home AC wall charging is excluded; the host believes the car could simply be sent out unoccupied to charge itself.
6. **Self-service and manual control features:** The Cybercab can autonomously drive itself unoccupied to a Tesla service center for scheduled maintenance and return when finished; uncovered repairs require owner approval. It includes touchscreen manual controls for very low-speed movement (described as up to about 2 mph), not intended for public roads, requiring a valid U.S. driver's license to use — relevant for maneuvering in driveways/parking lots.
7. **Tesla retains broad remote control and enforcement rights:** Tesla can remotely access, locate, restrict, control, or place the vehicle in a safe state; three or more strikes for improper/unsafe use trigger a one-week suspension of autonomous driving features (with no prorated refund). Modifications like wraps, tint, or wheel changes are restricted to Tesla-authorized providers, with violations risking subscription suspension.
8. **Open questions on commercial use, Starlink, and insurance:** A Tesla executive (Ashok Elluswamy) said the Cybercab will work unsupervised within the same geofenced areas as the robotaxi service, serving the owner's needs, and confirmed it can park in a driveway, but did not address questions about whether it could roam while waiting to be summoned. The host speculates that personal ownership may eventually allow owners to opt into Tesla's robotaxi network for extra income, though he believes Tesla will likely keep personal ownership and fleet/commercial programs as separate silos for now. It remains unknown whether these personally-owned units will include Starlink connectivity or a full voice-interactive Grok experience, and how auto insurers will handle liability for a driverless-by-design vehicle.
9. **Regulatory backdrop:** NHTSA's audit query into the Cybercab remains open with no public resolution; the host believes Tesla launching this program suggests confidence that the audit won't cause major issues, but flags a nonzero chance it could still delay December deliveries.
10. **MKBHD bet implications:** The host notes this launch likely means Tesla will win a public bet with tech reviewer MKBHD, who wagered Tesla would not sell the two-seat, wheel/pedal-less Cybercab to a regular customer for around $30,000 before 2027.
