## Energy

- **Largest Tesla energy storage deal ever — Nat Power:** Tesla and Nat Power signed a multi-year agreement covering 25+ GWh of Megapacks initially across Italy and the UK, targeting 100+ GWh across five projects total. Construction value is estimated at $4–5 billion with projected revenues exceeding $15 billion over 20 years — more than half of Tesla's entire 2025 deployment in a single contract. The deal also includes long-term revenue warranties via Tesla's Autobidder platform, and is structurally novel as the first battery storage contract financed and executed across multiple jurisdictions under a single integrated framework.

- **Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home virtual power plant:** The three companies announced an agreement aggregating 16.8 GW of flexible capacity — home battery systems from Tesla and Sunrun plus 8 million+ smart thermostats managed by Renew Home — targeting surging US data center power demand. Over 300 MW is immediately deployable in Virginia's "Data Center Alley," expected to grow to 500+ MW by 2030; if PJM accepts the proposal into its Reliability Backstop Process, over 1 GW unlocks immediately. Brattle Group analysis cited by the companies estimates better grid utilization could cut US electricity bills by $110–$170 billion over the next decade.

- **Q2 energy setup — UBS modeling 13.4 GWh:** Q1 2026 energy gross margin hit a record 39.5% on 8.8 GWh deployed. UBS estimates Q2 comes in at approximately 13.4 GWh — a 50%+ sequential jump — which the host of Herbert Ong's "Five Engines" video identifies as the most likely source of upside surprise at late-July earnings. Management has flagged some margin compression ahead due to tariffs on Chinese cells, so the record Q1 margin may not repeat.

- **Capacity buildout toward 130 GWh/year:** Lathrop runs ~40 GWh/year; Shanghai is ramping to a similar level; a new Houston-area Megapack factory expected online late 2026 at 50 GWh/year would push Tesla's combined storage manufacturing capacity toward approximately 130 GWh annually.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14.3.4 rolling out:** The new build carries identical release notes to its predecessor, indicating an incremental or stability-focused update rather than a feature expansion, per Sawyer Merritt.

- **EU-wide FSD vote pushed to October; Finland may move early:** The TCMV committee meeting scheduled for June 30 has no vote on the agenda — only further discussion — with the next realistic window for an EU-wide decision now estimated around October 2026. However, Finland's transport authority (Traficom) indicated it may approve FSD independently ahead of the EU-wide decision, stating the system "is considered to have significant potential to reduce accidents caused by human factors." Approximately 6,500 Finnish Teslas carry the required hardware.

- **FSD Supervised now in 13 countries; Spain likely next:** Individual European country approvals have come roughly monthly — Netherlands (April 10), Lithuania (May), Estonia, Denmark (June 9), Belgium (June 10) — with Spain reportedly next in line. Australia and New Zealand received FSD v14 for the first time this quarter. Each approval activates near-zero marginal cost software revenue on cars already on the road.

- **China FSD — full approval targeted Q3 2026:** Limited FSD launched in China on May 21; full fleet-wide rollout is Tesla's stated Q3 2026 target. FSD is priced as a one-time purchase at ~64,000 yuan (~$9,400 USD) with no subscription currently.

- **Tesla Semi spotted with ground-truth sensor rig in Sunnyvale:** A Semi was photographed carrying validation hardware used to cross-reference camera perception against real-world ground truth — an early but concrete step toward autonomous capability on the Semi. The late-2025 Semi redesign already included 10 external cameras, a driver-monitoring camera, and new side-mounted pods, which The Tesla Space argues were deliberate autonomy-readiness upgrades. FSD's supervised fleet has now logged 11.3+ billion miles (1 serious collision per ~5.3M miles vs. 1 per 660K miles for human drivers), providing the underlying stack Tesla would port to trucking.

## Robotaxi

- **One-year Austin anniversary — real tech, small fleet:** Austin's driverless service now covers ~245 square miles including the airport. Paid robotaxi miles nearly tripled in Q1 2026 to approximately 1.7 million cumulative miles. Fleet remains small; per RobotaxiTracker.com the active fleet (all modes, including Bay Area supervised) stands at 748. Over the first year, Tesla reported 17 collisions to federal regulators; the two most serious were caused by human teleoperation takeovers at under 10 mph, not the autonomous system.

- **Infrastructure permits signal scaling geography:** Building permits paint a clearer scaling picture than current fleet counts: a 36,000 sq ft "Cybercab phase 2 car wash" hub in Las Vegas (May 2026); a 24-acre, 35,000 sq ft depot in Irving, Texas with 212 autonomous vehicle parking spots; a 51,000 sq ft depot in Grand Prairie, Texas; private robotaxi-only charging hubs in Chandler and Mesa, Arizona (one with 56 fast-charge stalls); and a Nevada filing for permission to operate up to 5,000 robotaxis in Clark County within a year. The Irving site is currently stalled in zoning.

- **Cybercab NVH validation automated:** After footage of Cybercabs running test-track laps, Tesla supply chain commentator Wes Moral confirmed "Yes, this is happening" when asked whether the vehicles' onboard microphones and accelerometers are autonomously collecting noise, vibration, and harshness data and transmitting it centrally for ML analysis — removing humans from the manual processing step.

- **Cybercab structural manufacturing advantage:** Per FutureAzA's Brian, the Cybercab is expected to contain roughly 4,000 total parts — approximately half the Model 3's 8,000–10,000. Waymo's sensor suite alone reportedly consists of ~4,000 parts, illustrating the structural cost gap between purpose-built and sensor-bolted-on robotaxi approaches.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus V3 supply chain activating in China:** IT Home reported Tesla is collaborating with Merl Automation/Allied Automation on harmonic reducers and joint modules (co-produced with Shenzhen Kadali and Rayong), and with Asia Optical on the robot's vision system using spherical and aspherical lens technology. New components from Asia Optical are expected to enter mass production in H2 2026 into 2027. Both sources frame this as supplier-level mass production beginning ahead of Tesla's own ramp.

- **Giga Texas Optimus factory: steel to four floors, ~4,000+ feet long:** Drone tracking by Joe Tegtmeyer shows the dedicated Optimus facility at Giga Texas with steel erected four stories, projected to approach the full length of the main plant. Target capacity is ~10 million robots/year; high-volume production is aimed at 2027.

- **Fremont V3 line conversion on track for late summer:** The former Model S/X line at Fremont is being converted for Optimus production, expected to complete around late summer 2026, after which higher-volume V3 production is supposed to begin. V3's headline upgrade: degrees of freedom per hand doubled from 11 to 22, with actuators moved into the forearm to reduce hand weight and increase speed.

- **V3 reveal targeted late July or early August:** Elon Musk has stated the reveal will be deliberately timed with the start of production to limit competitor reverse-engineering. The host of Herbert Ong's video explicitly cautions that the 2026 targets of 50,000–100,000 units will not be met — "it's going to be much less" — and recommends grading Optimus on factory buildout progress rather than unit count for now.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Q2 deliveries report July 2–3; Goldman at 420K:** Wall Street consensus is ~400K; Goldman Sachs raised its estimate to 420,000, saying deliveries are "tracking ahead of consensus." Q1 2026 was 358K; Q2 2025 was 384K, so a low-400s print would be both sequential and year-over-year growth. Europe is carrying the quarter (+108% YoY in May registrations); China is tracking ~-11% YoY and the US is down mid-teens percent YoY.

- **Model YL targeting August–September North American launch:** Per Not a Tesla App (unconfirmed by Tesla), the longer-wheelbase Model Y is targeting an August–September 2026 launch built at Giga Texas. It adds ~6 inches of wheelbase, a 2-2-2 seating configuration with heated/ventilated captain's chairs in row 2, and is expected to be priced around $54,000 — roughly $4,000 above the current AWD Model Y.

- **Cybertruck earns 2026 IIHS Top Safety Pick Plus:** The Cybertruck is the only truck to receive the designation this year, following Tesla updating the front underbody structure and driver footwell geometry. The Model Y retains its Top Safety Pick Plus; the Model 3 holds only Top Safety Pick.

- **Tesla Model 3 and Model Y: #1 and #2 on 2026 American-Made Index** for the sixth consecutive year, covering both EVs and ICE vehicles — a domestic manufacturing narrative with particular relevance in the current tariff environment.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX–Tesla merger mechanics debated:** Jo Bhakdi (economist/investor, on Herbert Ong's channel) argues a "merger of equals" structure — no fixed price floor — is unworkable at this scale because Tesla shareholders would be asked to vote on a payout ranging from ~$900B to ~$4T in SpaceX shares with no certainty. His proposed solution is a "ratchet": a fixed-dollar floor (e.g., $600/share for Tesla, ~50% premium to the ~$400 price at recording) that adjusts upward for SpaceX holders if its market cap rises above the implied acquisition value. A complicating factor: Bhakdi flags a ~400% SpaceX share float unlock entering the market between August and mid-October 2026, creating acute selling pressure in exactly the announcement-to-vote window. Host speculates announcement could come as early as end of July 2026 with a shareholder vote in November.

- **Lucid cutting ~18% of US workforce:** Lucid filed notice of a workforce reduction covering full-time employees, contractors, and hourly production workers, expected to be substantially complete by end of Q3 2026.

- **Mitsubishi will not develop new EVs independently:** Mitsubishi announced it will pursue "collaboration" EVs — including one to be built by Foxconn in Taiwan — rather than fund new ground-up EV development, illustrating continued OEM struggles to independently finance EV programs.

- **Sunrun shares up 26%** on the Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home VPP announcement, reflecting market enthusiasm for AI power demand as a recurring revenue stream for distributed battery fleets.
