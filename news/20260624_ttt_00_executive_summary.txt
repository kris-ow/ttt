## Energy

- **Tesla–Nat Power: largest Megapack contract ever signed.** Nat Power signed a multi-year agreement covering 25+ GWh of Megapack deployments initially in Italy and the UK, targeting 100+ GWh across five projects total. Construction value is estimated at $4–5 billion; projected revenues exceed $15 billion over 20 years. For scale, Tesla deployed roughly 46 GWh for all of 2025 — this single deal exceeds half that figure, and dwarfs the previous record contract of ~15 GWh.

- **Autobidder revenue warranties included.** The Nat Power deal bundles long-term revenue guarantees via Tesla's Autobidder software, with Tesla effectively backstopping energy trading returns on the deployed storage. The host (Electrified) frames this as a notable statement of confidence in Autobidder's commercial performance.

- **Structural first: multi-jurisdiction, single integrated framework.** The host notes this is the first time battery storage has been contracted, financed, and executed across multiple European jurisdictions under one framework — simultaneously addressing manufacturing slots, grid connections, permitting, and financing.

- **Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home VPP: 16.8 GW targeting data center demand.** The three companies announced an agreement aggregating hundreds of thousands of home batteries (Tesla and Sunrun) plus flexible capacity from 8 million+ smart thermostats (Renew Home) into what they claim is the largest distributed virtual power plant in the US. More than 300 MW is available for immediate deployment in Virginia's "Data Center Alley," growing to at least 500 MW by 2030. No new hardware, land, or interconnection is required by off-takers — capacity can be activated in months. The deal also commits capacity to PJM's proposed Reliability Backstop Process, which could unlock over 1 GW immediately if accepted.

- **Q2 energy setup is strong.** Q1 2026 energy gross margin hit 39.5% — a record, nearly double automotive's ~21%. UBS models ~13.4 GWh of storage deployed in Q2, a 50%+ sequential jump. Tesla's combined annual storage capacity is tracking toward ~130 GWh/year once the Houston Megapack factory (~50 GWh/year) comes online late 2026.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14.3.4 rolling out.** The new build carries the same release notes as the prior version, signaling an incremental or stability update rather than a feature expansion, per Sawyer Merritt.

- **Finland may approve FSD ahead of October EU-wide vote.** Finland's transport authority Traficom stated the system "is considered to have significant potential to reduce accidents caused by human factors and to make traffic flow more smoothly," and indicated it may recognize the Netherlands' prior approval before the TCMV bloc-wide vote. Approximately 6,500 latest-generation Teslas in Finland would be eligible. The EU TCMV next discusses the approval process June 30th, with a formal vote still expected around October 2026.

- **EU-wide vote delayed; individual country approvals continue.** No vote is on the June 30th TCMV agenda — only further discussion. Nordic regulators (Sweden, Finland, others) have raised objections around speed limits and icy conditions, pushing the realistic EU-wide window to October or later. Spain is reportedly next in line for individual recognition. Each country approval activates FSD at near-zero marginal cost on compatible vehicles already in market.

- **China FSD: limited launch live since May 21st; full approval targeted Q3 2026.** Full fleet-wide rollout remains pending regulatory approval. China FSD is priced at ~64,000 yuan (~$9,400 USD) as a one-time purchase. Tesla's prior February/March target slipped; Q3 is now the stated goal, flagged as Tesla's target, not a regulatory commitment.

- **Tesla Semi spotted with ground-truthing rig in Sunnyvale.** The sensor equipment — used to create high-accuracy environmental reference data for validating autonomous perception — was observed on a Semi near Tesla's facilities, per The Tesla Space. The Semi's late-2025 redesign already incorporated 10 external cameras and new side-mounted camera pods, which the host argues was a deliberate autonomy-readiness platform decision. No deployment timeline has been given; this is characterized as early-stage validation work. Highway freight is highlighted as a structurally favorable early autonomy use case: long-haul trucks can exceed 100,000 miles/year versus ~12,000 for passenger cars, compounding efficiency and safety gains across a fleet.

- **FSD fleet milestone: 11.3+ billion supervised miles accumulated.** Tesla's safety data shows approximately one serious collision per 5.3 million miles under FSD supervised, versus one per 660,000 miles for U.S. human drivers, per The Tesla Space.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus Version 3 reveal targeted late July or early August 2026.** Elon Musk has stated the timing is deliberate — announcing alongside the start of production to limit competitor reverse-engineering. Version 3's headline hardware upgrade: degrees of freedom per hand doubled from 11 to 22, with actuators moved into the forearm to reduce hand weight and increase speed, plus four fingertip sensors added.

- **Giga Texas Optimus factory: steel up four floors.** As of mid-June 2026, the dedicated Optimus factory at Giga Texas has four floors of structural steel erected. Drone tracking (Joe Tegtmeyer) shows the building projected at 4,000+ feet in length — approaching the full span of the main Giga Texas plant. Target capacity is ~10 million robots/year, with high-volume production aimed at 2027.

- **Fremont conversion line on track for late summer.** The old Model S/X production line at Fremont is being converted to Optimus and is expected to complete the transition around late summer 2026, at which point higher-volume Version 3 production is supposed to begin. A pilot line has been running since January 2026.

- **Supply chain taking shape for Optimus 3.** Chinese supplier reporting (via IT Home) identifies Allied Automation (harmonic reducers, joint modules, jointly produced with Shenzhen Kadali and Rayong) and Asia Optical (spherical and aspherical lenses for the robot's "eyes") as active Optimus 3 suppliers, with mass production at the supplier level expected H2 2026 into 2027, per Electrified.

- **Honest baseline: no meaningful unit production yet.** Both Herbert Ong and Electrified's host are explicit: units operating today number in the hundreds and are used for internal learning, not productive work. The 2025 build target was missed. The 50,000–100,000 unit target for 2026 will not be met — Herbert's framing: "much less." Recommended investor metric: track the physical buildout, not current unit counts.

## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab structural parts advantage over Waymo.** Per FutureAzA's Brian (cited by Electrified's host), the Cybercab is expected to have ~4,000 total parts — roughly 50% of the Model 3's 8,000–10,000. Waymo's sensor suite alone reportedly consists of ~4,000 parts, added on top of a third-party vehicle. The host argues this illustrates a fundamental manufacturing cost advantage for Tesla's purpose-built robotaxi approach.

- **Cybercab NVH validation automated.** Following footage of Cybercabs on the Austin test track, industry watcher Wes Moral confirmed: "Yes, this is happening" — meaning Cybercabs are autonomously collecting audio and vibration data on test tracks and sending it to Tesla for machine learning analysis, removing humans from manual NVH processing. Electrified's host acknowledges some inference is involved but characterizes it as a meaningful automation of the validation loop.

- **Infrastructure buildout: permits signal geographic scaling.** Key filings highlighted by Herbert Ong: a 36,000 sq ft "Cybercab phase 2 car wash" facility in Las Vegas (permit May 2026); a 35,000 sq ft, 24-acre hub in Irving, Texas with 212 autonomous vehicle parking spots; a 51,000 sq ft support depot in Grand Prairie, Texas; private robotaxi-only charging hubs in Chandler and Mesa, Arizona (one with 56 fast-charging stalls); and a Nevada filing for permission to operate up to 5,000 robotaxis in Clark County within a year. The Irving site is currently stalled in zoning. These are filings and proposals, not completed facilities.

- **One-year Austin safety record: system itself has not caused a serious injury.** Over the first year of robotaxi operations, Tesla disclosed 17 collisions to federal regulators. The two most serious were both caused by human teleoperation takeover at low speed — one scraped a fence, one tapped a construction barricade, both under 10 mph. One minor injury total (to a safety monitor). The 17 includes incidents where the robotaxi was struck while stationary.

- **Active robotaxi fleet: 741 per RobotaxiTracker.com** (Bay Area 611, Austin 104, Dallas 15, Houston 11) as of June 24th.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Q2 deliveries report July 2–3; Goldman estimates 420,000.** Goldman Sachs raised its Q2 estimate to 420,000, saying deliveries are "tracking ahead of consensus." Wall Street consensus is ~400,000. Q1 2026 was 358,000; Q2 2025 was 384,000. A low-400s print would be sequential and year-over-year growth. Europe is the bull case: May registrations +108% year-over-year, with France posting its best May ever. Soft spots: China down ~11% year-over-year for the quarter; US down mid-teens year-over-year.

- **Model YL targeting August–September North American launch.** Per Not a Tesla App (unconfirmed by Tesla), the longer-wheelbase Model Y — already available in China, Australia, and New Zealand — is targeting Giga Texas production and a US launch this fall. The YL is ~6 inches longer in wheelbase and ~7 inches longer overall, enabling a 2-2-2 six-seat configuration. Expected pricing ~$54,000 before incentives (~$4,000 premium over AWD Model Y).

- **Cybertruck earns 2026 IIHS Top Safety Pick Plus.** The Cybertruck is now the only truck to receive the designation. Tesla updated the front underbody structure and driver footwell geometry to achieve the rating. The Model Y retains its Top Safety Pick Plus; the Model 3 holds only Top Safety Pick (no Plus, partly due to headlights).

- **Tesla Model 3 #1, Model Y #2 on 2026 Cars.com American-Made Index** — for the sixth consecutive year, per Sawyer Merritt and Electrified. First Ford on the list: #13 (Expedition). First GM: #25 (GMC Canyon).

- **Slate Auto prices barebones EV truck at $24,950.** The Jeff Bezos-backed startup's compact electric pickup — 205 miles of range, 181 hp, RWD only, no modem, hand-crank windows, unpainted for vinyl wrap — has 180,000+ reservations and is opening pre-orders. Deliveries targeted Q4 2026. Break-even is ~80,000 units/year against 150,000-unit annual capacity at its Warsaw, Indiana plant; the CEO says every unit built will be gross-margin positive. Currently building ~3 vehicles/day while completing federal certification.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX–Tesla merger structure debate: ratchet vs. merger of equals.** Jo Bhakdi (economist and investor, appearing on Herbert Ong's channel) argues a pure merger of equals — with no fixed price floor — is unworkable at Tesla's scale because it asks shareholders to vote on a payout ranging anywhere from ~$900 billion to ~$4 trillion in SpaceX shares with no certainty. His proposed solution: a "ratchet" mechanism, using $600/share (~50% premium to Tesla's ~$400 price) as a working example, guaranteeing Tesla shareholders a floor while adjusting upward for SpaceX shareholders if SpaceX's value rises. Bhakdi flags a compounding risk: a ~400% SpaceX share float unlock entering the market between August and mid-October 2026 would create acute selling pressure in exactly the window between a potential announcement and a shareholder vote. The host speculates an announcement could come as early as end of July 2026, with a shareholder vote potentially in November 2026. Bhakdi estimates Musk would retain ~78–80% voting power in the combined entity, which structurally requires SpaceX to be the surviving legal entity.

- **Sunrun shares up 26% on VPP announcement.** Wall Street read the Tesla–Sunrun–Renew Home deal as transforming Sunrun's battery fleet into a recurring AI-power revenue machine, per Electrek. The author cautions the 16 GW figure is a theoretical aggregate and the 300 MW immediately deployable in Virginia is "a fraction of a single hyperscale campus," with realization dependent on customer enrollment, utility approvals, and PJM acceptance.

- **Lucid cutting ~18% of US workforce.** The reduction covers full-time employees, contractors, and hourly manufacturing workers, with restructuring expected substantially complete by end of Q3 2026.

- **Mitsubishi concedes it cannot independently fund new EV development.** The company will pursue "collaboration" EVs instead, including one to be built by Foxconn in Taiwan, per Sawyer Merritt.

- **ArcBest Tesla Semi real-world efficiency: 1.55 kWh/mile.** Against Tesla's rated 1.8 kWh/mile and competing electric semis (eCascadia, Volvo, Mercedes) at ~2.1 kWh/mile, ArcBest's fleet pilot came in ~25% more efficient than rival electric trucks, per FutureAzA. ArcBest has expanded its Semi fleet following the pilot.
