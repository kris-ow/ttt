This video is a recording of Tesla's Semi truck production launch event at Giga Nevada, featuring Tesla executives (including Lars Moravy) walking through the new factory, the truck's engineering changes, and the business case for electric heavy trucking. The core thesis is that Tesla has re-engineered the Semi for volume production with lower cost, higher reliability, and validated real-world range, aiming to disrupt an industry where diesel trucks are a small share of vehicles but a large share of emissions.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Nevada Semi factory ramping to 50,000 trucks/year:** Tesla is producing the redesigned Semi out of Giga Nevada, a 1.7 million square foot facility, with plans to build 50,000 trucks a year (about 1,000 a week) and add more than 3,000 jobs during the ramp. The factory is highly vertically integrated — raw coils in, stamping on-site, in-house 4680 cells shipped from Giga Texas, automated cell unloading and consumption, and a large electric monorail system moving the vehicle skateboard, with no human touchpoints in the automated battery line.
2. **New Semi switches to in-house 4680 cells, cutting mass while holding range:** The truck moves from purchased 2170 cells to Tesla's in-house 4680 cells, lowering pack mass and kilowatt-hours (and therefore cost) while maintaining the same range through vehicle-wide efficiency gains. Executives state the truck has demonstrated over 500 real-world miles on a single charge at 82,000 lbs fully loaded (not derated), and could reach roughly 600 miles at a half load (~60,000 lbs). A new standard-range variant offering 325 miles of capability, with lower mass, will also be built at the factory alongside the long-range version.
3. **Drivetrain, steering, thermal, and lubrication overhauled for cost and reliability:** The Semi reuses a modified Plaid powertrain with a steel-caged rotor (replacing carbon-fiber sleeving) and a bar-wound stator shared with Cybertruck, cutting drive-axle mass by about 80 kilograms while improving reliability; efficiency improved from roughly 2,000 watt-hours/mile in early development to about 1.6–1.7 Wh/mile fully loaded (~25% reduction). The truck switches from three separate oils (motor, gear, hub) to an oil-free hub and a single common oil for gearbox/motor rated to last over a quarter million miles. It also moves to fully electric power steering (replacing hydraulic) using Cybertruck-derived steer-by-wire technology for precision and autonomy-readiness, and adopts a new "indirect thermal system" shared in core components with the Cybercab's cooling system, sealed at the factory with no exposed refrigerant lines.
4. **Fleet learnings and customer feedback drove design changes:** A pilot fleet of just under 200 trucks has logged 17.5 million miles, informing changes such as adding a roll-down window (replacing an insufficient pop-out window design), acoustic glass and extra insulation to cut cabin noise, and driver-profile personalization (seat, mirror, UI settings) tied to a driver's phone profile. Tesla reports fleet uptime of more than 98% this year and cites Supercharger network reliability of roughly 99.8%.
5. **Charging infrastructure buildout and unit economics pitch:** Tesla plans more than 30 Megacharger stations online by year-end, representing over 200 megawatts of charging capacity, alongside its existing 80,000+ Superchargers worldwide; a lower-power (~125 kilowatt) overnight base charging product is also offered for depot charging. During the event's live demo, a plugged-in truck reportedly gained about 60% charge in roughly 25–30 minutes while still drawing around 800 kilowatts. Executives pitch the truck's value proposition around lower and more stable electricity costs versus volatile diesel prices, reduced maintenance (no emissions after-treatment system, no exposure to changing emission standards), and note heavy trucks are about 1% of vehicles on the road but represent about 16% of emissions; Tesla says it will be its own first customer, deploying trucks into its own freight operations, with deliveries starting this week.
