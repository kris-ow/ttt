This episode of futureaza features a Giga Texas site-walk conversation with Joe Tegtmeyer, reviewing August 10 drone footage to assess Cybertruck production recovery, Cybercab/robotaxi testing infrastructure, the joint SpaceX-Tesla chip fab, Cortex 2 data center buildout, and new factory construction. The core thesis is that Tesla's heavy capex at Giga Texas — spanning chips, robotics infrastructure, and new production lines — is laying groundwork for multiple future revenue streams (robotaxi, Optimus, Roadster) that could justify current spending.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production shows signs of restart after parts dispute:** Tegtmeyer reports a large number of Cybertrucks spotted in a holding lot on Giga Texas's east side — the first time he's seen this many there in some time — suggesting production may be resuming after a stretch of low output. He attributes the slowdown partly to a surge in orders following a limited-time $59,000 all-wheel-drive offer in February, and partly to a legal dispute with a tooling/parts supplier that withheld access, some of which he says is still lingering. Vehicles held in the lot are likely missing a part or two but otherwise built, per Tegtmeyer.
2. **Roadster production line reportedly coming to Giga Texas "very soon":** Tegtmeyer says new production-line equipment observed on the east side may support the Roadster in addition to the Cybercab line, noting that a Tesla designer told Jay Leno the previous day that the Roadster was coming "very soon" during a ride-along; Tegtmeyer notes the program has otherwise had almost no public information leak out.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab test track activity and NVH refinement:** Footage shows Cybercabs doing continuous laps on a large oval test track at varying speeds, simulating stop signs, alongside an infield with deliberately rough road surfaces. Tegtmeyer says this appears aimed at refining noise/vibration/harshness performance, possible noise-cancellation features, and structural refinement of the new vehicle, while racking up many test miles close to the factory.
2. **Older Cybercab prototypes being crash-tested and retired:** Wrapped (non-gold) prototype/engineering Cybercabs — earlier units seen publicly around the country — are being crash-tested (pulley-impact, rollover, pole, wall tests) or disassembled as Tesla extracts final engineering data before retiring them, mirroring a similar retirement process Tegtmeyer says occurred with early Cybertruck release candidates. He notes crash testing on such vehicles is expected to continue for years, including replicating real-world incidents.
3. **Cybercab production line visible behind newly built factory walls:** New construction on the east side of Giga Texas houses what Tegtmeyer identifies as the relatively recently installed Cybercab production line, alongside continued equipment additions.
4. **A-pillar design advantage cited for driverless vehicles:** The host notes Cybercab A-pillars can be built much thicker than in human-driven cars because there's no human need to see out of them — only the car's sensors need visibility — allowing a structural safety advantage.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Cortex 2 nearing completion, functioning as "Optimus University":** Tegtmeyer describes Cortex 2, under construction since roughly the start of 2025, as the facility used to train Optimus's AI (and to a degree FSD), now nearing completion; it went partially operational in April using roughly half its planned Megapack and cooling capacity, with the second half currently being installed. He states the facility will use around 260 Megapacks once fully built out, plus large cooling towers and a new adjacent water-reclamation facility to reduce water use. He notes Cortex 2 was exempted from a new Texas state pause on new data centers because it is nearly complete.
2. **Robot (Optimus) production building rising rapidly at Giga Texas:** A new four-floor building near Cortex 2, estimated at roughly 3,000 feet long and 1,000–1,100 feet wide (smaller footprint than the main Giga Texas factory but comparable total square footage due to four floors), is under fast-paced steel construction, differing from Giga Texas's original phased build approach by erecting the full steel structure before pouring floor concrete. Tegtmeyer notes rounded (rather than 45-degree beveled) building corners, a design change from the main factory whose reason is unconfirmed (the host jokingly suggested EU regulations).

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX-Tesla advanced technology chip fab under construction as precursor to larger Terafab:** A joint SpaceX-Tesla facility on Giga Texas's east side, newly designated the "east support facility," is described as a rapid-iteration chip design center that will feed designs into the larger Terafab being built roughly 130 miles east. Chips developed there are intended for Optimus (paired with the nearby Optimus body factory and Cortex training facility) and for SpaceX's terrestrial data centers (including Colossus in Memphis and newer facilities) as well as new orbital AI data center satellites. A Tesla-affiliated speaker frames the buildout partly as a hedge against geopolitical/supply-chain risk given that most current chip production comes from Taiwan.

## Energy
1. **New large storm-water pond serves site resilience and future capex protection:** A newly constructed, permanently lined pond is being built for storm-water management, draining via a large manifold system to the Colorado River; Tegtmeyer says this and grade-raising work are designed to protect Giga Texas infrastructure from flooding similar to the Lake Travis overflow event that occurred in Austin in July, which would have flooded much of the site under the factory's original grading.
