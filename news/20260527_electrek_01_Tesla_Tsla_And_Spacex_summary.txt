Electrek reports that Elon Musk is reportedly considering merging Tesla and SpaceX, framing it as the fourth in a pattern of billion-dollar self-dealing transactions between companies Musk controls. The article argues this would be the largest such deal yet, raising serious fiduciary concerns for Tesla shareholders.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX IPO context and merger odds:** SpaceX filed its S-1 on May 20 and is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX on June 12, targeting a valuation of up to $1.75 trillion and raising approximately $75 billion — described as the largest IPO in history. Tesla's market cap currently sits around $1.6 trillion. Wedbush analyst Dan Ives puts merger odds at "80 to 90 percent" by early 2027; Kalshi prediction markets assign only 33% odds before May 2027. A combined entity could exceed $3 trillion in market cap. SpaceX reported revenue of $18.7 billion and a net loss of $4.9 billion ahead of the IPO.

2. **Pattern of self-dealing transactions:** The article catalogs three prior billion-dollar transactions: (1) Tesla's 2016 acquisition of SolarCity for $2.6 billion in stock, where Musk chaired both boards; other Tesla directors settled related litigation for $60 million. (2) Musk's $44 billion Twitter acquisition in 2022, which Fidelity later marked down ~65%; by October 2024 the platform was valued at roughly $9–10 billion. In March 2025, xAI acquired X for $33 billion ($45 billion including $12 billion in debt). (3) Tesla disclosed a $2 billion investment in Musk's xAI in January 2026; shortly after, SpaceX acquired xAI in a deal valued at approximately $250 billion. Musk subsequently admitted xAI was "not built right."

3. **Structural conflict of interest:** Musk holds approximately 20% of Tesla's equity but controls 85.1% of SpaceX's voting power through a super-voting share class. The article argues this asymmetry means Musk effectively negotiates merger terms with himself, with Tesla shareholders absorbing disproportionate risk. Tesla shareholders are already suing Musk for breach of fiduciary duty over the xAI investment.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues this potential merger represents the "logical culmination" of Musk using Tesla as a "piggy bank" for private ventures, contending that Musk's playbook — shuffling assets between entities he controls to stamp higher valuations on combinations — benefits his private holdings at Tesla shareholders' expense. Lambert is skeptical of Dan Ives' pro-merger thesis and notes Ives "has been extremely wrong in predicting Tesla's progress in robotics."
