This video from Dillon Loomis of the "Electrified" channel covers several breaking developments across the Tesla and SpaceX ecosystems, including Elon Musk's comments on orbital AI data centers, the SpaceX IPO and its implications for Tesla shareholders, robotaxi deployment updates, and a notable Tesla stock upgrade from JP Morgan. The core thesis is that while market noise around SpaceX and macroeconomic pressures are creating short-term volatility in Tesla stock, the fundamental Tesla story — particularly around robotaxi ramp and potential SpaceX merger — remains intact and misunderstood by the market.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas Motor Carrier VIN registrations rising:** The Texas Motor Carrier website now shows 51 registered VINs for Tesla's robotaxi service, up from 42 just a few days prior — an increase of nine vehicles. Dillon notes this represents the total number of vehicles registered and eligible for deployment, not necessarily all active at any given time.

2. **Unsupervised robotaxi activity paused in North Austin:** Community reporter "Cyber Owl" noted zero unsupervised robotaxis in North Austin since the day prior to her Thursday post. She reported her ride did include a safety monitor, the car lacked a robotaxi badge, wait times remain long, and pickup/drop-off logic still needs improvement.

3. **Cybercab steering wheel question:** Dillon highlights a key open question: what is involved in removing the steering wheel and pedals from the Cybercab before deployment? He speculates Tesla will want the Cybercab deployed as intended — without a steering wheel — for its debut experience. Since all Cybercabs currently seen in Austin, Houston, and Dallas still have steering wheels, he suggests deployment of the true driverless Cybercab form factor may be weeks away. He also raises unanswered questions about whether the removal can be done at any Tesla service location, select locations only, or via mobile service.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Belgium FSD supervised homologation test completed:** Tesla has officially completed the required 5,000-kilometer supervised FSD test in Belgium. The regulatory burden now shifts to the administration for a formal homologation advice. The Belgian mobility minister expressed support for the fastest possible homologation in Flanders and Belgium.

2. **Tesla AI team at CVPR:** Tesla's AI team presented at CVPR this week. Ashoke gave a presentation and stated the full discussion would be uploaded within a few days.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model Y North America upgrade:** Tesla has announced that all Model Y trims sold in North America will now come standard with a black headliner and the larger 16-inch center touchscreen, replacing the previously standard gray headliner. Dillon notes the 16-inch screen is also improved in quality, not just size, and expresses hope these upgrades will eventually extend to the Model 3.

2. **Tesla Roadster delayed to August:** Per The Information, Tesla is pushing the Roadster demo to August. The Roadster will use cold gas thrusters co-developed with SpaceX — a previously known detail. Dillon is skeptical the August timeline will hold.

## Market & Competition

1. **JP Morgan Tesla price target upgrade — with caveats:** JP Morgan raised its Tesla price target from $145 to $475 after new analyst Rajat Gupta took over coverage from the long-bearish Ryan Brinkman. Dillon flags a key conflict of interest: JP Morgan is part of the underwriting syndicate for SpaceX's IPO, giving them a direct financial incentive to maintain a positive relationship with Elon Musk. Jamie Dimon's recent in-person interview with Elon is cited as further evidence of this newly aligned relationship. Dillon characterizes the upgrade as JP Morgan "moving out of Tesla Fantasy Land" rather than a purely independent analytical call.

2. **Tesla stock down ~7% on macro conditions:** At the time of recording, Tesla stock was down nearly 7%, but Dillon attributes the move largely to broad market weakness — the NASDAQ was down over 4% — and Tesla's historical beta of 1.5 to 2.3. He also notes that better-than-expected labor market data likely means the Federal Reserve will lean toward tightening rather than loosening, which is a headwind for longer-duration companies like Tesla.

3. **SpaceX IPO and S&P 500 inclusion timing:** The S&P 500 will not allow SpaceX early entry into the index, meaning if SpaceX IPOs this month, the earliest it could be added to the S&P 500 would be June 2027. Dillon notes some speculation that this delayed inclusion could accelerate a Tesla-SpaceX merger, but he views this as a secondary issue and does not expect it to materially alter SpaceX's trajectory.

4. **SpaceX Google cloud deal — $920 million/month:** A SpaceX SEC filing revealed a cloud services agreement with Google covering approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs and related components. Google agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million per month starting in October of this year through June 2029, with capacity ramping up through September at a reduced fee. Either party may terminate after December 31st of this year with 90 days' notice. This represents over $11 billion in annualized revenue. Combined with a previously disclosed Anthropic deal running at roughly $15 billion annually, these two deals alone exceed SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue of just over $18 billion.

5. **SpaceX IPO and Tesla shareholder allocation:** Erade has a process for IPO share allocation for SpaceX specifically for Tesla shareholders who have held Tesla in their account for at least 10 years. Dillon notes this is limited to one platform and requires a lengthy holding period, falling short of what many Tesla shareholders had hoped for.

6. **Potential Tesla-SpaceX merger confidence:** Dillon states he is confident Elon will not let Tesla shareholders "draw the short straw" in any potential merger or acquisition scenario, even if it occurs before Tesla has fully scaled its robotaxi ramp.

## Financials

1. **SpaceX revenue context for AI compute business:** SpaceX's total revenue in 2025 was just over $18 billion. The newly announced Google deal alone at $920 million per month would exceed $11 billion annually. Combined with the Anthropic deal at roughly $15 billion annually, these two contracts far surpass SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue, illustrating how transformative the compute rental business could be as a revenue driver.
