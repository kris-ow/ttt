This episode of the show (hosted in this installment by Cern Basher, standing in for the regular host) opens with commentary on a recent aborted SpaceX Starship test flight, then moves into an extended roundup of Tesla FSD user-experience videos, closing with a detailed walkthrough of a Plug In America EV owner satisfaction survey. The throughline is that both SpaceX's launch engineering and Tesla's FSD software are demonstrating real-world capability gains, while broader survey data shows very high EV owner loyalty and satisfaction (with likely under-representation of Tesla in the sample).

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Starship test flight aborted mid-flight, host frames it as a success story:** SpaceX aborted a Starship launch attempt the day before recording, which the host says dropped the stock roughly $5 (about 2.5%) amid shareholder alarm. The host argues the abort was actually a strong engineering result: onboard systems detected an anomaly in multiple engines and shut down all 33 engines within milliseconds, which he contrasts favorably with a separate incident where a competitor's launch pad ("stage zero") was destroyed in an explosion described as equivalent to a nuclear blast. He also referenced a historical multi-engine Soviet moon rocket that suffered cascading engine failures leading to vehicle loss, as context for why large engine counts carry risk. Per Elon Musk (relayed by the host), the vehicle and pad could potentially be ready to attempt another launch as soon as the following Monday, though the host says he "wouldn't bet on" that specific date.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **93-year-old driver regains independence via FSD:** A Tesla-produced video features a 93-year-old woman who had been driving less due to fatigue; her son describes FSD as having expanded her mobility and independence, letting her drive across San Diego County to see family after 75 years of driving. The host shared a personal anecdote that FSD similarly helped his wife continue driving at night after a retinal tear made oncoming headlights briefly blinding/distorting.
2. **First-time riders react to FSD:** Multiple compilation videos (credited to creators DJ, Kristen, and Cyber Mike) show new users experiencing FSD for the first time, including navigating highway merges, an exit turn, and autonomous parking-spot selection, with riders expressing nervousness that turns into comfort within one or two drives.
3. **Cybertruck parking-obstacle course test:** A creator (Electric Cyber Solarmer) ran an FSD "parking escape test" for a Cybertruck, navigating a steep hill and a tight gap next to a house; FSD initially aborted one path before finding a workable route through a narrow gap.
4. **Nighttime deer avoidance:** A video (credited to Joey Bagonuts) shows FSD detecting a deer crossing at night while traveling near 47 mph and swerving into the other lane to avoid it; the host argues the multi-camera system likely detected the deer before a human driver could, due to camera height/field of view and near-instantaneous image fusion across cameras.
5. **FSD responds to police officer's hand signal:** A video from creator Nate (Tesla Driven Lifestyle) shows FSD correctly interpreting a small, subtle hand-wave gesture from a police officer directing traffic and proceeding accordingly — both hosts call this a notably difficult perception task.
6. **FSD backs up to accommodate a long turning truck (FSD 14.3.4):** Another Nate video shows the Tesla, several cars back in a queue, reversing slightly on its own initiative as a long truck maneuvers into the lane, coordinating with reverse lights on the vehicle ahead and a side view of the truck — illustrating fused multi-camera awareness. The host separately recounted a personal incident on FSD in Tennessee where the car accelerated and shifted lanes to avoid a car he estimated was approaching from behind at roughly 130 mph, which he says he didn't see coming himself.
7. **FSD navigates a blocked-road standoff without a traffic officer:** A video shows FSD navigating a complex multi-vehicle scenario (a tractor, cyclist, van, and a non-yielding oncoming car) around a median island with road work, waiting appropriately and using the island for protection — both hosts said they weren't confident FSD would handle it well.
8. **Hardware 3 vehicle completes coast-to-coast FSD 14 "light" drive:** A team driving a 2020 Model 3 with roughly 180,000 miles on hardware 3 completed a roughly 4,255-mile (drivers state "roughly 5,000 miles" diagonally) journey from Key West, Florida to Ruby Beach/Cape Flattery, Washington on an early beta version of FSD 14 "light," with no steering, brake, or pedal inputs from the driver. The trip is part of a larger planned journey (originally starting in Chattanooga, continuing toward Alaska), with the team roughly 60% through that longer route and about 9,000 miles remaining per their own account.
9. **David Moss's FSD intervention-free streak reaches ~13,000 miles:** Community member David Moss's current FSD streak has reached about 13,000 miles, surpassing a prior streak he'd held near roughly 12,800 miles; the host notes that across roughly 25,000 miles spanning his two streaks, about 99.9% was on FSD with minimal manual intervention.

## Market & Competition
1. **EV owner survey shows very low intent to return to gas:** A Plug In America survey (about 4,205 total respondents, ~3,109 EV drivers) found 89.8% would choose another EV, only 0.5% would go back to gas, and 96.7–96.8% of respondents across satisfaction measures (safety, convenience, reliability, cost) rated EV ownership highly; Tesla owners reported loyalty of 96.6%, similar to the overall EV population, which the host says makes Tesla's loyalty rate the highest among EV makers.
2. **Survey suggests Tesla may be under-represented in the sample:** Tesla Model Y was the most-cited primary vehicle but only at about 11% of respondents, with Model 3 and Model S also appearing; both hosts argue this likely understates Tesla's actual share of the US EV fleet, and question why "self-driving" wasn't offered as a distinct purchase-consideration category.
3. **Concerns fall sharply after EV ownership begins:** Pre-purchase concerns dropped substantially post-purchase across categories — e.g., battery range concern fell from about 70% to 27%, price concern from about 44% to 8.6%, and home charging access concern from about 27.6% to 4% — though cold-weather range concerns reportedly persisted.
4. **Tesla Supercharger network rated far more reliable than rivals:** In the survey, about 20–21% of Tesla Supercharger users reported encountering non-functional chargers in the past year versus roughly 52% for other networks; the host said this Tesla figure seemed too high based on his own extensive road-trip experience, since FSD/the car interface flags non-functional stalls before parking.
5. **EV research sources skew toward outlets seen as Tesla-critical:** In survey data on where EV shoppers research vehicles, automaker websites (~65-66%), EV-specific sites/forums (~65%), and YouTube (~52%) ranked highest, with X/Twitter far down the list; the host noted several top sources (EV-specific outlets, Consumer Reports, Edmunds, Reddit, Kelley Blue Book) are, in his view, generally unfavorable toward Tesla.
