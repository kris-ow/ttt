## Brief
- Cybercab passes 1 million unsupervised miles at Sept 3 launch event

- FSD approved in Slovenia, EU-wide vote expected around Oct 6
- Tesla Semi unveiled for Europe, ~550 km range

- Unconfirmed report: Tesla ordered parts for ~5,000 Optimus units

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's robotaxi program hit 1 million unsupervised miles as of the September 3 Cybercab launch, up from 380,000 disclosed on the Q2 call — implying roughly 620,000 miles added in six weeks. Unsupervised operation now spans six cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) with roughly 200 vehicles; Waymo separately announced 200 million autonomous miles the prior week.
- Rider screenshots show Cybercab pricing 35–40% below Model Y and roughly half of Uber on identical Austin routes ($7.77 vs. $12 Model Y in one case), though high demand later pushed some Cybercab prices above Model Y with over an hour wait — a supply constraint, not a fixed discount, and Tesla has not published an official per-mile rate.
- Texas DMV Cybercab registrations rose from 45 to 49 in days; total automated-vehicle registrations hit 432. Independent VIN-based estimates (Herbert, futureaza) suggest Tesla has built 2,000–3,000 Cybercabs nationwide, with perhaps 1,000 dedicated permanently to FSD validation rather than commercial service — his own estimate, not confirmed.
- Cybercab spec sheet shows a 47.66 kWh pack and ~6.1 mi/kWh (Tesla's impact-report figure) versus ~4 mi/kWh for Model Y; Musk called the car "optimized for lowest possible cost per mile, fully loaded." ARK Invest projects ~25¢/mile at scale versus ~70¢/mile to own a car.
- A financial-analyst deep dive (Cern Basher, via Herbert) argues Cybercab's design choices — two seats, no paint shop, front-wheel drive — target lifetime cost per mile over vehicle life rather than sticker price; his model shows fleet cost for 1 billion passenger miles could fall roughly 67% if vehicle life extends from 300,000 to 1 million miles.
- Cybercab is touring Japan for public display (Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya through late September) — a marketing push, not a deployment milestone.

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA opened an audit query into Tesla's Cybercab FMVSS self-certification process, covering an estimated population of 10,000 vehicles — a review of certification methodology, not a defect probe. TSLA dropped about 6% the following Friday.
- FSD Supervised gained approval in Slovenia, the sixth EU country and first new approval in roughly three months; an EU-wide technical committee vote is expected around October 6, needing 15 countries covering 65% of the population. France, Sweden, and Germany have raised specific concerns.
- FSD 14.3.9 began rolling out with active collision avoidance and wheel-grab intervention if a driver is distracted or disengaged; requires Hardware 4 and an active subscription, highway-only under 85 mph. Rollout was briefly paused Tuesday before resuming at small scale.
- A separate guest (Doctor Know-It-All, via futureaza) cites Tesla's EU safety submission as showing FSD roughly four times safer than a human driver overall and about half the accident rate within the same vehicles — an unverified claim attributed to the guest, not confirmed Tesla data.

## Financials
- FSD take rate in North America reportedly rose from roughly 5–10% to around 50% since shifting to a $99/month subscription — a guest estimate (futureaza) citing roughly 170,000 new paid subscribers in one recent quarter and a similarly large number the next, though the host cautions Tesla hasn't disclosed a clean take-rate breakdown.
- Morgan Stanley estimates Tesla's autonomous Semi trucking software could generate $12,000–$18,000 per truck monthly (at $0.85–$1.00/mile over ~18,000 miles), versus roughly $100/month for consumer FSD today; a fleet-wide revenue figure was referenced but not confirmed in the source.
- Speculation (unconfirmed) floats tiered FSD pricing — cheaper supervised tiers alongside a premium for unsupervised driving — as a plausible future Tesla move, not an announced plan.

## Electric Vehicles
- Model Y remains the global sales leader, up 19.6% year-to-date through July per Focus2Move. Giga Berlin is running under 6,500 vehicles/week against a ~7,500/week mid-October target, adding three mandatory extra shifts.
- China August retail sales were 50,000 units, down 12.4% year-over-year but up 84% from July; Tesla ranked fifth domestically. Tesla China announced $1,500–$5,000 discounts on in-inventory Model Y/3 through September 30.
- Tesla launched the Model Y Performance in China starting at RMB 369,000 (~$55,000), deliveries beginning within two weeks.
- Tesla unveiled the European Tesla Semi: ~550 km range, no side mirrors, 1.0 kWh/km consumption.

## Energy
- Tesla secured an approximately $800 million Megapack order from Zhero for 3 GWh of storage capacity in Italy.

## Humanoid Bots
- Chinese outlet GMN News reported, citing supply-chain sources, that Tesla placed parts orders in early September for about 5,000 Optimus units, with supplier guidance calling for 1,000/week in September ramping to 2,500/week by year-end — unconfirmed by Tesla, and the host stresses no 2026 production number has been officially given.
- Tesla has open Palo Alto listings for a hand design engineer and a "mass manufacturable hand" program manager, reflecting hands as Optimus's current bottleneck.
- Musk told the G20 innovation meeting (Sept 1) that AI could lift the global economy 20–30%, and projected "well over a billion humanoid robots in 10 years," later posting that AI and robots will more than double the global economy within a decade.
