The article, citing The Information, reports Tesla is planning a demonstration of its long-delayed Roadster as early as this month at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas rocket test site, where the car would be operated remotely with no driver and lift off the ground using SpaceX-developed cold gas thrusters, with spectators kept several hundred yards back due to noise.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Demo scaled back from original vision:** Sources told The Information that Tesla originally planned a far more elaborate demo — the Roadster racing up a magnetized roller-coaster-style ramp, driving upside down, righting itself, and hovering — which was previewed to Elon Musk before being delayed and scaled back. Musk has reportedly told staff internally that the demo will be hard to pull off and could go wrong, but will be entertaining regardless. It's unclear whether Tesla will livestream the event or release edited footage afterward.

2. **Design origin and development history:** The article recounts that in 2022 two internal design teams pursued different Roadster concepts — one based on a stretched Model S Plaid platform, the other an ultralight carbon fiber tub construction. Musk reportedly chose the carbon tub approach, prompting Tesla to hire engineers from Italy including former Lamborghini body engineering head Stefan Nothdurfter (who has since left Tesla). The design reportedly went through a Lamborghini Countach-influenced phase favored by design chief Franz von Holzhausen before settling into a more conservative version; a running two-seat prototype with butterfly doors was built late last year. The "A-71" thruster code name is said to reference the Lockheed SR-71 Blackbird, an aircraft von Holzhausen reportedly wanted the car to evoke.

3. **Limited-edition product, not a consumer car:** The version to be demoed reportedly won't be street legal and could cost hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars; Tesla has internally discussed selling it to a small number of buyers via a program modeled on Ferrari's XX Programme. Tesla also reportedly considered a single-seat version with a thruster in place of the passenger seat, and as currently designed a person likely cannot drive the car while thrusters are firing due to noise.

4. **Reservation holders still waiting:** Tesla took $50,000 reservations for the standard Roadster and $250,000 for the Founders Series in 2017 and has pushed the delivery timeline at least eight times since, per the article. Marques Brownlee and Sam Altman have both publicly said they canceled their reservations. Tesla filed new Roadster trademarks in May, described as the only concrete evidence of progress visible from outside the company.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert is highly critical, arguing the demoed vehicle isn't a real product since it can't be sold publicly, can't be driven with its signature feature active, and will be extremely expensive, while thousands of early reservation holders remain unfulfilled after nearly a decade; he characterizes it as "more demo than product," comparing it to Robotaxi.
