This is a casual, comedic weekly "Giga iOS" update from the futureaza channel, mixing drone footage speculation about Giga Texas construction with reader questions and a running joke segment ("wrong answers only"). Substantive Tesla content is thin, centered on a newly opened public Megacharger site and some construction observations tied to Semi production.

## Energy
1. **First public Megacharger site opens:** Tesla opened its first public Megacharger site in Bloomington, California (about an hour west of Los Angeles), using V4 cabinet hardware; a Carson, California site was actually first announced but the Bloomington hub went live first. The hosts note existing Megachargers elsewhere are privately owned and not publicly accessible.
2. **Bidirectional charging still absent:** In response to viewer comments, the hosts state Tesla Semi does not yet support bidirectional charging (which could theoretically power something like a hospital), noting other EV makers already offer it and hoping Tesla adds it in the future — but with no timeline given.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Semi charging time estimate:** Responding to a viewer question, the hosts estimate a Tesla Semi could charge in roughly two-and-a-half to three hours on a V4 Megacharger cabinet, noting the charger hardware differs in cooling design from car Superchargers even though cabinets look similar, and that whether a Semi can use a public Supercharger-style stall at all is uncertain.
2. **Giga Texas Semi lot footage shows movement:** New drone footage of the Semi test/staging area showed only three semi trucks parked where seven were seen previously, which the hosts interpret as trucks being shipped out; footage also showed new HVAC ductwork installed, concrete paving progress near an entryway, growing piles of crated/tarped equipment, and employee cars present even on a weekend, which the hosts speculate indicates weekend calibration work under a time crunch.
3. **UK Autopilot criticism addressed:** Responding to a viewer complaint about poor lane-keeping and junction handling from "advanced autopilot" in the UK, the hosts clarify that Tesla's older Autopilot has not been under active development for some time (Tesla shifted focus to FSD instead) and that Full Self-Driving is a separate product not yet approved for use in the UK.
