The video addresses reports that Anthropic, reportedly SpaceX's largest customer at about a third of last quarter's revenue, may not renew its compute contracts after mid-next year. Host Herbert and guest Larry Goldberg review Elon Musk's and CFO Bret Johnsen's reassurances, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's comments, and SpaceX's orbital compute plans. Their thesis is that the Anthropic risk is overblown and that SpaceX's speed, vertical integration and Starship reusability put it in a unique position in AI infrastructure.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Anthropic concentration risk:** Anthropic is reportedly SpaceX's largest customer, about a third of last quarter's revenue. Notes from Anthropic's analyst meetings reportedly said it might not renew after mid-next year. Once the initial fixed periods end at the start of next year, either side can terminate on 90 days' notice. Musk said on DealBook that Anthropic tells him it will most likely keep leasing and wants about another 10 gigawatts, though Anthropic has not confirmed that figure. He added that Anthropic has a worst case in mind where extreme restrictions on AI limit its ability to use the compute. SpaceX is currently adding about half a gigawatt per month. Musk also said SpaceX expects zero sales to OpenAI.
2. **Anthropic's own comments and the cancel-option framing:** Asked whether it might shift compute away from SpaceX, Amodei said the future can't be known but that Anthropic considers the compute high quality and loves working with SpaceX. Goldberg says he heard in meetings that Anthropic has a backup option, not a plan to use it, and that the language reflects standard S-1 risk disclosure. He stresses that having an option to cancel is very different from saying it will cancel. He is adding to his SpaceX position.
3. **Financing the buildout:** Johnsen said SpaceX can fund much of the expansion from operating cash flow, using customer deposits, extended supplier payment terms and tighter cash management. Hosting deals pay back in less than a year, sometimes much faster. SpaceX has also shown access to debt markets: investment-grade bonds issued in June and a $40 billion credit line with Apollo and Goldman Sachs for GPUs, whose public syndication reportedly began that day.
4. **Demand and competitive position:** Johnsen said SpaceX sees only rising compute demand. He attributes its edge to low cost and speed from vertical integration, building and engineering in-house. It didn't need large long-term contracts up front or outside funding, so it can be more active in the spot market for higher returns. He says it can deliver on committed dates months ahead of competitors. Johnsen added that if SpaceX moves according to next year's predictions, it could become one of the biggest, if not the biggest, AI infrastructure players. Goldberg says even SpaceX will be pressed to supply compute if Grok keeps growing.
5. **Orbital compute timeline:** Johnsen outlined first orbital compute satellites flying next year (2027), constellation scaling in 2028, and significant computing in orbit in 2029. Goldberg believes the first launch could come as early as the first quarter of next year and says the rest of the industry expected 2029. Musk argues an AI satellite is simpler than a Starlink V3 communications satellite: mainly a large solar panel and a radiator, plus a compute unit about a quarter the size of an NVL72 rack. He cites roughly 250 W/m² for the solar panels and about 1,200 W/m² radiated by the radiator, so roughly five times as much solar area as radiator area. SpaceX has over 11,000 satellites in orbit, which he says is about twice as many as the rest of the world combined. Goldberg says he is 100% confident SpaceX can deploy space data centers at significantly lower cost than on the ground.
6. **Brownsville data center, permits and Microsoft:** Cameron County commissioners voted 3-2 against tax breaks for SpaceX's $24 billion Brownsville data center. SpaceX sought 10 years of 100% abatement and said in its filing that it would invest elsewhere without it. Goldberg calls it politics and immaterial next to the asset's value, after touring the large site. SpaceX also received an emissions permit for the project's power plant: 35 gas turbines in the initial phase, about 750 megawatts, as Texas resumed considering data center permits. The host also cites Satya Nadella saying Microsoft would rent SpaceX compute if it has capacity, with the deal hinging on whether Musk is right about space data centers.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Ship catch:** The host and guest call the recent catch of the Starship upper stage the last big element, and say it makes orbital compute viable. The Super Heavy booster aborted its catch this time, though the guest notes three prior successful booster catches. Goldberg frames full reusability as a historic turning point and argues SpaceX will be a leading provider of space-based compute for years.

## Energy
1. **Tesla transformer product:** The host and guest highlight a Tesla product, Power Core, aimed at transformers, which they describe as a major bottleneck for energy buildout. Musk said Tesla had been planning the business for about three years, with launch in 2027. Goldberg argues Musk anticipated energy as the limiting factor for compute, and SpaceX has built large generation capacity as a result.
