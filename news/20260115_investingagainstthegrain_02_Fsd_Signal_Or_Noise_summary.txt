A panel of Tesla-focused commentators reacts to Elon Musk's announcement that Tesla will stop selling FSD outright after February 14th, moving to a subscription-only model, debating whether this reflects a planned business-model pivot toward "transportation as a service" or is timed around a California DMV legal deadline. They also discuss FSD version 14.3 timing, the reasons behind Tesla's slow robotaxi geographic expansion, a US House committee hearing on federal AV regulation, and rough comparisons of Tesla's robotaxi fleet to competitors.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD moves to subscription-only after Feb 14:** Elon Musk posted that Tesla will stop selling FSD outright after February 14th (a Tesla-imposed deadline about a month from the video's publish date), with FSD available only as a monthly subscription thereafter; no new price was stated. One panelist (Omar, cited by the hosts) noted the date coincides with a deadline for Tesla to appeal or seek court review in a California DMV case over Tesla's use of the term "Full Self-Driving," while others argued this was a long-planned strategic shift rather than a reaction to the lawsuit, since Musk has long said direct FSD sales would end once the product approached full unsupervised readiness.
2. **Supporting signals cited for a subscription/bundling pivot:** Panelists pointed to Tesla's expanding rental program (recently adding cities beyond the original set), a new bundled premium package, monthly windshield/tire insurance offerings, and Tesla insurance expanding to additional states (including Florida and Tennessee) after being stuck at 12 states for roughly three and a half years, as evidence Tesla is building toward bundled monthly subscription pricing (FSD + insurance + vehicle access).
3. **FSD 14.3 timing and rollout sequencing debated:** Panelists estimated under 50% (with one giving 20–40%) probability that FSD version 14.3 — described as a major upgrade some believe could be a near-final version — ships within about a month. One panelist argued Tesla will likely roll 14.3 out first to the supervised robotaxi fleet (using safety drivers for extra logging/shadow-mode testing) before pushing it to the broader customer fleet, and separately recalled Ashok Elluswamy mentioning on a past earnings call that Tesla trains some FSD models on data localized to specific regions, which would make broad simultaneous rollout less useful for testing.
4. **Possible reasons for slow geographic/robotaxi expansion:** Beyond localized model training, panelists speculated Tesla may be intentionally delaying wider robotaxi expansion to multiple cities to avoid a high-profile safety incident ahead of pending Congressional/NHTSA action on a federal AV framework, so as not to jeopardize favorable regulation.
5. **US House committee hearing on federal AV framework:** A bipartisan House committee hearing (cited by the panel as occurring this week) featured testimony framing a federal autonomous-vehicle framework and the "Self Drive Act" as necessary for US competitiveness with China, referencing NHTSA's voluntary safety self-assessment framework; one witness argued the current caps on the number of exempted fully autonomous vehicles (no steering wheel/pedals) make the exemption program largely unusable, and a speaker's remarks were read by the panel as an implicit call to remove those caps. Panelists flagged a risk that any resulting legislation mandating multi-sensor (lidar/radar) approaches could disadvantage Tesla's camera-only strategy.
