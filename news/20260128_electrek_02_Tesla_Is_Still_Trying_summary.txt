The article (an Electrek opinion/analysis piece) argues that Tesla's Q4/FY2026 earnings letter again uses misleading language to imply it operates driverless robotaxis in the San Francisco Bay Area, when in fact California law does not permit this and Tesla's Bay Area vehicles are human-driven.

## Robotaxi
1. **Earnings letter language scrutinized:** Under a "Robotaxi" heading, Tesla's Q4/FY2026 letter stated it "began testing driverless Robotaxis in Austin in December and began removing the safety monitor from customer rides in January on a limited basis," then separately described Bay Area operations as a "ride-hailing service" rather than driverless — a distinction the author says is designed to blur the line between the two for investors, since "Robotaxi" is used as a trademarked brand name rather than a literal description.
2. **Musk's "few cities" claim disputed:** The author notes Musk recently said Tesla's "rolled out Robotaxi service in a few cities," but argues Tesla currently operates only two geographic regions (Austin and the Bay Area), not "a few."
3. **Austin driverless rides called extremely limited:** Tesla says it began removing safety monitors from some Austin rides in January, moving the driver to a chase vehicle instead; the author claims some riders have been unable to find any unoccupied (truly driverless) vehicle since the announcement.
4. **No California AV permit:** The California Public Utilities Commission reportedly told Electrek (when checked again for this article) that there has been no change in Tesla's status since a prior statement that "Tesla does not hold a Drivered Pilot AV permit from the CPUC, so it cannot use a drivered AV in passenger service... Tesla is not allowed to test or transport the public (paid or unpaid) in an AV with or without a driver." Tesla instead operates in the Bay Area under a Transportation Charter Party (TCP) carrier registration, the same used by normal taxis, with human drivers actively operating Tesla's FSD-equipped vehicles.
5. **Permit applications elsewhere:** Tesla has applied for permits in Arizona (which the author says appears similar in scope to its existing California permit) and, per Musk's statements, in Florida and Nevada.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comparison to Waymo:** The article states Waymo operates driverless taxis in six US metro areas as of last week, versus what the author characterizes as zero-or-one for Tesla depending on interpretation. A top-voted reader comment (not the article's own claim) adds that Waymo is growing over 400% annually and could approach a billion dollars in revenue this year.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla is deliberately using ambiguous, technically-defensible wording ("Robotaxi" and "Full Self-Driving" as brand names, "ride-hailing" rather than "driverless" for the Bay Area) in its investor-facing earnings letter to make its autonomous driving progress appear further along than it is, calling it an attempt to pump the stock. The author also contrasts this with Musk's past public statement that Tesla would have Robotaxis in at least 47 metro areas by end of 2025, and his newer claim (made on the earnings call) that Robotaxis would cover 25-50% of the US population by the end of this year, expressing skepticism ("I guess we'll see... if the wolf is actually there this time").
