Elon Musk pushed back the Tesla Roadster unveil again during the Q1 2026 earnings call, saying it may debut "in a month or so" — marking at least the eighth delay since the prototype was first revealed in November 2017.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Latest delay and stated reason:** On the Q1 2026 earnings call, Musk said the Roadster may debut "in a month or so" (implying late May or June 2026), blowing past the "late April" timeline he had set just weeks earlier. He cited "testing and validation" as the reason, saying Tesla needs to ensure nothing goes wrong with the demo. This is a new excuse; previous delays were attributed to prioritizing the Cybertruck, Semi, and Optimus.

2. **Delay timeline:** The prototype debuted in November 2017 with production promised for 2020. Subsequent targets shifted to 2021–22, then 2023, then 2024, then early 2025 deliveries. On the Q3 2024 earnings call, production slipped to 2025–2026. At Tesla's November 2025 shareholder meeting, Musk set a demo for April 1, 2026, with production 12–18 months after — meaning 2027–2028 at the earliest.

3. **Customer commitments at stake:** Thousands of customers have held deposits since 2017. The Founders Series required a $250,000 commitment; standard reservations started at $50,000 (with a $5,000 initial deposit). Original specs promised a 200 kWh battery, 620-mile range, 1.9-second 0–60 mph, top speed above 250 mph, and a $200,000 base price. Musk has since added claims of sub-one-second 0–60 and optional SpaceX cold-gas thrusters.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek characterizes the delay as "absurd," arguing that after nine years and eight broken deadlines, the Roadster "functions more as a perpetual marketing tool than a real product program," often surfacing when Tesla needs to generate excitement or deflect from weak financial results. The author does believe the Roadster will eventually launch, citing new trademarks, manufacturing job listings, and composite seat patents as evidence of slow but real progress.
