Independent lab testing has found hexavalent chromium, arsenic, and elevated lithium levels in wastewater discharged from Tesla's lithium refinery in Robstown, Texas, prompting the Nueces County Drainage District No. 2 to issue a cease-and-desist letter demanding Tesla halt its 231,000-gallon daily discharge pending further discussion.

## Energy
1. **Toxic metals detected in refinery wastewater:** A 24-hour composite sample collected April 7 and tested by Eurofins Environment Testing on April 10 found hexavalent chromium at 0.0104 mg/L (just above the lab's 0.01 mg/L reporting limit), arsenic at 0.0025 mg/L, sodium at 302 mg/L, chloride at 382 mg/L, strontium at 1.17 mg/L, phosphorus at 0.527 mg/L, and ammonia at 1.68 mg/L. Neither hexavalent chromium nor arsenic appears in Tesla's TCEQ-issued discharge permit. The drainage district's consultant described the water as 10–20 times saltier than normal waterway conditions. Discharge flows into Petronila Creek and ultimately Baffin Bay.

2. **Regulatory gaps in TCEQ permitting:** TCEQ issued Tesla a discharge permit in January 2025 and investigated in February 2026, finding Tesla in compliance — but only tested for conventional pollutants, not heavy metals or lithium. The drainage district was not notified during the permitting process. District attorney Frank Lazarte called the findings "quite disturbing." Tesla's Senior Manager of Site Operations Jason Bevan said the company "routinely monitors and tests its permitted wastewater discharge" and is "reviewing the letter," adding it "looks forward to working cooperatively." The district's consultant has recommended Tesla install a multi-stage treatment plant with reverse osmosis, heavy metals removal, and either hazardous waste disposal for concentrated brine or a zero-liquid discharge system.

3. **Regional water crisis context:** The Robstown refinery is located 16 miles west of Corpus Christi, where Lake Corpus Christi sits at roughly 9% capacity and Choke Canyon Reservoir is below 8%. City officials project emergency water restrictions by September 2026. Industrial facilities currently consume up to 60% of the city's water supply. The article also notes that Elon Musk's xAI Memphis data center consumes up to 1.5 million gallons per day for cooling and prompted an $80 million wastewater treatment project.

4. **Contradiction of Tesla's "acid-free" marketing:** When Tesla unveiled the Robstown refinery earlier in 2026, it promoted its alkaline leach process as cleaner than conventional acid-roasting, claiming byproducts were benign sand and limestone suitable for concrete. The detection of hexavalent chromium and arsenic — even at trace levels — directly contradicts that narrative.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's author argues Tesla must address the contamination urgently and that the TCEQ permitting framework is deeply flawed for not requiring heavy metals or lithium monitoring at a lithium refinery. The author also suggests Tesla's pattern of locating facilities in Texas partly reflects the state's lighter regulatory environment.
