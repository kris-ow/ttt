Electrek reports that, per a Bloomberg report on xAI's widening losses and cash burn, xAI executives told investors the startup's goal is to develop self-sufficient AI to power robots like Tesla's Optimus — a claim the author argues undermines Tesla's "AI company" narrative and strengthens pending shareholder lawsuits against Musk.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **xAI reportedly targeting Optimus's AI brain:** According to the Bloomberg report cited by the article, xAI executives told investors their aim is to "develop self-sufficient AI to power robots like Tesla's Optimus." The article argues this contradicts Musk's earlier public position that Tesla's own "real-world AI" effort (developed through autonomous driving) would build Optimus's brain, and that no xAI licensing was needed — a claim Musk made in a quoted statement: "Tesla has learned a lot from discussions with engineers at xAI that have helped accelerate achieving unsupervised FSD, but there is no need to license anything from xAI."

## Financials
1. **Fiduciary-duty lawsuit implications:** The article states shareholders have sued Musk and Tesla's board over alleged breach of fiduciary duty tied to xAI's founding, seeking for Tesla to own Musk's xAI stake; it argues the new Bloomberg report could serve as key evidence given it blurs Musk's prior distinction between xAI (AGI-focused) and Tesla (real-world-AI-focused). The article also notes Musk previously demanded 25% voting control at Tesla by threatening to build AI products outside the company, a demand it says was effectively met via his reinstated and new pay packages.
2. **xAI cash burn:** The article states xAI is burning through roughly $1 billion a month, per its characterization of the Bloomberg report.
3. **Prior Electrek-reported pattern:** The article recaps its own past reporting: Musk allegedly moved AI talent from Tesla to xAI, diverted Nvidia H100 chips originally ordered for Tesla to xAI instead, and previously argued xAI does not compete with Tesla.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's editorial section argues Musk is running a "shell game with shareholder capital," transferring Optimus's core AI value to his privately controlled xAI while leaving Tesla as a hardware manufacturer; it calls this a "hardware commoditization trap" for Tesla shareholders and says the development strengthens the fiduciary-duty lawsuits, while also noting Musk is making a hypothetical Tesla–xAI merger less attractive given xAI's association with generating unauthorized sexualized images, including of minors. A top reader comment (not verified by the article) claims Tesla's board has a committee exploring a reverse-merger-style acquisition of xAI using equity.
