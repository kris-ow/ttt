## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla gets green light for up to 5,000 robotaxis in Nevada:** Tesla officially received its full Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit from the Nevada Transportation Authority, clearing the way to launch paid public Robotaxi service in Las Vegas. The approval allows Tesla to deploy up to 5,000 robotaxis over the next 12 months. This was posted (and duplicated) multiple times by @SawyerMerritt as an "exclusive," underscoring its significance — one instance drew nearly 4K likes and 500+ reposts. Waymo received a comparable but smaller Nevada permit the same day, capped at up to 1,000 robotaxis over 12 months for Las Vegas.

2. **Austin Cybercab public launch appears imminent:** During the same Nevada Transportation Authority general session (a 4-hour meeting), Tesla stated it plans to launch the first public Cybercab rides in Austin "imminently." Separately, Tesla held twelve first-responder training sessions with the Cybercab in Austin last week — cited as another signal that public rides are close. In Q&A at the meeting, Tesla also said it is open to opening additional robotaxi mission control centers beyond its existing two (Austin and the Bay Area).

3. **JPMorgan's Fremont readout — Tesla holding back Model Y robotaxi additions, bullish on FSD v15:** Following a recent meeting with Tesla in Fremont, JPMorgan relayed that Tesla is intentionally limiting Model Y additions to the robotaxi fleet, expressing confidence it can scale Cybercab quickly instead. Tesla reportedly characterized its upcoming FSD V15 release as a "step-change" improvement (specific capabilities not detailed in the post).

4. **Waymo's Nevada expansion and compute disclosure:** Alongside its Nevada AV permit, Waymo published a blog post describing its compute stack and custom silicon approach ("A look under our trunk"), positioning itself as a direct point of comparison for Tesla's robotaxi buildout. A separate opinion piece (The Atlantic) argued Democratic-led cities are lagging on driverless-car adoption relative to more permissive regulatory environments — framed as a broader tailwind for AV deployment nationally.

5. **Starlink aviation rollout continues:** Qatar Airways is on track to complete Starlink installation across its Boeing 787 fleet by end of 2026, having already equipped more than half — 150 widebody aircraft — including the airline's first Starlink-equipped 787-9.

6. **Tesla Semi coming to Europe:** Tesla announced it will share launch details for the Tesla Semi in Europe in mid-September at IAA Transportation in Germany.

7. **EV competitive landscape:** Hyundai's Genesis brand unveiled the all-electric GV90, a three-row flagship SUV with up to 310 miles of range, a 123 kWh battery, 350kW peak charging, and notable interior features (no B-pillar, rotating front seats, radiant floor heating) — a competitive data point for the U.S. EV market.
