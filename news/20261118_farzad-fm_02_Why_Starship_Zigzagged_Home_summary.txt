The video explains why Starship's upper stage (Ship 42) flew a zigzag path across Mexico and Texas on its return from orbit during Flight 15, instead of a straight line home. Its core argument is that the ship uses thin air as both brake and steering: lift keeps it high and slow-burning, banking turns bleed energy and avoid cities, and a final swoosh over the Gulf lines it up with the tower arms.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15 outcome:** Starship launched November 13, 2026 from Starbase with Block 3 Ship 42 on a Super Heavy booster. The booster's boostback burn ended early on a low fuel mass alarm caused by slight engine underperformance. The flight computer triggered an automated abort, and the booster was destroyed offshore in the Gulf when its termination system fired. The ship deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, orbited 14 times over about 22.5 hours, and was caught by the tower arms at about 3:20 p.m. Central on November 14.

2. **Physics of the return:** The ship has to shed about 17,000 mph without using engines as brakes, since carrying that much propellant would kill payload. Over 99% of orbital energy is scrubbed by the atmosphere, with Raptors handling only the last couple hundred mph. Drag acts as the brake. Lift from the tilted belly holds the ship high in thin air, spreading heat over thousands of miles and many minutes rather than plunging it into thick air.

3. **Bank angle as steering:** Pitching the nose down would expose the top of the ship to plasma, so the ship instead rolls onto its side by differentially moving its four flaps (two forward, two aft). This shrinks vertical lift and sends the horizontal component sideways (cross range). Holding one bank would drag the ship off course, so it alternates left and right in S-turns, lengthening the path and shedding more energy while ending at the same destination.

4. **Shuttle heritage and ocean rehearsals:** The space shuttle flew banked S-turns with roll reversals over land for 30 years, with a cross range of about 860 miles each side. SpaceX rehearsed Starship's banking maneuver over open ocean starting with Flight 11 in October 2025, then on two more missions in May and July 2026. Flight 14 in September 2026, the first full orbital flight, did another before a Pacific splashdown north of Hawaii.

5. **Route and safety design:** The overland path steers through sparsely populated northern Mexico and southern Texas and around Rio Grande Valley cities. The video cites FAA limits of under 1 in 10,000 collective public casualty risk and under 1 in a million individual risk. SpaceX built a four-layer abort ladder: the suborbital ascent arc, pre-cleared splashdown zones near Hawaii and Chile during the orbital coast, an early re-entry abort option (intentional tumble) over the Pacific, and a late option to fly past the beach into the Gulf. An FAA airspace flow program rerouted airliners, and sonic booms were heard at least 30 miles away, including in Laredo and Brownsville.

6. **The swoosh over the Gulf:** The final hook out over the water does three jobs. It points supersonic momentum away from land until the ship is confirmed healthy. It lets the flight computer tune altitude and energy before the dive. It aligns the ship with the single pair of tower arms and the catch fittings below the forward flaps. The ship lit three Raptors, flipped, stepped down to two, then one near hover as the arms closed.

7. **What remains:** Booster and ship have still not been caught on the same mission. Elon Musk posted the day after that the heat shield and engines looked great and the ship could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment, but that many small fixes are needed for airplane-like reuse with zero refurbishment. The video says the long-term goal is refly within hours, past 100 flights a year, toward Musk's stated target of more than 10,000 launches a year, with about 100 tons of payload per flight.
