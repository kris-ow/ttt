This is a livestream (with host commentary) of Tesla's 2020 Annual Shareholder Meeting followed by the Battery Day presentation. The shareholder meeting covered routine governance business and four shareholder proposals (including on human rights/cobalt sourcing and workplace arbitration), while Battery Day laid out a detailed, multi-year roadmap to cut battery cost per kWh and scale cell production toward terawatt-hour levels, alongside updates on Autopilot/FSD progress and a new structural battery pack architecture.

## Financials
1. **Fourth consecutive quarter of GAAP profitability:** Elon Musk stated Tesla achieved four consecutive quarters of GAAP profitability, calling it very difficult to achieve amid adverse circumstances, and noted average profitability over the last four quarters was around only about one percent, pushing back on the idea that Tesla is highly profitable relative to its valuation.
2. **Delivery growth guidance:** Musk said Tesla grew deliveries by 50% in 2019 and expects growth of roughly 30–40% in 2020 despite pandemic, wildfire, and production disruptions, which he called probably the most difficult year in Tesla's history.
3. **TSLA stock moved intraday during the event:** The host noted TSLA trading around the $420s-$430s range during the livestream, ticking up when Musk was on stage and down when he left, per the host's own on-screen tracker (exact figures per the host's tracker, not independently verified).

## Energy
1. **Terawatt-hour battery production goal:** Tesla set a goal of terawatt-hour-scale battery production, framing "terawatt" as the new "gigawatt" — roughly 1,000x a gigawatt — and said the world needs on the order of 10 TWh/year to electrify the vehicle fleet and roughly 20–25 TWh/year sustained for 15–25 years to convert the grid to renewables.
2. **Existing Nevada Gigafactory scale insufficient:** Drew Baglino said Giga Nevada is expected to produce around 150 GWh/year (0.15 TWh), and Tesla would need roughly 135 fully built-out Nevada-scale gigafactories to reach 20 TWh/year, illustrating why a fundamentally different manufacturing approach was needed.
3. **New 4680 cell and tabless "tablet" architecture:** Tesla unveiled a new cylindrical cell (roughly 46mm diameter, 80mm length) using a tabless design, claimed to deliver five times the energy, six times the power, and a 16% range increase from form factor alone versus the prior 2170 cell; cell form-factor change alone was said to cut cost by $14/kWh.
4. **Pilot cell production ramping now:** Tesla said it has begun ramping a 10 GWh/year pilot production facility for the new cells, expecting to take about a year to reach that annualized capacity; future production plants are expected to be on the order of 200 GWh or more.
5. **Planned in-house cell capacity and supplier relationship:** Tesla plans roughly 100 GWh of in-house cell production as a supplement (not replacement) to purchases from Panasonic, LG, and CATL, with a long-term target of about 3,000 GWh (3 TWh)/year, which Musk said he's highly confident of achieving by 2030 and thinks there's a good chance of achieving even earlier.
6. **Dry electrode coating process:** Tesla described a new dry (solvent-free) electrode coating process, acquired via Maxwell roughly a year prior and revised four times since acquisition, claimed to cut manufacturing footprint 10x and energy use 10x with major investment reduction, though Musk cautioned it is "close to working" but not yet at high yield.
7. **Formation process overhaul:** New formation equipment (charging/discharging cells) was said to cut formation investment by 86% and footprint by 75%, translating to roughly a 75% reduction in investment per gigawatt-hour versus current state of the art.
8. **Silicon anode breakthrough:** Tesla proposed using raw metallurgical silicon (rather than engineered silicon materials) stabilized with an elastic ion-conducting polymer coating, claimed to increase range by an additional 20% and reduce anode cost to $1.20/kWh, contributing a further 5% reduction in dollar/kWh at the pack level.
9. **New cobalt-free high-nickel cathode:** Tesla described a high-nickel cathode with zero cobalt, using novel coatings and dopants, yielding a 15% reduction in cathode dollar/kWh; a three-tier cathode chemistry strategy was outlined — iron-based for medium range, nickel-manganese as an intermediate, and high-nickel for long-range applications like Cybertruck and Semi.
10. **Simplified cathode manufacturing process:** A new process eliminating the intermediate "metal sulfate" step and recirculating water (zero wastewater) was said to cut capex investment by 66% and process cost by 76%, plus enable a planned North American cathode facility that would cut material transport distances by 80%.
11. **New sulfate-free lithium refining process:** Tesla described a novel process using table salt (sodium chloride) to extract lithium from clay ore, claimed as a first-of-its-kind, sustainable method, cutting lithium cost by 33%; Tesla said it has rights to a lithium clay deposit in Nevada spanning over 10,000 acres, and stated there is enough lithium in Nevada alone to electrify the entire U.S. vehicle fleet.
12. **Recycling plans:** Tesla said it currently recycles 100% of its vehicle batteries (via third parties) and will start pilot full-scale in-house battery recycling production at Giga Nevada (Reno) next quarter, aiming to eventually source new batteries primarily from old ones once the fleet reaches steady state.
13. **Structural battery pack and single-piece casting:** Tesla unveiled a structural battery pack design where the pack itself serves as a load-bearing chassis structure, paired with a large single-piece front/rear underbody casting using a newly developed high-strength aluminum alloy that doesn't require heat treatment; this was said to cut body mass by 10%, increase range by 14%, and reduce parts count by 370, plus cut investment per GWh by over 50% and floor space by 35%. This contributed an additional 7% dollar/kWh pack-level reduction.
14. **Total stacked reductions:** Combining all announced innovations, Tesla claimed up to a 54% range increase, a 56% reduction in dollars per kWh at the pack level, and a 69% reduction in investment per gigawatt-hour, though Musk said it would take roughly 1–1.5 years to start realizing these benefits and about three years to fully realize them.
15. **$25,000 EV target reaffirmed:** Musk said Tesla is confident it can design and manufacture a compelling, fully autonomous $25,000 electric vehicle in roughly three years, calling it a long-held company goal.
16. **Nickel supply outreach:** Musk said he personally spoke with CEOs of the world's largest mining companies to encourage them to increase nickel production, and Tesla executives said mining partners appeared motivated to ramp investment based on Tesla's stated growth plans.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD beta timeline and rewrite:** Musk said Tesla hopes to release a private beta of "full self-driving" Autopilot in about a month, following a fundamental rewrite of the entire Autopilot software stack and labeling system (moving from single-image labeling to full 3D/4D video labeling across all cameras), which he said had caused progress to plateau ("local maximum") before the rewrite.
2. **Autopilot safety statistics:** Musk cited the U.S. average collision rate as roughly 2.1 per million miles versus 0.3 per million miles with Autopilot engaged, and said Tesla is confident it can improve this to roughly ten times better than industry average over time.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model S Plaid track time:** Musk said Tesla took the latest Model S Plaid to Laguna Seca and recorded a 1-minute-30 lap time, with an expectation of shaving off three or more additional seconds, and stated confidence it will achieve the best production-car track time ever, available to order now with deliveries expected around the end of next year.
2. **Cybertruck order volume and production estimate:** Musk said Cybertruck orders were "gigantic" — over half a million, possibly six or six hundred thousand — and estimated unit volume potential of at least 250,000–300,000/year or more; he said Cybertruck is designed to U.S. specs and a smaller international version is likely, and confirmed Tesla can lease (but not yet directly sell) in Texas.
3. **Giga Berlin and Giga Texas progress:** Musk said Berlin is making rapid progress and Texas (Austin) is progressing even faster, with Texas chosen partly for logistics advantage (roughly two-thirds of the way across the U.S., reducing delivery cost/time to central and eastern U.S. markets); Model Y will also be produced there.
4. **Shanghai factory scale:** Musk highlighted Shanghai as Tesla's only 100%-owned foreign factory in China, built from "a dirt pile" to volume production in 15 months, with expectation it could eventually produce over a million vehicles/year.

## Market & Competition
1. **Industry growth comparison:** Musk noted Tesla's sales grew even as the broader auto industry declined, attributing much of Tesla's growth to word-of-mouth marketing from existing owners.
2. **Global EV/energy market still nascent:** Musk stated less than 1% of the global vehicle fleet is currently electric and less than roughly 0.1% of stationary storage needs have been addressed, framing the transition to sustainable energy as still in very early stages requiring massive additional effort industry-wide.
