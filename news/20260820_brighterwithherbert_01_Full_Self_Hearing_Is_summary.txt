This video, hosted with guest Brian White (of the FutureAzA channel), covers several newly launched or newly noticed Tesla features: vehicle-to-load (V2L) power sharing on Model Y Premium trims, a car-diagnostic "full self-hearing" capability described by Lars Moravy, the newly live Certified Pre-Owned program, and a discussion of rising used-Tesla resale prices. The throughline is that Tesla is layering incremental features and services onto existing hardware to add value and revenue without cutting prices.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Vehicle-to-load (V2L) launches on Model Y Premium:** Tesla has officially enabled V2L on all US Model Y Premium trims equipped with a newer power conversion system, letting owners use an $80 outlet adapter (120V/20A, 2.4kW) to power space heaters, tools, camping gear, or act as a home backup source for essentials like a refrigerator or computer during an outage. Owners can verify compatibility via their VIN in Tesla's parts catalog or the Tesla app's accessory page (though the app page reportedly isn't yet updated for everyone). Brian White noted this closes a gap versus competitors (e.g., Kia EV6) for buyers who specifically need power-sharing, such as demo-based EV educators, though he'd prefer an interior outlet option as well; he also clarified V2L is not the same as grid-selling capability, which would require a Powerwall gateway.
2. **Certified Pre-Owned program goes live in US/Puerto Rico:** The program includes a 150+ point inspection across cosmetic, functional, and structural components, Tesla-technician refurbishment, the remainder of the original 8-year battery warranty, and one free month of FSD Supervised plus premium connectivity. Brian White was skeptical of its incremental value, estimating the free FSD month is worth roughly $99 (or up to ~$108 with sales tax), and said Tesla's used inventory was already reputable — the main added benefit being assurance against hidden or unrecorded vehicle damage.
3. **Used Tesla prices rising, in some cases above new MSRP:** The hosts discussed (with an unconfirmed-accuracy caveat, sourced partly from a conversation with "Jeff Lutz") reports that Carvana is listing used Model 3s/Ys above new-vehicle sticker prices — e.g., a low-mileage base Model 3 RWD priced roughly 14% over a new one. Brian White separately checked a Portland dealer (Plat Auto) and found a 2025 Model 3 with about 9,000 miles priced near $40,000, and a 2024 Model Y with 34,000 miles priced at $40,000 — both citing strong resale. He attributed upward pressure primarily to the expiration of the federal EV incentive, which had lowered effective original purchase prices, plus longer new-vehicle lead times; the dealer's Tesla inventory turnover was described as very fast (dropping from ~7 to 0 units within about a week during recent checks).
4. **Other minor feature/program mentions:** The video also noted Tesla's Model Y HEPA air filter (99.97% particulate removal) being marketed in Malaysia and India but under-promoted elsewhere; a newly integrated home-charger installation flow in the US purchase process with pre-approved certified electricians; and a new India promotion offering a free wall connector with vehicle purchase, which Brian White linked to a claim (from an unnamed source "familiar with the matter") that Tesla's India pricing was set higher than some internally wanted, prompting non-price incentives instead of discounts.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **"Full self-hearing" self-diagnostic feature:** Referencing an earlier interview, the host relayed that Tesla VP Lars Moravy described a feature where robotaxi/factory vehicles autonomously drive over bump/rumble strips on their way out of Gigafactories, using in-cabin microphones and AI to detect squeaks and rattles and identify the responsible part, feeding data back for manufacturing improvement. A viewer's on-the-ground video showed a rumble-strip section at a not-yet-open Tesla Center in Haymarket, Virginia, prompting speculation (unconfirmed) that it may be used by service centers to diagnose repairs rather than for manufacturing testing; Brian White thought what the viewer heard was likely a loose mobile charger rather than a genuine rattle.
2. **Growing mainstream acceptance of autonomous driving:** The hosts noted anecdotally that non-Tesla-community influencers are increasingly experiencing FSD and expressing surprise at its capability, and that driverless vehicles are receiving more normalized news coverage. Brian White reiterated a view that Waymo's expansion, while notable, doesn't change his long-term outlook that Waymo won't be the ultimate winner, though he considers Waymo, Tesla, and (in his belief) Grok-related systems all likely safer than human drivers.
