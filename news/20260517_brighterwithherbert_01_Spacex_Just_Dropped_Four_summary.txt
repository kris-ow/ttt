## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO Imminent on NASDAQ:** SpaceX is reportedly going public on June 12th, with pricing expected on June 11th, under the ticker symbol SPCX on the NASDAQ. The prospectus is expected to drop the following week, with the official road show kicking off June 8th. The IPO is targeting a listing valuation of $70–75 billion, which the host describes as more than double Saudi Aramco's previous record for the largest IPO of all time. Goldberg notes the IPO is coming earlier than previously anticipated — he had expected a late June date around June 26th–28th.

2. **Massive Oversubscription Demand Reported:** Goldberg reports that early market chatter suggests the IPO is already approximately 4x oversubscribed after the first pricing round, and that expectations for the second round are 12–20x oversubscription. He calculates that at a $75 billion listing target, 12–20x oversubscription implies roughly $1 trillion in demand. He expects the market cap on IPO day to reach or exceed $2 trillion. Goldberg expresses that he personally is not a seller, though he acknowledges early venture investors with limited partner obligations will likely seek liquidity.

3. **Rapid Index Fund Inclusion Expected:** Goldberg references investor Ron Baron as the source of a claim that SpaceX is slated to be included in a major index fund just 5 days after IPO, with a second index fund inclusion 5 days after that, and a third 5 days after that. Goldberg notes uncertainty around lockup periods, saying traditional 3–6 month insider lockups may need to be shortened to free up sufficient float for index fund demand at the scale implied by SpaceX's market cap.

4. **FCC Approves SpaceX Spectrum Acquisition from Echostar:** The FCC has approved SpaceX's acquisition of 65 megahertz of nationwide spectrum from Echostar, enabling SpaceX to offer 5G connectivity directly from orbit to mobile devices. Goldberg describes this as a long-anticipated regulatory rubber stamp. The deal value is cited at $40 billion. In response, Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile announced a joint venture to address rural coverage dead zones using satellite-based technologies. SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell is quoted as saying, "Well, I guess Starlink Mobile is doing something right. It's David and Goliath. Goliath times three all over again. I'm betting on David, which is Starlink."

5. **Starlink V3 Satellites Will Dramatically Expand Bandwidth:** Goldberg argues that Starlink V3 satellites — each equipped with direct-to-cell capability — will represent an enormous leap in capacity. He claims that 10 Starship launches carrying V3 Starlink satellites would deliver more bandwidth than all approximately 10,000 Starlink satellites currently in orbit, and that at a cadence of 120 Starship launches per year, bandwidth could increase roughly 12x within a single year. Elon Musk is cited as saying new phone manufacturers will require approximately a two-year timeframe to integrate the necessary chipsets and modem support for full Starlink 5G capability.

6. **Google in Talks for Orbital Data Centers; AI Alliance Forming:** Reports indicate SpaceX and Google are in talks to launch data centers into orbit. Goldberg notes Google holds approximately 8% of SpaceX shares from its early investment that helped save SpaceX circa 2008. He speculates Google will simultaneously partner with Blue Origin to hedge. Google has separately announced plans to launch prototype satellites by 2027 under a project called "Project Suncatcher," with Planet Labs reportedly building the satellites. Separately, Anthropic has already signed a compute deal with XAI — described as 300 megawatts over 220,000 Nvidia GPUs to be delivered by end of May — with the deal reportedly including an orbital inference component. Goldberg describes an emerging AI alliance involving SpaceX/XAI, Anthropic, Google, and Cursor.

7. **XAI Described as Third-Place but Compute-Advantaged:** Goldberg offers a candid assessment of the AI competitive landscape, describing Anthropic as the clear frontier leader, with XAI in third place but closing ground. He argues XAI's key structural advantage is owning its own massive compute — something neither OpenAI nor Anthropic possess. He asserts that the cost of serving tokens will ultimately determine market winners, and that cost of compute depends primarily on energy costs and chip amortization. He predicts space-based AI data centers powered by cheap Starship launches will eventually dwarf current terrestrial AI infrastructure.

8. **Starship V3 Stacked; V4 Planned 15–20% Longer:** Starship V3 has been stacked and is described as the tallest rocket ever built, the third time SpaceX has broken that record in three years. A flight was anticipated around May 19th. Goldberg describes V3 as a ground-up redesign rather than an incremental update, calling it ultra high-risk to launch weeks before the IPO and days before the prospectus drops. He characterizes V3 as the intended production vehicle for the next three years, expected to support Starlink V3 satellite deployment, lunar missions, and in-orbit refueling. Production is expected to begin at one to two vehicles per week, eventually scaling to one per day, then three to five per day. Elon Musk has stated V4 will be 15–20% longer, with Goldberg estimating V4 is approximately three years away.

9. **New Spaceports Being Scouted Domestically and Internationally:** SpaceX confirmed it is exploring multiple domestic and international sites for new spaceports to support a target of thousands of Starship flights per year. A realtor reported a rumored land acquisition of approximately 136,000 acres of marshland in Louisiana south of Highway 82 toward the Gulf of Mexico. SpaceX publicly stated: "It's no secret. We intend to launch Starship a lot, targeting thousands of flights per year. That cadence will require the ability to launch from many different locations." Known production and launch facilities include Starbase in Texas and a site at Cape Canaveral on Roberts Road.
