This episode rebuts a rival YouTuber's (Meet Kevin's) claim that SpaceX's data-center economics are a "scam" driven by Google's stake, then covers the official announcement of the Terafab chip plant in Grimes County, Texas, alongside Megapack 3 production ramp, battery recycling milestones, ongoing Cybertruck-parts litigation with supplier Angstrom, and incremental Robotaxi/FSD updates.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Host rebuts "SpaceX data center scam" claims:** The host pushes back on Meet Kevin's video arguing Google's stakes in both SpaceX and Anthropic create a circular economy inflating SpaceX's Colossus data-center revenue figures. The host argues Anthropic has no incentive to overpay since it doesn't hold SpaceX equity, and that Kevin's headline chart (SpaceX at $2.7M build cost per megawatt vs. over $10M for competitors, and $30M annual revenue per megawatt vs. under $4.6M for competitors) is misleading because it compares SpaceX's brownfield Colossus 1 and 2 sites (existing buildings/power infrastructure) against greenfield competitor builds, and omits GPU costs entirely. The host does concede Kevin's fair points: SpaceX's compute deals with Anthropic and Google carry 90-day rolling cancellation terms, are not guaranteed long-term revenue, and SpaceX is currently spending a majority of its capital on AI infrastructure rather than rockets — raising an open question of how durable these contracts remain as customers bring their own greenfield data centers online.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Terafab officially confirmed for Grimes County, Texas:** Tesla and SpaceX jointly confirmed the Terafab chip manufacturing facility, following an April groundbreaking on a research fab at Giga Texas, with a stated goal of producing over 1 terawatt of compute per year — described as needed because both companies require far more chips than current global production can supply.

## Financials
1. **Initial Terafab phase pegged at $16.8 billion, split between Tesla and SpaceX:** The initial phase is estimated to require about $16.8 billion in capital investment from SpaceX and Tesla combined, with future expansion phases pushing total investment much higher; the host estimates the full project could exceed $100 billion in capex over the coming years, making it the largest project either company has undertaken. It's unclear whether the $16.8B is per company or split evenly (~$8.4B each); the host treats this as new, incremental capex not previously guided (on the last earnings call, Tesla had only mentioned a roughly $3 billion research fab, with Elon Musk reportedly saying SpaceX would cover the initial scaled-up Terafab phase). The host connects this to Tesla's previously arranged $30 billion debt facility as a likely funding source, and flags Tesla's stock closed at $319.53, down slightly on the day the news broke.
2. **SpaceX (SPCX) closed up over 6% despite expected share-unlock pressure:** SpaceX shares closed at $114.92, up 6.14% on a day when a share unlock had led many to expect a decline; the Nasdaq-100 was down 0.39% the same day.
3. **Musk's rough compute allocation for Terafab output:** Elon Musk reportedly gave an approximate estimate that Terafab's AI compute output would be split roughly 25% for Tesla Optimus and 75% for AI/spacecraft use, which the host notes implies close to 0% allocation for Tesla's vehicle FSD compute (expected instead to come from AI5 chips via TSMC/Samsung) — leaving open questions about Tesla's exact contribution, chip allocation, and return on its investment.

## Energy
1. **Megapack 3 enters production at new Texas Megafactory:** Tesla confirmed Megapack 3 is now rolling off the line at its Brookshire, Texas Megafactory, which went from groundbreaking to operation in 16 months with a design capacity of 50 gigawatt-hours per year dedicated to Megapack 3; Tesla indicated Megablock (bundling up to four Megapack 3 units with medium-voltage transformers and switchgear) should follow soon.
2. **Battery recycling scaling toward rivaling North American mining output by 2030:** Tesla says it recovers more than 90% of key battery materials (nickel, cobalt) from recycled cells, processing around 3,000 metric tons of material in Nevada this past year (equivalent to about 9,000 Model Y rear-wheel-drive vehicles' worth of material) and expects to process a similar ~3,000 metric tons at its Texas facility this year; Tesla states that by 2030 its recycled material output will rival the output of mines in North America.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla-Angstrom Cybertruck parts dispute continues amid bankruptcy:** Tesla won a court order to recover custom production equipment from supplier Angstrom's factory, but the factory owner's immediate bankruptcy filing triggered an automatic stay, halting the equipment transfer. Angstrom's countersuit alleges Cybertruck sales have been "extraordinarily disappointing" and disputes that any backlog reflects public consumer demand, instead characterizing it as vehicles purchased by Tesla affiliates — which the host speculates could refer to SpaceX.
2. **Ford unveils "Fathom" midsize electric truck:** Ford announced its midsize EV truck, called the Fathom, starting at $28,350, with preorders beginning early next year and deliveries starting later in 2027; it's the first vehicle on Ford's new "UEV" platform, built via a new "assembly tree" manufacturing process (a play on Tesla's unboxed method) at Ford's Louisville, Kentucky plant, with a stated goal of being cost-competitive with Chinese EV leaders and Tesla.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
1. **Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi now running limited night hours in Austin:** Per a commentator (Abby Manu), Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi service is now operating at night in Austin, though apparently only until around 9–9:30 PM depending on the day; the vehicles still are not taking highways, and supervised rides continue to operate in the city alongside unsupervised ones.
2. **New navigation update rolling out to some Tesla owners:** Some Tesla owners are receiving a navigation update; the host is uncertain whether it addresses FSD's broader navigation issues.
