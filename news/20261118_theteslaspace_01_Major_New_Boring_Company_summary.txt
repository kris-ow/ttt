This weekly news roundup leads with a Tennessee senator's comments on how fast Boring Company machines can dig. It then covers SpaceX's first-ever catch of a Starship upper stage on Flight 15, and Tesla opening Cybercab sales to individual customers at $29,990 with a $299/month autonomy subscription that Elon Musk called too high. The common thread is reusability and speed of deployment, in tunneling, in rockets, and in robotaxi ownership.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab opens to individual buyers:** The Tesla robotaxi account said individual customers are being invited to buy a Cybercab for personal use. The price is $29,990 before taxes, in line with Musk's earlier promise of under $30,000. Buyers pay a $1,000 deposit, and deliveries are expected in December in five Texas cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio and Fort Worth. With no steering wheel or pedals, any use requires a $299/month autonomy subscription.
2. **Musk calls the subscription too high:** Responding to a post by Marques Brownlee, Musk said the monthly fee should be in line with FSD plus connectivity on other Tesla models, which he put at about $150 per month. The host says $299 adds almost $3,600 per year. He describes $150 as about 62% cheaper, but $150 is roughly 50% below $299. A 62% cut would correspond to about $115, the host's figure for current FSD (Supervised) plus premium connectivity.
3. **Pricing implications (host speculation):** The host argues that because the Cybercab can't be human-driven, the subscription is effectively driverless FSD sold to the owner. He guesses that supervised FSD may eventually become standard on every Tesla, as basic Autopilot was before Tesla removed it from new US and Canadian cars earlier this year. He stresses this is only a guess and that Tesla has announced no such change.
4. **Restrictive ownership terms:** Tesla community member Spencer analyzed the sales contract. It reportedly allows one Cybercab per buyer, and each car arrives with about 250 miles from testing and transport. Owners cannot sell without Tesla's permission, on pain of a fine of at least $50,000. Tesla can buy the car back at fair market value at any time, and for now owners cannot add it to the robotaxi network. Charging is owner-paid and limited to NACS fast chargers such as Superchargers, with no home charging. Owners also need insurance that covers the car when it is driven empty for charging or maintenance.
5. **Expansion plans:** Musk has said the Cybercab will be available nationwide as regulatory approvals arrive. Tesla also announced a €3 billion Giga Berlin expansion with a new workshop able to produce up to 1 million Cybercabs per year from 2029.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **First upper-stage catch:** Flight 15 launched from Starbase on November 13, with all 33 Raptor 3 engines firing on Super Heavy. Ship 42 orbited Earth about 14 times and deployed 26 next-generation Starlink satellites. Three of those satellites photographed the ship's heat shield before reentry. The ship reentered belly-first, braked above Starbase, and was caught by the tower's "chopsticks" about 22.5 hours after launch. This was the first time an upper stage was caught, though the booster was first caught in October 2024. SpaceX's Dan Huot said: "We caught Starship, and now everything changes."
2. **Booster lost:** One engine failed during the Super Heavy landing braking maneuver, and the booster's automated systems chose not to attempt the catch. It steered into the ocean, and its flight termination system activated just before impact. Per preliminary data cited by SpaceX's Kate Tice, the booster stopped working earlier because of a low fuel level signal, and Musk attributed it to a slight decrease in engine power. The host counts this as the fourth loss in a row of SpaceX's newest booster and says the issue needs to be addressed.
3. **Press reaction and Florida:** Per a Sawyer Merritt review, CNN, Reuters, NPR and Fox Business all described the catch as historic, which the host calls rare for Starship coverage. Musk replied on X with one word: "cool." SpaceX says the Starship tower at Pad 39A in Florida should be ready by the end of this month, and a first Florida launch could follow soon.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **V3 operating, V4 in development:** A SpaceX representative reported that the Starlink V3 satellites launched on Flight 14 have reached operating orbit and are serving customers. The next-generation V4 is already under development.

## Market & Competition
1. **Boring Company tunneling speed:** Tennessee state Sen. Mark Pody, who wants tunnels in Wilson County, said two machines cover about 3 miles a month. The host calculates that as roughly 1.5 miles per machine per month, or about 260 feet per day. The source of the figure is unclear, though Pody has spoken with the company. In 2022 the company targeted up to a mile a week for Prufrock, and Nashville's hard limestone requires hard-rock machines. Pody said a Wilson County tunnel within five years is "definitely" possible. Tennessee's new law makes the state the sole regulator of underground transportation, limiting local delays.
2. **Machine redeployment:** Prufrock 5 began digging in San Antonio 29 days after finishing in Bastrop. In that time the 400-ton machine was extracted, trucked 100 miles and reinstalled for a nearly mile-long tunnel. Traditional boring machines are often abandoned underground, while Prufrock is designed to surface, be upgraded and move on. Prufrock 1 has dug at least eight tunnels.
