This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers several major SpaceX developments — including a reported plan to launch a standalone Starlink mobile service and the FTC-approved acquisition of Mesh Optical Technologies — alongside updates on Tesla Semi incentives, the Terafab compute ambitions, VW's potential restructuring, and a settled FSD-related lawsuit. The video's core thesis is that SpaceX is aggressively moving toward becoming a vertically integrated connectivity and communications company, while Tesla continues to advance its energy and autonomous driving roadmap amid political and legal headwinds.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX plans standalone Starlink mobile service:** Per reporting from the Financial Times and Reuters, SpaceX has told investors it intends to launch a Starlink retail mobile service for US consumers, which would put it in direct competition with Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile — its current direct-to-cell partner. SpaceX President Gwyn Shotwell reportedly disclosed this during the SpaceX IPO roadshow. The existing T-Mobile partnership (branded "T-Satellite") uses T-Mobile's LTE spectrum and current unmodified handsets, but the new direction would involve building out SpaceX's own terrestrial cell network, likely in dense urban areas where satellite coverage is physically limited by walls and ceilings.

2. **Echostar spectrum acquisition underpins the plan:** SpaceX previously purchased wireless spectrum licenses from Echostar — first for approximately $17 billion, then again for $2.6 billion — giving it the AWS4 and H-Block spectrum needed for a next-generation direct-to-cell service. These frequencies are distinct from the current T-Mobile partnership spectrum and are reserved for next-generation mobile satellites (called "V2 mobile satellites" in the S-1), which are expected to begin deploying on Starship in 2027. The S-1 also discloses that as of March 31, 2026, SpaceX's dedicated satellite-to-mobile constellation of approximately 650 V1 mobile satellites serves about 7.4 million monthly unique devices across roughly 30 countries.

3. **Terrestrial network build-out vs. T-Mobile acquisition:** Loomis argues that a full T-Mobile acquisition — which he estimates could cost around $300 billion — is unlikely due to the cost, legacy infrastructure baggage, and regulatory complexity, especially if SpaceX is also contemplating a Tesla acquisition at some point. Instead, he believes SpaceX will pursue a hybrid approach: continuing to expand satellite-based direct-to-cell coverage while selectively building terrestrial towers in the highest-ROI dense locations. He acknowledges this would be a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar undertaking regardless of which path is chosen.

4. **SpaceX acquires Mesh Optical Technologies:** Elon Musk received FTC regulatory approval to acquire Mesh Optical Technologies, a startup founded by former SpaceX engineers who previously worked on laser-link communication for Starlink. Mesh develops optical transceivers that use light instead of copper to transmit data between servers and racks within a single data center, offering improved power efficiency and lower latency. No financial terms were disclosed. Loomis notes the near-term application is for terrestrial data centers — potentially including Colossus — while longer-term the technology could translate to inter-satellite communication. He highlights that approximately 60% of optical transceiver manufacturing currently takes place in China, making the acquisition strategically significant for supply chain security, export control insulation, and AI infrastructure scaling.

5. **SpaceX stock and lockup period explainer:** Sawyer Merritt reported that the FTSE Russell will add SpaceX stock to its Russell US indices. Loomis uses this as a springboard to explain SpaceX's share lockup structure. He breaks shares into three buckets: (1) the current public float of roughly 639 million Class A shares (~5% of total); (2) a 180-day early release block of about 4.6 billion shares (~35%), staggered through the rest of 2026 with a full release by December 8, 2026; and (3) an extended block of about 7.8 billion shares (~60%), comprising Elon Musk's ~6.4 billion shares plus a separate ~1.76 billion share block, all set to unlock around Q2 2027. Elon's shares specifically are set to unlock June 12, 2027 — one year after the IPO. He expects significant index-driven buying (MSCI World, NASDAQ 100, and eventually S&P 500 in 2027) to partially offset selling pressure from unlocks, and notes that by end of 2026 roughly 40% of all SpaceX shares will be freely tradable.

6. **VW weighing massive restructuring:** Volkswagen is reportedly considering cutting up to 100,000 jobs and closing four German factories in what would be the largest restructuring in automotive industry history. The stated root cause is weak sales, not merely high costs. Loomis frames this as an early, predictable sign of legacy automakers crumbling under the pressure of the EV transition.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Semi traction control testing:** Tesla VP of Vehicle Dynamics Dan Priestley posted about Tesla Semi slip-traction control testing conducted in Alaska, noting that the trailer was loaded with a concrete block over the fifth wheel and solid steel bars, creating a low center-of-gravity load. He emphasized the multi-motor, high-resolution sensing control developed in-house. The Tesla Semi account simultaneously highlighted California incentive programs: the HVIP program offers $120,000 off per semi for up to 20 trucks, and the California Clean Fuel Rewards offers an additional $120,000 off with no maximum truck limit and no scrapping required — stackable for small fleets, effective immediately. That's a potential combined $240,000 off the purchase price per vehicle.

2. **Q2 delivery and energy expectations:** Per Sawyer Merritt citing analyst consensus, Tesla is expected to deliver 46,000 vehicles in Q2 2026 and deploy 13.8 gigawatt-hours of energy storage. Tesla stock closed at $379.71, up 1.22% on the day, outperforming the Nasdaq which was down 1.09%.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD lawsuit settled, investigation still open:** Tesla settled a lawsuit stemming from a 2023 fatal crash in Arizona in which a pedestrian was struck by a Tesla operating on FSD while attempting to direct traffic. Significant sun glare was cited as a contributing factor. Financial terms were not disclosed. Loomis notes that Tesla's own SGO reporting confirmed FSD was active during the incident, the investigation was upgraded to an engineering analysis, and it remains open. He distinguishes the FSD of 2023 from current FSD capability but urges continued vigilance.

2. **Senator's FSD criticism dismissed as misinformation:** Senator Blumenthal publicly demanded Tesla be held accountable for an alleged self-driving crash. Loomis dismisses this as fact-free narrative warfare, expressing frustration at political figures he believes are spreading misinformation about Tesla's autonomous driving systems without factual grounding.

## Market & Competition (Terafab / Compute)

1. **Terafab to cover memory, not just logic:** Loomis reminds viewers that Elon Musk clarified that the Terafab is intended to produce over one terawatt of compute every year, explicitly including logic, memory, and packaging — addressing speculation about whether memory production would be included. Loomis frames this as evidence that Elon identified the memory shortage bottleneck months before it became widely recognized.
