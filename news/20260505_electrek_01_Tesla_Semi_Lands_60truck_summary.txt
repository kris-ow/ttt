Two California port trucking operators — Big F Transport (40 units) and NICA Container Freight Line (20 units) — have placed a combined 60-truck Tesla Semi order through Forum Mobility, marking one of the largest drayage-sector fleet commitments for the Semi to date. Both fleets will operate out of Forum Mobility's FM Santa Fe depot in Rancho Dominguez, California, expected to begin operations in early 2027.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **60-truck combined order from port drayage operators:** Big F Transport committed to 40 units and NICA Container Freight Line ordered 20, both to be deployed from Forum Mobility's charging depot in Rancho Dominguez. Big F Transport notably already operates nine Daimler eCascadia trucks and conducted real-world Tesla Semi testing before switching — a direct competitive comparison that Tesla apparently won. NICA Container, a family-owned company founded in 2014, operates out of the Ports of Long Beach, Los Angeles, and Houston.

2. **Nevada Gigafactory production ramp underway:** Tesla confirmed last week that the first Semi rolled off its new high-volume production line at Gigafactory Nevada — a 1.7-million-square-foot facility designed for annual capacity of 50,000 trucks. The Long Range model is priced at $290,000 (500-mile range); the Standard Range is approximately $260,000 (325-mile range), per quotes obtained by Electrek.

3. **Dominant demand signal in California voucher program:** In California's Clean Truck & Bus Voucher program between January 2025 and February 2026, the Tesla Semi accounted for 965 of 1,067 applications. Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo combined received fewer than 100. Port drayage is emerging as the Tesla Semi's primary early market, with additional pilots and deliveries from MDB Transportation and Hight Logistics at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach.

## Market & Competition

4. **Charging infrastructure ecosystem expanding:** Tesla has revealed 64 Megacharger locations across 15 states and launched its Semi Charging for Business program, with Megacharger units starting at $188,000 for two posts. Forum Mobility — backed by Amazon's Climate Pledge Fund, Edison International, and Obvious Ventures, with $15 million in Series A funding and a $400 million joint venture with CBRE Investment Management — operates as a third-party charging depot provider targeting smaller drayage carriers. The FM Santa Fe depot will feature 14 megawatt-class chargers supporting over 200 zero-emission trucks. Forum Mobility plans to commission four additional shared depots in 2026 and into 2027.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues port drayage is the ideal beachhead market for the Tesla Semi given short, predictable routes and favorable TCO math in California. He flags production execution — not demand — as the key risk: Tesla's Nevada factory just began ramping and must prove it can deliver at scale.
