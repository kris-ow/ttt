## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Impact Report pegs Cybercab efficiency at ~165 Wh/mile, projecting nearly half the emissions per mile of a Model 3/Y ride due to higher utilization; new RIM paint process cuts paint-cycle time from hours to minutes and eliminates a traditional paint shop.
- Cybercab parts count ~4,000, versus ~10,000 for Model Y and ~30,000 for a Jaguar I-PACE — roughly matching the parts count of Waymo's bolt-on sensor suite alone, per the report.
- Miami's robotaxi rollout used a smaller pre-launch safety-driver team than Austin's 300+, per The Information; geofence estimated at ~15 square miles with likely under 10 vehicles deployed.
- Production Cybercab units reportedly carry upgraded dual-GPS hardware for improved positioning accuracy, distinct from current Model Y robotaxi (Project Halo) telematics — unconfirmed, sourced to an unnamed insider.
- The Information mapped ~31 U.S. robotaxi deployment/testing locations; a ~680,000 sq ft speculative warehouse near Giga Texas is seen as infrastructure buildout for fleet servicing.
- Cern Basher argues Tesla will likely keep all early Cybercabs in its own fleet before eventually selling units to individual owners, citing political/operational reasons to distribute ownership as the fleet scales.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's Impact Report (65M sampled 2025 miles) claims FSD Supervised is 5% more efficient than manual driving and cites 8x fewer collisions; major accidents per million miles reportedly fell from ~21 (2024) to ~0.19 (2025).
- NHTSA's head said the agency will "absolutely consider" dropping steering-wheel requirements for vehicles never intended to be human-driven; no legislation yet exists on this point.
- Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is "working on" natural-language destination memory for FSD, responding to a user request.
- Lars Moravy detailed four internal AI use-cases at Tesla — lessons-learned database, KPI/leaderboard drift detection, cross-system reasoning links, and shared service-center knowledge base reportedly reducing wait times.
- Cern Basher called HW3 inclusion in the robotaxi network "highly unlikely," estimating under 10% of HW3 owners bought FSD in full; AI4 inclusion seen as possible but narrowing as Tesla standardizes on AI5.
