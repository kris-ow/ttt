## Brief

- Tesla Impact Report details Cybercab efficiency, RIM paint process, safety stats
- Miami robotaxi launch used fewer safety drivers than Austin per The Information
- Tesla caps internal AI token spend at $200/week per engineer
- NHTSA open to dropping steering-wheel requirement for driverless vehicles
- Model Y tops both Netherlands and China sales charts in H1/June
- Waymo expands driverless testing to Las Vegas, San Diego, Denver, Tampa

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab reportedly hits ~165 Wh/mile efficiency and cuts parts count to ~4,000 versus ~10,000 for Model Y, per Tesla's Impact Report.
- Production Cybercab units are getting upgraded dual-GPS hardware for improved positioning accuracy, distinct from current Model Y robotaxi telematics, per Tesla-focused outlet reporting.
- Miami's robotaxi launch used a smaller pre-launch safety-driver team than Austin's 300+, with the geofence estimated at ~15 square miles and likely under 10 vehicles deployed, per The Information.
- A new RIM paint process reportedly cuts Cybercab paint cycles from hours to minutes and eliminates a traditional paint shop entirely.
- Commentary (futureaza) argues Tesla will initially keep all Cybercabs in its own fleet before eventually selling to individual owners, citing political and operational reasons for distributing ownership later.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's Impact Report cites major accidents falling from ~21 to ~0.19 per million miles year-over-year (2024 vs. 2025), with FSD Supervised 5% more efficient than manual driving on emissions.
- Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is "working on" natural-language destination memory for FSD, responding to a user request for conversational directions.
- NHTSA's head said the agency will "absolutely consider" dropping steering-wheel requirements for driverless vehicles, though no legislation yet exists on this point.
- Commentary (futureaza) argues HW3 vehicles are "highly unlikely" to ever join the robotaxi network; AI4 inclusion faces a narrowing window as Tesla standardizes on AI5.
- Lars Moravy detailed four internal AI use-cases at Tesla spanning manufacturing lessons-learned databases, KPI tracking, cross-system reasoning, and service-center knowledge sharing that is reportedly cutting service wait times.
