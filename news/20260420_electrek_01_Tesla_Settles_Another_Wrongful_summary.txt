Tesla settled a wrongful death lawsuit brought by the family of 18-year-old passenger Edgar Monserratt Martinez, killed in a 116 mph crash in Fort Lauderdale in May 2018, with terms undisclosed. The settlement was confirmed April 20, 2026, just as jury selection was set to begin.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Background — Speed Limiter Removal at Service Center:** After driver Barrett Riley received a speeding ticket for driving 112 mph, his father had Tesla install an 85 mph speed limiter on the family's 2014 Model S. The lawsuit alleged a Tesla technician at the Dania Beach service center later removed the limiter without parental consent. Riley subsequently crashed at 116 mph in a 30 mph zone, killing himself and Martinez; a third passenger survived. The Martinez family alleged both technician negligence and a defective battery that contributed to the fatal fire.

2. **Prior Trial Outcome and Tesla's Speed Limit Mode:** In a 2022 trial brought by the Riley family, a jury found Tesla only 1% negligent — assigning 90% blame to Barrett Riley and 9% to his father — resulting in a roughly $105,000 net award against Tesla from a $10.5 million total verdict. Notably, Tesla introduced its PIN-protected Speed Limit Mode (capping speed between 50–90 mph) via OTA update in June 2018, weeks after the crash, explicitly dedicating the feature to Riley's memory.

3. **Growing Pattern of Pre-Trial Settlements:** The article frames this settlement within a broader litigation trend. Since a Miami jury awarded $243 million in a fatal Autopilot case (finding Tesla 33% liable), Tesla has reportedly settled at least four additional wrongful death lawsuits before reaching a jury. Tesla's total legal exposure across more than 20 active litigation tracks is cited at up to $14.5 billion.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert notes that while this case is unrelated to Autopilot, it "fits into a broader pattern" of Tesla settling cases to avoid jury trials following a large adverse verdict — framing the settlement strategy as reactive to litigation risk.
