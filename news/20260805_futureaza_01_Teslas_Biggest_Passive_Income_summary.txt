This video is a sponsored/promotional conversation between the host and John from BoltWise, walking through a new "fleet calculator" tool meant to help individuals model the economics of buying a used Tesla (or eventually a Cybercab) to deploy into a future Robotaxi network. Rather than delivering new Tesla company news, it demonstrates the tool's assumptions and inputs, and answers viewer FAQs about BoltWise's used-Tesla valuation and search product.

## Robotaxi
1. **Fleet calculator models individual car economics for a future private Robotaxi network:** BoltWise's new tool lets a user input a specific used Tesla (e.g., a 3-year-old hardware 4 Model Y priced around $35,000), assumptions about when the Robotaxi network might open to private owners (John suggests roughly a year as "fairly reasonable," though this is fully adjustable), an assumed net annual profit per car (illustrated at $20,000), vehicle lifetime mileage, and a discount rate, then computes payback period, 5-year profit, and an XIRR return (illustrated at 44% in one example, rising further if the car was bought cheaply with FSD already paid for).
2. **Fleet composition speculation — Cybercab vs. Model Y:** John states the common industry take that over 90% of rides could be served by a one-or-two-person Cybercab, but argues a larger fleet should still include a meaningful share of Model Ys (he estimates 75–80% Cybercab / rest Model Y) to cover edge cases the Cybercab can't handle.
3. **Hardware 3 vs. hardware 4 risk framing:** John applies a lower discount rate (~12%) to hardware 4 vehicles he considers likely to enter the Robotaxi network directly, versus a higher discount rate (15–17%) for hardware 3 vehicles, reflecting the added risk/uncertainty around whether and when a paid hardware upgrade/conversion (which John estimates could cost at least $3,000, his own estimate, not company-confirmed) would be required.
4. **In-car compute/inference monetization treated as speculative upside:** John says Tesla has discussed potentially letting idle hardware 4 cars sell compute/inference capacity, guessing this could arrive in roughly nine months and generate perhaps $100, but he zeroes this out by default in the model since he views it as too speculative for most users to value.
5. **Interim income options while awaiting network access:** For hardware 3 owners (whose cars can't join the Robotaxi network until a paid conversion is available, if ever), the tool lets users model driving for Uber (personally or via a hired driver) as a bridge income source that improves projected ROI/IRR.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Used-Tesla valuation product and dealer knowledge gaps:** BoltWise's broader platform uses VIN sequencing and in-car screen data to identify hardware version (e.g., distinguishing hardware 3 vs. hardware 4 in 2023 Model Ys) and whether FSD is paid, addressing what John describes as widespread lack of Tesla-specific knowledge among independent used-car dealers; he cites his own informal test finding 92% of dealer listings include usable screen photos.
2. **Special Tesla legacy perks (SEO1) affect valuation:** John notes that Teslas with legacy free-supercharging trim (referred to as SEO1, mostly on 2016–2017-era S/X models) are rare and often kept far beyond normal vehicle lifespans (owners replacing batteries to preserve the perk), which the tool tries to account for separately from standard depreciation assumptions.
