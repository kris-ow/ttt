## Autonomous Driving
1. **BMW and Mercedes Retreat from Level 3 Autonomy:** The host references an April 21st article reporting that BMW and Mercedes have stepped back from Level 3 autonomy due to high costs and weak consumer demand. The host argues this vindicates Tesla's approach, noting the technology was essentially only useful in traffic jams — limited to specific highways, specific weather conditions, and a maximum speed of 35 mph in zones typically rated 60–70 mph — while costing consumers $2,500 per year ($208/month). The host had previously argued the compute was insufficient to scale, a position he says was vindicated.

2. **Tesla Expands FSD Hiring Globally:** Tesla has begun hiring FSD vehicle operators in nine new countries, described as following recent regulatory breakthroughs and successful testing campaigns in Europe. The host frames this as a significant step toward global FSD expansion, noting the breadth of the hiring wave across multiple regions.

3. **Tesla FSD Subscription Revenue Hits $546 Million ARR:** A newly released Tesla slide deck revealed that FSD subscriptions have exceeded $546 million in annual recurring revenue, described as transforming the driver assistance suite into a major software business. The host highlights that Wall Street places a premium on recurring revenue for its predictability and extrapolates this to over $5 billion over 10 years as a rough illustration of scale.

## Robotaxi
1. **Waymo Skips Austin Public Meeting After Ambulance-Blocking Incident:** Waymo declined to attend an upcoming public meeting in Austin regarding a robotaxi that blocked an ambulance the prior month, stating it had already addressed questions in private briefings. The host is sharply critical of this posture, framing it as dismissive of legitimate public accountability.

2. **Waymo Hit-and-Run Incident:** The host plays video footage showing a Waymo vehicle striking four cars and then leaving the scene — a hit-and-run. The host contrasts this with what would happen to a human driver in the same situation, expressing frustration at the lack of consequences for the company.

3. **Waymo Blocking Three Lanes of Traffic in Atlanta:** Additional footage shown depicts a Waymo vehicle stopped and blocking three lanes of traffic in Atlanta, apparently unable to navigate a malfunctioning stoplight operating as a four-way stop. The host attributes the failure to insufficient AI reasoning capability, arguing that sensor count matters less than the underlying decision-making architecture.

4. **Tesla Robotaxi App Drops for Android:** The host briefly acknowledges a viewer comment confirming that the Tesla robotaxi app has been released for Android phones, describing it as an exciting development.

## Market & Competition
1. **Uber's "Asset Maxing" Strategy Critiqued:** Uber is positioning its rider data as its core asset in what the company calls an "asset maxing" strategy. The host dismisses this, drawing an analogy to Movie Pass, which gathered enormous data on moviegoers but failed because the data had no actionable monetization path — concluding that knowing people take rides is not a competitive moat.

2. **China EV Sales — Tesla Leads, BYD Competitive:** A China March EV sales report shows Tesla holding the number one position in a ranked list, with BYD appearing at positions five, seven, and nine. The host acknowledges BYD's aggregate performance is strong and calls the competitive environment "huge," but notes Tesla is still performing remarkably well in the market.

3. **BYD and CATL Fast Charging Developments:** BYD has deployed megawatt-level public flash charging in China, confirmed as functional by journalist Kyle Connor on a recent visit. CATL (referred to as "CL" in the transcript) has demonstrated a battery capable of charging 10–80% in 3 minutes and 44 seconds, and 20–98% in approximately 9 minutes even at -22°F (-30°C). The host notes CATL holds over half the global EV battery market and that these are shipping commercial products, not concept announcements.

4. **ChargePoint Announces 600 kW Charger:** ChargePoint's new Express Solo charger can deliver up to 600 kW and charge up to four EVs simultaneously, supporting CCS and NACS (described as "Omniport technology"). The host notes this exceeds Tesla's V4 Supercharger at 500 kW but points out ChargePoint doesn't manufacture vehicles, raising questions about near-term real-world applicability.

5. **Tesla Semi Pricing vs. Volvo Electric Semis:** Volvo has sold significantly more electric semis than Tesla — roughly ten times as many — but at $400,000–$500,000 per unit, approximately double Tesla's apparent price point. California rebate program invoices suggest Tesla is selling the Semi at $240,000–$260,000, giving Tesla a substantial cost advantage.

6. **Cybertruck vs. Rivian R1T Safety Scores:** Both the Cybertruck and Rivian R1T scored well on IIHS safety tests. Tesla received the highest marks on all three tests evaluated; Rivian received the highest marks on two of three and a second-tier rating on the third.

7. **Aberdeen Hydrogen Bus Fleet Failure:** A hydrogen bus fleet in Aberdeen consisting of 25 buses, costing £14 million, has been described as a failure with vehicles stranded. The host frames this as confirmation of his long-held view that hydrogen for passenger/transit vehicles is not a viable path.

8. **Taipei Going Fully Electric for Scooters:** The city of Taipei is mandating a full transition to electric scooters, which the host welcomes based on personal experience with the noise of gas-powered scooter traffic in Asian cities.

9. **Model Y as Emergency Fleet in Melville, Western Australia:** The City of Melville in Western Australia has transitioned its entire emergency response fleet to Model Y vehicles, reporting $50,000 in annual savings from fuel and maintenance, with vehicles deployed 24/7 on 12-hour rotations.
