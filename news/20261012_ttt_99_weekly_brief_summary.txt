## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla rebranded FSD to "Tesla Assisted Driving" across Europe after Germany's transport ministry called the FSD name misleading; the deal caps overspeed at 10% over posted limits (vs. 50% in the Netherlands), with Germany now backing EU-wide approval using this concession. Electrek notes the rebrand is cosmetic — the German page is a translated US FSD page with the name swapped — and the US retains "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" branding and 50% overspeed behavior.
- Reuters reported emails showing Tesla pressured Dutch regulator RDW to shrink its FSD review scope and disputed a €365,000 invoice; seven safety researchers said Tesla's EU "evidence dashboard" compares small samples of professional-driver miles against surrogate behaviors (honking, hard braking) rather than crash data — the same methodology behind Tesla's original "10x safer" claim later revised to 7x.
- EU-wide FSD vote slipped again to no earlier than December; eight countries have approved (adding Czechia and Croatia this week), Slovakia expected ninth "within days," but Germany, Italy, Spain remain unapproved and France/Sweden maintain speeding objections.

## Robotaxi
- Texas DMV Cybercab registrations hit 169 (up 43 week-over-week), a pace Bhakdi calls roughly seven times faster than Model Y robotaxi scaling at the same launch stage; a separate tracker cited a record 868 Cybercabs in Austin this week, a materially higher figure than the DMV count, alongside large unconfirmed staging lots spotted in Houston (~100-200 units).
- A Tesla Cybercab lost its entire driver-side door panel in a low-speed Philadelphia crash; fault and FSD engagement are unknown, and Tesla is not on PennDOT's certified automated-vehicle list.
- NHTSA granted Tesla a 30-day extension (to October 30) on its Cybercab self-certification audit — shorter than the 8-week extension Tesla requested.
- Robotaxi hours extended to 6am-11pm; Musk says nighttime animal detection remains the key blocker to 24/7 operation, while riders and reporters note the service continued operating through heavy rain in Tampa and a storm in Austin without shutting down.

---

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX agreed to buy nationwide 800MHz low-band spectrum from Grain Management (reported ~$6B, undisclosed officially), which Musk called "the last critical piece" for complete US phone coverage; the FCC separately approved a 15,000-satellite Gen 2 Starlink Mobile constellation targeting ~150 Mbps/user versus ~4 Mbps today.
- AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile shares fell roughly 5-8% after-hours on the news, directly rebutting prior dismissive comments from AT&T's CEO and T-Mobile's CFO on satellite-to-phone viability; the three carriers formed a joint venture October 1 to pool spectrum in response.
- American Airlines expanded Starlink to its entire 1,030+ mainland fleet (up from 500), installations starting early 2027; United has committed through 2026, while Delta has instead backed Amazon's Kuiper.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX shares slid into the October 9 unlock of 328 million shares (the second such unlock in two weeks), sliding two days ahead of the date versus a one-day lead before the prior unlock; another 328M-share unlock is due October 24, with a larger ~1.3 billion-share unlock expected late October/November.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas reiterated overweight and a $300 price target, saying SPCX trades ~40% below mega-cap AI peers; he noted an informal poll of 40 institutional clients found zero owned the stock.
- SpaceX is reportedly seeking to raise $40 billion in debt to fund Nvidia chip purchases toward a 10-gigawatt 2027 compute target, on top of over $100B reported gross cash.
- Musk clarified the Terafab chip facility will be owned and operated solely by SpaceX/Tesla, with TSMC only potentially leasing floor space — a more limited role than earlier reporting suggested.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's "Other Models" category (mainly Cybertruck) fell to 8,295 Q3 deliveries, a five-year low, down 48% YoY, per Electrek — implying Cybertruck run-rate of roughly 28,000-32,000/year versus a planned 250,000/year line.
- Tesla Germany registrations rose 268% YoY in September to 12,552, the best month since December 2022, with Giga Berlin output reportedly rising to 7,500/week this month.
- Tesla filed a Clark County, NV permit for a dedicated Semi service center in Las Vegas (status "Awaiting Plans," not imminent), part of a planned ~20-location national Semi service network.
- Model Y was South Korea's #1 best-selling vehicle (any fuel type) for a third straight month in September, up 36% YoY.

## Energy
- Tesla's California VPP delivered a record 601 MW from 85,774 participating Powerwall homes (634 MW fleet peak) — the largest residential VPP event reported to date, part of a cumulative 5 GWh returned across 10 major 2026 events.
- Tesla launched Powershare Home Backup for Model 3/Y paired with Powerwall 3, offering "over two days" of backup; eligible only on US/Puerto Rico orders placed after October 1, with no grid-selling and no Model S/X support.
