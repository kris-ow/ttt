## Autonomous Driving
- Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents, framing it as validation of Tesla's camera-only, no-lidar approach.
- Unsupervised miles have grown at a double-digit weekly compounding rate for several months, per Elluswamy, with Tesla expecting that pace to continue through year-end; Musk separately said he believes it's "at least over 10%."
- FSD North America attach rate hit roughly 55% in Q2 (42% globally) across 1.48 million active subscriptions, a stat multiple hosts flagged as an underappreciated demand driver — Dillon Loomis calculates this implies roughly $791 million/year in recurring FSD revenue.
- Roughly 40% of FSD V15's planned improvement tracks are already merged into early builds running in the robotaxi fleet, per Elluswamy — an early, incomplete rollout with no full-completion timeline given.
- New-city launch effort is falling and should trend toward zero, per Elluswamy, with Tesla's stated goal shifting to statewide rather than city-by-city operation; Dillon Loomis believes full-state approval is unlikely this year.

## Robotaxi
- Cybercab manufacturing has been aligned to projected unsupervised-mile growth rates, per Elluswamy — analysts on two separate channels infer this points toward Cybercab entering paid robotaxi service within a couple of months.
- Dillon Loomis built an independent model from Tesla's disclosed 380,000-mile figure, estimating Tesla's unsupervised fleet at roughly 50 vehicles doing ~100 miles/day each as of mid-July, growing to perhaps 115-173 vehicles by late October under continued 10%-weekly growth — well short of "thousands" some had floated.
- Waymo has logged roughly 220 million fully autonomous miles (per Waymo) — one estimate cited by Loomis pegs Waymo at ~570,000 driverless miles/day versus Tesla's projected ~130,000 miles/week even at sustained growth, a comparison flagged as largely apples-to-oranges.
- Cybercab spotted undergoing winter testing in New Zealand, alongside a new interior seat glimpse, per Sawyer Merritt — qualitative signals with no new specs disclosed.
- Waymo is reportedly considering ending its Uber partnership amid a lobbying battle over AV rollout pace, with Uber said to be pushing to slow autonomous vehicle deployment — competitive friction among incumbents, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk reiterated Optimus has the largest opportunity but the least certain timing, citing all-new components and no existing supply chain; Tesla is repurposing its Model S/X line for million-unit Optimus capacity and building a new large-scale factory in Texas.
- Musk described Optimus plus AI as enabling a "quasi infinite economy" by giving digital intelligence "endectors" (end-effectors) to "shape atoms," removing labor-hour caps on production, and said work could become optional.
- Musk floated a digital-only version of Optimus as a possible independent software revenue stream, arguing the training also aids the physical robot's ability to operate human-designed interfaces; its edge over rival AI agents remains unproven.
- Dillon Loomis noted Optimus deployment timelines, unit costs, and third-party pricing remain essentially unknown, with early units expected to go first to an internal "Optimus Academy" rather than external customers.

## Financials
- Tesla's Q2 10-Q disclosed an AI hardware acquisition worth up to $1.95 billion in equity (~$1.73 billion contingent on milestones), which Dillon Loomis speculates may be Density AI, founded by a former Tesla Autopilot Hardware director who worked on Dojo.
- Energy gross margin fell from ~39% to ~20% in Q2; Tesla's CFO now guides energy margins to settle at 20-25% going forward, down from earlier expectations of 30-40%+.
- Regulatory credit revenue fell to $146 million in Q2 from a prior low of $380 million, attributed to expired CAFE penalties.
- Musk said Tesla secured a Micron memory allocation despite tight industry-wide supply, and is prioritizing execution speed over maximum capital efficiency at this stage.
- Musk's Economist interview, recorded the day after what the host called Tesla's "rough" Q2 print (worst stock week of the year), defended a 5-10 year investor horizon; he separately detailed personal tax exposure of ~45% at exercise plus ~45% estate tax, leaving him roughly a quarter of his wealth.
- Tesla's forward P/E for FY2027 sits near 140x per Dillon Loomis's estimate — well above Apple (35x), Amazon (23x), Google (22x), Microsoft (20x), Nvidia (17x) and Meta (17x) — which he argues still prices in substantial robotaxi growth and leaves downside risk; he says he isn't selling but wouldn't be surprised by a test of $260-280.

## Market & Competition
- Musk argued China's AI constraint is electricity and chips, not talent, claiming China's electricity generation exceeds the US, Europe, and India combined, and that China is "closer than most people realize" to solving chip lithography.
- Musk's proposed fix — solar-powered orbital data centers to bypass grid/permitting constraints — remains conceptual; the host notes no orbital data centers or training models exist yet, and the concept depends entirely on Starship's launch cadence and cost.

## Energy
- Megapack was framed by Musk as critical infrastructure for smoothing AI data-center power delivery, with falling cost per kWh expected alongside less price-sensitive data-center customers.
- Musk referenced upcoming solar manufacturing plans (location unconfirmed) and suggested an autonomous Semi could arrive around late 2026 or early 2027.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 13 (Version 3, second test flight) hit multiple milestones: all 33 Super Heavy Raptors ignited at liftoff, the booster completed its boost-back burn (missed on the prior flight) and splashed down in the Gulf, though not all planned landing engines lit, producing higher-than-targeted landing velocity.
- The ship reached a "nominal orbit insertion" call-out on a suborbital trajectory and executed what the broadcast called the softest Starship splashdown yet in the Indian Ocean, remaining intact and afloat.
- SpaceX demonstrated an in-space Raptor relight lasting ~14 seconds, up from ~3 seconds on a prior flight — a capability needed for future de-orbit burns.
- Six deployed Starlink V3 satellites carried engineering cameras and flashlights to image Starship's heat shield, supporting SpaceX's eventual goal of catching (recovering) the ship itself, which has not yet been achieved.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starship deployed 20 fully functional Starlink V3 satellites for the first time (versus mass simulators on a prior flight), with SpaceX confirming contact and laser links with all 20 within minutes; each V3 satellite offers ~1 terabit/second of downlink capacity, and a full V3 Starship launch (up to 60 satellites) delivers roughly 20x the constellation capacity of a Falcon 9/V2 launch.
- Louisiana partnered with Starlink to bring high-speed internet to more than 10,600 rural locations by end of summer, where fiber buildout would have been prohibitively expensive, per Sawyer Merritt.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX's first-generation AI satellite (unveiled June 9) specs: 150 kW peak compute, 70m wingspan, 20m deployed height, 110 sq m deployable liquid radiator.
- Roughly 38 days later (~July 16), Musk indicated SpaceX is already redesigning the satellite for 250 kW peak compute (+67%) with added batteries, reportedly to accommodate Nvidia's next-gen Vera Rubin NVL 72 rack — a host estimate puts combined gains at roughly 5x compute per satellite versus the original design.
- A Jo Bhakdi video frames orbital AI compute as a potential offset to a terrestrial data-center bottleneck (citing New York State curbs on centers over 100 MW and resistance in 20+ states), arguing gigawatt-scale orbital compute by 2028 would be "just in time" versus a 2030-2035 timeline causing broader AI-stock disruption — explicitly stating this news itself isn't expected to move SpaceX's stock for at least 12 months.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas turned bullish on SpaceX, citing "a disconnect between increasingly bearish investor sentiment and largely unchanged fundamentals" and calling current levels "an attractive entry point."
