## Robotaxi
- Tesla disclosed 380,000+ unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states, per Ashok Elluswamy on the Q2 call, with zero notable incidents (any reported incidents involved other actors hitting stationary vehicles).
- Elluswamy said unsupervised miles have grown at double-digit weekly rates for months and should continue through year-end; Musk separately put growth at "at least over 10%" weekly. Modeling by analyst Dillon Loomis, using a ~3,500-mile/week January baseline, estimates the fleet at roughly 50 unsupervised vehicles today, growing to somewhere between ~115 and ~173 by late October under sustained-vs-decaying growth scenarios — he expects up to ~200 deployed robotaxis by Q3, explicitly rejecting claims of thousands this year.
- Elluswamy said Cybercab production/deployment is being matched to the unsupervised-mile growth rate — analysts (Cern Basher, Herbert Ong) infer this points to Cybercab joining the paid network within a couple of months.
- New-city launch effort is falling and Tesla's stated goal is to move toward state-by-state and eventually near-instant activation rather than city-by-city, per Elluswamy; analysts expect full-state approval no earlier than next year.
- Cybercab spotted winter-testing in New Zealand, per Sawyer Merritt, alongside a fresh interior seat glimpse — validation ahead of wider deployment.
- Waymo, cited at ~220 million fully autonomous miles (per Cern Basher) and separately estimated near 570,000 driverless miles/day (a viral third-party figure cited by Dillon Loomis), dwarfs Tesla's current mileage — though commentators call the comparison largely apples-to-oranges given differing architectures (Waymo remote-operator fallback vs. Tesla's no-remote-operator approach).
- Waymo may be ending its Uber partnership amid a lobbying battle over AV rollout pace, per Sawyer Merritt — relevant competitive friction for Tesla's robotaxi ambitions.

## Financials
- Tesla's Q2 10-Q confirmed an AI hardware acquisition worth up to $1.95 billion in equity, ~$1.73B contingent on milestones; Dillon Loomis reiterates unconfirmed speculation this is Density AI, founded by a former Tesla Autopilot Hardware director who worked on Dojo.
- FSD attach rate hit 55% of North American deliveries (42% globally), spanning 1.48 million active subscriptions; Loomis calculates ~$791 million/year in recurring FSD revenue.
- Regulatory credit revenue fell to $146 million in Q2 from a prior low of $380 million, attributed to CAFE penalty expiration.
- Energy gross margin dropped from ~39% to ~20% in Q2; Tesla's CFO guided margins to settle at 20-25% going forward, below earlier 30-40% expectations.
- Musk, in an Economist interview recorded the day after "a very rough" Q2 print, defended a 5-10 year investment horizon against portfolio managers' "90-day mindset"; margins remain "barely above break even" with negative free cash flow.
- Tesla's forward P/E for FY2027 sits near 140x versus peers at 17-35x, per Loomis, who sees more downside than upside near-term (not ruling out $260-280) but says he remains a buyer on a 3-5 year view.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's FSD V15 (10x-parameter model) is already ~40% merged into early builds running in the robotaxi fleet, per Elluswamy — an unexpectedly early deployment with no completion timeline given.
- Musk said Starlink connectivity will eventually extend across the rest of the fleet, and FSD for Tesla Semi is targeted for later this year or early next year.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk reiterated Optimus has the least certain timing of Tesla's initiatives, citing all-new components and no existing supply chain; broad deployment appears generations away per analyst interpretation.
- Tesla is repurposing its Model S/X line for million-unit Optimus production capacity, with a new large-scale Texas factory under construction — read by analysts as a strong capex signal of confidence.
- Musk described a "quasi infinite economy" where humanoid robots plus AI remove labor-hour constraints on production, and separately floated a digital (software-only) version of Optimus as a possible independent revenue stream, though its edge over rival AI agents is unproven.
- Dillon Loomis notes Optimus deployment cost and pricing to third-party customers remain unknown, with early units expected to go to an internal "Optimus Academy" first.

## Energy
- Musk framed Megapack as key infrastructure for smoothing power delivery to AI data centers, with falling cost per kWh expected against less price-sensitive data-center customers.
- Musk referenced upcoming solar manufacturing plans (location unconfirmed) and an autonomous Semi possibly arriving late 2026/early 2027.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 13 (Version 3) hit multiple firsts: all 33 Raptors lit at liftoff, hot-staging succeeded, and the booster completed its boost-back burn — an objective missed on the prior flight — before splashing down in the Gulf, though not all 13 planned landing-burn engines lit, producing a higher-than-targeted landing velocity.
- The ship reached a "nominal orbit insertion" call-out on a suborbital trajectory and achieved what hosts called the softest Starship splashdown yet recorded in the Indian Ocean, remaining intact and afloat.
- SpaceX demonstrated an in-space Raptor relight lasting ~14 seconds, up from ~3 seconds previously — a capability needed for future de-orbit burns.
- Six Starlink V3 satellites carried engineering cameras to image Starship's heat shield, part of SpaceX's push toward eventually catching (recovering) the ship itself.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Flight 13 deployed 20 fully functional Starlink V3 satellites (not mass simulators), each offering 1 terabit/second of downlink capacity; SpaceX confirmed contact and laser links with all 20 within minutes.
- A Starship V3 Starlink launch delivers roughly 20x the constellation capacity of a single Falcon 9/V2 launch; a full flight carries up to 60 V3 satellites.
- Louisiana partnered with Starlink to bring high-speed internet to more than 10,600 rural locations by end of summer, per Sawyer Merritt.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX unveiled its first-gen AI satellite June 9 (150 kW peak compute, 70m wingspan, 20m deployed height, 110 sq m radiator); by July 16, Musk indicated an upgraded design boosting peak compute to 250 kW (+67%) with added batteries, reportedly to accommodate Nvidia's next-gen Vera Rubin GPU rack — a host estimate (Jo Bhakdi) puts potential compute gains at ~5x the original design.
- Bhakdi frames orbital AI compute as a potential offset to a terrestrial data-center bottleneck (citing New York State's ban on data centers over 100 MW and resistance in 20+ states), arguing gigawatt-scale orbital compute by 2028 would be "just in time," while a 2030-2035 timeline risks a "massive problem" for AI-linked stocks; he states this satellite news itself isn't expected to move SpaceX's stock for at least 12 months.

## Market & Competition
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas turned bullish on SpaceX, citing "a disconnect between increasingly bearish investor sentiment and largely unchanged fundamentals" and calling it "an attractive entry point."
- Musk argued China's AI constraint is electricity and chips, not talent, claiming China's electricity generation exceeds the US, Europe, and India combined, and that China is "closer than most people realize" to solving chip lithography.
