## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla files for Nevada Robotaxi permit — up to 5,000 vehicles in Year 1:** In the highest-signal post of the day (4,200 likes, 489 reposts), Tesla officially applied for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Nevada. The application seeks approval for **up to 5,000 robotaxis during the first 12 months** after the permit is granted. The geographic scope covers **Clark County (Las Vegas), including Harry Reid International Airport** — a commercially dense, high-fare corridor. This is a significant step in Tesla's robotaxi expansion strategy beyond its current California and Texas footholds. The Las Vegas market, with its high tourism traffic, airport demand, and Strip density, is arguably one of the most attractive unit-economics environments for autonomous ride-hailing. The 5,000-unit first-year ask dwarfs the current total active fleet of ~706 vehicles per RobotaxiTracker.com (as of 2026-06-04), signaling Tesla is preparing for a step-change in fleet scale pending regulatory approval.

2. **Used Cybertruck inventory restocked at higher prices:** Tesla sold out of used Cybertruck inventory within days, then restocked with higher average pricing. The new floor is **$72,900 for a used 2024 Foundation Series Cybertruck with 32,845 miles**, up from **$66,200** for a comparable used unit just days prior — a jump of roughly $6,700. While this is secondary-market pricing rather than new-vehicle demand, the rapid sell-through followed by a price increase suggests resilient consumer appetite for Cybertruck at current levels and gives Tesla room to capture more margin on used inventory.

3. **Lucid Gravity sighted in the wild (minor competition note):** A brief post noted a first real-world sighting of a white Lucid Gravity. Low signal for Tesla investors, but serves as a reminder that the luxury EV competitive set continues to expand incrementally.
