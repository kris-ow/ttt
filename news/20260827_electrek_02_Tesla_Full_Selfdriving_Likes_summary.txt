Electrek reports a Tesla running FSD v14.3.7 — Tesla's newest software build — nearly drove into an oncoming train at a railroad crossing this week, with the driver braking manually to avoid a collision; this is at least the third such incident Electrek has documented in 2026.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Latest FSD version fails at a railroad crossing:** Auto journalist Lei Xing (co-host of the China EVs & More podcast) posted dashcam video showing his Tesla, with "Self-Driving" active, approaching a crossing at 9 mph while crossing signals flashed and a train horn sounded audibly; the car began to slow but did not stop, and Lei braked manually to avoid the train. He confirmed the vehicle was running FSD v14.3.7, currently rolling out to AI4 vehicles. Electrek notes that Tesla's earlier v14.3 release notes claimed improved handling of objects "extending, hanging, or leaning into the vehicle path," which the article argues should logically cover crossing gates or a moving train, yet the failure persisted seven point-releases later.

2. **Pattern of prior railroad crossing failures:** The article cites earlier incidents it previously reported: a Tesla on FSD driving through lowered crossing barriers in West Covina, California in March without braking until after impact, and a Texas owner in April whose Tesla drove through a railroad gate seconds before an oncoming train, requiring him to accelerate to clear the tracks (the crossing arm struck his window). It also references a 2025 Pennsylvania incident in which a Tesla in "self-driving mode" turned onto tracks and was struck by a train. NBC News reportedly documented more than 40 accounts of Tesla vehicles failing at railroad crossings.

3. **Regulatory scrutiny ongoing:** NHTSA opened an investigation into FSD in October 2025 following 58 reported crashes involving the system running red lights and driving into oncoming traffic; that probe has since expanded to cover roughly 2.9 million FSD-equipped vehicles, described as one step short of a formal recall demand. Senators Ed Markey and Richard Blumenthal separately urged NHTSA to specifically investigate railroad crossing failures, calling them a "disturbing safety risk."

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues he expected Tesla to have fixed this failure mode by now given it is one of the most obvious "do not proceed" scenarios on the road, and contends the recurrence on the newest FSD build undercuts Elon Musk's stated timeline for unsupervised self-driving in consumer vehicles this year.
