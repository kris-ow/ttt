## Robotaxi
- Tesla's 2025 Impact Report confirms Robotaxi launched June 2025 in Austin with Model Y, with Cybercab to become the primary fleet vehicle. Cybercab is a steering-wheel-free two-seater (85% of ride-hail trips carry one or two riders) using 4680 cells, steer-by-wire and 48V architecture.
- A new reaction injection molding (RIM) process cuts Cybercab manufacturing cycle times from hours to minutes, cutting manufacturing GHG emissions 35% and VOC emissions 100% versus traditional paint, per the Impact Report. Tesla says Cybercab will avoid nearly 2x the per-mile emissions of Model 3/Y.
- Cybercab production figures remain contested: Lars Moravy previously hinted at "multiple hundreds" (250 cited elsewhere), but multiple commentators (citing drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer and other sightings) speculate actual output is now over a thousand, or north of 500 — these remain unconfirmed estimates, not tracker-verified figures.
- Robotaxi/Cybercab testing footprint is expanding: Model Ys with robotaxi-style camera washers and manufacturer plates spotted testing in New Orleans; testing reportedly underway in over 30 cities total, though few formal launches are confirmed. Miami is now live with an unknown but likely small fleet.
- Tesla conducted Cybercab accessibility testing with blind/visually impaired riders ahead of broader deployment.
- Tesla leased Austin's largest-ever spec industrial building (682,000 sq ft, ~5 miles from Giga Texas, completion expected January); purpose unconfirmed but speculated to support Robotaxi/Cybercab scale-up.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's Impact Report states FSD (Supervised) drivers see 8x fewer major collisions, 7x fewer minor collisions, and 6x fewer off-highway collisions versus the U.S. national average.
- FSD (Supervised) expanded to Australia, China, New Zealand and South Korea in 2025, with Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Lithuania and the Netherlands added in 2026.
- Tesla found FSD (Supervised) is 5% more energy-efficient than manual driving, based on 65 million sampled miles in 2025, with efficiency peaking at 25–35 mph.
- A new Tesla app update (not yet widely rolled out) will show whether FSD is actively engaged during a drive.
- Commentary (brighterwithherbert/CernBasher) argues Nvidia's Alpamayo self-driving demos look years behind Tesla's FSD, and that Tesla remains the only automaker offering consumer-testable point-to-point autonomy in North America.

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck, Model S, Model X and Semi combined for just 12,364 Q2 deliveries, a figure critics call inadequate for a production line designed for a quarter-million units/year; some of that total may include internally-transferred Cybercab units (per commentator James Stephenson).
- Speculation continues around a possible "Cyber SUV" built on the Cybertruck platform with composite body panels, potentially as a Model X replacement or dual-use human/autonomous design — explicitly unconfirmed, based only on a background model glimpsed near the Cybercab display.
- Model Y L continues rolling out in Japan following the country's best-ever Tesla sales month (~4,000 registrations, +183% YoY).

## Energy
- Tesla's Impact Report: Supercharger network 100%-renewable-matched for five years running (Giga Berlin, three years); customers saved over $1B on energy costs in 2025; VPPs prevented outages for 6.5 million homes and delivered 47 million backup hours; 213,000+ Powerwalls enrolled in VPP programs.
- New storage deals: Essiosoft (15+ GWh across UK, Western Europe, GCC, India) plus a previously announced ~25 GWh NAT Power deal total roughly 40 GWh — comparable to a full year of Megapack production and potentially nearing $10 billion in Tesla revenue over the next few years.
- "Tesla Home" launched as a branded home energy management system built on existing Opticaster AI optimization software, adding smart-breaker integration (reportedly via Eaton); framed by some as repackaging rather than new capability.
- Megapack fleet avoided ~470,000 metric tons of CO2e in 2025; Tesla Semi delivers 50% TCO reduction in California and 20% across the US.

## Financials
- JPMorgan reiterated Neutral, $475 price target, citing Q2 Model 3/Y, other-model and energy storage numbers all above expectations plus a strong European recovery. Baird set $522, citing Q2 deliveries beating estimates and energy deployments up 41% YoY.
- Both banks avoided committing to specific Robotaxi-fleet numbers; one commentator called Morgan Stanley's 30,000-Robotaxi-by-2030 estimate deliberately conservative.
- Tesla launched a "Cell Giga Challenge" inviting startups to pilot manufacturing tech on its live Giga Berlin 4680 line; applications close July 24, 2026, program starts August 2026. Tesla previously committed $250M to more than double Berlin cell capacity from 8 to 18 GWh, pushing cumulative cell-unit investment toward €1 billion.
- Q2 deliveries beat expectations but Tesla stock had one of its worst days in the past year the same week, closing at $42.90, down 4.02% — attributed to "buy the rumor, sell the news."
