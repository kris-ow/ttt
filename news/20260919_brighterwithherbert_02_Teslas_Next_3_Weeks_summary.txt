This is a weekly Tesla/SpaceX news roundup organized around a "next three weeks" calendar of dated catalysts: a new NHTSA policy speech and an active Cybercab audit, robotaxi fleet tracker data, a Roadster reveal date, the Nevada semi factory opening, and a Starship orbital flight carrying Starlink V3 satellites. The host's core argument is that this week shifted from "the launch" (Cybercab) to "everything that got scheduled because of it" — a string of checkable dated events rather than things investors have to take on faith, with NHTSA's policy shift being the single most important development.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi
1. **NHTSA sends Tesla a 21-question special order under oath:** On September 10th, NHTSA issued a special order under the Cybercab audit with 21 questions due September 30th, signed under oath by a Tesla officer, with penalties up to about $28,000/day. Questions probe which federal standards Tesla certified to, how it meets the foot-control brake rule without a pedal, whether temporary human controls were used in certification testing, and why Tesla didn't seek the 2,500-unit exemption.
2. **NHTSA head signals a new "can it drive" safety standard:** In a Thursday speech in Pittsburgh (summarized by Sawyer Merritt and reviewed by the host), NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison said the agency is developing a new federal standard focused on whether an autonomous vehicle can perform safely rather than on physical controls like steering wheels. He cited a Wednesday agreement with SAE launching the "Ascend" consortium, five proposed FMVSS amendments already underway, new guidance for "midscale driverless deployments," and described the approach as "using a scalpel," while stressing NHTSA remains "the cop on the beat." The host notes Cybercab is still not covered by the 2,500-unit exemption — Tesla self-certified, per Lars Moravy's April statement, which remains true.
3. **Texas robotaxi fleet growth stalls after launch surge:** Per the Texas Autonomous Fleet Tracker (cited by the host), Tesla had 439 vehicles registered to Robotaxi in Texas as of Monday (390 Model Ys, 49 Cybercabs), up from about 186 a month earlier — but most of that growth (314→420) came in one jump on September 2nd, the day before the Cybercab launch; only seven were added in the most recent week. Ethan McKenna, who runs the tracker, estimates roughly 300 of the 439 are driving with nobody in them. Miles remain at the 1 million unsupervised figure Ashok Elluswamy announced September 3rd; Tesla states >10% weekly mile growth, but the host calculates actual growth closer to 20%/week over the past month.
4. **Ashok Elluswamy previews FSD V15 improvements:** Responding to a driver's post about a swerve-to-avoid incident (September 11th), Ashok Elluswamy said version 15 will bring earlier hazard prediction, faster reaction time, and better collision avoidance. A week earlier he told another user that the next tech merge on the V15 plan, expected "next month or so," will enable 24/7 robotaxi operation; timing for customer cars was not given.
5. **Zurich Insurance rates FSD Supervised as lower risk in Australia:** Zurich, which underwrites Tesla's insurance there, began giving a discount (the host estimates around 5%, unconfirmed/not officially published) for FSD Supervised users this week — the first insurer in Australia and second globally after Lemonade in the US (which the host says offers around a 50% discount). Zurich's Alex Morgan cited Tesla's own safety-report data (the "seven times fewer collisions" figure, reposted by Elon Musk) as the basis.

## SpaceX — Starship / Starlink
1. **Starship Flight 14 slips to September 28th:** SpaceX moved its target for Flight 14 from September 22nd to "as early as" September 28th pending regulatory approval, with no reason given. The flight (new Block 3 ship and booster) will fly roughly six orbits over about 10 hours, splashing the ship in the Pacific west of Chile and the booster in the Gulf; no catch attempt on either.
2. **Flight 14 will carry 26 Starlink V3 satellites, framed as SpaceX's first "revenue generating flight":** Each V3 satellite is designed for one terabit/second, meaning 26 terabits on this flight — roughly 10x a Falcon 9 launch's current capacity, per the host. SpaceX's CFO called it a revenue-generating flight.
3. **Elon Musk and Cathie Wood float a speculative $10 trillion Starship revenue scenario:** Musk posted that the V3 constellation will "ultimately" deliver over 100x the bandwidth of the current ~11,000-satellite constellation. Ark Invest's Sam Korus estimated (from June) $19M in connectivity revenue per terabit/second, implying roughly $1B revenue per Starship launch at a full 61-satellite load — a projection, not a confirmed figure, and Korus himself noted revenue-per-terabit will decline as capacity floods in. Cathie Wood combined this with Musk's stated goal of 10,000 Starship flights/year to project $10 trillion/year in Starship revenue by 2030, calling SpaceX's prior $1.75 trillion valuation a "deep value opportunity" in that scenario; Musk replied it's "not impossible." The host cautions this stacks multiple unconfirmed assumptions (end-state constellation bandwidth, full-load launch economics, and a flight cadence far beyond Starship's 13 total flights to date) and flags Musk's own separate figure of roughly $1 trillion in SpaceX 2030 revenue as inconsistent with the $10T figure.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute / Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX CFO discloses a new, unnamed hosting deal worth ~$1.1B/month starting December 1st:** Brett Johnson said at a Goldman Sachs conference (Thursday) that a newly closed hosting deal adds roughly $13 billion in ARR starting December 1st, and that SpaceX has "even more conviction" in a $100 billion ARR target by end of year. He did not name the customer; the host speculates it could be Microsoft but says this is unconfirmed. Existing named deals are with Anthropic and Google.
2. **Musk moves up timeline for orbital AI compute to "next year":** Musk said Sunday he is "highly confident" SpaceX will be running Nvidia GB200-class (NVL72) AI computers in space next year — earlier than the "as early as 2028" stated in SpaceX's May IPO filing, the third time this date has moved earlier per the host. Gwynne Shotwell argued (per a writeup referenced by the host) that space offers free, infinite real estate and radiators facing deep space for free cooling, framing SpaceX as "as much an AI business as a space business."
3. **Musk declines to rule out a Tesla-SpaceX merger:** At the All-In Summit, Musk was asked why Tesla and SpaceX remain separate given their deep collaboration and gave a sarcastic non-answer implying eventual convergence; Shotwell added that SpaceX and xAI are "integrating faster than they thought" but not fully integrated yet. The host characterizes this as signaling a merger is likely eventually but stresses no terms, timeline, or filing exist, so the investor impact depends entirely on an unknown future exchange ratio and stock prices. Musk separately said the goal for the Tesla/SpaceX joint chip fab ("Terafab") R&D pilot at the Giga Texas campus is to produce something useful by end of 2027, ahead of a larger-scale Terafab planned for Grimes County.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's US EV market share rises to 52% in a shrinking market:** The Wall Street Journal, citing Motor Intelligence data, reported Tesla holds 52% of US EV sales year-to-date through August, up from 43% a year earlier — but Tesla's own US sales are down 16% while the overall US EV market is down about 30%, meaning share gains reflect competitors (Ford, GM, Honda, VW) pulling back faster. Model Y alone is about a third of all EVs sold in the US.
2. **AI5 chip production reportedly pulled forward:** Korean media reported (citing industry sources, not confirmed by Samsung or Tesla) that Samsung's Taylor, Texas fab began trial production of the AI5 chip on a 2nm node, ahead of a previously planned November start, with yield verification by year-end and volume supply in 2027. This aligns with Musk's on-record statement that AI5 will sample this year at both TSMC Arizona and Samsung Texas with high-volume production next year.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Reports of a mass-production supplier audit for Optimus in China:** Chinese media reported a Tesla team arrived in Ningbo Tuesday and began a mass-production audit of suppliers Wednesday; unit-count figures across reports don't agree with each other, and Tesla has given no 2026 production number. The host flags this as an unconfirmed rumor consistent with prior reporting of a 5,000-unit parts order, and notes Musk's July comment that ramp will be "extremely slow at first." Optimus V3 has not yet been shown, and Musk did not discuss Optimus at the All-In Summit.

## Financials
1. **Morgan Stanley models the Semi as an FSD revenue product:** Analyst Andrew Percoco wrote (Friday Sept 11) that with diesel above $6/gallon, an autonomous electric Semi could be roughly 20% cheaper per mile, more than double annual miles since the truck doesn't need rest, and lift profit per truck from about $37,000/year to a figure the analyst frames as a 400%+ increase (the transcript's stated "$22,000" appears to be a transcription error given the described direction of change). He prices Tesla's software take at 85 cents–$1/mile, equating to $12,000–$18,000/month per truck — comparable to 120–180 FSD subscribers' worth of revenue per truck. The host stresses this model is hypothetical pending actual autonomy on the Semi.
