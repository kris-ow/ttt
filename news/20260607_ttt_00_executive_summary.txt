## Robotaxi
- **Nevada permit application — 5,000 vehicles in Year 1:** Tesla formally applied for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Nevada, seeking authorization for up to 5,000 robotaxis during the first 12 months after approval. The geographic scope covers Clark County (Las Vegas), including Harry Reid International Airport — a high-fare, high-tourism corridor that ranks among the most attractive unit-economics environments for autonomous ride-hailing in the U.S. The application dwarfs the current operational fleet per RobotaxiTracker.com and signals Tesla is engineering a step-change in fleet scale pending regulatory clearance.

- **World Cup as Cybercab launch catalyst:** Bhakdi identifies the FIFA World Cup — beginning this weekend — as the most compelling near-term window for an unsupervised Cybercab public launch, noting Cybercabs are already physically present in Dallas, Houston, and Austin, and that Dallas hosts major World Cup matches. He stops short of calling it a certainty but says he "would really not be surprised if it happens," and believes a successful launch could push Tesla to $490.

- **2027 flagged as robotaxi inflection year:** Randy Kirk (FutureAzA) explicitly identifies robotaxi scaling as one of two primary catalysts — alongside Optimus — that drives Tesla's profit trajectory to "go crazy" in 2027, though no fleet targets or unit economics were specified.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD delay acknowledged:** Bhakdi concedes FSD has been "totally delayed" and that timeline uncertainty persists, contrasting it unfavorably with Starlink's execution track record. No new software version or regulatory approval was reported today.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla's casting pivot was strategic, not a retreat:** Casting expert Luca Greco (FutureAzA) clarified that Tesla cancelled its 15,000–16,000-ton casting machine order to pursue the "unbox" process using ~6,000-ton machines — a deliberate architectural choice made redundant by the structural battery pack, not a failure. The Cybercab and Cybertruck both retain front underbody castings, confirming the technology is alive and targeted.

- **Vertical integration is Tesla's durable casting edge:** Conformal cooling — developed in-house — cut Tesla's casting cycle time from over 170 seconds to 80 seconds or less. Greco's core point: supplier-dependent OEMs "can forget rapid iteration the way Tesla did" because they lack the financial incentive and process control to pursue such innovations on a customer's behalf.

- **Used Cybertruck prices rising on rapid sell-through:** Tesla restocked used Cybertruck inventory at a new floor of $72,900 for a 2024 Foundation Series unit with ~33K miles, up from $66,200 days prior — a ~$6,700 jump following rapid inventory sell-through, suggesting resilient demand and expanding used-market margin capture.

- **Q2 2026 vehicle sales "up substantially":** Randy Kirk asserts Tesla vehicle unit sales are materially higher in Q2 2026 versus prior quarters, which he expects to reduce the need for discounting and improve margins.

## Energy
- **Megapack deployments accelerating sharply in Q2:** Randy Kirk states Tesla is on track to deploy roughly double the Megapacks in Q2 2026 versus Q1 2026, calling the implied revenue and profit uplift from the energy segment "massive."

## Humanoid Bots
- **Figure AI at one robot per 90 minutes:** Figure AI's production line is reportedly building one humanoid robot approximately every 90 minutes, and the company's valuation reached $39 billion at its Series C (fall 2025) — roughly 15x its early-2024 valuation of $2.6 billion. No comparable Tesla Optimus production rate data was reported today.

- **Optimus radio silence read as bullish:** Bhakdi agrees with the view that the absence of recent Optimus news is a positive signal, attributing it partly to Elon Musk's focus on the SpaceX IPO process rather than any operational setback.

- **Apptronik valuation triples to $5B+ in 2026:** Apptronik — backed by Google and Mercedes — has seen its valuation triple to over $5 billion this year, per FutureAzA/Robo Strategy coverage, underscoring the broader private-market acceleration in humanoid robotics that frames the competitive context for Optimus.

## Financials
- **JP Morgan Tesla price target: $150 → $475:** Bhakdi notes JP Morgan raised its Tesla target to $475 and attributes the timing directly to JP Morgan's role as a SpaceX IPO bookrunner rather than a fundamental analytical revision — suggesting the upgrade may reflect relationship dynamics more than a model change.

- **Tesla trailing GAAP earnings under scrutiny:** A Fortune analysis cited Tesla's trailing GAAP earnings collapsing from $15 billion to $3.9 billion, with core operating earnings excluding regulatory credits and Bitcoin gains at $2.3 billion. Randy Kirk and Brian (FutureAzA) reject the framing as cherry-picking, arguing it ignores growing FSD subscription revenue and the Q2 recovery in vehicle sales and Megapack deployments.

## Market & Competition
- **Friday selloff: NASDAQ –4.18%, Tesla –6.56%:** Bhakdi characterizes the move as profit-taking after a ~25% NASDAQ rally from late March through early June, not the start of a sustained downturn. He notes Tesla's decline was actually below its implied beta-adjusted move, partly cushioned by the JP Morgan upgrade.

- **Tesla near-term range: $350 to $490:** Bhakdi outlines a wide 10-day band — $490 if a World Cup Cybercab launch materializes and the market recovers; $350–$380 if the SpaceX IPO creates additional selling pressure and the broader selloff extends. He assigns 95% probability to a Tesla–SpaceX merger within two years, with a Tesla price target of "maybe $750" at merger.

- **SpaceX Google compute deal: $920M/month:** Bhakdi flags a breaking deal in which Google agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million per month for Colossus data center access, on top of the previously reported $1.25–1.5 billion/month Anthropic is paying — implying SpaceX is now generating roughly $25+ billion annualized in high-margin compute revenue from two customers alone.
