## Robotaxi
- **Nevada permit application — 5,000 vehicles in Year 1:** Tesla formally applied for an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit in Nevada, seeking approval for up to 5,000 robotaxis during the first 12 months after grant. The geographic scope covers Clark County (Las Vegas) including Harry Reid International Airport — a high-fare, high-tourism corridor that ranks among the most attractive unit-economics environments for autonomous ride-hailing in the U.S. This would represent a step-change in fleet scale relative to current deployment.
- **World Cup as unsupervised launch catalyst:** Bhakdi flags the FIFA World Cup — opening weekend of June 7 — as the most compelling near-term trigger for an unsupervised Cybercab public launch, noting Cybercabs are already physically present in Dallas, Houston, and Austin, and that Dallas hosts major World Cup matches. He stops short of a guarantee but says he "would really not be surprised if it happens."

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD delay acknowledged:** Bhakdi concedes FSD has been "totally delayed" and that timeline uncertainty persists, contrasting its trajectory unfavorably with Starlink's scaling arc. No new software update or regulatory approval was reported today.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Used Cybertruck pricing power signal:** Tesla sold out of used Cybertruck inventory, then restocked at a new floor of $72,900 for a 2024 Foundation Series unit with ~32,845 miles — up roughly $6,700 from the $66,200 comparable price just days prior. Rapid sell-through followed by a price increase suggests resilient secondary-market demand.
- **Q2 vehicle sales recovering:** Per Randy Kirk (FutureAzA), Tesla vehicle sales are tracking "up substantially" in Q2 2026 versus prior quarters, which he expects will reduce the need for discounting and support margin improvement.
- **Giga casting competitive moat deepening:** Casting expert Luca Greco detailed how Tesla's vertical integration — engineers from casting, body-in-white, and die design working in the same room — enables iteration speed unavailable to supplier-dependent OEMs. Conformal cooling, developed in-house, cut casting cycle times from over 170 seconds to 80 seconds or less. Both Cybertruck and Cybercab use front underbody castings, confirming the technology remains central to Tesla's manufacturing roadmap.

## Energy
- **Megapack doubling in Q2:** Randy Kirk states Tesla is on track to deploy approximately double the Megapacks in Q2 2026 versus Q1 2026, calling it a "massive increase" in energy segment revenue and profit — a meaningful contributor to the broader Q2 earnings recovery thesis.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Optimus silence read as bullish:** Bhakdi agrees with viewer sentiment that the absence of recent Optimus news is a constructive signal, though he attributes part of the quiet to Elon Musk's bandwidth being consumed by the SpaceX IPO process.
- **Private humanoid market context:** Figure AI hit a $39 billion valuation at its Series C (fall 2025), up from $2.6 billion in early 2024, and is producing approximately one robot every 90 minutes. Apptronik's valuation tripled to over $5 billion in 2026. These figures reinforce the competitive backdrop Optimus is scaling into.

## Market & Competition
- **Friday selloff context:** NASDAQ fell 4.18% and Tesla dropped 6.56% (~$27/share) on June 6 — the worst NASDAQ session in over a year. Bhakdi frames it as profit-taking after a ~25% NASDAQ rally from late March through early May, not structural breakdown. Tesla's decline was below what its historical beta would imply (~8.3–8.5%), with JP Morgan's target upgrade to $475 cited as partial support.
- **JP Morgan target: $150 → $475:** Bhakdi attributes the upgrade directly to JP Morgan's role as a SpaceX IPO bookrunner rather than an independent analytical revision — a caveat worth noting.
- **Tesla–SpaceX merger probability:** Bhakdi assigns 95% probability to a Tesla–SpaceX merger occurring within two years, with a personal Tesla price target of ~$750 at merger (implying ~$2.2–2.5 trillion market cap). A Fortune analysis pegged a combined entity at ~$3.4 trillion ($1.75T SpaceX + $1.65T Tesla), which would be nearly 3x the largest merger ever completed.
- **SpaceX compute deals add $12B+ annualized revenue:** Google has agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million per month for Colossus compute access, on top of Anthropic's $1.25–1.5 billion per month deal. Bhakdi's preliminary SpaceX DCF (flagged as incomplete) suggests fair value of $196–$221/share for 2026, with his gut view that SpaceX is "dramatically undervalued" at its ~$2 trillion IPO price.
