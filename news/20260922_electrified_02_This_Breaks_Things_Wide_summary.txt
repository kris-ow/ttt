This Electrified video is a Tesla-focused roundup covering the ongoing NHTSA regulatory scrutiny of Tesla's Cybercab brake-pedal design, a landmark Tesla Semi order via a new truck-leasing alliance, updates on Grok integration in Tesla vehicles, Cybercab fleet/registration growth in Austin, and Mercedes-Benz choosing Waymo (alongside Nvidia's Alpamayo) over Tesla FSD for future autonomous driving efforts.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab NHTSA brake-pedal dispute clarified and expanded:** The host clarifies a prior video, noting that while Zoox and Tesla made a similar "brake pedal not required" argument to NHTSA, Zoox's overall exemption needs were broader than Tesla's. NHTSA's audit query on the Cybercab (question 19) asks Tesla to justify how it can certify compliance with FMVSS 135's requirement that service brakes be activated by a foot control; question 7 separately asks how removing temporary human driver controls complies with US code 30122, which bars making inoperative a safety-compliance device or design element. The host notes NHTSA amended its Part 555 exemption 34 days before the Cybercab launch so that any future exemption would retroactively cover already-deployed vehicles, and speculates (unconfirmed) this may have been done with Tesla in mind. Per-vehicle fines were described as around $27,000, meaning even 1,000 non-compliant Cybercabs would total roughly $27 million — a figure the host calls immaterial for Tesla, supporting his theory that Tesla may be strategically delaying compliance while FMVSS rules are updated. Tesla has 8 days left to respond to the audit query as of this video.
2. **Cybercab fleet and VIN registrations grow in Austin:** Tesla added Cybercabs to its registered Austin fleet overnight, per the host's own tracker, bringing the total to 69 (from 67) with 478 total registered Tesla vehicles (up from 476); Texas DMV VIN numbers were seen reaching roughly the mid-3,300s, which the host says suggests Tesla now likely has produced over 3,000 Cybercabs/Foundation Series units combined across the country. He speculates this buildup may relate to an upcoming X-platform-related event.
3. **Sample Cybercab customer UI spotted in Tesla showroom:** Footage reportedly from a Tesla showroom (via a third-party source) showed a Cybercab user interface resembling that of customer-owned Tesla vehicles, which the host takes as further evidence Tesla intends to eventually sell Cybercabs directly to consumers — though he still believes that timeline is further out than many expect.

## Market & Competition
1. **Mercedes-Benz partners with Waymo for autonomy, alongside Nvidia's Alpamayo:** Mercedes announced a global partnership integrating Waymo's "AI driver" into future Mercedes-Benz vehicles starting within the next two years, rather than partnering with Tesla FSD. The host notes Mercedes is running parallel autonomy tracks — Nvidia's Alpamayo reportedly for models like the CLA and S-Class, and Waymo for other vehicles — calling it a hedge because, in Mercedes' view, no single autonomy strategy has yet proven dominant. The host is skeptical of Waymo's claim to be "the global leader in embodied AI" and of claims that its system avoids city-specific retraining, arguing real-world deployment will still require per-city validation, as Tesla has experienced.

## Financials
1. **New Tesla Semi order via ZET Alliance leasing model:** A newly formed US shippers group called ZET Scale Alliance (jointly run by Catalyst Mobility, formerly Calstart, and the Smart Freight Center) placed an order for 2,500 Class 8 trucks, naming Tesla as the primary truck maker (Volvo, Rite, and Kenworth also eligible). This is described as the biggest electric truck order in US history, though the host stresses it is not confirmed to be all Tesla Semis. Shippers named include Microsoft, Pepsi, and Red Bull; the alliance will buy trucks and lease them back to shippers, shifting residual-value risk off individual carriers and onto the alliance. First deployments are planned across roughly ten freight hubs including California, Seattle, Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, Chicago, Atlanta, and the New York/New Jersey area. Even at half the headline number (1,250 units), the host notes this would roughly double the verified Tesla Semi order count on his own tracking dashboard. Tesla's official account confirmed being chosen as primary OEM for this order.
2. **TSLA and SpaceX daily close:** Tesla stock closed at $378.90, up 0.96% on the day; SpaceX (SPCX) closed at $154.72, up 1.89%, while the Nasdaq-100 was up 0.82%.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Grok/Grokbot integration expands in Tesla vehicles:** Tesla rolled out Grok connectors allowing in-car voice control of email, calendar, files, and tasks, plus "Grokbot" for placing orders (e.g., DoorDash, Uber Eats, OpenTable) and scheduling — currently limited to Super Grok Heavy subscribers at $300/month, with more tiers to follow. The host argues that in its current form, in-car Grok is largely a voice proxy passing prompts to a user's existing Grokbot team, so most users could achieve the same results more cheaply by prompting Grokbot directly from their phone rather than paying for Super Grok Heavy; he expects Tesla to build more robust in-car functionality over time. A Tesla-affiliated engineer (Yun Tasai) said the front camera cleaning sensitivity issue has been addressed, with some users reporting the nag resolved in a recent FSD version.
2. **Grok 4.7 rollout draws mixed early reviews:** Grok 4.7 began rolling out to some users; the host's own brief hands-on testing produced factual errors (including wrongly denying Grokbot was live in Tesla vehicles). A third-party AI commentator (Theo) reported Grok 4.7 was less token-efficient than 4.6 by 30-80%, scored worse on various benchmarks, was slower, and cost more than double Grok 4.6 in real-world use. Elon Musk had previously hyped 4.7 as exceeding all current models, then more recently said it should be roughly on par with a rival model, "better in some ways, worse in others," and flagged multimodal performance as needing improvement.
