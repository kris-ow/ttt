Tesla confirmed plans to hire 1,000 additional workers at Giga Berlin, targeting 7,500 vehicles per week by October, as European sales stage a meaningful recovery after two years of declines.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Second hiring wave at Giga Berlin:** The new 1,000 hires follow a separate 1,000-worker expansion announced in April, intended to lift output to ~6,000 vehicles per week by end of June. The October target of 7,500 units/week would represent a further ~25% increase, equating to roughly 390,000 vehicles per year — the factory's highest-ever sustained output, though still below the 500,000-unit annual capacity Tesla targeted at launch. Giga Berlin produces only the Model Y for the European market.

2. **Battery staffing also expanding:** In May, Tesla announced plans to recruit more than 1,500 employees for battery cell production in Germany, revisiting Elon Musk's 2020 pledge to make Grünheide the world's largest battery factory.

## Market & Competition
3. **European demand rebound:** After roughly a 27% sales drop last year, Tesla's EU registrations have grown for several consecutive months — up 67% year-over-year in April, with European registrations reportedly more than doubling in June. A key external driver is rising fuel prices across Europe since late February, which have broadly boosted EV adoption; battery-electric cars reached 19.7% of new EU registrations through April, up from 15.3% a year earlier. Renewed national incentive programs and a recovering overall auto market have added to the tailwind, with France, Sweden, Denmark, and Ireland all posting triple-digit percentage increases in April.

## Editorial Stance
Lambert views the hiring push as a clear signal Tesla's European slump is over for now, but cautions that the recovery is driven largely by external tailwinds — rising fuel prices and a low prior-year comparison — rather than product strength. He flags Chinese competition (BYD in particular) and any fading of the fuel-price tailwind as the real tests of whether Tesla can sustain these gains.
