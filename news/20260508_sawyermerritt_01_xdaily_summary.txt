## Executive Summary
Today's posts were headlined by two significant regulatory and milestone developments: NHTSA officially certifying the 2026 Tesla Model Y as the first vehicle to pass its new advanced driver assistance system tests, and Tesla announcing that FSD (Supervised) has surpassed 10 billion miles of real-world driving. Supporting these was strong global EV sales data showing record-breaking April performance across multiple markets, reinforcing demand recovery narratives heading into Q2 2026.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **NHTSA Certifies 2026 Model Y as First Vehicle to Pass New ADAS Tests:** In a high-engagement post (3.5K likes, 541 reposts), Sawyer Merritt reported that NHTSA officially announced the 2026 Tesla Model Y is the first vehicle model to pass the agency's newly established advanced driver assistance system tests. Vehicles manufactured on or after November 12, 2025 successfully met the new criteria across four test categories. NHTSA itself stated: *"By successfully passing these new tests, the 2026 Tesla Model Y demonstrates the lifesaving potential of driver assistance technologies and sets a high bar for the industry. We hope to see many more manufacturers develop vehicles that can meet these requirements."* This is a meaningful regulatory endorsement that reinforces Tesla's ADAS leadership, potentially influencing future policy frameworks, fleet purchasing decisions, and the regulatory pathway for more advanced autonomy deployments.

2. **FSD (Supervised) Crosses 10 Billion Miles:** Tesla sent an official email to customers announcing that FSD (Supervised) has now surpassed 10 billion miles of real-world driving. The email noted that Tesla's Self-Driving computer has "navigated nearly every driving scenario possible and continues to learn, adapt and improve on each additional drive." This milestone is significant for the autonomous driving thesis: the cumulative real-world data corpus underpinning Tesla's neural network training is a core competitive moat argument, and 10 billion supervised miles represents a scale that is extremely difficult for competitors to replicate.

3. **Tesla Semi Battery Specs Revealed via Regulatory Filing:** A regulatory filing disclosed the official production battery specifications for both Tesla Semi trims. The Long Range variant features an 822 kWh usable battery capacity using NMCA chemistry (4680 cells), a 500-mile range at 82,000 lbs gross combination weight, and a peak charging speed that was partially truncated in the post. While not directly tied to the robotaxi or Optimus thesis, this confirms large-scale 4680 cell deployment in a commercial vehicle context, relevant to Tesla's manufacturing scale and energy cost dynamics.

4. **Global EV Sales Show Strong April Recovery:** Multiple posts highlighted record-breaking April 2026 sales across key markets. Tesla China-made EV deliveries jumped 36% year-over-year to 79,478 units — the best April ever for the China operation. In South Korea, Tesla sold a record 13,190 vehicles in April, its highest monthly total ever in the country, exceeding the prior record (set in March 2026) by approximately 2,000 units, and outselling BMW and Mercedes combined. Tesla sales in Germany also posted the best April ever, with Merritt characterizing Europe broadly as seeing a "BIG comeback." Collectively, these data points suggest meaningful demand recovery following the challenging early-2026 period, which is relevant to near-term revenue and margin trajectory.

5. **OTA "Recall" Media Coverage Criticized:** Merritt pushed back on media framing of a Tesla software recall, noting that Tesla issued a free over-the-air update to resolve the issue, and that 30 articles covering the story buried this context — misleading readers into implying a physical recall. While not a new data point, this is relevant for investors monitoring headline risk around Tesla's regulatory relationship with NHTSA.

6. **Tesla Semi Hauling Cybercabs (Visual):** A video post showed a Tesla Semi transporting Cybercabs, generating notable engagement (2.5K likes, 199 reposts). While primarily visual, it signals active Cybercab logistics and distribution activity, consistent with scaling fleet operations.

7. **Lexus TZ All-Electric SUV Unveiled (Competition Context):** Lexus unveiled the TZ, an all-electric three-row SUV for the US market, with up to 300 miles of range, 77 kWh or 96 kWh battery options, 150 kW peak charging, a 35-minute 10–80% charge time, and NACS port compatibility. Merritt noted — somewhat dismissively — that the vehicle includes fake gear shifts and simulated gas engine sounds, a design choice he described negatively. The 150 kW peak charging rate and 300-mile range ceiling position the TZ below Tesla's performance benchmarks in comparable segments.
