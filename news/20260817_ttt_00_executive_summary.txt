## Robotaxi
- Nevada's Transportation Authority approved Tesla for just 10 fully autonomous vehicles in Las Vegas, capped at a limited geofence, 45 mph roads, no airport pickups — versus the 5,000-vehicle, county-wide permit Tesla requested. Zoox, by contrast, was approved for up to 100 vehicles and is already charging for rides on the Strip; Waymo and Uber's Aviari applications remain pending. Electrek estimates Tesla's entire national unsupervised fleet at roughly 20 vehicles.
- An unsupervised Robotaxi drove through plastic bollards during a right turn in Austin over the weekend; no damage or injuries reported, but flagged by multiple sources as a scaling concern given fleet growth plans.
- Cybercab reportedly set to launch without a steering wheel in Austin by end of August, per a report cited by Electrified — employee rides first, public rollout days later; Tesla has reportedly completed emergency-responder training for the vehicle.
- Safety data through mid-July shows zero at-fault Robotaxi incidents in June and (partial) July; Tesla has logged only two at-fault incidents total since February, both described as minor.
- Austin wait times reportedly improved to single-digit minutes at random times per one rider account; a new "edit trip" feature allows multi-stop rides without re-hailing.
- Waymo received CPUC approval to expand across the Bay Area, LA, Sacramento and San Diego.

## Autonomous Driving
- Electrek's Fred Lambert reports his Tesla on FSD v14.3.6 activated a turn signal and pulled toward the shoulder to take an exit already passed, decelerating from 110 to 102 km/h before he disengaged — on a clear, low-traffic Quebec highway, conditions he calls ideal for the system. He argues FSD's high reliability breeds dangerous complacency and calls the unsupervised-driving promise gap "probably the worst move Tesla has ever done" (his characterization).
- Tesla reported a record 207 Autopilot/FSD crashes to NHTSA in a single month, per Electrek; narrative details were redacted on 99.9% of 3,763 driver-assist crash reports filed since 2019.
- Tesla's v14.3.6 release notes cite an upgraded reinforcement-learning training stage, an MLIR-rewritten AI compiler for 20% faster reaction time, and improved handling of system degradations to reduce unnecessary disengagements.
- FSD adoption rate reportedly hit 55%, above Morgan Stanley analyst Andrew Percoco's expectations, who called it a bright spot for the robotaxi thesis.
- Tesla's supervised FSD is active in 12–13 countries versus Waymo's three (US, UK, Japan); an EU regulatory vote (TCMV) expected in October could widen European rollout.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Elon Musk economically owns about 48% of SpaceX but controls over 80% of voting power via dual-class shares (Class A: 1 vote, Class B: 10 votes); five of SpaceX's nine board seats are elected solely by Class B holders, giving Musk board control even through dilution scenarios tied to a possible Tesla acquisition, per Electrified's analysis.
- Argus upgraded SpaceX to buy with a $160 price target; Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas reiterated a $300 base/$600 bull case, citing Cursor's growth (estimated $8B ARR by year end) as a bridge to the bull case.
- Norway's sovereign wealth fund disclosed a $1.2B SpaceX stake (~0.5%); board member Antonio Gracias disclosed a 6.5% stake.
- Deutsche Bank sees SpaceX's push toward a $100B annual revenue run rate by year end as achievable, with AI/cloud compute deals (Anthropic, Google) estimated at $45–50B.
- SPCX closed at $146.23, up 4.45% on the day, versus a 0.17% decline in the Nasdaq-100.

## Humanoid Bots
- Cortex 2, Tesla's Optimus/FSD training data center at Giga Texas, is nearing completion and will use ~260 Megapacks once fully built; it was exempted from a new Texas data-center construction pause due to its near-complete status.
- A new four-floor robot-production building (~3,000 ft long, 1,000–1,100 ft wide) is under rapid steel construction adjacent to Cortex 2.
- Hyundai's Atlas robot handed off the game ball at a World Cup match, a mix of pre-trained sequences and teleoperation per hosts assessing the demo; Atlas is described as far less complex than Optimus, Figure's robot, or 1X's Neo.
