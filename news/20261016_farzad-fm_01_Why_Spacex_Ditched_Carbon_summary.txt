This video explains why SpaceX abandoned an early plan to build Starship from carbon fiber and switched to stainless steel around 2018–2019, framing the decision around cryogenic performance, heat tolerance, cost, and manufacturing speed.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Carbon fiber was the original plan:** Starship was initially designed to use carbon fiber, and SpaceX even built a carbon fiber tank with a 12-meter diameter before abandoning the approach in 2018–2019 in favor of stainless steel.
2. **Cryogenic and heat performance favor steel:** Many metals become brittle at the extremely cold cryogenic temperatures needed to store Starship's fuel, but stainless steel gets stronger when cooled. On re-entry, carbon fiber's resin bonding begins breaking down at temperatures of only a few hundred degrees, while steel melts only around 1400°C, giving a much larger safety margin and letting SpaceX reduce heat shielding in less-exposed areas.
3. **Cost and manufacturing speed drove the switch:** The video cites Elon Musk's estimate that carbon fiber costs roughly $135 per kilogram versus about $3 per kilogram for steel, and notes steel can be welded and fixed quickly in a tent in South Texas, whereas carbon fiber requires large furnaces and careful, time-consuming layup. Musk reportedly argues that steel's weight penalty is largely offset once reduced thermal protection needs are factored in.
4. **Iteration speed as the core rationale:** The video's own framing — building, launching, learning from failures, and building the next iteration quickly — is presented as the main reason for the material choice: a rocket that can be manufactured fast enables faster learning, which it argues matters more than using the most advanced material.
