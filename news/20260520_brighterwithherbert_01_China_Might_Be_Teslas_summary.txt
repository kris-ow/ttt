This video from brighterwithherbert features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur behind pioneerlands.org, discussing the current state of the race to self-driving and robotaxi deployment. The episode covers new Tesla robotaxi sightings in Chicago, Elon Musk's long-term autonomous driving predictions, the Wave AI CEO's market outlook, a Chinese automaker's candid assessment of FSD as the industry benchmark, and Tesla's aggressive hiring push in China suggesting an imminent FSD rollout there.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Acknowledged as Industry Benchmark by Chinese Automaker:** The head of autonomous driving at Chinese automaker Li Auto stated in a recent interview that Tesla's FSD remains the industry benchmark, praising its safety and capabilities. He criticized the frequent claims from Chinese competitors that they have already surpassed Tesla, calling the gap "still enormous." He argued the only reason such claims go unchallenged is that FSD has not yet fully entered the Chinese market — and once it does, those competitors "will be completely exposed." Li Auto's own autonomous driving team is described as still aiming to reach Tesla's level.

2. **FSD Version 14.3 Nag Reduction and Near-Unsupervised Behavior:** A recent FSD update (described as version 14.3, going wide) has reportedly reduced or effectively eliminated the driver attention nag. Tester Jeff reportedly could not get the nag to trigger, signaling the system is approaching unsupervised behavior in practice even if not yet formally announced as such. Additionally, a parking garage looping bug — where the vehicle would circle indefinitely when blocked — was reportedly resolved in version 14.3.3, with tester Omar confirming the car successfully rerouted rather than looping.

3. **Elon Musk's 10-Year Autonomous Driving Prediction:** In a recent interview, Elon Musk stated confidently that 10 years from now, probably 90% of all distance driven will be driven by AI in a self-driving car, making human driving a niche activity. Jo Bhakdi assessed this as plausible for the US, Europe, Australia, and Canada, though perhaps overstated on a global scale given countries like India. Both host and guest emphasized that technological S-curves are non-linear — once 10,000 robotaxis exist, millions and tens of millions could follow in short order.

4. **Wave AI CEO's Market Assessment:** Alex Kendall, CEO of Wave AI, stated that of 100 million cars produced every year globally, only 15% have advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), and there are fewer than 10,000 robotaxis on the roads today. He predicted that in five years, any automaker selling a car without self-driving technology will see demand "fall off a cliff." He noted luxury manufacturers are already integrating the necessary compute hardware (Nvidia, Qualcomm GPUs with surround sensing), and cited Nissan as announcing plans to bring this technology to vehicles from financial year 2027.

5. **Tesla China FSD Hiring Push:** Tesla China is actively recruiting autonomous driving test technicians in nine cities — including Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen, Wuhan, Chengdu, and Chongqing — with the latter cities described as having especially complex road conditions. Tesla is also urgently seeking data labelers in Shanghai to label data from its China fleet, as well as Optimus data labelers. Additionally, Tesla is urgently seeking three FSD-related testing and validation experts, including an ADAS proving ground engineer, an Autopilot test engineer, and an ADAS test operator. Jo Bhakdi interpreted this activity as consistent with a Q2 or Q3 2026 FSD approval window in China, and noted Tesla's CFO had previously expressed hope for Q3 approval.

6. **Jo Bhakdi's Competitive Assessment of Chinese AV Companies:** Jo Bhakdi argued that no Chinese competitor is close to Tesla on the underlying resource metrics — compute, data miles, time, and team size. He estimated that even the best Chinese competitors have at most roughly 1% of Tesla's hard resources, and that even a 10x efficiency advantage would only bring them to ~10% parity. He identified Xpeng as the one Chinese company most resembling a "Tesla type," noting their strategy of offering their FSD equivalent for free to maximize data collection, and their work across humanoid robots and flying cars. He said the rest of the field is not competitive.

## Robotaxi

1. **Chicago Robotaxi Sightings — New City on the Map:** For the first time, a fleet of unbadged Model Y robotaxis was spotted in Chicago, specifically in the Avondale area, which is described as downtown Chicago's home base since late March. The vehicles feature a rear camera exclusive to robotaxi vehicles. Two Cybercabs were also spotted in Chicago, focused on interstate miles, downtown streets, and side streets, with the tailgate housing an extra enclosure widely assumed to contain a Starlink unit. A fleet of robotaxis — including ultra red and black variants — was also seen staged in a Chicago parking lot, suggesting active city-level preparation.

2. **Fleet-on-the-Ground as Leading Indicator:** Jo Bhakdi argued that the physical hardware deployed across cities is a more reliable leading indicator of robotaxi progress than the RobotaxiTracker count of commercially operating unsupervised vehicles. He described the relationship as potentially "L-shaped" — where supervised/validation vehicles accumulate quietly and then commercial unsupervised operation launches suddenly. He noted Tesla is hiring in approximately 38 cities and spotting Cybercabs in many of them, suggesting a broad simultaneous rollout is being prepared rather than a slow city-by-city expansion.

3. **Infrastructure Buildout — Service Facilities Across Cities:** Herbert noted that Tesla has been acquiring large facilities in multiple cities for cleaning, servicing, and charging robotaxi fleets. A 36,000 sq ft facility was previously reported in Las Vegas. Additional facilities are being established in Austin and Dallas. These are in addition to Tesla's existing service center network. Herbert noted he intends to calculate fleet capacity implied by the number of chargers (referencing approximately 270 chargers) at these facilities.

4. **Cybercab Production Ramp and Fleet Size Projections:** Jo Bhakdi stated that Tesla is already producing hundreds of Cybercabs per month and predicted the run rate will hit thousands per month within approximately the next three months. He projected a minimum of 10,000 Cybercabs produced by year-end 2026, with the likely range for commercially operating robotaxis at 10,000–20,000 vehicles by end of 2026. Herbert's own projection is 15,000 robotaxis on the road by year-end, distributed across 30+ cities, with many cities reaching approximately 1,000 cars each.

5. **Robotaxi Safety Data from NHTSA:** Herbert referenced the latest safety data from NHTSA covering Tesla robotaxi incidents since June (implied June 2025). There have been 17 total incidents reported. Of those, seven were determined to be Tesla's fault, with several attributed to curb rashes and a couple to human teleoperator error. Herbert described this as a strong safety data point suggesting the safety dimension has largely been resolved.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla EV Sales Momentum and Competitive Positioning:** Jo Bhakdi expressed strong bullishness on Tesla vehicle sales in the near term, citing FSD improvement, persistent oil price uncertainty damaging consumer psychology (especially in Europe), and what he described as a resolution of the political headwinds around Elon Musk. He noted Tesla sales are already recovering and setting new records in multiple markets. He described Tesla as the most profitable EV company in the world by an enormous margin, and placed Tesla alongside Toyota as one of the top two most profitable car companies globally.

2. **Competitor Implosion Thesis:** Jo Bhakdi argued that traditional automakers are losing money on EVs and cannot sustain losses for much longer, predicting that 2027 will be a "year of reckoning" where multiple competitors go out of business. He characterized legacy automakers' claimed self-driving capabilities (e.g., Mercedes Level 3) as severely restricted and not comparable to Tesla's FSD. His view is that Tesla's manufacturing efficiency — concentrating volume on a small number of models — creates an insurmountable cost and margin advantage that competitors with 200 models and minimal scale simply cannot match.

## Market & Competition

1. **Global AV Market Still in Early Innings:** The Wave AI CEO's data framed the current market as nascent: only 15% of new cars globally have any form of ADAS, and fewer than 10,000 robotaxis exist worldwide. Jo Bhakdi and Herbert both emphasized that this near-zero base makes the coming transformation appear slow but will likely accelerate non-linearly once the technology and regulatory infrastructure matures, similar to the rapid displacement of horse-drawn carriages by automobiles in New York between roughly 1900 and 1911.
