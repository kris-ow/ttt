## Robotaxi
1. **Unsupervised rides expand to Miami:** The host reports unsupervised Robotaxi rides launching in Miami, with a community member reportedly taking a ride there over the July 4th weekend; unsupervised operation is also noted continuing in Dallas, Houston, and Austin, alongside more Cybercab sightings nationally, including a reported sighting in Colorado Springs.
2. **Speculation on a Giga Texas announcement:** The host says he expects a Cybercab-related announcement at Giga Texas, based on a secondhand account of comments from Tesla VP Lars Moravy relayed to another commentator ("Herbert"); the host speculates it could relate to a "cyber SUV" concept but stresses this is unconfirmed ("That's not guaranteed. I don't know that").

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Fatal crash tied to driver override, not FSD:** Vehicle data in the Michael Butler crash (in which a Tesla hit a house, causing a fatality) reportedly shows the car was in FSD approaching a stop sign and slowing down when the driver pressed the accelerator, escalating to 100%, with the brake never applied; officers reportedly found search history showing the driver had searched for FSD being "not aggressive enough" and "too timid," which the host argues undermines any claim that FSD caused the crash.
2. **Y L (compact/next-gen vehicle) delayed:** In Q&A, the host and a viewer discuss "YL" (a next Tesla vehicle project), with the host saying he doesn't expect it before September and has not seen first delivery dates.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Q2 deliveries beat estimates:** Tesla reported deliveries of 480,000 for the quarter, beating Wall Street's expectation of 406,000 and the host's own projection of ~431,000; among trackers cited, James Stephenson was closest, followed by Randy Kirk (~490,000) and Troy Teslike (~460,000).
2. **UK EV market and Tesla growth:** The UK EV market reportedly surged 30%, with Tesla UK sales up 42% year over year, attributed to product strength rather than incentives.
3. **California's new EV rebate — Tesla mostly qualifies:** California is introducing a $3,500 rebate (funded partly by a $135 million state pool) for new EV buyers, requiring manufacturer price-matching and first-time EV buyer status; a cap on vehicle price applies but is higher for California-headquartered automakers (benefiting Rivian and Lucid), while Tesla, no longer HQ'd in California, faces the standard cap — though the host notes roughly half of Tesla's lineup still qualifies under it.
4. **BYD sustains overseas momentum despite domestic softness:** BYD sold 160,000 vehicles (including hybrids/PHEVs) overseas in May, its highest overseas monthly total ever, even as China domestic NEV sales have been soft industry-wide; BYD plans to showcase eight new vehicles across three brands at the Goodwill Festival of Speed in July, including the Denza Z Roadster (1,582 hp), launching in the UK on July 9.
5. **EV battery longevity better than expected:** Citing Stanford researchers, EV batteries held up roughly 40% longer than earlier lab-test predictions under real-world conditions; average EVs are expected to last 12–15 years of active service, comparable to combustion vehicles.
6. **Polestar 4 discounted amid US sales ban:** Due to a ban on Polestar 4 sales in the US, dealers are discounting inventory to around $31,400 (roughly half price), which the host calls a "heck of a bargain" despite the car lacking self-driving features; the host speculates the ban stems from software/national-security sourcing issues rather than the vehicle itself.

## Market & Competition
1. **Michigan startup building Tiny/TE electric truck:** Schwab Industries has signed on to build bodies-in-white for an electric truck targeting ~42,000 units in production this year, with a split battery pack supporting 400V or 800V charging; positioned as more capable but pricier than the "Slate," while Ford targets 30,000 units for its own entry-level electric truck next year.
2. **EU combustion ban pressure easing:** Per the EU climate chief, rising EV sales are reducing political pressure to roll back combustion engine phase-out plans, with some suggestion of a shift toward a ~90% emissions reduction target rather than a full ban, as more EV models now cover more market segments.
3. **Chinese automakers expanding manufacturing overseas:** Chery has taken over a former Nissan plant, creating roughly 3,000 jobs in Southeast Africa, part of a broader Chinese push to build (not just sell) cars abroad; separately, China's EV charging network has grown to 22.5 million charging points, described as the world's largest, with China's NEV fleet at 44 million and 12.9 million new registrations in the prior year.
4. **Sodium-ion battery materials use coconut-derived carbon, not burned coal:** A story framed around China's sodium-ion battery supply chain clarifies that "coal" references relate to biomass/hard-carbon lattice structures (including coconut-shell-derived precursors) used in electrode manufacturing, not coal combustion.
