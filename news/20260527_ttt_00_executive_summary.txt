## Autonomous Driving

- **Lithuania is the second EU country to activate FSD Supervised**, using the Dutch mutual recognition framework to complete its own approval in under 40 days — versus the 18+ months the Dutch Vehicle Authority spent evaluating 1.12 million miles of European driving data. Tesla had pre-positioned for this: FSD ride-alongs at its Vilnius showroom began in late April, and the company had been hiring FSD vehicle operators in Lithuania and eight other European countries simultaneously.

- **Greece is formally in queue as the third EU country.** The Greek Secretary General of Transport has publicly stated the country will adopt FSD Supervised via the same Dutch mutual recognition framework as part of an upcoming transportation bill. A clear country-by-country momentum pattern is now visible: mutual recognition eliminates the need for country-level ground-up evaluation, compressing future approval timelines dramatically.

- **Combined FSD mileage in the Netherlands and Lithuania crossed 12.5 million miles (20 million km)**, reaching that figure less than three days after the same markets crossed 9.3 million miles — implying roughly 3 million miles added in under 72 hours. Dutch owners alone logged 6.2 million miles in under one month post-launch. Tesla reports FSD Supervised reduces collision probability by up to 7x per kilometer versus manual driving.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Tesla Roadster is confirmed for Giga Texas production.** Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Morvi stated on the Ride the Lightning podcast: "We can say it's going to be built in Texas. We've made first plans on that and you start to see a lot of things start to unfold in the next months." The allocation of production space implies tooling, engineering validation, and factory planning are actively converging. Giga Texas now houses Cybercab, Optimus, Terafab, and the Roadster — Tesla's most ambitious programs in a single location.

- **Roadster's competitive context has changed since its 2017 reveal.** Original specs — 0–60 in 1.9 seconds, 620+ miles range, 250+ mph top speed — were then unprecedented; today, Tesla's own Model S Plaid and rivals including Lucid, Rimac, and Xiaomi's SU7 Ultra match or approach those figures. Elon Musk has framed a far more extreme vehicle than originally planned, including a cold-gas thruster SpaceX package and a sub-1-second 0–60 claim. Per his Q1 2026 earnings call: "Long-term, the only manually driven car will be the new Tesla Roadster" — positioning it as the last great driver's car before full autonomy.

- **Volvo will give EV drivers access to 20,000+ Tesla Superchargers across 29 European countries starting Q4 2026**, accessible directly through the Volvo Cars app. This adds another major OEM to Tesla's European NACS/Supercharger partner list, reinforcing the network as a recurring revenue and competitive moat asset.

- **Tesla opened its first Supercharger in Colombia**, extending the network into a new market.

## Energy

- **A $60 million Tesla Megapack installation came online in New Zealand**, capable of powering the equivalent of approximately 44,000 homes for up to two hours. The system stores renewable energy during low-demand periods and dispatches during peak demand — a textbook grid services deployment confirming continued international Megapack rollout.

## Market & Competition

- **SpaceX filed its S-1 on May 20 and is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq (ticker: SPCX) on June 12**, targeting a valuation of up to $1.75 trillion and raising approximately $75 billion in what would be the largest IPO in history. SpaceX reported $18.7 billion in revenue and a $4.9 billion net loss in its IPO filing. Tesla's current market cap sits around $1.6 trillion; a combined entity is projected to exceed $3 trillion.

- **Electrek's Fred Lambert frames a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger as Musk's fourth billion-dollar self-dealing transaction**, cataloging SolarCity ($2.6B, 2016), Twitter ($44B, 2022), and Tesla's $2B xAI investment (January 2026) as precedents. Musk controls ~20% of Tesla equity but 85.1% of SpaceX voting power through a super-voting share class. Wedbush's Dan Ives puts merger odds at "80–90 percent" by early 2027; Kalshi prediction markets assign 33% before May 2027. Tesla shareholders are already suing Musk for breach of fiduciary duty over the xAI investment. Lambert is skeptical of the pro-merger thesis.

- **Grok V9 medium (1.5 trillion parameters) has completed pre-training** — three times the size of the current V8 small model serving all production traffic. Cursor coding data was incorporated in supplementary training; reinforcement learning is starting imminently; public release is targeted mid-to-late June 2026. xAI is currently marked at approximately $200 billion in private rounds. Tesla holds a board-approved equity position in xAI, meaning any upward remarking moves Tesla's balance sheet line — real exposure, but bounded.

- **Jo Bhakdi's central thesis on xAI's Cursor option:** The $10 billion call option on Cursor (exercisable within 60 days of Cursor's IPO, estimated by August 2026, at a $60 billion acquisition price) matters not because of Cursor's LLM — it has none — but because Cursor's scaffolding layer is, per benchmark evidence, the most advanced in the world. Bhakdi cites Cursor achieving near-parity with top OpenAI and Anthropic models while using a lower-tier base model as proof. He also notes Anthropic is currently paying $1.5 billion per month to SpaceX for compute on Colossus, with a ~60-day cancellation window — creating optionality for SpaceX to terminate the deal if its own AI product matures.

- **Starlink continues geographic and OEM expansion:** American Airlines (announced across 500+ narrowbody aircraft, installations beginning Q1 2027) brings Starlink's commercial airline partners to 38 airlines and 6,300+ aircraft under contract or installed. Starlink is now live in 130+ countries. A $2.29 billion U.S. Space Force contract for a Starshield satellite communications backbone was also reported. Elon's long-term target is 100,000+ satellites across V3/V4/V5 generations, a ~10x buildout from the current ~10,000-satellite operational fleet.
