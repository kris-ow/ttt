## Autonomous Driving
- **Lithuania becomes second EU country for FSD Supervised.** Using the EU mutual recognition framework, Lithuania completed its own approval in under 40 days by leveraging the Netherlands' 18-month, 1.12-million-mile evaluation — no restart required. Tesla had quietly staged the rollout by opening FSD ride-alongs at its Vilnius showroom in late April and pre-hiring FSD vehicle operators across nine European countries.
- **Greece is next in the mutual recognition queue.** The Greek Secretary General of Transport publicly stated Greece intends to adopt FSD Supervised via the same framework as part of an upcoming transportation bill, having narrowly missed being second behind Lithuania.
- **Combined Netherlands + Lithuania FSD mileage hit 12.5 million miles (20M km)**, with roughly 3 million miles added in under 72 hours — Dutch owners alone logged 6.2 million miles in under one month post-launch. Tesla reports FSD Supervised reduces collision probability by up to 7x per km versus manual driving.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Roadster production confirmed at Giga Texas.** Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Morvi and chief designer Franz von Holleshausen told the Ride the Lightning podcast: "We can say it's going to be built in Texas. We've made first plans on that and you start to see a lot of things start to unfold in the next months." The production allocation signals tooling and factory planning are now active, not conceptual.
- **Roadster's strategic identity has shifted.** Elon stated at Q1 2026 earnings: "Long-term, the only manually driven car will be the new Tesla Roadster" — reframing it as the last great driver's car before full autonomy, not merely a performance halo. Original 2017 specs (1.9s 0–60, 620+ mile range, 250+ mph) no longer differentiate in 2026; the production version is expected to be substantially more extreme, including a potential SpaceX cold-gas thruster package.

## Market & Competition
- **SpaceX IPO filed May 20; expected to begin trading June 12 under SPCX**, targeting a valuation of up to $1.75 trillion and raising approximately $75 billion — described as the largest IPO in history. SpaceX reported $18.7 billion in revenue and a $4.9 billion net loss ahead of the offering.
- **Tesla/SpaceX merger debate active.** Wedbush's Dan Ives puts merger odds at "80–90%" by early 2027; Kalshi prediction markets assign only 33% before May 2027. Electrek's Fred Lambert frames the potential deal as the fourth in a pattern of self-dealing transactions — following SolarCity ($2.6B), Twitter ($44B), and the Tesla xAI investment ($2B disclosed January 2026) — and argues Musk's 85.1% SpaceX voting control vs. ~20% Tesla equity creates structural conflict. Tesla shareholders are already suing over the xAI investment.
- **Grok V9 medium (1.5T parameters) pre-training complete**, triple the size of the current V8 small (0.5T) model serving all production traffic. Cursor coding data incorporated in supplementary training; reinforcement learning beginning imminently; public release targeted mid-to-late June 2026. Tesla holds a board-approved equity stake in xAI, currently marked around $200 billion in private rounds — any upward remarking moves the Tesla balance sheet.
- **Jo Bhakdi's "scaffolding" thesis:** The strategic differentiator in AI is no longer the base LLM but the prompt architecture and agent orchestration layer around it. Bhakdi argues Cursor — already embedded across the Fortune 500 — holds the world's best scaffolding, demonstrated by Cursor using a second-tier model achieving near-parity with top Claude and ChatGPT outputs. The proposed SpaceX option ($10B call option, $60B acquisition price, exercisable ~60 days post-Cursor IPO, deadline ~August 2026) is framed as Elon acquiring what xAI cannot build internally.
