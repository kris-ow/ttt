This video features futurist Brian Wang walking through a detailed comparison of live AI compute capacity and buildout plans across SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic, arguing that SpaceX currently leads in actually-energized compute and is best positioned to reach massive scale by owning its own power, land, chips, and customer (Grok/xAI) rather than renting capacity or depending solely on LLM revenue.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX currently has the most live compute:** Per the host's opening framing, OpenAI has 421 megawatts live, Anthropic has 910 megawatts live, and SpaceX has 1,286 megawatts live — more than either rival — with Tesla's Cortex compute counted separately. Wang's own slides put current confirmed/likely totals slightly differently (Anthropic ~1.4GW, OpenAI ~1.7GW, SpaceX ~1.3GW), reflecting the uncertainty in what counts as confirmed versus rented/shared capacity.
2. **Colossus complex and chip counts:** SpaceX's Colossus 1 has roughly 300 megawatts and was built in about 122 days. The broader Colossus 2 complex (which Wang says includes three buildings — referred to as Macrohard, "Macroharder," and a newer "Mini Hard" building) is targeted at roughly 990,000 GPUs total (550,000 + 220,000 + 220,000) by Wang's estimate, with third-party research firm Epic AI suggesting a more conservative ~770,000 chips by Q1. Wang treats semi analysis's numbers as more reliable than Epic AI's given semi analysis's larger tracking team.
3. **Anthropic pays SpaceX roughly $1.25B/month:** The host states Anthropic writes SpaceX a check for about $1.25 billion a month for compute, likely continuing through May 2029, and that analysts estimate SpaceX can charge $30–50 million per megawatt per year for fast-turnaround capacity because few competitors can deliver in three months.
4. **Path to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027:** Semi analysis's model (as relayed by Wang) projects SpaceX scaling from just under 2 gigawatts now to roughly 10 gigawatts by the end of next year, with the build rate accelerating through 2027 — from ~100–200 megawatts/month in Q1 to over a gigawatt/month by Q3/Q4. Reaching this requires scaling chip installation roughly 10x, to about 500,000 chips per month.
5. **SpaceX's structural advantages — power, land, and vertical integration:** Wang says semi analysis identified 7–20 properties suitable for gigawatt-scale data centers and 7–20 gigawatts of available turbine/power capacity tied to SpaceX/xAI/Elon Musk corporate relationships, including a roughly $3 billion partnership with Caterpillar for mobile solar turbines and a company (APR Energy) that converts retired airplane engines into natural-gas turbines. He also describes a data-center build straddling Mississippi and Tennessee state lines to route around single-state power-generation permitting limits, and notes Tesla's China supply relationships as an added sourcing advantage. Wang frames the land/power acquisitions as a deliberate land-grab strategy — locking up cheap options now to block competitors before full buildout.
6. **Grok and vertical monetization as SpaceX's edge:** Wang argues that unlike OpenAI and Anthropic — which rely on LLM revenue that is becoming commoditized — SpaceX/xAI monetizes compute directly and is "its own customer" much like Starlink, giving it more financing leverage. He cites Grok (reportedly now top-three among frontier models, with a further version expected in the coming weeks that Elon Musk has said will be top-tier) and a coding-agent product built by Cursor as additional revenue angles, plus potential future upside from Optimus. Wang states SpaceX's profitability from launch, communications, and compute leasing gives it "staying power" that unprofitable competitors (per a Wall Street Journal report cited regarding OpenAI) may lack if they fall from the top AI ranking.

## Market & Competition
1. **OpenAI's build is heavily dependent on partners and has slipped before:** Wang says OpenAI relies primarily on Microsoft Azure, Coreweave, and a slower-than-announced Oracle-linked Stargate project (~420 megawatts built so far versus the originally touted 10-gigawatt ambition), plus newer, largely unstarted deals with SoftBank and Nvidia financing. He notes the OpenAI–Microsoft relationship has visibly frayed, adding uncertainty to OpenAI's compute and revenue plans; the host separately relays an unconfirmed report of OpenAI cutting its expected revenue run-rate.
2. **Anthropic is compute-constrained despite strong revenue growth:** The host states Anthropic's run rate has reached over $60 billion and is targeting $100 billion, but that compute shortages are throttling model capability and usage limits; Anthropic is pursuing an IPO some believe could value it near $2 trillion, potentially raising around $80 billion.
3. **Competitive dynamics favor whoever isn't dependent purely on being "smartest":** Wang argues that OpenAI and Anthropic need to remain a clear or tied number one to survive, since they lack diversified profit sources — while SpaceX/xAI can remain competitive even without leading, thanks to profitable adjacent businesses (launch, Starlink) and compute-leasing income.
