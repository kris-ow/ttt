This Tesla Beat Podcast episode (#150) recaps a news-heavy week: record-breaking Q2 delivery numbers that beat Wall Street estimates and sent the stock down anyway, the US launch of the Model Y L, early Cybercab engineering testing in Austin, FSD's "V14 Lite" rollout to Hardware 3 vehicles, an Optimus production update from Elon Musk at the Fremont factory, and robotaxi network expansion to Miami. Hosts frame the quarter and product cadence as validating Tesla's ongoing thesis despite lingering market skepticism.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y L launches in the US at $61,990:** The six-seat, three-row Model Y L launched in the US around the Fourth of July with a "launch series" including a year of FSD supervised, a year of Supercharging, a year of premium connectivity, and free choice of paint/interior/wheels. Hosts highlighted a usable third row (325 miles of range, vehicle-to-load capability, power thigh extenders, 18-speaker audio) and argued the extra 3-inch wheelbase makes second/third-row comfort better than the Model X's six-seat configuration. Hosts speculated (unconfirmed) that cheaper all-wheel-drive (~$53–55K) and rear-wheel-drive (~$49K) trims could follow in coming months, and that added manufacturing cost for the longer wheelbase is roughly $2,000–$2,500.
2. **Q2 deliveries beat estimates sharply:** Tesla delivered approximately 480,000 vehicles and produced approximately 452,000 in Q2, versus a Wall Street consensus around 406,000 — a record Q2 despite the stock falling roughly 8% afterward. Hosts attributed the beat to inventory restocking after a Q1 drawdown, rising gas prices, and strong performance in the US, China (with growing exports), and parts of Europe (Germany described as quadrupling sales; France and Sweden also cited as strong, though specific figures were not given).
3. **Model Y L reportedly being produced only at Giga Texas so far:** Hosts said they'd heard no confirmation of Fremont producing the Y L, inferring Texas has spare capacity while Fremont is expected to be reserved for other production (including robots), though this allocation was presented as inference rather than confirmed fact.
4. **Giga Berlin ramping to 7,500 units/week:** Giga Berlin, previously at ~5,000/week and recently raised to 6,200/week, will reportedly increase to 7,500/week by around October, adding roughly another thousand jobs.
5. **Semi incentives could cut effective price sharply:** Citing Grok, hosts said the Tesla Semi price is around $260,000+, with California incentives (clean truck/bus voucher plus clean fuel rewards) potentially totaling around $240,000 in rebates, making the effective cost dramatically lower; California semi operation was cited as already ~50% cheaper than diesel trucks.
6. **Cybertruck dry-cathode 4680 cell revealed:** Hosts noted Tesla confirmed the all-wheel-drive dual-motor Cybertruck uses a dry-cathode 4680 cell accessible via a small "smugglers bay" behind a handful of bolts, sparking discussion of potential aftermarket parts.
7. **Tesla launches in Uruguay:** Tesla vehicles are now available for purchase in Uruguay, a small market already seeing notable EV adoption (mostly Chinese-made EVs); hosts expect only low single-digit-thousands in annual sales but framed it positively as market expansion.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab engineering testing begins in Austin:** Tesla has begun engineering tests of the first production Cybercab in Austin and published an extensive first-responders guide detailing vehicle structure and voltage architecture, similar to guides previously issued for the Model Y robotaxi.
2. **Robotaxi network expands to Miami and Houston:** Tesla added Houston and Miami to its robotaxi network, short of the five-to-six new markets by June 30 that hosts said was projected on a Q4 earnings call; more city expansions are hoped for by year-end. Hosts said they personally care more about fleet density in existing cities than new city counts, citing expanded Cybercab testing in Seattle, Bellevue, Kirkland, and Redmond.
3. **Active Robotaxi fleet: 752 (Bay Area 620, Austin 105, Dallas 16, Houston 11) per RobotaxiTracker.com; Unsupervised subset: 39 as of 2026-05-09 (stale), Austin 28, Dallas 5, Houston 6.**
4. **Waymo drops Uber partnership in Phoenix:** Waymo robotaxis are no longer available via the Uber app in Phoenix, ending a nearly three-year partnership; hosts framed this as Waymo no longer needing Uber's markup and pushing riders to Waymo's own app.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD "V14 Lite" rolling out to Hardware 3 vehicles:** A lighter FSD version is being released to AI3 (Hardware 3) vehicles to help close the gap with AI4 performance, though it reportedly lacks Mad Max mode, updated visuals, parking visual squares, a bigger neural network, and FSD "streaks," and the app itself wasn't updated. One host who tested Hardware 3 pre-update called it "the best system in North America... apart from hardware 4" and said this release "is the worst it will ever be" and will improve over time.
2. **European FSD/regulatory expansion framed as major growth lever:** Hosts said FSD supervised is now approved in roughly five (possibly more) European countries, including the Netherlands, Estonia, Lithuania, Denmark, and possibly Belgium, and argued regulatory catch-up elsewhere in Europe has left competitors' driver-assist systems on par with where Tesla was several years ago, making data collection Europe's key current bottleneck.
3. **Facial recognition patent speculation:** A Tesla patent related to facial recognition sparked speculation about linking FSD to individual driver profiles (rather than the vehicle) for purposes such as anti-theft, driver check-ins, or preventing unauthorized use; one host said he'd "talked to somebody" suggesting Tesla may move FSD access to a profile-based rather than vehicle-based model, though this was described as secondhand and unconfirmed. Hosts debated whether this could mean per-driver FSD charges, which one host strongly opposed.
4. **Rebuttal of critic's account of a Tesla incident:** A commentator (not among the tracked interview list) publicly disputed a critic's (Dan O'Dowd's) account of an incident involving Ross Gerber's vehicle, asserting the claimed details (running a stop sign at 37 mph) were inaccurate based on the actual stop-sign geometry and vehicle behavior.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production update from Fremont:** Elon Musk posted about a walkthrough of the Optimus production line at the Fremont factory with the Fremont general manager, stating Optimus production will be "extremely slow at first" as Tesla builds new manufacturing muscle memory, consistent with typical slow ramps for new products.
2. **Hosts argue Tesla's manufacturing, real-world AI, and actuator expertise give Optimus a durable edge:** Citing points from Musk's and Ashok Elluswamy's discussion at the Elon and Jeff Lutz interview referenced by the hosts, they argued Tesla's existing vehicle manufacturing/supply chain, real-world AI experience, and actuator production capability (shared with vehicle production) will let Optimus scale faster than competitors like Boston Dynamics or Unitree, who they argue lack a comparable "brain." Hosts noted SoftBank sold its roughly 10% Boston Dynamics stake back to Hyundai at a cited market cap valuation of about $3 billion.

## Market & Competition
1. **Contrast drawn between SpaceX's stock performance and Tesla's:** Hosts noted SpaceX's share price is up roughly 8% week-over-week and argued Wall Street undervalues Tesla similarly to how it dismissed SpaceX for years pre-IPO, calling Tesla's stock "flat" over a period they estimated at about five years since 2021.
2. **Seattle robotaxi economics contrasted with Uber:** Hosts described Seattle as the world's most expensive rideshare market due to local regulations guaranteeing Uber drivers a minimum wage (cited as roughly $18–23/hour), which they said has paradoxically reduced rideshare usage and driver earnings; they argued cheap robotaxi rides (e.g., an example of $10 to the airport vs. $120+ via Uber) could shift public sentiment toward Tesla in traditionally anti-Musk markets like Seattle.
