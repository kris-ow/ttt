## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla dominates Canada's China-EV import quota:** Canada's EV import quota for China-made cars (24,500 units allotted for the first of two annual windows) is mostly going to Tesla because it is the only manufacturer with existing ground infrastructure in Canada; unused allotment rolls forward rather than being lost. The hosts note Tesla qualifies as a "Chinese-made" car for this purpose because the vehicles are manufactured at Tesla's China factory, regardless of headquarters location.
2. **Chinese automakers maneuvering to enter Canada:** BYD quietly launched a Canadian "coming soon" website (no lineup, pricing, or ordering info yet, though it lists global crash-test ratings for models including Seal, Sealion 7, Dolphin, Atto 3, Han, and Tang) and is reportedly considering roughly 20 branded dealership locations. Separately, Chery is reportedly aiming to sell cars in Canada as early as late 2026 and has been spotted testing on Canadian roads; Dongfeng displayed six models at an outdoor event in Montreal; Xpeng and Xiaomi are also mentioned as possible future entrants, per the hosts.
3. **Doug Ford pushes back on Stellantis China-EV manufacturing talk:** Ontario Premier Doug Ford reportedly wants Stellantis to bring full manufacturing (workers, parts, supply chain) to its idled Ontario plant rather than "knockdown kit" assembly of a China-based model, arguing Ontario's existing manufacturing base can support real production immediately.
4. **Canadian EV sales rebounding after rebate reinstatement:** A federal EV rebate (described as $5,000) was reinstated in 2026 after sales dropped when a prior credit was removed; per Canada's government EV dashboard, adoption is climbing back, though the referenced sales chart is missing roughly the second half of the year (Q3/Q4), a seasonally strong period. British Columbia leads Canadian EV adoption, followed by Quebec, then Ontario. Separately, Canada is reportedly rolling back a prior EV sales mandate (percentage targets for manufacturers) in favor of rebate-driven, voluntary demand.
5. **EV vs. ICE repair cost gap narrowing:** The gap between EV and ICE repair costs in Canada has reportedly narrowed to a record low as EV parts become more common and more third-party manufacturers enter the parts market, per an article discussed on the show.
6. **Nationwide EV battery recycling network launched:** 31 vehicle manufacturers reportedly joined together to create a uniform, nationwide Canadian program for recovering and recycling EV batteries at end of life (~15 years), standardizing the return process regardless of region.
