This video features Armen Pashnovski from Munro Live interviewing representatives from Eaton (Sai Murhari, Global EVCI Product Manager) and ChargePoint (Erdog, Product Marketing) at ACT Expo 2026 in Las Vegas. The segment focuses on a newly announced joint product — the ChargePoint Express Solo — a 600 kW standalone DC fast charger, and the broader partnership between Eaton's power distribution expertise and ChargePoint's EV charging platform to address EV infrastructure bottlenecks.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **ChargePoint Express Solo — Fastest Standalone Charger on the Market:** The ChargePoint Express Solo is described as the fastest charging standalone solution currently available, capable of delivering up to 600 kW of output at the system level. It can charge two vehicles simultaneously at up to 300 kW per port, and can also function as a cabinet to distribute power to a side dispenser, supporting up to four ports charging simultaneously (averaging 150 kW each). The product was announced just prior to the show and is a brand-new market entry.

2. **Current EV Charging Speed Benchmarks:** The fastest-charging EV currently for sale in North America is cited as the Lucid Gravity, which was observed charging at 400 kW during testing at ChargePoint's facilities. In a theoretical scenario sustaining that power level, it could charge from 20% to 80% in approximately 15 minutes. No production EV currently on the North American market is capable of utilizing the full 600 kW output of the Express Solo.

3. **Cable Theft Prevention — Dual-Layer Security System:** ChargePoint and Eaton have developed a two-level cable theft deterrent system. The first layer consists of cut-resistant cables with embedded high-tensile metal invisible to the user. The second layer is an alarm system called "ChargePoint Protect," which triggers simultaneous alarms and flashing alerts across all chargers at a site when any single cable is cut, and notifies authorities — deterring further theft attempts.

## Energy
1. **Grid Infrastructure Bottleneck:** Eaton's representative emphasized that the existing grid infrastructure is not ready to support high-power charging installations like the 600 kW Express Solo. In approximately 99% of cases, installing such a charger would require a service upgrade, potentially including a new transformer and switchboard. This is a core problem the Eaton–ChargePoint partnership aims to address end-to-end for site owners.

2. **Direct DC Bus Integration with Battery Storage:** A technically distinctive feature of the Express Solo is its internal DC bus architecture, which allows battery storage to be integrated directly into the charger without a separate AC inverter. This reduces capital expenditure, lowers total cost of ownership, and optimizes physical footprint compared to conventional setups where battery storage requires its own inverter stack.

3. **Energy Arbitrage and Battery Storage Trends:** The representatives discussed using co-located battery storage as a practical solution for sites that cannot support 600 kW from the grid alone — charging batteries overnight at low utility rates and discharging during peak demand hours. They noted that in 2025, the U.S. added approximately 19 GW of battery storage capacity, representing roughly 30% growth over the prior year, signaling an accelerating trend toward on-site energy resilience.

4. **Prefabricated Skid Solution for Simplified Deployment:** Eaton is developing a prefabricated, pre-wired skid product that bundles a four-port charging hub, the Express Solo charger, a PowerLink dispenser, and a switchboard into a single deliverable unit. This is designed to eliminate the complexity of piecemeal procurement and installation for site operators. Eaton's Power Distribution Experience Center in Pittsburgh (described as "Disney World for engineers") will serve as a live demo site.

5. **Deployment Timeline:** ChargePoint expects the Express Solo to be available in the field by the end of 2026. One unit is already installed at ChargePoint's headquarters for ongoing testing, and an installation at Eaton's headquarters is planned.
