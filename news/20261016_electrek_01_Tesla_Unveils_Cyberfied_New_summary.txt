Tesla livestreamed an updated reveal of its long-delayed next-generation Roadster, showcasing new "cyberfied" styling and cold-gas thrusters enabling jumps and flips, but still provided no pricing and only a vague 2028 production target from Elon Musk.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Nine-year delay continues:** Tesla first showed the next-gen Roadster prototype in November 2017 with a promised 2020 production date; that slipped repeatedly through 2026 (April, late April, post-Q1-earnings, August, October 1) before this stream finally aired. Musk's only production comment was "2028, actually."

2. **Design and materials:** The car's design evolved from Tesla's S3XY lineup toward the Cybertruck/Cybercab/Optimus aesthetic, with stated inspiration from the SR-71 and F-35. Tesla describes hidden headlights, full-length taillight, flush aero, "Ano White" paint said to borrow from rocket coatings, tinted windows meant to evoke a spacesuit visor, a steer-by-wire yoke, three displays, a fixed seat, manual controls to save weight, and magnesium/titanium/carbon-fiber construction.

3. **Three trims revealed post-stream via X:** Performance (two seats, active aero, "multi-mode suction system," 0-60 mph under 1.6 seconds, available by reservation); Thrust (two seats, rear-mounted cold-gas thrusters, 0-60 mph under 0.9 seconds, limited release); Rocket (one seat, thrusters around the car's perimeter for upward/rotational movement, 0-60 mph under 0.59 seconds, top speed over 230 mph, invite-only, and per Tesla's own Roadster page not approved for public road use). Tesla quotes range over 300 miles across the lineup.

4. **Thruster and suction tech:** Tesla says adapting SpaceX-style cold-gas thrusters to work in atmosphere (rather than vacuum) required new hardware/controls, with carbon-fiber overwrapped pressure vessels (COPVs) packed into unused space to store compressed gas for "flight time." Tesla claims the thrusters produce four to five times the force tires can handle, bypassing the traction limit that caps acceleration in other cars. The suction system reportedly pulls enough air from under the car to hold it upside down while stationary, and in Track Mode lowers the car and deploys active wings driven by the same actuators developed for Optimus, with Tesla claiming downforce "approaching F1 levels."

5. **Reservations reopened, specs reduced from 2017 promise:** Reservations require $5,000 (refundable) today plus $45,000 due within 10 days — the same $50,000 total Tesla charged in 2017. Compared to the original 2017 promise of 620-mile range, 250+ mph top speed, and 2+2 four-seat layout, the new car is now quoted at over 300 miles range, 230+ mph (Rocket trim only), and a maximum of two seats. Acceleration claims, however, exceed the original 1.9-second 0-60 mph promise.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the engineering (atmospheric cold-gas thrusters, ceiling-holding suction fans, Optimus-actuator wings) genuinely impressive and likely a first-of-its-kind combination in one car, but argues the headline stunts apply only to invite-only/limited, non-road-legal trims that will likely void warranties and that Tesla will probably sell in only token numbers for marketing. He criticizes Tesla for roughly halving the originally promised range and cutting seating from four to two after collecting $50,000–$250,000 deposits from customers nine years ago, noting he personally holds two Founders Series Roadster referral awards and has been waiting since.
