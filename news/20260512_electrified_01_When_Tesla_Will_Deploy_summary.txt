This video from Dillon Loomis of the "Electrified" channel centers on the question of when Tesla will deploy the Cybercab onto its paid robotaxi network. Loomis argues that despite production of Cybercabs already underway at Giga Texas, a September 2026 deployment timeline is more realistic than the 1–2 month window many observers are expecting. The video also covers a notable Piper Sandler analyst note projecting Tesla's revenue across 17 business lines through 2045, along with updates on Optimus, FSD regulatory validation, Uber's AV lobbying push, and several Tesla business items.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab Production Underway but Deployment Not Imminent:** Joe Tegtmeyer reported that Cybercab production is proceeding slowly — as Tesla said it would — but that dozens of Cybercabs are now visible in the outbound lot at Giga Texas. Loomis interprets this as validating activity rather than pre-deployment staging, arguing that Tesla needs real-world and simulation miles to validate the new vehicle platform before any paid rides begin.

2. **Predicted Cybercab Robotaxi Launch: ~September 15, 2026:** Loomis's central prediction is that Tesla will deploy the first Cybercab onto the paid robotaxi network around September 15, 2026 — approximately six months from the start of Cybercab production. He bases this on the Cybertruck FSD precedent, where it took roughly a year-plus to fully retrain and validate Tesla's neural network for that new vehicle platform, and notes that the Cybercab presents even more novel engineering challenges.

3. **Why Cybercab Integration Is More Complex Than Cybertruck:** Loomis argues that for the Cybertruck, the primary challenge was not perception but the control stack — specifically, remapping Tesla's mechanically linked steering rack model to the Cybertruck's variable-ratio steer-by-wire and four-wheel steering system. The Cybercab introduces additional novel challenges: it is Tesla's first front-wheel-drive-only vehicle, it has no steering wheel or pedals (requiring the FSD stack to output entirely new control commands), different camera angles, different suspension, and different B-pillars. Unlike the Cybertruck, Cybercab will likely launch unsupervised for paid rides, raising the stakes for validation.

4. **Model Y Continues to Anchor the Robotaxi Fleet:** Loomis notes that Tesla does not need the Cybercab to keep expanding its robotaxi network — Model Y deployments can continue scaling without issue. This removes urgency and allows Tesla to take whatever time is needed to get the Cybercab right before fleet introduction.

5. **FSD V15 as a Potential Gating Factor:** Loomis raises — without asserting — that Elon Musk has indicated in a roundabout way that they are waiting for FSD V15 to really scale, and flags that Tesla might also wait for V15 before deploying the Cybercab. He does not argue this is likely, but includes it as a consideration.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Model Y Passes All NHTSA Advanced Driver Assistance Tests:** Tesla's Model Y became the first — and as of the video, only — vehicle to pass NHTSA's new advanced driver assistance system tests. NHTSA confirmed that Model Y vehicles made on or after November 12, 2025 successfully met criteria for four newly integrated safety tests: pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping assistance, blind spot warning, and blind spot intervention. NHTSA's administrator said the 2026 Model Y "sets a high bar for the industry." Loomis emphasizes this is a significant federal-level validation of Tesla's vision-only system at a moment when congressional debates about LiDAR vs. vision-only are ongoing.

2. **Tesla Hiring Autopilot Test Engineers Across Chinese Cities:** Tesla is hiring for Autopilot test engineer roles in China, with Shanghai listed as the preferred location and additional cities including Shenzhen, Chongqing, and Wuhan also listed. Loomis notes this is the first time Tesla has specified cities and provinces for this role, suggesting active expansion of FSD testing and validation in China.

3. **Elon Musk's China Trip and FSD Approval Prospects:** The White House invited Elon Musk to join President Trump's trip to China. Loomis draws a parallel to Elon's April 2024 surprise Beijing visit, after which Tesla passed a key data privacy assessment and secured a mapping deal with BYD — both steps that enabled Tesla's current limited FSD approval in China. Loomis suggests this trip could advance the broader FSD approval in China.

4. **Uber Launches AV Policy Push:** Uber's director of autonomy announced a new public policy paper advocating for autonomous vehicle deployment across the US, including in challenging markets like Chicago, Boston, and New York City. Uber argues for a hybrid network where AVs and human drivers coexist long-term, while calling out infrastructure (charging/cleaning), mapping/validation, and density as the three main barriers. AV legislation has stalled in at least eight states. Loomis frames Uber's lobbying as a net positive for the broader AV industry and for Tesla.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus V3 Entering Small-Scale Trial Production in May 2026:** Chinese supply chain account Ming — who Loomis says has a credible track record — reported that Optimus V3 has successfully cleared its engineering verification and is entering small-scale trial production in May 2026. Core suppliers are expected to receive their first mass production orders in mid-July, paving the way for a large-scale production ramp in August. Seven key Chinese partners have already passed Tesla's PPA (production part approval) certification.

2. **Piper Sandler: Optimus Still Too Unknown to Model Formally:** Piper Sandler analyst Alex Potter acknowledged that the humanoid robot and "inference as a service" businesses "arguably will be worth more than Tesla's other businesses combined," but has not yet formally attempted to forecast them. Loomis notes that Optimus remains too uncertain even for firms like ARK Invest to model seriously. Potter's current base case assigns zero value to Optimus, while arguing that at ~$400/share, investors are effectively getting the Optimus business for free.

## Financials

1. **Piper Sandler 17-Business-Line Tesla Model:** Analyst Alex Potter published a new Tesla model covering 17 product lines, yielding a price target of approximately $400 per share without assigning any value to Optimus. The model includes previously overlooked business lines such as in-house insurance, Supercharging, and a standalone robotaxi evaluation separate from the FSD software business. Potter's near-term estimates for 2026 and 2027 are below consensus, reflecting declining deliveries from discontinued products and lower regulatory credit contributions, but he argues traditional metrics are becoming less relevant as FSD subscriber counts and robotaxi metrics take center stage.

2. **Piper Sandler Long-Range Revenue Projections (Selected Figures):**
   - FSD subscriptions: ~$600 million in 2026, growing to $10.6 billion by 2030
   - FSD licensing: $0 through 2031, then ramping to $400 billion by 2045
   - Robotaxi rides: $400 million in 2027, $2.2 billion in 2028, $18.6 billion by 2030
   - Energy revenue: $13.1 billion in 2026, growing to ~$20.9 billion by 2030 (Loomis believes this is too conservative given Tesla's solar and Megapack plans)
   - Total Tesla revenue: ~$96 billion in 2026, $155 billion in 2030, $1.7 trillion by 2045
   - First trillion-dollar revenue year projected: 2039
   - Regulatory credits projected to go to zero by 2029
   - Vehicle sales revenue projected to begin a steady decline after 2031 as Tesla transitions to transportation-as-a-service

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla Stock Performance — Relative Context:** Tesla stock closed at $445, up 3.89% on the day, with volume 18% above average, while the Nasdaq 100 was up 2.9%. Tesla is up 32% over the prior 35 days. However, Loomis cautions against attributing this to a Tesla-specific catalyst: the S&P 500 is up 16% over the prior six weeks, which is the 11th-largest six-week gain for that index since 1950 — and the only instance in the top 20 that did not occur during or just after a bear market.

2. **Ford Enters Battery Energy Storage Market:** Ford announced a new subsidiary, Ford Energy, planning to deploy at least 20 gigawatt-hours of battery storage annually, with first customer deliveries planned for late 2027. Ford is repurposing existing US battery manufacturing capacity in Glendale, Kentucky. Loomis frames this as validation of the attractive margins in the BESS market, consistent with a prior interview discussion about EV battery factories being repurposed for energy storage.

3. **Redwood Materials: Tesla's Former CFO Joins:** Tesla's former CFO Deepak Ahuja joined Redwood Materials as CFO, citing his longstanding relationship with JB Straubel. Redwood recently cut approximately 10% of its headcount (135 people) to refocus resources on its energy division.
