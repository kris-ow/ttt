The article reports that Tesla Powerwalls supplied the bulk of a record 745 MW grid dispatch in California on October 6, part of a joint Tesla-Sunrun virtual power plant event, with the author noting both companies are largely taking credit for the same underlying battery fleet.

## Energy
1. **Record dispatch and Tesla's share:** On October 6, more than 85,000 Tesla Powerwall owners in California dispatched over 600 MW to the grid, which Tesla called a new record for the largest Powerwall VPP event (announced October 8). Sunrun's release (issued October 14) reported a combined total of 669 MW on October 5 and 745 MW on October 6 from "the companies" without breaking out the Tesla/Sunrun split; the article calculates that Tesla's 600+ MW figure represents roughly 80% of Sunrun's 745 MW total. This followed a 580 MW record set about a month earlier (per the article, a 28% increase), itself up from a 539 MW record that had stood from July 29, 2025 until September 9, 2026.
2. **Per-home output suggests newer hardware:** The article calculates that 600+ MW from roughly 85,000 homes works out to about 7 kW per home — above the 5 kW continuous rating of a Powerwall 2 — which the author says points to a growing share of Powerwall 3 units (rated 11.5 kW) and/or homes with multiple batteries in the fleet.
3. **How the dispatch was triggered:** The events occurred during an October heat wave at the request of the California Energy Commission and state utilities, with California's Demand Side Grid Support (DSGS) and Emergency Load Reduction Program (ELRP) both issuing dispatches simultaneously across all participating investor-owned utility territories for the first time this year, per Sunrun. DSGS activates once CAISO's day-ahead price exceeds $200/MWh. Sunrun says the 745 MW dispatch could cover every household in San Francisco and San Jose combined during peak hours, and that it now has more than 120,000 home batteries enrolled in California grid programs. Sunrun CEO Mary Powell said customers are being compensated for helping strengthen grid reliability.
4. **San Jose data center VPP project advancing:** Sunrun says a Google-funded distributed power plant project with PG&E in the San Jose area plans to enroll nearly 21,000 devices involving Tesla and Everyday Electric (formerly Renew Home), with initial dispatches expected this month. This is described as the first concrete step toward the 16.8 GW virtual power plant plan for data centers that Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced in June; the article notes the Tesla/Everyday Electric portion remains under a non-binding letter of intent.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Tesla's key advantage is its large installed base combined with software able to coordinate tens of thousands of homes to respond to a single grid signal, calling this capability unmatched by competitors in residential storage. In a reader-comment-style addendum, the author also critiques both companies for reporting only peak power (MW) rather than energy delivered over time (MWh/duration), arguing this makes it hard to judge the dispatch's real grid value, and notes the economics depend on California-specific payment programs (DSGS/ELRP) that most states lack.
