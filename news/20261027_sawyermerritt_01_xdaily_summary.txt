## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Cybercab production line outpacing robotaxi deployment:** In a new interview, Tesla VP Lars Moravy said Tesla "doesn't run the [Cybercab] line every day" because it can produce faster than robotaxi deployment can absorb — calling it "a bit of a race right now to see who can go faster." This suggests Cybercab manufacturing capacity is not currently the bottleneck for robotaxi scaling; regulatory/operational deployment pace is.

2. **Robotaxi geofence expansion, led by a large Houston increase:** Updated geofence sizes (square miles): Austin 272, Houston 254 (described as a 916% increase "today"), Dallas 81, Miami 31, Orlando 26, Tampa 24 — all unsupervised/fully autonomous zones. A separate post notes Tesla officially expanded the Houston geofence to a "MUCH larger" area, humorously shaped like a Starship rocket. These are substantive, dated expansions distinct from the stale RobotaxiTracker.com fleet figures.

3. **Large robotaxi fleet sighting and approaching 1,000 registered vehicles in Texas:** A viral video (5.3K likes) reportedly shows the largest number of Tesla Cybercabs and Model Y Robotaxis seen together on public roads, heading back to base; the poster states Tesla is nearing 1,000 robotaxis registered in Texas to date (poster's own figure, not from RobotaxiTracker.com).

4. **FSD regulatory and commercial expansion in Europe and via Turo:** Tesla FSD Supervised (branded "Tesla Assisted Driving" in Europe) was approved in Latvia, the 10th European country, joining Belgium, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, Estonia, Slovenia, Czechia, Croatia, and others (full list cut off in source). Separately, car-rental marketplace Turo announced the first verified Tesla FSD (Supervised) rental experience in the U.S., starting October 28, letting guests search and book FSD-equipped Teslas via direct API integration — a notable consumer-facing distribution channel for FSD exposure.

5. **SpaceX secures major AI compute financing and demand commitments:** Anthropic's IPO filing disclosed it expects to spend up to $120.5B on SpaceX compute through 2029, with monthly payments up to $3.1B (~$37.2B annualized), and states SpaceX is expected to deliver all contracted computing capacity by December 2026. Separately, SpaceX announced a $40B master finance lease with an entity managed by Apollo Global Management — described as the largest GPU financing ever — to fund next-generation GPU and related equipment for its compute buildout. Together these represent some of the largest disclosed compute/financing commitments tied to SpaceX's AI infrastructure ambitions.

6. **Starlink commercial aviation wins and Verizon pushback on Starlink Mobile:** easyJet announced it will install Starlink across its A320/A320neo/A321neo fleet, with installs starting H1 2027 and rollout completing by early 2028. Separately, Verizon's CEO publicly argued SpaceX's Starlink Mobile U.S. carrier buildout would take at least 4-6 years and $300B-$500B to fully compete with terrestrial networks, especially in suburban/urban areas where Verizon derives 98% of its revenue — a competitive pushback worth tracking as a counter-narrative to Starlink Mobile's ambitions.

7. **Analyst defense of Tesla's Q3 earnings tone; Tesla Semi production update:** Canaccord Genuity analyst George Gianarikas pushed back on characterizations of Tesla's Q3 call as "subdued," calling that take "flatly wrong" and describing the call as one of the most "unapologetically bullish" in recent memory (per his note). Separately, 55 new Tesla Semi trucks were spotted in Nevada, cited as a sign production is progressing well.
