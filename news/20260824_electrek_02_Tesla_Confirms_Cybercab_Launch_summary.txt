Electrek reports that Tesla confirmed a Cybercab launch event in Austin on September 3, an invite-only affair for top Robotaxi rider sweepstakes scorers (winners announced August 26, RSVP deadline August 30), which Tesla will also livestream. The article argues the event amounts to adding a new vehicle body to Tesla's existing, still-small driverless service rather than a real scaling milestone.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab joins existing Austin service:** The two-seat, steering-wheel-free Cybercab will become hailable through Tesla's existing Robotaxi app in Austin, running on the same FSD software stack already used by Model Y robotaxis — including the stack the article says drove through a row of bollards in Austin the prior week with no one in the driver's seat.
2. **Fleet size unchanged over a year:** The article states Tesla's and independent trackers' estimates put Tesla's active unsupervised fleet at roughly 20–30 vehicles across all markets, peaking around 25 in spring and drifting sideways or lower since the June 2025 Austin launch. Tesla told investors it had driven about 380,000 cumulative unsupervised miles as of its July earnings call.
3. **Comparison to Waymo:** The article cites Waymo running roughly 3,000 driverless vehicles, completing more than 500,000 paid trips a week, and having logged over 220 million rider-only miles — framing Tesla's cumulative unsupervised mileage as less than 0.2% of Waymo's total.
4. **Cybercab efficiency vs. capacity tradeoff:** Tesla rates the Cybercab at about 165 Wh/mile (3,113 lbs, 48 kWh battery), which the article calls the most efficient EV Tesla has built, versus roughly 250–280 Wh/mile for a Model Y. However, the Cybercab seats only two with minimal cargo space versus the Model Y's five seats, so the efficiency gain only applies to smaller rides.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the Cybercab launch is a "car swap dressed up as a milestone" — efficiency isn't the bottleneck holding Tesla's robotaxi program back, autonomy scale is, and the Cybercab doesn't address that. He states the real test will be whether Tesla's unsupervised fleet count grows meaningfully beyond ~20-30 vehicles a month after the event.
