The host, streaming live ahead of Tesla's September 3rd Cybercab launch event in Austin, argues TSLA is poised to break out of its recent slump as commercial robotaxi deployment finally becomes tangible. He walks through same-day catalysts (a bullish Morgan Stanley note, a new Tesla robotaxi fleet-sales webpage, and an updated Robotaxi app/website listing Cybercab across six cities) and explains why he added to his Tesla exposure despite a large single-day rally, while fielding a long stream of viewer questions on strategy, risk, and market context.

## Robotaxi
1. **Morgan Stanley turns more bullish pre-event:** Morgan Stanley (price target $400) said it goes into the Cybercab launch event with a "positive bias," citing Texas DMV data showing Tesla had registered roughly 45 additional Cybercabs for commercial operation (the firm said "circa 40"), which it read as evidence of a larger-scale rollout; the host called 45 vehicles still a modest number in absolute terms.
2. **New Tesla fleet-sales webpage surfaces:** Tesla launched a webpage inviting expressions of interest in "Cybercab fleet vehicle purchasing," mobility hubs/infrastructure, and event collaboration — which the host read as Tesla moving earlier than he expected toward selling Cybercabs (rather than only operating its own fleet), a shift he says would be a major near-term earnings boost if priced well above cost, though he still believes Tesla makes more money long-term by operating fleets itself rather than selling units.
3. **Robotaxi app and website updated same day:** Tesla's Robotaxi website and app were refreshed on launch day to say autonomous robotaxi rides are "currently being offered" across Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa, with Cybercab featured ahead of Model Y and the Robovan (described as "arriving soon"); the host says this implies Cybercab commercial availability across all six cities, a detail he says surprised him.
4. **Host's fleet-ownership vs. fleet-sale thesis:** The host proposes Tesla will own and directly operate robotaxi fleets in major U.S. metros (New York, LA, Chicago, the Texas cities, etc.) where it can control infrastructure, but will sell Cybercab fleets to third-party operators in smaller/remote U.S. markets and most international markets (citing Boise, rural Idaho, parts of Europe) where building local infrastructure and navigating jurisdictions doesn't make sense for Tesla to do itself — with Tesla taking a cut of fleet-operator revenue. He estimates Cybercab margin per unit at roughly $20,000–$40,000/year and floated a hypothetical ~$50,000 purchase price, calling that math attractive for third-party buyers.
5. **Cost and production estimates:** The host affirmed a viewer's estimate that Cybercab production cost is around $16,000–$18,000, and said costs should fall below $20,000 once production reaches the hundreds of thousands of units, which he expects around 2028. He also estimated the current Cybercab production count at roughly 3,000, en route to 5,000–6,000 by October, and expects "100-plus" in active deployment over the coming weeks.
6. **Launch event held under embargo, no livestream:** Tesla did not livestream the Cybercab launch event and reportedly required attendees to sign NDAs preventing disclosure of details until the event concluded; the host argues this makes it unlikely the market will get a single clear "how many Cybercabs" number at launch, and expects the true scale of rollout to become apparent only over the following days/weeks via robotaxi trackers and rider reports rather than at a discrete news moment.
7. **Canonical fleet data context:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the active Robotaxi fleet (last reported ~4 weeks prior to this video) stood at 839 vehicles (Bay Area 657, Austin 135, Dallas 22, Houston 25), with an unsupervised subset of 48 (Austin 32, Dallas 6, Houston 6, Miami 1, Tampa 3) — both metrics stale as of this video's publish date.

He also notes trading volume was above average (~43M shares vs. ~33-39M average).

3. **Host's positioning:** The host said he modestly increased his Tesla exposure (not heavily leveraged) after the day's news, citing TSLA being down about 16% year-to-date and roughly 20% below its December all-time high despite what he views as major fundamental de-risking of Optimus and robotaxi since then. He said he could tolerate a pullback to $350–$360 without concern.
