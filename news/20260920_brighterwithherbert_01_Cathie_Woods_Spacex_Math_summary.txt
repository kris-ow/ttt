The video covers Cathie Wood's (ARK Invest) projection that SpaceX could generate up to $10 trillion in annual revenue if Elon Musk's goal of 10,000 Starship flights/year by 2030 is realized, alongside Gwynne Shotwell's comments on in-space data centers, a new Starlink direct-to-cell deal with Japan's KDDI, and the upcoming Starship Flight 14 launch carrying the first Starlink V3 satellites. Host and guest Larry Goldberg (a VC investor) largely endorse the bullish framing while flagging the 10,000-flights-by-2030 figure as unrealistic on that specific timeline, though achievable within roughly five years.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **ARK's $10 trillion revenue scenario:** Cathie Wood/ARK's Sam Korus calculated that at $19 million in connectivity revenue per terabyte-per-second and 61 terabytes/second of capacity per Starship launch, each launch could represent roughly $1 billion in revenue; at Musk's stated goal of 10,000 flights/year by 2030, this implies roughly $10 trillion in annual revenue, which Wood called a scenario making SpaceX's reported $1.75 trillion IPO valuation look like a "deep value opportunity" in hindsight. Musk responded that this outcome is "not impossible." Goldberg said he does not believe the 10,000-flights-by-2030 timeline specifically, but believes SpaceX could reach that cadence within roughly five years, and that per-launch revenue will decay from the current unsaturated-market figure as capacity fills.
2. **Boeing contract comparison:** Gwynne Shotwell said Boeing has been paid more than SpaceX for its human capsule program and that SpaceX isn't retiring the Dragon capsule from the market yet, needling Boeing with a "minivan road trip" comparison. Goldberg added that neither company received a cost-plus contract — both got fixed fees that were later adjusted upward, with Boeing's adjustment "very significant" and Tesla's/SpaceX's "somewhat," and asserted (his own characterization) that Boeing has failed to deliver while SpaceX has "over-delivered," including providing what he called a rescue for Boeing's program shortfalls.
3. **Culture and hiring narrative:** Goldberg pointed to a recent SpaceX video (part of a series) as evidence of the caliber of people Musk and Shotwell have hired, retelling an anecdote (his account) that Shotwell met Musk after dropping off another interviewee at the factory, leading to her own hire in sales. He argued former SpaceX/Tesla employees have gone on to found companies using skills learned there, a broader effect he credits to Musk's hiring approach.

## SpaceX — Starship
4. **Flight 14 launch details:** Starship Flight 14 is targeted for Tuesday, September 22, pending regulatory approval — the first Starship flight planned to reach orbit, completing approximately six orbits over a roughly 10-hour flight at an altitude near 270 km, with splashdown targeted in the Pacific west of Chile. The booster's primary objective is a successful ascent, stage separation, boost-back burn, and landing burn at an offshore point in the Gulf of America.
5. **Capacity milestone:** Even carrying less than half its planned normal satellite load (26 of a typical ~60), Flight 14 will already be the heaviest commercial cargo ever lifted to space, according to Goldberg. He expects launch cadence to progress from roughly one per week to multiple per day over time.
6. **Launch site expansion:** SpaceX is reportedly aiming for around 13 launch towers within roughly two to two-and-a-half years; Goldberg believes there will likely be additional sites beyond that count, including potentially another Starbase-like location.

## SpaceX — Starlink
7. **Starlink V3 payload and bandwidth:** Flight 14 will deploy the first 26 Starlink V3 satellites, each adding about one terabyte/second of capacity (26 TB/s total) — roughly 10 times the capacity of a single Falcon 9 launch of V2 Mini satellites, and about 25x when compared against a full V3 load (~60 satellites). Musk stated the V3 constellation, once deployed, will ultimately deliver more than 100 times the bandwidth of the current ~11,000-satellite constellation; Goldberg noted SpaceX intends to launch around 100,000 of these satellites, potentially exceeding that 100x figure.
8. **KDDI direct-to-cell deal:** Shotwell signed a new Starlink mobile deal with Japan's KDDI for broadband and voice calls on regular phones in dead zones, targeted by the end of 2027. Goldberg noted that while data-only direct-to-cell should work with current iPhones/Android phones, native voice calling likely requires a bandwidth change unless SpaceX can access spectrum it doesn't currently hold — KDDI may possess relevant spectrum itself, which remains unconfirmed.
9. **Skepticism from a competitor CEO:** The host referenced a T-Mobile CEO publicly doubting SpaceX's ability to deliver satellite-to-phone connectivity through obstacles like windshields and buildings. Goldberg dismissed this, drawing an analogy to a former head of a SpaceX launch competitor who wrote an essay (his account, unnamed) arguing reusable rockets were uneconomical shortly before SpaceX proved otherwise, and to a French space industry official who reportedly called SpaceX's reusability efforts a "losing venture."

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
10. **In-space data centers ("supercompute"):** Shotwell argued space-based data centers avoid the costs and delays of Earth-based ones — citing skyrocketing real estate prices once a data center site is known, plus lengthy permitting and equipment lead times — and described orbital real estate as effectively infinite and free, with free cooling via radiators facing deep space and satellites that can face the sun continuously. She and Musk agreed to rebrand "data centers" as "supercompute." Goldberg noted SpaceX is building its own space-specific solar panels (distinct from Tesla's Earth-focused solar panels), with SpaceX aiming to be self-reliant on its own solar panel supply. Goldberg said data center satellite launches are expected to begin next year, alongside continued Starlink satellite replenishment as older satellites' orbits decay.
