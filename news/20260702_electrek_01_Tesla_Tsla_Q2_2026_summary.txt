Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2 2026, a 25% year-over-year increase that beat Wall Street's consensus estimate by roughly 74,000 units — the company's first quarterly delivery growth after two consecutive years of decline.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Record Q2 deliveries, massive beat vs. expectations:** Tesla produced 451,758 vehicles and delivered 480,126 in Q2 2026. Model 3/Y accounted for 442,936 produced and 467,762 delivered; Other Models (S, X, Cybertruck, Semi) contributed 8,822 produced and 12,364 delivered. The result surpassed analyst consensus of 406,024 and beat even the most bullish estimates from Goldman Sachs and Barclays (~418,000–420,000) by more than 60,000 units.

2. **Inventory drawdown marks reversal from Q1:** Tesla delivered ~28,000 more vehicles than it produced in the quarter, working down existing inventory — a reversal from Q1 2026, when the company built roughly 50,000 excess vehicles it couldn't sell.

3. **Historical context:** Q2 2026 is Tesla's best-ever second quarter, surpassing the 466,140 units delivered in Q2 2023. It also represents a 34% sequential jump from Q1 2026's 358,023 deliveries. Tesla's all-time quarterly record remains 497,099, set in Q3 2025.

## Energy
1. **Energy storage up 40%+ year-over-year:** Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh of energy storage in Q2 2026, up more than 40% from 9.6 GWh in Q2 2025, though slightly below analyst expectations of ~13.8 GWh.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla closing gap with BYD:** BYD delivered 557,090 fully electric vehicles in Q2 2026, maintaining its lead in global BEV sales. However, BYD's BEV deliveries fell ~8% year-over-year while Tesla's rose 25%, narrowing the gap from over 220,000 units a year ago to roughly 77,000 units.

## Editorial Stance
The author characterizes Q2 as a "genuinely strong" and real inflection point, attributing the demand surge primarily to a spike in gas prices triggered by the war in Iran, which drove EV demand while Tesla held ~50,000 units of excess inventory from Q1.
