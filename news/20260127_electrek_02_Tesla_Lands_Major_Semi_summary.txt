Pilot, the largest U.S. travel center operator, has partnered with Tesla to install Semi chargers at select locations along major highway corridors, with first sites expected to open Summer 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Pilot-Tesla Semi charging partnership:** Pilot, a Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary operating over 900 travel centers across 44 states and five Canadian provinces (serving ~1.2 million guests/day), will build Tesla Semi charging stations along corridors including I-5 and I-10, targeting locations "where the need for heavy-duty charging is highest." Pilot SVP of alternative fuels Shannon Sturgil said the move extends the company's alternative fuel exploration and praised Tesla's "turnkey solutions" deployed quickly. Pilot already partners with Volvo Group (since 2022) on heavy-duty EV charging and with GM/EVgo on over 200 passenger EV charging sites across 40 states.

2. **Broader Semi charging network context:** This follows Tesla's first public Semi charging station (reported last year) and Tesla's previously announced plan for 46 Semi charging stations by 2027. Tesla also recently released video showing the Semi charging at 1.2 MW.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls Tesla Semi "one of the rare programs at Tesla that still excites" him, and argues the Pilot deal is significant because Tesla's proven charging business model makes third-party partnerships easier than in the early Supercharger days. He notes I-5 and I-10 are logical first corridors given early Semi customers like PepsiCo, and says that if the network is operational by Summer 2026 as planned, it would meaningfully help make the Semi viable beyond the current handful of fleets, which mostly use it for local distribution rather than long-haul trucking.
