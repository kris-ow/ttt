This episode of brighterwithherbert, featuring guest Larry Goldberg, covers SpaceX's newly revealed "Starmind" orbital AI compute satellites, Google's first orbital AI chip test aboard a Falcon 9, an FCC spectrum win for Starlink, and a historic week of launch cadence including Starship's first orbital flight with revenue-generating payload. The throughline is that SpaceX's infrastructure — Starship, Starlink, and now orbital data centers — is compounding into a uniquely dominant position in space-based compute and connectivity.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Starmind satellites unveiled:** Elon Musk described a new satellite called "Starmind" (referred to in the video as a roughly 75-meter wingspan AI compute spacecraft) carrying an SpaceX-built Nvidia-based supercomputer, with each unit generating over 250 kilowatts of solar power — about 20% more than the International Space Station. Musk said he is "cautiously optimistic" SpaceX can run its version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips at close to 250 kilowatts of average constant power (peak roughly 10% higher), calling this "a big deal if it pans out" that would also benefit SpaceX's ground-based systems, crediting close co-design work with Nvidia engineering.
2. **Community revenue estimate:** A commentator (Aaron Bernett, referred to in the video as "Mach 33") estimated each Starmind satellite could generate $25–50 million in revenue, based on rough comparisons to an Nvidia GB-class rack, assuming 50% utilization and roughly a third of current Grok token pricing; Goldberg called the math "very broad strokes" but said the economics look highly profitable given Starship's expected launch costs and volume.
3. **Google tests orbital AI compute via SpaceX:** Google sent four of its own AI chips into orbit aboard a Falcon 9 (as part of Project Suncatcher, riding with a Planet Labs satellite among roughly 300 payloads on the mission), marking the first real-world test of solar-powered AI compute in space. Goldberg noted Google already rents terrestrial data center capacity from SpaceX in Memphis and argued Starship will remain the only viable means of launching large-scale space data center infrastructure "for years to come."

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **FCC spectrum and environmental-review win:** The FCC voted to open roughly 500 MHz of new spectrum (cited bands of 12.7–13.25 GHz and 42–42.5 GHz) for fixed satellite services like Starlink, a roughly 25% increase in downlink capacity, and voted to exempt satellite constellations from federal environmental reviews. Goldberg said the spectrum will go to open bidding but expects SpaceX to be the primary beneficiary given Starlink is currently the only vendor able to fully utilize such bandwidth.
2. **Starship's first revenue-generating flight:** Starship reached orbit and deployed V3 Starlink satellites for the first time in what the host described as the vehicle's first revenue-generating commercial flight, rather than a test. Each V3 satellite reportedly increases total Starlink constellation bandwidth capacity by roughly 0.1%, against a current base the host described as roughly 11,000 satellites (mostly V2 Minis) — with Starship eventually carrying 60 V3 satellites per launch.
3. **Capacity and pricing trajectory:** Goldberg projected Starlink capacity could grow 30–40% next year and potentially double within two years, with consumer pricing (cited around $150/month urban, $55 rural) potentially falling toward $30/month as bandwidth multiplies, though he expects total Starlink revenue to grow more slowly than capacity due to falling prices.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Unprecedented single-day cadence:** Three Falcon rockets stood vertical across pads in Florida and California in one week, with the host and guest highlighting a day on which SpaceX launched three major rockets (including a Falcon Heavy mission for the NROL and a crewed Falcon 9 carrying NASA's Crew 13, Dragon's 21st human spaceflight mission), deployed over 300 satellites, and sent four astronauts to orbit — a cadence Goldberg said no country or company has matched.
2. **Fastest ISS transfer on record:** The Crew 13 Dragon mission delivered astronauts to the ISS in under 8 hours (stated as 7 hours 55 minutes), which Goldberg called the fastest crew transfer to the space station the U.S. has achieved, attributing it partly to favorable ISS positioning at launch time.
3. **Falcon Heavy's 14th flight and market position:** The Falcon Heavy mission marked the vehicle's 14th launch; Goldberg noted it is currently the only operational U.S. heavy-lift vehicle besides Starship, citing Blue Origin as grounded following an explosion and ULA's heavy-lift vehicle as non-functional at this time.
4. **Starship orbit anniversary:** Starship reached orbit for the first time on September 28, 2026 — exactly 18 years after Falcon 1's first successful orbital flight on September 28, 2008 — which both host and guest framed as a milestone bookending SpaceX's run of "firsts" in spaceflight history (first private liquid-fueled orbital rocket, first booster return and reuse, first Falcon Heavy launch, among others).
5. **Elon's hourly-launch target:** Responding to a post from the show, Musk said SpaceX's plan is to reach hourly launches within two to three years; Goldberg noted the week's three-launches-in-one-day achievement as an early signal toward that cadence goal.
