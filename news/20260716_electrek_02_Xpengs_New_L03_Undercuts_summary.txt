Xpeng launched its L03 electric SUV-coupe in Europe this week starting at €34,990, undercutting the Tesla Model Y by roughly €3,400 in Germany and about $10,400 in Norway, with deliveries starting in Q4.

## Market & Competition
1. **Xpeng's aggressive European launch:** Xpeng unveiled the L03 at its Global Brand Event in Munich, opening orders across more than 60 markets simultaneously — the company's biggest global launch to date. It first debuted in China in May under the Mona sub-brand at around 150,000 yuan (~$20,500).
2. **European pricing details:** The L03 starts at €34,990 in France and Belgium, €35,600 in Germany, €36,600 in Austria, €36,990 in the Netherlands, 299,900 kroner in Norway, 239,995 kroner in Denmark, and 399,900 kronor in Sweden. In Germany it comes in four battery-electric LFP variants; a range-extender version (L03 Power X) arrives this fall from €38,600 with claimed range up to 1,000 km.
3. **Specs vs. Model Y:** The L03 charges at up to 236 kW (10-80% in 20 minutes) versus the Model Y Standard's 175 kW peak, and comes standard with a heat pump, 6 kW vehicle-to-load, heated/ventilated front seats, and a panoramic roof. The top Ultra trim adds two Turing AI chips running Xpeng's VLA 2.0 driver-assist system. The Model Y Standard offers more range (534 km WLTP vs. 445 km for the L03).
4. **Norway pricing tied to VAT threshold:** Norway began phasing out its EV VAT exemption this year, applying 25% VAT above 300,000 kroner; the L03's 299,900 kroner price sits exactly 100 kroner under that threshold. Tesla lists the Model Y from 399,990 kroner (~$41,500) in Norway versus the L03's ~$31,100 — a gap of roughly $10,400.
5. **US dollar comparison and market access:** Stripped of European VAT (19% Germany, 20% France), the L03's price equates to roughly $33,000-$34,000 in US terms, versus the Model Y Standard's $39,990 US MSRP and Tesla's ex-VAT German price of about $37,500. Current US tariffs on Chinese vehicles bar Xpeng from the US market entirely, so the competition is confined to Europe, where Xpeng registered more than 4,600 vehicles in June — a fourth straight monthly record, per industry registration data cited by the article.
6. **Tesla's own pricing move:** Tesla launched the stripped-down Model Y Standard at €39,990 last year to counter Chinese competition; its German configurator now lists the Model Y from €38,970 after a price adjustment last month.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues this marks Chinese price competition truly arriving in Europe's top-volume EV segment, with Xpeng undercutting Tesla's defensive Model Y Standard while offering more standard equipment even after EU tariffs. He notes counterarguments — Xpeng's thin European service network, Q4 delivery start, and driver-assist features likely gated by regulators until next year — versus Tesla's Supercharger network, brand, and Berlin production, and flags a report that Xpeng is considering European production to avoid tariffs. He concludes that Tesla's US pricing is currently insulated by tariffs rather than competitiveness, and that Europe will show what the market looks like without that insulation.
