## Robotaxi
- Nevada Transportation Authority approved Tesla as an autonomous network company but capped the fleet at 10 vehicles, far below the 5,000 requested in Tesla's June 5 application; operations restricted to a ~4-mile Las Vegas Strip corridor, barred from roads over 45 mph, and no airport pickups without separate authorization. Incidents must be reported within five business days.
- Waymo has applied for the same Nevada permit and appears close to approval, aided by a new multi-year partnership with the Las Vegas Raiders at Allegiant Stadium.
- Large Cybercab sightings reported across Texas and Tennessee (38 in Fort Worth, 85 in Houston, ~10 in Franklin, TN); VIN observations reached the 2,300 range, the highest yet, though VIN-based tracking is described as low-precision.
- Multiple commentators estimate 1,500–2,000+ Cybercabs staged nationally awaiting wider deployment, with one host predicting 5,000 within two months and 15,000–20,000 by December — explicitly his own unverified forecast.
- Tesla is hiring fleet support specialists for day/night shifts in Las Vegas, and a Texas DMV site that tracked vehicle registrations (last reading: 181) has reportedly gone offline.
- New Cybercab features spotted: integrated Starlink connectivity, RGB customizable light bars (including purple and flag-colored variants in Austin), and specialized Continental tires designed exclusively for the autonomous fleet.

## Autonomous Driving
- Motor Trend, which previously criticized Tesla's system and gave GM's Super Cruise its 2025 award, named FSD version 14 the best driver-assistance system for 2026 — a notable reversal.
- Tesla's fleet has logged 13 billion FSD miles, described as exceeding 90% of all self-driving miles logged industry-wide; active FSD subscribers reached 1.48 million in Q2, up 56% year-over-year, with over 55% of new North American deliveries including an FSD subscription.

## Financials
- TSLA closed at $339.96 (+3.8%) while SPCX fell 3.33%, against a 1.15% Nasdaq-100 gain — a divergence one host called unhelpful for merger speculation.
- Tesla posted its best Q2 in company history, beating delivery estimates by ~20% with global deliveries up 25% year-over-year even as U.S. EV sales fell 20%; Model 3/Y made up over 97% of global deliveries, a concentration risk given weak Cybertruck volume.
- Wall Street average price targets cited at ~$500 for Tesla and ~$231 for SpaceX; one analyst's own fair-value estimate for Tesla is $495–$499, rising to $698–$798 under merger/Optimus scenarios (explicitly speculative).

## Energy
- Tesla Electric launched a Powerwall lease in select Texas retail-choice areas: two Powerwalls at ~$122/month before a $87/month Backup-plan credit, netting ~$35/month plus tax and a $100 one-time fee; solar homes are excluded, and the credit depends on staying enrolled in Tesla Electric, which dispatches stored energy to ERCOT during price spikes.

## Market & Competition
- Sweden's IF Metall ended its 1,021-day strike — the longest labor conflict in modern Swedish history — after Tesla individually bought out all remaining striking members; Tesla still has not signed a collective bargaining agreement. Tesla's Swedish registrations fell from 21,894 (2024) to 7,252 (2025), which Electrek attributes to political backlash against Musk rather than the strike.
- A new 124-stall V4 Supercharger station has been filed for San Francisco, open to the general public with only two bathrooms planned — prompting speculation of heavy expected robotaxi usage.
- Construction is reportedly underway on Tesla's "Terafab" chip fabrication facility at Giga Texas, a Tesla-SpaceX joint venture to build chips for cars, robots, and data centers.
- A back-of-envelope analysis argues Terafab's full-scale target of 1 million wafer starts/month would require 300–500 EUV lithography machines — versus roughly 400 currently installed worldwide — implying tooling capex exceeding $100 billion at full scale.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Speculation continues around a Tesla-SpaceX merger; one analyst flagged Elon Musk's SEC 13G showing ~6.44 million SpaceX shares (~48%), including 350 million Class B options at an ~$8 strike expiring end of August, as a possible internal deadline — explicitly caveated as unconfirmed.
- A separate guest's working theory (labeled speculative) posits SpaceX would be the surviving entity in any merger, with Tesla shareholders receiving SpaceX Class A shares; a "change of control" clause would preserve only market-cap-based milestones in Musk's compensation plan rather than operational ones.
- A Kalshi market cited showed under 50% odds of a merger announcement within a year.
