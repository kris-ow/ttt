## Brief

- Nevada caps Tesla robotaxi permit at 10 vehicles, not 5,000
- Cybercab VINs spotted above 2,300 across Texas, Tennessee

## Robotaxi
- Nevada Transportation Authority approved Tesla as an autonomous network company but capped the fleet at 10 vehicles, far below the 5,000 requested in Tesla's June 2026 application; operations confined to a ~4-mile Las Vegas Strip corridor, barred from roads over 45 mph, no airport pickups granted. Waymo has also applied and appears close to approval, aided by a new Raiders/Allegiant Stadium partnership.
- Cybercab sightings intensified: 38 units plus 8 Model Ys in Fort Worth, 85 Cybercabs/Cybertrucks in Houston, ~10 in Franklin, Tennessee — versus the usual four at demo events. VIN analysis puts the fleet above 2,300, the highest yet observed, though tracking methodology is imprecise (per brighterwithherbert, jobhakdi).
- Joe Tegtmeyer reported 43 of 44 pictured Cybercabs lack steering wheels, and observed overnight endurance/test-track running with chase vehicles — read by commentators as evidence commercial-only deployment is approaching.
- First Cybercab with integrated Starlink connectivity unveiled (~Aug 10), plus RGB customizable lighting and a specialized Continental run-flat tire built exclusively for the autonomous fleet.
- Multiple hosts (unverified, own estimates) pegged the deployed-but-unlaunched Cybercab fleet at 1,500–2,000+ nationally, distinct from RobotaxiTracker.com's stale 839 active/48 unsupervised counts.
- Host commentary described unsupervised vehicle counts growing from ~30 in April to an estimated 90–100 now, with mile-doubling roughly every 5–8 weeks (jobhakdi/brighterwithherbert panel estimate, not RobotaxiTracker.com).

## Financials
- Tesla's Q2 2026 beat prior delivery estimates by ~20% with deliveries up 25% year-over-year even as US EV sales fell 20% after the federal tax credit expired; Model 3/Y comprised over 97% of global deliveries, a concentration risk (per farzad-fm).
- TSLA is down 27% year-to-date versus the Nasdaq/S&P's ~15% gains and Magnificent Seven peers' 12–25% gains, which one panelist ties to unclear forward guidance versus SpaceX's clearer revenue framework.
- Wall Street average price targets cited: ~$500 for TSLA, ~$231 for SPCX; one panelist's own fair-value estimate for Tesla ranges $495–$798 depending on merger premium and Cybercab/Optimus catalysts (speculative, not consensus).
- SpaceX reportedly paid $329 million this year for Megapacks from Tesla, described as a small line item.

## Autonomous Driving
- Motor Trend, which previously criticized Tesla's system and gave its 2025 award to GM's Super Cruise, named FSD version 14 the best driver-assistance system for 2026 — a notable reversal.
- Tesla's fleet has logged 13 billion FSD miles, described as above 90% of all self-driving miles logged industry-wide; 1.48 million active FSD subscribers, up 56% year-over-year, with over 55% of new North American Teslas delivered with an FSD subscription.
- Cybercab light bars spotted in Austin cycling colors (purple, then American-flag themed), suggesting event customization or rider-identification use.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- A newly filed SEC 13G shows Elon Musk holding ~6.44 million SpaceX shares (~48%), including 350 million Class B options at an ~$8 strike expiring end of August — flagged by one panelist as a possible internal deadline ahead of any merger announcement, explicitly caveated as unconfirmed.
- Merger mechanics remain speculative across two sources: theories include SpaceX absorbing Tesla as the surviving entity, a change-of-control clause that would preserve only market-cap-based compensation milestones for Musk, and calls for an exchange-ratio floor to protect Tesla shareholders — none of this confirmed as actual deal terms.
- A Kalshi market showed under 50% odds of a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement within a year.
- Terafab, a Tesla-SpaceX joint venture to build custom chips for cars, robots, and data centers (space and ground-based), was discussed as a possible precursor to merger integration; one source noted Terafab's contracts are reportedly filed mainly under SpaceX's name despite Tesla funding much of the R&D.

## Market & Competition
- Sweden's IF Metall union ended its 1,021-day strike against Tesla — the longest labor conflict in modern Swedish history — after Tesla individually bought out all remaining striking members; Tesla still has not signed a collective bargaining agreement. Tesla Sweden registrations fell from 21,894 (2024) to 7,252 (2025), which Electrek attributes to political backlash against Musk rather than the strike.
- A FTSE Russell letter reportedly confirmed no coordination with Musk/SpaceX before the index's fast-track inclusion rule change, rebutting a manipulation claim raised by commentators.
- New 124-stall V4 Supercharger station filed in San Francisco with a public amenity building; construction reportedly underway on Tesla's "Terafab" chip facility at Giga Texas.

## Energy
- Tesla Energy launched a Powerwall Lease in select Texas retail-choice areas: two leased Powerwalls at ~$122/month (3% annual escalator) offset by an $87/month Tesla Electric Backup-plan credit, netting ~$35/month plus tax and a $100 order fee. Credit depends on staying enrolled in Tesla Electric and granting dispatch rights; solar homes are ineligible. Tesla says it has installed over 1 million Powerwalls worldwide.
