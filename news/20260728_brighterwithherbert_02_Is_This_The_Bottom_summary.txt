Host Herbert and guest Joe Bhakdi (Pioneer Lands) discuss Tesla's sharp stock decline after earnings alongside a broader AI-stock selloff (Google, Micron, Nvidia), and debate whether visible progress in Austin's driverless Cybercab rollout signals a bottom despite the market pricing in a slow robotaxi ramp. Bhakdi argues Tesla and peers like Google/Micron are fundamentally undervalued given locked-in backlogs and execution, but says extreme market nervousness — not fundamentals — is driving the drop, and separately flags the SpaceX–Tesla merger overhang as a real complicating factor for Tesla's price action.

## Financials
1. **TSLA fell sharply into earnings and after:** The host notes TSLA dropped roughly 17% over five days heading into the discussion; Bhakdi says he cut his Tesla position heavily before earnings and estimates the earnings-specific drag on Tesla was only about 2-3%, with the rest of the week's decline attributable to the broader AI/market selloff (Korea's market crash, Google's post-earnings drop, Micron's collapse).
2. **Retail sentiment at multi-year low:** Bhakdi says his research shows retail inflows into stocks overall are at a five-year low, including below pandemic-panic levels, reflecting extreme investor nervousness across the AI trade, not Tesla-specific issues.
3. **Bhakdi's view that Google/Micron sell-offs are irrational:** Bhakdi argues Google's ~$500 billion cloud backlog and Micron's roughly $400 billion in already-contracted earnings over the next ~30 months make their sell-offs (Micron falling from $900B to $800B market cap in a day, per Bhakdi) fundamentally unjustified, and uses this as evidence the market is in a "delusional bear mood" rather than pricing fundamentals — a dynamic he says also weighs unfairly on Tesla's capex-heavy bets (Optimus factory, Terafab, Cybercab).

## Robotaxi
1. **Driverless Cybercab sightings surge in Austin:** The host cites (attributing to Joe Tegtmeyer footage) that driverless Cybercab sightings in public roads around Giga Texas rose roughly 2.5x from the first two weeks of July to the second two weeks, which the host frames as evidence the rollout is progressing past employee-only supervised testing toward driverless public-road operation.
2. **Cybercab production/robotaxi ramp estimate pushed back a quarter:** Bhakdi says his and the host's prior estimate of roughly 15,000 Cybercabs by year-end should likely be pushed back about one quarter (toward Q1) given the slower-than-hoped rollout commentary on the earnings call, though he notes this doesn't change his 6-12 month bullish thesis.
3. **Bhakdi sees no path to Tesla being "punished" for robotaxi success in current sentiment:** He argues that in the current market mood, even major robotaxi scaling milestones (e.g., reaching large fleet sizes) would likely be sold off the way Google and Micron were, illustrating why he reduced his Tesla exposure despite unchanged long-term conviction.
4. **Remote-operator fallback dismissed as a bear case:** The host rebuts a claim attributed to a YouTuber (meet Kevin, per the host) that robotaxi is "dunzo" and Tesla will abandon the rollout, arguing that even a worst-case scenario — hiring more remote operators similar to Waymo and accepting lower gross margins — still allows Tesla to scale, so full rollout is not actually blocked.

## Market & Competition
1. **China lithography news spooked the AI trade:** The host notes a China memory/lithography-related IPO and news event triggered a broad selloff in memory and AI-adjacent stocks (Micron, Nvidia), even though (per a commentator referenced as Jeff Lutz) production-scale impact is likely years away; Bhakdi says the sell-off reflects irrational market jitteriness rather than new fundamental information.
2. **Elon's personal focus areas, per Bhakdi's assessment:** Bhakdi speculates (his own inference, not sourced to Musk directly) that Musk's current priorities are Grok, AI satellites, and Starship, with Optimus and robotaxi lower priority because robotaxi is "baked into the cake" and doesn't need his direct attention.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **SpaceX–Tesla merger overhang discussed as a real complication:** Bhakdi says SpaceX going down in value now, combined with upcoming share unlocks, makes a near-term merger deal harder; he floats a possible "ratchet" mechanism where Musk sets a high minimum per-share floor for Tesla (host suggests a hypothetical $600-700+ floor) to guarantee shareholder approval and avoid legal challenges, but says timing matters because SpaceX's valuation could be at its weakest around a November-type vote window, while a later vote (mid-next-year) could see SpaceX back near $2 trillion if Colossus-related progress continues.
2. **Diverging analyst views on merger timing:** The host notes Alexandra Merz reportedly still believes a deal could happen soon (after an upcoming August earnings call), while Bhakdi, along with commentators the host names as Larry Goldberg and Cathie Wood (ARK Invest), believe it's unlikely this year and more likely next year or later.
