## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Texas Robotaxi coverage jumped from ~751 to ~1,200 square miles overnight after a San Antonio launch plus Austin expansion, a 59% gain the host pegs at roughly 4x Waymo's Texas footprint despite launching months after Waymo. Per Sawyer Merritt's tracking, San Antonio launched unsupervised service Nov 9 at ~275 sq mi (Tesla's largest-ever initial service area), while Austin added 171 sq mi (272→443 sq mi, +63%).
- Unsupervised geofences now span 8 cities (San Antonio, Austin, Houston, Fort Worth, Dallas, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) covering 4.2 million people, or 1.2% of the US population, up from 1.0% the prior week — per Sawyer Merritt's running tally, distinct from RobotaxiTracker.com's stale canonical figures (839 active fleet, 48 unsupervised, both unchanged since mid-2026).
- A San Antonio fare reportedly jumped several dollars within 11 minutes of launch as cars were fully booked; Joe Bhakdi expects prices to normalize as deployment scales, as happened early in Austin.
- ~53 Cybercab test units are staged at a Las Vegas lot ahead of a likely launch; Nevada has approved up to 5,000 robotaxi units, but Tesla's permit reportedly expires if vehicles aren't launched by December 18.
- Charlotte, NC permits for a "robotaxi"-labeled supercharger site (24 V4 stalls, sealed Oct 29) and 64 total stalls across two sites suggest expansion planning, despite no announced service there. Robotaxi technician hiring now spans 14 Texas cities including smaller markets (Flower Mound, El Paso, Lubbock), read as evidence of statewide rather than metro-only ambition.
- A third-party autonomous-driving analyst ("Jenma") argued scaling to 1 million owned Cybercabs would require ~20,000 new supercharger stalls/year versus Tesla's current ~6,000/year, with 12-month grid interconnection times and Megapacks largely sold out through 2027 limiting workarounds. Bhakdi countered that ~6,000 stalls/year supports roughly 400,000 vehicles (including non-Cybercab Teslas) and projects ~50,000 Cybercabs next year, scaling to ~200,000 the year after.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla's October China domestic sales fell to 8,817 units, down 66% year-over-year and the worst month since July 2022, per CPCA data cited by Electrek; exports hit 59,012, pushing year-to-date exports (420,984) above domestic sales (389,905) for the first time. Fred Lambert argues the drop is too large to be explained by normal seasonal patterns and reflects fading Chinese demand for an aging Model 3/Y lineup, not just VAT-rebate-driven export front-loading.
- Tesla Model Y and Model 3 ranked #1 and #2 globally in September EV sales (2 million total plug-in registrations, +3% YoY), with the Model Y leading by ~100,000 units; notably zero legacy automakers appeared in the global top 10.
- Cybertruck is sold out across all trims through 2026, with new orders now quoted January–March 2027 delivery; Musk said Tesla won't fulfill all pre-shutdown orders and deposits will be refunded for "a lot of people."
- Tesla unveiled the 2027 Model Y L (longer wheelbase, three-row) in a Jay Leno segment with Lars Moravy and Franz von Holzhausen: EPA-rated 332-mile range, 4.4s 0-60, 496 hp, 0.216 drag coefficient, 10 airbags including third-row coverage, and launch-series pricing at $61,000 expected to drop once that period ends.

## Financials

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Chamath Palihapitiya argued on the All-In podcast that SpaceX's Anthropic compute deal carries a 90-day mutual termination clause and that Musk could resell that capacity at 2-3x spot pricing; Musk countered that the same leverage cuts both ways if compute becomes cheap and abundant ("no tears in the casino for either party").
- SpaceX is pursuing major new data center sites: a ~$9 billion Arizona campus (Tombstone Resources, 4.4M sq ft, 747 acres) projected to cut county property taxes up to 20%, plus a reported Louisiana incentive offer for a $100 billion "Starbase"-style facility. One host estimates SpaceX could reach ~13 GW of compute capacity by year-end 2027 at $30-50B/year capex.
- Musk reaffirmed a forecast of $500 million in annual SpaceX AI-data-center revenue for next year, unchanged despite Texas permit-freeze concerns.
- Google board member Don Harrison stepped down from SpaceX's board (~Nov 4) over what Musk called "too many conflicts of interest with Alphabet," amid reported Google-SpaceX compute ties including a cloud agreement worth roughly $920 million/month (October 2026–June 2029) and Google's ~$900M 2015 SpaceX investment now valued near $84 billion.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX shares fell about 4% following the midterm elections, tied to Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa's campaign platform on freezing new AI data-center permits; she has since softened the stance toward higher taxes, water-supply requirements, and union wages rather than an outright freeze. Tesla shares also reportedly sold off on the same headline.
- Musk pushed back on a Wall Street Journal report alleging preferential treatment from Texas officials, saying Tesla and SpaceX moved to Texas due to California's taxes/regulation and implying both could relocate again if Texas turns restrictive.
- SpaceX is building a gas-fired power plant within Starbase, Texas on 444 acres purchased from Brownsville, with first-phase costs estimated around $24 billion; a public hearing is scheduled for December 9.
