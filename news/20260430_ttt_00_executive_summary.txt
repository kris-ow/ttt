## Robotaxi
- **Unsupervised fleet ramp is real but modest.** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, the unsupervised fleet stands at 26 vehicles (Austin ~20, Dallas 3, Houston 3) — the clearest evidence of active expansion after months of stagnation, though utilization remains below 30% and the planned H1 2026 five-city expansion is already behind schedule.
- **Cybercab production rate: ~10–20 units/day at Giga Texas.** Per Joe Tegtmeyer's tracking (cited by Bhakdi), Cybercabs are rolling off the line at that rate, with vehicles spotted in Illinois, Massachusetts, Florida, Bay Area, Phoenix, and potentially Georgia. Some units are shipping without a steering wheel, signaling autonomous-only intent. Bhakdi predicts thousands of units per month by June 2026.
- **Bhakdi's "forcing function" threshold: 1,800 unsupervised robotaxis by September–November 2026.** At that scale he calculates the annualized free cash flow contribution would equal roughly 5% of Tesla's entire 2024 FCF — enough to force analyst recognition. He calls this a 6-month slip from his prior projection but maintains the thesis.
- **Elon Musk walked back near-term guidance on the Q1 call.** He stated robotaxi will not be "super material" to the bottom line by end of 2026, but will be material in 2027. Herbert (Brighter) notes he had already adjusted expectations in December 2025 after the "doubling every month" cadence Musk cited in January 2026 failed to materialize. Herbert's own end-of-2026 Cybercab deployment estimate: ~15,000 units.
- **Waymo's unit economics are deteriorating sharply.** Alphabet Q1 2026 Other Bets revenue fell to $411M (from $450M in Q1 2025) while operating losses grew to $2.1B from $1.2B. Dillon Loomis estimates Waymo alone is losing ~$800M/quarter, implying costs of roughly $15–$20 for every $1 in ride revenue. Sundar Pichai has said Waymo won't be profitable until 2027 at the earliest. Waymo raised $16B in February at a $126B valuation and continues scaling.
- **Multi-party robotaxi models highlight Tesla's vertical integration advantage.** The Uber/Lucid/Nuro Bay Area service (targeting end-of-2026 launch) has brought in Hertz affiliate Oro Mobility to manage charging, maintenance, cleaning, and depot staffing — each additional party extracting margin. Loomis contrasts this with Tesla's fully integrated approach.
- **Robotaxi unit economics vs. car sales.** Herbert frames the core thesis: ~$5,000–$7,000 one-time profit per vehicle sale versus ~$30,000/year recurring revenue per robotaxi for at least three to five years.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD 14.3.2 rolling out, community calls it a "magnitude change" over 14.3.1.** Improvements cited include cleaner parking behavior, faster reactions, and early pothole-avoidance behavior. A new disengagement popup now requires a response and includes a navigation option — Tesla is still actively collecting edge-case data. Loomis estimates ~90% of the fleet has yet to receive FSD 14.3.
- **Smart Summon unified with FSD and robotaxi stack in 14.3.2.** Previously a separate code path, Smart Summon now runs on the same end-to-end neural network, meaning weight updates propagate across all driving modes simultaneously. Community videos show dramatically faster, smoother behavior; the "Banish" self-parking variant is flagged as a potential viral moment.
- **FSD V14 Lite confirmed for Hardware 3 vehicles and international markets.** Tesla announced two paths for the ~200,000 estimated remaining US HW3 FSD purchasers: trade-in discount toward a new car, or a direct hardware swap (new cameras and computer, drop-off time ~one week, Tesla expected to bear the cost given owners paid $8K–$15K). V14 Lite — covering parking to highway scenarios — is expected in June 2026 or later in Q2. HW3 will not support unsupervised FSD under current hardware.
- **EU supervised FSD approval converging on a June 30 TCMV vote.** Most EU nations are waiting for that group decision rather than acting country-by-country. Italy's Senator Carlo Calenda filed a formal parliamentary question urging the government to fast-track FSD approval; the Italian government responded no decision has been taken at the European level. An EU-wide summer approval remains on the table.
- **China suspends new AV licenses after Baidu Apollo Go mass stall in Wuhan.** More than 100 Apollo Go robotaxis simultaneously stopped on city streets in late March; regulators are requiring safety reviews. The suspension blocks new permits, new test projects, and city expansions but does not affect current operations (except BYD). No direct impact on Tesla, which operates at Level 2, but constrains Chinese AV competitors.
- **Xpeng VLA 2.0 positioned as credible FSD rival in Chinese scenarios.** Xpeng CEO publicly bet that if VLA 2.0 matches FSD 14.2's Silicon Valley performance within China by August 30, 2026, he will build a Chinese-style canteen in Silicon Valley; if it falls short, his head of intelligent driving runs naked across the Golden Gate Bridge. Global VLA 2.0 rollout expected 2027 with Volkswagen as first launch partner in China.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla Semi mass production officially begins at Giga Nevada.** The first production-line Semi (Long Range) features ~500-mile range, 4680 cells, fully electric steering, Cybertruck-derived actuators, 48V architecture, and three independent rear motors. Customer pricing: long-range ~$290,000, standard-range ~$260,000 (~325-mile range), versus a 2024 average zero-emission Class 8 truck cost of $435,000. California fleet buyers may receive HVIP incentives exceeding $100,000/unit. Musk said production will be slow before going exponential toward end of 2026 and into 2027; Loomis sets a modest bar of 1,000+ Semis for the remainder of 2026.
- **Cybercab engineering detail confirmed: front-wheel drive.** Joe Tegtmeyer, citing engineer conversations, confirmed the unboxed process places the drive unit, cooling system, and all electronics in a single front subassembly module bolted to the chassis — eliminating rear wiring runs, drivelines, and brake lines. Rear wheels are 21-inch aluminum alloy (rolling resistance); front wheels are 18-inch steel (cost/durability). Target specs: ~5.5 miles/kWh, sub-50 kWh pack, ~300-mile range. Estimated production cost: ~$20,000–$25,000.
- **Paint shop elimination saves ~$500M and removes a major cost and footprint burden.** Cybercab's injection-molded colored plastic panels eliminate a process that typically costs $200M–$1B to build, occupies ~50% of factory footprint, and accounts for ~60% of both factory energy use and CO2 emissions. Lars Munro confirmed the ~$500M savings figure without pushback.
- **Cybercab cooling infrastructure is significantly larger than expected.** Joe Justice observed the cooling duct appears larger than on a Model S Plaid, theorizing a "data center on wheels" architecture designed to run distributed compute workloads when not at full utilization during rides.
- **Used Tesla prices up ~18% over the past 90 days** per CarGurus, versus +6% for the broader index. Used EVs without a Tesla badge are down more than 10% over the past year. Tesla Model X was the fastest-selling used car in March 2026.
- **Giga Berlin Q1 2026: 61,000 Model Y units (record quarter), 1 millionth drive unit produced, 1,000 jobs being added.** Factory is shipping vehicles to Canada due to tariff dynamics. Brian White describes European demand as "picking back up."
- **Tesla annual deliveries remain flat at ~1.8M vehicles.** The 20 million units/year target by 2030 is described by Brian White as "absolutely off the table." Cumulative deliveries stand at approximately 9–10 million vehicles.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Elon Musk described Optimus Gen 3 as "special"** — no further technical details provided. Bhakdi estimates Gen 3 is approximately 90 days away (implying ~late July 2026).
- **Factory scale targets reiterated.** Fremont factory conversion is targeting 1 million Optimus units/year; a new factory near Giga Texas is planned at 10 million units/year capacity. Both are medium-term targets contingent on program execution.
- **Tesla opened an Autonomy Pop-Up in Miami (April 29–May 3) with Optimus on display** alongside autonomy demonstrations — a consumer-facing public engagement effort ahead of a likely broader commercial push.
- **Competitor Figure is manufacturing 55 humanoid robots per week (~1/hour)**, establishing a production rate benchmark against which Optimus's ramp can be measured.
