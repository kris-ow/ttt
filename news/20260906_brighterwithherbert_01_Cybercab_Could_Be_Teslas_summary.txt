Hosted by brighterwithherbert alongside guest Cern Basher (who attended the Tesla Cybercab event with him), this video covers the Tesla Cybercab launch event and the following morning's rides, focusing on newly disclosed fleet-growth data, vehicle design/economics disclosures from Tesla executives and engineers, and pushback against recent media claims about regulatory exemptions.

## Robotaxi
1. **Tesla discloses 1 million unsupervised autonomous miles and 17% weekly growth:** Tesla VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy said at the event that Tesla has surpassed 1 million unsupervised self-driving miles, and Cern Basher confirmed the week-over-week mileage growth rate at 17% (within the 10-15% range Elluswamy had previously guided on the earnings call). A third-party chart-based projection (attributed to "Genma JP") extrapolated this rate to a Q4 2027 EPS estimate of $2.55, adjusted for a production ceiling the analyst modeled using an assumed 125,000-annual-vehicle Cybercab production limit (Cern noted Tesla has not given official guidance on that capacity figure).
2. **Fleet-size projections through year-end 2026:** Using the 17% weekly growth rate and an assumption of ~150 miles/day per vehicle, Cern Basher's model projected roughly 137 active vehicles currently, growing to potentially a couple thousand vehicles by year-end 2026; under a more conservative 100 miles/day assumption, the model showed 2,000–3,000 vehicles by year-end. Cumulative mile projections under the same growth rate reached roughly 14.2 million miles by year-end 2026, up from about 1 million miles currently and a projected 3.5 million miles by the Q3 earnings call.
3. **Tesla solicits fleet-purchase interest via new form:** Tesla released a form asking whether people are interested in purchasing a Cybercab fleet, mobility hubs/infrastructure, or event collaboration, and their deployment region. Cern Basher called it a positive directional signal for gauging demand shape, but does not expect fleet sales to consumers/businesses to happen soon — his estimate was a year or a year and a half out, at minimum, and Herbert added it could be longer.
4. **Design philosophy: total lifetime cost over lowest cost of goods:** Cern Basher reported that Tesla engineers described designing Cybercab for lowest total lifetime cost rather than lowest unit cost, citing a target battery life of at least 500,000 miles (with some speculation among engineers of up to a million or more) and confirming most vehicle components are engineered to last that mileage, tires excepted. Cern noted his own financial models have generally already assumed 500,000-mile vehicle life for Cybercab, and said a Tesla robotaxi program lead (Eric Early) told the hosts the vehicle is designed to last five years or longer, not the roughly three years used in some outside forecasts.
5. **Cybercab drive unit and brake system disclosed at event:** Tesla described the Cybercab drive unit as using a simplified bar-wound stator, with a fully automated sub-10-second production cycle time, and stated it is smaller and lighter and more efficient than other top-performing EV drive units. Elon Musk stated the Cybercab motor uses no rare-earth metals while maintaining the same range, which Musk called extremely hard to achieve; Cern noted this also reduces supply-chain risk. Tesla also disclosed the vehicle uses individual electric actuators on each brake caliper with no brake lines or hydraulic fluid, which Musk said avoids the complexity of routing hydraulic plumbing and Cern suggested eases modular ("unboxed") manufacturing.
6. **Starlink connectivity and public rollout:** Cybercab is equipped with Starlink hardware (soon to be upgraded to V5), which Musk said will enable low-latency gaming and 4K video streaming even in areas with poor cellular service; the hosts noted Wi-Fi connectivity wasn't yet functional during their ride. As of the Friday following the event, Cybercab became available to hail anywhere in Austin at any time (no longer geofenced to the event area), and Tesla is reportedly working on 24/7 service, with Elluswamy indicating a "next month or so" software update tied to the V15 plan (memory and reasoning) would enable it, though neither host was certain what specific technical gap this addresses.
7. **Cybercab vs. Waymo cost/efficiency comparison:** A social media graphic compared $1 million worth of Cybercabs (at roughly $18,000 per vehicle, i.e., about 55–56 vehicles) versus $1 million worth of Waymo Gen 6 vehicles (at $125,000 each, about 8 vehicles), noting Waymo's per-vehicle cost is already roughly half its previous level. Cern noted the comparison doesn't capture vehicle lifetime differences. Herbert relayed that a Tesla robotaxi program lead confirmed his understanding that one Cybercab is operationally equivalent to roughly three Waymo vehicles, because Waymo vehicles need to return to charge after limited range while Cybercab can keep operating longer; Musk also replied that the cost comparison doesn't account for Cybercab's operational efficiency.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **New active collision avoidance feature:** Herbert noted Tesla has launched (or is launching) an always-on active collision avoidance feature that can take over the vehicle to avoid a collision regardless of whether Full Self-Driving is engaged; it was unclear from available information whether this is limited to vehicles with FSD purchased or available to all Tesla vehicles. Both hosts expressed hope Tesla makes it standard across all vehicles given its safety value.
2. **Pushback on Bloomberg reporting regarding regulatory exemption:** Cern Basher disputed a Bloomberg article (forwarded by analyst Gary Black) claiming Tesla has not applied for a needed federal exemption and would be capped at 2,500 vehicles once granted. Cern reiterated a position the two hosts say they confirmed as far back as March: Cybercab is self-certified and meets all federal motor vehicle safety standards without a steering wheel, pedals, or side mirrors, so no exemption (and no 2,500-vehicle cap) applies. Both hosts said Tesla engineering VP Lars Moravy and robotaxi program lead Eric Early reaffirmed this at the event, and that NHTSA has an ongoing collaborative relationship with Tesla rather than being blindsided by the Cybercab rollout, despite a reported news story about a new NHTSA file/investigation into Cybercab.
