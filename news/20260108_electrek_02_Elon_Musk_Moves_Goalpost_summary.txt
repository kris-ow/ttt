Electrek reports that Elon Musk has stated Tesla needs roughly 10 billion miles of driving data to achieve "safe unsupervised self-driving," a figure the author contrasts with Musk's earlier claims that unsupervised robotaxis would launch in Austin imminently, and with a previously stated threshold of 6 billion miles.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **New 10-billion-mile threshold:** Musk said Tesla needs about 10 billion miles of data to reach "safe unsupervised self-driving," a figure the article says is up from a previously stated 6-billion-mile threshold.
2. **Mileage gap at time of "unsupervised" claims:** The article states that in December 2025, Tesla's cumulative FSD mileage was about 7 billion miles — short of the newly cited 10-billion threshold — despite Musk previously saying unsupervised robotaxis would launch in Austin within weeks.
3. **Electrek's projected timeline:** Based on current fleet growth and FSD engagement rates, Electrek projects Tesla will cross the 10-billion-mile mark around July 2026, after which training (on Dojo and NVIDIA clusters), validation, and edge-case debugging would still be required — leading the author to estimate "safe unsupervised" FSD is likely at least another year away from that point, if not longer.

## Editorial Stance
The author is highly skeptical of Musk's shifting metrics, framing the new 10-billion-mile figure as a retroactive goalpost move that contradicts earlier claims and videos of Musk and Ashok Elluswamy driving what they described as unsupervised robotaxis in Austin in December 2025. The piece characterizes Musk's decade-long history of FSD timeline predictions as consistently and substantially wrong, and the "Electrek's Take" section accuses Musk of inconsistency bordering on dishonesty ("the problem with consistently lying is that you have to keep track of your lies").

Notable quotes and data:
- Musk's new claim: Tesla needs ~10 billion miles for "safe unsupervised self-driving."
- Prior Musk claim (per article): 6 billion miles needed.

- Electrek's projection: 10-billion-mile threshold reached around July 2026; full unsupervised capability likely "at least a full year" after that, per the author.
