No one programs AI
to recognize a
stop sign.  He learns
this by making mistakes a
million times.  So
how can mistakes even
teach a machine anything?    A
neural network is
layers of simple
mathematical nodes,
and each connection between
them has a number
called a weight.
Think of it as a
giant panel with
volume controls, there are
billions of them.  At the beginning,
they are configured
randomly, so
the network works
randomly.  Now you
show her
the image.  She
makes a guess: a
cat.  But it was a
stop sign.  So you
measure how
wrong she was.  This
number is called
the loss.  A big
mistake is a big
loss.  But now
you have a problem: one
number says it all
went wrong, and
billions of regulators could be to blame
.  Which
ones should I spin?  This is the
most interesting part.
Using
mathematical analysis,
you work your way back
through the network and, for
each regulator
, figure out which
way to adjust it
to reduce the loss.
This is called
error backpropagation.  Then you
turn
each knob up a little bit.  This is a
gradient descent.
One image, one
tiny
tweak that, by
itself,
does almost nothing.  But
do this billions of
times on millions of
examples, and these
random regulators
turn into
something that actually
works.  Nobody
wrote the rules.
The rules are in the scales.  And this is
not just some
theory from the audience.
Tesla says that in
version 12 of the autopilot system,
hundreds of thousands of lines of
handwritten
code have been replaced with neural
networks.  And Elon
demonstrated how
it drives in real
time.
Well, again, there's no
line of code that says
"stop at the
stop sign" or "wait for
another car."
This driving came about
thanks to regulators
set up in the same
way.  Guess,
measure the error,
tweak, repeat.  This is the
same basic idea behind
Grok, behind
Tesla's autopilot, behind
Optimus.  The only real
difference is scale.
More data, more
regulators, and much
more
computing
power—that's why
everyone is building
giant AI
clusters now.  And I think that's what the
whole race is about, because better
data and more
computation gives you
better tuning.
None of these systems
ever knew what a
stop sign was.
