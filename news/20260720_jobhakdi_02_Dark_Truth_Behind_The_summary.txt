The host uses Micron's valuation as a case study to argue Tesla could face the same fate even if robotaxi deployment succeeds spectacularly, and closes with a macro thesis about an approaching "event horizon" of AI-driven disruption around 2028-2029 that is driving market uncertainty regardless of underlying fundamentals.

He states he might be off by a month or two, or it could slip to Q1 2027, but insists Cybercab is currently in production and cites recent footage/engineering tests showing production Cybercabs without steering wheels as evidence.
2. **Hypothetical 2 million robotaxi scenario and valuation clash:** As an illustrative (not predictive) scenario, the host models Tesla deploying 2 million robotaxis by December at roughly $40,000–$50,000 in earnings per robotaxi (~$100 billion in additional earnings, about 20–25x current earnings), growing to 4 million robotaxis and $200 billion in earnings by 2027. He argues that if the market applied the same valuation logic it currently applies to Micron, Tesla's stock could actually fall — to roughly $350–$360 a share — even after such an announcement, illustrating what he calls market irrationality.
3. **TSLA trading near $370 ahead of earnings:** The host notes TSLA sliding into the $370 area heading into earnings, which he says is happening despite what he views as genuine robotaxi progress.

## Financials
1. **Micron valuation cited as an "inverse bubble" example:** The host cites a UBS note stating Micron could repurchase over 40% of its shares by the end of 2028, once its buyback restriction expires in December 2026, and that UBS expects Micron to generate over $400 billion in free cash flow through 2028 — against a roughly $1 trillion market cap. He notes Micron trades near 6x earnings while growing earnings over 100% a year, and uses this to argue AI-adjacent hyper-growth companies are being valued at unusually low multiples rather than in a bubble.
2. **Nvidia valuation compared to dot-com era Cisco:** The host disputes a YouTube video's comparison of Nvidia to Cisco at the peak of the dot-com bubble (Cisco allegedly traded at 120x earnings), pointing out Nvidia currently trades around a 17x forward P/E, which he argues is one of the lowest valuations for the company in a long time and undermines the "AI bubble" narrative.
