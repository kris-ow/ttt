Elon Musk claimed at the Smart Mobility Summit in Tel Aviv (via video link) that Tesla's unsupervised FSD will be "widespread in the US by the end of this year" — a statement the article frames against a decade of missed autonomous driving timelines and a current unsupervised fleet of approximately 30 vehicles across three Texas cities.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Musk's "widespread" unsupervised FSD promise:** Speaking at the Smart Mobility Summit, Musk said Tesla already has "some vehicles operating with no people inside and no safety monitors in three cities in Texas" and that the service will "probably be widespread in the US by the end of this year." He also described FSD in vivid terms, saying "the car feels like it is sentient" and that drivers can "feel the sentience growing in the car."

2. **Contradictory safety claims:** At the Summit, Musk described FSD as being on a "path" to being "at least an order of magnitude safer than humans." This conflicts with a statement he made on X six weeks prior claiming FSD is already "10X safer" than human drivers and that "the statistics are unequivocal." The article notes these two positions cannot simultaneously be true.

3. **Tesla's safety data criticized:** The article details methodological flaws in Tesla's Vehicle Safety Report: FSD/Autopilot usage is concentrated on highways (already the safest road type), Tesla only counts airbag-deployment crashes while NHTSA baselines include all police-reported crashes, and Tesla's driver/vehicle demographics skew toward lower-crash profiles. Tesla also suspended publishing these reports for over a year, and upon resumption, Autopilot safety data had regressed. Waymo, by contrast, has published peer-reviewed safety data with matched baselines independently verified by insurers.

4. **Timeline history and recent goalposts:** A documented list of missed promises spans 2015–2026. Most recently: Musk promised unsupervised FSD by June 2025; the Austin robotaxi launch occurred but with safety monitors. In January 2026, Musk raised the data threshold for safe unsupervised driving from 6 billion to 10 billion miles. During Q1 2026 earnings (April 2026), he pushed unsupervised FSD for consumer vehicles to Q4 2026 "at the earliest," citing "complex intersections," "bad road markings," and "weather challenges." The current Summit claim came less than a month after that admission.

## Robotaxi
1. **Current unsupervised fleet size:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla's unsupervised fleet stands at approximately 30 vehicles across Austin, Dallas (5 vehicles), and Houston (6 vehicles). The article argues that scaling from ~30 vehicles in three Texas cities to "widespread" US coverage in seven months would require an expansion rate Tesla has shown no evidence of being capable of.

## Editorial Stance
The author characterizes Musk's promise as part of a predictable, decade-long pattern of missed deadlines and goalpost-shifting, highlighting the contradiction between his safety claims depending on audience. The author does, however, agree with Musk's broader claim that the majority of vehicle mileage will be autonomous within 5–10 years.
