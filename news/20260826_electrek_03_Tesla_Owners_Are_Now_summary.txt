The article reports on a trend of Tesla owners filming themselves misusing Full Self-Driving (sleeping, gaming, watching TV, working on laptops while the car drives) and posting the videos for social-media attention, arguing this exposes both a driver-behavior problem and a legal/liability risk for Tesla.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Viral misuse videos:** Multiple examples circulating on Instagram, TikTok, and X show Tesla drivers asleep at highway speed, gaming with a controller, streaming live TV, or typing on a laptop while FSD operates the vehicle, often captioned to boast about what the car "lets" them do.
2. **Methods to defeat driver monitoring:** Owners are reportedly using tricks — a weight on the steering wheel, or a folded bill placed over the cabin camera — to bypass Tesla's driver-attention monitoring (steering-wheel torque plus eye-tracking "strikes" that can suspend FSD for a week). The article also says apps and dongles are being marketed to unlock video playback while driving, defeating Tesla's "video in motion" lockout.
3. **Tesla's monitoring changes in 2026:** The article states Tesla has been tightening camera-based driver monitoring throughout 2026, including moving toward using the cabin camera to verify driver identity before FSD engages.
4. **Legal status of FSD:** The piece emphasizes Tesla markets the feature as "FSD (Supervised)" with a disclaimer that it requires active driver supervision and does not make the vehicle autonomous, meaning the driver remains legally responsible; the article states behaviors shown in the videos (sleeping, gaming, watching screens) constitute illegal distracted/careless driving in essentially every jurisdiction where the clips were filmed.
5. **Musk comment on texting:** The article notes Elon Musk conceded last year that texting behind the wheel of a Tesla is "illegal in most states," made while promoting software that eases doing so.
6. **Legal and safety exposure figures:** The article cites Tesla facing up to $14.5 billion in lawsuits tied to driver-assist systems, a $243 million verdict lost over a fatal Autopilot crash, and a NHTSA probe escalated to an engineering analysis covering 3.2 million vehicles. It also states Tesla's own data shows Autopilot/FSD crashes hit a record 207 in a single month, and that Tesla has reported to NHTSA that over 50 people have died in crashes involving Autopilot and FSD.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the viral videos represent gambling with lives for engagement, and blames Tesla for a decade of marketing that shows hands-off/inattentive use of FSD while shipping weak driver-monitoring (defeatable by a folded bill) and then relying on legal fine print to blame drivers after crashes. He contends Tesla benefits from these videos both as promotion and as self-produced evidence against drivers in litigation.
