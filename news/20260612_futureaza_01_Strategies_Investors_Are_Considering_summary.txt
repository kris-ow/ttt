This video from FutureAzA covers the SpaceX IPO on its opening day, exploring valuation concerns, trading dynamics, ETF strategies, and S&P 500 inclusion prospects. Host Brian interviews Will Rind, founder and CEO of GraniteShares, an ETF issuer with 25 years of industry experience, to break down what investors should expect and how they might position themselves around the historic listing.

## Market & Competition

1. **Largest IPO in history:** Will Rind confirms that the SpaceX IPO is the largest IPO ever from a historical and financial markets perspective, breaking all prior records. The IPO priced at $135 per share, with approximately 7–8% of the company floated — a relatively small free float in percentage terms, though still a large absolute dollar amount for the market to absorb.

2. **Oversubscription and retail allocation:** The IPO is reported to be approximately four times oversubscribed, meaning demand significantly exceeds the supply of available shares. Roughly 30% of the float has reportedly been allocated to retail investors, though individual allocations may be minimal — analogous to cases where retail participants requested hundreds of shares and received only one. Rind notes that international capital, including from Japan, is contributing to demand, making this a global phenomenon rather than a US-only story.

3. **Day-one volatility expectations:** Rind expects significant volatility on the first day of trading. On the bullish side, the small float and excess demand could push the stock meaningfully higher — he floats the possibility of the stock reaching $200 from the $135 IPO price. On the bearish side, early investors who acquired shares over the years and are not subject to lockup restrictions could sell, creating downward pressure. Rind estimates a realistic swing of 10–20% in either direction, though he considers a 50% move extreme given the company's size and valuation.

4. **Lockup overhang as key risk:** A key uncertainty flagged is the behavior of pre-IPO investors — not current employees subject to lockup agreements, but former employees and early backers who hold shares with no lockup restrictions. Their selling behavior is described as the primary bearish overhang on the stock in the near term. The host notes that property values in Brownsville, Texas may rise as newly liquid SpaceX employees and insiders monetize their holdings.

5. **GraniteShares launching leveraged SpaceX ETFs:** GraniteShares is launching two leveraged ETFs on Monday following the IPO: **SPAL** (2x long SpaceX) and **SNK** (2x short SpaceX). These products are designed to give retail investors leveraged exposure to SpaceX's stock price movements in either direction, catering to both bulls and bears looking to trade around the IPO.

## Financials

1. **Valuation described as extremely high by any fundamental metric:** Rind states that SpaceX is trading at roughly 70 times revenue at the IPO valuation, compared to Nvidia — which he describes as one of the most profitable companies in the world with arguably the strongest AI moat — trading at approximately 22 times revenue. He argues that by any traditional fundamental measure, the valuation is difficult to justify, and that buyers must be investing based on future growth potential rather than current financials.

2. **SpaceX framed as a "future belief" story, analogous to Tesla:** Rind draws a direct parallel to Tesla, saying that SpaceX, like Tesla historically, is not a valuation story but a belief story centered on future revenue lines — Starlink, AI infrastructure (including recently announced deals with Google and Anthropic for AI compute), and the broader rocket and space opportunity. He suggests that investors focused strictly on price-to-earnings ratios will struggle to justify participation, while those with conviction in the company's future state are the natural buyers.

3. **S&P 500 inclusion delayed by approximately 12 months:** The S&P 500 index committee — which Rind clarifies operates via human decision-making rather than a purely rules-based mechanism — has opted not to immediately include SpaceX, instead imposing a seasoning period of approximately 12 months. Rind notes this removes a near-term passive buying catalyst (since passive funds must buy index constituents regardless of price), but describes the delay as reasonable given the early-stage volatility of the IPO. He views eventual S&P inclusion as a longer-term bullish factor but cautions that index inclusion alone should not be the primary reason to buy.
