Tesla renamed "Full Self-Driving" to "Tesla Assisted Driving" in Germany and across its German website, following pressure from Germany's Federal Ministry of Transport, which had called the FSD name misleading.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Name change follows German regulatory pressure:** Germany's Federal Ministry of Transport (BMV) stated on October 6 that "FSD – Full Self-Driving Supervised" was misleading since the driver must remain attentive and fully responsible. Tesla offered the new name "Tesla Assisted Driving" during talks with Transport Minister Steffen Bilger in September, per the ministry.

2. **Speed concession tied to EU approval support:** Germany and Tesla agreed the system can go up to 10% over the speed limit, a position Germany will back in the EU approval process; Tesla will need to modify FSD to comply since it currently often exceeds that threshold. The article notes this is a real concession, citing Reuters reporting that the Netherlands-approved version allows speeds up to 50% over the detected limit. In return, Bilger said he wants Tesla drivers able to use the system across the EU as quickly as possible, and Musk thanked Germany on X with "Danke schoen."

3. **Rebrand is largely cosmetic:** The new German page is a translated version of Tesla's US FSD page with only the product name swapped; it retains claims the car will drive "almost anywhere" under active supervision, the "7x safer" claim (measured against Tesla's own estimated US average per its footnote), and promotion of Robotaxi and Cybercab under a "The future is autonomous" section. In the US, tesla.com/fsd still sells "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" for $99/month.

4. **EU approval status and timeline:** Tesla's page lists eight EU countries where the system is approved: Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia, Czechia, and Croatia; Germany is not yet on the list. The EU-wide approval vote has reportedly slipped to December at the earliest, requiring 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of the EU population; France and Sweden have raised speeding-related concerns.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the "Tesla Assisted Driving" name is the honest one and should be used everywhere, criticizing "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" as self-contradictory. He notes Tesla has missed self-driving timeline targets (citing 2016 hardware claims and Musk's 2019 "feature complete"/robotaxi predictions) and references a California judge's December ruling that called the FSD name "unambiguously false and counterfactual." He contends Tesla only dropped the name in Europe because a regulator it needed demanded it, and asks why the same change hasn't happened in the US.
