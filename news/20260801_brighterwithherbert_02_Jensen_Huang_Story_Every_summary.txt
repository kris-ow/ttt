This video from brighterwithherbert uses Jensen Huang's account of Nvidia's history (from a How I Built This podcast episode) as an extended analogy for Tesla's investment thesis, arguing that Tesla is currently in a "flat stock, compounding platform" phase similar to Nvidia's multi-year sideways stretch before CUDA paid off — while also examining the Elon Musk–Jensen Huang relationship and Nvidia's compute allocation to Tesla/SpaceX-linked projects.

## Financials
1. **Tesla Q2 earnings recap:** The host cites Tesla's Q2 earnings (reported July 22) showing revenue of $28.2 billion, up 26% year-over-year and beating estimates, while EPS missed at $0.33 versus an expected $0.49. Capital expenditure was $5.79 billion, up 142% year-over-year, which the host frames as the reason for the earnings miss — money going into future platforms (Optimus, FSD/AI infrastructure) rather than near-term profit.
2. **TSLA trading roughly flat over five years:** The host notes TSLA trading around the mid-$300s to $390 range at time of recording, comparing it to where the stock first traded in late 2020, framing this as a five-year sideways stretch analogous to Nvidia's six-to-seven-year flat period from roughly 2009–2015.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi service footprint cited as evidence of platform growth:** The host points to Tesla robotaxi operations running in Austin, Dallas, Houston, and three Florida cities as public evidence of usage building beneath a flat stock price, alongside FSD subscriptions (~1.5 million, up 56% year-over-year) and energy storage deployment (13.5 GWh in a quarter) and 50% services revenue growth with record margins.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus framed as a "zero billion dollar market" bet:** The host argues Tesla is pouring multiple billions into Optimus production this year for a robot with no mass market yet today, directly comparing it to Nvidia's pre-CUDA-adoption spending on a market that didn't exist.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD-capable hardware as installed-base strategy:** The host compares Tesla shipping FSD-capable hardware in every car (regardless of software purchase) to Nvidia embedding CUDA into every GeForce chip years before a market existed — both described as "seeding the installed base" strategies.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Colossus supercomputer buildout speed praised by Jensen Huang:** Per Elon Musk's own account (referenced by the host), xAI's Colossus supercomputer in Memphis, built with 100,000 Nvidia H100 chips, went from start to finish in 122 days, with only 19 days between hardware arrival and training start. Jensen Huang, speaking on the BG2 podcast, called this "superhuman" and said, "As far as I know, there's only one person in the world who could do that," praising Musk's understanding of engineering and large-scale construction.
2. **Priority Nvidia chip allocation to Musk ventures:** The host states that in December 2024, Musk secured priority delivery of roughly $1.08 billion worth of Nvidia GB200 systems by paying a premium, and that Colossus 2 is now a multi-gigawatt campus running hundreds of thousands of Nvidia GPUs including newer GB300 chips. The host explicitly notes that a separately circulated claim of a $52 billion SpaceX order for a million Nvidia chips was called "fake news" by Musk himself and is excluded from the analysis as unverified.
3. **Host's theory on why Nvidia prioritizes Musk:** The host offers a personal theory (not sourced from the transcript's interview material) that Jensen Huang prioritizes Musk's orders for three reasons: Musk's deployments serve as effective advertising for new Nvidia chip generations, Tesla/SpaceX are seen as financially reliable large-order buyers, and the two are described as friends with parallel founder histories. The host raises an open, unresolved question about how this dynamic might change as Tesla ramps its own AI5 chips and potentially needs Nvidia less.

## Market & Competition
1. **Nvidia's near-death origin story:** Per Jensen Huang's account, Nvidia's first chip (the NV1) failed commercially after betting on a graphics approach the industry didn't adopt, leaving the company with about 30 days of runway and roughly $30,000 in the bank; Huang flew to Japan and convinced Sega — holder of a $12 million contract — to convert $5 million of that contract into an equity stake, which kept Nvidia solvent.
2. **CUDA as a decade-long, initially money-losing bet:** Per Huang, Nvidia began embedding CUDA (general-purpose computing capability) into every GeForce gaming chip starting in 2006, at added cost per unit, years before a market for it existed — a strategy Huang calls addressing "zero billion dollar markets." He says this cost north of $1 billion in development, and Nvidia's market cap stayed roughly flat (around $8.5–10 billion) from roughly 2009 to 2015 while Wall Street analysts pushed for spending cuts, criticizing it as misallocated capital.
3. **Nvidia's eventual payoff:** The host, citing analyst Beth Kindig's posted figures, states Nvidia returned approximately 26,420% over the ten years through 2025 (a roughly 75% compound annual growth rate), with the inflection triggered around 2012 when AI researchers found GPUs were the most cost-effective hardware for training neural networks, making CUDA suddenly essential.
4. **Tesla's 2019 precedent cited as historical parallel:** The host recounts Tesla's 2014–2019 sideways trading range (roughly $180–$390 pre-split), including 2017–2018 Model 3 "production hell" and a scary early-2019 stretch with a $1 billion bond payment, layoffs, and store closures. The turning point came in an October 2019 earnings report showing a $143 million profit ($1.86 per share) against an expected loss, plus news that the Shanghai factory was built in ten months at roughly 65% lower per-unit capacity cost than the U.S. line — after which the stock rose about 20% overnight and roughly 10x over the following 18 months.
5. **Bear case acknowledged:** The host raises counterarguments including that not every long-duration bet succeeds (citing GE's power/digital investments as a value-destroying counterexample), that Musk's timelines and targets (e.g., a stated goal of a million robots per year) often slip, and that Tesla investors should not assume the "inflection" will arrive on any particular schedule — explicitly stating that further multi-year flat stretches are possible.
