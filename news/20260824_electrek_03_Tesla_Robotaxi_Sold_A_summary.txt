The article documents multiple viral, first-hand accounts of failed Tesla Robotaxi rides in Texas from self-identified Tesla fans, including botched routing, missed pickups/drop-offs, and reliance on remote human intervention.

## Robotaxi
1. **66-minute estimate for a 2.3-mile trip:** Tesla Ambassador Reggie Overton posted on August 22 that a Robotaxi trip estimated 2.3 miles took an estimated 1 hour 6 minutes; support called it a bug, the estimate briefly dropped to 16 minutes with no route change, then rose back to 57 minutes. Support said it could not reroute the vehicle and suggested he "leave a review." The group exited and called an Uber; Overton says he initially received no refund. The post reached over 630,000 views, after which Tesla's official Robotaxi account replied that a refund had been issued "earlier today" and it would "continue to improve the rider experience at every level" (direct quotes from Tesla's response).

2. **Running log of failures from a repeat rider:** An account called TexasTSLA, self-described as a four-time Tesla owner riding within Tesla's approved Dallas geofence, documented several incidents: on August 17 a Robotaxi dropped him 3 miles from his car late at night, with support saying nothing could be done since it was the end of the driver-support shift; the app separately displayed a message saying his destination exceeded the vehicle's remaining service time at 9:30 p.m. On August 19, of three rides taken in one day, one required rider support to "manually drive" the vehicle to the destination after it missed the drop-off, and another repeatedly failed to stop at the destination, forcing the group to walk. On August 21 he filmed a Robotaxi stuck in the middle of the road while his own vehicle missed drop-offs; he stated he is "done taking these rides." A separate pinned post from Tesla's April Dallas launch showed a Robotaxi missing a highway exit and attempting to pull over in fast highway traffic.

3. **Context cited by the article:** The piece notes Tesla launched Robotaxi in Dallas and Houston with small geofenced areas in April, and references prior reporting on wrong drop-offs, highway avoidance, and remote-attendant interventions. It states Waymo runs more than 500,000 paid, driverless rides weekly across 10+ U.S. cities, and notes Tesla's Cybercab event is scheduled for September 3 in Austin.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that these complaints, coming from Tesla's most loyal fans, indicate a real problem; he notes having seen roughly a dozen similar incidents in the past week despite what he describes as low reported usage. He states Tesla confirmed 380,000 unsupervised miles from January to July, which he calculates as roughly 15,000 miles per week, and argues that volume of incidents at that low mileage is concerning. He characterizes the refund given to Overton only after the post went viral as "PR triage" rather than a scalable support process, and concludes that Robotaxi's driving may be improving but the surrounding service (routing, pickups, drop-offs, and reliance on remote human intervention) is not ready to be a paid product.
