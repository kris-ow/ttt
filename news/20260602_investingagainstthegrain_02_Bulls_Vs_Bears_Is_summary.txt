This video from the "investingagainstthegrain" channel addresses the wave of bearish market commentary expected as the stock market sits at all-time highs entering June 2026. The host argues that most widely-cited valuation indicators are noise rather than signal, and walks viewers through a momentum-based framework — the BX Trender — as a more actionable tool for timing market exposure. The core thesis is that investors should focus on monthly momentum indicators and the yield curve rather than headline-grabbing metrics like the Buffett Indicator or CAPE ratio.

## Market & Competition

1. **Buffett Indicator dismissed as noise:** The host explains that the Buffett Indicator — which compares total U.S. stock market capitalization to U.S. GDP — is currently sitting at 237%, an all-time high. However, he argues it is fundamentally flawed because it only captures domestic GDP while roughly 40% of corporate profits now come from overseas. He dismisses it as a metric that generates clicks primarily because of Warren Buffett's name, not because of genuine predictive value.

2. **CAPE Ratio also characterized as unreliable:** The Cyclically Adjusted Price-to-Earnings (CAPE, or Shiller) ratio is currently at 42.7 — second only to the dot-com bubble peak. The host acknowledges this sounds alarming but argues the metric fails to account for productivity gains driven by AI, software, and SaaS, and is distorted by COVID-era economic anomalies from 2022. Like the Buffett Indicator, it only reflects the U.S. market and ignores global context.

3. **Margin debt and credit metrics labeled cherry-picked:** Margin debt is currently at a historic $1.3 trillion, and the host notes that credit card debt and delinquency rates are also at record highs. He argues these figures are misleading in isolation because wealth, money market balances, home values, and virtually every financial aggregate are also at all-time highs — a natural consequence of a larger, inflation-affected economy. He characterizes the selective use of these figures as fearmongering.

4. **Inverted yield curve identified as the one legitimate signal:** The host singles out the inverted yield curve — where short-term yields (e.g., the 2-year) exceed long-term yields (e.g., the 10-year) — as the only macro indicator with a consistent track record, having predicted every recession since the 1950s. He notes a key caveat: during the lead-up to the Great Recession, the yield curve inverted at the end of 2006 but the market didn't peak until approximately 18 months later in 2007, meaning early action would have felt premature. As of the video's publication, the yield curve is not inverted — the 2-year sits at approximately 4% and the 10-year at approximately 4.45%, which the host describes as healthy and normal.

5. **BX Trender (monthly) presented as the primary actionable tool:** The host introduces the BX Trender, used on a monthly timeframe, as his preferred momentum indicator. He walks through three historical case studies: (1) January 2022 — the monthly turned red at S&P 4,778, and a sell signal would have avoided a drop to approximately 3,200–3,300; (2) COVID crash — the monthly turned red near a peak of approximately 3,382, and following the signal would have avoided approximately a 25% S&P decline; (3) October 2007 — the monthly turned red the following month after the Great Recession peak near 1,600, and exiting around 1,400 would have avoided a 50–60% market collapse. He also demonstrates how the transition from dark red to lighter red bars signals upward momentum reversal.

6. **Current market momentum assessed as healthy:** As of the end of May 2026, the monthly BX Trender has printed green. The prior month (April) printed light red, and March before it printed dark red — a sequence the host says correctly tracked the rough March followed by strong April and May. He states he is not currently worried about the market based on this indicator. His personal risk-management plan, as a self-described all-in Tesla investor, is to sell covered calls if the monthly BX Trender turns red, as a defensive hedge on his holdings.

7. **RSI dismissed alongside other overbought indicators:** The host warns that the Relative Strength Index (RSI) suffers from the same flaw as the CAPE and Buffett indicators — it can remain in "overbought" territory for extended periods without being actionable. He cites the example of the RSI staying above 70 from approximately mid-1996 through mid-1998 to 1999, a stretch during which exiting the market based on RSI would have been a costly mistake.
