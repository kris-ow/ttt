## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab first responder guide published on Texas DPS website — final pre-deployment hurdle cleared.** The guide confirms key specs: 400V HV battery, 48V low-voltage Etherloop architecture, 10 airbags, active pedestrian hood system (15–32 mph), two-way B-pillar speakers, no wireless charging. Loomis notes that when Tesla published the Model Y first responder guide, commercial deployment followed within weeks — though the initial Cybercab fleet size is expected to be under 10 vehicles for at least the first week or two.

- **Lars Moravy confirms Cybercab production lines installed, running, and automation ahead of schedule.** Lines are over 90% automated end-to-end. The final assembly line has roughly 20–25 cells versus ~200 for a conventional general assembly. When pressed on the reported ~150-unit burst build count, Moravy said: "I'll stick with that number." Most units produced so far have entered the Autopilot training fleet; Tesla has just begun burn-in testing on the dedicated track at the Giga Texas campus edge.

- **Moravy teases a major announcement on July 7, 2026 related to scaling activity at Giga Texas** — twice in the interview. He declined to elaborate. The most widely held interpretation is a Cybercab deployment or production milestone, though Moravy left the door open to other scaling initiatives.

- **Cybercab stockpiling visible across Houston, Dallas, and Phoenix — but no deployment decals.** 78 Cybercabs have been spotted in Houston staging lots (per SE Robinson), with dozens more visible in Dallas (per a Reddit image cited by Merritt). Per the first responder guide, the Cybercab wordmark decal is present on vehicles in active robotaxi service; the absence of decals on all staged vehicles indicates none are yet deployment-cleared in those cities.

- **Cybercab Houston launch rumored for mid-to-late July**, per Loomis citing Kobe Pierce and a "very reputable source." The reported service area would be roughly 50% larger than Waymo's current Houston footprint and could encompass NRG Stadium and George Bush Intercontinental Airport. Flagged as speculative.

- **Active Robotaxi fleet: 749 vehicles across Bay Area, Austin, Dallas, and Houston**, per RobotaxiTracker.com (as of 2026-07-01). Austin now has 84 registered robotaxi vehicles per the tracker, with 15 added on June 27th.

- **Tesla missed its self-imposed H1 2026 target to launch in five new cities** (Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas). The Q1 2026 slide deck had downgraded the goal to "preparations underway." Loomis attributes the delay to Tesla internally deciding to wait for FSD V15 before expanding geographies.

- **Cybercab unit economics will "blow minds," Moravy says.** He argues published analyst outlooks — including Ark and Wells Fargo — don't understand the operational cost efficiency of Tesla's vertically integrated stack (raw materials through charging through software), and predicts real-world cost-per-mile figures will surprise the Street when they emerge.

- **Waymo exits Uber's Phoenix app, ending a nearly three-year partnership.** Waymo framed it as a "productive pilot" and said Phoenix vehicles have been absorbed into its own app fleet. Waymo remains on Uber in Austin and Atlanta; the two will compete directly in London later in 2026. Uber is teasing an unnamed new autonomous partner for Phoenix — speculation has centered on Lucid/Mobileye, not Tesla, per Loomis.

- **VIN-based community analysis (attributed to "Xander") estimates ~850 Cybercabs may have been produced**, up significantly from earlier estimates of 100–200. Moravy's confirmed figure on record remains ~150 from the burst build; the gap between the two figures is unresolved.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD V14 Lite begins rolling out to Hardware 3 early access customers**, with broader rollout expected over the following weeks. The update distills HW4 V14 driving behavior into HW3's camera and compute configuration via a knowledge-distillation approach. Elon Musk congratulated the AI team on X, noting HW3 has only ~15% of HW4's effective memory bandwidth — making the port technically demanding. New features include speed profiles, start-from-park capability, destination arrival options, and significantly improved safety. Missing versus HW4: MadMax profile, FSD stats app, streak counters, and unified smart summon.

- **HW3 fleet monetization runway is large.** The host estimates 3–4 million HW3 vehicles exist; fewer than 1% had V14 Lite at time of recording. FSD subscriptions are highly margin-accretive. A free 30-day trial to drive conversions is anticipated once the broad rollout completes.

- **FSD correctly responded to a police officer directing traffic**, stopping at a green light and proceeding only after the officer waved the vehicle through — apparently anticipating the signal slightly before it was given, per a video shared by DJ.

- **Moravy describes "Full Self-Hearing" as a live manufacturing quality tool.** Tesla vehicles now drive themselves end-of-line to the logistics yard and independently traverse the bump, squeak, and rattle track, using onboard microphones to detect noise anomalies and feed findings back to the production team in near-real time.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus production line 1 has been delivered and installation has begun at Fremont**, where Model S/X production has ended. The automation equipment was built by Tesla's own teams in the Midwest and Germany; units are in final field acceptance testing before commissioning. Moravy: "The first line has landed… we've already started to install that."

- **Approximately 40 more Optimus production lines remain to be installed**, per Moravy. The high line count reflects the many subcomponent lines — actuators, limbs, torso, battery, and other subassemblies — feeding into a final assembly in a tributary layout Moravy likens to the Unboxed manufacturing philosophy. A completed line can theoretically be taken down, shipped, and recommissioned in roughly one week.

- **Moravy cites three structural advantages for Tesla in humanoid robotics:** (1) scale manufacturing expertise, (2) proprietary high-power-density electric motors and actuators unavailable to competitors, and (3) real-world AI implementation. He pushes back on analogies to consumer electronics, arguing functional safety requirements and multi-axis mechanical complexity make humanoid robots far more like vehicles.

- **"Limbic resonance" is the design mandate for first-generation Optimus**, per Elon Musk's direction to Moravy. The robot must feel natural and non-threatening in human spaces before any non-humanoid form factors are pursued.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Giga Berlin is expanding in two stages**: from 5,000 to 6,200 vehicles/week beginning in July (1,000 new hires), then further to 7,500 vehicles/week ramping from October (another ~1,000 hires), totaling approximately 3,500 additional employees in 2026. This is occurring simultaneously with Volkswagen closing four German factories and cutting up to 100,000 jobs.

- **A camouflaged Model Y L was spotted in Palo Alto on 19- or 20-inch wheels**, with observers describing a US launch as imminent. The Model Y L has already launched in other global markets; North America is the last region to receive it. Jeff Lutz suggests it could enter North American production this quarter and become a major volume driver through the remainder of 2026, citing Americans' demonstrated preference for larger vehicles.

- **Model Y refresh across four factories was completed in approximately three to four weeks**, per Moravy, who calls it the largest automated product changeover at that scale since Ford's Model T-to-Model A transition (which took six months under different conditions).

- **Q2 2026 delivery consensus stands at ~406,000 units** (Tesla's published figure); Polymarket implies ~450,000; FutureAzA's own estimate is ~490,000 or higher. The delivery report is expected Thursday, July 3, 2026. FutureAzA's production estimate is 420,420 units, with deliveries of 431,210 — the higher delivery figure reflecting expected drawdown of ~50,000 units of Q1 inventory overhang.

## Financials

The position is one leg of a broader basket also shorting Nvidia (at $198.09), Caterpillar ($1,060.98), SOXX ($642.80), and Applied Materials ($729.40). FutureAzA attributes roughly 4 percentage points to macro beta (Tesla typically runs ~2x NASDAQ), with the remainder likely reflecting beaten-down catch-up buying ahead of the Q2 delivery report and anticipation of a strong delivery beat.

- **Q2 2026 energy deployment consensus of 13.8 GWh flagged as likely too low.** Q1 2026 came in at 8.8 GWh; the Leipzig Megapack factory alone can produce ~10 GWh. FutureAzA attributes some gap to logistics: units must be shipped and installed before counting as deployed.

## Market & Competition

- **VW is ending its autonomous driving partnership with Bosch** after approximately $1.7 billion invested, with internal assessments concluding the technology was not competitive — specifically citing Tesla's FSD as the benchmark, per Loomis. This follows VW's announcement of potential 100,000-job cuts and closure of four German factories. VW is said to be seeking a new hardware/software partner; Nvidia is the most commonly speculated candidate.

- **JD Power Initial Quality Study released ~two days before the interview: Tesla ranked number three**, per Moravy. He attributes the result partly to AI-driven quality anomaly detection that connects upstream manufacturing data to downstream quality signals in near-real time.

- **Grok 4.5 entered private beta for internal use at SpaceX and Tesla**, per Bhakdi citing a Musk tweet. The model is described as a 1.5-trillion-parameter version 9 foundation with Cursor training data. Separately, SpaceX AI reportedly acquired Cursor — the leading enterprise AI coding platform — for $60 billion, giving the Grok ecosystem visibility into enterprise coding workflows across customers including Nvidia, Stripe, Adobe, and OpenAI. Bhakdi frames the acquisition as primarily a training-data play targeting a $22 trillion enterprise AGI TAM cited in SpaceX internal documents.

- **Burry's TSLA short framing vs. Electrek's editorial view:** Electrek author Fred Lambert agrees Tesla is "grossly overvalued" (~295x earnings, ~3.6% annual dilution) but calls shorting it "super risky" given hype-driven price dynamics and a potential SpaceX–Tesla merger narrative. Lambert views Optimus as "vaporware."
