## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck production winding down at Giga Texas around mid-2027 to free capacity for Cybercab, Musk confirmed. Tesla simultaneously raised U.S. Cybertruck prices up to 9% (Premium AWD $84,990→$92,490; Cyberbeast +2.5% to $102,490) and paused new orders on the base $75k Dual-Motor AWD trim. Per sawyermerritt, Tesla sold roughly 8,000 Cybertrucks in Q3; electrified's host pegs installed Cybertruck capacity above 125,000 units/year against actual sales near 25,000, calling the line likely never profitable.
- Model Y was China's best-selling car of any type in September (~50,000 units), per Musk at the Baron conference, also topping charts in March and June.
- Giga Shanghai local supply-chain localization hit 98%, up from 95%, with over 400 local tier-1 suppliers (60+ now in Tesla's global supply chain).
- Tesla will launch sales in Argentina on November 20, its fourth South American market after Chile, Colombia and Uruguay.
- EVgo opened its first Chicago-area Supercharger-network station (12 stalls, Magic Dock-enabled for CCS vehicles), part of a push toward 500 NACS stalls nationwide by year-end.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's registered Texas robotaxi count (~1,244) is closing in on Waymo's (~1,259), a gap of about 15 vehicles; one host expects Tesla to overtake Waymo within one to two weeks, though another argues 2,000 vehicles is the more meaningful threshold.
- Tesla executives said on the earnings call they're "hyper-focused" on a ~30 cents/mile cost target, aiming eventually for rides under a dollar; Ashok Elluswamy reportedly said twice Tesla looks forward to deploying "millions" of robotaxis in coming months, contingent on regulatory approval.
- Nevada has reportedly issued Tesla a permit for up to 5,000 units, with a 90-day launch rule implying a Las Vegas launch before year-end.
- Tesla expanded robotaxi operating hours and, per Elluswamy, is enabling highway operation, higher speeds, and wider weather conditions.
- Cybertruck-derived manufacturing techniques (including structural battery pack learnings) are reportedly being applied to Cybercab production.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Musk said SpaceX could exceed $100B annualized revenue by this December and floated (as "best guess, not a promise") ARR topping $500B by December 2027, with a 2% chance of eventually hitting $1T annually. SpaceX's CFO has separately guided $33–37B for next year's Q1; JP Morgan's most bullish 2027 full-year estimate is about $254B — a materially lower figure, though on a different basis (full-year vs. December run-rate).
- SpaceX revenue grew from ~$7.8B to ~$14.6B (Q2→Q3, +80%+), with Musk guiding ~70% growth this quarter to ~$25B; over half of Q3 revenue (~$7.6B) came from SpaceX's AI/compute business.
- A new SpaceX data center campus (via applicant "Tombstone Resources," confirmed by SpaceX) is proposed in Cochise County, Arizona: 6 GW full buildout across nine buildings, 1.5 GW first phase targeted for H2 next year at a stated $50B cost, with 24 GWh of battery storage. A SpaceX representative reportedly told a public meeting the project would cut local residential electricity bills up to 40% (per a third-party account) and contribute $53M/year to education; a county hearing is set for December 9.
- SpaceX board member Don Harrison stepped down effective November 4 after over a decade of service.
- Tesla's shutdown-merger speculation was pushed back on by electrified's host, who argues the DOJ/FTC antitrust review remains the key hurdle rather than midterm politics, though he flags a Tesla shareholder vote requiring over 50% of all outstanding shares as a bigger structural risk.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's next flight (Flight 15, "Ship 42") is targeted for Friday, November 13, attempting to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the ship simultaneously — which would be the first time all parts of an orbital rocket returned to Earth intact. Musk called it a critical step toward reusability and lowering launch costs below $100/kg.
- Separately, documentary footage (farzad-fm) detailed an earlier orbital flight (14/LVN2) that reached orbit despite an engine anomaly, deployed 26 Starlink satellites — Starship's first operational payload delivery — and completed a long-duration coast and controlled splashdown near Hawaii, validating reentry survivability.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Musk said Starlink V4 satellites will carry ~100 laser links each at over 10 terabits/second, roughly 10x V3's capability (V3 itself already ~10x V2 per a Starlink team member), which SpaceX argues could let the network out-compete undersea cables on latency and routing.
- Competitor Viasat has lost 83% of its U.S. fixed broadband subscribers since Starlink's 2020 launch, falling from 603,000 to 100,000, per sawyermerritt.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Musk pointed to space-based data centers (Starshield, with NVIDIA) as the long-term answer to U.S. grid constraints, citing ~30% more efficient solar power in space; he floated 100 GW of orbital compute generating ~$1T/year at $10/watt, explicitly flagged as speculative and contingent on full Starship reusability, with first satellites possibly launching Q3 next year.
- A rebuttal video (jobhakdi) argues a viral astrophysicist critique of orbital compute got the physics largely right but erred by not comparing costs to Earth-based data centers; using the critic's own mass assumptions, the host models orbital compute becoming cheaper than terrestrial by 2028 (~$44B vs. ~$50B+ for a gigawatt), driven by falling Starship launch costs (~$500/kg in 2027 → ~$200/kg by 2029–30).

## Energy
- A California judge (San Diego Superior Court) found Tesla liable for fraud and negligent misrepresentation in a Solar Roof case (Conroy v. Tesla) after repeated discovery sanctions ($15,467.50 and $13,500) and a missed deadline; the roof was never finished, never inspected, and never produced power, per the complaint. The homeowner estimates repair costs "well over $2.5 million" against Tesla's reported $5,000 settlement offer; Tesla is appealing the sanction as "unduly punitive," with a trial readiness conference set for December 4.

## Humanoid Bots
- Musk told Ron Baron that Optimus will likely be rented rather than sold, estimated to cost around $20,000–$30,000, and projected to be roughly five times more productive than a human worker. He estimated the U.S. would need 20–30 million robots to match current output from ~120 million "highly productive" workers.

## Market & Competition
- Musk said combined announced U.S. chip factories (citing TSMC Arizona and a Samsung facility reportedly tied to Tesla's AI5/AI6 designs) would still represent only ~10% of Asia's chip output, calling the gap a geopolitical risk.
- Waymo's parent "other bets" segment is reportedly losing about $2B/quarter per Google's figures, with hosts criticizing its multi-OEM conversion strategy (Zeekr, Hyundai, Jaguar, newly Toyota) as costly versus Tesla's purpose-built Cybercab.
- Texas's governor-elect reportedly said already-permitted data centers can finish construction, but new ones must commit to not raising residential electricity prices — a signal of potential political friction for future Tesla/SpaceX data center expansion.
- Blue Origin unveiled a new "Northstar" engine claiming 715,000 lbf thrust (11.7% more than BE-4), 30% lighter with 50% fewer parts; AST SpaceMobile separately announced plans for its own reusable rocket ("Blacklift") by late 2028 to reduce SpaceX launch dependency.
