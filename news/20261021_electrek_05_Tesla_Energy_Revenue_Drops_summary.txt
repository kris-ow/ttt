Tesla's energy storage and generation business grew deployments but saw revenue and profit decline in Q3 2026, with the drop attributed in part to tariff-related customer refunds that Tesla did not quantify.

## Financials
1. **Energy revenue falls despite higher deployments:** Energy generation and storage revenue fell 16% year-over-year to $2.88 billion in Q3 2026, even as storage deployments rose 10% to 13.7 GWh. Tesla's shareholder letter attributes the revenue decline "partially" to "tariff-related customer refunds," without disclosing the refund amount, which customers received them, or the accounting rationale.
2. **Margin and profit trends:** Gross margin recovered to 33.1% from 20.4% in Q2 2026, but energy gross profit still fell to $954 million from $1.07 billion a year earlier. Tesla cited lower energy gross profit as a factor in its 56% operating income decline. Year-to-date, Tesla has deployed 36 GWh (up 11% from 32.5 GWh in the same period of 2025), while year-to-date energy revenue is down 6% to $8.43 billion. Q3 deployments also came in well short of the roughly 15.9 GWh analysts had expected.
3. **Tariff effects cut both ways in 2026:** In Q1 2026, Tesla had cited "energy one-time benefits related to tariffs" as a driver of improved profit, with energy gross margin hitting 39.5%; the article notes Tesla never disclosed a dollar figure for that benefit either. The author speculates, without confirmation from Tesla, that the Q3 refunds may relate to tariff charges no longer owed after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February.

## Energy
4. **LFP cell production ramp:** Megafactory Texas began production with 50 GWh of planned capacity, alongside existing capacity of 40 GWh in California and 20 GWh in Shanghai. Tesla shipped its first Megapacks built entirely with its own LFP cells manufactured in Nevada, where it has 7 GWh of cell capacity in early ramp.
5. **Demand and xAI purchases:** Tesla describes storage demand as at its "highest-ever order rate and backlog," driven by data centers needing power faster than the grid can supply. xAI, now part of SpaceX, purchased an additional $269 million of Megapacks this year, following $430 million in purchases from Tesla in 2025.

## Editorial Stance
The author argues Tesla's energy segment has been volatile all year (margins of 39.5%, 20.4%, then 33.1% across the three quarters) largely due to unquantified tariff effects, and credits Tesla for apparently passing refunds to customers rather than keeping them — while pointedly noting Musk's own role in the tariffs' original implementation.
