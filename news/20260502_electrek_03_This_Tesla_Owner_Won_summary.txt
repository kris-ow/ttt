A Texas Tesla owner, Ben Gawiser, won a $10,672.88 small claims court judgment against Tesla after the company failed to deliver the Level 5 Full Self-Driving software he paid $10,000 for in August 2021. Tesla has continued to delay payment through procedural maneuvers rather than mounting a substantive defense.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD promise vs. reality — the core legal argument:** Gawiser purchased FSD for $10,000 in August 2021, when Tesla and Elon Musk were actively promising imminent full self-driving capability. Musk stated in January 2021 that "the car will drive itself for the reliability in excess of a human this year." Five years later, Tesla has not delivered Level 5 autonomy to any customer. Gawiser cited specific FSD failures including the vehicle stopping in the middle of the road, requesting takeover within minutes of activation, and failing to slow for a school zone. His core argument: he was sold a Level 5 system, and FSD remains Level 2.

2. **HW3 admission strengthens Gawiser's case:** During Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call on April 22, 2026, Musk admitted that HW3 vehicles — the hardware generation in Gawiser's Model 3 — would never be capable of full self-driving and would require hardware upgrades built in new factories. No such factories have been announced or started. Gawiser cited this admission directly in his court filing as evidence that Tesla has no "meritorious defense," referencing the legal standard from *Craddock v. Sunshine Bus Lines*.

3. **Small claims process and Tesla's non-response:** Gawiser filed suit in Travis County, Texas small claims court for $72.88 in filing fees. Tesla failed to respond before the hearing, resulting in a default judgment on April 1, 2026. Tesla then missed a subsequent April 22 deadline and filed a request for a 5-day extension without submitting any substantive defense. Gawiser has since filed a writ of execution ($240 in additional court fees), which would authorize Texas law enforcement to seize Tesla property to satisfy the judgment.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek expresses strong skepticism toward Tesla's FSD promises, calling them "constant false promises" and stating that the HW3 retrofit "microfactory" plan is "unlikely to actually happen." The piece also notes broader legal exposure from class action suits in the US, China, Australia, and Europe that could collectively result in "billions in liability."
