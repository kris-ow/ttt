This video (recorded the day before SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company) features hosts Brian (futureaza) and Randy Kirk discussing SpaceX's roughly 37% stock decline from its IPO peak, arguing the drop reflects an overheated initial run-up and upcoming share unlocks rather than any change in fundamentals, and previewing what to watch in the earnings call and subsequent lockup expirations.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Post-IPO volatility framed as normal, not a red flag:** The hosts note SpaceX suffered the largest month-over-month market cap loss of any US company since 1970 (per Axios), but Randy Kirk argues this followed an equally unprecedented rally from the IPO price (around $150) to roughly $220–225 in a single day, calling the runup "way over its skis" and comparing it to Rivian's early post-IPO spike and subsequent decline. He states the fundamentals have not changed and, if anything, have improved since the IPO.
2. **Small float amplified price swings:** Randy attributes much of the initial price spike to a very small tradeable float (he estimates around 4–5%), meaning upcoming share unlocks could roughly double available shares and exert downward pressure.
3. **Lockup expiration schedule:** Most holders face a 180-day lockup (running to the end of the year), but Randy says a first window allowing sale of up to 20–30% of shares could open on a price trigger on the second full trading day post-IPO (around August 6), with unlocks expected to continue in waves through December rather than all at once.
4. **Four dynamics shaping near-term price action:** Randy lays out: (1) institutions/employees/retail holders trimming oversized positions regardless of price view; (2) heavy short interest (he estimates around 30%) that could unwind into a short squeeze if the stock ticks up; (3) unlocked sellers likely staggering sales over days/weeks rather than dumping at once; (4) long-time private owners/employees who already had chances to cash out (he references an opportunity roughly six months earlier) and may be less eager to sell now despite the drop from recent highs.
5. **Analyst price targets and valuation trajectory cited:** The hosts say 30 of 32 analysts following SpaceX have an average price target around $225/share, and cite (unnamed) analysts' expectations that SpaceX grows from under $20 billion in value last year to over $40 billion by the end of this year and toward $80 billion next year. Adam Jonas is cited by name with a target of $300/share. Randy also relays a profit prediction from a person the hosts refer to as Stephen Mark Ryan (unconfirmed reliability, per Brian's skepticism) of around $100 billion in profit next year — Brian explicitly says he doesn't trust this predictor's track record.
6. **Bull case rests on data-center rental, Cursor deal, and Starship's strategic role:** Randy argues most current retail/institutional shareholders don't yet appreciate three drivers: revenue from renting SpaceX's data centers, a not-yet-closed deal with Cursor expected to contribute starting in the back half of the year, and Starship's role as infrastructure enabling space-based manufacturing and data centers — comparing its significance to the transcontinental railroad.

## Financials
1. **No profit expected in the immediate earnings report:** Randy states with certainty that SpaceX will not report a top-line profit or positive free cash flow in the upcoming earnings release, but says it's unclear whether that remains true by year-end — some (including a source called "Grock," which Brian dismisses as unreliable/"likes to brag") reportedly expect the company to approach breakeven by year-end and turn profitable in 2027.
2. **Profit margin comparison:** Randy characterizes SpaceX's business as running profit margins around 35–40%, contrasted with the roughly 5–6% margins typical of the industries a lower-margin comparison company (e.g., Walmart-scale revenue business) would show, though he says SpaceX won't reach Walmart-level revenue.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink revenue metrics cited as relatively predictable:** Randy says Starlink currently earns an average of $44 per dish per month, with additional revenue streams from trains, planes, and now sea vessels, describing this segment as easier to forecast than other parts of SpaceX's business (and easier than Tesla's earnings, in his view) because deployment cadence is known.
