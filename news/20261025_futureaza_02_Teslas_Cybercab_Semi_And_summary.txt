This futureaza episode reacts to Tesla's Q3 earnings report and call, contrasting it unfavorably in presentation style with a recent SpaceX call while arguing the underlying financial and operational results were strong. The hosts walk through FSD connection-rate data, a geofenced customer-car robotaxi timeline for Austin, Optimus production updates, and capex guidance, concluding the investment thesis is "almost ready" but profit realization is pushed to next year.

## Financials
1. **EPS miss but cash and free cash flow improve:** Tesla's Q3 EPS came in at 22 cents versus a Wall Street consensus of 23 cents, a one-cent miss. Despite this, the hosts highlight positive free cash flow of $1.5 billion and cash reserves of $43 billion, which one host says surprised him given heavy ongoing spending on the Semi plant, Cybercab line, a lab near Giga Texas, Cortex Two completion, and a likely Terafab buildout in Grimes County.
2. **Capex guidance cut for next year:** Capital expenditure expectations for next year reportedly dropped from $25 billion to $20 billion, which the hosts read as a sign of growing capital efficiency.
3. **ZEV credits no longer material:** One host states ZEV credits are "almost exhausted," arguing this undercuts the recurring criticism that Tesla's profitability depends on regulatory credit sales.
4. **Call delivery criticized, video format promised:** The hosts contrast Tesla's less-polished conference-call-style earnings presentation with SpaceX's professional, scripted broadcast, noting some investors (including a commentator named Alexandra) were upset by the delivery despite strong underlying numbers. They say Elon Musk and Tesla's investor relations lead, Travis, responded by saying a video format is planned for next quarter's call.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD subscription attach rate reaches 75%:** Tesla's CFO reportedly stated the FSD "connection rate" — the share of new car buyers who take a paid monthly FSD subscription after a free trial period — is now 75%, which the hosts frame as evidence FSD has become a primary purchase driver rather than a secondary feature.
2. **FSD v15 safety record cited:** Ahead of earnings, Tesla reportedly announced zero accidents, zero airbag deployments, and zero injuries since the (unspecified) program began, which the hosts take as a sign of confidence heading toward broader, less-restricted deployment (highways, varied weather, faster trips) in coming months.

## Robotaxi
1. **Customer-car robotaxi integration targeted for Q2 next year:** Both Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy reportedly indicated that unsupervised customer-owned cars could begin joining the robotaxi fleet by the second quarter of next year, starting with geofenced operation in Austin rather than unrestricted city-wide rollout — a faster timeline than one host expected (he had anticipated end of next year or 2028).
2. **Cybercab sales to customers expected this year, scaling in H2 next year:** Management reportedly said Cybercab units would be sold to customers this year, potentially starting with small fleet sales (e.g., to a business) that could later be folded into the robotaxi network. The hosts expect a significant production ramp in Cybercab output in the third and fourth quarters of next year, which they frame as the point where real unit economics become visible.
3. **Production vs. deployment framed as internal race:** Lars Moravy and Ashok Elluswamy reportedly described their respective production and deployment efforts as a friendly competition, with Musk and others saying regulatory approval — not software or hardware readiness — is now the main bottleneck to scaling.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus in production; no customer sales until end of next year:** Tesla reportedly confirmed Optimus is already in production, but Musk was cautious that units won't be available to customers until the end of next year at the earliest; manipulator design, supply chains, manufacturing, and AI are all still being iterated.
2. **Training strategy: build ~15,000–20,000 units to learn through broad experience:** Musk reportedly reiterated a plan to build roughly 15,000–20,000 Optimus units, even with imperfect manipulators, and have them learn through real-world "play" and task repetition — a "broad learning" approach the hosts contrast with competitor Figure's strategy of using human-recorded egocentric video demonstrations (while noting Tesla is also pursuing simulation and other training methods).
3. **Possible industrial leasing/partner use before consumer sales:** One host speculates — explicitly as his own read, not a management statement — that industrial customers (he names Pepsi and Frito-Lay as hypothetical examples) could lease or borrow Optimus units for training purposes even before formal sales begin; he separately notes Musk said industrial sales are targeted to start toward the end of next year, around Q3 2026.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Roadster demo footage and battery/motor tech:** A video of the Roadster reportedly went viral this week, showing performance the hosts call almost unbelievable, with Musk reportedly inviting many people to witness it. The hosts highlight that the vehicle could showcase a faster-charging 4680-type battery with modified chemistry and a smaller, lighter, more powerful motor — technologies they say are too costly for the Model 3 currently but could filter into Tesla's mass-market lineup over time.
