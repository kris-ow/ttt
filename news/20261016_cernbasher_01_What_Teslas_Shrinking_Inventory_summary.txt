This video is a Tesla production/delivery data review focused on Q3 figures, cumulative milestones, and what shrinking inventory implies about demand and the Q4 outlook. The hosts argue that low inventory combined with strong delivery numbers points to FSD adoption becoming a significant, accelerating demand driver for Tesla.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Q3 production was the fourth-best quarter on record:** Tesla's Q3 production was described as the fourth-best quarterly production figure ever, with the all-time high at 494,000 in Q4 of a prior year (Tesla has not yet produced over 500,000 in a single quarter). Q3 production compared favorably to the same quarter a year earlier, which was 447,000.
2. **Q3 deliveries were third-best ever, closing the gap versus last year's EV-incentive-driven peak:** Q3 deliveries were the third-best on record. The prior year's Q3, the last quarter with EV incentives in place, saw 497,099 deliveries; this year's Q3 came in about 10,500 units below that figure despite the loss of incentives, which the hosts called very encouraging. One host suggested the gap could have been smaller still if a shipment hadn't arrived late.
3. **Cumulative production and shipments both passed 10 million units during the quarter:** Tesla likely crossed 10 million cumulative vehicles produced and 10 million cumulative vehicles shipped at some point in Q3, probably early in the quarter. The hosts noted Tesla did not publicize either milestone with an announcement or photos, calling it a missed opportunity, though it may be highlighted in the earnings presentation.
4. **Production-vs-delivery "inventory" gap has stayed roughly flat for several years:** Tracking the cumulative gap between total production and total deliveries since Q3 2012 (a rough proxy for stockpile, acknowledging some cars may be unaccounted for), the gap is currently around 120,000 vehicles and has fluctuated between roughly 100,000 and 160,000 over the last two to three years rather than growing — which the hosts view as unremarkable given Tesla sells nearly all it produces on a trailing-12-month basis.
5. **Year-over-year delivery gains in 2025 and the path to a record year:** Tesla delivered about 21,000 more vehicles in Q1 of this year than the prior year's Q1, about 96,000 more in Q2, and in Q3 (impacted by the loss of 2025 incentives) was only about 10,500 behind the prior year's Q3 of 497,000 — a cumulative year-to-date gain of about 106,000 vehicles versus last year. The hosts note Tesla needs to deliver 311,000 vehicles in Q4 to beat last year's full-year total, and 483,000 in Q4 to set an all-time annual delivery record.
6. **Low inventory raises a production-dependent Q4 risk:** With inventory running low, the hosts flagged that a strong Q4 depends on Tesla resolving the battery-pack production constraints mentioned on the prior earnings call; if those issues were resolved early or during Q3, they expect an encouraging Q4, but if further battery difficulties are reported on the upcoming earnings call, their expectations would be more restrained.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD framed as an accelerating, non-linear demand driver:** The hosts argue FSD is increasingly driving Tesla vehicle demand, citing a growing volume of social media testimonial videos (referencing the X/Instagram account "No Safe Words" as a prominent documenter) from FSD users — including people who say they previously disliked Elon Musk but now love their Tesla and recommend it to others. They characterize this as likely to be a non-linear, accelerating trend rather than steady incremental growth, driven by compounding public perception, though one host cautioned it's hard to isolate FSD's specific contribution from other factors like fuel prices.
2. **Voice-command functionality expected with a future FSD version:** One host stated that the current FSD version is already good enough to generate enthusiasm, with no safety issues and fewer erratic maneuvers than before, and suggested that an upcoming version (described as "version 15") enabling conversational voice commands to the car (e.g., "park in front of the mailbox") would be a major step change for adoption ("game over," in the host's words).
