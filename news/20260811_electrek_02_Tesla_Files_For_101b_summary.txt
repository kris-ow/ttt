Tesla has filed a Texas tax-incentive application for "Project Crystal Sun," a proposed $10.1 billion vertically integrated solar cell factory in Fort Bend County — the largest US manufacturing investment Tesla has filed for, per the article.

## Energy
1. **Filing details and scale:** The application, signed July 22 by a Tesla tax attorney and prepared by consulting firm Kroll, seeks a 10-year property-tax break under Texas's JETI program via Lamar Consolidated ISD; it surfaced publicly on August 6. The plant is projected at 9,712 permanent jobs on roughly 3,050 acres near Richmond, Texas, with commercial production starting Q1 2029.
2. **Investment breakdown:** The $10.116 billion total is split into about $1.5 billion in real property and $8.6 billion in equipment, to be spent across 2026–2028. The equipment list covers a fully vertically integrated process — ingot manufacturing, wafer manufacturing, coating, metallization/printing, cell testing, and cleanrooms — meaning raw polysilicon in and finished cells/modules out, which the article notes is rare for US solar manufacturing (most domestic operations are module assembly using imported cells).
3. **Site and economic claims:** The 3,050-acre greenfield site spans five parcels along FM 762 and FM 1994 south of the Brazos River near Richmond; the project would only occupy portions of these parcels within a reinvestment zone that Fort Bend County has yet to create. Kroll's economic impact statement claims roughly $107 billion added to Texas GDP and $6.4 billion in state/local tax revenue over 38 years.
4. **Incentive leverage:** Tesla states in the filing it is evaluating "various locations across multiple U.S. states" and that without the JETI break plus local abatements, the Fort Bend site is less economical than an unnamed competing out-of-state site. The filing also references federal Section 45X and Section 48D manufacturing tax credits.
5. **Link to Musk's 100 GW target:** The article ties the project to Musk's January Davos statement that SpaceX and Tesla teams are working toward 100 GW/year of US-manufactured solar power within roughly three years. It also references a prior report (from March) that Tesla was in talks to buy $2.9 billion in Chinese solar equipment for this push.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert cautions that JETI filings are negotiating tools for tax breaks and Tesla explicitly reserves the right to build elsewhere, so the $10.1 billion figure and job count should be treated as a negotiating position, not a commitment. He also notes Tesla's history of unmet solar manufacturing promises (the SolarCity Buffalo Gigafactory's 10 GW/year target and the underperforming Solar Roof), but argues that if built, a true vertically integrated US solar plant would be genuinely significant given Energy is currently Tesla's strongest business segment while its car business stagnates.
