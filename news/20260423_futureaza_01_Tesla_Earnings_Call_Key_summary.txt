This video features Brian (Futurazza) and Herbert (Brighter) breaking down Tesla's Q1 2026 earnings call highlights, debating what is genuinely positive versus concerning for long-term investors. The hosts work through the slide deck and earnings call to assess margins, cash flow, FSD subscriber growth, Megapack dynamics, and the multi-year capex buildout ahead. Their core thesis is that short-term cash flow pain from aggressive capital spending is not only acceptable but desirable, given Tesla's positioning in robotaxi, Optimus, and AI infrastructure.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD Subscriber Growth Surpasses Expectations:** Tesla added 170,000 new FSD subscribers in Q1 2026, primarily monthly subscribers, generating an estimated $50 million per quarter in additional revenue. Total FSD revenue is now running at approximately $350–$380 million per quarter. The hosts note this is U.S.-only and that Europe and China represent significant untapped upside as FSD expands internationally.

2. **FSD Confirmed as a Demand Driver:** Tesla confirmed on the earnings call that FSD is now a genuine demand driver for vehicle sales. The hosts observe that Tesla has repositioned its marketing to put FSD front and center — framing the vehicle as "a self-driving car that happens to also be a car" rather than a car with an optional self-driving feature. Anecdotally, free trial conversion rates are improving as the software performs well across a wider variety of geographies and driving styles, though some regions still need further training data.

3. **European FSD Regulatory Approval Timeline Uncertain:** Elon Musk addressed Europe FSD approval on the call, noting the Netherlands approval and that a May 5th regulatory agenda date exists in Europe, but deliberately declined to commit to a timeline. The hosts interpret this as strategic positioning to avoid confrontation with regulators. FSD version 14 in multiple European countries remains contingent on regulator pace. FSD is currently available on U.S.-manufactured Model S and Model X vehicles sold in South Korea due to an existing trade agreement.

4. **FSD Version Trajectory and Hardware Upgrade:** The hosts reference FSD versions 14.3 and 14.3.1 as current, with version 15 expected next year (2027). They also note that hardware 3 vehicles will be upgradeable to hardware 4, which the hosts suggest will increase the residual value of used Teslas, particularly hardware 4 vehicles with FSD already included.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab Production Ramp Across Six Factories:** Tesla indicated that six factories are expected to begin Cybercab production this year (2026). The hosts view the associated capital expenditure and depreciation drag as entirely acceptable given the long-term revenue potential. Herbert estimates that each Cybercab, once deployed, could generate approximately $30,000 per year, sustained over multiple years — a figure he characterizes as conservative.

2. **Demand Aggregation Strategy Signals Fleet Deployment:** Tesla's earnings report included language around "demand aggregation," which the hosts interpret as Tesla signaling its intent to operate its own robotaxi fleet rather than exclusively selling vehicles to customers. The hosts see this as the beginning of a transition where an increasing share of Tesla's vehicle production is directed toward robotaxi usage.

3. **Cybercab Depreciation as a Near-Term Drag:** The hosts flag that as Cybercab production launches, depreciation costs will begin hitting financials well ahead of meaningful robotaxi revenue. They estimate this drag could last a few quarters to the rest of 2026, but frame it as a necessary and positive investment.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus Capex Is a Known Unknown:** The hosts discuss Optimus capital expenditure as sitting between chip infrastructure (very long payoff horizon) and solar (more predictable). They acknowledge uncertainty about the total cost and revenue timeline but express high confidence that humanoid robots will be a transformative product. They frame this as a race that Tesla cannot afford to lose.

## Energy
1. **Megapack Revenue Ahead of Deployment Recognition:** The hosts note that a significant number of Megapacks have already shipped but have not yet been counted as "deployed," meaning final payments and revenue recognition are still incoming. This dynamic contributed to the stronger-than-expected free cash flow in Q1 and is expected to continue benefiting financials in subsequent quarters.

2. **Shanghai Megapack Factory Still Ramping:** Contrary to earlier assumptions that the Shanghai Megapack factory had fully ramped, the hosts confirm it is still ramping. They interpret ongoing demand and continued production growth as a bullish signal for the energy segment for the remainder of 2026.

3. **Energy Margin Compression Warning:** Despite the bullish production picture, management explicitly warned of expected "energy compression" in margins due to both tariffs and increasing competition. The hosts note that Q1 energy deployments came in at 8.8 gigawatt hours, a decline. Management did reaffirm that full-year 2026 energy deployments will exceed 2025 and will be record profits.

4. **LG Cell Deal as Tariff Mitigation:** The hosts highlight Tesla's ability to tap into an LG deal for LFP cells manufactured in Lansing, Michigan as a potential offset to tariff pressures on Megapacks. Domestically manufactured and assembled cells would qualify for domestic manufacturing tax credits. A Megapack 2XL is cited as carrying approximately $39,000 in module assembly credits alone, with total battery credits potentially reaching approximately $200,000 per unit.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **21% Automotive Gross Margin — Back to 2022 Highs:** Automotive gross margin came in at 21%, a level the hosts describe as shocking given current market conditions and pricing pressure. This matches Tesla's best margins from 2022 and is described as putting Tesla in a league of its own among automakers. The hosts note that some tariff-related cost impacts were present but did not prevent margin expansion.

2. **Inventory Build and Near-Term Demand Outlook:** Tesla is carrying approximately one month of vehicle inventory, representing roughly $16 billion in unsold vehicles. The hosts view this as an opportunity rather than a concern, noting that rising global oil prices — not yet reflected in Q1 — are expected to serve as a tailwind for EV demand in Q2 2026. Management expressed bullishness about vehicle sales for the remainder of 2026, citing improving trends in the U.S., Europe, and China.

3. **International Market Growth — Korea, Japan, Southeast Asia:** Overseas EV sales are growing rapidly in new markets including South Korea, Japan, Malaysia, and Singapore. The hosts note South Korea in particular has seen extraordinary growth — one figure of 3,000% year-over-year increase is referenced, though the hosts acknowledge this reflects a small base. Tesla is doubling service and sales center capacity in Japan. The hosts frame this as sustainable growth with substantial runway ahead of FSD availability in these markets.

## Financials
1. **Free Cash Flow Positive — But Not Expected to Continue:** Tesla posted free cash flow of $1.7 billion in Q1 2026, significantly exceeding expectations. Alexandra Merz is cited as being particularly struck by this figure. However, the hosts caution that this is unlikely to repeat in the near term given Tesla's announced increase in capital expenditure to $25 billion. Negative free cash flow in subsequent quarters is described as expected and appropriate given the investment cycle.

2. **Operating Income and Net Income Beat:** Operating income came in at $0.9 billion, with the hosts noting that a foreign exchange benefit of roughly the same amount played a role — meaning underlying results were roughly breakeven on some line items without it. Net income, cash flow, and free cash flow all moved higher. The FX benefit is flagged as non-recurring and not a reliable ongoing contributor.

3. **R&D Spend at Record High:** Research and development costs hit their highest level ever in Q1 2026, which the hosts view as appropriate given the breadth of products under development. They note Tesla's R&D spending remains relatively low in absolute terms compared to peers because of its engineering culture, where all employees are effectively contributors to product development.

4. **Regulatory Credits — $380 Million in Q1:** ZEV/regulatory credits contributed approximately $380 million in Q1 2026 — roughly $3 million per day for the quarter — contradicting persistent bear arguments that regulatory credits would disappear. The hosts express frustration at analysts and critics who continue to dismiss this revenue line despite consistent delivery.

5. **Capex $25 Billion — The Market's Concern:** When Tesla disclosed $25 billion in planned capital expenditure on the earnings call, the stock initially rose on free cash flow strength and then fell on the capex announcement. The hosts dismiss this reaction as short-sighted, arguing that capital deployment into Cybercab factories, Terafabs, AI data centers, Optimus, and robotaxi infrastructure is precisely what long-term investors should want to see.

## Market & Competition
1. **Comparison to Mag 7 on AI Spending:** The hosts contextualize Tesla's $25 billion capex by comparing it to Amazon and Google, each spending approximately $200 billion on AI infrastructure. They acknowledge those companies generate far more operating cash flow, but argue Tesla is in the same strategic race for AI and humanoid robotics and must invest aggressively despite its smaller current cash generation.

2. **FSD as a Competitive Differentiator Globally:** The hosts argue that no other automaker has a comparable FSD product nearing broad deployment, and that as FSD rolls out internationally it will increasingly differentiate Tesla in markets where competitors currently offer EVs without autonomous driving capability. Rising used Tesla prices — particularly for hardware 4 vehicles — are cited as a downstream effect of this competitive advantage.
