Tesla has begun producing its next-generation Megapack 3 battery at its new Brookshire, Texas Megafactory, which went from groundbreaking to production in 16 months and is designed for 50 GWh/year of capacity.

## Energy
1. **Megapack 3 specs:** Each unit holds about 5 MWh, up from 3.9 MWh on the Megapack 2 XL — roughly 28% more storage in the same footprint, achieved via a larger 2.8-liter cell format. Tesla also cut thermal-system connection points by 78% versus the prior generation, reducing leak risk and assembly/service labor. The Megapack line has grown from ~3 MWh at its 2019 launch to 3.9 MWh (2022, 2 XL) to 5 MWh now.
2. **Megablock design:** A Megablock bundles up to four Megapack 3 units (~20 MWh) with transformer and switchgear pre-integrated at the factory, moving medium-voltage wiring work that used to happen in the field into the factory. Tesla claims this enables 248 MWh per acre density, 1 GWh deployed in 20 business days, and installation about 23% faster than the current approach.
3. **Brookshire factory scale:** At 50 GWh/year dedicated to Megapack 3, Brookshire could become Tesla's largest energy storage manufacturing site, exceeding Lathrop's full output on its own; it was announced to add capacity amid rising competition from CATL, Fluence, and BYD.
4. **Recent demand signal:** Tesla recently signed a $5 billion, 25 GWh Megapack deal with NatPower in Europe, and the article states data-center operators are buying storage as fast as Tesla can build it.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the Megablock's factory-integrated wiring — not the 5 MWh cell density — is the more important development, comparing it to Tesla's Supercharger playbook of removing deployment bottlenecks. He flags an open risk: filling a 50 GWh factory requires large-format cells, and Tesla's own cell production isn't near that scale, with the company already reporting lower energy-business gross margins due to high tariffs on imported LFP cells.
