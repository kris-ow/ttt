Reuters obtained documents via public records requests showing Tesla presented its own inflated "Full Self-Driving" safety statistics directly to European regulators in Sweden and the Netherlands as part of formal lobbying efforts for FSD approval.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Tesla's misleading safety data used in official regulatory correspondence:** Tesla's November 2024 letter to RDW (the Dutch road authority) included a link to its self-published FSD safety report, claiming increased FSD usage "leads to safer roads." Tesla policy manager Ivan Komusanac subsequently emailed Swedish regulators after the Dutch approval on April 10, attaching a slide presentation claiming FSD-equipped Teslas travel more than seven times farther between crashes than the average U.S. human driver, and that FSD could have "saved 32,000 lives" and prevented 1.9 million injuries. Ten of 11 independent traffic-safety researchers who reviewed Tesla's methodology called it misleading marketing, not a serious safety study.

2. **Methodological flaws in Tesla's safety claims:** The "7x safer" figure stems from comparing FSD-Tesla crash rates (triggered by airbag deployments only) against a U.S. crash rate that includes far less severe accidents — an apples-to-oranges comparison that independent researchers say inflates the safety margin by a factor of three. Tesla also benchmarks against the average U.S. vehicle, which is approximately 12 years old, rather than comparably new vehicles with modern safety features. The "32,000 lives saved" figure extends this flawed methodology by assuming every vehicle in the U.S. — including freight trucks and motorcycles — would be replaced by an FSD-enabled Tesla.

3. **European regulatory responses are mixed:** RDW approved FSD in the Netherlands in April and is now seeking EU-wide approval on Tesla's behalf, though it stated it "does not rely on marketing claims" and conducts its own testing — without clarifying whether it actually assessed Tesla's U.S. statistics. The Swedish Transport Agency said it looks "beyond headline figures." Norway's roads administration was more skeptical, noting Tesla's figures "are self-produced," making it "difficult to find correlation with the authorities' accident statistics." A Greek regulator cited Tesla's data favorably. The European Transport Safety Council called for independent peer review before any safety claims are accepted. Four countries — the Netherlands, Lithuania, Belgium, and Denmark — have already granted national FSD approval. EU-wide approval requires representatives of 55% of member states comprising 65% of the bloc's population to vote yes.

## Market & Competition

1. **FSD approval is strategically critical for Tesla in Europe:** Tesla has identified European FSD approval as key to recovering market share following a sharp sales decline tied to backlash over Elon Musk's political activities. BYD has outsold Tesla in Europe for multiple consecutive months, with Chinese EV makers continuing to gain ground.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues that Tesla presenting self-debunked, self-serving safety statistics in official regulatory correspondence is qualitatively more serious than Musk's social media claims, and calls on European regulators to require independent peer review and rigorous on-road testing rather than accepting Tesla's "slide deck" lobbying materials.
