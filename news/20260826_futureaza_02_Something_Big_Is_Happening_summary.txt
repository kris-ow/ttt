This futureaza video, hosted by Brian with guest Cern Basher, discusses the Tesla/SpaceX joint chip fabrication venture Terafab, covering its feasibility, cost, competitive response from rivals, and construction progress at Giga Texas and a new site in Grimes County, Texas.

## Financials
1. **Terafab cost estimates dwarfed by rivals' spend, but likely to run over:** Cern Basher cites TSMC's projected spend of about $290 billion on its Arizona fabs and SK Hynix's plan to spend $400 billion on four fabs, versus the highest Terafab cost figure he's seen of $119 billion — which he believes will likely be exceeded, noting that fab capital expense is essentially never-ending as equipment and improvements continue indefinitely (as seen at Giga Texas).
2. **SK Hynix chairman reportedly skeptical of US chip talent:** Per a CNBC special Cern Basher watched, the SK Hynix chairman, when pressed on expanding into the US, indicated skepticism that the US has the talent and know-how to build memory chips — Cern Basher says this "remains to be seen."

## Market & Competition
1. **Rivals appear to be accelerating expansion in response:** Both hosts agree the Tesla/SpaceX chip fabrication announcement likely spurred competitors — Samsung and TSMC have announced additional US fab expansions, which Cern Basher frames as a win regardless of whether Terafab fully succeeds, and notes it may buy Elon extra time (a year or two) to get Terafab operational.
2. **Terafab hiring concentrated in Austin:** Of roughly 35 job postings for Terafab-related roles, about 34 are based in Austin, with one in Palo Alto, per Cern Basher.
3. **Potential partners and vertical integration questions:** Cern Basher says Nvidia would be "a fool" not to get involved in some capacity, and flags Intel as an existing partner; he also raises open questions about how much of the semiconductor supply chain (e.g., specialty equipment, chemical purity issues like those TSMC faced) Tesla/SpaceX will need to vertically integrate themselves, similar to Tesla's EV supply chain approach.
4. **Construction progress at Giga Texas and Grimes County:** Cern Basher expects "first steel" soon at the Terafab site within the Giga Texas compound, while the Grimes County site (heavily forested) is still in land-clearing and foundation-prep stages; Brian notes skeptics dismissing the early dirt-moving work as "not construction," comparing it to similar criticism of early Giga Shanghai footage.
5. **SpaceX allegedly builds data centers with far fewer workers than peers:** Citing a claim from SemiAnalysis, Cern Basher says SpaceX's Colossus 2 site (involving Macrohard and a smaller related site) uses about 3,000 workers per day, versus roughly 10,000 workers per day at a similarly sized site elsewhere (location unspecified), suggesting SpaceX builds these facilities faster with less labor.
