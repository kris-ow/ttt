Jo Bhakdi hosts a live session reviewing Tesla's and SpaceX's price action on a day dominated by the Federal Reserve's first press conference under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Tesla sold off broadly in line with the NASDAQ, driven by macro concerns rather than any Tesla-specific news. The core thesis of the session is that Cybercab production is visibly ramping at Giga Texas, a senior Texas DOT official publicly endorsed the coming scale-up, yet the stock market remains unresponsive — creating both frustration and, in Bhakdi's view, a strategic buying opportunity for patient investors.

## Robotaxi

1. **Over 100 Cybercabs spotted at Giga Texas:** Drone footage reviewed on the stream — credited to Joe Tegtmeyer and a count attributed to Soya Merritt — showed more than 100 Cybercabs in the Giga Texas parking lot on this date. Some units were identified as production-spec Cybercabs without steering wheels, which Bhakdi and Genma JP interpret as units most likely destined for the first commercial deployments rather than supervised testing vehicles.

2. **Bhakdi's personal estimate: over 1,000 Cybercabs deployed:** Bhakdi states his own estimate that Tesla has now deployed strongly over 1,000 Cybercabs across multiple U.S. cities. He notes that Waymo has approximately 3,000 vehicles in operation and suggests Tesla could reach a comparable fleet size within roughly five weeks at the current production pace.

3. **Texas DOT Executive Director endorses dramatic scale-up:** Mark Williams, Executive Director of the Texas Department of Transportation, publicly stated at the 2026 Texas Innovation Invitational that he experienced a production version of the Tesla Cybercab firsthand. He noted that Tesla is currently operating robotaxis using Model Y vehicles around Austin, Dallas, and Houston in Full Self-Driving mode, and said "the introduction of the Cybercab will dramatically scale these operations over the coming months." Bhakdi argues that Williams, as the government official responsible for overseeing autonomous vehicle operations in Texas, almost certainly had prior briefings from Tesla before making this statement.

4. **Lack of public FSD supervision evidence for Cybercab:** Referencing a write-up by Genma JP, Bhakdi notes that despite clear evidence of production-spec Cybercabs accumulating at Giga Texas, there is still no strong public evidence of Cybercabs running FSD under supervision even for testing purposes.

5. **Model: Cybercab and robotaxi economics coming next week:** Bhakdi previews an upcoming model covering various aspects of Cybercab and robotaxi economics, expected to be published the following week. He does not believe Cybercab consumer sales will happen anytime soon, saying it makes no sense to divert vehicles from the robotaxi network at this stage.

6. **End-of-year Tesla price target conditional on deployment:** Bhakdi states that if Tesla deploys Cybercabs and scales to 15,000 units, he expects Tesla stock to reach $800–$1,000 by year-end (i.e., end of 2026). He frames this as entirely contingent on commercial deployment materializing.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Tesla Model Y operating as robotaxi in multiple Texas cities:** Per the Mark Williams quote, Tesla is currently running Model Y vehicles in Full Self-Driving robotaxi mode across Austin, Dallas, and Houston — confirming that the current operational fleet is not exclusively Cybercab.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla trades in line with NASDAQ on Fed day:** Tesla's intraday decline tracked the NASDAQ closely, bottoming near -1.6% before recovering to close down approximately 1.34%. Bhakdi attributes the entire move to macro sentiment around the Warsh press conference rather than any Tesla-specific development, and does not view it as a serious selloff.

2. **Waymo fleet comparison:** Bhakdi cites Waymo's fleet at approximately 3,000 vehicles as the competitive benchmark, arguing Tesla is closing in rapidly as it produces Cybercabs by the hundreds per week.

3. **SpaceX IPO and valuation concerns:** Bhakdi discusses his view that SpaceX is tactically overvalued despite being fundamentally undervalued. He sold naked calls at a $250 strike with one-month expiration, collecting $7 per contract, as a small bearish position. He predicts SpaceX will drop below its IPO price of $135 at some point before December 2026, driven by a large unlock event starting in mid-August when up to approximately 1.6 billion shares (roughly 20% of outstanding shares) could enter the market against a current float of only about 5% of outstanding shares. He also sold a small number of contracts expiring the next day at the $200 strike as a speculative trade.

4. **SpaceX stealing the spotlight from Tesla:** Bhakdi argues that Tesla is currently in a "valley of tears" partly because SpaceX has become the dominant Elon Musk-related investment narrative, drawing attention and capital away from Tesla. He believes investor frustration with SpaceX will eventually pivot attention back to Tesla, but cautions that the "hype era" is over for both stocks and results must now be demonstrated.

5. **Possible Tesla–SpaceX merger:** Multiple viewer questions touch on a potential merger. Bhakdi says he believes it is a real possibility, speculating that SpaceX would acquire Tesla, rename the combined entity X Corporation, and house Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI as divisions underneath it. He acknowledges complications around SpaceX's defense/national security classifications but suggests a conglomerate structure with separate divisions could resolve those issues.

## Financials

1. **Bhakdi's options strategy — back to conservative churn:** Bhakdi describes having made overly aggressive short put trades around $435 per share beginning of June 2026, which he characterizes as a mistake driven by excessive conviction that June would be an inflection point. He is shifting back to a disciplined weekly "churn strategy" — selling at-the-money or slightly out-of-the-money weekly puts. As an example, he sold $400-strike puts expiring the following Wednesday/Thursday for approximately $10 in premium, giving him a breakeven down to approximately $390.

2. **Federal Reserve: no rate change, hawkish undertone:** The Fed held rates steady at a target range of 3.5%–3.75%. Warsh did not submit a dot plot. The post-meeting statement was dramatically shortened to approximately 130 words. The 2-year Treasury yield rose approximately 14.4 basis points on the day, and the 10-year moved from approximately 4.43% to a peak near 4.50%. Bhakdi interprets the market reaction as an overreaction to Warsh's repeated emphasis on "price stability," which traders read as potentially hawkish, and does not expect a rate hike given anticipated employment pressures.
