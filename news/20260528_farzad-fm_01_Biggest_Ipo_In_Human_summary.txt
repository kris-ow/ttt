This video covers the SpaceX S-1 IPO filing submitted on May 20th, 2026, which the host argues represents the largest IPO in human history, targeting a valuation nearing $2.5 trillion. The core thesis is that SpaceX is no longer just a rocket company — it has quietly transformed into three distinct businesses (rockets, satellite internet, and AI) and a landmark $15 billion/year compute deal with Anthropic fundamentally reframes what looks like a money-losing enterprise into a strategic infrastructure play for the AI era. Tesla is briefly mentioned as a parallel example of a company using a cash-cow business to fund long-term moonshots.

## Market & Competition
1. **SpaceX IPO targeting record-breaking valuation:** SpaceX filed an S-1 with the US government on May 20th, 2026, setting the stage for what is described as the largest IPO in stock market history. The previous record was Saudi Aramco's ~$25 billion raise in 2019; SpaceX is reportedly targeting roughly three times that amount at a valuation nearing $2.5 trillion. Shares are set to go live on NASDAQ on June 12th.

2. **SpaceX acquires xAI, transforming into a three-segment company:** On February 2nd of 2026, SpaceX closed an all-stock deal to acquire xAI — Elon Musk's AI company — which includes the Grok chatbot, the X social media platform (formerly Twitter), and data centers called Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 housing over half a million GPUs. As a result, SpaceX now reports financials across three segments: Space (rockets), Connectivity (Starlink), and AI (xAI).

3. **Anthropic deal: $15 billion/year in compute revenue from a single customer:** In May 2026, SpaceX signed an agreement for Anthropic — a top-tier AI company behind the Claude models and a direct competitor to Grok and ChatGPT — to pay $1.255 billion per month through May 2029 for access to SpaceX's computing capacity. This amounts to approximately $15 billion per year from a single customer. Either side can cancel on 90 days' notice, but the host considers cancellation unlikely given AI compute demand. Elon Musk publicly framed this as SpaceX offering "AI compute as a service at significant scale" and noted discussions with other companies for similar arrangements.

4. **AnySphere/Cursor deal and potential acquisition:** In April 2026, SpaceX also signed a computing deal with AnySphere, the company behind the AI coding tool Cursor, and SpaceX holds an option to acquire Cursor outright at an implied value of $60 billion, which the host suggests SpaceX is likely to exercise post-IPO based on recent reporting.

5. **SpaceX as the "picks and shovels" of the AI era:** The host frames SpaceX's strategic ambition as becoming the dominant infrastructure provider for AI — owning the compute, power, and eventually the orbital data center capacity that other AI companies must pay to use. This is explicitly compared to the Gold Rush analogy: the companies selling the tools to miners, not the miners themselves, got rich. Tesla is cited as a parallel, using car sales to fund Full Self-Driving software and the Optimus robot.

## Financials
1. **Total 2025 revenue of $18 billion, dominated by Starlink:** SpaceX's 2025 total revenue was approximately $18 billion. The Connectivity (Starlink) segment contributed roughly $11 billion, representing approximately 61% of total revenue — up nearly 50% year-over-year. The Space segment contributed roughly $5 billion, and the AI segment contributed approximately $3 billion.

2. **Starlink is massively profitable at software-like margins:** The Connectivity segment generated over $4 billion in operating income. On an adjusted EBITDA basis, Starlink produced over $7 billion on $11.4 billion in revenue — approximately a 63% margin, which the host describes as comparable to software company economics. As of end of March 2026, Starlink had roughly 10 million subscribers across 164 countries, with over 10,000 satellites in low Earth orbit, and its direct satellite-to-phone service was reaching approximately 7 million devices per month.

3. **AI segment losing over $6 billion, but Anthropic deal changes the math:** The AI segment (xAI) had an operating loss of over $6 billion (specifically cited as $6.4 billion) in 2025, which is the source of the headline-grabbing ~$5 billion overall company loss. SpaceX poured $3 billion in R&D into Starship in 2025 alone, dragging the Space segment to an operating loss of approximately $657 million. However, the host argues that with the Anthropic deal pouring $15 billion/year into the AI segment, the entire company could turn profitable as early as 2026.

4. **Severe cash burn in Q1 2026:** SpaceX's cash position fell from approximately $24 billion at the end of December 2025 to approximately $15 billion at the end of March 2026 — a roughly $9 billion decline in a single quarter. The host notes this burn rate is expected to slow significantly once Anthropic deal revenues begin flowing.

5. **Governance: Elon Musk controls ~85% of voting power pre-IPO:** SpaceX uses a dual-class share structure — Class A shares (1 vote each, available to the public) and Class B shares (10 votes each, held by insiders). Prior to the IPO, Elon Musk controlled approximately 85% of total voting power. Post-IPO, this percentage will decrease slightly but Musk will remain firmly in control. SpaceX qualifies as a "controlled company" under NASDAQ rules, allowing it to bypass certain independent board requirements. The host draws a parallel to Mark Zuckerberg's voting control at Meta.

6. **Orbital AI compute satellites planned for 2028:** The S-1 filing states SpaceX expects to begin deploying orbital AI compute satellites as early as 2028. The thesis is that satellites in sun-synchronous orbit can access effectively unlimited solar power (the filing cites the sun as holding ~99.8% of all energy in the solar system), eliminating the terrestrial grid and cooling constraints that are currently limiting AI data center expansion.
