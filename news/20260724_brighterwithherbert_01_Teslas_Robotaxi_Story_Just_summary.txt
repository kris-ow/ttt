A panel of Tesla-focused commentators (Jeff Lutz, Alexandra Merz, Matt Smith, host Herbert) dissects Tesla's latest earnings call, splitting over whether robotaxi commentary and weak margins signal delay or disciplined scaling, while extensively debating whether a Tesla–SpaceX merger could be announced within weeks.

## Robotaxi
1. **Unsupervised mileage and safety record cited by Tesla:** Ashok Elluswamy stated Tesla has driven over 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with zero notable incidents, framing this as validation of Tesla's camera-only approach versus lidar/HD-map competitors.
2. **Growth rate ambiguity sparks disagreement:** Elluswamy said the fleet has grown at double-digit week-over-week rates since the start of the year and expects this to continue through year-end; panelists disagreed on whether this implies only modest absolute growth (a few thousand cars by year-end, per the host's math) or an inflection toward state-by-state expansion, noting Tesla didn't specify the base rate being compounded.
3. **Shift from city-by-city to statewide framing:** Elluswamy said time-to-launch in new cities is trending toward zero and described a future where Tesla operates by state rather than city, though panelists disagreed on whether he explicitly said city-by-city expansion is ending, with Matt Smith noting Tesla's own materials still list upcoming city launches.
4. **FSD v15 rollout is early but ahead of some expectations:** Elluswamy said the current robotaxi fleet runs early builds of FSD v15, with roughly 40% of seven planned major improvement tracks merged, and expects it to make the car "ridiculously safe and capable"; Jeff Lutz noted this isn't "ahead of schedule" in a strict sense since it's still incomplete.
5. **Governor now is zero-incident rate, not fleet size:** Jeff Lutz argued the key change from prior calls is that Tesla is now gating expansion on maintaining a zero-incident rate rather than a specific mileage or fleet target, which he says is more cautious but harder to communicate.
6. **Decline in monthly robotaxi miles unexplained:** Matt Smith noted the chart of cumulative robotaxi miles showed a declining monthly rate of additions, which was not explained on the call; he cited a third party's theory that the transition to v15 software slowed the fleet down, but found it odd Tesla wouldn't just replace supervised with unsupervised rides directly.
7. **Cybercab described as complete and on track:** Elluswamy called the Cybercab "a phenomenal product" and said Tesla has aligned manufacturing targets to match the projected growth rate of unsupervised miles, with the same FSD v15 stack powering both Model Y and Cybercab.
8. **Competitive positioning unchanged:** Jeff Lutz argued Waymo (roughly 3,700 vehicles, per his figure) remains unprofitable and structurally non-scalable compared to Tesla's camera-based, lower-cost approach (a vehicle he says is designed to cost $18,000–$20,000 to build, targeting under 30 cents per mile), a point he felt Tesla under-emphasized on the call.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Regulatory risk in Europe tied to safety record:** Alexandra Merz argued that any publicized safety incident could jeopardize FSD approval across Europe, since a bloc of countries representing a required population threshold could block approval for the whole continent, even in countries like the Netherlands that have given provisional approval.

## Financials
1. **Q2 earnings miss driven by multiple factors:** Matt Smith cited declining credit revenue, lower energy gross margins (now seen as a "new" ~20% level per Alexandra Merz), higher expenses, and weaker-than-expected automotive margins as combining to produce the earnings miss, with the cause of the margin decline (product mix, Model S/X phase-out, promotions, or ramp costs) left unclear by Tesla's disclosures.
2. **Free cash flow turned negative amid rising capex:** Panelists noted roughly $4 billion of operating cash flow was insufficient to cover nearly $6 billion in capex, pushing free cash flow negative — a first in about a decade per Matt Smith — which he says spooked short-term sentiment despite representing long-term-oriented investment.
3. **New $30 billion credit facility:** Tesla is establishing a $30 billion credit line, up from roughly $7 billion in prior smaller facilities, which Alexandra Merz says signals the company can't fully fund its scaling plans through operating free cash flow alone.
4. **FSD subscription growth exceeded expectations:** Matt Smith said incremental FSD subscription additions (approaching roughly 200,000 per quarter by his estimate) came in higher than his ~46,000 assumption, though he estimates the current run rate implies only about $1 billion of incremental gross margin per year — meaning it would take about five years to become a $5 billion business at that pace.
5. **Delaware litigation resurfaces:** Alexandra Merz noted Tesla's 10-Q disclosed that plaintiffs are appealing to the Delaware Supreme Court after a vice chancellor had dismissed related cases in April, reopening a legal matter many had considered closed.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation intensifies around SpaceX's August earnings call:** Alexandra Merz reiterated her hypothesis (explicitly labeled speculative) that a Tesla-SpaceX merger could be announced before mid-August, tied to SpaceX's August 4 earnings call as the "absorbing entity," and pointed to Tesla General Counsel Brandon Ehrhart's unusual presence on the Tesla earnings call as a signal that merger-related legal groundwork is active; Musk himself deflected an analyst's merger question on the call, saying an earnings call isn't the right venue.
2. **Valuation math complicated by diverging stock prices:** Merz argued that a high Tesla share price combined with a low SpaceX share price (SpaceX around $118, Tesla around $319–320 at time of discussion) makes a merger exchange ratio harder to construct favorably than her prior "Goldilocks" scenario of a 4.2x multiplier on a $200 SpaceX price; she suggested Musk might need to set a price floor in any offer.
3. **Retail shareholder trust seen as a risk to merger approval:** Matt Smith warned that years of missed Tesla milestones (robotaxi timelines, Optimus, past products like Solar Roof) have eroded retail investor trust, and that Tesla/SpaceX would need a large "roadshow" period — potentially including a Terafab groundbreaking, Roadster event, and Optimus presentation — to rebuild enthusiasm and secure sufficient retail votes for any merger.
4. **Prior consideration of SpaceX–xAI merger reported:** Alexandra Merz cited a Bloomberg report that discussions occurred in January regarding whether SpaceX going public should merge first with Tesla or with xAI, as background to the current merger speculation.
