This video from FutureAzA covers Tesla's surprise launch of the rear-wheel drive Model 3 Premium in Canada at approximately $29,000 USD — the cheapest new Tesla available in North America. Host Brian is joined by Canadian guest Mark to break down the pricing strategy, the Canadian government's Chinese import quota allowance, and what this means for Tesla's sales momentum heading into Q2 and Q3 2026.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Canada's $29K Model 3 Premium Launch:** Tesla quietly listed a rear-wheel drive Model 3 Premium on its Canadian website overnight, priced at approximately $29,000 USD (roughly $39,990 CAD). This is a significant reduction from the previous starting price of approximately $79,990 CAD, which had been elevated due to cross-border tariff complications stemming from the suspension of USMCA. The vehicle is built in China and uses an LFP battery, which Mark and Brian note carries the advantage of being safely chargeable to 100% without the degradation risk associated with high-nickel NMC or NCA batteries. For comparison, the same Model 3 rear-wheel drive Premium on the US website is listed at $42,490 USD — over $13,000 more than the Canadian price.

2. **Chinese Import Quota as the Pricing Mechanism:** The dramatically lower price is made possible by Canada's policy allowing 49,000 Chinese-built vehicles to be imported at reduced tariff rates in the current year, with the quota growing to close to 70,000 per year over the 5-year term of the deal. Critically, only 50% of the quota must consist of vehicles with an import price below $35,000 CAD — the retail price can be higher. Tesla's Shanghai-built Model 3 apparently qualifies with an import cost under the $35,000 CAD threshold, enabling the aggressive retail pricing. Mark and Brian speculate that Tesla has likely claimed a significant portion of this quota and that ships from Giga Shanghai are reportedly already en route to Canada's west coast, while Giga Berlin-sourced Model Y units are being delivered to the east coast via Halifax, Nova Scotia.

3. **Tesla's Competitive Advantage in Capturing the Quota:** Mark emphasizes that Chinese EV brands like BYD and Geely, while rumored to be scouting dealership locations in Canada (BYD reportedly looking countrywide, Geely looking regionally in Ontario), have not yet established the sales networks, parts distribution, or warranty infrastructure needed to capitalize on the quota. Polestar, while having a Canadian presence through Volvo, does not have qualifying vehicles in the right price range. This leaves Tesla positioned to capture not just the majority but potentially nearly all of the 49,000 quota in the first year — split roughly between the lower-priced standard models and the premium/long-range/performance variants.

4. **Strong Demand Signals and Delivery Timelines:** Mark visited a Tesla dealership in Brampton, Ontario and observed a steady stream of customers inquiring about the new Model 3 offer since the announcement. Delivery estimates have already slipped from June to October 2026, indicating strong order intake. Mark advises Canadian buyers on the fence to act quickly, warning the allocation may run out. Brian contextualizes this within broader Tesla sales momentum: the Model Y ranked #1 among all cars (not just EVs) in Colombia for two consecutive months, and globally for March 2026, the Model Y and Model 3 ranked #1 and #2 respectively among EVs.

5. **Model Y Long Range (Model Y L) in Canada:** Discussion briefly touched on the possibility of Tesla eventually importing the Model Y L (long range variant) from Shanghai to Canada. Mark believes it is possible but expects a delay, as Tesla is focused on the current Model 3 launch first, and any additional Chinese-built imports would draw from the same 49,000-vehicle quota. Brian suggests Tesla may time the Model Y L Canada launch to coincide with a US release.

## Market & Competition

1. **Chinese Brands Not Yet a Threat in Canada:** Despite geopolitical narratives around Chinese EV dominance, Mark confirms that BYD, Geely, and Xiaomi currently have no sales or distribution presence in Canada. They are in early scouting phases for dealership locations but have not signed leases or opened any sites. The practical requirements of warranty coverage, parts distribution, and service networks mean these brands cannot realistically compete for the 2026 import quota allocation.

2. **Tesla's Margin and Competitive Position:** Brian notes that contrary to narratives about Tesla losing customers, sales numbers are coming in stronger than expected in most markets, and Tesla continues to lead on margin compared to competitors. The Canadian pricing strategy is cited as a further demonstration of Tesla's ability to leverage its global manufacturing and supply chain flexibility in ways that rivals currently cannot replicate.
