Electrek's coverage today centered on the proposed Tesla–SpaceX merger, with Fred Lambert reporting on a Financial Times account of a year-end target for an agreement and arguing the political calendar, not the negotiation, is setting the pace.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Tesla–SpaceX merger races a year-end deadline:** Per the Financial Times, as relayed by Electrek, the all-stock merger talks are "moving towards a potential year-end deadline," though people familiar with the talks say an agreement isn't close. The combined group would be worth about $3.5 trillion. A deal signed by December 31 would land just before the new Congress is seated on January 3; Electrek notes Democrats have promised scrutiny of Musk's companies. Tesla's special committee is Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung (adviser: Evercore); SpaceX is represented by Roelof Botha and Randy Glein (advisers: Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs). Per the FT, Texas governance rules keep Musk out of active negotiations. Any deal needs more than 50% of all outstanding Tesla votes; Electrek notes Musk holds about 20% of Tesla's voting power and about 84% of SpaceX's. Regulatory scrutiny could come in the US, China, Europe and the UK. Lambert argues Congress can't block the deal but can subpoena records, that a politically driven deadline means the side wanting a fast close gives ground, and that the only meaningful vote is a majority of Tesla shareholders other than Musk. ## Editorial Stance
Electrek's opinion is that the merger is a Musk self-deal, that the Tesla committee negotiating it is not independent of him, and that Denholm has historically done what Musk wanted.
