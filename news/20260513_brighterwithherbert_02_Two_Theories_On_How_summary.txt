This video from "brighterwithherbert" presents two contrarian theories about how Tesla's Cybercab will actually roll out into commercial robotaxi service. The host argues that despite Cybercabs actively rolling off the line at Giga Texas, the vehicle will stay out of live passenger service for at least two to three months while accumulating form-factor-specific training miles — and that when it does launch, it will do so at scale across multiple cities simultaneously rather than as a slow trickle.

## Robotaxi

1. **Cybercab production is ramping but not yet in passenger service:** Based on drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer, Cybercabs are currently being produced at an estimated 20–40 units per day at Giga Texas. By early March, 25 units were spotted in a single day across three locations; by late April, groupings of 50–60+ were visible in the staging lot. Despite this production ramp, not a single Cybercab has entered the live robotaxi fleet — all 39 unsupervised vehicles currently in service (per RobotaxiTracker.com) are Model Ys, split across Austin (28), Dallas (5), and Houston (6).

2. **Theory One — Cybercab stays out of live service for two to three months:** The host argues Tesla needs to accumulate roughly 50–100 million miles of Cybercab-specific real-world driving data before trusting the new form factor with unsupervised passenger service. The Cybercab has a different ride height, camera placement, weight distribution, sensor configuration, and autonomous-only steering and braking system compared to the Model Y fleet that generated Tesla's 10 billion FSD supervised miles. The host estimates a fleet of roughly 1,000 Cybercabs driving constantly for two to three months — with Tesla employees behind the wheel and engineers in the passenger seat — would be sufficient to validate the platform's unique characteristics.

3. **Brand protection as a second rationale for slow-rolling Cybercab:** The host cites the Cruise implosion in 2023 — where a single pedestrian-dragging incident shut down the entire program — as a cautionary tale. He argues Tesla cannot afford an early high-profile Cybercab incident, since the vehicle is the visible face of the company's entire autonomous thesis. Any symbolic one-off Cybercab passenger ride before the full launch (e.g., at a Q2 earnings event or via an Elon Musk tweet) should be treated as a PR moment, not a real transition.

4. **Theory Two — Fleet composition of 100 Model Ys plus 900 Cybercabs per city:** The host proposes a target fleet structure based on Uber and Lyft ride-share data showing roughly 90% of rides involve two passengers or fewer. Since Cybercab seats exactly two and Model Y seats five, the host argues the natural fleet ratio is 90% Cybercab and 10% Model Y. At 1,000 vehicles per city, that means 900 Cybercabs handling the bulk volume and 100 Model Ys covering the 10% of rides requiring more than two seats. This is described not as a fleet composition but as a deliberate service design.

5. **Phased rollout timeline — summer Model Y density, Q3/Q4 Cybercab launch:** The host projects that by August or September 2026, the Model Y unsupervised fleet will have grown to 100 vehicles each in Austin, Dallas, and Houston, and Tesla will have launched Model Y robotaxi service in expansion cities including Phoenix, Miami, Las Vegas, Orlando, Tampa, and possibly San Antonio. After Cybercab accumulates ~70–80 million internal miles, the host expects Tesla to deploy roughly 1,000 Cybercabs simultaneously in each of the three Texas cities — choosing a large simultaneous launch over a trickle to create a demonstration moment.

6. **33-city signal from Tesla job postings:** The host highlights that a researcher reviewing Tesla's open autonomy operations job postings on X identified 33 US cities where Tesla is actively hiring for robotaxi-related roles. The host treats this as a more reliable forward signal than any public Elon Musk statement, noting that quiet staffing decisions reflect real operational intent. At even 100 vehicles per city across all 33, that yields 3,300 vehicles from expansion markets alone, before factoring in Texas density.

7. **End-of-year fleet size range — 3,300 to 15,000 robotaxis:** The host lays out a production math check: at 30 Cybercabs per day average over roughly 150 days from May to October, Tesla could produce approximately 4,500 units, with the ramp expected to accelerate to potentially 5,000–10,000 by year-end. Three Texas cities at ~1,100 vehicles each (~3,300) plus 5–7 expansion cities at 500–1,500 vehicles each yields a combined range of roughly 6,000–12,000, putting total fleet near 15,000 in the optimistic scenario. The conservative floor of 3,300 assumes only Texas density and 33-city breadth at minimal vehicle counts.

8. **Three investor scenarios for H2 2026:** Scenario one: Tesla executes the full plan — Model Y density grows through summer, Cybercab data period runs in parallel, Q3/Q4 brings a simultaneous multi-city Cybercab launch, year ends at ~15,000+ robotaxis, and the stock gets re-rated as a transport platform. Scenario two: Tesla executes a careful version — Cybercab pushes to early 2027, year ends at 3,000–5,000 robotaxis, stock holds current multiples without a re-rating. Scenario three: A regulatory snag, tech issue, or high-profile incident leaves the fleet under 1,000 unsupervised vehicles at year-end, with a negative stock impact. The host suggests the market is currently pricing in somewhere between scenarios two and three, implying real upside if scenario one materializes.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Version 15 as a gating event for full-scale launch:** The host notes Tesla has stated it is waiting for FSD version 15 before launching at "super scale." He flags the FSD version release cadence as a key monitoring signal — a version bump to 15 would be a meaningful indicator that the large-scale Cybercab rollout is imminent.

2. **10 billion supervised FSD miles — but almost all on Model Y and Model 3:** Tesla recently hit the 10 billion FSD supervised mile milestone, which Elon Musk previously cited as the threshold needed for safe unsupervised driving. However, the host emphasizes that nearly all of those miles were accumulated on Model Y and Model 3 platforms, meaning the Cybercab restarts a form-factor-specific learning curve — not from zero, but not from 10 billion miles either.

## Market & Competition

1. **Waymo as the baseline for fleet scale context:** The host notes that even with 100 unsupervised Model Ys per Texas city (300 total), Tesla's fleet would still be well below what Waymo operates in Phoenix and San Francisco, framing this as a meaningful growth step but not yet competitive parity in those markets.

2. **Cruise as the cautionary tale for premature deployment:** The host explicitly references Cruise's 2023 collapse — a single pedestrian-dragging incident led to the entire program being shut down and the company effectively folding — as the primary case study driving Tesla's expected caution. This is presented as the institutional memory shaping Tesla's brand-protection rationale for delaying Cybercab's live passenger debut.
