This video is a site-analysis deep dive into SpaceX's AI compute buildout, using satellite and drone imagery to map the Colossus 2 data center complex near Memphis, Tennessee/Mississippi, and a newer site near Starbase, Texas. The host and a co-presenter walk through building sizes, power/cooling infrastructure, and land holdings to argue SpaceX is rapidly templating and scaling a repeatable data center "module" (nicknamed "Mini Hard") across multiple sites.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Colossus 2 Memphis complex structure:** The Colossus 2 site comprises three main buildings: Macrohard (the first facility, ~210 MW of compute and about 110,000 GB200 servers), Macrohard "larger" building — an acquired and renovated existing warehouse with roughly 443 MW of compute and about 220,000 GB300 servers — and a newer, smaller building under construction nicknamed "Mini Hard."
2. **Mini Hard as a repeatable, shrinking template:** Despite packing comparable or greater compute density, the Mini Hard building is physically much smaller than its predecessors — Macrohard's footprint is about 810,000 square feet versus roughly 312,000 square feet for Mini Hard — leading the hosts to describe Mini Hard as an evolving, productized design being replicated across sites rather than a one-off building.
3. **Power and cooling infrastructure at Memphis:** The site features extensive cooling towers and large water-circulation piping around the Macrohard buildings, plus an estimated ~1,200 Megapacks visible on-site (a figure the hosts say likely includes Megapacks from both Colossus 1 and Colossus 2), underscoring cooling and power supply as major infrastructure bottlenecks.
4. **Additional Memphis-area expansion sites:** SpaceX has acquired an old refrigerated-goods warehouse at a nearby road junction that could become a future (sixth) data center pending retrofitting, and is building a dedicated on-site power plant with gas turbines to the south. SpaceX also acquired an H-shaped former police training ground via land exchange, planned as the site's fifth data center, described as resembling "two small Mini Hards put together" though slightly shorter than the existing Mini Hard building.
5. **New Texas data center site near Starbase:** Via drone footage, the hosts identify a 444-acre SpaceX-owned parcel south of the Brownsville Port (recently annexed into Starbase, putting permitting authority under Starbase rather than the City of Brownsville) where two new buildings are under construction, believed to be a larger variant of the Mini Hard design (roughly 60% bigger, an estimated ~500,000 square feet, though exact figures are unconfirmed). An adjacent 157-acre parcel south of Highway 4 and an undeveloped 893-acre parcel north of the port (next to a planned gas pipeline) are also SpaceX-owned, with no confirmed construction plans yet on the larger northern tract.
6. **Site capacity estimates for future buildout:** The hosts estimate the 444-acre Texas site could eventually hold roughly six to eight Mini Hard-type buildings. They note Brian Wang reportedly suggested a need for 24 such facilities, which the hosts say would require tapping the additional 893-acre northern parcel — a scenario they characterize as speculative, since no construction has yet begun there, though SpaceX's ownership of the land is confirmed.
