## Robotaxi
- **Austin night operations begin; record daily unsupervised sightings.** Tesla's unsupervised Model Y robotaxis began running at night in Austin for the first time — previously service ended mid-afternoon. The same day set a record for new unsupervised vehicle sightings, with 7 new vehicles spotted across Austin, Dallas, and Houston combined.
- **Las Vegas and Doral, Florida identified as next unsupervised cities.** Tesla is actively hiring robotaxi field response specialists in both cities — the same role it staffed in Dallas and Houston ahead of those launches. Bhakdi and Herbert interpret active field operator hiring as confirmation that deployment is already in motion, not merely planned.
- **Unsupervised fleet in Houston and Dallas expected to reach 10–15 vehicles each within two to three weeks,** per Dillon Loomis, describing current growth as slow but in line with expectations.
- **California unsupervised paid service unlikely before 2027.** The CPUC confirmed to Loomis that Tesla has not applied for any AV permits and currently operates under a TCP (charter/limousine-type) permit. Tesla is actively commenting on new CPUC rulemaking for AV passenger services, suggesting it is shaping the framework before formally entering it. Loomis estimates 6–9 months minimum for permit approval once Tesla applies, putting commercial unsupervised California service into 2027.
- **New California AV rules effective July 1, 2026:** Law enforcement may issue traffic citations directly to AV manufacturers; a 50,000-mile safety test is required before any commercial permit; remote operators must meet state licensing standards; vehicles must connect first responders to a company rep within 30 seconds; and local officials may impose temporary AV geofencing during emergencies.
- **Cybercab fleet projection: 10,000–15,000 robotaxis deployed by end of 2026.** Herbert and Bhakdi converge on this estimate, using Cybertruck's first-year ramp (~30,000 units in nine months) cut in half as a conservative floor. Bhakdi's personal year-end target is above 10,000; Herbert's consistent estimate is 10,000–15,000. Bhakdi cautions Cybercab will need substantial accumulated miles before a public robotaxi launch to avoid reputational risk from an early accident, though he argues the similarity of Cybercab's camera layout and HW4 system to Model Y minimizes the fine-tuning required.
- **Waymo context:** Bhakdi acknowledges Waymo operates approximately 3,000 robotaxis — roughly 90x Tesla's current unsupervised fleet — but argues Tesla's vision-only approach is more scalable and represents "real autonomy" versus lidar-dependent systems requiring manual adjustments.

## Autonomous Driving
- **EU-wide FSD vote targeted for late June or early October 2026.** The Dutch RDW presented 18 months and 1 million miles of FSD testing results to EU member states at a TCMV meeting in Brussels. A vote on bloc-wide approval — which would not require individual country retesting — requires at least 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of EU population. Bhakdi puts approval odds at 30–50%, calling it an upside surprise; his base case is rejection, followed by individual country approvals in the Netherlands, Czech Republic, Sweden, and potentially Bavaria.
- **Flanders moves to fast-track FSD homologation.** Immediately after the Brussels meeting, Belgium's Flanders Minister of Mobility publicly asked Tesla to transfer its FSD test dossier to her administration and instructed her team to provide clarity on a rapid homologation process by end of the week. Bhakdi and Herbert note she governs Flanders specifically, not all of Belgium, but characterize the move as a meaningful early signal.
- **FSD 14.3.2 rolling out to HW4 fleet; ~55–60% penetration.** Early user feedback is mixed — most report no major issues, but some report degraded performance versus the 14.2 branch, including jittery behavior and at least one immediate takeover request. Loomis concludes the software is approaching but has not yet reached true wide release. Bhakdi characterizes FSD v14.3 as an important breakthrough enabling high iteration speed.
- **Tesla crosses 10 billion supervised FSD miles.** Loomis contextualizes this as equivalent to ~3.5 million US coast-to-coast trips, but cautions it does not appear to be a "light switch" moment for scaling unsupervised operations.
- **Netherlands FSD adoption: 6.2 million miles in 23 days** (~270,000 miles/day) since the April 11 launch of FSD subscriptions. Tesla announced it will end the one-time FSD purchase option on May 15, 2026, leaving only the monthly subscription. FSD now accounts for approximately 0.25% of all miles driven by humans in North America annually.
- **FSD performance in Amsterdam used as generalization evidence.** Video of FSD navigating Amsterdam's narrow streets, cyclists, and canal bridges — contrasted with rural South Dakota and urban China — cited by Bhakdi and Herbert as evidence of a deeply generalized AI rather than a route-memorization system.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Model X line shut down at Fremont for Optimus production conversion.** Tesla shared a photo of the last Model X ever produced, consistent with Elon Musk's earnings call statement that S/X lines would be shut down in May to prepare for Optimus manufacturing.
- **Optimus scale targets: 1M units/year at Fremont, 10M units/year at a larger facility,** per Herbert. Jeff Lutz cautions Tesla will "stair step" capacity investment in line with market acceptance rather than commit full capex upfront, reducing enterprise risk. Lutz frames autonomy cash flow as the financial backstop that makes Optimus risk manageable: by the time Optimus is genuinely scaling, FSD/robotaxi revenue will be "throwing off so much cash" that the capital exposure is relatively small.
- **Lutz's competitive filter: 80–90% of humanoid companies will never reach market or never become a real business.** He draws a direct parallel to the Chinese EV market (80–100 entrants, one or two viable), and argues success requires proven mass manufacturing, vertical integration, proprietary AI, and ideally a proprietary chip — avoiding what he characterizes as ~70% gross margin payments to Nvidia. He states no other humanoid competitor currently combines all of these under one roof.
- **Western labor arbitrage makes humanoid ROI stronger in the US than in China.** Lutz identifies a counterintuitive dynamic: displacing $55/hour US union labor yields an immediate, large ROI; displacing $3.50–$5.00/hour Chinese labor requires a much longer payback curve, with additional political disincentives for China to displace large numbers of workers at scale.
- **Figure 3 comparison point:** Figure released factory access content showing its Figure 3 robot (~130 lbs, 5'6", 40 lb lift capacity, 4–5 hour battery). Brett Adcock reported 55 robots produced in one week, up from one per day 120 days prior. Stated factory capacity: 40,000–50,000 units/year. New customer announcements expected within 90 days.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Cybercab production rate: 25–30 units observed at Giga Texas site, with vehicles being transported away.** Joe Tegtmeyer's drone footage identified this on-site count; Bhakdi estimates cumulative Cybercabs already outside the factory at well over 200. Production rate estimates diverge: Bhakdi and Herbert estimate 10–25 units/day (300–500+/month); Loomis estimates a more conservative 5–15 units/day at this early stage.
- **Model Y L spotted testing in Palo Alto.** Multiple sightings reported; Loomis notes a source told him months ago the Model Y L was coming to the US despite Musk's skeptical public comments. He views these sightings as indicating an imminent launch, with timing well-suited to fill the gap left by the Model X line closure.
- **Canada Model 3 price drops sharply after tariff deal.** Following Canada's deal reducing Chinese EV import tariffs from 100% to 6% (capped at ~49,000 vehicles/year, growing to ~70,000 by 2030), the Model 3 from Giga Shanghai is now available in Canada at approximately USD $29,000 equivalent for the premium RWD variant — among the lowest North American pricing seen in some time. The performance variant dropped over 15%. The Model 3 from China does not qualify for Canada's $5,000 EV incentive.
- **60-truck Tesla Semi order from California port drayage operators.** Big F Transport (40 units) and NICA Container Freight Line (20 units) placed a combined order through Forum Mobility, operating from the FM Santa Fe depot in Rancho Dominguez — expected online Q1 2027. Big F already operates nine Daimler eCascadia trucks and conducted direct Tesla Semi testing before switching, making this a concrete competitive win.
- **Washington State $175,000 WEZI rebate on Class 8 electric trucks** reduces the Long Range Tesla Semi's effective price from ~$290,000 to ~$115,000 — below the average diesel Class 8 truck cost of ~$175,000.
- **Tesla Semi dominates California voucher program: 965 of 1,067 applications** between January 2025 and February 2026. Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo combined received fewer than 100. Gigafactory Nevada's new high-volume Semi production line — 1.7 million sq ft, designed for 50,000 trucks/year annual capacity — began ramping last week.
- **New 125 kW Tesla Semi base charger announced.** Priced at ~$20,000 (vs. ~$94,000 for the Megacharger), it uses V4 post electronics, features a 6-meter cable, can be daisy-chained up to three units per breaker, and adds up to 60% range in 4 hours. Deliveries estimated to begin early 2027. Tesla charges commercial charging site operators 8¢/kWh all-inclusive.

## Market & Competition
- **EU mandatory driver monitoring law takes effect July 7, 2026.** Every new car registered in the EU from that date must include interior cameras tracking eye movement, head position, and attention levels, with mandatory distraction/drowsiness alerts. Applies to all manufacturers. Herbert frames this as making FSD a more attractive proposition: either the car drives itself or the driver faces constant monitoring.
- **Ford filed in-cabin AI surveillance patents in April 2026,** including lip-reading, facial expression, emotion sensing, and biometric systems. A separate patent envisions preventing the vehicle from shifting out of park if onboard sensors detect the driver is emotionally unfit to drive.
- **European Tesla registrations rebounded sharply in April 2026:** France +112%, Sweden +111%, Denmark +102%, Ireland +100%, with Europe's overall BEV share of new registrations reaching 20.5% in Q1 2026, up from 13.2% in Q1 2025. Loomis cautions one month of data is noisy and prefers Q2 year-to-date figures.
- **Musk vs. Altman / OpenAI trial underway.** Trial began April 27, 2026 in U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers). The core legal question: whether a 501(c)(3) charity can convert to for-profit while insiders hold equity, setting precedent affecting every hospital system, university endowment, and foundation in the US. Claims proceeding: breach of charitable trust and unjust enrichment. Damages sought: $134–$150 billion (would flow to OpenAI's charitable arm, not Musk personally). OpenAI Foundation retains ~26% equity in the for-profit entity, valued at ~$130 billion at conversion — larger than Ford Foundation and Gates Foundation combined. This case has no direct Tesla financial impact but carries headline risk for Musk's attention and public positioning.
- **SEC settles with Musk for $1.5 million** over the delayed 2022 Twitter stake disclosure, with no admission of wrongdoing. Removes a lingering regulatory overhang; immaterial to Tesla financials.
