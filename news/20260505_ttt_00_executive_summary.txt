## Robotaxi
- **Austin unsupervised service expands to evening hours.** Tesla's driverless Model Y robotaxis began operating at night in Austin for the first time as of last Sunday — previously service ended mid-afternoon. A single day also set a record for new unsupervised vehicle sightings: 7 new driverless Model Ys spotted across Austin, Dallas, and Houston combined.
- **Las Vegas and Doral, Florida are next.** Tesla is actively hiring robotaxi field response specialists in both cities — the identical pre-launch role it staffed in Dallas and Houston ahead of those deployments. Bhakdi and Herbert interpret active hiring of field operators as confirmation that deployment is already in motion, not merely planned.
- **Bhakdi and Herbert converge on 10,000–15,000 Cybercab robotaxis by end of 2026.** Bhakdi's floor: even halving Cybertruck's first-year ramp of ~30,000 units over nine months yields 15,000 Cybercabs. Herbert holds his consistent estimate of 10,000–15,000. Both note Cybercab's unboxed manufacturing method makes it structurally easier to ramp than Cybertruck.
- **California unsupervised service unlikely before 2027.** Per Dillon Loomis's direct contact with the CPUC, Tesla has not applied for any AV permit and currently operates under a TCP (chauffeur-class) permit. New CPUC rulemaking is underway; Tesla is on record submitting public commentary, suggesting it is shaping the framework before formally entering it. Loomis estimates 6–9 months minimum to obtain a true AV permit once applied for, putting commercial unsupervised paid service in California no earlier than sometime in 2027.
- **New California AV rules effective July 1, 2026.** Law enforcement may begin issuing traffic citations directly to AV manufacturers. Additional requirements: 50,000-mile safety test before commercial permit issuance; remote operators must hold state licenses; vehicles must connect first responders to a company rep within 30 seconds; local officials can impose emergency geofencing zones.
- **Bhakdi year-end target: above 10,000 unsupervised robotaxis; $490 near-term stock target.** He argues Tesla is a fundamentally stronger company than at its December 16 all-time high of $490, citing open driverless operations in three cities, Cybercab mass production underway, and the Tesla Semi in production. He would turn cautious on a $490 print if the unsupervised fleet is still under 100 vehicles at that point.

## Autonomous Driving
- **EU-wide FSD approval vote set for late June or early October 2026.** The Dutch RDW presented 18 months and 1 million miles of FSD testing data to EU member states at a Brussels TCMV meeting. Approval requires 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of EU population. Bhakdi assigns 30–50% probability to passage, calling it a meaningful upside surprise; his base case is rejection followed by individual-country approvals in the Netherlands, Czech Republic, Sweden, and potentially Bavaria.
- **Flanders Minister of Mobility immediately requests Tesla's FSD test dossier.** Following the Brussels meeting, the minister posted publicly that she instructed her administration to clarify a rapid homologation path for Flanders by end of week — a concrete first-mover signal from a regional authority.
- **Tesla crossed 10 billion supervised FSD miles.** Loomis contextualizes this as ~3.5 million US coast-to-coast trips. He cautions this does not appear to be a trigger for immediate unsupervised scale-up. Netherlands FSD (supervised) launched April 11 and generated 6.2 million miles in 23 days (~270,000 miles/day), illustrating strong pent-up European demand.
- **FSD 14.3.2 rolling out to ~55–60% of HW4 fleet in North America.** Early feedback is mixed: most users report no issues, but some report jitteriness, start-stop degradation, and at least one system-initiated takeover request versus the 14.2 branch. Not yet wide release. FSD subscription purchase option ends May 15; subscription-only model takes effect. FSD now accounts for ~0.25% of all miles driven in North America annually.
- **FSD v13/14.3 cited as an inflection enabling high iteration speed.** Bhakdi describes it as the version that proved unsupervised-grade capability, enabling rapid software improvement cycles.

## Humanoid Bots
- **Last Model X produced; Fremont line converting to Optimus.** Tesla shared a photo of the final Model X off the line, consistent with Elon Musk's Q1 earnings call statement that S/X lines would shut down in May to prepare for Optimus production — a concrete factory conversion step.
- **Optimus factory scale targets reiterated: 1M units/year at Fremont, 10M/year at a larger facility.** Herbert and Lutz discuss these figures, with Lutz cautioning Tesla will stair-step capex in line with market acceptance rather than commit full capital upfront — reducing enterprise risk.
- **Lutz: only 1–2 humanoid companies will reach viable scale; Tesla uniquely positioned.** Drawing a direct parallel to the Chinese EV market (80–100 starters, 1–2 real businesses), Lutz argues 80–90% of humanoid showcases will never become commercial businesses. The filter: proven mass manufacturing, vertical integration, engineering talent, proprietary AI/autonomy, and an in-house chip. Lutz states no other humanoid competitor combines all of these under one roof.
- **Western labor economics strongly favor humanoid ROI over China.** Displacing $55/hour US union labor yields immediate large-margin ROI; displacing $3.50–$5.00/hour Chinese labor requires a much longer payback curve. China also faces political disincentives to displace workers at scale. Lutz acknowledges China's supply chain advantage in linear actuators and planetary rollers but argues the US must develop domestic sourcing.
- **Figure 3 context: 55 robots produced in one week, 40–50K/year factory capacity.** Per Dillon Loomis's coverage, Figure's facility targets 1M units/year long-term. Battery life is 4–5 hours; wireless recharge via foot pads at 2 kW takes ~1 hour. New customer announcements expected within 90 days. Provided as competitive context for Optimus.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Cybercab production rate: 10–25 units/day implied by drone footage.** Joe Tegtmeyer identified 25–30 Cybercabs around Giga Texas with many being transported away over the prior week. Bhakdi estimates cumulative Cybercabs already outside the factory is likely over 200. Note: Loomis's independent read of the same footage yields a narrower estimate of 5–15/day, reflecting different assumptions.
- **Tesla Semi: 60-truck combined port drayage order.** Big F Transport (40 units) and NICA Container Freight Line (20 units) will both operate from Forum Mobility's FM Santa Fe depot in Rancho Dominguez, CA, with operations expected to begin early 2027. Big F already runs nine Daimler eCascadia trucks and tested the Semi directly before committing — a direct competitive switch. Gigafactory Nevada's new high-volume line (50,000-truck annual capacity) confirmed its first Semi last week.
- **Tesla Semi dominated California's clean truck voucher program.** Tesla Semi: 965 of 1,067 applications between January 2025 and February 2026. Daimler, PACCAR, and Volvo combined: fewer than 100.
- **Washington State $175,000 rebate cuts Tesla Semi's effective price to ~$115,000.** The WEZI program rebate on Class 8 electric trucks would bring the Long Range Semi below the ~$175,000 average price of a new diesel Class 8 truck.
- **Model Y L spotted testing in the US (Palo Alto).** Multiple sightings reported. Loomis notes a source previously confirmed the model is coming to the US despite Elon's skeptical public comments; timing would fill capacity freed by Model X line shutdown.
- **Canada Model 3 (Giga Shanghai) now priced at ~$29,000 USD equivalent** following Canada's deal reducing Chinese EV import tariffs from 100% to 6% (capped at ~49,000 vehicles/year). Performance variant dropped over 15%. Model 3 from China does not qualify for Canada's $5,000 EV incentive.

## Market & Competition
- **EU driver monitoring mandate takes effect July 7, 2026.** All new cars registered in the EU must include interior cameras tracking eye movement, head position, and attention levels, with mandatory distraction/drowsiness alerts. Herbert frames this as a bifurcation forcing consumers toward either FSD or constant monitoring nags — a structural tailwind for FSD adoption.
- **Tesla European registrations up sharply in April.** France +112%, Sweden +111%, Denmark +102%, Ireland +100%. Europe's BEV share of new car registrations reached 20.5% in Q1 2026, up from 13.2% in Q1 2025. Loomis flags single-month noise and prefers Q2 year-to-date data before drawing conclusions.
- **Musk v. Altman / OpenAI trial underway.** Trial began April 27 in U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers. Core question: whether a 501(c)(3) charity can convert to for-profit with insiders holding equity. Musk seeks $134–$150 billion in damages (flowing to OpenAI's charitable arm, not Musk). OpenAI Foundation retains ~26% equity in the for-profit entity (~$130 billion at conversion); Microsoft holds ~27%. Jury verdict is advisory only; judge makes binding ruling. Directly relevant to Tesla: resolution removes a lingering distraction risk for Musk.
- **SEC settles with Musk for $1.5M over 2022 Twitter disclosure delay.** Musk pays without admitting wrongdoing; his attorney states he has been "cleared of all issues related to the late filing." Dollar amount is immaterial; the settlement removes a regulatory overhang.

- **Tesla Q1: beat GAAP EPS estimates; FSD subscriptions added 170,000 new users.** Megapack revenue also grew. Post-earnings stock dropped to the high $360s — Bhakdi attributed this to Musk's low energy on the call, not fundamentals — and has since recovered toward ~$392–$395.
