The video walks through a new SpaceX web page describing a supercomputing campus in Cochise County, Arizona, with up to 6 GW of power and up to 24 GWh of battery storage. The host's thesis is that SpaceX's approach to securing power is the "trick" Elon Musk hinted at on the earnings call: rather than building new power plants, it invests in grid infrastructure and batteries to unlock capacity from Arizona's existing system. The host also expects Tesla to benefit from the battery demand.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **6 GW campus announced:** SpaceX's page describes a campus of nine supercomputing buildings plus support buildings, with up to 6 GW of power, to train SpaceX superintelligence models and support uses such as research, manufacturing and national security. The host notes this is about twice SpaceX's total compute by the end of 2026 and that construction starts in 2027, so a single site would double the company's capacity.
2. **Community terms and water use:** SpaceX lists $50 billion of regional investment, up to $103 million in annual property/tax revenue for the county, up to $250 million in one-time county sales tax benefit, 1,000 permanent jobs and 5,500 construction jobs. The campus is dry cooled with no evaporative spray, zero industrial water use and no new wells, with water use comparable to 20 to 30 Arizona homes. The closed-loop water is filled once and reused for up to 10 years, and SpaceX aims to reuse wastewater from the nearby Apache generating station when possible. Outdoor lights will be off except where work is underway, in line with dark-sky rules. The host sees this as SpaceX responding to political and community backlash after a contentious public meeting he watched.

## Energy
1. **Grid and cooperative payments:** SpaceX says it will reduce electricity costs for rural Arizona communities, with a projected 10 to 40% drop in bills for Sulphur Springs Valley Electric Cooperative members for the life of the project. It will transfer $3.6 billion of grid infrastructure upgrades to Arizona Electric Power Cooperative members at zero cost. At full build-out it will provide up to $260 million a year to the Sulphur Springs cooperative for bill relief and capital credits, plus up to $200 million a year to the statewide Arizona cooperative.
2. **24 GWh battery system:** The campus includes up to 24 GWh of battery storage, which the host calls the largest in the United States. By his math that could run 6 GW for about 4 hours, and he calls it likely good business for Tesla. He does not say who supplies the batteries, though he describes the plan as "SpaceX but with Tesla batteries".
3. **The host's "hack" thesis:** The host notes the materials mention no power plant build-out, only transmission, grid upgrades and batteries. He speculates that SpaceX aims to gain gigawatts from the existing Arizona system by reducing waste from mismatched supply and demand, with grid upgrades and storage doing the work. He guesses SpaceX could get roughly 1 to 3 GW from the existing grid, or 3 to 5 GW, and that 1 to 3 GW of added solar will probably still be needed. He says he does not know how much of the 6 GW this approach could cover, and these are his own estimates.
