This video features Brian Wang of Next Big Future walking through his revised financial model for SpaceX's AI/compute business following SpaceX's Q2 earnings call, arguing the company is on track to hit a $100 billion+ annualized revenue run rate by the end of 2026 and potentially far higher by 2027, driven by an aggressive buildout of terrestrial data center power capacity, premium spot-pricing for scarce Nvidia chips, and vertically integrated AI/robotics businesses.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **$100B+ run rate confirmed for year-end:** Wang states Elon Musk confirmed on the earnings call that SpaceX will exceed a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate by the end of the year, with Musk indicating the actual figure could be higher; Wang now projects roughly $110 billion by December, revised up from his prior $96 billion "bull case."
2. **$6.7 billion in new locked-in business:** SpaceX disclosed $6.7 billion of new business commitments starting in October, which per Wang implies at least two additional deals (beyond Google-scale) worth roughly $2 billion each need to start by December to hit guided targets.
3. **AI capex is scaling sharply, raising investor concern:** SpaceX reported $15.8 billion of AI-related capex already spent in Q2, which Wang says drove TSLA's stock price down (from about $124 to about $114) on concern over ballooning capital spending; he expects capex to keep rising each quarter ($30B+ in Q3, $50-60B+ in Q4 by his estimate).
4. **Payback period may shorten to 6-10 months:** Wang argues that premium "spot pricing" for scarce Nvidia chips (his own estimate, not company-stated) could let SpaceX recoup gigawatt-scale data center capex in roughly 9-10 months rather than 12-13, with further efficiency gains from standardized "mini hard" (Macrohard-style) data center units potentially shrinking this to 6-7 months.
5. **Speculative Nvidia/Micron financing partnership:** Wang speculates (his own theory, not confirmed by either company) that Nvidia and Micron may offer SpaceX favorable financing — using their strong credit ratings to lower SpaceX's borrowing costs and discount chip/memory pricing — in exchange for a revenue-share stake (he floats an 80/20 split) once SpaceX's capex is paid off.
6. **Bull case: $390B run rate by end of 2027:** Under an aggressive scenario (~8 gigawatts of AI compute), Wang models a $390 billion annualized revenue run rate by December 2027 — roughly three to four times Tesla's current annual revenue — but says he's only "50/50" confident this materializes; a more conservative low-end scenario (adding ~3 gigawatts at high spot pricing) yields roughly $300 billion.
7. **Trillion-dollar 2030 revenue seen as plausible via terrestrial-only buildout:** Wang argues Musk's stated target of ~$1 trillion revenue by 2030 could be achieved through terrestrial AI data centers alone (without orbital compute) if SpaceX can add roughly 20 gigawatts of capacity per year, though he notes this depends on scaling standardized "mini hard"/"megapod" units and possibly repurposing Tesla's Supercharger network (SpaceX has cited about 7 gigawatts of existing Supercharger power) into megapod-based data centers.
8. **Grock and enterprise AI model roadmap:** New Grock model releases are expected roughly every two to three weeks, including a "Grock 4.6" release imminently and a larger model in the following weeks; a further model (referred to as Grock 5) is expected before year-end. A cursor-specific coding/planning model is also anticipated, which Wang says could meaningfully grow SpaceX's share of enterprise AI API spend (currently estimated by him at only 5-10%, versus larger shares for Anthropic and OpenAI).
9. **Vertical integration thesis for "digital Optimus":** Wang and the host discuss Musk's strategy of vertically integrating AI into owned end-businesses (transportation, robotics, finance) rather than merely selling LLM compute or code — with Wang framing this as a way to avoid commoditization risk from cheaper open-source Chinese LLMs, though the host raises skepticism about whether the AI/digital-labor market is as large as claimed given free/open-source alternatives.

## Energy
1. **SpaceX power buildout on track through year-end:** Wang notes SpaceX had roughly 1.4 gigawatts of power live in July (with another ~200 megawatts of turbines installed but not yet connected), and reaffirms his prior call that the company will exceed 2 gigawatts of terrestrial power by end of 2026, which he says is now essentially locked in and not a bottleneck; further scaling toward a stated 15-20 gigawatt target by end of 2027 depends heavily on additional turbine deliveries (including large units from a Korean supplier) arriving through Q1 of next year.
