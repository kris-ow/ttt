This Munro Live video is a deep engineering interview with Rivian's R2 vehicle hardware engineering lead and program chief engineer, covering the R2's structural battery pack, cell architecture, crash-load design, and front suspension. Tesla is invoked only as a benchmarking reference point when discussing structural battery pack layouts and front casting design, making this primarily a competitor deep-dive with limited direct Tesla investment relevance.

## Market & Competition
1. **Rivian R2 battery pack compared structurally to Tesla packs:** Munro's host notes the R2's structural battery pack architecture "looks very very similar" to Tesla's Model Y and Cybertruck packs, prompting Rivian engineers to explain their benchmarking process — they say they study competitor packs to understand where technology is heading but design components based on R2-specific requirements rather than copying designs.
2. **Front casting design contrasted with Tesla's giga-casting approach:** The host observes that Tesla's Model Y and Cybertruck battery packs interface with the vehicle front via a single Giga Casting, whereas Rivian's R2 uses a two-part casting with bolted-on components and stepped geometry engineered for controlled fracture behavior in different crash scenarios, and to manage manufacturing variation between the body and battery pack.
3. **Rivian's R2 cost and mass reduction versus R1:** Rivian engineers state R2's chassis structure components achieved roughly a 49% cost reduction and about an 8% weight reduction versus R1 by switching from aluminum castings to stamped steel for lower control arms, subframes, and rear links, while also cutting high-voltage cabling costs by roughly 70% through electronics consolidation into a single serviceable "powerhouse" unit under the rear seat.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **R2 battery pack architecture and cell details:** The R2 uses a 4695-format cell with 7,776 cells per pack (versus R1's 2170-format cell, also using 7,776 cells), in a structural, non-serviceable pack potted directly into the enclosure; engineers say the design saved 7mm of vertical space versus a non-integrated floor/pack approach, which was reallocated to ground-strike protection for off-roading and additional cell venting space.
2. **Front suspension redesign for R2:** R2 moves from R1's semi-active dampers, hydraulic roll control, and airbag suspension to a simplified coil spring, steel anti-roll bar, and MacPherson strut setup, with the front drive unit disconnecting to bias the vehicle toward rear-wheel drive and engage all-wheel drive via a front clutch; engineers describe this as their third-generation version of that disconnect system.
3. **Drive unit and inverter packaging for frunk space:** To maximize front trunk volume, Rivian relocated the front drive unit's inverter to the side of the unit rather than the top, requiring in-house-designed motor controller boards to fit the new geometry — a vertically integrated engineering effort the team says required years of coordinated design work across packaging, structural, and electronics teams.

Notable quotes and data:
- R2 pack: 4695-format cells, 7,776 cells per pack (vs. R1's 2170-format cell, also 7,776 cells)
- High-voltage cabling cost reduced ~70% from R1 to R2 via electronics consolidation
- 7mm of packaging space saved by integrating body floor structure into the battery pack
- Front longitudinal structures in the pack spec'd to withstand 120 kN of frontal load
- Chassis structure components: ~49% cost reduction and ~8% weight reduction via aluminum-to-steel material swaps
- "Make the egg structural" — host's characterization of the shift from protecting the battery pack in a crash to using it as part of the crash-mitigation structure
- Battery pack potting forms a structural bond to the outer enclosure, making the pack non-serviceable at the cell level by design
