## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Texas robotaxi tracker logged a jump of 58 vehicles added overnight on July 15, called the largest single-day increase observed to date, per jobhakdi. Tesla has also begun adding door-projection "Robotaxi" logo lighting to Model Y robotaxis, a visible branding update, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Cybertrucks are reportedly piling up in lots nationwide — over 100 at a Houston robotaxi hub as of July 10, roughly 100 in Dallas, with sightings in New York and the Bay Area — which jobhakdi's host reads as a leading indicator of anticipated scaled deployment, though he stresses this is deduction from capex, not confirmed Tesla guidance.
- Electrek notes the flagship Austin service still runs only about 20 more vehicles than at launch over a year ago, and that Tesla quietly softened its "1H 2026" five-city expansion commitment to vaguer "preparations underway" language in April.
- A construction-focused analyst on brighterwithherbert estimates Tesla could produce roughly 2,000 Cybercabs a week on one shift (annualizing toward a quarter- to half-million units) if software and operational constraints were removed, arguing manufacturing already outpaces deployment readiness; he pegs 100–300 operational vehicles per city as the threshold for a credible robotaxi program.
- Cleaning-depot construction confirmed near Las Vegas, a cleaning-depot permit seen in Dallas, and a 25-stall fleet charging permit reportedly spotted in Texas, per the same analyst.

## Financials
- Tesla's Q2 earnings call is July 22; nearly 300 investor questions have been submitted via Say Technologies. The two top-voted questions (each carrying 4.8 million TSLA shares in votes) cover Optimus Gen 3 production/task ramp by end-2027 and constraints on robotaxi expansion — far ahead of the third-place question (under 975,000 shares), which Electrek's Fred Lambert suggests points to a single large shareholder pushing friendly questions to the top.
- A heavily upvoted question notes Tesla has missed short-term robotaxi guidance for three straight earnings reports, from 50% US coverage by end-2025 to seven new cities in 1H26. Lambert argues the real bottleneck is software/safety validation on the shared Model Y robotaxi/Cybercab stack, not production capacity.
- Another top question presses Tesla on its plan for HW3 owners who cannot get unsupervised FSD — free upgrades, transfers, refunds, timeline, and accrued cost — after Musk confirmed the HW3 limitation for a second consecutive year; Lambert expects Tesla to avoid a concrete answer.
- Shareholder questions also probe a possible Tesla-SpaceX merger and a "Digital Optimus" collaboration with SpaceXAI (capital contribution, revenue share), reflecting fear that AI value is migrating to a company shareholders don't own.

## Humanoid Bots
- A speaker on jobhakdi claims Tesla is installing an Optimus production line sized for roughly 1 million robots/year and predicts an August launch, calling it unpriced by the market — explicitly his own interpretation, not a confirmed Tesla disclosure.
- Separately, a construction analyst on brighterwithherbert pegs the Giga Texas robotics factory shell as roughly 14 months from completion, with full production readiness about two to two-and-a-half years out, since the Optimus design is still being iterated on the Fremont pilot line.
- On a separate brighterwithherbert episode, guest analyst Brian argues Chinese humanoid robots (e.g., Xiaoang) show real, fast progress, but says comparisons to Optimus are unfair since Tesla's most recent public Optimus demo is about two years old and newer capability is being withheld until closer to production.
- Randy Kirk (futureaza) notes Mitsubishi has announced humanoid robot manufacturing/deployment plans targeting 2027, arguing a maturing robotics supply chain (hands, actuators, batteries) is lowering barriers for new entrants.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's Cortex 2 AI training data center at Giga Texas is roughly 70% built, over 100 megawatts, with about half already operational (training Optimus) and the other half now in commissioning, per the brighterwithherbert construction analyst.
- NTSB's preliminary report on last month's Model 3 house-crash found the driver pressed the accelerator to 100%, overriding FSD Supervised — a finding that supports Tesla's defense that driver action, not FSD malfunction, caused the crash, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Guest analyst Brian (brighterwithherbert) says HW3/AI3 FSD is closing the gap with newer hardware and relays a secondhand account of a Florida-to-Pacific-Northwest road trip on the newest "14 Lite" release with zero disengagements; he doesn't expect Tesla to abandon HW3 owners given subscription revenue, though he doesn't expect old AI3 cars to join the robotaxi network.
- jobhakdi's host reiterates FSD is not yet ready to support commercial robotaxi scaling, arguing Tesla's Cybercab capex ramp implies an internal belief the software will hit a critical inflection point in H2 2026 — explicitly his own deduction, not confirmed Tesla guidance; he also flags his own failed June 2026 scaling prediction.

## Market & Competition
- Xpeng launched the L03 SUV-coupe across 60+ European markets from €34,990, undercutting the Model Y by about €3,400 in Germany and roughly $10,400 in Norway; deliveries start Q4. It out-charges the Model Y Standard (236kW vs. 175kW) but offers less range (445km vs. 534km WLTP), per Electrek, which notes Xpeng logged a fourth straight monthly European registration record (4,600+ vehicles in June).
- Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla's US pricing is currently insulated by tariffs rather than competitiveness, and that Europe — where Xpeng isn't tariff-blocked — will show what the market looks like without that insulation.
- Guest analyst Brian says reported BYD discounts as steep as ~40% (vs. Tesla's 5–8%) are unverified but would be concerning if true; he disputes claims that most Chinese EV makers are unprofitable, estimating a majority are gross-margin positive even if net-negative from expansion spending. He also says BYD's 180–230+ day supplier payment deferrals were banned by China's government and largely unwound by year-end.
- A survey cited by Sawyer Merritt found only 0.5% of EV drivers would switch back to gas/diesel if buying today, underscoring strong EV retention.

## Energy
- Megapack is framed as financially significant but secondary to autonomy/robotics in Tesla's valuation, per the brighterwithherbert construction analyst: roughly 10 GWh/quarter from Lathrop and 7–10 GWh/quarter from Shanghai, with the Houston factory (~25% more capacity than Lathrop) expected to start production by year-end but not contribute meaningful revenue until second half of next year.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 13's countdown was scrubbed during final Raptor ignition on the Super Heavy booster; SpaceX confirmed a full-day scrub with propellant offload underway and no immediate word on cause or next attempt, per futureaza's livestream coverage. Planned objectives had included a boost-back burn, in-space engine relight, and deployment of 20 real V3 Starlink satellites — the first flight to carry operational satellites rather than mass simulators.
- SpaceX stock dipped in after-hours trading following the scrub, which hosts attributed to reactive retail/algo trading rather than a serious technical issue.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Guest analyst Brian (brighterwithherbert) argues against a near-term Tesla-SpaceX merger, calling relative valuation "unknowable" until Starship, orbital data centers, and Optimus/autonomy ramp further; the host countered that running joint projects (Terafab, Optimus, AI training) across two separately governed companies creates inefficiency, making a merger more efficient long-term.
- The two speakers disputed how well Tesla retail shareholders fared in the SpaceX IPO (~$85 billion), with Brian citing allocations as small as one or two shares for some retail investors as inadequate, while the host called Tesla's retail allocation percentage higher than any recent IPO he's aware of.
