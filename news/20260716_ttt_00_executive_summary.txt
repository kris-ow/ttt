## Brief
- NTSB: driver hit 100% accelerator, overrode FSD in Model 3 house crash

- Shareholders flood Q2 Q&A with missed robotaxi/FSD/Optimus targets, HW3 refund questions

## Robotaxi
- Active Robotaxi fleet: 769 per RobotaxiTracker.com (Bay Area 633, Austin 105, Dallas 17, Houston 14). Tesla registered 58 additional Model Ys for the Texas fleet, and door-projection lights displaying the "Robotaxi" logo are now appearing on Model Y robotaxis, per Sawyer Merritt.
- A Texas AV tracker cited by jobhakdi showed 58 robotaxis added overnight on July 15 — the largest single-day increase observed to date; Cybercabs are reportedly piling up in lots in Houston (100+ as of July 10), Dallas (~100), New York, and the Bay Area, which the host reads as inventory staging ahead of a scaling push.
- One analyst (brighterwithherbert) estimates Tesla could produce ~2,000 Cybercabs/week on one shift — potentially a quarter- to half-million units annualized — arguing manufacturing already outpaces software/deployment readiness; the same analyst frames 100-300 vehicles per city as the threshold for a credible service, versus ~50/city being inadequate.
- Waymo's Jaguar I-Pace retrofit was described by the analyst as having roughly as many parts as an entire Cybercab (corroborated by Dillon Loomis of Electrified), framed as a costly "steampunk" approach versus Tesla's purpose-built "uncar."
- Cleaning-depot construction is underway near Las Vegas with another permitted in Dallas; a 25-stall fleet-charging permit was reportedly spotted, likely in Texas.
- Shareholder Q&A ahead of the July 22 earnings call highlights that Tesla has missed short-term robotaxi guidance three quarters running, most recently on a seven-new-cities-in-1H26 target; Austin's fleet is said to be only about 20 vehicles larger than at launch over a year ago, per Electrek.

## Autonomous Driving
- Cortex 2, Tesla's second Giga Texas AI training cluster (100+ MW, 12 cooling towers), is ~70% built, with half already training Optimus and the other half in commissioning, per brighterwithherbert's analyst guest.
- Hardware 3 (AI3) is reportedly closing the capability gap with newer hardware in some tests, per Brian on brighterwithherbert, though the gap widened again on a later comparison; both speakers expect Tesla to keep supporting HW3 owners for subscription revenue but doubt HW3 vehicles will join the robotaxi network.
- Shareholders are pressing Musk for a concrete plan — refunds, transfers, or free upgrades — for HW3 owners who paid for FSD, given his confirmation (for a second year running) that HW3 cannot reach unsupervised FSD; Electrek's Fred Lambert expects Tesla to avoid a direct answer on the earnings call.
- NTSB's preliminary report on last month's Model 3 house-crash found the driver pressed the accelerator to 100%, overriding FSD (Supervised) — supporting Tesla's defense that driver action, not FSD, caused the crash.

## Humanoid Bots
- Optimus factory at Giga Texas has foundations in with heavier footings than the main campus; one analyst expects the building shell done in ~14 months but full production scale ~2-2.5 years out, with the design still being iterated on Fremont's former Model S/X line.
- A separate speaker (jobhakdi) claims the line under construction is sized for ~1 million robots/year and predicts an Optimus launch "very likely" in August — explicitly his own speculative interpretation, not a confirmed Tesla disclosure.
- Chinese humanoid robots (e.g., Xiaoang) are advancing quickly, but comparisons to Optimus are premature since Tesla's last public demo is ~2 years old and newer capability is being withheld, per Brian on brighterwithherbert; Figure's and Xiaomi's recent demos were characterized as unimpressive or merely on par with old Optimus footage.
- Mitsubishi announced plans to manufacture humanoid robots, reportedly targeting 2027, joining a maturing supply chain (actuators, hands, batteries) that lowers entry barriers versus when Tesla started Optimus, per futureaza.

## Electric Vehicles
- Xpeng's L03 launched across 60+ European markets from €34,990, undercutting the Model Y by roughly €3,400 in Germany and about $10,400 in Norway (where its price sits just under a VAT-exemption threshold); it out-charges the Model Y Standard (236kW vs 175kW) but offers less range (445km vs 534km WLTP). Fred Lambert (Electrek) argues this shows Tesla's US pricing is currently insulated by tariffs rather than competitiveness, and that Europe will reveal the market without that insulation.
- A third-party distraction test cited by futureaza found screen-heavy interiors like Tesla's caused less eyes-off-road distraction than button-heavy rivals (Mercedes CLA ~69 ft vs. a Nissan model at roughly half a mile), pushing back on the common criticism of Tesla's minimalist controls.
- A survey found only 0.5% of EV drivers would switch back to gas/diesel if buying today, per Sawyer Merritt.
- Tesla partnered with 7-Eleven Japan on Superchargers, opening its first site July 11 in Kawasaki — Japan's 150th Supercharger location.

## Market & Competition
- China's EV price war may be intensifying — discounts as steep as ~40% at BYD were cited versus ~5-8% at Tesla, though Brian (brighterwithherbert) said he hadn't verified the BYD figure and pushed back on claims that most Chinese automakers are unprofitable, estimating a majority remain gross-margin positive.
- BYD's practice of deferring supplier payments 180-230+ days was banned by Beijing and largely unwound by year-end, per Brian, contradicting some US analysts who claimed it continued; BYD also discontinued the Seal 7 domestically ahead of the flash-charging Seal 8.
- Chinese auto plants toured by Brian, including BYD's, were described as modern and comparable to Tesla's Fremont or Ford's Rouge facility, with BYD's campus (~2mi x 2mi) larger than all Tesla factories combined.

## Financials
- Nearly 300 shareholder questions have been submitted via Say Technologies ahead of the July 22 Q2 call; the top two (Optimus Gen 3 ramp, robotaxi expansion constraints) carry 4.8 million TSLA shares in votes each, far ahead of third place, which Electrek's Fred Lambert suggests indicates whale-driven question-stacking. Tesla posted a record Q2 with 480,126 deliveries.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Flight 13 (second flight of Starship V3/Booster V3 with Raptor 3 engines) was scrubbed when a hold/abort triggered during booster ignition; SpaceX confirmed a full scrub with propellant offload underway and no word yet on cause or next attempt. Planned objectives included deploying 20 real V3 Starlink satellites for the first time (versus mass simulators), an in-space engine relight, and splashdown in the Indian Ocean.
- SpaceX stock dipped in after-hours trading following the scrub; futureaza hosts attributed this to reactive retail/algo trading rather than a technical red flag.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- Debate over a Tesla-SpaceX merger continued on brighterwithherbert: guest Brian argued against a near-term merger, calling relative valuation "unknowable" until Starship, orbital data centers, and Optimus/autonomy mature further, and noting Musk already controls both companies; the host countered that split governance creates friction on shared projects like Terafab and Optimus.
- Shareholder questions ahead of Tesla's earnings call reflect fear that Musk could merge Tesla into SpaceX on terms favoring his larger SpaceX stake, following SpaceX's $1.25 trillion all-stock absorption of xAI in February; one top question asks Musk to commit to hitting at least half his 2025 pay-package goals before considering any acquisition offer.
