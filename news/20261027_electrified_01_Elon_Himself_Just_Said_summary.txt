This video from Dillon Loomis covers SpaceX's large capex needs and Starlink Mobile buildout plans, Tesla's robotaxi fleet surpassing key milestones versus Waymo, new FSD safety data, Optimus development expectations, and a technical read on TSLA/SPCX stock momentum.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **SpaceX capex outpacing cash on hand:** The host says SpaceX's Q4 capex alone is set to exceed $40 billion, implying roughly $85 billion for the year, with analyst consensus (per the host) around $250 billion of capex for next year; he notes SpaceX's cash balance (gross, not net) is in the neighborhood of this spending, which he argues explains why the company recently secured a reported $40 billion in chip financing. He adds that SpaceX's investment-grade status likely secures favorable borrowing rates, and frames continued debt-market access as a competitive moat if, as Elon Musk reportedly said, debt markets have tightened for AI companies without outside revenue.
2. **Starlink Mobile timeline and cost debate:** Verizon's CEO reportedly said Starlink Mobile buildout would take 4–6 years and $300–500 billion; Elon Musk replied it would take two to three years. SpaceX COO Gwynne Shotwell said on the Q3 call that the near-term priority is flying the mobile V2 constellation through next year and integrating spectrum purchases to turn on service in early 2028. The host reconciles these as not contradictory, estimating a fuller buildout around late 2028–2029, and notes Musk did not dispute the spending figures, implying roughly $400 billion at the midpoint.
3. **Cell tower leasing talks for hybrid network:** SpaceX is reportedly looking to secure access to roughly 80,000 cell sites in the US (about half of the ~158,000 purpose-built towers nationally), reportedly in talks with Crown Castle (~40,000 towers), American Tower (~42,000 sites), and Vertical Bridge (~18,000 sites) to pair traditional towers with its own femtocell technology for Starlink Mobile's terrestrial network. The host flags this reporting as speculative pending an official SpaceX presentation.
4. **CTIA pushes FCC on satellite rules:** The CTIA, the main US wireless trade group (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile), sent a letter asking the FCC to remove TDD bans from consideration for supplemental coverage from space (SCS) and to raise performance requirements for direct-to-device satellite service — moves the host characterizes as protecting incumbent carriers against competition from Starlink's hybrid network.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas robotaxi fleet growth:** Tesla has 151 robotaxis registered in Texas, of which 544 are Cybercabs — meaning Cybercabs now outnumber Model Ys in that registered fleet — with 505 robotaxis added in October alone, according to the host (note: the 151 and 544 figures as stated appear internally inconsistent, likely a transcription issue; reported as stated).
2. **Fleet size vs. Waymo:** The host notes Waymo has about 4,000 vehicles in its active fleet, up from roughly 3,000 at the end of last year (~1,000 added over the year), and argues that with Tesla adding 500 robotaxis in a single month, there's a real chance Tesla surpasses Waymo's total fleet size by the first half of next year.
3. **Weekly mileage surpassing Waymo in Austin:** A chart shared from a robotaxi tracker reportedly showed Tesla doing about 438,000 miles per week in Austin for the first time, exceeding Waymo's expected weekly mileage of about 406,000 miles per week; Elon Musk responded calling it "never a real contest" and separately commented "Log curve in blue, exponential in red" on the chart.
4. **Musk's robotaxi social media activity:** Over the prior 48 hours Musk posted about robotaxi expansion repeatedly, including commenting "coming soon" when asked about extending the Tampa/Miami geofence to include beaches, and sharing posts comparing Tesla's Houston geofence favorably to Waymo.
5. **Cybercab validation fleet growth:** Cybercab is set to launch public service in Dallas in November; a shared video showed a large lot of Cybercabs (the host estimates roughly 300) without public-service badging, indicating they remain validation vehicles. A separate video showed an unbadged Cybercab stopped beyond a railroad crossing gate (believed to be in Seattle, not in live service), which the host says highlights an edge case Tesla still needs to solve.
6. **NHTSA rulemaking proposal (FMVSS 108):** NHTSA is proposing to amend FMVSS 108 (lamps, reflective devices, and associated equipment), which could remove requirements like hand/foot-operated turn signals and introduce optional cyan marker lamps signaling a self-driving vehicle — a feature the host notes Cybercab's front light bar can already support. This remains only a proposal.
7. **NHTSA Cybercab audit response due:** Tesla's response to NHTSA's audit query into the Cybercab is due October 30.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **New FSD safety data released:** Tesla updated its FSD safety data and active miles-driven tracker, now over 16.7 billion miles, claiming FSD improves US road safety by over 80%, with an estimate of 32,000+ lives potentially saved and 1.9 million+ injuries potentially avoided annually. The host explains this is derived by comparing Tesla's miles driven per collision under FSD to the US average, then extrapolating that improvement to NHTSA's national fatality/injury data; he notes a Tesla on FSD drives about 10 times more miles than the US average before a major collision and 8 times more before a minor collision.
2. **Future FSD improvements expected:** The host expects Tesla's upcoming FSD version and new in-house mapping feature to address remaining limitations like speed limits, parking, and navigation, and highlights a comment from Tesla's Ashok Elluswamy that the system will eventually "know you better than you know yourself," alongside potential voice-instruction integration via Grok.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Skepticism on Optimus hand dexterity:** Computer programmer and game developer John Carmack reportedly said no current robot can perform fine-motor tasks like changing batteries in a jack-o'-lantern or extracting a screwdriver and unscrewing tiny screws, arguing there's a long way to go to replicate full human hand capability — echoing Musk's own comments about the difficulty of replicating human finger dexterity.
2. **Musk sets expectations for Optimus V3 demo:** Musk listed extreme benchmark tasks (assembling a Swiss watch one-handed while looking elsewhere, catching a 100 mph baseball blind, juggling flaming chainsaws on a unicycle) as examples of "real benchmarks," but the host pushes back on community speculation that these specific feats are the actual plan for the upcoming Optimus V3 demo, stating Musk has instead indicated the demo will show Optimus performing a task autonomously in a previously unseen environment.
