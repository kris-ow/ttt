Tesla released a new "Emergency Drive Away" feature allowing drivers to shift into Drive and leave a Supercharger while still plugged in, two months after a fatal shooting at a Twin Falls, Idaho Supercharger station.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **New feature bypasses charge port lockout:** Tesla's charging team announced "Emergency Drive Away" on X, removing the prior requirement to unlock the charge port (via touchscreen, app, or handle button) and wait for the latch before shifting out of Park while plugged in. Tesla explicitly warned that using the feature "will damage your vehicle and the Supercharger," said it is intended for emergencies only, and that deliberate misuse will bring unspecified "additional penalties."
2. **Requirements and limits:** The feature requires software update 2026.38.3; Model S and Model X vehicles built before 2021 are not supported. Tesla said it works at all Superchargers in the US but is limited to the US "for now."
3. **Context — Twin Falls shooting:** On August 1, a gunman killed three people and injured seven at an In-N-Out in Twin Falls, Idaho, firing on people in two Teslas at an adjacent Supercharger station; one victim, a 66-year-old man from Salt Lake City, died. Tesla's announcement did not mention the shooting. The article notes this was not the first deadly Supercharger shooting, citing a 2023 killing of a Tesla driver at a Colorado station. Following Twin Falls, the article says hundreds of owners began promoting a $299 breakaway adapter called EVject, which Tesla had sued in 2024 over claims it could reach 100°C during high-current charging.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert credits Tesla for shipping a fix within two months of Twin Falls, matching the site's prior call for Tesla (as controller of both car and charger) to build a gated solution. However, he criticizes it as a crude implementation — a software lockout removal that risks damaging the car and charger rather than a clean power-cut/latch-release solution — and questions why the feature is US-only despite Canada using the same connector hardware. He ultimately agrees the US-only limitation reflects that such shootings are largely a US problem, and reiterates his August view that the real solution is gun control and mental health investment, not a software update.
