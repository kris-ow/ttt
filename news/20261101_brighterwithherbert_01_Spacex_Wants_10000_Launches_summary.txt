This video covers a cluster of SpaceX developments: a filing to the FAA revealing SpaceX's ambition to eventually launch 10,000 times a year (primarily from its Starbase Louisiana site), progress on the Raptor 4 engine, the upcoming Starship flight 15 (and plans for flight 16 from Florida), NASA's updated Artemis lunar lander timeline, and Starlink's subscriber growth including new mobile service in Brazil and a favorable FCC spectrum vote. A guest, identified as a venture capital investor and entrepreneur, joins to discuss the implications.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **SpaceX tells FAA it wants 10,000 launches/year:** In comments to the FAA — which is soliciting input on spaceport siting and airspace priority ahead of a planned capacity of 10,000 launches/year by 2035 — SpaceX stated that rockets are no longer the bottleneck; the FAA's air traffic processes are. SpaceX said its Starbase Louisiana site is designed for 30 launches and re-entries per day, and Elon Musk stated that the 10,000-launches-per-year figure is from Starbase Louisiana alone. The guest noted this would require roughly 8–12 launch platforms at that site and large-scale oxygen, helium, nitrogen, and methane supply infrastructure.
2. **SpaceX built its own airspace management tool:** To address what it calls the limiting factor (outdated FAA air traffic processes), SpaceX has developed its own air traffic/spacecraft management software — a tool the host says is already used for free by most major US airlines — rather than waiting on government systems to modernize.
3. **FAA licensed 182 launches in all of 2025:** The host notes SpaceX alone is now seeking more than 50 times that annual figure, illustrating the scale of the proposed expansion.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Raptor 4 targets 300 tons of thrust:** Elon Musk said Raptor 4 will produce roughly 300 tons of thrust at sea level, hopefully at about the same mass as Raptor 3. While that's only ~7% more than Raptor 3's rated figure, Musk noted Raptor 3 currently only reliably reaches about 230 tons, making the real-world jump closer to 30%. The guest speculated (own estimate, not confirmed) that Raptor 3's reliable thrust could still climb to around 250–260 tons with further development, and guessed Raptor 4 is roughly two years out, cautioning this is highly uncertain given SpaceX's parallel work on multiple launch complexes, test stands, and vehicle versions.
2. **Ship 42 static fires complete, clearing Flight 15:** Ship 42 finished static fire testing, clearing the way for Starship Flight 15, expected next month (November). This flight is planned to attempt catching both the booster and the ship. Flight 16 is expected to be the first Starship launch from Florida (pad 39A, the Apollo 11 launch site), with booster 23 reportedly running parallel flows to support both flights; the host described a December target for Flight 16 as achievable only if logistics (barging booster and ship components to Florida, stacking, integration) go very smoothly.
3. **NASA posts new Artemis/lunar lander milestone timeline:** NASA shared an updated SpaceX development timeline: propellant transfer demo and cabin testing in Q1 (early next year), landing leg and airlock demos plus barge-to-Cape in Q2, uncrewed demo tanker and lander flights in Q3, an uncrewed lunar lander mission in Q4, with the crewed Artemis mission itself targeted for 2028. In-orbit fuel transfer tests (filling a tanker, then transferring fuel to a moon-bound Starship) are slated for next year. The guest characterized this as a tight race against Blue Origin, whose lander currently appears ahead of SpaceX's progress, though Blue Origin also faces its own setback (needing to rebuild a launch pad after an explosion) before returning to flight.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink subscriber base surpasses AT&T Fiber:** Starlink now has more global subscribers than AT&T has on Fiber — roughly 13.6 million versus 12.6 million — after tripling its subscriber base in seven quarters. Starlink is adding roughly 1.6 million subscribers per quarter versus about 400,000 for AT&T Fiber. The guest noted Starlink's addressable market is global while AT&T's is US-only, giving Starlink a structurally larger total addressable market, though competing against AT&T's cellular network specifically within the US remains a separate, harder challenge.
2. **Starlink Mobile goes live across all of Brazil:** Starlink Mobile launched nationwide in Brazil, reportedly reaching 50 million potential customers and already connecting 7 million phones for apps and messaging in cellular dead zones (voice/video not yet supported). It's described as the first service to cover an entire country this way.
3. **FCC approves expanded satellite spectrum over AT&T/Verizon/T-Mobile objections:** The FCC voted to approve roughly 482 MHz of additional spectrum for direct-to-device satellite services like Starlink Mobile, expanding eligible bands (the host cited a move from 5 to roughly 10–13 bands). AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile had lobbied against the move. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr was described by the guest as strongly favorable toward the measure, which passed.
