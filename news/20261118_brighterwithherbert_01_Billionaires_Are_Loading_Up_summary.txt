The video reviews Q3 13F filings showing large investors adding to Tesla and SpaceX, then discusses what the different buyers' motives imply for the possible Tesla–SpaceX merger. The guest, Joe Beckby, argues SpaceX is fundamentally stronger but that a merger premium favors Tesla shareholders, so he sees both stocks as equally interesting. He also warns of heavy macro and cybersecurity risk.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Nvidia and Alphabet are the big SpaceX holders:** Nvidia added about 7.7 million SpaceX Class A shares in Q3, lifting its stake 6.3% to about 130 million shares worth nearly $20 billion. That makes SpaceX Nvidia's largest holding by value, ahead of Intel. Alphabet added about 3.85 million shares to reach 555 million shares worth about $83 billion. The guest argues strategic holders such as Google, Nvidia and Anthropic understand SpaceX best as suppliers or customers. He speculates Nvidia's buying could be circular financing but concedes that open-market purchases would not send money to SpaceX.
2. **Financial funds add smaller positions:** David Tepper's Appaloosa raised its stake 17-fold to $557 million, Ark bought about $53 million, and Stanley Druckenmiller opened a roughly $28 million position. The guest sees Tepper, a value and distressed-asset investor, as a signal that SpaceX is a real cash-flow story rather than a fairy tale.
3. **Altimeter rotates from SpaceX to Tesla:** Brad Gerstner's Altimeter opened a Tesla position of about $680 million and cut its SpaceX stake 41% (about $159 million of sales). Nvidia remains Altimeter's largest holding at 22% of a $10.3 billion portfolio, and Meta rose 25%. The guest's read is that Gerstner is positioning for a merger in which Tesla holders get a premium, effectively buying SpaceX at a discount through Tesla. He notes these are September 30 snapshots.

## Financials
1. **Merger math favors Tesla holders:** The guest expects a merger next year, possibly announced this year. He says the premium is paid by SpaceX, so it is an incentive for Tesla and an obstacle for SpaceX, and that SpaceX's profits would also accrue to Tesla holders. He guesses a realistic premium of 20–40%. He argues Tesla approval needs 50% plus one of all shares, so institutions must be won over, and that Musk cannot influence the special committees. A post cited by the host has Musk replying "of course" to the idea that Tesla's $1.6 trillion market value already includes moderate Cybercab success.
2. **SpaceX earnings power:** The guest cites SpaceX annualized revenue of $100–150 billion over the next six weeks and forecasts of roughly $250 billion in profit next year, which he equates to about 5 million Cybercabs operating. He says SpaceX will become the world's largest company by revenue within three years at most. He also points to orbital AI compute planned for 2028–2029. These are his own forecasts.
3. **Duan Yongping adds to Tesla:** His firm H&H added 699,000 Tesla shares (+20%) in Q3, for about 4 million shares worth $1.45 billion. That is 8.4% of its US-disclosed assets, up from 7.4% in Q2.
4. **State Street crosses 3%:** Per a 13F report cited from Alexandra Merz, State Street now holds over 3% of Tesla. The guest notes that threshold matters for bringing suits under Texas rules, and that State Street voted no on Musk-related board proposals.

## Market & Competition
1. **Macro and cyber risk:** The guest flags an unusually risky market: Nvidia leadership turmoil, a wave of suspected cyberattacks, and the possibility of a 30% crash or a cyber black swan. He says he trusts no price predictions right now. He considers SpaceX the most resilient and Tesla also on the sturdy side, since neither depends on AI capex or neo-cloud financing. He also warns of AI-driven job-loss risk around 2029 and says to avoid margin and short puts.
