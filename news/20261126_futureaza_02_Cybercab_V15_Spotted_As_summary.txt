FutureAzA host Brian walks through recent drone footage of Giga Texas with Joe Tegtmeyer, the long-running chronicler of the site's construction. Their thesis is that the site is reconfiguring quickly around Tesla's next phase. The Cybercab is in volume (V1.5 units are everywhere), Cortex 2 is being expanded as the training hub for Optimus, and Model Y L demand, 4680 cells and casting logistics are driving visible production changes.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab V1.5 coincides with the Dallas launch:** Tesla opened Cybercab access in Dallas and calls the vehicles V1.5. The most visible change is a new camera in the rear hatch. The hosts say it lets the vehicle detect items left in the trunk and helps with security against misbehaving riders. Joe believes Cybercabs seen leaving the factory's east side the day before the Dallas opening were likely these V1.5 units.
2. **Crash-test fleet and engineering samples:** Dozens of Cybercabs have rotated through the crash test center over roughly the last year. They span early engineering versions and later builds, both with and without steering wheels, plus units with Starlink cutouts. A separate group of unbadged Cybercabs, which Joe reads as engineering samples mixing older wrapped units and newer ones, has sat in storage with a nearly unchanged count for at least two months. He speculates Tesla may be gathering data on long-term storage without charging.
3. **Production units with a darker Starlink panel:** A lot of badged Cybercabs, which Joe says means no steering wheel or pedals, show a darker Starlink hatch panel. Joe thinks the change is deliberate. The person responsible for plastic panel production has told him the team uses his photos to assess panel quality. He thinks some of these units could go to customers, though he considers that early since Tesla is talking about December.
4. **Enclosed transport returns:** Cybercabs are again being shipped in closed transport trucks after a period on open carriers. Joe reads this as an effort to move them with less attention, while calling it speculation. Cybercabs are now so numerous on the test track that the hosts joke Tesla has more than it knows what to do with.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Cortex 2 as "Optimus University":** Joe describes Cortex 2 as where Optimus's brain is trained, with the Optimus plant just to the north and an advanced chip factory under construction nearby, putting brain, body and chips in one cluster. Recent changes include:
   - an addition of roughly 25% more internal space, probably for more compute;
   - a pit that looks like a third cooling system;
   - a water reclamation system that captures cooling water, replacing the two much larger reservoirs in the original site plans with two smaller tanks.
2. **Chip fab building with unusual roof trusses:** The fab under construction uses massive roof trusses not seen elsewhere at Giga Texas. Steel frames sit on the west and east sides with one central column, implying a largely open interior. Joe guesses this gives maximum flexibility for chip-making, test and lithography equipment, but stresses it is a guess.
3. **Powering the Optimus plant:** The electrical substation has been doubled in size. Twin underground concrete channels now run from it toward Cortex 2. Joe is watching whether one branches north, since he expects that is how power will reach the Optimus factory.
4. **Warehouse moves to clear space:** A newly paved warehouse and wheel-storage area, shown in footage taken Wednesday, November 18, is absorbing the east-side warehouses. Joe says this frees room for the advanced chip factory.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y L demand driving shift changes:** Joe says Tesla is overwhelmed with Model Y L orders and is lengthening shifts and sometimes adding new ones, some reportedly tied to Cybercab and some to Model Y. He expects something substantial in the new year (2027).
   - The Model Y drop-off point has moved, and a red Model Y L is seen leaving in autonomous mode.
   - Joe relays Musk's recent remark that the Model Y L might become all Tesla makes, and thinks Musk is exaggerating.
2. **4680 expansion:** A dedicated cathode building now supplies 4680 cells. Tesla says 4680 production is ramping well yet still cites a cell shortage, and Joe believes both are partly true. Inside the 4680 building, Joe has seen:
   - the first floor and upper-floor stations reworked;
   - new equipment brought in;
   - sunken truck docks added;
   - large cooling pipes installed.
   
   He assumes this means a significant capacity expansion but has not been inside recently.
3. **Casting logistics overhaul:** A former secondary glass entrance has been rebuilt into six new loading gates. It now has a vertical reciprocating conveyor about 80–85 feet tall that lifts casting racks into the building, similar to a system Joe saw at Giga Berlin in June.
4. **Column reinforcement in casting and stamping:** In the casting and stamping area, the concrete columns needed steel hoops because they could not handle the weight and vibration of the giant overhead cranes.
5. **Delivery logistics and the Boring tunnel:** A large north parking lot now serves the direct delivery center. The east lot is being converted to employee parking with charging. A Boring Company tunnel is being built near the Tesla Road and River Road intersection. It is a single-tube, two-way tunnel running under the Colorado River to Tesla land on the east bank, revived after revised permits. Joe thinks it could be mostly done by the end of summer 2027 if drilling starts promptly.

## Energy
1. **Megapacks and solar:** Joe counts over 400 Megapacks at three locations on site, serving Cortex 2, Cortex 1 and the main plant, and expects more once Cortex 2 is finished. The roof carries roughly 30,000–35,000 solar panels against an original goal of about 70,000. Tesla told Joe it wants to finish planned roof work first, because repeated ventilation and roof changes forced it to dismantle and move panels. Joe notes that roofs at Nevada (except the Panasonic side), Buffalo, Fremont, Berlin and Shanghai are fully covered.
