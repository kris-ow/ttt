The article reports that Tesla has built its 10 millionth vehicle, but argues the milestone masks two straight years of declining deliveries and significant underutilized factory capacity, as the company's next-generation, cheaper vehicle has yet to materialize.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Delivery decline continues:** Tesla peaked at 1.81 million deliveries in 2023, then posted its first-ever annual decline in 2024 (about 1.79 million), followed by a further drop to 1,636,129 in 2025, down 9% year over year.
2. **"Second growth wave" still missing:** In its Q1 2024 shareholder update, Tesla said it was "currently between two major growth waves" — the first being Model 3/Model Y, the second a promised cheaper next-generation vehicle that, per the article, still hasn't arrived more than two years later.
3. **Lineup stagnation:** The article notes Model 3 and Model Y have been on sale since 2017 and 2020 respectively; the Cybertruck "flopped," the Cybercab and Semi are still ramping, and the Roadster remains in "design development."

## Financials
1. **Capacity vs. output gap:** By Tesla's own reported figures, installed annual manufacturing capacity totals over 2.375 million units (950,000+ Shanghai, 550,000+ Fremont, 375,000+ Berlin, 250,000 Model Y plus 125,000 Cybertruck and 125,000 Cybercab in Texas). In Q2 2026, Tesla produced 451,758 vehicles and delivered 480,126, putting it on pace for roughly 1.8 million for the year — over half a million units short of installed capacity.
2. **Demand-constrained, not supply-constrained:** The article states Tesla delivered 1.636 million cars in 2025 despite capacity for over 2 million, concluding the company is underutilizing manufacturing assets it built.

## Market & Competition
1. **Robotaxi/Optimus pivot noted:** The article states Tesla's robotaxi fleet is shrinking rather than growing and that Optimus development keeps slipping, adding that neither initiative adds to car sales.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues Tesla's EV growth story ended in 2023 and calls the current period not a pause between growth waves but evidence Tesla stopped investing in new EVs, betting instead on robotics and autonomy — a bet he says was made too early, hasn't paid off, and may be losing ground to competitors in autonomy.
