The host reviews a broad post-election market selloff driven by fears that Democratic wins (including a "blue tsunami" that flipped Texas) will lead to restrictions on AI data centers, argues the selloff is an overreaction, and separately covers several Tesla-specific developments: the planned wind-down of Cybertruck production at Giga Texas to make room for Cybercab, a surge in Cybertruck orders ahead of that change, sighting of what he believes is a Cybercab in Dallas, and a Texas state legislator's call to investigate Elon Musk.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck production wind-down confirmed by Musk:** The host relays Elon Musk's statement that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production, which the host frames as part of Musk's recurring pattern of retiring older vehicle lines (citing the Model S and Model X, and noting the Roadster's status) to reallocate engineering talent toward autonomy.
2. **Cybertruck order surge ahead of line closure:** Per Musk, the Cybertruck order backlog is now larger than Tesla can fulfill before the line is dismantled for Cybercab expansion around June–July next year, prompting Tesla to add an extra shift; the host attributes this to buyers rushing orders before the line closes, and explicitly rejects the idea (raised by some commentators) that the wind-down is politically motivated due to the Cybertruck's association with Republican-aligned buyers.

## Robotaxi
1. **Possible Cybercab sighting in Dallas:** The host highlights a Robotaxi-related social media post showing what he believes (not with certainty) is a Cybercab photographed in Dallas, consistent with prior statements from Musk and Tesla VP Lars Moravy that Dallas would be a next city for Cybercab deployment; he treats this as a sign Cybercab deployment there is imminent but flags his own uncertainty about the location.

## Market & Competition
1. **AI/data-center selloff framed as political overreaction:** The host attributes a sharp market selloff (the semiconductor-focused SOX index's second-worst day on record, behind a 2020 COVID-era drop) to fears that Democratic election wins — particularly Texas flipping blue — could lead to restrictions on AI data center buildout; he argues this is an overreaction, citing Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa's stated plan to let currently permitted data centers finish construction and impose future conditions (union labor, no cost pass-through to consumers, no NDAs, no subsidies) rather than block data centers outright.
2. **Analyst Dan Ives' capex growth call disputed:** The host cites analyst Dan Ives projecting that big tech/hyperscaler capex growth now peaks in 2027 (versus a prior 2028–2029 view) due to the political landscape, especially in Texas; the host disagrees, characterizing Ives as emotionally reactive rather than data-driven.
3. **Bipartisan energy/data-center bill pending Senate vote:** The host notes a bipartisan bill (he does not name sponsors beyond "bipartisan") that would speed environmental reviews for energy infrastructure, require data centers to pay for their own grid upgrades rather than passing costs to other ratepayers, and require upfront financial commitments from large data center developers — expected to come up for a Senate vote within the week.
4. **Elon Musk political controversy in Texas:** A Texas state legislator, per the host, called for Musk to be investigated and prosecuted, alleging unspecified "illegality" tied to Musk's Texas ventures (SpaceX, xAI); the host states the legislator did not specify any actual crime and suggests the underlying issue is Musk's funding of Republican campaigns. Musk responded on social media comparing the legislator to Soviet-era secret police.
