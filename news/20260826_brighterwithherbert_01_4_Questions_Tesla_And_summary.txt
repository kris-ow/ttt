This video features economist/investor Jo Bhakdi (of pioneerlands.org) discussing his personal trading approach to Tesla stock ahead of the September 3 Cybercab launch, his thesis for why SpaceX stock could see a major repricing in September, and why Grok's rapid capability gains could boost the SpaceX investment case. The conversation blends short-term options trading commentary (explicitly framed as not investment advice) with longer-term fundamental arguments about Robotaxi scaling, Starlink/data-center compute economics, and AI competitive dynamics.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab launch as a near-term catalyst:** Bhakdi frames the September 3 Cybercab launch as a "magnet" pulling Tesla stock upward from currently depressed levels, but cautions it could become a "sell the news" event if TSLA runs up sharply beforehand (he flagged $400–420 as a danger zone); he said reaching thousands of Cybercabs by year-end, potentially 10,000, would be a strongly bullish signal for further re-rating.
2. **Robotaxi scale needed for earnings vs. SpaceX:** Bhakdi contrasts Tesla's robotaxi economics with SpaceX's, saying Tesla would need roughly a million robotaxis to reach $50 billion in earnings, whereas SpaceX could reach comparable incremental earnings from a single additional data-center compute deal.

## Financials
1. **Tesla stock "Goldilocks" setup, host's personal trading:** Bhakdi says he holds a self-described north-of-85% conviction that TSLA is likely to move toward $380 in the near term (explicitly a short-term/tactical view using synthetic leverage via short puts, not investment advice), citing depressed AI-momentum-stock sentiment as boosting Bitcoin and eventually rotating money back into Tesla. He described exiting most of his core Tesla position shortly before the last earnings call, then re-entering around $325 after a market dip he attributed to unrelated macro news.
2. **Path back to all-time highs:** Bhakdi argues Tesla could return to prior all-time highs (he cites $488, and separately references a December 18 prior-year high of $480) by year-end if Cybercab scaling continues, framing this as a function of the market re-rating Tesla relative to the rest of the market rather than an absolute price target.
3. **SpaceX AI revenue could roughly double quarter-over-quarter:** Elon Musk reportedly told employees AI-segment revenue would surpass all other SpaceX revenue combined by next quarter; last quarter AI revenue was $2.56 billion against $5.25 billion in other revenue, meaning AI revenue would need to roughly double. Bhakdi expects a new compute-leasing deal (he speculates, unconfirmed, that the counterparty could be Microsoft, citing a semi-analysis article making the same case) to be announced around September, potentially adding tens of billions in revenue at high margins.
4. **SpaceX stock lockup dynamics at $135:** Bhakdi says SpaceX employees/investors are reportedly unwilling to sell shares below the $135 IPO reference price even amid diversification pressure, creating a technical floor with selling pressure capping upside near that level until lockups fully resolve — a dynamic he says has played out as he predicted and which he is trading via short puts at the $135 strike.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Satellite/data-center buildout timeline:** Bhakdi believes SpaceX can plausibly reach about 2 gigawatts of capacity by the end of this year and 5 gigawatts by the end of next year, but considers Musk's stated 10-gigawatt next-year target unrealistic. He expects satellite-based data centers to reach real scale around 2028, with a full gigawatt in space possibly by 2029, calling the long-term revenue potential from space-based compute "tens of trillions of dollars" once demonstrated.
2. **Colossus 2 build-out, not demand, is the bottleneck:** Bhakdi and the host agree that customer demand for SpaceX-hosted compute is not in question (citing high pricing for even older GPU generations as evidence of persistent compute demand); the real constraint is how quickly SpaceX can finish building out the second half of its Colossus data center capacity before it can be leased out.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Grok's cost/performance positioning:** Bhakdi asserts Grok (version 4.6, with 4.7 expected within about two weeks per Musk's stated timeline) is now near the frontier of AI price-performance, close to leading models' capability at a fraction of the cost, and argues this combined with the "Grokbot" coding-agent tool (built on Cursor's harness) could push SpaceX's AI unit close to AGI-level competitiveness. He suggested Grok 4.7 may include proprietary SpaceX engineering data that could give it an edge in scientific/technical domains.
2. **Competitive read on OpenAI/Anthropic/Google:** Bhakdi says he's become increasingly bearish on OpenAI given competition from Chinese models and Grok, since OpenAI lacks sufficient proprietary compute. He and the host discuss Anthropic's revenue run rate (Bhakdi estimates around $60 billion currently, up from roughly $5 billion a year prior, though he expresses uncertainty about current figures) as the bar Grok's business would need to approach to be taken seriously by markets. He also describes Google as having strategically deprioritized frontier-model competition in favor of owning implementation and inference layers.
