## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's Q2 earnings beat expectations: revenue $7.81B (vs. $6.81B expected), up 92% YoY and 66% QoQ; net loss narrowed to $541M from $1.008B; adjusted EBITDA hit $3.5B; cash on hand $100B; capex $18.3B for the quarter. CFO Bret Johnsen called it a "momentous year" with "strong operating leverage."
- Musk said internal projections for SpaceX reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "nonzero chance" of 2029 — one of the most aggressive targets he's voiced in over a decade tracking the company, per one host.
- SPCX shares dropped 13.6% to $108.27; one host attributes the move mainly to uncertainty ahead of a 900M+ share unlock rather than earnings substance, though some investors flagged concern over ballooning AI capex ($15.8B spent in Q2, per one estimate).
- A separate speaker argued SpaceX's fair trading multiple is unknowable via fundamentals alone but should land above Nvidia's and below Tesla's, with the earnings report and share unlock as the key near-term catalysts.
- One host said he's now open to a Tesla–SpaceX merger "at a reasonable offer," citing reassurance from the earnings call on capex-to-payback economics, while flagging that bullish SpaceX commentary ahead of the lockup could also serve Musk's incentive to keep the SPCX share price high for any future all-stock deal.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- AI/compute revenue tripled quarter-over-quarter to $2.6B (up 213% sequentially, 247% YoY), driven by new Cloud Service Agreements and Grok/X subscriptions; SpaceX disclosed $6.7B of additional cloud-services revenue already contracted for Q3, ramping over six months starting October.
- CFO said new compute capex is generating sub-one-year payback periods; one analyst (Brian Wang) speculates premium Nvidia chip spot-pricing could shrink this further to 6–10 months.
- SpaceX has 1.4 GW of compute live, expects to exceed 2GW by year-end, and Musk said cumulative compute will be "closer to 10GW than 5GW" by end of next year; ~15 GW of power has been secured for the broader buildout.
- SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia hardware going forward (Vera Rubin chips), with Musk saying Nvidia will allocate SpaceX a "significant percentage" of its 2027 GPU supply; a new joint "Starmind AI1" orbital compute payload was also announced.
- Grok 4.6 is expected next week, Grok 4.7 in 3–4 weeks, and Grok 5 by year-end incorporating "the entire corpus of SpaceX data."
- Independent modeling (one host) casts the $1T-by-2030 path as requiring unprecedented ~180% sustained AI-revenue CAGR and ~26GW of compute — a bar no company has cleared before — calling it improbable but not impossible.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Musk said on the earnings call he considers Starship's heat shield problem "solved" based on data and visual inspection, while cautioning he didn't want to "jinx it."
- Starship's 13th flight achieved the vehicle's first intact ocean landing in the Indian Ocean after deploying next-generation Starlink V3 satellites, continuing to transmit data post-splashdown.
- Flight 14 will attempt the first-ever catch of Starship's upper stage using the launch tower's mechanical arms; Musk said flight-13 data showed the ship landed precisely enough that a catch would have succeeded.
- Musk floated a 5–7 year timeline for humans walking on Mars, with a robotic lander arriving a few years ahead of astronauts; in his plan, Optimus robots would land first to build infrastructure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink hit 12 million subscribers by end of Q2, doubling YoY; connectivity revenue rose 66% YoY to $4.3B. Shotwell called it the "best quarter to date."
- Shotwell cited a roughly $600B/year addressable market across Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile for Starlink Mobile, saying SpaceX expects to "acquire quite a few" of their customers; shares of the three carriers reportedly fell ~4.5% after-hours on the comments.
- SpaceX plans a hybrid direct-to-cell Starlink Mobile combining next-gen V2 satellites with new femtocell devices using acquired EchoStar spectrum; one host speculates an independent consumer mobile plan may not launch until early 2028.
- Musk said it's "not out of the question that Starlink will deliver a majority of the world's internet," citing bandwidth demand from autonomous vehicles and robots.

## Robotaxi
- Cathie Wood reiterated her robotaxi thesis, projecting Tesla gross margins shifting from 16% in EVs to 80–90% in robotaxi and a global autonomous-ride market exceeding $10 trillion within 5–10 years; a Tesla commentator (Bhakdi) countered with his own estimate that Tesla's per-vehicle cost could run $15,000–$20,000 versus Waymo's roughly $40,000–$45,000 at scale.
- Bhakdi said he expects Tesla to hold off on a real paid Cybercab robotaxi rollout until FSD reaches a more complete, safety-validated version — potentially within about three months — with near-term Cybercab sightings remaining one-off demonstrations rather than fleet deployment.
- Samsung has reportedly begun mass production of Cybercab camera modules, with LG joining the supply chain later this year; combined initial orders are enough to cover 20,000–30,000 vehicles, though no production timeline was confirmed.

## Autonomous Driving
- A car-review YouTube channel with over a million subscribers gave FSD an enthusiastic first-time test on a Model Y, calling it "the future," which commentators frame as organic sentiment-building alongside growing TikTok coverage.
- Some Hardware 3 owners reported car-computer failures after updating to FSD "v14 Light," with roughly 20% of one informal poll's respondents reporting the issue; no official cause has been confirmed, and one source flagged third-party advice against installing the update on HW3 vehicles.
- Tesla is testing Semi trucks with LIDAR-based ground-truth equipment to validate FSD sensor readings; one guest cited a claim that Semi FSD could work by "early next year," though the host expects 2027 given Musk's history of missed timelines.
- Nvidia unveiled "Alpamayo 2 Super," an open frontier model for robotaxis targeting rare edge-case scenarios; Waymo opened fully autonomous rides to everyone in Dallas, having served nearly 150,000 riders since its February launch.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla is installing its first Optimus production line at Fremont, in space previously used for Model S/X production; initial units are intended to generate training data rather than be sold.
- The dedicated Optimus factory building at Giga Texas has reached roughly 720 feet of steel skeleton and is still growing, per construction trackers.
- Analyst James Altucher argued treating Tesla purely as an automaker is "the mistake of the decade," pointing to a $60 trillion labor-market opportunity for Optimus; the claim is attributed to Altucher's analysis, not confirmed Tesla guidance.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla built its 10 millionth vehicle, a diamond black Model Y, at Fremont — the first EV-only automaker to reach the mark; the most recent million units took about 7 months versus roughly 12 years for the first million.
- Tesla sold 4,778 vehicles in Australia in July, its best July ever (+404% YoY), with more than half being Model Y L units; China-made EV sales rose to 93,579 units in July (+37.8% YoY), Tesla's best July ever and best month of 2026.
- Tesla is preparing to launch sales in Argentina (Buenos Aires job listings posted) and is staffing for Semi's entry into the French market.
- Gigafactory Nevada turned 10 years old and will now host Tesla Semi production at scale.
- California is offering first-time EV buyers $3,500 off eligible new Model 3/Y inventory for orders placed on or after August 3.
- Edmunds and Motor Trend gave positive early reviews of the Model Y L, praising ride quality and FSD performance.

## Energy
- Tesla Megapacks now power Ørsted's newly connected 250 MW/500 MWh Old 300 Storage project in Needville, Texas, operating independently of the adjacent 430 MW solar farm; the combined projects are expected to generate ~$110 million in local property tax revenue.
- Tesla's Megacharger network is expanding faster than expected, with 64 locations listed as "coming soon" and dozens expected to open this year, including an upgraded four-stall site at Giga Texas.
- California fleets are sitting on over $500M in unredeemed EV-truck vouchers (must be used within 18 months, 77% for Class 8 trucks); the state plans an additional $1B in heavy-duty EV incentives.

## Financials
- Bhakdi said he added back Tesla exposure after reducing it ahead of earnings, arguing the stock's ~14% post-earnings decline (versus Google's ~7%) reflects a market-wide momentum-stock selloff rather than Tesla-specific issues; he called Tesla a buy near $320 and would turn cautious near $380.
- The Boring Company is building a new tunnel-boring machine (Proof Rock MB3) at its Bastrop, Texas facility, shipping to Nashville in September to join two existing machines on the Music City Loop.
