## Brief

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX's Q2 revenue hit $7.81B, up 92% YoY and 66% QoQ, beating $6.81B estimates; net loss narrowed to $541M and adjusted EBITDA reached $3.5B, per the company's 10-Q. CFO Bret Johnsen called it a "momentous year."
- SPCX shares closed down 13.6% at $108.27, which one host attributes mainly to uncertainty ahead of a 900M+ share unlock rather than earnings substance; another source flags Wall Street concern over ballooning AI capex ($15.8B in Q2) as a contributing factor.
- Musk said SpaceX's internal timeline for $1 trillion in annual revenue has moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" in 2029 — a target one host calls the most ambitious he's heard from Musk in 14 years covering the company. Independent modeling from another host calls the underlying AI-revenue growth rate (~180% CAGR needed) "unprecedented" and without historical precedent at this scale.
- SpaceX disclosed $295M of Tesla Megapack purchases in Q2 for its datacenters, bringing cumulative Megapack spend to over $800M since last year — a direct Tesla Energy revenue link to SpaceX's AI buildout.
- One host says he's now open to a Tesla–SpaceX merger "at a reasonable offer," while flagging that Musk and SpaceX executives may have incentive to talk up the company ahead of the insider lockup expiration.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- AI/compute revenue tripled quarter-over-quarter to $2.6B (up 213% sequentially, 247% YoY), driven by new Cloud Service Agreements and Grok/X subscription revenue. SpaceX's CFO said new compute capex is generating under one-year payback, citing $6.7B of additional cloud services revenue already contracted for October start.
- SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia hardware going forward (Vera Rubin chips), with Musk saying Nvidia will allocate SpaceX a "significant percentage" of its 2027 GPU supply. A new Nvidia partnership will design the "Starmind AI1" orbital compute satellite payload.
- SpaceX has 1.4GW of compute power live, expects to exceed 2GW by year-end, and Musk said cumulative compute will be "closer to 10GW than 5GW" by end of next year.
- Grok 4.6 is coming next week, Grok 4.7 in 3-4 weeks, and Grok 5 by year-end.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship's 13th flight achieved the vehicle's first-ever intact ocean landing in the Indian Ocean after deploying next-gen Starlink V3 satellites, continuing to transmit data post-splashdown.
- Flight 14 will attempt the first-ever catch of Starship's upper stage using the launch tower's mechanical arms; Musk said flight-13 data showed the ship landed precisely enough that the arms would have caught it had they been active.
- Musk said the Starship heat shield problem is "solved" based on data and visual inspection, while cautioning he didn't want to "jinx it."
- Musk floated a 5-7 year Mars timeline for the first crewed landing, with an uncrewed lander arriving a few years earlier; Optimus robots would land first to build infrastructure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Starlink hit 12 million subscribers by end of Q2, doubling YoY; connectivity revenue rose 66% YoY to $4.3B. Shotwell called it the "best quarter to date."
- Shotwell said Starlink Mobile targets a roughly $600B/year addressable market across Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile; those three carriers' shares reportedly fell ~4.5% after-hours on the comments. Musk said it's "not out of the question that Starlink will deliver a majority of the world's internet."

## Robotaxi
- Cathie Wood reiterated a bull case that robotaxi gross margins could rise from Tesla's 16% EV margin to 80-90%, projecting the global autonomous-ride market past $10 trillion within 5-10 years; Jo Bhakdi pushed back on parts of this, citing his own estimate that Tesla's per-vehicle cost (~$15,000-$20,000 at scale) undercuts Waymo's (~$40,000-$45,000).
- Bhakdi said he expects Tesla to hold off on a real paid Cybercab robotaxi service until FSD reaches a more complete, safety-validated version — which he estimates is roughly three months out — with near-term Cybercab appearances limited to demonstrations rather than fleet rollout.
- Samsung has begun mass production of Cybercab camera modules and LG will join production later this year; combined initial orders reportedly cover 20,000-30,000 vehicles, though no production timeline was confirmed.

## Autonomous Driving
- A car-review YouTube channel with over a million subscribers tested FSD for the first time and reacted with strong surprise, calling it "the future" — cited by one host as an organic-trust signal alongside growing TikTok FSD coverage.
- Scattered reports surfaced of Hardware 3 computer failures after installing FSD "v14 Light," including one 2021 Model Y failing 24 hours post-update and ~20% of poll respondents reporting failures; the host stressed this is early, uncorroborated user data.

## Humanoid Bots
- Tesla is installing its first Optimus production line at Fremont, in space formerly used for Model S/X, with initial units intended to generate training data rather than for sale.
- The Giga Texas dedicated Optimus factory building's steel skeleton has reached roughly 720 feet and is still growing, per construction trackers.
- Tech analyst James Altucher argued Optimus represents a mispriced $60 trillion labor-market opportunity, framed as "Uber for physical labor"; the host expressed skepticism about some specifics of this framing.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla sold 4,778 vehicles in Australia in July, its best July ever there, up 404% YoY, with over half being Model Y L units. China-made EV sales hit 93,579 units in July, up 37.8% YoY, marking nine straight months of growth per CPCA data.
- Tesla built its 10 millionth vehicle, a diamond black Model Y, at Fremont — the first EV-only automaker to reach the mark; the most recent million units took about 7 months versus 12 years for the first million.
- California is offering first-time EV buyers $3,500 off eligible new Model 3/Y inventory for orders placed on or after August 3.
- Tesla is preparing to launch sales in Argentina (multiple Buenos Aires job listings) and hired a Business Development Manager to support Tesla Semi's entry into France.
- Edmunds and Motor Trend both gave positive reviews of the Model Y L, with Motor Trend citing no real weaknesses in ride quality and the latest FSD version.

## Energy
- Tesla Megapacks are powering Ørsted's newly connected 250MW/500MWh Old 300 Storage battery in Needville, Texas, part of ERCOT; the project sits beside a 430MW solar farm but operates independently. Combined projects are expected to generate ~$110M in local property tax revenue.
- Tesla has 64 Megacharger locations listed as "coming soon" for its Semi network, with dozens expected to open this year; Giga Texas's site was upgraded from one charger to four stalls. Giga Nevada turned 10 and will host Tesla Semi production at scale.
- California fleets are reportedly sitting on over $500M in unredeemed EV-truck vouchers, 77% earmarked for Class 8 electric trucks, with the state planning an additional $1B in incentives.

## Financials
- Bhakdi said he added back Tesla exposure after reducing it ahead of earnings, arguing the stock's ~14% decline (versus Google's ~7% over the same period) reflected a market-wide momentum-stock selloff rather than Tesla-specific issues; he called current levels "undervalued" and named $320 as his buy point versus more caution near $380.

## Market & Competition
- Waymo opened fully autonomous rides to everyone in Dallas, having served nearly 150,000 riders since its February launch. Nvidia unveiled "Alpamayo 2 Super," a new open frontier model targeted at robotaxis and autonomous vehicles for edge-case scenarios.
- A leveraged hedge fund (Situational Awareness, run by Leopold Aschenbrenner) reportedly blew up on semiconductor/AI bets, with Citadel stepping in to buy distressed positions; the host claims the forced liquidation was a central factor in a broader momentum-stock selloff that also hit Tesla, though this framing is his own characterization.
