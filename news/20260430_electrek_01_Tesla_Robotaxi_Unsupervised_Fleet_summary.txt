Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi fleet has grown to 25 cumulative vehicles across three Texas cities, per RobotaxiTracker.com — the first meaningful sign of growth after nearly a year of stagnation, though the numbers remain far below CEO Elon Musk's predictions and well behind competitors.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised fleet reaches 25 vehicles across three cities:** Per RobotaxiTracker.com, Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi fleet stands at 25 cumulative verified vehicles: Austin (19), Dallas (3), and Houston (3). Dallas and Houston were added on April 18, each starting with a single vehicle. The unsupervised count climbed from single digits in January to 25 by end of April — breaking a pattern of near-total stagnation but not constituting a rapid ramp.

2. **Total active fleet is 165, dominated by supervised FSD:** The broader active fleet (seen in the last 30 days) totals 165 vehicles, with the Bay Area accounting for 107 of those — all running supervised FSD, not the unsupervised service. Only 17 unsupervised vehicles were active in the last seven days per the tracker.

3. **Utilization and expansion timeline remain weak:** Tesla's unsupervised vehicles have been operating less than 30% of the time — well below what a viable ride-hailing business would require. The planned expansion to five additional cities in H1 2026 has already slipped; only Dallas and Houston have launched.

## Market & Competition

4. **Waymo contrast is stark:** Waymo operates approximately 3,000 robotaxis across 10 U.S. cities, completing over 500,000 paid trips per week. The company raised $16 billion in February for international expansion to London and Tokyo and is rolling out its new Ojai purpose-built robotaxi platform.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert notes that Tesla's Robotaxi program is underperforming even his own "bearish" expectations — which called for ~100 unsupervised vehicles by end of 2025 — and acknowledges some early signs of growth while emphasizing Tesla has a long way to go to build a viable business, especially against Waymo's scale.
