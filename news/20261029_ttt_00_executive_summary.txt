## Brief

- Cybercabs now outnumber Model Ys in Texas fleet
- Starship Ship 42 completes static fire, dual catch eyed before Thanksgiving
- Ex-Autopilot chief Sterling Anderson blasts Tesla's vision-only FSD
- SpaceX stock capped near $145-150 on share-unlock pressure
- FCC proposes expanding Starlink direct-to-cell spectrum bands
- Tesla hiring "market attendant" roles across eight new cities

## Robotaxi

- Texas Cybercab registrations hit 573 per Texas DMV, with 222 added in the past week; multiple hosts converge on this figure as the Texas robotaxi fleet grows (one host cites a total fleet of ~1,051-1,080 vehicles, materially above RobotaxiTracker.com's stale August count). Cybercabs (544) now outnumber Model Ys (507) in the Texas fleet for the first time, per Munro Live's interview with Tesla's Lars Moravy.
- Moravy said the Cybercab assembly line can build faster than vehicles are being deployed, calling it "a bit of a race," with manufacturing currently ahead of deployment.
- Houston's robotaxi geofence shows rough edges: inaccessible pickup/drop-off zones and inconsistent downtown navigation reported by early riders; Tesla is reportedly offering free first rides in Dallas and Houston.
- Tesla posted "market attendant lead" roles for robotaxi charging in Las Vegas, Phoenix, Orlando, Miami, San Antonio, Dallas, Houston and Austin — a signal pointing toward 24/7 operations and approaching launches in several new cities.
- Jo Bhakdi cites a tracker showing 38 Cybercabs deployed in the past week and 222 registered in Texas, with the total "approaching" 2,000 and "guaranteed" to hit that mark by year-end, per his own framing.

## Autonomous Driving

- Sterling Anderson, Tesla's former Autopilot chief (2015-2016) and now GM's Chief Product Officer, told The Verge that vision-only self-driving is "a weird religious stance that ignores physics," arguing radar is cheap and necessary in fog and for velocity measurement; GM's Cadillac Escalade IQ will debut an "eyes-off" lidar/radar/camera system in 2028. Electrek's Fred Lambert editorialized that Anderson is right and that Tesla's approach reflects Musk's 2019 dismissal of lidar rather than engineering logic. A separate host disputed the criticism, noting Tesla sued Anderson after his 2017 departure (settled, no wrongdoing admitted) over alleged data misuse.
- NHTSA upgraded its probe into FSD in reduced-visibility conditions to an Engineering Analysis covering 3,203,754 vehicles, now counting nine crashes including a November 2023 pedestrian death, per Electrek; the agency found FSD's camera-degradation detection failed to flag sun glare, fog, and airborne dust before crashes.
- Germany and Italy signaled support for FSD's EU approval; one host estimates this moves Tesla to roughly 46% of the required 65% population threshold.
- A South Korean court rejected a 2019-2022 FSD buyer lawsuit seeking refunds, ruling Tesla's FSD option is an indefinite contract tied to future regulatory approval.

## Humanoid Bots

- Moravy confirmed Optimus will plug in Cybercabs for charging until automated infrastructure (e.g. wireless pads) is ready, and could eventually service vehicles — framed as a "Cybercab, Optimus, and robotaxi" trifecta.

## Electric Vehicles

- Cybercab's production line uses 25 general assembly stations (vs. 5-10x more for Model Y), is ~78% automated, and targets ~125,000 units/year capacity in half the floor space, per Moravy.
- The Cybercab is designed for 500,000+ miles with a modular, bottom-serviceable battery pack Moravy graded "A minus" for buildability, versus a "D" for the Model Y pack.
- Tesla began hiring for sales, service and charging roles in Vietnam ahead of a market entry, though no launch timeline was given; import duties could limit early volume to low thousands of units annually.
- Ford's Model e division lost $1.1 billion in Q3 (EBIT margin -86.5%), with EV revenue down 33% YoY to $1.2 billion after discontinuing the F-150 Lightning and scaling back the Mach-E.

## Financials

- A pre-production (circa 2022) Tesla Semi caught fire in Hayward, CA with no known cause; current production Semis use different cells (4680 vs 2170) and thermal systems, making the incident less representative of today's trucks.
- Waymo's Q3 revenue was $372M (+$26M YoY) while its Other Bets operating loss widened to $2.05B (+$621M YoY) — one host calls this evidence of unsustainable economics.
- Tesla closed down roughly 2-3% and SpaceX fell under 1% amid a broader Nasdaq-100 decline of 1.38%.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation

- SpaceX shares are capped near $145-150, which Jo Bhakdi attributes to share-unlock mechanics: ~1.3 billion shares unlocked October 22/24 (exceeding 50% of float), with selling pressure expected to exhaust around Nov 2-3 and a rally toward $170-180 to follow, per his own projection.
- Merger discussion continues: Herbert (FutureAzA) revised his merger-probability estimate from "over 99%" to roughly 70%, citing risk that Tesla's and SpaceX's special merger committees fail to agree on terms. Analyst Alexandra Merz reportedly argues a deal would need to be announced by around December to close by next September — flagged by the hosts as her opinion, with a track record of similarly timed predictions that didn't materialize.
- Shareholder-vote mechanics remain a key hurdle: approval requires 50% of all outstanding Tesla shares (non-votes count as "no"), and post-merger Tesla holders would lose voting rights to Musk's special control — a dynamic the hosts say complicates winning over institutional holders.
- Bhakdi argues confirmation of merger committees on both sides signals market expectation of a deal proceeding, which he believes could deliver Tesla a 30-40% valuation premium.

## SpaceX — Starship

- Ship 42 completed a full six-engine static fire plus a single-Raptor burn simulating a de-orbit maneuver; both vehicles for the next flight have finished major engine tests, with a dual tower-catch attempt hoped for before Thanksgiving pending regulatory clearance.

## SpaceX — Starlink

- The FCC proposed expanding direct-to-device spectrum bands from five to 13 (up to 482 MHz additional), rejecting a push by major telecom carriers to strip key bands from the framework; comment period could run into early 2027 with final rules possibly not effective until H2 2027.
- SpaceX filed comments accusing T-Mobile and AFTRCC of pushing rules designed to hamper a competitor, arguing proposed buffer zones would disenfranchise millions from direct-to-cell coverage.
- SpaceX is in early-stage talks to acquire ~4,000 acres from the Vermillion Parish School District adjacent to Starbase, Louisiana, for employee housing and staging.
- Gwynne Shotwell is targeting early 2028 for SpaceX to launch direct-to-phone carrier service on its own spectrum; the connectivity division posted roughly $5.25B in quarterly revenue with ~$2.37B operating profit, per a separate analysis video.
