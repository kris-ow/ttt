## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Cybercab official documentation reveals production specs and imminent public launch:** Multiple posts drew from newly released official Tesla Cybercab documentation, collectively painting the clearest picture yet of how the vehicle is designed. The standard production Cybercab has **two seats and is not equipped with a steering wheel, acceleration pedal, or brake pedal** — a hardware-level commitment to full autonomy. Vehicles spotted with a steering wheel are explicitly classified as engineering or test vehicles operating at **SAE Level 2**, not the production autonomous configuration. The documents also confirmed several notable hardware details: an **Active Hood** system that raises during certain pedestrian impacts to reduce injuries; **pressurized air canisters and camera washers** for sensor maintenance; and emergency protocols for situations where a Cybercab is stranded in the roadway or in a dangerous position. Sawyer's framing — "feels like the first public Cybercab rides are going to start very soon" — signals he reads the documentation release as a pre-launch signal. This is a meaningful milestone: official safety and operational documentation is typically finalized just ahead of public deployment. (Posts [1]–[5]; post [5] had 2.4K likes and 152 RTs, the highest single-post engagement of the day on a Tesla topic.)

2. **Tesla Semi spotted testing with autonomy validation gear:** A Tesla Semi was filmed in Palo Alto with **ground truth validation equipment** mounted on its roof — hardware used to benchmark and verify autonomous driving perception systems. The vehicle features **10 cameras and camera washers**, and Tesla's own materials describe it as **"autonomy ready."** This is a concrete data point that Tesla is actively developing autonomous capability for the Semi platform, beyond passenger vehicles. For investors tracking the long-term autonomous freight opportunity, this confirms active field testing is underway. (Post [8]; 4.6K likes and 319 RTs — the highest-engagement Tesla post of the day.)

3. **Waymo Nashville failure anecdote — competitive context:** Sawyer shared a firsthand negative Waymo experience in Nashville: the vehicle became confused and stuck within 4 minutes, required ~10 minutes of remote intervention from rider support, was unable to continue after that, and required escalation to a second specialist before ultimately completing the trip late. He noted the experience was "not great" (implied by the anecdote's structure). While one data point isn't statistically meaningful, this kind of visceral competitor stumble — shared by a high-profile Tesla bull to a large audience — reinforces the narrative that Waymo's operational reliability has room to improve, which contextually benefits Tesla's robotaxi positioning as it prepares to launch. (Post [6]; 2K likes, 129 RTs.)

4. **NHTSA closes power steering probe on 376,000 Tesla vehicles:** The NHTSA formally closed its preliminary evaluation (opened July 2023) into loss of steering control reports affecting **Model 3 and Model Y** vehicles. Tesla resolved the issue via an **over-the-air software update** (recall) issued last year, which satisfied the regulator. This is a clean regulatory closure with no hardware action required — a minor but positive signal for Tesla's regulatory standing and its OTA-update-as-recall model. (Post [7].)

5. **SpaceX joins Nasdaq-100 (tangential):** Nasdaq officially announced SpaceX will join the Nasdaq-100 effective **July 7, 2026**, with index funds beginning share purchases after market close on **July 6**. While SpaceX is a separate entity from Tesla, Elon Musk's involvement in both companies means SpaceX's rising institutional profile is a peripheral consideration for Tesla investors monitoring Musk's attention and capital allocation. This is low-signal for Tesla specifically but noted for completeness. (Post [10].)
