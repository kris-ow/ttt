This video from FutureAzA features host Brian in conversation with Randy Kirk, covering the anticipated SpaceX IPO, speculation about a potential SpaceX acquisition of Nokia, and a critical assessment of Ford's business trajectory. The core thesis is skeptical of both the Nokia acquisition rumor and Ford's long-term viability, while bullish on SpaceX's vertical integration and growth prospects.

## Market & Competition

1. **SpaceX IPO Pricing and Scale:** The video discusses a freshly published report indicating SpaceX's IPO is expected to be priced at $135 per share, which would raise over $1 billion — described as the largest raise of any company in history. The hosts indicate Brian plans to cover the IPO live when it goes public. Proceeds are expected to fund Nvidia chip purchases and buildout of their own Terafab infrastructure.

2. **SpaceX Nokia Acquisition Rumor:** A Yahoo Finance article speculating that SpaceX might acquire Nokia (estimated at potentially around $100 billion) rather than merge with Tesla is discussed and largely dismissed. Randy Kirk argues that SpaceX and Tesla together already possess essentially everything needed across the AI/tech stack, and that Nokia would not add sufficient value to justify the cost. Any acquisition SpaceX pursues, he argues, would more likely be done via stock rather than cash, given the massive capital requirements for building out Terafab and scaling Starship production to potentially thousands of units per year.

3. **SpaceX Starlink Subscriber Growth:** SpaceX recently announced it has surpassed 12 million active Starlink subscribers, which the hosts note makes it larger than many historically prominent ISPs. Randy Kirk adds that Starlink has been adding approximately 1 million subscribers per month, and references analyst Brian Wong's projection that with a current building expansion underway, the growth rate could reach 1.5 million per month either later in 2026 or into early 2027.

4. **XAI and Compute Revenue:** Brian notes that XAI (Elon Musk's AI company) is generating billion-dollar deals renting out compute, and is building and standing up data centers faster than competitors. The broader point is that AI infrastructure has become a legitimate revenue model, with companies spending millions monthly on tokens for staff productivity improvements.

5. **Ford's Dividend and Valuation — Skepticism:** The hosts address a Yahoo Finance article touting Ford's 4.06% dividend yield as a "no-brainer" buy in June 2026, following a 44% share price surge over the past 12 months and three out of four recent quarters beating analyst estimates. Ford's shares currently trade at a price-to-earnings multiple of 11.3x, a 56% discount to the broader S&P 500. Brian and Randy are deeply skeptical, arguing the low valuation is justified by absent long-term vision, and that a dividend cut would wipe out more than the 4% yield gain.

6. **Ford's Structural Problems:** Randy Kirk details Ford's persistent issues with warranty claims and recalls, noting Ford has led in actual factory recalls for multiple consecutive years through at least 45 days prior to the video. A former Ford engineer who helped write their warranty management software told Randy the current system is insufficiently proactive. Randy draws a parallel to Sears and Kmart, warning Ford faces a similar fate if it fails to develop a credible EV and autonomous driving strategy. Both hosts note Ford has hired former Tesla employees and is now quoting Elon Musk's manufacturing philosophy (e.g., "the best part is no part"), but question why these lessons weren't applied earlier and why Ford hasn't pursued FSD licensing.

7. **Ford's Brand and Entry-Level Strategy Failure:** Randy argues Ford made a critical error by discontinuing entry-level vehicles like the Ford Escort and Ford Focus. While those cars weren't highly profitable, they built brand loyalty. With no affordable entry point — and first purchases now at around $80,000 — Ford has cut off the pipeline of new, loyal customers. GM is also briefly criticized for continued stock buybacks, which the hosts suggest are artificially propping up GM's share price.
