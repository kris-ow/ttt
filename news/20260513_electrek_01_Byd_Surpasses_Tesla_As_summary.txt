BYD overtook Tesla in 2025 to become the world's largest battery energy storage system (BESS) integrator, capturing 13% of the global market versus Tesla's 10%, according to Benchmark Mineral Intelligence data.

## Energy
1. **BYD ships 60+ GWh vs. Tesla's 46.7 GWh in 2025:** BYD shipped over 60 GWh of energy storage systems globally in 2025, ranking first among all BESS integrators. Tesla deployed 46.7 GWh — a 49% year-over-year increase — but still lost its top position it had held in 2023 and 2024. Sungrow ranked third at 9% market share, followed by CRRC Zhuzhou, CATL, and Hyper Strong (6% each), Huawei and Envision (5% each), and Fluence and Sunwoda (4% each). Eight of the top 10 integrators are Chinese companies.

2. **Global BESS market nearly doubled:** Global BESS installations jumped 51% in 2025 to approximately 315 GWh, while cell shipments for stationary storage nearly doubled to over 600 GWh. China installed 65 GWh of large-scale battery storage in December 2025 alone — more than the entire U.S. deployed over the full year.

3. **BYD's HaoHan vs. Tesla's Megapack 3:** BYD unveiled its HaoHan storage system in September 2025 with a 14.5 MWh standard configuration (nearly three times Tesla's Megapack capacity), plus a 20-foot container variant at 10 MWh. A HaoHan deployment of 12.5 GWh is underway in Saudi Arabia with the Saudi Electricity Company. Tesla responded in the same month with Megapack 3 and Megablock, featuring 5 MWh per unit (up from 3.9 MWh in Megapack 2). Tesla is building its Houston Megafactory toward 50 GWh annual capacity by late 2026 and signed a $4.3 billion LFP battery deal with LG Energy Solution for cells starting August 2027.

4. **Structural cost dynamics — integrators vs. vertically integrated players:** Benchmark data shows vertically integrated cell-BESS companies have been losing market share since 2023 as falling battery prices expanded options for pure integrators. Pure integrators grew their combined share from 20% in 2023 to 30% in H1 2025. BYD is the exception — its battery division produced over 113 GWh in the first three quarters of 2025 alone. Tesla, by contrast, sources Megapack cells from CATL, BYD, and soon LG.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues BYD's ascent was inevitable given HaoHan's capacity advantage and BYD's vertically integrated structure, and warns that Tesla's reliance on competitors (CATL and BYD) for cells is a structural vulnerability the Houston Megafactory and LG deal must urgently address, as Chinese dominance of the market is already overwhelming.
