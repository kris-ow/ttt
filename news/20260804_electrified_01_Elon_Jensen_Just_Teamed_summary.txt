This video is a rapid-reaction recap of SpaceX's latest earnings call, arguing that despite high capex and a net loss, SpaceX's compute buildout, Starlink growth, and a new Nvidia partnership make it "the cool kid on the block" relative to a currently sluggish Tesla. The host frames SpaceX's cloud service agreement (CSA) economics and rapid compute scaling as the most exciting growth story he's seen since Tesla's peak growth years.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute scaling and payback economics:** SpaceX executive Brett said some capital deployments (e.g., data center buildouts) have roughly a one-year payback period. SpaceX had about 1.4 GW of compute online at the end of Q2 (up from 1 GW at the end of Q1) and expects to exceed 2 GW online by the end of 2026. Elon Musk said SpaceX will end 2027 with between 5 and 10 GW of compute online; a separate figure of 15-20 GW referenced power and cooling equipment sourcing, not compute itself.
2. **Grok training needs only a fraction of capacity:** Musk said SpaceX longer-term expects to need only about 10% of its compute capacity to train Grok, implying roughly 90% of new compute will serve CSA deals with partners including Anthropic and Google, which management said carry increasingly favorable economics that support margins.
3. **Nvidia partnership for Star Mind AI satellites:** Musk said the Star Mind AI satellite will be built exclusively with Nvidia compute, calling it the best available architecture. SpaceX plans to begin launching these AI1 satellites next year and will also deploy the same satellite design (minus solar panels and radiator) terrestrially in data centers, which Musk said is a simplified, cheaper, more efficient version of standard NVL72 racks. The host frames this as a deep Musk-Nvidia/Jensen Huang engineering partnership.
4. **Revenue run-rate guidance:** Management said that if nothing further changes, SpaceX is on track to exit 2026 (by December) at an annualized revenue run rate that the host cites as potentially over $100 billion, with Musk suggesting the actual figure will likely be higher. This compares to under $19 billion in total revenue last year. The host separately speculates that hitting 10 GW of compute by late next year could imply roughly $500 billion in revenue on a compute-scaling basis alone, before accounting for Starlink or launch growth — this projection is the host's own extrapolation, not a company guidance figure.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber growth accelerating:** Starlink added 1.4 million new customers in Q1 and 1.7 million globally in Q2, with management expecting this pace to accelerate as V3 satellites deploy.
2. **New spectrum boosts Starlink Mobile capacity:** Gwynne Shotwell said the current direct-to-cell system operates on 5 MHz of bandwidth via local telco partners, but a recent EchoStar spectrum purchase will expand that to 65 MHz, enabling major capability increases. She specifically called out Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile's combined $650 billion in annual revenue as a market SpaceX aims to capture by eliminating dead zones. V2 satellites for Starlink Mobile are planned to launch next year, distinct from V3 satellites for consumer broadband.
3. **Enterprise/government revenue growth:** Enterprise and government revenue grew over 108% year-over-year, described by management as sticky and durable; SpaceX said it has never lost an enterprise customer. Aviation penetration is still only about 10%, leaving substantial headroom, and management expects enterprise revenue to eventually significantly exceed consumer revenue.
4. **$1 trillion revenue target pulled forward:** Musk said internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in revenue have been pulled forward from 2031 to 2030, with a non-zero chance of hitting it in 2029.
5. **V3 satellite performance timeline:** Musk estimated (as a guess) that roughly 1,000 V3 satellites would need to be deployed before consumers see materially improved Starlink performance, guessing that could happen around Q2 of next year.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 14 to deploy V3 satellites; heat shield progress:** Musk confirmed Starship flight 14 will be the first to deploy V3 satellites into orbit, with SpaceX hoping to catch both the booster and ship this year — possibly as early as flight 14. Musk said he believes the heat shield problem is largely solved, though the team still wants to examine a returned ship stage (referred to by the host as "S40") once it lands in Australia to confirm.

## Financials
1. **Net losses narrowing:** An SpaceX executive (Brett) said net losses have already been narrowed by half year-over-year, which the host expects to continue improving given the CSA-driven payback economics.

## Market & Competition
1. **Grok roadmap tied to SpaceX data:** Grok 4.6 is expected next week, Grok 4.7 roughly a month out, and Grok 5 hopefully before year-end incorporating SpaceX's full data corpus into training — which the host argues could make it the top model for engineers and benefits both Tesla vehicles (via Grok integration) and Optimus (as its "brain").
