This Munro Live episode features Mark Lovell, VP of Sales for Grip Metal (parent company New Cap Industries), explaining a metal surface-modification technology that raises thousands of directional hooks on sheet metal to create mechanical attachment without adhesives. The conversation covers its 25-year history in brake pad manufacturing and speculative extensions into automotive overmolding, mass timber construction, and concrete reinforcement — with only a passing, non-committal mention of Tesla.

## Market & Competition
1. **Speculative Cybertruck application raised, not confirmed:** The Munro Live host speculates that Grip Metal's surface treatment could improve adhesive bonding on the large structural castings used on Tesla Cybertruck, which are currently glued to the rest of the body. He explicitly frames this as untested: "we don't know the answer to that... this is now a how" — not a claim that Tesla uses or has evaluated the technology. Lovell agrees only generically that the technology "could help improve" adhesive bonding in "any automotive application."

## Electric Vehicles
1. **No direct Tesla usage confirmed:** Aside from the speculative casting-adhesive discussion above, the transcript contains no confirmed Tesla application, deal, or evaluation of Grip Metal technology — the entire segment is a hypothetical raised by the host during a teardown-style interview about a third-party supplier's product.

Notable quotes and data:
- Grip Metal's brake-pad technology has been in use for about 25 years, with over 1 billion units sold globally through licensees, per Lovell, with "not one failure."
- The technology can be applied to sheet metal as thin as 10 thousandths of an inch, in sheets up to 4 feet wide, and works on steel, aluminum, titanium, and reportedly tungsten.
- Current non-automotive application: mass timber/wood construction joints via European partner Rothoblaas (Italy), improving shear strength and enabling taller wood buildings.
- Grip Metal is running pilot projects applying the technology to concrete construction (as a rebar substitute/embedded reinforcement).
- Host quote on the Cybertruck idea: "Even if we have to keep the adhesive... this also improves the use of the adhesive... we don't know the answer to that. I can't say yes, that will solve the problem."
