This video is a live-reaction and recap of SpaceX's first earnings call as a public company (Q2 2026), hosted by "investingagainstthegrain" (Nick) with guests including the host of FutureAzA. The group reacts in real time to the earnings release, walks through prepared remarks from Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell, and CFO Brett (SpaceX's finance chief), sits through the analyst Q&A, then debriefs on implications for Starship, Starlink, AI/Grok, and the stock.

## Financials
1. **Strong topline beat across segments:** Q2 2026 revenue was $7.88 billion, up 92% year-over-year from $4.1 billion, with a quarterly net loss of $541 million (an improvement of $467 million year-over-year) and adjusted EBITDA of $3.5 billion, up 191% from $1.2 billion.
2. **Segment breakdown:** Space segment revenue was $962 million (up 29% YoY, 55% sequentially) with segment adjusted EBITDA a loss of $205 million due to Starship R&D. Connectivity (Starlink) revenue was $4.3 billion (up 66% YoY), with operating income up 79% YoY to $1.7 billion and adjusted EBITDA up 64% YoY to $2.6 billion. AI segment revenue was $2.6 billion (up 247% YoY, 213% sequentially), turning adjusted EBITDA positive at $1.1 billion for the quarter.
3. **Capex and cash position:** Total company capex was approximately $18.4 billion in the quarter, with roughly $15.8 billion supporting AI compute infrastructure. SpaceX ended the quarter with $100 billion in cash/equivalents/marketable securities and $47.5 billion in backlog (defined by the hosts as signed contracts/orders not yet fulfilled or recognized as revenue).
4. **Capital markets moves:** SpaceX completed its IPO raising approximately $85.7 billion in net proceeds, followed by a $25 billion inaugural investment-grade senior notes offering (partially used to repay a $20 billion bridge loan) issued across five tranches with a weighted average interest rate of 5.855% and average maturity of 11.7 years.
5. **$100B ARR target and accelerated trillion-dollar revenue timeline:** Management guided to over $100 billion in annualized revenue run-rate (ARR) by December 2026, driven by all three segments, with the AI cloud services piece the largest contributor; Musk said this figure is what would be achieved "if we basically did nothing" and that actual results will likely be higher. Musk also said internal projections for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue have moved up from 2031 to 2030, with a "non-zero chance" of 2029.
6. **Sub-one-year payback on AI compute capex:** CFO Brett said current economics translate into a less than one-year payback on new AI compute capital deployments, and cited an additional $6.7 billion in cloud services revenue contracted in the first few weeks of Q3, ramping from October.
7. **Stock reaction and lockups:** SpaceX shares initially spiked after hours (up roughly 1%, reaching about $126) before reversing to trade down roughly 7% by the end of the call, a move the hosts characterized as irrational given the results. The hosts also discussed upcoming insider lockup expirations (first lockup opens two days after the earnings call) but argued a mass sell-off is unlikely, framing the print as a strong first public earnings report.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Two successful V3 test flights and heat shield progress:** SpaceX completed two successful Starship V3 flights in the prior 90 days; flight 13 demonstrated core orbital capabilities and a return to Starbase for catch, and Musk said the heat shield issue is likely solved based on data and visual inspection of the recovered ship floating in the ocean, though continued improvements are planned.
2. **Mass-to-orbit ambitions:** Musk said SpaceX currently delivers roughly 2,500 tons per year to orbit via Falcon (versus roughly 300 tons for the rest of the world combined) and aims to eventually deliver "well over a million tons," ultimately targeting around 10 million tons per year via Starship.
3. **Flight cadence and reusability targets:** Management expects to attempt catching the ship as soon as the next flight (tentatively end of the month), and expects Starship flight cadence to reach at least one flight per day within about a year.
4. **Human landing system and Artemis timeline:** SpaceX expects Starship to reach a level of safety needed for humans "probably by the end of next year," with the Artemis 3 mission (docking with Orion) slated for next year, an uncrewed direct-to-lunar cargo mission to follow, and a goal of "boots on the moon" in 2028.
5. **Starship infrastructure buildout:** The company is accelerating Raptor and launch vehicle production, building out "gigabays," and progressing on activating multiple launch pads at Starbase and Cape Canaveral (pad 39A and pad 37). Starship is designed to quadruple payload capacity and cut launch costs tenfold versus Falcon 9.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Record subscriber growth and stable ARPU:** SpaceX added more than 1.7 million net new Starlink subscribers in Q2 (its best quarter to date, up from 1.4 million in Q1), while holding average revenue per user (ARPU) stable at $66/month. The constellation grew to roughly 10,200 operational satellites (about 9,600 broadband) as of June 30, delivering roughly 800 terabits per second of downlink capacity, with service in 167 markets.
2. **V3 satellites framed as a step-change:** Musk said the V3 Starlink satellite is roughly an order of magnitude more capable than V2, and SpaceX plans to launch roughly an order of magnitude more of them — implying a roughly two-order-of-magnitude increase in delivered bandwidth even before monetization gains, which he argued means the market is underestimating Starlink.
3. **Enterprise, government, and aviation growth:** Enterprise/government revenue grew 108% year-over-year; SpaceX signed a major agreement with American Airlines and activated service with Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus, noting Starlink is under 10% penetrated in aviation. The company won more than $6 billion in U.S. government contracts in Q2 supporting Space Force programs.
4. **Starlink Mobile expansion:** SpaceX plans to launch next-generation Starlink Mobile (V2) satellites next year and begin providing mobile service by the end of next year, leveraging the recently FCC-approved EchoStar spectrum transfer (65 MHz) — described as up to roughly 100x better capability versus the current ~5 MHz system. Musk floated eventually competing for share of the ~$600 billion U.S. mobile carrier market (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) using low-cost, phased terrestrial small-cell buildout rather than large upfront spectrum/tower investment.
5. **Long-term internet market thesis:** Musk said it's plausible that within less than 10 years Starlink could deliver a majority of the world's internet traffic in countries where it's permitted to operate, driven partly by surging bandwidth demand from AI and robotics.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute capacity ramp and exclusive Nvidia deal:** SpaceX expects to end 2026 with over 2 gigawatts of nameplate compute (up from 1.4 GW at Q2-end, 1 GW in Q1, 400 MW a year earlier), and cumulative compute could reach roughly 10 gigawatts (versus 5) by the end of next year; Musk separately said the tentative target for power/cooling capacity is 20 gigawatts by year-end next year, with a more likely outcome near 15 GW. SpaceX announced it will build exclusively on Nvidia going forward, citing the Vera Rubin architecture as the best available.
2. **Colossus-driven AI revenue:** New cloud services agreements tied to the Colossus and Colossus 2 sites contributed $1.6 billion of incremental AI infrastructure revenue in the quarter; AI segment net operating loss narrowed to $1.3 billion.
3. **Grok roadmap and cursor acquisition:** Grok 4.5 was released last month with token consumption tripling after release; Grok 4.6 is expected roughly next week and Grok 4.7 about three to four weeks out, with Grok 5 expected before year-end incorporating SpaceX's full historical data corpus. The pending Cursor acquisition (reported at $60 billion) is said to be close to clearing regulatory hurdles.
4. **Monetization outlook:** Musk estimated (as "just a guess") monetization per watt of Vera Rubin-based compute at roughly $30–$50, and said memory supply is growing ~20% per year while demand is growing on the order of 200% per year, implying rising rather than falling prices. Only roughly 10% of compute is expected to be reserved for internal Grok training over time, with the rest for inference/rented compute.
5. **Star Mind AI satellite:** Musk described the upcoming Star Mind satellite as essentially an optimized Nvidia NVL72-based computer, to be deployed both in orbit and on the ground (without solar/radiator components), expected to begin launching next year.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Merger speculation:** Hosts noted a prediction-market estimate (attributed to "Kelshi," unconfirmed by the hosts) putting odds at 40% that the word "merger" would be said during the SpaceX earnings call, though no merger announcement occurred.
2. **Leadership commentary on AI/robotics strategy:** Gwynne Shotwell said robotics — especially AI-driven autonomous vehicles and humanoid robots — represents an underappreciated driver of future connectivity demand for SpaceX, and floated long-term ambitions around lunar manufacturing and mass accelerators.
3. **Host speculation on device strategy:** One host speculated (explicitly as personal theory, not from the call) that SpaceX/Tesla may eventually offer Grok as an at-cost "connective tissue" service across devices (car, phone, Optimus, home) rather than a standalone Tesla phone, monetizing indirectly rather than through the LLM itself.
