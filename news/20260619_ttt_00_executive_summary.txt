## Brief

- Tesla receives SFO ride-hailing permits — Bay Area Robotaxi footprint extends to airport.
- Elon: Grok voice control of car "in about 3 months."
- FSD to remember parking preferences; destination parking now top intervention reason.
- 102 Cybercabs staged at Albon lot near Giga Texas — record accumulation.
- Elon exercises 304M options at $44/share; ownership reaches ~19.9%, locked to 2033.
- Q2 delivery consensus 397K–420K; report expected July 2–3.

## Robotaxi

- **SFO ride-hailing permits approved.** Tesla received the necessary permits to operate ride-hailing at San Francisco International Airport — a concrete regulatory milestone extending the Bay Area Robotaxi footprint into a major high-traffic transit hub. No launch date was disclosed.

- **102 Cybercabs staged at Albon lot near Giga Texas.** Per Herbert, this is a record accumulation, up from earlier sightings of roughly 20, then 50 units. Jeff Lutz noted that in manufacturing, value-added steps (decals, branding, hubcaps) are only applied close to deployment — making the off-factory staging a bullish signal for imminent commercial readiness.

- **Cybercabs now leaving Giga Texas autonomously.** Joe Tegtmeyer reportedly observed a Cybercab driving itself out of the factory and self-parking — described by the panel as a first. Separately, Cern Basher noted units are now exiting the factory with service branding already installed, indicating production intended for actual deployment rather than testing.

- **Cybercabs spotted in New Zealand.** The panel interpreted the sightings as likely right-hand-traffic and possibly cold-weather testing — consistent with Tesla's prior use of New Zealand for vehicle validation.

- **Q2 city expansion window still open.** Cern Basher noted Tesla had guided for additional city rollouts before end of Q2 (June 30, 2026), flagging Miami as a candidate. Jeff Lutz was skeptical of calendar-driven timing, and noted that the EPA certificate of conformity may be a regulatory gate before Tesla can charge customers for Cybercab rides.

- **Robotaxi safety record.** Per Sawyer Merritt, every incident involving the Robotaxi fleet in recent months was caused by other human drivers — no at-fault incidents attributed to the autonomous system.

## Autonomous Driving

- **FSD parking memory announced by Elon.** Upcoming FSD releases will remember individual parking preferences — home, office, school drop-off — allowing the car to navigate autonomously to the correct spot. Elon called destination parking "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD," and described critical safety interventions as "extremely rare."

- **Grok voice control of FSD arriving in ~3 months.** Elon confirmed conversational Grok control of the vehicle — real-time commands such as "turn right here" or "park far away" — is roughly three months out. Jeff Lutz noted this implies existing HW4 processors can handle the workload, contrary to prior expectations that AI5 would be required; he flagged cloud-based latency as a problem Elon's timeline implicitly assumes will be resolved on-device.

- **Memory as the next FSD frontier.** The panel discussed persistent memory enabling personalized route preferences, garage-specific navigation, and per-driver profiles. Larry Goldberg flagged that existing vehicles were not designed for large-scale persistent storage and suggested a low-cost memory stick upgrade (5–10 GB) as a likely future option.

- **FSD safety claim: ~4 months without an at-fault incident.** Host Herbert stated FSD has had no at-fault incidents since February 2026 — flagged as unconfirmed by the panel.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus milestone cited as merger pre-condition.** Larry Goldberg argued that getting Optimus units "into the factory" by June 30, 2027 is a key valuation milestone that should precede any SpaceX-Tesla merger announcement, to allow proper price discovery for both companies.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Q2 2026 delivery estimates converging above 400K.** Goldman Sachs and Wolf Research project ~420,000 units; Barclays at 418,000; consensus per Grok at 397,000–401,000. Bear analyst Gordon Johnson (GLJ Research) carries the highest estimate at 426,000 — noted as ironic by the panel. Delivery report expected July 2–3.

- **Demand signals positive heading into quarter-end.** Jeff Lutz noted Tesla entered Q2 from a strong inventory position and that the absence of meaningful end-of-quarter discounting or incentives suggests healthy underlying demand across Europe, China, and rest of world.

- **New Model Y "Coastal Blue" color launches in Europe.** Available exclusively on the base Model Y variant; minor product update signaling continued differentiation focus in the European market.

## Financials

- **Gross margins may surprise to the upside in Q2.** Jeff Lutz cited continued COGS outperformance and factory utilization-driven fixed-cost absorption. He expects elevated capex to pressure free cash flow but not operating margin. Tesla will also need to mark up its SpaceX equity stake on the balance sheet.

- **Energy business: large Q2 upside possible but uncertain.** Larry Goldberg flagged significant work-in-progress Megapack inventory carried into Q2; if it converts to revenue, it could be a "big number." Jeff Lutz noted Q1 energy came in ~40% below the ~14.5 GWh expectation due to interconnection queue timing, and recommended evaluating the segment on a trailing 12-month basis given its inherent lumpiness.

- **Cybercab likely to appear as "other" in production reports initially.** The panel expects Tesla will break out Cybercab volumes as a separate line item once they reach roughly 5–10% of total production, and expressed hope Tesla will simultaneously begin reporting robotaxi ride miles and FSD adoption rates.

## Market & Competition

- **Elon exercises 304M Tesla options; ownership ~19.9%, locked to 2033.** At an average strike price of $44/share, the exercise consolidates Elon's voting power significantly. Shares cannot be sold until 2033 — no equity was liquidated. The panel's consensus read is that this positions Elon for a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger vote, though all guests framed the analysis as probability assessment, not advocacy.

- **SpaceX-Tesla merger timeline debate.** Larry Goldberg argued a formal merger cannot close before June 30, 2027, citing OG shareholder lockups expiring that date, months-long due diligence requirements, and the need for key milestones (robotaxi scale, Optimus factory) to enable proper price discovery. Jeff Lutz countered that Elon moves fast, synergies are undeniable — shared AI, GPU clusters, Terafab, solar — and that Elon's silence on rampant public speculation is itself a signal.

- **SpaceX positioned as AI infrastructure giant, per Brian Wang.** In a separate interview, futurist Brian Wang argued SpaceX is transitioning from a launch/satellite company into a major AI compute rental business. He projected SpaceX revenue growing from ~$4.6B/quarter in Q1 2026 to ~$15B/quarter by Q4 2026, reaching ~$46B for full-year 2026, driven by Colossus compute rentals (Anthropic, Google), the Cursor acquisition (~$4B run rate), and Rubin chip deployments. Wang cited ~20% of Nvidia's Rubin allocation going to XAI/SpaceX (per Altimeter's Gerstner and Baker), with Rubin offering ~10x the inference compute of Blackwell. These projections are Wang's own; investors should weigh them accordingly.

- **Tesla stock at ~$400 amid broad market strength.** S&P 500 near 7,500 and NASDAQ above 26,000 (near all-time highs); Tesla is underperforming. Jeff Lutz attributed this to Tesla's long-duration earnings profile being sensitive to a perceived hawkish Fed pivot, plus potential liquidity rotation into SpaceX post-IPO.
