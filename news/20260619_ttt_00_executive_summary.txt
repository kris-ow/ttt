## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab EPA Certificate of Conformity received.** The Cybercab has cleared the federal emissions compliance requirement — a prerequisite for commercial deployment and fare collection in the US. Randy Kirk (FutureAzA) confirmed the box is now checked.

- **102 Cybercabs staged at Giga Texas Albon lot — a record.** The count has grown rapidly from earlier sightings of 20, 30, then 50 units. Jeff Lutz noted that staging vehicles away from the factory and adding branding/service markings signals proximity to deployment, as rework costs rise with each value-added step. Cybercabs are also now exiting Giga Texas autonomously and parking themselves, per Joe Tegtmeyer's observation.

- **Cybercabs spotted in New Zealand**, interpreted as right-hand-traffic and possibly cold-weather testing. Separately, units have been sighted in Las Vegas, Florida, and the Boston area, suggesting Tesla is pre-positioning across multiple target cities.

- **Tesla receives SFO ride-hailing permits.** Tesla has been granted permits to operate ride-hailing at San Francisco International Airport — a high-traffic, high-revenue venue. No launch date was given, but the permit is a concrete regulatory step extending the Bay Area footprint into a major transit hub.

- **Texas commercial launch: "about four weeks away at the most,"** per Randy Kirk relaying an estimate attributed to Jeff Le. Randy cited Texas as already having permitting in place and not subject to the federal 2,500-vehicle cap, characterizing the remaining hold-up as likely a software-readiness issue. Active Robotaxi fleet stands at 732 per RobotaxiTracker.com (as of 2026-06-20).

- **Q2 city expansion still possible.** Cern Basher noted Tesla had indicated additional city rollouts before end of Q2 (June 30). Miami was floated as a candidate. Jeff Lutz was skeptical of calendar-driven timing, believing deployment will follow operational readiness criteria. The one-year anniversary of the first Robotaxi falls around June 22.

- **Robotaxi safety record:** The only recent incidents involving Tesla's Robotaxi fleet have been caused by other drivers striking the autonomous vehicles — not autonomous-system faults, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Autonomous Driving

- **Grok voice control of FSD arriving in "about 3 months."** Elon replied to a user request for conversational vehicle control — commands like "turn right here" or "drop us off here" — with a ~3-month timeline. Jeff Lutz noted this implies HW4 (AI4) can handle the task, contrary to earlier expectations requiring AI5, and flagged that cloud-based Grok latency must be resolved for real-time vehicle control.

- **FSD parking memory announced by Elon.** Upcoming FSD releases will remember users' parking preferences, navigating autonomously to the correct spot at home, office, or school drop-off. Elon stated destination parking is "by far the biggest reason people now intervene with FSD" and that critical safety interventions are now "extremely rare." Guests noted memory storage on existing hardware may be a limiting factor, with a low-cost memory stick upgrade ($5–10 GB) discussed as a potential path.

- **FSD approval rumor in Spain by end of June 2026,** per Randy Kirk citing a source he described as "very reliable" but unconfirmed. Randy advised treating it with caution.

- **Sweden blocking FSD approval** unless Tesla removes the ability to exceed the speed limit. The panel found this unworkable in practice. Brian (FutureAzA) noted EU-level approval would ease but not override individual country restrictions.

- **FSD operating in Australia** — driving on the left side of the road — with Joe (FutureAzA) describing the footage as impressive.

- **FSD safety claim:** Host Herbert cited approximately four months without an at-fault FSD incident since February — flagged as unconfirmed.

## Humanoid Bots

- **US Trade Representative Ambassador Greer toured Tesla Fremont,** viewing the production line and Optimus, per Joe (FutureAzA). The Texas Department of Transportation's executive director also visited to observe the Cybercab. The panel framed both visits as meaningful government recognition of Tesla as an American manufacturing leader.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Cybertruck Smart Summon live via V14 update** — described by the FutureAzA panel as one of the biggest Cybertruck updates since FSD itself rolled out. A $60,000 Cybertruck variant is also rolling out.

- **Q2 2026 delivery outlook:** Goldman Sachs and Wolf Research project ~420,000 units; Barclays at 418,000; consensus per Grok at 397,000–401,000. Randy Kirk (FutureAzA) sees a credible path to 475,000–525,000, with a max scenario of 575,000, and expects a positive surprise. Q2 delivery report expected around July 2–3.

- **EV tax credit removal framed as competitive advantage** for Tesla. Competitors relying on those credits now face higher effective prices, while Tesla's optimized cost structure insulates it, per the FutureAzA panel.

- **New "Coastal Blue" Model Y color** launched in Europe on the base trim — minor product differentiation, limited investment significance.

## Financials

- **Q2 gross margins may surprise to the upside,** per Jeff Lutz (brighterwithherbert). Tesla has been outperforming on COGS; factory utilization drives fixed-cost absorption. Elevated capex will pressure free cash flow but not operating margin.

- **Energy deployment: Q2 could be a "big number."** Tesla's Megapack manufacturing capacity is ~17 GWh/quarter; Q1 deployed only 8.8 GWh. Randy Kirk estimates Q2 deployment could range from 14 to 25 GWh depending on carryover timing — a potential major positive surprise. Jeff Lutz noted Q1 came in ~40% below the ~14.5 GWh consensus estimate and recommended evaluating energy on a trailing 12-month basis.

- **SpaceX mark-to-market: ~$2B / ~$0.75/share potential Q2 boost.** Now that SpaceX is public, Tesla must mark its SpaceX stake to market each quarter. Randy Kirk cited this figure (sourced to a major financial publication he believed to be Fortune, unconfirmed) as a meaningful earnings contributor in Q2.

## Market & Competition

- **Elon exercises 304M Tesla options at $44/share average,** bringing ownership to ~19.9%. Shares are locked up until 2033; no stock was sold. The panel's consensus read: consolidating voting power positions Elon for a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger or acquisition vote. Per Grok (flagged as unverified), Elon faced an August 15, 2026 exercise deadline — contradicting Gemini's answer that no deadline existed this year; the panel acknowledged the AI disagreement.

- **Merger timeline debate.** Larry Goldberg argued a deal cannot close before June 30, 2027 — citing OG shareholder lockups, months of required due diligence, and better price discovery after key milestones (Robotaxi scale, Optimus factory). Jeff Lutz countered that Elon moves fast, synergies are undeniable (shared AI, GPU clusters, Terafab), and Elon's silence amid rampant speculation is itself a signal.

- **SpaceX stock at ~$185 at time of recording** (IPO'd at $135; estimated intraday high ~$200–$210). Brian Wang (nextbigfuture.com, via brighterwithherbert) projects SpaceX reaches ~$46B in full-year 2026 revenue, up from ~$18.7B in 2025, driven by AI data center rental. Wang argues SpaceX's terrestrial data center business alone justifies a ~$2T valuation floor today, rising to $5T+ by end of 2027, and draws comparisons to Broadcom and Nvidia's revenue/valuation profiles.
