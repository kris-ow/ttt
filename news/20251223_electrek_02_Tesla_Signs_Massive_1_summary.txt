Tesla Energy has signed a full EPC agreement with Matrix Renewables for a 500 MW / 1 GWh standalone battery storage system (Megapacks) in Eccles, Scotland, part of the UK's strong market for Tesla's large-scale energy storage projects.

## Energy
1. **New 1 GWh UK Megapack project:** Matrix Renewables announced a full EPC agreement with Tesla for a 500 MW / 1 GWh battery energy storage system in Eccles, Scotland. The press release refers to it generically as a BESS, but the article states the project size and Tesla partnership indicate it uses Megapacks. The site sits along transmission corridors between Scotland and England, a location relevant to grid flexibility given that Scottish wind generation often exceeds local demand. Matrix says all planning conditions have been discharged and full consent secured to begin construction. Tesla VP of Energy and Charging Mike Snyder and Matrix Managing Director Sergio Arbeláez both commented on the partnership, with Arbeláez noting the project demonstrates Matrix's ability to execute utility-scale storage through long-term partnerships.

2. **Context within Tesla's broader energy pipeline:** The article notes this project joins a growing list of GWh-scale Megapack deployments globally, and that Tesla has been ramping production at its Lathrop Megafactory in California to meet this type of demand. It also references a previously reported multi-billion dollar US contract for over 15 GWh of deployment, and mentions Tesla's earlier Pillswood project in the UK, once one of Europe's largest.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls this a huge project, noting 1 GWh can power hundreds of thousands of homes for the battery's two-hour duration. He states that Tesla's energy division is the only part of the business currently growing financially, and frames large UK projects like this as essential to reducing wind curtailment by storing cheap energy for peak demand. He speculates the project may also use Tesla's Autobidder software, which he says has been standard on most of Tesla's large UK deployments for real-time energy market trading.
