Investor Larry Goldberg presents a self-built Monte Carlo valuation model (20,000 simulations) for SpaceX, arguing that the company's value is overwhelmingly driven by a business that barely exists yet — orbital (space-based) data centers — far more than by Starlink, terrestrial data centers, or the launch business. The model shows enormous dispersion: a base case well above the current share price, a meaningful chance of a wipeout if orbital compute economics fail, and a near-certain need for large outside funding within roughly two years.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Base, high, and low case valuations:** Goldberg's model puts a base-case SpaceX share value of $322, a high case of $1,337, and a low case of $8 (the model technically cannot go negative, so it floors near zero), looking forward roughly two years from today and discounting back to present value.
2. **Monte Carlo distribution and probabilities:** Across the 20,000 simulated trials, the median (P50) value is $228 versus a cited close of $156. About 59% of trials land above today's close, 39% land above $300/share, and 11% land above $500/share; roughly 10% of trials produce a negative or near-zero outcome.
3. **Orbital capex per watt is the single biggest value driver:** On Goldberg's "tornado" sensitivity chart, orbital capital cost per watt is by far the largest swing factor in the model — he states it alone swings value by over $560/share in both directions — dwarfing revenue, orbital gigawatts deployed, Starlink subscriber counts, Starship flight rate, and terrestrial data-center economics.
4. **Terrestrial data centers are high-revenue but low-impact on value:** Despite potentially generating on the order of $100+ billion per year in revenue, Goldberg says this business "fades into insignificance" in valuation terms compared to the capital required and risk embedded in the orbital compute buildout; he argues that without the orbital ambition, the terrestrial data center business alone would support a higher, less risky stock price.
5. **Elon Musk retweeted the analysis:** Goldberg says Musk retweeted his valuation thread but subsequently posted that he expects SpaceX revenue to reach the trillions within under ten years, implying Musk is considerably more bullish than Goldberg's own "conservative" model.
6. **Goldberg's personal position:** He describes himself as a long-term SpaceX shareholder currently locked up from selling, says he was buying shares up to about $160 and has paused purchases above that level, and frames his decision ahead as whether to hold, trim, or add once unlocked.
7. **Key risk-resolution timeline:** Goldberg expects clarity on orbital compute's true costs and throughput before mid next year, once a data-processing satellite (potentially using a Vera Rubin-class chip or an earlier chip) is operating in orbit; he expects broader market risk clarification by the end of next year, potentially pushing the share price into the $500–$600 range if the mid-range assumptions prove correct.
8. **Competitive moat view:** Goldberg argues that if SpaceX can execute orbital compute economically at scale, "nobody can compete," estimating China is roughly three to five years behind and Amazon/Blue Origin-based efforts roughly a year or more behind.
9. **Tesla–SpaceX merger seen as de-risking funding:** Goldberg argues a Tesla–SpaceX merger would make raising the large sums needed easier, noting Tesla has roughly $40 billion on hand and SpaceX roughly $100 billion, a combined $140 billion (excluding Terafab-related spend) that he says still falls short of what's needed.

## Financials
1. **Cash runway and funding gap:** Goldberg's model shows SpaceX's cash effectively hitting zero around Q3–Q4 2027 if a roughly $15 billion minimum cash buffer is maintained, with the company needing new funding on the order of $62.5 billion by Q4 2028; he describes every one of his 20,000 simulated paths as requiring outside capital by the end of 2028, with a median funding gap he cites elsewhere as around $77–80 billion. He does not view raising this capital as difficult given current market conditions, but flags credit-market conditions as a major swing risk.
2. **Rumored $40 billion credit facility excluded from model:** Goldberg confirms his model does not include a reported $40 billion credit line because, as he understands it, it has not been finalized/signed — he treated it as an unconfirmed rumor and excluded it, noting it would otherwise extend the funding runway by an estimated three to four quarters.
3. **Three top risks identified:** Goldberg frames SpaceX's risk as centered on (1) access to credit/capital amid a fragile debt market, (2) technical execution risk in building orbital compute (cooling, communications, cluster configuration), and (3) whether orbital compute can be done economically at sufficient scale/cost-per-watt — he says resolving any one of these materially raises the valuation and lowers risk.
