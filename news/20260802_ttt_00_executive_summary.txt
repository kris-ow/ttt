## Brief

- WSJ Tesla-China-sale story flatly denied by Musk and Tesla China
- SpaceX earnings due August 4, share-unlock August 6
- Q2 revenue $28.2B beat, EPS 33¢ missed estimates

- Zoox gets capped 2,500-vehicle NHTSA exemption, Cybercab uncapped
- NHTSA/SAE launch 3-year push for national AV standards

## Autonomous Driving
- NHTSA and SAE Industries Technology Consortium launched a 3-year, $5 million effort to build the first national AV performance standards, aimed at replacing today's state-by-state patchwork, per brighterwithherbert.
- Zoox received a temporary NHTSA exemption capping commercial deployment at 2,500 vehicles/year for two years, reportedly tied to Zoox failing self-certification crash testing on its backward-facing seating; Cybercab has already passed self-certification with no stated deployment cap, per Lars Moravy as cited on the show.
- A Detroit News piece profiled seniors, including a 78-year-old retired GM engineer, using FSD at very high rates to preserve independent mobility — cited by futureaza as evidence FSD's real-world impact is already tangible, not just a future robotaxi benefit.

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's dual-fleet plan — company-owned Robotaxis plus private FSD owners joining the network — is likened by brighterwithherbert to Amazon's FBA model, though the host stresses owner-fleet participation remains roadmap, not live; today's fleet is company-owned only.
- Robotaxi rides continue across Austin, Dallas, Houston, and three Florida cities with no dedicated robotaxi revenue line in financial disclosures yet — a future breakout being watched as a potential re-rating signal.
- Jo Bhakdi says Cybercab won't move Tesla's earnings needle for nine months without fleet scaling into the thousands, but states he "strongly believes" Tesla hits 1,000 robotaxis this year; he also argues Musk currently sees Robotaxi as a mature, "boring" program not requiring his personal attention.

## Humanoid Bots
- Jo Bhakdi expects an eventual Optimus V3 reveal to be impressive but unlikely before around December.
- Musk reportedly overhauled Optimus's team over the past year after calling the program "a mess," per Jo Bhakdi, with the program now characterized as back on track.

## Financials
- Q2 revenue was $28.2B, up 26% YoY, beating estimates; EPS came in at 33¢ vs. 49¢ expected, a miss the host attributes to reinvestment (capex $5.79B, up 142%) rather than weakness.
- Services revenue hit $4.58B, up 50% at record margins; energy deployment reached 13.5 GWh, up 40%+ YoY — segments brighterwithherbert argues the market still undervalues as "car company" revenue.
- Tesla trades near 156x forward earnings and has been roughly flat near $389 for five years, a stagnation the host compares to Amazon's pre-AWS-disclosure decade.
- Jo Bhakdi notes Tesla fell to ~$298 mid-week before closing near $311 Friday (+1.4% weekly, -20% trailing month), attributing the drop largely to broader market contagion from a hedge-fund unwind rather than Tesla-specific news.

## Market & Competition
- Michael Burry is reportedly short Nvidia, Tesla, and the semiconductor index, betting on a 30–40% crash by March 2027; brighterwithherbert partially counters that Tesla's flat five-year price means an AI premium hasn't been priced in.
- A leveraged hedge fund ("Situational Awareness," run by a 23-year-old former Anthropic investor) reportedly peaked near $45B in assets before margin calls cut it to roughly $10B, absorbed by Citadel — Jo Bhakdi links this collapse to July's severe momentum-stock selloff, including Tesla's decline.
- CXMT, China's largest memory chipmaker, jumped 466% on IPO day to a roughly $500B valuation, adding competitive pressure that reinforces the strategic case for Tesla/SpaceX's Terafab chip initiative, per brighterwithherbert.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SpaceX stock has fallen roughly 44–49% from its post-IPO high (from a high near $225 to ~$113–114); futureaza's hosts attribute this mainly to an imminent stockholder unlock expanding float from ~5% to an estimated 25–35%, plus Starship test-flight uncertainty.
- SpaceX earnings are set for August 4, with a share-lockup unlock around August 6 that Jo Bhakdi calls a key signal for selling pressure; he plans to watch price action through roughly August 13.
- Wall Street's average 12-month SpaceX price target is $230 (30 of 32 covering institutions), with a high estimate around $800 — though futureaza's host cautions analyst targets were nearly worthless for Tesla between 2017–2020.
- Alexandra Merz (speculative, unconfirmed) theorizes a Tesla-SpaceX merger could include a clause waiving Chinese regulatory approval as a closing condition, estimates only a 10-15% chance of a full Tesla China divestiture, and floats an announcement window of August 10-15, 2026 — all explicitly framed as her own speculation, not confirmed fact.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Jo Bhakdi argues Colossus (terrestrial compute) is the single most important near-term earnings driver for SpaceX's upcoming report, more than Starlink or Starship, and says he'll publish detailed projections before the August 4 call while pushing back on what he calls overly exuberant gigawatt-scale buildout expectations.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Falcon 9 Block 5 has the best reliability record of any rocket in history, per Randy Kirk, while Starship continues working through engineering issues; SpaceX has caught the booster twice, reflown one, and achieved a gentle second-stage splashdown.
- Jo Bhakdi expects Starship to reach orbit and reduce costs by roughly 2027–2028, but not to be commercially ready in 2027 or to meaningfully drive earnings in that window.
