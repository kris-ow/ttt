This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi — an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur — discussing Elon Musk's Forbes interview, the dismissal of his OpenAI lawsuit, SpaceX's AI computing ambitions, the anticipated SpaceX IPO, and the upcoming Starship V3 test flight. The core thesis is that Elon is strategically positioning SpaceX/XAI as the dominant provider of space-based AI compute infrastructure, while forming pragmatic alliances with Anthropic and Nvidia rather than treating them as adversaries.

## Market & Competition

1. **OpenAI Lawsuit Dismissed on Statute of Limitations:** A California court dismissed Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI, ruling that the five-year filing window exceeded the three-year statute of limitations. Elon announced he will file an appeal with the Ninth Circuit, arguing the ruling sets a dangerous precedent allowing individuals to "loot charities" by keeping enrichment quiet for a few years. He maintains that Sam Altman and Greg Brockman did in fact enrich themselves by converting a charity, and that the judge and jury never ruled on the merits of the case.

2. **Lawsuit as Strategic Aggression, Not Just Legal Action:** Jo Bhakdi frames the lawsuit as a calculated political move rather than a genuine legal effort. He argues Elon's team almost certainly knew the statute of limitations would be a problem, but the real objectives were achieved regardless: massive distraction for OpenAI through depositions, public exposure of damaging internal emails, reputational damage to the OpenAI leadership team, and likely delay of OpenAI's IPO. Bhakdi characterizes this as a "win" for Elon's broader strategic goals even in legal defeat, with SpaceX now positioned to IPO ahead of OpenAI and Anthropic potentially second.

3. **Elon's Alliance Strategy — Anthropic and Nvidia as Partners, Not Rivals:** Rather than positioning XAI as a head-to-head competitor with Anthropic or Nvidia, Elon publicly defended Anthropic when a social media post claimed Cursor's Composer 2.5 model would "destroy" them. Elon stated: "Anthropic will not be destroyed. The AI plus the harness goes far beyond coding and Opus 4.7 is still better than Composer 2.5, albeit a lot more expensive. Cursor is however an important piece of the puzzle to make Grok much better." Jo Bhakdi interprets this as Elon recognizing Anthropic's formidable lead in AI models and Jensen Huang's dominance in chips, and choosing to build a strategic alliance — owning the compute and physical AI stack — rather than attacking competitors in their areas of strength.

4. **XAI Positioning as "EWS" — Elon's AWS Equivalent:** The hosts describe Elon's evolving strategy for XAI and SpaceX as building something analogous to Amazon Web Services — renting out space-based data centers and compute to customers including Google (already in talks to rent orbital data centers) and, they predict, Microsoft next. The argument is that what began as internal compute for Tesla cars and Optimus robots is now being commercialized as a vendor business. Cursor recently began using XAI's data centers, and Bhakdi notes they "haven't even used all of it yet."

5. **Nvidia Delivers First Vera CPU to SpaceX/XAI:** Nvidia Vice President of Hyperscale Ian Buck hand-delivered the first Vera CPU servers to a select group of launch customers: Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX, and Oracle. Vera is described as Nvidia's first custom CPU purpose-built for the age of agentic AI — a shift beyond LLMs toward AI agents that need significantly more CPU power (88 CPU cores) to execute tasks outside the LLM itself. The hosts note that SpaceX/XAI being on this short list confirms Elon's priority standing with Nvidia, and that this milestone is reminiscent of Jensen Huang's first GPU gift to OpenAI that "kicked off AI."

6. **Space-Based AI Data Centers — Elon's Core Thesis:** In his Forbes interview, Elon argued that data centers in space are "much easier than people may think," citing SpaceX's current 10,000 satellites in orbit and projecting launches of over 10,000 communication satellites per year with Starship in the future — each significantly more capable than current satellites. He stated that AI satellite tonnage will dwarf communication satellite tonnage. Bhakdi frames this as Elon's decisive strategic domain: owning the space compute layer of the AI stack while letting Anthropic and Nvidia lead on models and chips respectively.

## Financials

1. **SpaceX IPO Expected Imminently — S1 Filing Anticipated:** The hosts report that the SpaceX IPO prospectus S1 filing is expected to drop the following day (2026-05-20) on the SEC's EDGAR database. The IPO date is rumored to be June 12, 2026. Rumored underwriters are four equal co-leads — JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Bank of America — with an unusual 30% retail allocation (versus a typical ~10%). Broker access varies: Vanguard reportedly gets no allocation with limit-only day-one orders; Robinhood reportedly gets allocation as a "likely lot type"; Fidelity reportedly considers IPO allocation for accounts holding $100,000 or more.

2. **Starship V3 Test Flight Stakes for IPO Valuation:** A Starship V3 test flight was scheduled for the day after the video's publication (approximately 2026-05-20). V3 incorporates improvements from V1 and V2, including a reduced-mass Raptor engine (1,500 kg), fewer grid fins, and a new launchpad. While Elon publicly downplayed the stakes — noting SpaceX has roughly 10 more ships and about half that number of boosters in production this year — Jo Bhakdi argues the flight is enormously consequential for the IPO. He notes that if Starship is carrying 150 tons of GPUs as cargo, the insured value could approach $1 billion, and a 20% failure rate would add approximately $200 million of risk cost per launch on top of the ~$90–100 million base launch cost. A successful flight, by contrast, could send the stock "to three trillion."
