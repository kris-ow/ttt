## Autonomous Driving

- **17 NHTSA crash reports unredacted:** Tesla had been the only ADS operator to fully redact its crash narratives under NHTSA's Standing General Order 2021-01, citing potential "financial harm" to competitors. Tesla has now removed the confidential designation across all 17 reports covering July 2025–March 2026. All incidents involve a 2026 Model Y with ADS engaged and a safety monitor present in Austin, TX. Injury breakdown: 13 property damage only, 2 no injuries, 1 minor non-hospitalization, 1 minor with hospitalization. Seven of 17 incidents occurred while Tesla was stationary, indicating no Tesla fault. April 2026 added 2 incidents, both at 0 mph, zero new injuries. Per Electrek's Fred Lambert, the safety profile largely mirrors Waymo's rear-ending patterns — Tesla's year-long redaction was a "self-inflicted wound" — but teleoperator failures and spatial awareness gaps are flagged as genuine concerns.

- **Teleoperator failures documented twice:** Two incidents involved remote operators causing accidents after taking control of the ADS. In July 2025, a teleoperator drove the vehicle up a curb into a metal fence at 8 mph. In January 2026, a teleoperator drove a stopped vehicle into a construction barricade at ~9 mph, scraping the front left fender. No passengers were present in either incident. Per Dillon Loomis (Electrified), these incidents represent a systemic concern for scaling, particularly as Tesla moves toward removing safety monitors.

- **ADS spatial awareness failures identified:** Several incidents reflect difficulty detecting small or unusual obstacles — reversing into a wooden electrical pole at 1 mph, clipping a dump trailer's gooseneck hitch at 18 mph, contacting a metal chain at 6 mph, and hitting a curb while reversing at 2 mph. Dillon notes the camera system still struggles to consistently detect thinner objects at certain height levels; Electrek's Lambert suggests ultrasonics and radar may have helped prevent some incidents.

- **~2 million autonomous miles reached:** Tesla's Austin robotaxi program is approaching approximately 2 million autonomous miles driven since the June 2025 launch, per Electrified.

- **FSD 143.2 sets Cannonball Run record:** A team completed a coast-to-coast run from California to New York — 2,833 miles — in 49 hours and 55 minutes with zero disengagements or human interventions, beating the prior FSD Cannonball record by 8.5 hours. Human input was required only for charging stops.

- **Tesla Vision superhuman night capability:** Elon Musk posted a comparison between a standard RGB image and Tesla AI's photon-count reconstruction, demonstrating why FSD performs well at night and through glare. FSD V14 was highlighted as a significant improvement over V12/V13 in adverse weather, with one host noting the car autonomously reduced its maximum available speed in rain without driver input.

- **EU expansion underway:** Ireland is in active talks with Tesla on FSD approval, planning to adopt EU-level regulations rather than create independent rules. The Netherlands has already approved FSD; Belgium was cited as likely next. Tesla is proactively engaging individual member states ahead of an EU-wide FSD policy vote.

- **FSD data labeling job posted in China:** Tesla posted a China role for a data labeler to label fleet images and videos for its deep learning network, alongside Optimus data. Per Electrified, this signals Tesla is now collecting China-specific data to train a localized FSD model — identified as the primary limiting factor for FSD performance in China.

- **FSD approval in China viewed as imminent:** Tesla's China job postings recently added the word "urgent," and a robotaxi-specific role appeared in China for the first time. The brighterwithherbert panel's consensus: the logical sequence is FSD approval → robotaxi launch → Cybercab production in China, with Giga Shanghai or a new facility required given the impracticality of US imports. A Germany-style FSD community transit pilot is already live in Germany's Eifel region, transporting students and seniors in rural areas with limited public transit.

## Robotaxi

- **Las Vegas Cybercab maintenance hub permitted; launch ~45 days out:** Tesla filed a permit with Clark County on May 12 for a "Tesla Center Cybercab Phase 2 Car Wash" — approximately 36,000 sq ft with ~55 parking spots, involving a car wash enclosure, tire service equipment relocation, and power raceway installation. The "Phase 2" designation implies concurrent Phase 1 work at the same site. Per Dillon Loomis, Tesla is targeting a Las Vegas robotaxi launch "hopefully sometime in the next 45 days."

- **Austin operational gaps post-5 PM:** Rider Ethan McConno reported that unsupervised robotaxi rides in Austin effectively stop around 5:00 PM on weekdays, with only 1–2 unmarked supervised cars operating after that window, generating 20–30 minute wait times. McConno praised driving performance in complex, high-traffic scenarios as "incredibly impressive" but catalogued a "long list of important product issues and bugs" — bad pickup/drop-off locations, broken audio, connectivity issues, and UI/UX problems.

- **Scaling constraints flagged:** Per Electrified, Houston's and Dallas's geofences remain very small, with Houston's zone excluding downtown. Dillon states he is "very confident you're going to end up disappointed unless we see significant geofence expansion" if expectations of 1,000 robotaxis across Austin, Dallas, and Houston are held.

- **Cybercab spotted with Starlink Mini:** A Cybercab was photographed with a Starlink Mini mounted on the back — reported as the first known public sighting of this configuration. Tesla Beat hosts noted this could improve fleet resilience, referencing the San Francisco power outage that caused Waymo vehicles to fail while Tesla's vision-based system was unaffected. Dillon Loomis treats it as a testing observation only, not confirmed production integration.

- **~70 Cybercabs in Giga Texas outbound lot:** Approximately 70 Cybercabs were spotted in the outbound lot at Giga Texas, up from prior sightings of 30–40 units. Per Joe Tegtmeyer, most appear to be the same units from the prior day, meaning units sit for multiple days before being moved — the figure does not represent single-day output. The brighterwithherbert panel interpreted this as evidence of a ramping production line.

- **Waymo recall and San Antonio suspension:** Waymo issued a recall for 3,800 vehicles for software allowing robotaxis to drive onto flooded roadways; an interim software update has shipped. San Antonio service remains suspended after a vehicle drove into a flooded road and was swept into a creek (no passengers on board).

- **Uber-Waymo rift escalates:** Uber executives publicly criticized Waymo's deployment strategy and shared a "scary Waymo moment" video. Uber's CEO Dara Khosrowshahi stated AVs are "still far from capable of meeting the level of reliability and ubiquity that customers and cities expect." Per Dillon Loomis, Waymo's CPUC permit expansion to Oakland is suspended through June 27, 2026 — meaning Uber's claim that Waymo is withholding service there is inaccurate on its face. The broader framing: Uber's business model is directly threatened by Tesla and Waymo operating independently.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Optimus V3 design reportedly signed off:** Per an unverified but trusted source on X cited by Randy Kirk (FutureAzA), Optimus V3 has completed design finalization. Randy frames this as a strong signal the robot meets Elon Musk's standards. Key milestones he is watching: production start, factory ramp at Fremont, pricing/billing model announcement, and real-world capability demonstration.

- **Optimus production line replacing Model S/X at Giga Texas:** Tesla has stopped Model S/X production and is installing an Optimus production line at Giga Texas targeting capacity for one million robots. Foundation work is underway. Tesla is also hiring data collection operators for Optimus across approximately 12 offices and factories, consistent with Elon's stated goal of ~5,000 Optimus units training in Tesla facilities. Reported stress tests involve continuous 30-pound loads — beyond sustained human capacity for a full workday.

- **Wall Street still not modeling Optimus:** No major Wall Street analysts — including Piper Sandler's Alex Potter — have included Optimus revenue in their models. Piper Sandler's note explicitly states Tesla investors are "getting Optimus for free" at $400/share. Per the brighterwithherbert panel, ARK Invest is working on Optimus research papers but may wait for a robotaxi inflection point before adding it to their model.

- **Figure AI cross-training benchmark and F.04 update:** Figure AI's live-streamed robot marathon exceeded 50 hours with four robots sorting over 63,000 packages. CEO Brett Adcock disclosed that Figure's F.04 robot has completed design lock and is expected to debut in fall 2026 — described as the biggest performance leap between versions. A notable cross-training result: fridge-loading reliability jumped from 60% to 90% after adding kitchen shelving training to the same model. Figure's supply chain is nearly fully localized away from China, expected complete by Q3 2026. Panelists on brighterwithherbert noted Figure's claw-style hands without forearm actuators limit it to sorting/domestic use cases versus Optimus's targeted heavy industrial work. Elon Musk recently indicated Tesla has changed course on the tendon-driven forearm hand design described in its original patent, making Optimus hand architecture a key area to watch.

## Energy

- **Tesla Semi E-PTO delivers 25 kW to refrigerated trailers:** Tesla Semi's Electric Power Take-Off eliminates the need for diesel auxiliary motors on refrigerated loads. Hosts cited ~50% cost-per-mile savings in California versus diesel. An early pilot at Covenant Transport received positive results, with VP of Sustainability Matt Mullen praising the trip planner's integration of distance, traffic, wind, grade, and weather inputs for range management. More economics data forthcoming.

- **$250M invested in Giga Berlin 4680 cell factory:** Tesla announced a $250 million investment targeting 18 GWh of annual cell production and 1,500 new jobs. For comparison, Giga Nevada runs 35 GWh — the Berlin factory's smaller footprint reflects meaningfully improved energy density per square foot of manufacturing space.

## Electric Vehicles

- **Panasonic raises US battery forecast +19% to 46 GWh, citing Tesla recovery:** Panasonic told investors it expects US EV battery sales to grow to 46 GWh in its fiscal year ending March 2027, explicitly citing Tesla's ongoing market share recovery "despite significant revisions to their model lineups." Panasonic projects the broader US EV market to be roughly flat year-over-year, implying Tesla is expected to outperform the segment.

- **Model S and Model X production ends at Fremont:** The last Model S and Model X units have rolled off the Fremont line — ending 14 years of Model S production and 11 years of Model X production. The final Model S is expected to go to Steve Jurvetson, who received the first one. The line is being repurposed for Optimus manufacturing.

- **Giga Berlin hits 750,000 Model Y milestone:** Giga Berlin has produced 750,000 Model Y vehicles and separately reached 1 million drive units produced. 4680 cells are being or will soon be manufactured on campus.

- **Model Y L confirmed for Singapore/Southeast Asia; US arrival expected by end of 2026:** Kyle Connor (6'1") confirmed adequate third-row knee clearance at the Beijing Auto Show. The vehicle is being delivered in Singapore and spreading across Oceania and Southeast Asia. A brief appearance on Tesla's US website and Bay Area test sightings are cited as signals for a 2026 US launch.

- **Tesla China denies Model Y price increase:** Tesla China officially denied reports of a domestic Model Y price increase despite rising global DRAM memory chip costs — estimated by NIO's CEO to add 3,000–5,000 yuan (~up to ~$750) to high-end EV costs. Tesla and BYD are reportedly among the two largest global DRAM consumers. Tesla is working to bring memory production in-house as a long-term mitigation.

- **Honda books first annual loss in ~70 years:** Honda reported more than $9 billion in EV restructuring costs, scrapped its 20%-EV-by-2030 target, abandoned its 2040 full-EV/fuel cell transition goal, and indefinitely suspended an $11 billion EV and battery manufacturing investment in Canada.

Breakdown: ~62.8% automotive, ~17.5% energy storage, ~6.9% FSD subscriptions, ~6.9% regulatory credits, small slice from robotaxi/services/charging. The FSD subscription share was described as higher than expected given the recurring, high-margin nature of those revenues.

- **Piper Sandler (Alex Potter) initiates at $500; Optimus excluded entirely:** Potter's model includes FSD licensing, automaker hardware purchases, and robotaxi revenue but pushes most dates to 2030 and beyond. Key figures: FSD subscription revenue modeled at $900M (2027) → $1.8B (2028) → $4.1B shortly after, more than doubling repeatedly through 2031. Megapack modeled at $9.7B in 2026, not exceeding $12B until 2030 — which the brighterwithherbert panel characterized as inconsistent with company guidance. Tesla insurance modeled at $1.6B in 2026, growing to $26B by 2035 and ~$300B by 2045. Optimus not modeled; the explicit thesis is that investors are "getting it for free" at $400/share. Potter is scheduled to join the brighterwithherbert panel within approximately two weeks.

- **Bhakdi revises 2030 base case to $4,000/share:** Jo Bhakdi disclosed a downward revision from $6,000 to $4,000 as his 2030 base case, citing increased caution on timing of robotaxi and Optimus scaling. A 10x outcome (~$4,500+ from current levels) remains possible, he said, "if you see millions of robotaxis and a million humanoids in 2030." Pioneer Alpha fund is up ~36% YTD and ~166% over two years and five months.

Bhakdi attributes the move to "sell the news" dynamics following the Trump-Xi Beijing summit and characterizes the low volume as a weak bearish signal. Despite the daily decline, Tesla is up ~8% over the prior month and ~20% since April 9.

- **SpaceX IPO targeting June 12 at ~$2 trillion valuation:** Multiple sources cite June 12, 2026 as the expected IPO date under ticker SPCX, with a target valuation of approximately $2 trillion. Road show reportedly slated for approximately June 8. Ron Baron reportedly stated SpaceX will be added to three index funds within 15 days of listing. - **Anthropic purchasing inference compute from xAI:** Anthropic is buying inference compute from xAI after being unable to source adequate capacity elsewhere, including from AWS. Reported deal estimates range from $4B to $16B annually, with $5–6B cited most commonly. Randy Kirk (FutureAzA) frames this as xAI monetizing spare capacity from one of the world's largest compute clusters, with an even larger facility being built adjacent.

- **Uber data collection play challenged by Tesla's existing scale:** Uber announced ambitions to outfit human-driven vehicles with sensors to collect real-world AI training data, arguing even a fraction of its global driver fleet would create an unmatched data platform. Per Dillon Loomis, Tesla already has millions of vehicles functioning as integrated data collection platforms globally — with far greater consistency than third-party sensor kits bolted onto Uber vehicles.
