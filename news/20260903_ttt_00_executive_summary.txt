## Brief

- Tesla holds Cybercab launch event at Giga Texas, no livestream, under NDA
- Texas DMV Cybercab registrations jump from 1 to 45
- Texas DOT reportedly certifies Cybercab as Level 4 vehicle
- TSLA closes up ~5.4% on launch day, volume above average
- Nevada grants Tesla permit for up to 5,000 robotaxis
- SpaceX completes full 33-engine static fire ahead of Flight 14

## Robotaxi
- Tesla's Cybercab launch event took place September 3rd at Giga Texas, invite-only via rider sweepstakes; no livestream and attendees reportedly signed NDAs, so the market won't get a single clear fleet-size number at launch — actual scale will likely emerge over coming weeks via trackers and rider reports (per jobhakdi).
- Texas DMV data showed Cybercab registrations to Tesla Robotaxi LLC jumping from 1 to 45 in hours, alongside 269 new Model Ys; Morgan Stanley (price target $400) cited the ~45 figure ("circa 40") as reason for a "positive bias" going into the event. Multiple hosts caution registered and in-service are different things — estimates for actual first-week deployment range from under 25 (Herbert) to "100-plus" over coming weeks (jobhakdi).
- Separately, Tesla added 106 Model Ys to its Texas robotaxi fleet per DMV data, bringing total registered robotaxi vehicles in Texas to 420 (per Sawyer Merritt) — a different data cut than the DMV Cybercab-specific figures above.
- Texas DOT reportedly completed Level 4 autonomous certification for Cybercab the Monday before the event, which futureaza's hosts frame as pushing back on claims Tesla couldn't reach Level 4/5 without AGI.
- Tesla launched a new webpage soliciting interest in Cybercab fleet-vehicle purchasing and mobility-hub partnerships, and refreshed its Robotaxi app/website to list Cybercab as "currently being offered" across Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa, with Robovan listed as "arriving soon" (per jobhakdi).
- Nevada issued Tesla a statewide autonomous network permit for up to 5,000 robotaxis in Clark County/Las Vegas, roughly five times the 1,000-vehicle permit Waymo received the same day in a market it has operated for years (per brighterwithherbert).
- Austin's robotaxi service area reportedly grew ~9% north along I-35 and Dallas's coverage more than doubled to ~80 square miles over the weekend before the launch, per a crowdsourced tracker cited by brighterwithherbert; the same tracker showed the unsupervised Model Y robotaxi fleet across Austin, Dallas, and Houston growing roughly 7x in three weeks (a fresher figure than RobotaxiTracker.com's stale ~48 count).
- Cost/production estimates (jobhakdi, unverified): Cybercab production cost currently ~$16,000–$18,000, falling below $20,000 once production reaches hundreds of thousands of units (~2028); current production estimated at ~3,000 units, rising to 5,000–6,000 by October.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's head of AI Ashok Elluswamy posted video of Cybercabs driving themselves around Austin with no steering wheel, pedals, or occupant, saying "the streets won't be the same anymore"; Musk subsequently confirmed Tesla intends to flood Austin with the vehicles (per brighterwithherbert).
- Elon Musk met with France's Minister of Transportation to advance FSD (Supervised) approval; a quoted statement indicates French authorities are working with Tesla on "technical adaptations" toward a regulatory green light (per Sawyer Merritt).

## Financials
- TSLA closed up roughly 5.4% on launch day (intraday highs near 7%), on above-average volume (~43M shares vs. ~33–39M average); commentators attribute the move partly to the Morgan Stanley note and Cybercab news, though the broader market rallied that day too (Snowflake +16%, MicroStrategy +17%, Robinhood +16%), per jobhakdi.
- Both futureaza hosts argue product events historically don't durably move TSLA (citing Battery Day, Investor Day, AI Day 1/2), and expect any post-event pop to fade within days absent a real acceleration signal — e.g., confidence in a newer FSD version pushing toward 1,000 robotaxis within a month, beyond the ~1,000–2,000 by year-end the market is pricing in.
- Deutsche Bank reportedly bought $4 billion of Tesla stock, cited as evidence of institutional buying (per futureaza).
- Host jobhakdi previewed a DCF-based fair-value estimate of ~$740 for TSLA by December 2026 (detail pending), calling Morgan Stanley's 30,000-robotaxi-by-2030 projection "completely ridiculous" and too conservative.

## Energy
- SpaceX is reportedly building an in-house gas turbine blade foundry near a Starlink plant in Bastrop, Texas, addressing an industry-wide turbine component bottleneck (per The Information, cited by brighterwithherbert); job postings for the role reportedly predate the report by about four months.
- Musk said SpaceX and Tesla are each building 100 GW/year of solar production capacity, with natural gas as a multi-year bridge, and that in-house blade casting could accelerate gas turbine deployment "by up to 18 months" — described by the host as Musk's projection, not a delivered outcome.
- Tesla filed a $10.1B tax incentive application for a solar cell factory in Fort Bend County, Texas ("Project Crystal Sun"), construction starting this year, targeted completion ~2028, commercial production early 2029; the filing itself calls the plant "foundational" to a 100 GW/year domestic solar target. No ground has been broken, and Tesla has cited a competing out-of-state site still in negotiation.

## SpaceX — Starship
- SpaceX completed a full-duration static fire of all 33 engines on the Super Heavy booster for Flight 14; Musk called it "the most powerful moving object ever made by humans."
- Flight 14 is planned to carry roughly 20 Starlink V3 satellites — Starship's first real payload — with a targeted mid-September launch window. Musk said on SpaceX's earnings call he considers the heat shield problem "solved at this point" (with a self-described "jinx" caveat); a tower catch of the upper stage is reportedly still a few months out and not expected on this flight.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX has outlined plans for orbital data centers — solar-powered server satellites — with prototypes reportedly planned for next year; Musk has said space could become the cheapest venue for AI compute within two to three years, with Flight 14 framed as a prerequisite gate for that roadmap rather than confirmation of it.

## Market & Competition
- Uber reportedly announced layoffs of around 3,000 (some chat commentary cited "10%"), which jobhakdi suggested reflects competitive pressure from autonomous ride-hailing.
- California will offer Uber/Lyft drivers up to $20,300 (new EV) or $14,200 (used EV) toward EV purchases starting this month, plus up to $1,170/year for charging — a potential demand tailwind given Tesla's rideshare presence (per Sawyer Merritt).
- Range Rover unveiled its first EV, the Range Rover Electric SUV, starting at $138,000 with 333 miles EPA range and a native NACS port in North America — another legacy automaker adopting Tesla's charging standard.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla published battery safety data claiming no evidence of a single spontaneous battery failure leading to a vehicle fire across over 265 billion miles of operation (Model 3, Model Y, Cybertruck, Semi) through end of 2025, alongside a post detailing its "layered approach" to containing thermal reactions from single-cell issues.
