The article reports on car reviewer Doug DeMuro's prediction that used Tesla Cybertruck prices will fall sharply, reflecting weak resale value and soft demand for the truck.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Cybertruck price collapse predicted:** Doug DeMuro, founder of auction platform Cars & Bids, said on his podcast that he currently sees used Cybertrucks selling in the $60,000s, "knocking on the door of the $50,000s," and predicted that in 18 months the truck will be selling for $35,000.
2. **Pricing history and value loss:** Tesla originally set a $40,000 base price for the Cybertruck at its 2019 unveiling, but it launched in 2023 starting at $80,000 with reduced range and fewer features than promised. Early "Foundation Series" trucks sold for over $100,000 (often $120,000+ with taxes and fees), and Tesla continued producing them into 2025 before switching to the regular Cybertruck, priced from $80,000, in late 2024. The article notes a drop to $35,000 would mean owners lose about $45,000 in depreciation over 3-4 years.
3. **Weak demand context:** The article references prior Electrek reporting that Tesla has worked through its Cybertruck reservation list much faster than expected because the conversion rate from the roughly 2 million pre-orders was much lower than that headline figure suggested, and that inventory has been piling up at delivery centers. It also notes Tesla sells the Cybertruck at only about 10% of its production capacity, and that SpaceX purchased over 1,000 Cybertrucks this quarter, which the article says is unlikely to meaningfully affect used prices.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls DeMuro's $35,000 prediction "harsh" and says he personally thinks it's "a bit aggressive" for 18 months out, suggesting prices might instead settle around $40,000. He speculates that if DeMuro is right, the Cybertruck could fail as a luxury status symbol but "succeed accidentally" as a cheap work truck rather than the influencer toy it was marketed as. A top reader comment (from Jo Borras, 27 likes) agrees $35,000 for a Cybertruck with around 100,000 miles at that age "sounds spot-on," comparing the depreciation pattern to niche luxury vehicles like the Mercedes S-Class.
