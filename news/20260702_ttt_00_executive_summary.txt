## Electric Vehicles
- **Q2 2026: 480,126 deliveries, 451,758 produced — best Q2 in Tesla history.** Result beat Wall Street consensus of 406,024 by ~74,000 units (+18%), surpassing even the most bullish sell-side forecasts (Goldman Sachs/Barclays at ~418,000–420,000) by more than 60,000 units. YoY growth of 25%, QoQ growth of 34% from Q1's 358,023. Model 3/Y accounted for 467,762 deliveries; Other Models 12,364. Tesla delivered ~28,000 more vehicles than it produced, drawing down inventory built in prior quarters.
- **Giga Berlin ramping from 5,000 to 7,500 units/week** through end of 2026, a pace that could add roughly 100,000 incremental annual units per FutureAzA's James Stephenson, who cited the ramp as a key input in the closest community forecast (483,000).
- **China deliveries up 24% year-over-year in June**, per community data shared during the FutureAzA live stream.
- **Model Y L launches in the US at $61,990** for the initial Launch Series trim — above the ~$54,000 analysts had expected based on the ~$4,000 premium the L commanded over the standard Model Y in China (where it launched in August 2025). The stretched wheelbase (+150 mm) accommodates a 2+2+2 six-seat layout, 325 miles of EPA-estimated range, and 0–60 mph in 4.4 seconds. Rivals Kia EV9 ($54,900) and Hyundai Ioniq 9 ($58,955) both undercut the Launch Series price; a lower non-Launch-Series trim has not yet been announced.
- **Q2 2026 earnings call set for Wednesday, July 22, 2026**, after market close, at 4:30 p.m. CT. Net income, margins, and cash flow will be reported then.
- **Cybertruck earned the top safety pick** across all pickup trucks — EV or ICE — per the sources.

## Robotaxi
- **Production Cybercab confirmed on Austin public roads with no steering wheel, no pedals.** Elon Musk confirmed the vehicle is in an "engineering test" with a safety monitor present but not operating the car; Ashok Elluswamy separately confirmed it is driving autonomously. This is the first public confirmation of a production-spec Cybercab (no driver controls) operating on open roads.
- **Cybercab manufacturing line is 90% automated**, per Lars Moravy — ahead of his own internal expectations. Lars attributed the pace to lessons from prior vehicle launches. The vast majority of units currently produced are being used for FSD training rather than commercial robotaxi service, which Lars confirmed is the primary reason paid service has not yet scaled.
- **Lars Moravy signals production scale ambition of 2 million to 10+ million Cybercabs per year.** He described 2 million units annually as "almost a given" given the efficiency of the Unboxed platform, and said he expects Cybercab to outsell every other Tesla vehicle in company history. The Unboxed platform uses only 20–25 manufacturing cells versus ~200 in a traditional vehicle line, a concept in development since 2014.
- **Cybercab FSD requires dedicated retraining — not a copy-paste from Model Y.** Lars confirmed the model has different weights, needs real-world data, and cannot rely solely on simulation. This is the official explanation for why commercial launch has been slower than expected.
- **Tesla missed its end-of-June five-city robotaxi expansion target**, a commitment made in investor materials and on earnings calls — not an informal Elon prediction. Lars Moravy teased a "cool" announcement around July 7, which Herbert and FutureAzA's Brian interpret as most likely a multi-city robotaxi launch. Nevada regulators are reportedly expected to issue a final permit around that date; Tesla has applied for 5,000 robotaxi permits in Nevada. Cities cited as candidates include Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Miami.
- **Texas DPS added the Cybercab to its connected autonomous vehicles first-responder interaction page** — a formal regulatory prerequisite for Level 4 operations in Texas, giving police, fire, and EMS standardized protocols for driverless Cybercab incidents.
- **Coral Gables, Florida fire personnel completed a Cybercab safety training session with Tesla**, covering battery systems, emergency access points, and autonomous vehicle incident protocols. Tesla has no active Miami-area service; both brighterwithherbert and Sawyer Merritt frame this as a near-term launch signal.
- **Cybercab efficiency: ~165 Wh/mile, yielding 300–400 miles of range**, per FutureAzA. James Stephenson noted the vehicle is more efficient than his personal 2018 Model 3 and estimated its per-unit cost at roughly one-third of Waymo's, with Waymo's sensor suite alone costing more than an entire Cybercab.
- **Cybercab self-drives from end of production line to logistics yard** with no human driver, and autonomously completes the "Full Self-Hearing" NVH test track — an operational live use of FSD within the factory environment.
- **Phil Beisel (cernbasher) predicts Austin unsupervised commercial service launches within two weeks to two months**, estimating the announcement will involve a fleet of approximately 150 cars. He flags non-AI operational complexity — customer service, fleet maintenance, incident response automation — as the binding constraint beyond FSD itself.
- **Cybercab staging depots spotted in Houston and Coral Gables**, per community members. A Cybercab was also seen by cernbasher on Yerba Buena Road in a remote southeast San Jose neighborhood, roughly 20 minutes from Palo Alto.
- **Wireless charging pads are reportedly being installed** at Cybercab operating locations, per FutureAzA, which the hosts said will reveal where Tesla is establishing service hubs.
- **Cybercabs deployed to the robotaxi network will be reported as deliveries to Tesla Robotaxi LLC**, a wholly owned Tesla subsidiary, per James Stephenson — who argues auditors require revenue recognition on internal transfer and that Tesla cannot accumulate produced-but-undeployed Cybercabs indefinitely without reporting them.

## Humanoid Bots
- **First Optimus production subline has arrived at Fremont** (former Model S/X area), per Lars Moravy. Machines are in final field acceptance testing; a single subline takes two to three days to install and about a week to reach operational status. Elon Musk posted a photo of himself walking the line, confirming active installation.
- **40 additional sublines planned** once the first is proven, covering actuators, torso, battery, and limbs. This is a staged rollout, not a sequential 40-week process. A Tesla post (attributed to Tesla Zoa) states these are being validated in Germany.
- **Musk directly rejected the "4D chess" theory** that Optimus delays signal hidden progress, saying plainly: "Optimus production will be extremely slow at first, as everything is new. This is not like making a car." On Q1 2026 earnings he described 2026 output as "literally impossible to predict" and noted the robot has roughly 10,000 unique parts. Fremont production start is targeted for late July or August 2026.
- **Unverified supply chain figures (treat as rumor):** A Nomura research report cited by brighterwithherbert claims Tesla raised Fremont's annualized Optimus Gen 3 capacity target from 50,000 to 70,000 units; plans ~70,000 units of Austin capacity in 2028; targets 1.5 million units/year long-term; estimates ~25,000 units shipped in 2026 (±10,000); and a 1,000-unit/week target by September 2026. Brian White cautioned these figures could be directionally accurate but are frequently not met. A separate UBS note (per Herbert, unconfirmed) places the Gen 3 official launch window in July–August 2026.
- **Tesla's stated 2026 Optimus targets remain 50,000–100,000 units**, with Fremont targeting a 1-million-unit annual run-rate and Giga Texas eventually targeting 10 million units/year — against a track record of every major Optimus timeline having slipped since 2022.
- **Lars Moravy: Optimus could eventually cost less to build than a Tesla car.** He also emphasized that building Optimus is more analogous to building a car than a consumer device, given functional safety requirements and three-dimensional mechanical complexity — and that Tesla's automotive supply chain, multi-sourcing, and production software expertise transfer directly.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD v14 "light" began rolling out to Hardware 3 vehicles by June 30**, per Ashok Elluswamy's prior commitment. A small number of HW3 cars — including some in Canada — received it by that date. Brighterwithherbert's own HW3 vehicle had not yet received it as of filming. Brian White believes v14 light has narrowed the performance gap between HW3 and HW4 that had widened through mid-2026, though neither he nor Phil Beisel expect HW3 to ever achieve unsupervised FSD capability.
- **Hardware 3 has roughly 1/8 the memory bandwidth of Hardware 4; HW4 is 3–5x faster overall for FSD workloads**, per cernbasher's Phil Beisel. He estimates 3–4 million HW3 vehicles exist globally and frames the v14 light effort as partly honoring prior commitments and partly as constraint-driven optimization that benefits the broader FSD stack.
- **Germany's automotive regulator formally declined to approve FSD independently**, stating it will await an EU-wide decision — potentially months away. Lithuania and other smaller European countries have already moved forward. FutureAzA's Brian suggested Germany may be slow-walking approval to protect domestic automakers.
- **"Full Self-Hearing" — Tesla's AI-powered NVH quality system:** Lars Moravy described Cybercabs autonomously driving over a squeak-and-rattle test track post-production, using onboard microphones and ML to detect, classify, and localize NVH issues, then communicating findings directly to factory AI to trace root causes back to the stamping stage. Lars called it his favorite example of AI in manufacturing. Brian White estimated the burn-in track costs approximately $0.30 per car in electricity.
- **Katy, Texas crash involved no FSD.** Per cernbasher, data showed FSD was manually overridden with the accelerator pressed to 100%, reaching 73 mph. NHTSA and NTSB investigations are ongoing; both Elon Musk and Phil Beisel criticized media framing that implied FSD involvement.
- **FSD driver identity verification is in development**, using the interior cabin camera to confirm an authorized driver before allowing FSD activation, per Sawyer Merritt. Implications include subscription misuse prevention and potential accountability infrastructure for unsupervised deployment.
- **Tesla Insurance filed for a new program in Washington state**, with a proposed start date of September 1, 2026, featuring a distinct "Safety Score with FSD (Supervised)" variant — differentiating pricing based on FSD engagement, per Sawyer Merritt.

## Energy
- **Q2 2026: 13.5 GWh of energy storage deployed** — up 40.6% year-over-year and 53.4% sequentially from Q1's 8.8 GWh, slightly below analyst consensus of ~13.8 GWh. Lumpiness attributed to large projects that cannot be revenue-recognized until switched on.
- **Major European Megapack deal: 25 GWh Phase 1, targeting 100+ GWh total.** For context, Tesla sold nearly 47 GWh of Megapack globally in all of 2025. The deal includes Autobidder trading software with long-term revenue warranties — a notable re-emergence of the AI-powered grid arbitrage product, per cernbasher.
- **NAT Power deal covering UK and Italy** represents roughly half a year's production from one Megapack factory, per FutureAzA. A handful of similar deals could require two to three additional Megapack factories globally.
- **New Texas Megapack factory coming**, with estimated annual capacity of ~50 GWh versus ~40 GWh at existing facilities, per FutureAzA.
- **Megapack cell chemistry confirmed as LFP** (lithium iron phosphate), per FutureAzA, with most cells currently sourced from China. Tesla confirmed the chemistry but declined to name suppliers.
- **Tesla filed a trademark for "Megapod."** Cernbasher theorizes this refers to Megapack-backed AI inference compute clusters at Tesla Supercharger sites (~7 GWh of provisioned capacity across the network), using cheap off-peak power buffered by Megapacks to run inference workloads potentially leased to third-party AI consumers.
- **Powerwall lease program launched in Massachusetts and Connecticut**, tied to virtual power plant participation.
- **Tesla announced a VPP partnership with Sunrun and Renew Home** targeting more than 16 GW of flexible distributed capacity aggregated from home batteries, smart thermostats, and other devices — described by cernbasher as the largest distributed VPP in the US, aimed at hyperscalers, data centers, and utilities.

## Market & Competition
- **BYD delivered 557,090 fully electric vehicles in Q2 2026, down ~8% YoY**, while Tesla rose 25% — narrowing the BEV delivery gap from over 220,000 units a year ago to roughly 77,000 units, per Electrek.
- **GM Q2 2026 EV sales fell 41% YoY to 27,395 units**, with the Equinox EV down 62%, Blazer EV down 68%, Hummer EV down 57%, and Silverado EV down 26%, per Sawyer Merritt.
- **Tesla hired Gary Jang — ~18 years at Intel — as Director of Terafab**, the planned chip fabrication facility in Austin, per brighterwithherbert. Tesla (not SpaceX) made the hire. Cernbasher notes Tesla reportedly acquired ~2,700 acres east of Austin as the likely Terafab site, comparable in size to current Giga Texas.
- **Tesla stock surged ~8% on June 29, 2026** — one of its largest single-day gains in over a year — before giving back some gains in pre-market trading following the Q2 delivery release, which the FutureAzA hosts attributed to the move having been partially priced in.
- **Tesla–SpaceX merger logic discussed at length by cernbasher**, citing deepening cross-company engineering collaboration, the impracticality of related-party transactions at scale (e.g., SpaceX purchasing millions of Optimus units), Elon's early option exercise ahead of an August deadline, and the fact that Tesla — not SpaceX — made the Terafab director hire. Hosts stopped short of calling a merger certain.
- **Volkswagen reportedly switched autonomous driving technology partners** while simultaneously claiming it will have autonomy solved within a year — a timeline FutureAzA's Brian characterized as evidence of a fundamental underestimation of the problem's scope.
