This episode of Electrified covers Tesla's robotaxi fleet dynamics in Austin versus Waymo, regulatory and technical developments in Full Self-Driving, Optimus's video-game training progress, and several SpaceX updates including a requested launch cadence increase at Starbase, plans to attempt a double catch on Starship flight 15, and a bullish Morgan Stanley note on SpaceX's compute thesis. The host frames the weekend's Cybercab-vs-Waymo camera data as a temporary, context-limited "flip" rather than a durable trend, while cautioning viewers against premature robotaxi unit-economics extrapolations.

## Robotaxi
1. **Austin camera data shows a brief Cybercab-Waymo "flip," not a durable trend:** Over a weekend X takeover event, Austin traffic camera snapshots (per a third-party tool built by a Tesla summer intern, Ethan McConna) showed Cybercab sightings exceeding Waymo's during the 2–5pm peak window both days, with Cybercab hitting 21.6 per 10,000 snapshots versus Waymo's 18.3. The host stresses this was a limited weekend anomaly; looking at the prior 24 hours on a normal day, Waymo was at roughly 3 sightings per 10,000 vehicles versus Cybercab at about 1.57.
2. **Cybercab fleet scaling and feature updates:** Tesla stated one month after launch it had scaled to 300+ Cybercabs serving Austin, an 8x increase in 30 days (319 total registered). Ashok Elluswamy said Cybercabs are "everywhere" in Austin now, with Dallas launch expected soon after first-responder training there already occurred. Updates mentioned at the X takeover: reduced wait times from increased supply and orchestration algorithms, Grok bot now available in-vehicle (persists across rides once signed in, auto-logs out on exit), windows can only be opened about an inch and a half, turn signal sound restored, new seats and on-demand rear/repeater camera access in development per Tesla staff.
3. **Robotaxi unit economics remain speculative:** The host pushes back on a viral Grok-generated estimate (implying ~$1.25/mile net margin) as unrealistic, noting Tesla's long-term 20-cents-per-mile operating cost goal was paired by Elon with an estimated ~40-cents-per-mile customer price, implying a long-term net margin closer to 20 cents/mile, not $1.25. ARK Invest projects sub-20-cents-per-mile cost by 2030 (Elon called this plausible), but the host estimates Tesla's current cost is likely 70–80 cents/mile. Goldman Sachs said Cybercab could run 5–30 cents/mile cheaper than rival robotaxis at scale; Morgan Stanley's most recent estimate put Tesla's robotaxi cost around 81 cents/mile versus about $140/mile for Waymo's current system.
4. **24/7 operations and V15 rollout timing:** Ashok Elluswamy said (on September 4th) that moving to 24/7 robotaxi operations should happen roughly the following month, enabled by the upcoming V15 tech stack. V15 is already partially live but only about 40% of its new features have been deployed so far.
5. **Las Vegas launch deadline approaching:** Tesla has 120 days to launch robotaxi service in Las Vegas after receiving its permit, putting the deadline around December 18th — meaning a launch must occur within roughly the next 65 days.
6. **Texas vehicle registrations and NHTSA scrutiny:** Waymo has about 1,185 vehicles registered in Texas versus Tesla's 739. Separately, NHTSA is reportedly examining whether Tesla robotaxis have exceeded speed limits in school zones, following a petition citing video evidence; the preliminary probe covers an estimated 1,000 vehicles, and the agency has not yet decided whether to open a full investigation.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Dynamic headlight leveling spotted in testing:** Tesla is quietly testing an unannounced feature, dynamic headlight leveling, seen on some Model 3 and Cybertruck vehicles. It automatically adjusts low-beam aim based on pitch changes (e.g., hills, hard acceleration) to reduce glare, distinct from the existing matrix-LED adaptive high beams. The host describes it as early-stage and still in a data-gathering phase.
2. **Supervisor takeovers reported in Bay Area rides:** A commenter said that during Bay Area robotaxi rides over the past month or two, supervisors have been taking over manually for pickups and drop-offs — something they hadn't seen before. The speculation (unconfirmed) is that this relates to parking-logic data collection for V15.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Digital Optimus progress in gaming:** Elon posted that Digital Optimus can now play about halfway through the Diablo campaign purely from screen input, is skilled at Counter-Strike and other fast-paced games, and is in training for League of Legends. The stated goal is generalizing across games as a high-bandwidth evaluation environment for future general computer-use agents, with skills expected to transfer to browsers, spreadsheets, and enterprise software.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Flight 15 double-catch attempt planned:** Elon said at the X takeover that SpaceX now plans to attempt catching both the ship and booster on flight 15, reversing the host's earlier expectation (post-flight 14) that a catch attempt was unlikely. Elon characterized the prior engine issue as a minor, easily fixable software glitch, said the heat shield looked robust, and estimated flight 15 could occur a bit before Thanksgiving after a four-to-six-week turnaround. He also said he'd like flight 16 to reuse both the ship and booster entirely.
2. **Ship 40 now vertical at Starbase:** SpaceX announced Ship 40 is vertical alongside three other Starships in a mega bay, intended to provide first-of-its-kind data on a flown Starship's heat shield, Raptor engines, and avionics.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **SpaceX requests major launch cadence increase at Starbase:** The FAA and DOT opened a public comment period (through November 30) on SpaceX's request to raise annual launch/landing limits at Boca Chica, Starbase, Texas. Current approval is 25 annual launches and 50 annual landings (25 Starship, 25 Super Heavy). The new request seeks up to 456 annual launches and 912 annual landings in a mature "Phase 2," with an interim Phase 1 of up to 100 launches and 200 landings; Phase 1 launches/landings could occur any day/time, while Phase 2 launches would be limited to weekdays.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Morgan Stanley highlights SpaceX's "distributed inference cloud" thesis:** Adam Jonas argued the most underappreciated part of the SpaceX story is its hybrid orbital-terrestrial AI compute network, combining Star Mind satellites with terrestrial data centers to serve latency-sensitive inference for a growing "mobile swarm" of physical agents (robotaxis, Optimus) and software agents, all connected via Starlink. Elon responded calling this "a very big deal."
2. **Why Tesla isn't replicating SpaceX's compute-leasing business:** The host argues Tesla won't pursue SpaceX-style compute leasing because it lacks excess capital (already free-cash-flow negative, with a $30 billion credit facility and planned $25B+ annual capex), because frontier chip supply is too thin to support a side leasing business, and because Tesla has a clearer internal ROI path via FSD, robotaxi, and Optimus; SpaceX's leasing business arose incidentally from overbuilding capacity for Grok training.

## Electric Vehicles / Market & Competition
1. **EV sales rebound in Q3 despite tax credit expiry:** Per Cox Automotive, Q3 US EV sales were the strongest since the federal EV tax credit ended, with EVs at 6.5% of new car sales (up from 5.8% in the prior quarter), a 7.7% quarter-over-quarter increase — the best result in a year.
2. **Tesla's US EV market share rose year-over-year without the tax credit:** Tesla's US EV market share was 41% in Q3 a year earlier versus 52% in the most recent Q3, which the host cites as evidence Tesla did not depend on the tax credit the way legacy automakers did.
3. **New "Tesla Cyber Center" trademark filed:** Tesla filed a trademark covering data storage, energy R&D, data migration services, and an online portal for energy storage/EV charging/solar partners — speculated to be a future branded site pairing power generation with data storage.
