Early January 2026 registration data from five European markets shows Tesla registrations down 44% year-over-year, extending a multi-year decline with no sign of bottoming, according to Electrek.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **January 2026 country-level declines:** Across five reporting European markets, Tesla registrations fell 44% year-over-year overall. Norway (Tesla's second-biggest 2025 European market) collapsed 88%, which the article attributes partly to the end of most EV incentives there on January 1, 2026, and the resulting pull-forward of sales into Q4 2025 — though the author calls the size of the drop surprising even accounting for that. France fell 42% and the Netherlands fell nearly 67%, without the same incentive-expiration explanation. Sweden and Denmark posted year-over-year "gains" versus a depressed January 2025, but Sweden is still down 29% versus January 2024, which the article characterizes as rounding errors against a larger collapse. In the Netherlands, Tesla dropped from the top EV brand to fifth place in January.

2. **Multi-year European decline trend:** Tesla's full-year 2025 European registrations fell 27.8% to about 235,000 units, down from roughly 326,000 in 2024; 2024 itself was down roughly 10% from 2023. The article frames this as three consecutive years of decline, each worse than the last (~10% in 2023→2024, 27.8% in 2024→2025, 43.9% in the January 2026 data point), describing the trend as accelerating rather than flattening.

3. **Cited causes:** The article attributes the decline to several compounding factors: an aging Model Y product (now over four years old in its current form) facing newer rivals from BYD and Volkswagen; brand damage the author links to Elon Musk's political activities, which it says have alienated environmentally-conscious European buyers; growing Chinese competition, particularly from BYD; and reduced or restructured EV incentives in several countries that disproportionately affect Tesla given its higher price points.

4. **More data pending:** Data from the UK, Germany, Italy, Spain and other markets is expected within the following week. Germany, Tesla's former largest European market, saw registrations drop 48% for full-year 2025.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Tesla has gone from near-monopoly status in European EVs five years ago to fighting for scraps against newer, cheaper competitors with less controversial leadership. He contends the incoming "standard" Model Y and Model 3 may help only at the margins because the brand itself has become toxic to many European buyers, a problem he says pricing and engineering cannot fix, and states that based on the data Tesla has not yet hit bottom in Europe.
