The article covers the opening days of a California racism trial against Tesla, reporting that a Tesla in-house attorney's contact with a witness—and Tesla's outside counsel's opening argument—drew scrutiny from the presiding judge.

## Market & Competition
1. **Trial background and stakes:** California's Civil Rights Department (CRD) is suing Tesla in a bench trial (Alameda County Superior Court, Judge Peter Borkon) on behalf of more than 6,000 Black workers at its Fremont factory; damages are uncapped and the trial is expected to run through October. The article notes Musk announced a "hardcore litigation department" reporting directly to him in May 2022, three months after the CRD first sued, and had pledged Tesla would never settle an unjust case—though the article states Tesla has since settled another racism lawsuit and partly settled the Vaughn class action, with about 580 workers still pursuing individual claims.

2. **Witness contact allegation:** The CRD's first witness, former Tesla worker Ryan Edwards, testified that Tesla senior corporate counsel Timothy Kim called him last week, identifying himself only as an attorney "handling the Tesla civil rights case." According to Edwards's testimony, during a roughly 30-minute call Kim steered him toward saying Tesla was a good employer, offered to represent him despite Edwards already having his own lawyer, and asked about settlements—Edwards said he realized Kim was on Tesla's side only when Kim named his former managers.

3. **CRD request to bar contact:** CRD attorney Juan Gamboa told the court the agency has been unable to reach at least four other listed witnesses in recent days and suggested Tesla's lawyers may have reached them first, via payment or persuasion not to testify. Gamboa asked Borkon to bar Tesla's counsel from contacting CRD witnesses; Borkon did not rule immediately and told the CRD to file a formal motion. Tesla has not commented and, per the article, no longer has a PR department to respond.

4. **Defense opening and testimony:** Tesla's trial counsel, Tyree P. Jones Jr. of Polsinelli PC, argued in his opening statement that the N-word is used as "a term of respect and endearment" in the Black community, citing rap lyrics, and said the case should ask "what is there to enjoin?" answering "nothing," citing Tesla's policies, recordkeeping, discipline practices, and job creation in Fremont. Edwards then testified that a non-Black coworker called him the N-word, cursed at him, and threatened to beat him up; he reported it but was later written up over the incident and passed over for jobs despite applying to more than 100 positions. CRD's Brett Watson said evidence will show managers referring to Tesla as "the plantation" and to themselves and Elon Musk as "slave masters," plus swastikas, "KKK," and noose graffiti left on factory walls. A second witness, former regional security manager Ozell Murray, testified that his supervisor Ray Sethna (now Tesla's senior director of global security) told him to warn a Black job candidate that "he'll be called those words."

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert argues the attorney's call to Edwards appears to violate professional conduct norms (contacting a represented party, obscuring which side the lawyer was on) and calls it comparable to witness tampering; he views the "term of endearment" defense as poorly suited to a case centered on racial slurs and slavery references, and frames the episode as an ironic outcome of Musk's "hardcore litigation" push.
