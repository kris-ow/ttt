## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Tesla 2025 Impact Report — Cybercab manufacturing & efficiency details:** Tesla published its 180-page 2025 Annual Impact Report, revealing several new Cybercab specifics: the vehicle will be the "primary vehicle" in Tesla's Robotaxi fleet, eliminating steering wheel/pedals to reduce weight and operating costs; it's built on a "next-generation platform" with a powertrain expected to deliver "at least 6.1 miles per kWh," which Tesla claims is better than anything else on the market; and it uses a new reaction injection molding (RIM) process that cuts paint-shop cycle times from hours to minutes by injecting paint during molding. Tesla also stated Cybercab will emit nearly half the GHG emissions per mile compared to Model 3/Y. New manufacturing-line photos were also shared.

2. **FSD emissions/efficiency data point:** Tesla sampled real-world data from 65 million miles driven in 2025 and found FSD (Supervised) is 5% more efficient (in terms of emissions) than manual driving — a new efficiency data point from the impact report, not previously highlighted.

3. **Sustainability metrics from the impact report:** Tesla recycled over 14,000 metric tons of battery material in 2025 (equivalent to 46,000 long-range battery packs, +20% YoY); Giga Berlin used only ~0.3 million cubic meters of water in 2025 (down from 0.45M in 2024) against a 1.4M permitted allowance; Gigafactory Texas plans a waste-heat recovery system for its data center to reduce paint-shop chiller water needs; Tesla solar customers generated over 2.1x the renewable electricity needed to power all Tesla facilities globally; and Tesla customers avoided ~37 million metric tons of CO2e in 2025 (equivalent to ~90 billion ICE miles).

4. **Tesla Energy — Megapack momentum:** Over the past six weeks, more than $9 billion in new Tesla Megapack projects have been announced, totaling 43+ GWh of storage (claimed capacity to power 5.9 million homes). Separately, Tesla secured a new Megapack order worth up to $3 billion from Esyasoft for 15+ GWh of battery storage across the UK, Western Europe, GCC, and India — commentary included from Mike Snyder, VP Tesla Energy & Charging.

5. **Analyst price target activity — RBC raises TSLA target on SpaceX optionality:** RBC Capital raised its Tesla price target to $500 (from $475), maintaining Outperform, explicitly citing a 25-30% premium tied to a "potential SpaceX acquisition scenario" — a notable read-through for Tesla investors given cross-holding speculation, though this is analyst framing, not confirmed corporate action.

6. **SpaceX developments (tangential, contextual):** Multiple banks (Raymond James, Needham, Morgan Stanley) initiated coverage on SpaceX with bullish targets (Raymond James: Strong Buy, $800 target/$10.5T market cap; Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas: $300 target, $600 bull case/$8T cap, projecting $319B revenue by 2030 and $3.3T by 2040; Needham: Buy, $200 target). Jonas also highlighted Starlink's long-term opportunity connecting AI-enabled robots/AVs/drones and SpaceX's vertical integration in datacenter compute. SpaceX also raised Starlink Business Aviation pricing (~100% increases across some tiers), filed with the FCC for a 100,000-satellite third-gen constellation, and reportedly paused new Starlink signups in several Kenyan cities due to capacity constraints. These are relevant mainly as sentiment/valuation context given cross-company linkage, not direct Tesla fundamentals.
