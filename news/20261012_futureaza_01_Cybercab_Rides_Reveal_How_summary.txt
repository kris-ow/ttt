This is an interview-style video from futureaza featuring a conversation with a Tesla owner/content creator (referred to as "Teslatino") about his recent Cybercab rides and his multi-year ownership experience with a Cybertruck, including early FSD struggles, long-term reliability, and charging tradeoffs. The piece is anecdotal and first-person rather than data-driven, offering a ground-level view of Cybercab polish and Cybertruck durability rather than fleet-wide statistics.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab rides described as flawless:** The interviewee says his two recent Cybercab rides were flawless and compares the driving behavior favorably to his own car's Autopilot/FSD, noting similar organic behaviors like shifting slightly off-center to give pedestrians more space. He says he'd like his personal car to run the same software version as the Cybercab.
2. **Remaining rough edges inside the Cybercab:** He flagged the seat material as feeling like plastic and uncomfortable for him personally due to heat retention, and said control locations (e.g., window switches located under the screen rather than on the door) are not obvious. He also noted the door requires pulling a lever rather than pressing, and pulling too hard can trigger the emergency exit system — something he felt isn't intuitive for all users.
3. **Proximity to a Cybercab distribution/service hub:** The interview was filmed near what the host describes as a Cybercab distribution center within a service area, which he jokingly says is "purely coincidental" for why Cybercabs were visible nearby.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Long-term Cybertruck ownership and reliability:** The owner has put roughly 130,000 miles on his Cybertruck (about 20,000 miles remaining before warranty expiration) and reports only one notable non-warranty repair in the last 30,000 miles — a suspension-related valve block failure (described as a "scuba" valve block controlling suspension inflation/deflation) that cost him $1,000 out of pocket.
2. **Off-road use case and tires:** He bought the Cybertruck specifically for more capable off-road travel compared to his lifted Model Y, installing off-road tires (KO2s) just three days after purchase; those original tires have over 80,000 miles on them and are still holding up, though noisy. He describes the truck as very capable off-road despite its weight, citing over 600 horsepower and strong low-end torque.
3. **Charging speed and range tradeoffs:** He reports energy consumption in the range of roughly 350–450 watt-hours per mile and says he's not personally bothered by charging speed on normal trips, but acknowledges a multi-car cross-country "Diner Dash" trip revealed strain on charging infrastructure — not every stop had enough chargers for 30+ cars traveling together, and demand pricing kicked in. He believes towing users would most benefit from more range/faster charging.
4. **Added exterior accessories will reduce range:** He mentions recently finishing installation of unspecified exterior add-ons that he expects will modestly reduce his vehicle's range.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Early FSD on Cybertruck was rough:** He drove his Cybertruck 32,000 miles without FSD access initially (manually, over about 6 months) before getting an early version of FSD (12th major version), which he describes as "really bad" — citing repeated instances of the car clinging to curbs and one case of noticeably drifting left out of its lane toward the shoulder, leading him to turn the system off multiple times.
2. **FSD improved substantially by versions 13–14:** He says version 13 was when he became genuinely convinced of FSD's trajectory after some standout experiences, and that version 14 "really opened my eyes" as a major leap in quality; his Cybertruck now has roughly 120,000 miles logged on FSD. He says his remaining complaints with the current version (14.x) are mostly about parking-spot destination choices rather than safety issues, and estimates safety-relevant problems occur in well under 1% of drives.
3. **View on FSD safety versus human drivers:** He argues that real safety concerns require extremely low-probability events (he cites a 0.01% threshold) and contrasts this with countries banning FSD over minor issues (e.g., low-speed curb contact), while human drivers he observes daily feel far less safe to him despite being a professionally trained rider himself.
