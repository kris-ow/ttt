This video from brighterwithherbert features host Herbert and guest Jo Bhakdi (economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur at pioneerlands.org) discussing what they argue is Tesla's biggest challenge: not competition, but hostile media narratives and misinformation. The episode covers a viral Autopilot crash story that they contend was falsely framed, Cybercab fleet testing progress, FSD expansion into new countries, Optimus factory construction, and a broader market sentiment discussion around why Tesla stock is underperforming despite what they see as meaningful operational progress.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Texas crash misattributed to Autopilot:** Multiple major media outlets — including Business Insider — reported that a Tesla on Autopilot crashed into a Texas home, killing a woman. According to Ashok Elluswamy (identified in the transcript as Tesla's AI VP), the driver had manually overridden the self-driving system by pressing the accelerator pedal to 100%, reached 73 mph in a residential area, and had the accelerator pressed even after the crash. Elon Musk also responded publicly, stating that FSD drives slowly through neighborhood streets and that a high-speed crash was inconsistent with how FSD operates. Sawyer Merritt noted that Tesla's vehicle logs would clarify whether any system was active, and Chuck Cook called the reporting "blatantly irresponsible." The hosts argue this is part of a pattern — they claim there has not been a single confirmed case where a legal investigation proved FSD caused a crash.

2. **FSD expansion into Europe:** FSD version 14.2.2.6 reportedly shows two new countries — Spain and the UK — appearing in the software, suggesting those markets may be in active testing or approaching regulatory approval. Tesla is hiring Autopilot data collection supervisors in London, Paris, and Barcelona, which the host interprets as a standard pre-launch signal. A source cited in the transcript — described as employees the host spoke with when testing FSD in Spain — reportedly expected approval in Spain to come around June 30, 2026. A fleet of vehicles in Spain reportedly grew from 19 to approximately 30 cars and has driven around 80,000 kilometers since November 2025. The Netherlands and Estonia are also mentioned as recently having come online for FSD.

## Robotaxi

1. **Largest Cybercab pack spotted at Giga Texas:** Joe Tegtmeyer captured video of approximately 10 Cybercabs testing together in formation at Giga Texas on what the host notes was the one-year anniversary of the robotaxi service going live. The test track where this occurred appears to have been newly built, possibly specifically for Cybercab testing. Joe Tegtmeyer described it as the largest group of Cybercabs he had seen on the track at any one time and expressed hope that they would soon be taking paying passengers.

2. **Cybercab deployment to multiple cities accelerating:** Jo Bhakdi notes that Cybercab counts in parking lots in Houston and Dallas have been climbing — he references a figure of approximately 45 units growing to around 55 in one city (per a ground reporter on X, attributed by the host to Houston or Dallas, unconfirmed). The hosts draw a contrast with how Model Y robotaxi testing operated, arguing that deploying dozens of vehicles in multiple new cities is consistent with launch preparation rather than mere testing. Cities mentioned as targets include Austin, Dallas, Houston, Los Angeles, Silicon Valley, Las Vegas, Miami, and Phoenix — though the hosts acknowledge the full seven-city rollout promised in Q1 2026 has not yet materialized as of June 23, 2026.

3. **Four months of no at-fault incidents:** The host states that Tesla's robotaxi safety data shows four months without any at-fault issues, which they argue provides a strong basis for expanded deployment.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus factory construction at Giga Texas progressing:** Joe Tegtmeyer documented the construction of Tesla's dedicated Optimus factory at Giga Texas with time-lapse footage from March through April and into subsequent weeks. As of the time of recording, the steel structure assembly is visible, the structure appears to have four floors, and footings have been completed. The host states the factory is planned for a target capacity of 10 million units per year once fully ramped and that the structure is currently on track for a late 2027 completion.

2. **Optimus supply chain expanding with new supplier deals:** Per a report in Taiwan Economic Daily cited by the host, Tesla has signed deals with Merl Automation and Asia Optical to accelerate mass production of Optimus 3. Merl Automation will supply harmonic reducers and joint modules, and has established a joint venture in Thailand to manufacture harmonic reducers, robot actuators, and other precision parts, taking advantage of existing factory space. Asia Optical will supply spherical and aspherical lens components to serve as the robot's "eyes." These parts are slated to enter mass production in the second half of 2026 and into 2027. The host notes that the scale of Tesla's orders is large enough that suppliers are creating entirely new joint ventures and factories.

3. **Strategic framing of Optimus scale bet:** Jo Bhakdi argues that Elon Musk's key strategic insight with Optimus is the same playbook used with compute (xAI's Colossus): rather than guaranteeing the best underlying technology, Tesla is making a decisive bet on massive manufacturing scale. The logic, per Bhakdi, is that if Tesla builds the world's largest humanoid robot factory, any competitor with superior technology will ultimately need to partner with or be acquired by Tesla. He draws a parallel to how Google and Anthropic reportedly became customers of xAI's Colossus compute infrastructure despite potentially having superior AI models.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla stock down ~5% on day of recording; inverse relationship with SpaceX suspected:** As of recording on June 23, 2026, Tesla was down approximately 5.18% while SpaceX was up approximately 5.16% — nearly mirror images. Jo Bhakdi notes that the prior day showed the reverse: SpaceX down approximately 16% while Tesla was up approximately 1.5% against the broader market. He speculates this reflects a mechanical cash-flow relationship between the two stocks, possibly related to a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger thesis (with a dedicated episode on merger pricing teased for the following day). He explicitly attributes less than half a percent of the day's Tesla decline to the Autopilot crash headlines.

2. **Investor rotation into "shiny object" AI and semiconductor stocks:** Jo Bhakdi argues Tesla is experiencing selling pressure from investors rotating into high-momentum names like Micron Technologies (noted as up sharply in recent months and down approximately 11% on the day of recording) and SpaceX. He warns that chasing high-momentum stocks is a common beginner mistake and that Tesla's upside could be hyperbolic once robotaxi or Optimus scaling becomes tangible — speculating that meaningful robotaxi progress could push the stock not to $600–$700 but potentially to $1,000 and beyond in the current market sentiment environment, given how quickly SpaceX moved in a short period. He compares SpaceX's recent run to a $3 trillion valuation as an example of how quickly sentiment can shift.
