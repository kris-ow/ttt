Electrek reports that a week after Elon Musk and Tesla announced "unsupervised" Austin Robotaxi rides (no in-car safety monitor), no riders have been able to confirm actually getting one, and the article argues the announcement was timed to boost the stock ahead of a winter storm and Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings.

## Robotaxi
1. **Zero confirmed unsupervised rides despite 42 attempts:** Tesla enthusiast David Moss traveled to Austin specifically seeking an unsupervised ride; across 42 Robotaxi rides, all 42 had a safety monitor present, and he said it was his fifth consecutive ride with the supervisor in the driver's seat. He also noted difficulty getting rides on the app while observing several cars mapping the area. Other riders reportedly described similar experiences.

2. **Musk's announcement and Elluswamy's caveat:** On January 22, Musk said Tesla had started Robotaxi rides in Austin with no safety monitor in the car, and the stock rose 4% following the announcement. Tesla VP of Software Ashok Elluswamy acknowledged the same day that Tesla was starting with only "a few unsupervised vehicles mixed in with the broader Robotaxi fleet with Safety Monitors," saying the ratio would increase over time; neither Musk nor Elluswamy mentioned trailing chase vehicles with safety monitors that Electrek had previously reported were following the supposedly unsupervised cars.

3. **Storm disruption and fleet details:** A major ice storm hit Austin starting January 24, reducing Robotaxi operations for about two days and making independent verification harder. The article notes that, prior to the pause, Tesla's Austin Robotaxi fleet reportedly consisted of only a handful of operating vehicles, and that reported "new" Robotaxis (tracked via license plates) appear mostly attributable to Tesla swapping in new Model Ys with upgraded hardware rather than fleet growth. Since the service resumed, the vast majority of rides reportedly have safety monitors back in either the driver's or passenger seat.

4. **Comparison to June 2025 "driverless delivery":** The article recalls Tesla's June 2025 claim of a "first fully autonomous delivery," a Model Y that Musk said drove 15 miles from Giga Texas to a customer's home in Austin; the article states Tesla never repeated this driverless delivery, and seven months later customers still pick up cars normally.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the January 22 announcement was a one-time "marketing stunt" rather than evidence of real unsupervised capability, asserting Tesla timed it before the storm, before Q4 earnings, and after Musk's Davos remarks that Tesla had "solved autonomy," following what the author calls a repeated pattern of bold claims followed by quiet walk-backs. The article also highlights a reader comment arguing Tesla's credibility on autonomy claims would be better demonstrated by applying for a California DMV permit to test without a safety driver, noting six other companies hold that permit.
