This Tesla Beat Podcast episode (hosts futureaza/John, Brian, Joe, with guest Randy Kirk) breaks down Tesla's and SpaceX's latest earnings calls, Tesla's robotaxi/FSD expansion, Optimus and energy updates, and extended discussion of a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger following Elon Musk's comments on SpaceX's October 20 earnings call. The hosts argue both earnings were strong despite "doomer" narratives, with Tesla's regulatory-credit falloff failing to dent profitability and SpaceX posting triple-digit revenue growth.

## Financials
1. **Tesla Q3 results beat doom narratives:** Revenue was $28.05B (flat vs. $28.1B a year ago, despite last year's tax-credit pull-forward), non-GAAP EPS $0.48 (vs. $0.45 consensus), GAAP EPS $0.22, non-GAAP net income $1.7B, operating cash flow $6.3B, free cash flow $1.5B, and cash plus investments at a record $45.4B (up $1.9B). GAAP gross margin was 19.6%, with automotive revenue of $20.56B and energy revenue of $2.8B. Capex rose 110% year-over-year to $4.7B. Hosts stressed this came even as regulatory credits largely disappeared, countering claims the company would collapse without them.
2. **SpaceX Q3 revenue up sharply, profitable:** Revenue grew 161% year-over-year (vs. ~$12.7B consensus, per the host), with net income of $4.4B. Segment breakdown: AI/connectivity-related compute ~$7.5B, Starlink/connectivity $5.25B, and space/launch $1.77B — the host noted launch-related revenue is now a small fraction of total SpaceX revenue. Operating income: connectivity $2.3B, AI $431M (including $1.2B of Cursor-related stock compensation). CFO Brett Johnson reportedly guided Q1 revenue to $33–37B, Q4 capex to $35–40B, and reiterated a ~$100B ARR target by the end of 2026.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab/robotaxi fleet scaling fast:** The Cybercab fleet in Austin has grown roughly tenfold since launch, with host estimates putting combined Cybercab and Model Y robotaxi count in Austin around 500 (uncertain, self-described estimate). Tesla's robotaxi safety report (released October 21, with a live mile counter) showed zero injuries, zero airbag deployments, and zero pedestrian/cyclist incidents as of October 18. Unsupervised miles by city: Austin 2.1 million, Dallas 600,000, Houston 225,000, with other cities around 50–60K each; fleet-wide the service now averages 80,000 unsupervised miles per day. Cumulative unsupervised robotaxi miles reportedly grew 8x quarter-over-quarter, approaching 2 million by end of Q3.
2. **Expansion plans:** Tesla said Cybercab service is launching soon in Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio, and expanding to Dallas next month. California was called out by the hosts as notably absent from unsupervised expansion, attributed to political/regulatory friction (including tension between Elon Musk and Governor Gavin Newsom); one host speculated Elon Musk's EU and California timelines may hinge partly on political dynamics, including an upcoming California governor's race.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate jumps:** FSD attach rate for North American deliveries hit 75%, up 20 percentage points in a single quarter. Active FSD subscriptions reached 1.7 million, up 67% year-over-year.
2. **V15 and international rollout:** Next-gen FSD software (V15) is reportedly coming to customers; Elon Musk is said to expect EU approval within roughly a quarter, though hosts expect delays given EU regulatory complexity.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production framing:** Elon Musk reportedly described early Optimus production on the high-volume Fremont line as requiring "10,000 things to go right," with reaching 20,000 units/week taking at least a year or more. The AI5 chip, described as an order-of-magnitude compute increase over prior chips, is currently earmarked for Optimus. Giga Texas's Optimus facility is under continuous (reported 24/7) construction per hosts who visited the site.
2. **Optimus seen as larger long-term driver than robotaxi:** Guest Randy Kirk argued that nearly all of Tesla's stock movement historically has been tied to robotaxi expectations, not Optimus, but that Optimus could eventually generate several times robotaxi's revenue/profit — citing Elon Musk's comments (on the earnings call) about pursuing industrial and 24/7 commercial customers willing to pay the most, effectively an auction-like pricing model.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Production capacity increases:** Tesla raised its headline Austin production target from 250,000 to 375,000 Model Y units, and is adding capacity in Berlin. One host said Giga Berlin is reportedly running at most two shifts (not yet 24/7), with newly hired staff used to speed up the existing line rather than add a shift; the Berlin factory is reportedly at full floor-space utilization and would need to expand northward to grow further.
2. **Competitor contrast:** Hosts contrasted Tesla's production scale with Rivian ("barely getting over a hump") and Lucid (praised product, limited volume), noting both have sold far fewer vehicles cumulatively than Tesla produces in a matter of days.

## Energy
1. **Megapack and backlog growth:** The Megapack factory in Texas began production, targeting roughly 50 GWh of Megapacks using Nevada-made LFP cells; Tesla is reportedly almost sold out of 2027 capacity. Energy order rate and backlog were both described as record highs.

## Market & Competition
1. **California regulatory gap:** Hosts criticized California for lagging other states/regions in approving Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi operations, while Waymo already operates there; one host noted uncertainty over whether Tesla has even formally applied for the relevant permit, citing a parallel UK situation where FSD approval was stalled partly because Tesla reportedly hadn't applied yet.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink subscriber and spectrum growth:** Starlink subscribers reached 13.6 million, up 1.6 million quarter-over-quarter. A 800MHz spectrum deal for "Starlink Mobile" was announced, with hosts framing direct-to-cell connectivity (potentially via roughly 80,000 towers, per discussion) as a major new growth vector. A viewer-sourced, Grok-cited figure of around 168,000 maritime Starlink customers was mentioned, with per-terminal revenue for large ships (cruise ships etc.) speculated to run near $1,000/month.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute business scaling rapidly:** Hosts highlighted SpaceX's compute/AI business (Colossus, Grok, Cursor integration) as a major surprise growth driver, with guest Randy Kirk suggesting combined Grok/Cursor enterprise sales efforts (reportedly newly announced, targeting major banks and institutions) could generate on the order of $100–150 billion a year, and floating a "$300–500 billion a year run rate by the end of next year" and potentially a trillion dollars annually per Elon Musk's ambitions — all framed as speculative, forward-looking estimates by the guest rather than confirmed guidance.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
1. **Elon Musk addresses Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation:** On SpaceX's October 20 earnings call, Elon Musk said both Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees at shareholder request because he is a conflicted party, and said he is excluded from those discussions. He stated that while he thinks a merger "would be a great idea," shareholders must support it, and that "assuming a merger is a certainty would be overstating it," calling the process "an odd kabuki dance." He noted Tesla would need approval from 50% of all shareholders, not just a majority of votes cast. Bloomberg was cited as saying such a deal would be the largest corporate tie-up ever.
2. **Host and guest views on merger terms:** Guest Randy Kirk said he believes a merger is highly likely and that Tesla shareholders may receive a premium (he floated a range of roughly 35–50%, his own estimate) because Optimus and robotaxi value is not yet fully priced into Tesla stock, while SpaceX's five-year value is comparably mature; he attributed a similar "fairness" argument to a commentator he could not identify with certainty (possibly Dillon Loomis). One host countered that a merger isn't necessarily inevitable, and that investor enthusiasm shouldn't substitute for actual execution.
