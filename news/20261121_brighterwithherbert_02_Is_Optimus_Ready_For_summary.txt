The video examines Elon Musk's statement at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin that most humanoid-robot demos are staged and programmed, and that Tesla will give a real, unscripted Optimus demo in "a month or two." The host argues this sets a public pass/fail bar for Tesla, notes that Tesla's own track record of remote-controlled demos and shifting Optimus timelines invites skepticism, and says the factory buildout matters more to investors than any single demo.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Musk's "apple test" and the demo promise:** Speaking by video link from Shenzhen, Musk said the humanoid demos people see are staged and programmed, not real, and that no robot can take arbitrary tasks on objects it has never seen. His test: ask a robot to pick up an apple, then move the table or the apple, and a scripted robot fails. He said Tesla will hold a real demo "maybe in, I don't know, a month or two." The host notes Musk said nearly the same on the October 21 earnings call, so the window has not moved, and he treats "a month or two" as a guideline only. He expects the real demo to come later on Tesla's own channels rather than at a December event.

2. **Household-chore timeline and first use case:** Musk said Optimus should be able to do chores like taking out the trash and washing dishes in about 12–18 months, and that no humanoid can do the full dish task today. He said the first big job is heavy industry such as mining and ore processing, with home use later, when he expects almost everyone will want one or two.

3. **Tesla's own demo history:** The host notes that Optimus units at the October 2024 We, Robot event were reported by Bloomberg days later to be remotely controlled, and that about 10 days ago an Optimus handing out snack bags at the Coral robotics conference in Austin was teleoperated. A Tesla engineer at the booth reportedly said cool autonomous demos would come soon. The host adds that teleoperation to collect training data is legitimate; the problem is demos that don't disclose it. Musk's Optimus V3 was also supposed to appear in the first quarter and has not been shown.

4. **Figure CEO's critique:** On Peter Diamandis' Moonshots podcast, Figure CEO Brett Adcock said he hasn't seen anything special from Tesla in two or three years, only remote-controlled robots. He did not specify which robot, and the comment predates Musk's remarks. The host discloses that a fund tied to his other channel invests in Figure and Apptronik. Adcock also said China is racing mainly to show gymnastics, and described Unitree robots as having no arms.

5. **Evidence on rivals' demos:** The host agrees Musk is largely right about choreographed and remote-operated shows. Adcock described how dance demos rely on motion-capture suits, and a Pantera Capital team in Shenzhen reported that demos were pre-programmed, that researchers said off the record that Chinese AI relies on distillation, and that China's hardware advantage is greater than expected. 1X's Neo has an "expert mode" with remote human control, and the host found no confirmed Neo delivery to a buyer by mid-November. The host disputes "everyone": NVIDIA's Jim Fan posted unedited, real-speed Groot footage of a chemistry-lab task, and Google DeepMind's demo with Apptronik's Apollo at Coral took live requests from passersby in a semi-automated setup (results not reported). Adcock says Figure has more humanoids than humans on its campus (over 1,000 by his estimate on the podcast) and hopes for hours of household chores in any home within 6 months, without a guarantee; Helix 3 results have not been released.

6. **Harder tests and hand engineering:** Building on a John Carmack post that no robot can change jack-o'-lantern batteries, Musk replied that these are real standards and added a robot that assembles a smartwatch with one hand while catching a 100 mph baseball with the other. Musk described an internal Optimus test of opening a toy-car box, unscrewing the battery cover, inserting batteries, refastening it and driving the car. The host notes the hand and forearm actuators are the hardest engineering and says delicate hand work on unseen objects is what he will watch most.

7. **Where to see Optimus soon:** Humanoid Summit (December 1–2, San Mateo) says it is partnering with Tesla so attendees can see Optimus and take guided Full Self-Driving demo rides. The host calls a live demo there possible but unconfirmed, since the organizer did not mention it. Competitors on the speaker list include Apptronik, Google DeepMind, 1X, Unitree and Agibot. Tesla's annual meeting follows on December 3.

8. **Skeptic case:** The host lays out the bear argument: Musk calls others' demos fake because he has nothing to show yet; even an authentic demo is a video Tesla selected; and Musk's own estimate for external deliveries is closer to the end of 2027. On the October call he also said he wanted to lower expectations because a lot remains to overcome, taking about a year.

## Financials
1. **Factory buildout ahead of the demo:** The host cites Musk's October remarks that most of the line for 1 million Optimus per year is already installed in Fremont, and that a roughly 7 million square foot Optimus factory is being built in Austin (the host equates this to 91 football fields, larger than the Fremont plant), per aerial photography from Joe Tegtmeyer. Musk has said Optimus will cost $20,000–$30,000 and be leased rather than sold, creating recurring revenue. Tesla has given no 2026 or 2027 production target, and Optimus is still being tested in Tesla factories. The host's view is that until a real demo, investors are taking Musk's word on the factory.
