A video posted to Reddit shows a driverless Tesla Robotaxi in Austin stopping repeatedly in front of a row of plastic bollards closing off a curb lane, then pushing through them and continuing on — an incident the article contrasts with Tesla's recent claims of an "impeccable" safety record.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Bollard incident details:** A Tesla Model Y Robotaxi with no safety monitor onboard turned into a closed-off triangular curb pocket at an Austin intersection sealed with flexible plastic posts (in place since at least February 2024, per Google Street View). The car stopped, crept forward and backed up multiple times, sat still, then drove through the posts with an audible impact; no one was hurt. The passenger who filmed it, Reddit user bladerskb, said "it hit em, it hit em."
2. **Author's interpretation of the failure:** The article argues the car clearly detected the bollards (it stopped for them repeatedly), so the failure was not sensing but decision-making — it had committed to what it read as a right-turn lane and, unable to work out how to reverse out of the mistake, chose to drive through the obstacle instead. This is framed as distinct from lidar-related sensor debates, since perception appeared to work.
3. **Possible remote assistance involvement:** Tesla runs remote assistance for situations like this, but the article says there is no way to tell from the video whether a remote operator was involved. It notes that in July, a Tesla remote operator reportedly crashed a Robotaxi into a tree stump in Houston while trying to extract it from a dead-end street.
4. **Elluswamy's "impeccable" safety claim:** On Tesla's Q2 2026 earnings call (July 22), VP of AI Ashok Elluswamy told investors Tesla has driven more than 380,000 unsupervised Robotaxi miles across six cities in two states with "zero notable incidents," calling the record "impeccable."
5. **Scale comparison and fleet size:** The article notes Waymo has logged over 220 million rider-only miles and runs roughly 500,000 paid trips a week, making Tesla's 380,000 cumulative unsupervised miles under 0.2% of Waymo's total. It also cites a prior Electrek report that only 14 Tesla vehicles were operating unsupervised across all markets in June, down from a peak of 25.
6. **NHTSA reporting and Cybercab timeline:** Tesla has filed 22 automated driving system incident reports with NHTSA to date; the article says whether this bollard incident gets added is "Tesla's call to make." Separately, Tesla is targeting the end of August 2026 to put the Cybercab — a two-seat, steering-wheel-and-pedal-free vehicle running the same software — on Austin streets.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert argues the incident, while minor in itself (plastic posts, no injuries), exposes a decision-making flaw rather than a sensing gap, and that the same failure against concrete bollards, a festival barricade, or a work zone with people present could be dangerous. He contends Tesla's 380,000 unsupervised miles across seven markets since January represent a shrinking pilot rather than a scaling program, noting the fleet is smaller now than when safety monitors were removed in Austin in January.
