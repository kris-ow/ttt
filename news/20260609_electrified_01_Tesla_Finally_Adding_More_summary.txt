This episode of Electrified, hosted by Dillon Loomis, covers several Tesla and SpaceX developments including growth in Tesla's Texas robotaxi fleet registrations, FSD safety data from the Netherlands and new country approvals, the EU regulatory timeline for supervised FSD, and a deep numerical analysis of SpaceX's newly revealed AI1 orbital compute satellite. The video's core argument is that while Tesla's robotaxi expansion is progressing (with more VINs registered and Cybercabs spotted in multiple states), several timelines — including the Q1 slide deck city launch targets and EU-wide FSD approval — are under significant pressure. On SpaceX, Dillon runs the math to argue that Elon Musk's target of 1 gigawatt of annualized orbital AI compute by end of 2027 is highly ambitious given current Starship launch cadence.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas fleet registrations growing rapidly:** Tesla now has 59 VINs listed on the Texas Motor Carrier website, up from just over 40 approximately two weeks ago. Dillon clarifies this does not mean all 59 vehicles are currently active in the fleet or that any are unsupervised — rather, it represents the maximum Tesla could theoretically deploy in Texas right now. All 59 are currently Model Ys.

2. **Cybercabs accumulating in Houston and Dallas lots:** A flyover of the Houston lot shared by SC Robinson showed 21 Cybercabs visible, with numerous Model Ys also appearing to be staged for deployment. A separate video of the Dallas lot showed dozens of Cybercabs lined up. Dillon cautions that until a Cybercab without a steering wheel and without pedals is spotted on-site, expectations for near-term Cybercab deployment should remain low.

3. **Cybercab testing now spanning ~20 US states:** The Cybercab has been spotted testing in approximately 20 different US states, indicating broad geographic preparation even ahead of commercial launch.

4. **Arizona first responder filing reveals broad operating domain:** A Tesla first responder interaction plan for Arizona, dated April 24th of this year, lists the robotaxi service's design domain as operating at SAE Level 4 autonomy across Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Maricopa County, Mesa, Paradise Valley, Phoenix, Scottsdale, and Tempe — all within Maricopa County, which has a population of approximately 4.8 million people. The robotaxi tracker lists Phoenix as the only officially called-out Arizona city, so Dillon expects the first Arizona launch there with expansion to follow.

5. **Q1 city launch timeline in serious jeopardy:** Tesla's Q1 slide deck indicated launches in five different cities within a specific window. With only 20 days remaining on that timeline as of the publish date, Dillon acknowledges that most observers would agree the target is not looking achievable.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **Netherlands FSD safety data shows strong performance:** Over the last two months, Tesla reported supervised FSD has been over three times safer than manual driving on Dutch roads, with 3.5 times fewer collisions. Additional metrics include nearly 15 times fewer automatic emergency braking events, 8.8 times less harsh acceleration, 7.3 times less harsh braking, and 8 times fewer hard swerves. On highways specifically, FSD achieved 3.4 times safer performance across 16.6 million km with zero collisions. On non-highway roads, it is 1.6 times safer across 7 million km with three collisions. Combined, this implies FSD has now covered 23.6 million km in the Netherlands from April 10th to June 5th, 2026.

2. **Denmark approves supervised FSD:** Elon Musk announced that supervised FSD has been officially approved in Denmark, with rollout to begin soon. This brings the total to four countries with approval: the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, and Denmark. Across these four countries, approximately 215,000 Teslas are active on the roads (a cumulative figure, with the majority in the Netherlands and Denmark), though hardware filtering for older AI4 and Hardware 3 vehicles would reduce the addressable pool.

3. **EU-wide vote unlikely before October 2026, rollout possibly delayed to early 2027:** The agenda for the June 30th Technical Committee of Motor Vehicles (TCMV) meeting shows only a continuation of discussions on the Netherlands' Act 39 authorization request — no vote is listed. Dillon assesses the probability of a surprise vote as near zero, since a formal draft act would normally be circulated to member countries at least two weeks in advance. The next window for a possible EU-wide vote would be October 2026, meaning actual software rollout across the EU could slip into early 2027.

4. **Risk of provisional approvals lapsing if EU formally rejects FSD:** Per a Danish outlet, the current approvals in the Netherlands and Denmark are provisional and only apply to EU countries that choose to recognize the Dutch approval. If the EU Commission ultimately rejects the system, the Dutch provisional approval would become invalid after six months, causing the Danish approval to lapse as well, and supervised FSD could no longer be marketed or sold in EU countries. Dillon notes that with each additional country adopting the provisional approval, a formal rejection becomes increasingly disruptive.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model 3 RWD tops Edmunds real-world efficiency test:** Edmunds named Tesla's Model 3 rear-wheel drive the efficiency champion in their real-world range test. The tested vehicle achieved 393 miles on a full charge versus its EPA estimate of 363 miles — 30 miles further, or 8.3% beyond the official figure. On energy consumption, the car returned 21.7 kWh per 100 miles (4.61 miles per kWh), versus the EPA rating of 25 kWh per 100 miles (4 miles per kWh), making it 13.2% more efficient than the EPA estimate and the most efficient EV currently in production tested by Edmunds.

2. **Tesla durability study ranks competitively among luxury brands:** An iSeeCars study covering 174 million vehicles found Tesla ranks sixth overall and third among luxury brands for the likelihood of reaching 250,000 miles, with a 4.6% probability. This places Tesla above Cadillac, Mazda, Subaru, Volvo, Mercedes, BMW, and Porsche. Toyota led overall at 17.8%, with Lexus second at 12.8%. The luxury segment average was 3.2%, and the overall industry average was 4.8%.

3. **Tesla Semi commercial groundwork in Europe:** Tesla posted a job listing for a Business Development Manager for the Tesla Semi, based in Parsdorf near Munich, Germany — a B2B sales role focused on lining up fleet customers and expanding Semi's customer base in the region. Dillon does not expect the Tesla Semi to be deployed or sold in Europe until 2027 at the earliest.

## Market & Competition

1. **US Trade Representative visiting Tesla Fremont factory:** Jameson Greer, the US Trade Representative, is scheduled to tour Tesla's Fremont factory as part of a manufacturing tour promoting the Trump administration's reindustrialization agenda. Dillon notes that in March of last year (2025), Tesla had submitted a formal letter to Greer warning about retaliatory tariff exposure for US exporters and flagging that certain lithium battery components remain difficult or impossible to source entirely within the US despite onshoring efforts. Tesla is expected to continue making its case during the visit.

2. **SpaceX AI1 orbital satellite — numbers vs. Colossus:** Dillon runs detailed math comparing SpaceX's newly revealed AI1 satellite to ground-based compute. The AI1 satellite spans 70 meters across and 20 meters tall, with a peak power of 150 kW (0.15 MW) and sustained power of 120 kW (0.12 MW), and a power density of 70 kW per ton, implying each satellite weighs approximately 2 tons (~4,400 lbs, roughly the weight of a Model Y). SpaceX is targeting 1 gigawatt of annualized orbital AI compute by end of 2027. Using Colossus 1 at 500 MW as a benchmark, it would require between 3,333 satellites (at peak power) and 4,000 satellites (at sustained power) to match it. With Starship V3 capable of carrying ~100 tons per launch (roughly 50 satellites at 2 tons each), matching a 500 MW data center would require 67–83 Starship launches. Reaching the 1 GW annualized target would require approximately 150 Starship launches, or more than 12 per month by December 2027 — a cadence Dillon views as highly optimistic given Starship has flown only 12 times in three years and has not yet reached orbit.
