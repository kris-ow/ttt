Tesla will operate public Megacharging at three new Forum Mobility electric truck depots in California, marking a shift toward Tesla-operated chargers on partner-owned real estate rather than Tesla-owned stations.

## Electric Vehicles / Energy
1. **New depots and locations:** Tesla will run public Megacharging at FM Francis (Ontario), FM Adeline (Oakland), and FM Coliseum (Oakland). A fourth depot, FM Santa Fe in Rancho Dominguez, has contracted capacity for over 330 Tesla Semis but Forum says it remains open to the broader drayage community. All four use pull-through lanes and combine Megawatt Charging System (MCS) with CCS ports; Forum states the MCS chargers add up to 60% of range in about 30 minutes. Forum also added two Tesla MCS Megachargers to its existing Port of Long Beach depot (FM Harbor), which has run since December 2024 with 44 CCS ports and 9 MW of capacity.

2. **Business model — Tesla operates, Forum owns:** Tesla makes the Semi and the Megacharger hardware and now operates charging at Forum's depots, collecting revenue without owning the real estate. According to the article, Tesla sells the Megacharger for $188,000 and takes $0.08 per kWh on third-party charging sites, described as 20% less than its Supercharger for Business rate. The hardware uses the open MCS 3.2 standard, allowing it to charge trucks from Daimler, Volvo, and Scania in addition to the Tesla Semi.

3. **Network scale:** Tesla has 66 planned Megacharger locations mapped, with most targeted for this year (2026); partner depots like Forum's are described as filling in a large portion of that map. Forum Mobility currently counts 25 carrier customers with contracted capacity, over 40 MW of charging online or under construction, reservations for more than 330 Tesla Semis, and is adding 30 MW of new heavy-duty charging capacity. Forum's next planned depots are in the Inland Empire, Central Valley, Las Vegas, Phoenix, Houston, Dallas, Seattle/Tacoma, and Chicago; the California sites were partly funded by state and regional grants.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert states the Tesla Semi is the Tesla vehicle program that most excites him, arguing that if Tesla applies its EV-scaling capability profitably to Semi, it could meaningfully help electrify North American freight transport, which he says lags the rest of the world. He views the Forum Mobility partnership and megawatt charging infrastructure as accelerating that goal.
