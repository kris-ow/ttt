## Brief

- Terafab breaks ground, $16.8B combined Tesla/SpaceX capex commitment
- Nevada grants Tesla AV permit, capped at 10 vehicles
- Sweden's IF Metall ends nearly 3-year strike against Tesla
- Cybertruck PCS warranty extended, reimbursements issued to owners
- FSD driver logs 25,000 disengagement-free miles
- SpaceX satellites redesigned for Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs

## Robotaxi
- Las Vegas/Clark County approves 10 robotaxis, initially limited to Strip-area rides; Waymo has also applied for a permit there.
- Nevada Transportation Authority separately granted Tesla an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit, capped at a maximum fleet of 10 fully autonomous vehicles within an approved Operational Design Domain.
- Houston robotaxi lot reportedly holds around 200 Model Ys and Cybercabs combined, per a Grok-sourced figure cited by hosts; testing also expanding in Sacramento and the Seattle region.
- Gary Black's thesis that Uber will win robotaxi via aggregation was rebutted by FutureAzA/Brighter hosts, who argue Tesla's manufacturing scale beats fragmented AV-partner networks; they also flagged a reported Waymo–Uber rift over vehicle-routing failures.
- Move, an AV fleet-services company, raised $250M for cleaning/charging/depot services and holds Waymo depot contracts; Tesla is described as building its own automated cleaning facilities instead.

## Autonomous Driving
- A Tesla driver (David Moss) extended a disengagement-free FSD streak to roughly 25,000 miles in a Model 3, more than double a prior ~12,000-mile milestone.
- Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov said camera-only autonomy can only approximate human performance and argued LiDAR/radar are necessary; FutureAzA hosts dismissed this as self-interested and noted no comparative accident data has been provided by Waymo.
- FSD now visually detects and displays bus lanes, per an FSD-focused observer.
- FSD take rate cited at 15.2%, up from 11% in 2024, per hosts discussing recurring-revenue implications.
- A European-market FSD demo video showing yielding, cyclist stops, and scooter avoidance is reportedly part of an effort to win EU regulatory approval.

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck PCS (power-conversion-system) warranty extended: 2026 models now 7yr/70,000 miles (up from 50,000); 2024–2025 models now 8yr/150,000 miles; Tesla reportedly reimbursing owners who previously paid for PCS failures out of pocket.
- Model 3 long-range battery replacement price cut from ~$14,500 to ~$8,000; a cited analysis of ~31,000 used Tesla listings found only ~0.17% had lost more than 25% of original range.
- Tesla tops US auto brand loyalty at 64.3% per an S&P Global study cited on brighterwithherbert, ahead of Ferrari, Toyota, Subaru, Honda, and Ford.
- Cybertruck VIN count disputed: one creator reported VINs surpassing 100,000, but another host's independent research estimates actual production closer to 75,000–80,000 units.
- Cybertruck parts shortage linked to Tesla's lawsuit against supplier Angstrom Automotive, which subsequently filed for bankruptcy after a court ordered equipment returned; trucks reportedly sitting in Giga Texas's east lot awaiting parts.
- Tesla Semi: Nevada factory reportedly producing trucks weekly (full capacity target 50,000 units/year, not expected this year); freight carrier Bali Express completed a three-week Semi test.

## Market & Competition
- Lucid unveiled the Gravity GT-S, from $126,000, 373-mile range, 1,070 hp, 0–60 in 3.1s.
- Rivian unveiled 2027 model-year R1S/R1T with new features including optional heated fold-flat second-row captain's chairs.

## Energy
- Tesla launched a Powerwall lease option in Texas bundled with Tesla Electric, offering whole-home backup from $35/month for two units, plus a $100 order fee and no installation cost.
- New 124-stall San Francisco Supercharger station, one of the largest in the world, described as standard-stall-only.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- SpaceX is redesigning satellites to house Nvidia Vera Rubin GPU racks, upgrading satellite power capacity to 250 kW from 150 kW (a 67% increase) to match an Nvidia NVL72 rack's draw.
- Grok 4.6 reportedly performs near Anthropic's and OpenAI's top models on several benchmarks, with investor Gavin Baker cited as saying it matches Anthropic's flagship at roughly an 85% cost discount.
- Grok now leads GDPval, a benchmark measuring model performance against median human workers across job verticals, per the host.
- Elon Musk stated (Aug. 12 post) Grok 4.7 will be "significantly better" than 4.6, ready in three to four weeks, now incorporating SpaceX proprietary data in training; model updates to come monthly.
- SpaceX launched Grokbot, a desktop agentic enterprise product that can be assigned business roles and log into work tools autonomously.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- Hosts on the Beat Podcast discussed Starlink V3 satellites potentially delivering ~100x more total bandwidth than V2, via ~10x more usable spectrum (from acquired Hughesnet spectrum) and ~10x higher per-satellite throughput.
- Starlink connectivity is now reportedly fully integrated into production Cybercabs, enabling 4K streaming/recording and continued connectivity during power outages.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX shares rebounded from around $108 to about $140; one host sees $135 as a near-term selling ceiling, expecting possible consolidation before a breakout.
- Terafab, a joint Tesla-SpaceX compute project, officially began construction, with the initial phase requiring $16.8 billion in combined capital investment; future expansion phases are expected to push total investment higher, per Sawyer Merritt.
