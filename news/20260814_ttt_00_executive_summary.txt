## Brief

## Robotaxi
- Gary Black's thesis that Uber wins autonomous ride-hailing (via aggregator dominance) is rebutted by FutureAzA/Herbert, who argue Tesla's manufacturing scale beats fragmented AV-partner networks and that Uber's model breaks down once an operator owns enough vehicles.
- Reported Waymo-Uber rift: executives on each side blame the other for incidents of Waymo vehicles stranded without routing, with Uber's mapping/logistics role in question.
- Move, a Dubai-headquartered AV fleet-services company, raised $250M for cleaning/charging/depot services; it holds Waymo depot contracts and is reportedly in talks to buy Waymo vehicles, though Waymo appears unreceptive. Tesla is described as unique in building its own automated cleaning facilities (citing a ~36,000-sq-ft robotic center) rather than relying on third parties.
- Nevada Transportation Authority granted Tesla an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit, capped at a maximum fleet of 10 fully autonomous vehicles within an approved Operational Design Domain.

## Autonomous Driving
- A Tesla driver (David Moss) reportedly extended a disengagement-free FSD streak to roughly 25,000 miles in a Model 3, more than double a prior ~12,000-mile milestone; hosts say several other drivers have completed disengagement-free cross-country trips.
- Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov said camera-only self-driving can only approximately match human performance and that LiDAR/radar are necessary for full autonomy; FutureAzA hosts dismissed this as self-interested messaging and noted no comparative safety data has been provided to support the claim.
- A reported incident had two Waymo vehicles collide such that their LiDAR sensors made contact, per commentary characterizing LiDAR as bulky and power-hungry.
- FSD cited as a purchase driver: a California Post reporter traded a Lincoln Navigator for a Model Y citing FSD; Paul Graham described his wife's reaction to Tesla self-driving as a "religious revelation."
- A European-market FSD demo video shows the system yielding to traffic, cyclists, and scooters, part of an effort to win EU regulatory approval.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
- Terafab, a joint Tesla-SpaceX compute project, began construction with an initial-phase capital commitment of $16.8B split between the companies; future expansion phases are expected to push total investment much higher (no figure given).
- SpaceX plans to integrate Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin GPUs into satellites, upgrading satellite power capacity to 250 kW from 150 kW (a 67% increase) to match an Nvidia NVL72 rack's draw; Vera Rubin is estimated at 2-3x the productivity of Blackwell-generation B200 chips, with up to 5x more compute per satellite depending on counting method (per jobhakdi).
- Grok 4.6 reportedly performs near Anthropic's and OpenAI's flagship models on several benchmarks at roughly an 85% cost discount, per investor Gavin Baker as cited by jobhakdi; it also leads GDPval, a benchmark measuring replacement of median human job performance across sectors.
- Musk said (Aug. 12 post) Grok 4.7 will be "significantly better" than 4.6, ready in three to four weeks, now incorporating SpaceX proprietary engineering data, with monthly model updates going forward.
- SpaceX launched Grokbot, a desktop agentic enterprise product built on Grok that can be assigned business roles and work autonomously across tools like LinkedIn, GitHub, and Slack.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- SPCX shares rebounded from about $108 to about $140; one commentator (jobhakdi) sees $135 as a near-term selling ceiling and expects possible consolidation before a breakout.

## Electric Vehicles
- Tesla ranks first in US auto brand loyalty at 64.3% of owners buying another Tesla, per an S&P Global study cited on Brighter with Herbert, ahead of Ferrari, Toyota, Subaru, Honda, and Ford.
- Model Y was Korea's top-selling model overall in July and top imported brand for six straight months, reportedly outselling Mercedes and BMW combined; also cited as Switzerland's best-selling EV.
- Tesla states its batteries retain 80% capacity after 200,000 miles with ~1% annual degradation; US warranty covers repair/replacement (often remanufactured pack) for a minimum of 100,000 miles/8 years.
- An MIT study cited on the show finds EVs cut lifecycle emissions 40-60% versus gas vehicles, with over 90% of Americans living where the average EV beats the most efficient gas car (59 mpg).

## Energy
- Tesla launched a Powerwall lease option in Texas bundled with Tesla Electric, offering whole-home backup from $35/month for two units, with a one-time $100 order fee and no installation cost.

## Market & Competition
- Tesla Semi: freight carrier Bali Express completed a three-week test, calling electrification "a strategic commitment"; the Nevada Semi factory is reportedly building trucks weekly, with 4680 cell supply seen as the likely production bottleneck.
- Cybertruck VIN count disputed: one creator reported VINs surpassing 100,000, but another's independent estimate puts actual production closer to 75,000-80,000 due to non-contiguous VIN blocks.
- Tesla sued supplier Angstrom Automotive over Cybertruck parts equipment; a judge ordered equipment returned, but Angstrom then filed for bankruptcy, leaving Tesla unable to recover it, reportedly leaving trucks awaiting parts at Giga Texas.
- Tesla is building a new 124-stall Supercharger station in San Francisco, described as one of the largest in the world.
- Lucid unveiled the Gravity GT-S ($126,000, 373-mile range, 1,070 hp); Rivian unveiled 2027 model year R1S/R1T updates including optional heated captain's chairs.
