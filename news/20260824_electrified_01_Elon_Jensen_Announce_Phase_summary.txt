This episode of Electrified covers SpaceX AI's new Nvidia Vera CPU partnership for orbital and terrestrial AI compute, grid/power constraints that the host argues make space-based data centers inevitable, and several Tesla robotaxi/FSD developments including a Cybercab spotted testing at a previously flagged problem site, a German pilot for Tesla-based autonomous rural transit, and a viral robotaxi navigation complaint. The host's overarching thesis is that SpaceX's unique heavy-lift capability via Starship positions it to dominate the emerging orbital AI compute market once launch cadence scales.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **SpaceX AI adopts Nvidia Vera CPUs for Grok and orbital compute:** Nvidia announced SpaceX AI will deploy its new Vera CPUs to support agentic AI workloads behind Grok and its first Starmind AI satellite, an orbital rack-scale system based on an optimized Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 design. Musk said this optimized system will be ready for launch to orbit in Q4 next year (2027) with significant scale reached in 2028, and described the design as "significantly simpler, lower cost, denser, and lighter than a traditional rack." The host applies a personal "Elon Multiplier" and instead expects the first Starmind satellites to reach orbit in 2028, with real scale not until late 2028 into 2029, noting Starship launch cadence and the need to also loft Starlink V3 satellites are key constraints; the same optimized NVL72 rack design is also planned for ground-based data centers, which could begin scaling as soon as late next year.
2. **Grid interconnection data underscores case for orbital compute:** Citing Berkeley Lab's 2026 interconnection queue report, the host notes roughly 2.6 terawatts of generation and storage capacity are seeking grid interconnection, with a median project taking about 61 months (roughly 5 years) from request to commercial operation, and only about 2% of queued 2024 capacity reaching operational status in 2025 (versus a historical 2000–2020 average of 13%). A separate June Berkeley Lab study estimates data centers could reach about 12% of total US electricity demand by 2030, versus under 5% today; the host also cites reports of 2025 US shortfalls of roughly 30% for power transformers and 10% for distribution units, and says nearly half of planned 2026 data center openings have been delayed or cancelled due to power/grid constraints rather than capital. He argues this bottleneck, combined with SpaceX's unique heavy-lift capability, will eventually drive significant capital toward SpaceX's orbital compute ambitions.
3. **Nvidia's financial stakes in SpaceX and Intel:** The host notes Nvidia disclosed a stake in SpaceX worth about $21 billion earlier this month, and separately holds roughly a $22 billion investment in Intel, which is a partner alongside SpaceX and Tesla on the Terafab project.
4. **Grok voice handling Starlink customer support at scale:** SpaceX AI said Grok voice is resolving over 15,000 inbound Starlink customer support and sales calls daily, diagnosing hardware issues, shipping replacements, and fulfilling over 3,000 orders per week across voice and chat.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab spotted testing at previously flagged parking garage issue site:** Following an earlier video showing supervised FSD nearly driving into metal wiring on the sixth floor of a parking garage in a college town near Houston and Austin, a viewer named Aron shared a photo of a Cybercab testing at that same location, parked near where the original disengagement occurred. The host believes this is not a coincidence and suggests Tesla AI likely saw the earlier post and sent a vehicle to investigate.
2. **Cybercabs multiplying in Austin, Dallas, Houston ahead of September 3 event:** Short videos over the weekend showed Cybercabs in Austin with ride-hailing decals, some with no occupant and others with a person in the passenger seat (described as non-Tesla employees). The host also notes the Dallas lot is now full of Cybercabs ("dozens"), interpreting this as preparation for active ride launches expected around Tesla's September 3rd event.
3. **Viral robotaxi navigation complaint:** A rider named Reggie reported a 2.3-mile robotaxi trip that was initially estimated at 1 hour 6 minutes; support called it a bug and revised the estimate to 16 minutes, but the actual route never changed, and support said they could not reroute the vehicle. Reggie ultimately exited and used Uber instead; the post reportedly received over 630,000 views. Tesla Robotaxi responded that a refund was issued and the company would continue improving rider experience.
4. **Waymo vehicles blocking a Miami bridge:** A video from the prior day showed multiple Waymo vehicles blocking a bridge in Miami; the host notes this has reportedly happened multiple times this year, though he has seen no official Waymo statement on the incident.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **German rural transit pilot eyes Tesla for autonomous shuttles:** A German outlet reported that Brandenburg's rural public transport system, facing staffing and funding gaps, is in discussions with Tesla about deploying driverless vehicles to supplement an existing on-demand bus system, with a pilot already running in the Eifel region (with a Tesla employee currently required in the driver's seat). A state minister said legal approvals are still needed but he could envision widespread rural deployment once cleared. The host views this as a positive signal that could improve (though not guarantee, ahead of a vote later this year) Tesla's chances of winning supervised FSD approval in Germany.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Tesla Semi production activity increasing:** A third-party Tesla Semi reporter said he believes Tesla may now be producing European-spec Tesla Semis (relevant ahead of expected European Semi launch details next month) and that the "Atlas" version of the Tesla Semi is now ready for customer deliveries, though no footage of the European variant was shown.
