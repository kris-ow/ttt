Tesla reported Q3 2026 deliveries of 486,532 vehicles, down 2.1% year-over-year but beating Wall Street's consensus estimate by about 24,558 units; energy storage deployments rose year-over-year but missed analyst expectations.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Deliveries beat estimates despite YoY decline:** Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3 2026, down 2.1% from the record 497,099 delivered in Q3 2025 (when US buyers rushed to claim the expiring $7,500 federal tax credit), but up 1.3% from Q2 2026's 480,126. This beat the company-compiled analyst consensus of 461,974 (range: 421,758 at Cantor Fitzgerald to 482,000 at JPMorgan) and exceeded every individual estimate on that list.
2. **Production vs. deliveries:** Tesla produced 464,391 vehicles and delivered 486,532, meaning it delivered 22,141 more vehicles than it built — the second consecutive quarter of working down inventory, which the article says has now cleared the roughly 50,000 excess vehicles built in Q1.
3. **Model breakdown:** Model 3 and Model Y accounted for 478,237 of deliveries; "Other Models" (Cybertruck, Semi, and remaining Model S/X inventory) totaled 8,295, down 48% from 15,933 a year earlier.
4. **Year-to-date performance:** Tesla has delivered 1,324,681 vehicles through the first three quarters of 2026, up 8.8% from 1,217,902 over the same period in 2025.

## Energy
1. **Storage deployments miss expectations:** Tesla deployed 13.7 GWh of energy storage products in Q3 2026, up 9.6% from 12.5 GWh a year ago and up slightly from 13.5 GWh in Q2, but below the 14.2 GWh record set in Q4 2025 and well short of the 15.9 GWh analysts expected.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert (Electrek) argues high gas prices are propping up US demand lost from the tax credit expiration, while Shanghai exports compensate for weak Chinese retail sales; he notes volume isn't profit, citing Tesla's Q2 profit miss and a recently arranged $30 billion credit line. He also contrasts Tesla's results with BYD reportedly outselling Tesla by about 276,000 EVs in the quarter, and suggests Tesla would be better positioned with a lower-cost ($25,000–$30,000) model in production rather than its current focus on autonomy.
