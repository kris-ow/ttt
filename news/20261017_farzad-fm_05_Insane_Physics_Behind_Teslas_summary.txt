This video breaks down the engineering behind Tesla's new Roadster, unveiled October 15th at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas test site, focusing on its three variants (Performance, Thrust, and Rocket) and the fan- and jet-based systems Tesla says let it exceed normal tire-grip limits. The core thesis: suction fans are the everyday performance edge owners would feel, while the SpaceX-derived cold-gas thrusters are a flashier but more niche, track/off-road feature.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Three Roadster variants with escalating hardware:** Tesla is building three versions — a Performance model with rear suction fans and movable wings/skirts; a Thrust version adding a carbon-wrapped gas tank in the trunk feeding four rear jets; and a limited-run Rocket version that replaces the passenger seat with a second tank and adds six more jets (four pointing down for lift, two in the nose), which Tesla says can make the car fly.
2. **Suction system to beat tire grip limits:** Tesla stated a normal tire tops out at roughly 1.25 g's of sideways grip; the Roadster's rear fans and floor skirts pull air out from underneath to create about 2 g's of downward suction (twice the car's weight), which Tesla says works even at low speeds when aerodynamic wings would do little. This suction let the car hang from an arch during the demo.
3. **Cold-gas thrusters based on SpaceX rocket tanks:** The Thrust and Rocket versions carry a carbon-wrapped tank (COPV), which Tesla told Top Gear is based on SpaceX rocket tank technology, holding gas at up to 10,000 PSI (over 3x a scuba tank). A valve driven by an Optimus robot motor releases gas through nozzles for thrust; Tesla VP Lars Moravy said the system was sized to produce roughly the thrust of a fighter jet to overcome gravity and air resistance on Earth. On stage Tesla cited over 80 kN (~18,000 lb) of thrust against a target car weight of about 3,700 lb.
4. **Performance targets:** Tesla says two rear motors produce over a megawatt of power (described as roughly 1,000 microwaves' worth), enabling a claimed 0-60 mph in under 1 second (a run shown at the reveal took 1.81 seconds per the video's reading), a 330 mph top speed target, and over 300 miles of range.
5. **New battery cell for sustained high power draw:** To handle repeated megawatt-level draws without overheating, Tesla developed a special 4680 cell cooled from both sides; this also supports a 15–80% charge target of about 10 minutes.
6. **Design hides the hardware:** Chief designer Franz von Holzhausen aimed for a clean exterior — chin wings swing open up to 80°, side panels pop out, and the rear wing tilts up using the same actuators Optimus uses for its ankles — so the car looks like a smooth long-tail coupe when parked.
7. **Jet fuel runs out fast, refill is slow:** Top Gear was told a full gas tank lasts about 5 seconds of thrust use, and refilling currently takes 2 to 6 hours, though Tesla hopes to get that under 30 minutes.
8. **Prototype status, 2028 production target, legal caveats:** Only four or five units have been built so far; Tesla is targeting 2028 production and cautions that final numbers may change. The Rocket variant was described in the Top Gear interview as off-road use only due to jet blast risk to bystanders, and Elon Musk said on stage he's unsure the car is legal in all jurisdictions.
9. **Strategic framing — "cherry on top":** Musk reiterated that flashy halo products like the Roadster follow the harder work of scaling the EV industry (via Model 3/Model Y), and said on stage that long-term, the Roadster may be the only car Tesla makes that a person actually drives, as the rest of the lineup moves toward autonomy.
