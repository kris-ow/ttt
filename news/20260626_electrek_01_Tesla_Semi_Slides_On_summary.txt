Tesla Semi program lead Dan Priestley shared a video demonstrating the truck's Vehicle Dynamics Control (VDC) system recovering from a slide on icy surfaces, with the trailer loaded with concrete blocks and steel bars during the test.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **VDC system demonstration on ice:** Priestley posted a video showing the Tesla Semi briefly losing traction on ice before regaining control, and clarified the trailer was loaded — "concrete block over the fifth wheel and solid steel bars on the rest of the trailer," described as a "low CG load for this kind of testing." The truck's 800 kW tri-motor drivetrain producing 1,072 hp features independently controllable rear motors, allowing torque modulation at individual wheels within microseconds — significantly faster than hydraulic traction-control systems on conventional diesel Class 8 trucks.

2. **Electric architecture safety advantages:** The article explains two structural advantages over diesel: (1) the battery pack's low chassis position reduces rollover risk compared to diesel tractors with mass mounted high; (2) instant, granular torque control allows the stability system to correct slides proactively rather than reactively. Tesla has claimed since the Semi's 2017 unveiling that independent motor control makes the truck "impossible to jackknife," though no independent third-party safety data on the production Semi has been published.

3. **Production ramp context:** The safety messaging coincides with Tesla ramping Semi production at its new Nevada facility, which began rolling trucks off its high-volume line in April 2026. The facility is designed for up to 50,000 trucks per year. Confirmed battery sizes are 822 kWh (500-mile trim) and 548 kWh (shorter-range trim). The article notes fleet buyers are particularly sensitive to uptime and insurance costs, making rollover and jackknife safety a meaningful commercial argument.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert affirms the underlying physics are sound — low center of gravity and independent torque control are genuine advantages — but stresses that a controlled demonstration clip is not validation, and that Tesla's "impossible to jackknife" claim from 2017 still lacks independent, third-party safety data from real-world fleet miles.
