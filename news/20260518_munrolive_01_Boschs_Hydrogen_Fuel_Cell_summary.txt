This video is an interview conducted by Armen from Munro Live at ACT Expo 2026, featuring Peter Tadros, President of Power Solutions in North America at Bosch. The conversation covers Bosch's hydrogen fuel cell power module (the C300), its applications in heavy-duty trucking and stationary power generation, as well as Bosch's battery management systems and an autonomous trucking collaboration with Kodiak. The core thesis is that Bosch embraces a "multi-energy platform" philosophy — no single silver bullet — and is developing technology-neutral solutions ready for the market at whatever pace adoption occurs.

## Market & Competition

1. **Bosch C300 Hydrogen Fuel Cell Power Module:** Bosch unveiled the C300, a second-generation hydrogen fuel cell power module rated at 300 kilowatts. This is an upgrade from the previous generation, the C190, which was rated at 190 kW. The C300 features two horizontal fuel cell stacks with a single shared power transfer unit and integrated controls, making it more efficient by using one set of components to control both stacks. Peter Tadros confirmed this is a functioning unit with Start of Production (SOP) imminent — not yet in full production. The module is designed to drop into a Class A (Class 8) truck in the same footprint as a diesel engine, fitting between the frame rails.

2. **Design Changes from C190 to C300:** The transition from 190 kW to 300 kW required the stacks to be oriented horizontally to maintain the same width envelope required for Class A trucks. The unit is slightly longer and slightly taller than the C190, but the width constraint — critical for fitting between frame rails — was preserved. The turbo pump at the rear of the unit serves a circulation function.

3. **Stationary Power / Data Center Application:** Tadros highlighted an adjacent use case for the C300 in stationary power generation, particularly for data centers facing power demand crunches. The module can serve as a primary, secondary, or backup power source. He noted that hydrogen pipelines already run through parts of the country, enabling data centers located near those pipelines to use hydrogen as a primary energy source. Because the unit was developed and validated to automotive standards — which are more demanding (vibration, corrosion, temperature swings) than stationary environments — adapting it to stationary use is relatively straightforward, requiring no new R&D from scratch.

4. **Hydrogen Sourcing and Heavy-Duty Trucking Use Case:** Tadros acknowledged that hydrogen mobility in the US has slowed but emphasized the product is ready when the market catches up. For long-haul trucking, hydrogen's key advantages over battery-electric include faster refueling (under 45 minutes per Bosch's fueling solutions on display), no battery weight penalty (which reduces payload and revenue for fleet operators), and higher uptime — a critical total cost of ownership (TCO) factor. The practical near-term model is deploying hydrogen trucks on fixed corridors where hydrogen supply is already established, building operational data, then expanding infrastructure.

5. **Battery Management System with Dual-Use Application:** Bosch also displayed a battery management system capable of monitoring, controlling, and disconnecting battery systems. Like the hydrogen module, this product has a dual-use philosophy: it serves both mobility applications (hybrid and electric vehicles) and stationary energy storage. Tadros noted the company is receiving significant customer interest and requests for the energy storage application of this system.

6. **Autonomous Trucking Collaboration with Kodiak:** Bosch showcased a sensor pod developed in collaboration with Kodiak for autonomous Class A trucks. The sensor pod — featuring radar and cameras — replaces one set of side mirrors on existing conventional trucks, enabling driverless operation without requiring a new electric or hydrogen truck platform. The stated goals are addressing driver shortages and extending operational hours. Tadros noted that extensive testing and route validation work is ongoing with Kodiak. For stationary hydrogen applications specifically, Bosch is collaborating with a company called PowerCell, which serves as Bosch's sales channel for that market segment.
