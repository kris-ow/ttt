This video from Jo Bhakdi covers Nvidia's announcement at a Taiwan event where CEO Jensen Huang (referred to as "Yansen Wang" in the transcript) unveiled the RTX Spark chip and a new line of AI-capable personal computers built in partnership with MediaTek and Microsoft. Bhakdi's core thesis is that this launch represents a fundamental reinvention of the PC market, dramatically expanding Nvidia's total addressable market and cementing its dominance in AI compute — now extending from data centers to edge and home computing.

## Market & Competition

1. **Nvidia RTX Spark chip announcement:** Jensen Huang unveiled the N1X chip, branded as RTX Spark, described as the product of 33 years of Nvidia development. The chip features a Blackwell RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores, one petaflop of AI performance, a custom 20-core Grace CPU built in partnership with MediaTek, 128 GB of unified memory, a TSMC 3-nanometer process, and 70 billion transistors. It is designed to run the full Nvidia software stack — including CUDA applications spanning digital biology, seismic processing, astrophysics, genomics, AI, and computer graphics — as well as all Windows applications.

2. **Microsoft partnership and agentic Windows platform:** The chip was developed in close collaboration with Microsoft over approximately three years, resulting in a Windows platform optimized for AI agents. Huang indicated a joint appearance with Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella was planned for the following night. Bhakdi frames this as bringing AGI and agentic intelligence directly into consumers' homes via personal computers.

3. **PC market TAM expansion thesis:** Bhakdi argues this announcement will effectively double the total addressable market for home computing — laptops and desktops. He contends average computer prices will rise from roughly $900 to $2,000 or more, with premium configurations reaching $3,000–$4,000. He estimates Nvidia will capture approximately half of that incremental revenue, representing a massive expansion of both market size and Nvidia's margins beyond its existing data center business.

4. **Edge compute and free local AI tokens:** Bhakdi highlights that running AI inference locally on these devices means tokens are effectively free for the end user, removing the per-token cost associated with cloud-based AI services. He views this as a compelling enough value proposition for consumers to justify the higher hardware prices, and notes it also represents a form of distributed compute that could alleviate some energy demands at scale.

5. **Nvidia stock and personal investment position:** Bhakdi states Nvidia is his second-largest holding after Tesla and expresses strong conviction, indicating he plans to double down. He cited Nvidia's price-to-earnings ratio as "super low" and described it as one of the most undervalued stocks given its cash generation. He predicted Nvidia stock would reach $300 during the current year (2026), noting it was already up approximately 6% on the Monday prior to recording (with the stock trading around $226 at time of recording on Tuesday, June 3, 2026).

6. **Apple competitive pressure:** Bhakdi asserts that Apple will be forced to adopt Nvidia chips in response to this announcement, arguing Apple cannot independently develop chips competitive with Nvidia at this level. He predicts Apple will eventually announce adoption of similar chips, which he expects to be bullish for Apple's stock, though he acknowledges Apple's historically slow technology adoption cycle could mean a delay of approximately one year.
