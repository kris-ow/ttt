The host recaps SpaceX's Q3 earnings call, framing it as extraordinarily bullish and arguing SpaceX's terrestrial compute buildout, Starlink growth, and the Tesla–SpaceX merger discussion are the standout storylines. He contrasts his current enthusiasm for SpaceX with his long-standing Tesla bull case, stating he still holds roughly 97% of his personal investable assets in Tesla and about 3% in SpaceX, and urges Tesla investors not to be discouraged by SpaceX's current momentum.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Aggressive GB300 and Vera Rubin rollout pace:** Elon Musk said SpaceX is on track to bring 200,000 GB300 chips online every month this year at Colossus 2 in Memphis, a pace he said would continue next year, with a similar or accelerated pace expected in 2027 as they move to the Vera Rubin chip line. Musk reportedly spends about four days a week at the Colossus 2 site.
2. **Compute capacity and revenue targets:** On the call, Musk floated a target of roughly 10 gigawatts of compute online by the end of 2027, and said he'd be surprised if SpaceX brought online less than 13 gigawatts of total compute by then; SpaceX also said it could have 3 gigawatts online by the end of this year and an incremental 10 gigawatts next year. The host calculates that renting out 10GW at a stated leasing rate of $30–50/watt (using the high end of $50/watt) would imply roughly $500 billion in revenue if fully leased, or roughly $650 billion if 13GW were fully leased at that rate — though SpaceX said the actual plan is to lease only about half of capacity and reserve the rest for internal projects/inference. Musk said he expects an annual revenue run rate exiting 2027 of between $500 billion and $1 trillion, and separately posted that $1 trillion in SpaceX revenue in 2028 is "no longer impossible" (Musk's own characterization/projection, not a confirmed outcome).
3. **Power/cooling/networking as the bottleneck:** The limiting factor for compute buildout is not chip supply but power, cooling, and networking infrastructure; the team is targeting 20 gigawatts of power/cooling/networking capacity online by 2027 to maintain excess capacity relative to compute.
4. **Shorter-term leasing strategy and rising rates:** SpaceX said it is deliberately pursuing shorter-term compute lease deals rather than long-term contracts because the team expects demand to keep rising and leasing rates to increase over time (citing older chips like the H100 now leasing at higher rates than when first released). Musk said SpaceX is being approached by every hyperscaler and dozens of smaller companies seeking compute access, and claimed SpaceX could re-lease its entire compute capacity "several times over" if it accepted all requests (Musk's characterization).
5. **Training cluster and Grok ambitions:** Musk said SpaceX expects to have the world's most powerful training cluster online by Q2 next year, built around roughly 440,000 Vera Rubin chips, which he said should help give Grok a shot at being the best AI model in the world, or at minimum competitive. He also said Grok Bot usage is growing faster than anything he's seen, and suggested Grok Bot could eventually make up a majority of SpaceX's AI compute usage long-term (an unconfirmed, speculative framing per Musk).
6. **Grok Bot paid subscriber growth:** Gwynne Shotwell said Grok Bot had 800,000 paid weekly users as of earlier this month, describing this as a doubling from the prior month (implying roughly 400,000 the month before), with growth driven largely by enterprise/B2B adoption, which she said is just beginning to ramp.
7. **Terafab chip timeline:** Musk said he hopes for the first chip off the research fab line in Giga Texas — a simple SRAM-type test chip — to be functional around June of next year, with an internal target of Q3 2028 for volume production at the main Terafab site at Gibbons Creek. The host says he personally doubts this timeline will be met.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Subscriber and ARPU growth:** Starlink added 1.6 million new consumers in the quarter, bringing retail subscribers to 13.6 million. Brett Johnson said average revenue per user rose to $69/month, up from $66 last quarter, reversing the expected trend of declining ARPU over time.
2. **Starlink Mobile network ambitions:** Musk posted after the call that SpaceX is building an entirely new mobile network architecture that industry veterans don't fully grasp, and said the team sees a clear path to hundreds of billions of dollars in annual Starlink revenue; a more detailed presentation on this is expected.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Tesla–SpaceX merger update:** Musk confirmed both Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to negotiate a potential merger at shareholders' request, but said he personally cannot be involved in talks due to being a conflicted party. He said twice that he personally believes merging the companies would be a good idea, but stressed no deal is certain, and that committees have not yet reached an agreement. The host speculates (his own opinion, not confirmed) that an announcement is unlikely before 2027.
2. **Q4 capex guidance:** Brett Johnson said SpaceX expects capex of $35–40 billion in Q4 alone, driven by rising compute demand.
3. **Compute payback period compressing:** Musk said compute payback periods, already around 12 months, could shrink to about six months next year as leasing demand and rates increase.
4. **Q1 revenue signal:** Brett Johnson said SpaceX expects on the order of $40 billion in revenue in Q1, which the host cites as evidence of near-term visibility into compute-driven revenue growth.
