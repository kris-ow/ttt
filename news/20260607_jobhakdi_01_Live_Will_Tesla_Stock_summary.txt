Jo Bhakdi hosts a live session reacting to a sharp Friday market selloff (NASDAQ down ~4.18%, Tesla down ~6.56%), arguing it represents a profit-taking breather in a strong bull market rather than the start of a sustained crash. He previews an in-progress SpaceX DCF model and shares his evolving view that SpaceX may be dramatically undervalued at its ~$2 trillion IPO valuation. ## Market & Competition

1. **Friday Selloff Characterized as Profit-Taking, Not Panic:** Bhakdi describes the Friday session as the worst single day for the NASDAQ in over a year, with the NASDAQ falling 4.18% and Tesla dropping 6.56% (~$27). He argues the selling was unusually linear — not the accelerating-into-the-close pattern typical of true panic — and frames it as an inevitable breather after the NASDAQ rallied approximately 25% over the March–May period. He attributes the catalyst to widespread profit-taking in semiconductors and other high-momentum names, dismissing the strong labor data narrative as an after-the-fact rationalization.

2. **Tesla Outperformed Relative to NASDAQ Beta:** Bhakdi notes that given Tesla's historical beta versus the NASDAQ, a 4.18% NASDAQ decline would ordinarily imply a roughly 8.3–8.5% Tesla decline. Tesla's actual 6.56% decline, he argues, reflects partial support from JP Morgan's price target upgrade from $150 to $475 — which he attributes directly to JP Morgan's role as a bookrunner for the SpaceX IPO rather than a genuine analytical revision.

3. **Near-Term Tesla Price Range: $350–$490:** Bhakdi outlines a wide near-term range, noting Tesla could reach $490 within days if a Cybercab World Cup launch catalyst materializes and the broader market recovers, or could fall to $350–$380 if the market extends its selloff and/or SpaceX IPO dynamics create additional selling pressure in Tesla. He emphasizes that both scenarios are "really, really reasonable" in the next 10 days.

4. **SpaceX IPO Volatility Risk for Tesla:** Bhakdi flags the SpaceX IPO as an additional complicating variable, noting possibilities in both directions: some Tesla investors could sell Tesla to fund SpaceX purchases, while SpaceX enthusiasm could also draw new capital toward Tesla. He states the IPO is approximately $75 billion in size, which he considers manageable relative to the ~$2.5 trillion single-day market cap destruction seen on Friday. He gives a 95% probability to a Tesla–SpaceX merger occurring within two years.

5. **S&P 500 Exclusion Limits SpaceX Index Buying:** Bhakdi notes that the S&P 500 has indicated it will not include SpaceX this year, which removes what would have been the largest forced-buying driver. He expects NASDAQ 100 index inclusion to provide some upward pressure but considers it substantially less powerful. His base case remains that SpaceX shares will rise at IPO, then drop, and potentially stay depressed longer than he previously expected before recovering.
