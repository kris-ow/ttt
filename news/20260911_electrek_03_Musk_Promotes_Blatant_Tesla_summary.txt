The article reports that Elon Musk amplified a video of a 98-year-old Tesla owner (Larry) using FSD (Supervised) while openly admitting he no longer watches the road, and argues this behavior directly contradicts Tesla's legal defense in ongoing crash lawsuits.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Viral video and Musk's endorsement:** The Fast Lane published a video of Larry, a 98-year-old Model Y owner in Farmington, New Mexico, describing how he uses FSD to avoid watching intersections and traffic, saying "I trust it 100%" and "I don't want to look, it's just doing it." Sawyer Merritt clipped the video and Musk quote-tweeted it with a heart emoji; the clip reportedly surpassed 9 million views. Larry also acknowledged the risk, saying the car "hasn't made mistakes" in his two or three months of use but that he is "taking a chance."

2. **Conflict with Tesla's own terms:** The article notes Tesla's disclaimer states FSD (Supervised) "require[s] active driver supervision and do not make the vehicle autonomous," and argues Larry's admitted behavior — not watching intersections — violates this core requirement. The article also states the car makes a driving error in the same video (missing a restaurant sign), which it presents as evidence FSD is not infallible despite Larry's "no mistakes" claim.

3. **Litigation exposure:** The article states Tesla's legal defense in FSD/Autopilot crash lawsuits centers on driver responsibility for supervision, and asserts that Musk publicly endorsing a driver who says he doesn't supervise undermines that defense. It cites a prior Florida jury verdict finding Tesla partly liable in a fatal Autopilot crash with a $243 million judgment, and states Tesla faces up to $14.5 billion in exposure across roughly two dozen lawsuits. The article also references its own prior reporting (June and August) alleging Tesla's official marketing and some owners have similarly showcased FSD misuse.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues that Tesla cannot simultaneously market FSD as usable hands-off/eyes-off (implicitly, through Musk's promotion) while telling courts drivers are always responsible for supervision, calling this contradiction something plaintiffs' lawyers have already exploited. He states FSD is impressive but not reliable enough for the way Larry uses it, criticizes the lack of a hands-on-wheel requirement, and expresses hope not to have to report on a future incident involving Larry.
