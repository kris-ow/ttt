Electrek reports results of an informal reader sidebar survey (nearly 3,000 votes) asking who should be held responsible when an autonomous taxi is involved in a fatal or damaging incident, prompted by recent Tesla accident headlines and Waymo parking tickets.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Survey results and reader sentiment:** About 3.5% (102 votes) said the person in the driver's seat should bear responsibility even when a vehicle is self-driving; nearly 90% of votes instead blamed the developers of the AI system, with the article noting the vehicle owner/registered owner argument (raised in reader comments) as a middle position based on existing product-liability-style logic (e.g., "the car is insured, not the driver").
2. **Context for the survey timing:** The survey ran around the time of Tesla's Cybercab re-unveiling in Austin, Texas, and news that the Tesla Semi began series production in Nevada, though the article does not provide additional detail on either event beyond noting the timing coincidence.

## Editorial Stance
Author Jo Borrás says he personally shifted from initially believing drivers/owners should bear liability to favoring an owner/AI-developer shared-liability framing after reading reader comments, ultimately endorsing a commenter's view that liability should be split evenly between the car manufacturer and the AI developer.

Notable quotes and data:
- ~3.5% (102 of ~2,900 votes) blamed the human occupant/driver.
- Nearly 90% of votes blamed AI/software developers.
- Reader quotes cited: Ian ("the liable person is the owner of the car... The car is insured, not the driver"); pushedbyvolts ("the registered owner is the responsible party for putting a lawbreaking vehicle on the street"); Stephen Fields (owner not liable absent failure to follow manufacturer guidance); BCGeiger (liability should be split evenly between car manufacturer and AI manufacturer).
