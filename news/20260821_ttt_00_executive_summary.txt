## Brief

## Robotaxi
- **Nevada grants Tesla an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permit for up to 5,000 robotaxis over 12 months**, replacing a prior 10-vehicle Las Vegas Strip-only approval and covering all of Clark County; Waymo and Uber (with Motional/Zoox) received parallel approvals capped at 1,000 vehicles each. Tesla told regulators it would be pleased to reach 2,000–2,500 vehicles within 12 months and said it's "pretty well positioned" to begin within 120 days.
- **JPMorgan reiterated a $475 price target** after a Fremont factory visit, telling investors Tesla is deliberately holding back Model Y additions to the robotaxi fleet out of confidence in Cybercab, and that Cybercab is just the "initial form factor" with more purpose-built variants to follow.
- **Public Cybercab launch appears imminent.** Riders in Austin can win one of five spots at a launch event via a referral contest running through August 23, with winners notified August 25; commentators expect the actual public rollout in late August or the first week of September, likely a modest-scale event with roughly a hundred vehicles. Tesla also ran 12 first-responder training sessions with the Cybercab in Austin last week.
- **Zero Tesla-caused robotaxi crashes mid-June to mid-July**, per NHTSA data cited by one panel; two incidents involved human drivers hitting robotaxis. A separate low-speed bollard strike in Austin on August 15 reportedly got a navigation fix within two days.
- Austin's phase-two wireless charging hub (reportedly ~80 stalls) has been approved but is not yet operational; new WiTricity-derived patents cover hot-object detection and 400V/800V-compatible pads, seen as key to depot-density scaling.

## Autonomous Driving
- **China recalls 2,740,642 domestically built Model 3/Y vehicles** to add cabin-camera driver-attention monitoring via OTA, after regulators found existing steering-torque monitoring insufficient under Autosteer; some Chinese drivers had defeated the camera using doll heads near the mirror.
- FSD V15 is characterized by JPMorgan's note as a "huge leap" comparable to the v13-to-v14 jump, targeted by year-end; ~40% of its features were previously said to be in robotaxi testing already.
- Multiple disengagement videos circulated: FSD 14.1 on hardware 3 drove over a large log at 30+ mph without braking; a separate report described a low-speed incident approaching a fence near a multi-story drop while exiting a parking garage; another hardware-3 clip showed failure to react to a tree in the road.

## Electric Vehicles
- **Tesla recalls nearly 3 million vehicles in China** (2,975,910 units, its largest-ever China recall) over an emergency door release regulators say is too hard to identify and use in a crash; remedy is warning labels plus an OTA update changing window behavior, effective September 25. Eight other automakers filed similar recalls the same day.
- Tesla Semi launch details for Europe coming September 15 at IAA Transportation in Hanover; deliveries not expected before 2027. Amazon reportedly ordered 500 Tesla Semis via Einride.
- Vehicle-to-load capability rolled out for all US Model Y Premium trims via an $80 adapter (120V/20A, ~2.4kW).

## Energy
- **Tesla has discontinued its Solar Roof tile product**, per Electrek sources, telling installers only conventional panels will be supplied going forward; Tesla concluded the product wasn't financially viable. Peak installations reportedly reached only ~21-32 roofs/week versus Musk's original 1,000/week target, with roughly 3,000 total US installations over seven years and a $6 million class-action settlement in 2023.

## SpaceX — Starship
- **Flight 13 achieved a soft, controlled splashdown in the Indian Ocean** after re-entry from orbital velocity, using its ceramic tile heat shield; Musk reportedly called the heat shield problem "solved." A 60-second static fire of all six Raptor engines was also shared.
- Ship-catch tower capability is delayed and won't be ready for flight 14 (expected in September, targeting orbit); first ship reflight expected late this year or early next year.
- White House space policy sets a goal of 1,000 launches/re-entries per year by 2030; Musk said SpaceX is targeting 30+ Starship launches per day by 2030 (~10,000/year).
