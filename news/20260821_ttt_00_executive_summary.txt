## Brief

- Nevada approves up to 5,000 Tesla robotaxis over 12 months
- JPMorgan hikes Tesla PT to $475 after Fremont visit
- Tesla discontinues Solar Roof tiles, cites poor economics
- China orders recall of 5.7M Tesla vehicles combined
- Cybercab public launch event expected late August/early September
- Starship flight 13 splashdown proves heat shield works

## Robotaxi
- **Nevada green-lights up to 5,000 robotaxis over 12 months**, expanding from a prior 10-vehicle Las Vegas Strip-only permit to statewide Clark County coverage. Tesla told regulators it would be pleased to reach 2,000–2,500 vehicles within 12 months; Uber and Waymo each received separate approvals for up to 1,000 vehicles.
- **Cybercab public launch appears imminent.** Tesla is running a rider referral contest with five winners announced August 25; panelists and hosts expect the actual public launch before month-end or in early September, likely modest in scale (roughly a hundred vehicles per one host's expectation). Twelve first-responder training sessions were held in Austin last week as a further signal.
- **JPMorgan reiterates $475 price target after a Fremont factory visit**, telling investors Tesla is deliberately holding back Model Y additions to the robotaxi fleet because of confidence in Cybercab, and that Cybercab is just the "initial form factor" with more robotaxi-purpose vehicles to follow.
- **Zero Tesla-caused robotaxi crashes mid-June to mid-July**, per NHTSA data cited by one panel; two incidents involved human drivers hitting robotaxis. A separate Austin incident saw a robotaxi strike plastic bollards on August 15, with Tesla reportedly pushing a navigation fix within two days.
- **Wireless charging infrastructure advancing in Austin**, with an approved (not yet operational) 80-stall phase-two hub; new WiTricity-derived patents cover hot-object detection and 400V/800V compatibility.

## Autonomous Driving
- **FSD v15 framed as a "step-change"/"huge leap"** comparable to the v13-to-v14 jump, targeted by year-end; Tesla has said roughly 40% of its planned features are already being tested in the robotaxi fleet.
- **Multiple FSD disengagement videos surfaced**: HW3 on FSD 14.1 failed to react to a large log and separately a large tree at speed; a separate report described a low-speed incident near a parking-garage fence with a six-story drop.
- **China orders a 2.74M-vehicle recall** of domestically built Model 3/Y to add cabin-camera driver-attention monitoring via OTA, after regulators found existing torque-based monitoring insufficient and drivers were reportedly defeating cameras with dolls and figurines.

## Electric Vehicles
- **China orders a separate, larger recall of ~3M vehicles** over a hard-to-find/operate emergency door release; remedy is warning labels plus an OTA update to auto-lower windows after a crash, effective September 25. Eight other automakers filed similar recalls the same day.
- **Tesla Semi Europe launch details coming September 15** at IAA Transportation in Hanover; no EU deliveries expected before 2027.
- **Amazon reportedly ordered 500 Tesla Semis via Einride** for North American delivery routes.
- **Vehicle-to-load capability launched for Model Y Premium** via an $80 adapter (120V, 20A, ~2.4kW).

## Energy
- **Tesla discontinues Solar Roof tiles**, per Electrek sources, telling installers only conventional panels will be supplied going forward; Tesla reportedly concluded the product wasn't financially viable. Peak installations reportedly reached only ~21–32/week against Musk's original 1,000/week target, with roughly 3,000 total US installations over seven years and a $6M class-action settlement in 2023.

## SpaceX — Starship
- **Flight 13 achieved a soft, controlled Indian Ocean splashdown** from orbital velocity, which Musk reportedly called proof the heat shield problem is "solved" — a capability farzad-fm says no other rocket has demonstrated.
- **Ship catch tower not ready for flight 14**; Musk said catch capability is a few months out, with first ship reflight expected late this year or early next.
- **White House sets a 1,000 launches/re-entries per year goal by 2030**; Musk said SpaceX is targeting 30+ Starship launches per day by 2030 (~10,000/year).

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- **Merger voting-rights analysis**: one panelist estimates Musk could hold over 70% voting power in a merged Tesla-SpaceX entity, based on 350 million SpaceX Class B options reportedly converting before end of August.
- **SpaceX shares facing resistance near $135**, per one host, tied to lockup-related selling pressure expected to persist into November/December; SPCX closed one day at $136.97, up 2.22%.
- **S&P 500 inclusion of a merged entity floated as a catalyst**, though timing and mechanics remain speculative per multiple sources.

## SpaceX — Starlink & AI & Compute
- **Starlink generated ~$11B revenue and ~$4.4B operating income in 2025**, per SpaceX-reported figures cited by farzad-fm; one Starship V3 launch could carry up to 60 Starlink V3 satellites, ~20x the downlink capacity of a single Falcon 9 launch.
- **Orbital AI data centers targeted from 2028**, initially ~10 gigawatts/year, against a long-term 100-gigawatt goal requiring thousands of Starship launches annually.
- **Current compute build cited at ~1.4–1.5 gigawatts**, targeting 2–2.4 GW by year-end and 6–10 GW next year, per futurist Brian Wang's estimates.

## Market & Competition

- **Rivian claimed point-to-point driver-assist later this year**; hosts on one podcast expressed skepticism given Rivian's more limited compute and data versus Tesla.
- **Porsche to discontinue the Taycan sedan**, shifting focus to Macan and Cayenne EVs.
