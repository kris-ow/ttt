A host and guest Larry Goldberg discuss several recent SpaceX developments — Falcon 9's autonomous flight software, the evolution of the Raptor engine toward simplicity, Starship's launch scale relative to China, and SpaceX's acquisition of turbine maker APR Energy to accelerate AI compute buildout — framing SpaceX as having built capabilities that competitors, including China, cannot easily replicate. The video closes with a defense of Elon Musk's wealth against criticism from Senator Bernie Sanders, arguing his net worth is productive company stock rather than idle cash.

## SpaceX — Launch Business
1. **Falcon 9 autonomy:** A commentator described Falcon 9 as executing nearly the entire mission — liftoff, stage separation, orbital insertion, booster boost-back, and landing on an offshore droneship — autonomously via flight software, physics, sensors and control theory rather than generative AI. Musk (per the transcript) replied that there have been over 600 autonomous rocket landings so far. Guest Larry Goldberg said Falcon 9 will keep flying for years because SpaceX holds multi-year (3-year) military contracts, with payloads gradually shifting to Starship as it matures; he named Stoke Space as a likely contender to build replacement rockets for smaller-payload missions in the interim.
2. **China launch comparison:** A commentator cited China having conducted 61 orbital launches in 2026 so far, delivering an estimated 145 tons to low Earth orbit; a single Starship test flight carrying 30 V3 Starlinks would place roughly 60 tons into orbit (about 41% of China's 2026 LEO payload to date), or about 55% if carrying 40. Goldberg noted the actual flight in question was reported to carry around 20, roughly two-thirds of the 30-satellite scenario, adjusting the comparison down to about 25%. He separately noted Falcon 9 flew about 180 missions last year, contrasting one company's output with that of a nation-state.
3. **Historical and 2030 scale projections:** A commentator cited a cumulative figure since Sputnik (1957) of 7,117 launches and over 22,196 tons of payload delivered to space by more than 20 countries and companies combined. Using Musk's stated 2030 target of 30+ Starship launches per day, and assuming 150 tons per launch, the commentator calculated roughly 4,500 tons/day, or 22,500 tons over 5 days — comparable to the entire historical global total, attributed to Musk's stated goal of extending activity to the Moon and Mars.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **APR Energy acquisition:** SpaceX confirmed (per the discussion, referencing an ARK Invest analysis by Daniel Maguire) its acquisition of natural gas turbine manufacturer APR Energy, aiming to bring turbine blade and vane casting in-house using SpaceX's rocket-pump material science. Goldberg said only three companies currently manufacture turbine blades and are booked out to 2030, and that SpaceX is building a large factory in Boston to manufacture these components, leveraging manufacturing techniques from rocket pump blades (which he said undergo even greater pressure than power-turbine blades, though the pressure types differ).
2. **AI compute buildout timeline:** Per the discussion (attributed to ARK Invest's Daniel Maguire), the turbine vertical integration could pull forward SpaceX AI's compute buildout by about 18 months — moving from roughly 2 gigawatts toward closer to 10 gigawatts by the end of next year. Separately, a rival turbine entrant, Boom Supersonic, repurposed its Symphony supersonic engine core into a 42-megawatt data-center turbine called "Superpower," with a backlog reported above $1 billion as of last December.
3. **Solar manufacturing targets:** Musk is said to expect SpaceX and Tesla to reach 200 gigawatts of combined solar manufacturing capacity by 2029 — described as nine times the incremental solar capacity added by all U.S. players in 2025, though still only about two-thirds of the incremental capacity China added in 2025. Most of this capacity is intended to support SpaceX AI's satellite ambitions, which are expected to begin launching via Starship in Q4 next year. Tesla will manufacture solar panels in Houston for terrestrial power; SpaceX will manufacture a different panel type in Texas for space-based power generation.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Argentina rural connectivity deal:** Argentina's government (Minister Sandra Pedovllo, with Capital Humano and ANAC/ENACOM) signed an agreement with Starlink to install 6,000 kits providing internet to rural schools and isolated communities across the country, part of a broader connectivity initiative.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Musk responds to Bernie Sanders wealth criticism:** Senator Bernie Sanders claimed Musk "owns more wealth than the bottom half of American households," alongside citing statistics on Americans living paycheck to paycheck, homelessness, and uninsured rates. Goldberg disputed Sanders' wealth comparison as off by a factor of roughly 10 and challenged the other statistics as misapplied, though he acknowledged not knowing the exact correct homelessness figure. Musk responded (as quoted) that he holds stock in SpaceX and Tesla rather than cash, and that as his companies "do more and more useful things," their value rises and "all shareholders benefit... including most retirement programs." Senator Mike Lee separately said Musk's wealth belongs to him, not the government.
