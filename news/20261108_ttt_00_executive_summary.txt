## Brief

- Tesla, SpaceX plunge Thursday in broad AI selloff, rebound Friday
- Texas governor-elect's data center stance sparks conflicting interpretations
- Robotaxi launches driverless in Fort Worth, 7th city
- Starship Flight 15 targeted Nov 13, first dual-catch attempt
- Cybertruck production winding down at Giga Texas by mid-2027
- FSD fleet surpasses 17 billion supervised miles

## Financials & Market Reaction
- TSLA fell ~10.8% Thursday to ~$343, SPCX fell ~9% to ~$124, amid a broad AI/chip-stock rout (chip index -12.3%, worst since COVID) triggered by weak Qualcomm and Arm earnings.
- Both rebounded Friday: TSLA up ~4% to ~$358, SPCX up ~8% to ~$134, leaving Tesla down ~10% and SpaceX roughly flat for the week.
- Friday's jobs report (165,000 added vs. ~65,000 expected) raised odds of a December Fed rate hike, a headwind for long-duration growth names like Tesla and SpaceX, per brighterwithherbert.
- Elon reposted an April 2022 "don't panic" tweet urging investors to hold companies whose products they believe in, and later endorsed a Buffett market analogy.
- Elon said SpaceX government revenue will be under 10% of total this quarter and under 5% in a year, addressing post-election contract-exposure concerns.
- Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas nearly doubled his 2027 SpaceX revenue forecast to ~$175 billion and called the stock cheap versus the S&P 500, per brighterwithherbert's characterization.

## Market & Competition
- Democrats won the Texas governorship for the first time in 36 years; governor-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on pausing new data-center construction pending protective legislation, with Dan Ives estimating 25–30% of planned US data-center gigawatts are slated for Texas.
- Gavin Baker characterized Hinojosa's position as allowing already-permitted projects to finish while new centers face tax, electricity-price, water, and union-labor requirements — Elon called this "interesting" — but Hinojosa's own remarks, per video circulated by Sawyer Merritt, were vaguer, and the host flags Baker's framing as optimistic interpretation, not confirmed policy.
- Texas Legislature meets only once every two years (Jan–May), with Republicans still controlling the Senate, limiting near-term legislative risk mainly to post-2028 projects, per Tesla blogger Alexandra Merz.
- SpaceX's Arizona data center (Cochise County) faces a December 9 public hearing; SpaceX says it will cut electricity bills 40% for co-op members and avoid industrial water/gas turbines, addressing most concerns raised in Texas.
- jobhakdi separately argues the post-election selloff is an overreaction, citing a reported (unverified) account that Hinojosa's first call was to Meta's president to reassure on data centers.

## Robotaxi
- Fort Worth launch: Tesla's robotaxi began driverless operation in its seventh city, with a starting geofence of about 140 square miles versus Austin's 270 and Houston's 250.
- Texas DMV registrations hit 1,244 vehicles (up from 59 in June), trailing Waymo by about 15 before Waymo added more; Robotaxi Radar estimated Tesla added ~20 vehicles/day in October.
- Las Vegas expansion signals: 43 vehicles reportedly spotted at a Las Vegas robotaxi lot; Nevada has granted Tesla a permit, with service possibly starting November 24 or by a December 18 deadline (unconfirmed).
- Hiring data shows fleet-support and night-technician roles posted in 17 cities without active service (including Atlanta, Chicago, Nashville) and nine additional Texas cities, signaling future expansion targets.
- Pricing comparisons were mixed: Tesla beat Uber on short Houston trips but lost on some longer cross-city routes; beat Waymo on price in 12 of 17 tracked Tampa comparisons.
- A separate Cybercab tracker (Pioneer Lands, cited by jobhakdi) read ~2,643 units deployed with ~24 added the prior week, a slowdown the host expects to reverse once the fleet passes roughly 1,000 units.

## Electric Vehicles
- Cybertruck production will wind down at Giga Texas around mid-2027; Elon framed it as reallocating Tesla's limited engineering talent toward other priorities rather than a demand failure, even as an extra shift is added to clear backlog. US Cybertruck sales fell to ~20,000 units last year and now run ~3,500–3,700/quarter, far below the original 250,000/year target.
- The vacated Cybertruck line at Giga Texas is intended for Cybercab production; Fremont Model S/X space is reportedly shifting to Optimus.
- About two dozen Cybercabs are undergoing winter testing in Detroit with safety drivers, per the Detroit News.
- European Tesla registrations rose 69% YoY in October (Germany +290%, Spain +271%, Norway +131%); South Korea registered ~9,100 units (+111%, ninth straight month as top import brand); Australia surged 342%, with Model Y now the country's best-selling passenger car YTD.
- China wholesale sales were ~68,000 in October, up 10% YoY but down ~29% from September — the lowest monthly figure since February, though possibly timing-related.
- Elon teased a new liquid silver Cybercab color variant.

## Autonomous Driving
- Tesla's fleet surpassed 17 billion cumulative supervised FSD miles, now covering 1,000 miles every 1.8 seconds (~18 billion miles/year annualized pace), up 35% since July.
- Dutch regulator RDW approved renaming FSD (supervised) to "Tesla assisted driving" in Europe; functionality is unchanged, but the maximum settable speed limit had to be reduced to satisfy regulators.
- Japan's Ministry of Transport reportedly plans to approve driverless vehicles without steering wheels or pedals as a new certification category starting December, per Nikkei — potentially relevant to future Cybercab-type deployments.

## SpaceX — Starship
- Starship Flight 15 is officially targeted for November 13, 5:45 PM ET, aiming for the first attempt to catch both Super Heavy and Starship — a full dual-catch milestone that, if successful, would be a first for the program.
- SpaceX has requested an increase in its permitted annual launch cadence from the site, from 25 to 100 per year.

## SpaceX — Starlink
- SpaceX announced "Starlink Mobile," a nationwide satellite-and-terrestrial 5G network combining ground base stations (Qualcomm chips) with Starlink satellites, targeting 2027 deployment; Qualcomm called it its largest 5G deal yet. SpaceX is also reportedly in talks with EchoStar about Boost Mobile, per the Washington Post.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation
- About 348,000 SPCX shares (~$47 million) were bought Wednesday despite the broader selloff.
- jobhakdi projects SpaceX could reach a $400–450 billion revenue run rate by end of 2027 and a share price around $1,500 by end of 2028 — his own speculative estimate, not confirmed guidance — while disclosing a loss on a SpaceX position entered near $153.
- A third-party, unconfirmed estimate put Anthropic at ~34.5% of SpaceX's prior-quarter revenue, flagged as a concentration risk given reports Anthropic could leave after early 2027.
