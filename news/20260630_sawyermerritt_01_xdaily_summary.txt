## Executive Summary
Today's posts centered on three high-signal Tesla themes: the rollout of FSD V14 Lite to HW3 vehicles, accelerating Cybercab deployment signals in Texas (both Dallas fleet expansion and a state regulatory registration), and competitive dynamics favoring Tesla as VW exits its Bosch self-driving partnership and Waymo ends its Uber integration in Phoenix. ## Key Posts / Themes

1. **FSD V14 Lite Rolls Out to HW3 Vehicles:** In a significant software milestone, Tesla officially began rolling out FSD V14 Lite to HW3 hardware owners. The release notes describe the update as distilling intelligence from HW4 V14 into HW3 via a knowledge-distillation approach, allowing HW3 to learn from HW4 V14's capabilities — effectively unlocking improvements previously exclusive to HW4. Highlighted new features include speed profiles, the ability to start self-driving from park, and arrival options, among others. Merritt noted that 7+ year-old Teslas with HW3 now running FSD V14 Lite have a more capable self-driving system than any brand-new non-Tesla vehicle currently sold in North America — a commentary on the competitive gap in the segment. The FSD V14 Lite rollout post garnered 4,700 likes and 512 reposts, indicating strong community interest.

2. **Cybercab Fleet Expansion and Texas Regulatory Recognition:** Two posts pointed to accelerating Cybercab deployment in Texas. First, a Reddit-sourced image showed over 70 Cybercabs now staged in Dallas, a notably packed lot. For context, the canonical RobotaxiTracker.com figure for Dallas active robotaxis (as of 2026-06-25) stood at 15 — the Dallas staging count of 70+ suggests a large number of vehicles are pre-positioned and not yet counted as operationally active, implying a significant near-term fleet expansion may be imminent. Second, the Texas Department of Public Safety added the Cybercab to its Connected Autonomous Vehicles First Responder Interaction Plans page, a procedural but meaningful regulatory step signaling that Texas authorities are formally preparing for Cybercab operations. Merritt flagged both developments as signs Tesla is "getting closer."

3. **Competitive Landscape Shifts in Tesla's Favor:** Two posts highlighted competitors retreating from autonomous driving. VW announced plans to end its self-driving partnership with Bosch, citing cost-cutting pressures and an explicit determination that Bosch's technology was not competitive with systems like Tesla's FSD. This follows a separate report that VW plans to cut 100,000 jobs. Separately, Waymo confirmed it is no longer available in Phoenix on the Uber app, ending a nearly three-year partnership in that city. Waymo framed the exit as a "productive pilot," but the withdrawal from a major U.S. market via a key distribution channel is a notable competitive data point. Both developments reinforce Tesla's positioning as the structurally leading autonomous driving platform in North America.

4. **India EV Policy — Marginal Tailwind:** New Delhi announced a cash incentive of over $1,000 for owners who scrap old vehicles in favor of EVs, alongside a road tax exemption for EVs priced up to 3 million rupees. This is a positive policy signal for EV adoption in India, though Tesla's direct exposure to the Delhi market at current price points is limited; the post was included with no Tesla-specific commentary.

5. **Cybertruck Domestic Content:** Merritt noted that the Cybertruck has 65% US/Canada parts content versus 45% for the Ford F-150, positioning the Cybertruck as the most American-made full-size pickup available today despite F-150's domestic final assembly. This is relevant in the context of ongoing tariff and trade policy discussions.

6. **X Money Launch (Low Tesla Relevance):** Several posts covered Merritt's access to X Money, including details on 6% APY, up to $10M FDIC insurance, unlimited 3% cashback, and transfer/spending limits. While X is an Elon Musk-affiliated platform, this has no direct Tesla investment thesis relevance and is noted here only for completeness.
