FutureAzA and guest Dr. Know-it-all (John) look at the new FSD v15 visualization, which they call a step change from earlier versions. Drawing on a technical post by Elon Musk, they explain how a separate generative model renders the car's surroundings using only a small slice of AI4's compute. They argue the feature is a strong emotional selling point for FSD and hints at why Optimus will need AI5-class compute.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Visualization is now generated, not templated:** Earlier visualizations placed stock objects in the scene, so any truck, including a Cybertruck, appeared as a generic truck. The hosts say v15 instead renders what the cameras actually see. Examples circulating include clouds rendered in a clip posted by Dirty Tesla's Chris, and Christmas lights on a house during a night drive. John says the system interprets and draws the scene rather than texturing camera images onto objects. He has done 3D animation for about 35 years and compares the moment to first seeing *Avatar*.

2. **Architecture, per Musk's post:**
   - Tesla has had a pixel-accurate world model since last year, used to generate synthetic training data. The hosts note it replaced the Unreal Engine approach shown at AI Day 2022.
   - Synthetic data works like this: a rare event, such as a moose crossing the road, is turned into 100,000 to a million variants for training.
   - The in-car version is much smaller. It is a conditional mean flow model fed by Tesla's Universal Visual Encoder features, the same features that feed the 3-billion-parameter encoder model that produces driving actions.
   - A separate diffusion model of about 1 billion parameters renders a rotatable 3D scene.
   - John believes the full v15 stack is about 10 billion parameters, while noting that figure is dated.

3. **Runs on half of one TRIP engine:**
   - AI4 has two SoCs, each with three TRIP engines, for six in total. John believes the two SoCs are no longer purely redundant backups.
   - Per Musk, once Tesla moved a tiny encoder into SRAM, SoC B still had half a TRIP engine free. The visualizer runs on that slice, roughly 1/12 of the board's compute.
   - It produces 8-frame clips at 36 fps but updates at 18 Hz, half the driving stack's rate. This causes some lag and jerkiness when rotating the view.
   - Musk says interleaving the new encoder features with the diffusion transformer could cut latency by about 35%.
   - TRIP supports only 8-bit integers, so Softmax has to be emulated in 28 steps.

4. **Real-world FSD impressions:**
   - Kyle Conner tested v15 in Mad Max mode in Los Angeles. The hosts describe its gap-shooting as trained on California drivers.
   - The host, using v14 near Springfield, Virginia, calls it amazing and plans to try New York next. He abandoned FSD there after about five minutes on v10.
   - A viewer's v15 clips showed the car backing up and taking a wider path around a fallen tree, and entering a gated community to park in the correct spot.

5. **Visualization as a demand driver:** The host argues new buyers won't grasp driving feats like the fallen-tree maneuver, but the visualization delivers an instant "this is crazy" reaction on demo drives, YouTube or TV coverage. John agrees it is a strong first-impression hook.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **AI5 may matter more for Optimus than for cars:** Musk maintains AI4 is enough for cars, and AI5 offers roughly 12x the compute. The hosts suggest Optimus will need that level of compute, partly because it needs similar world-visualization capability.

## Robotaxi
1. **Prediction-market odds on robotaxi expansion:** In a sponsored Kalshi segment, the market for robotaxi in Georgia in 2026 stood at about 6%. The host agrees it is unlikely this year. Arizona's odds had climbed as high as 94 before falling back to the mid-30s.
