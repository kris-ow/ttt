This weekly outlook from Jo Bhakdi focuses primarily on Tesla's volatile stock action and his trading strategy around the pending Cybercab rollout, framed alongside a broader discussion of a "sentiment shift" in AI stocks (Grok 4.5, Meta's new frontier model, OpenAI's GPT update) and geopolitical/space-race commentary. ## Electric Vehicles / Financials
1. **Tesla stock recap and range-bound thesis:** The host recaps a volatile month for TSLA: a drop to $375 on June 22 "for no reason," a rally following strong delivery numbers (480,000 vehicles delivered), another drop despite the good news, and a snap-back to $419. He expects Tesla to trade in a range — roughly $420s on the upside down to $370–390 on the downside — until there's tangible Cybercab deployment news, which he says could happen "any day" or take another two to three months.
2. **Trading strategy:** The host describes lightening up on positions near the $420s and adding back near $390, using short puts (weekly and medium-term) and holding both medium-range and August calls, while keeping overall exposure above 100%. He frames this as balancing tactical trading around a maintained core long position, given uncertainty on Cybercab timing.
3. **Scenario-based price targets:** He states Tesla could hit $1,000/share (even without Optimus) if the company suddenly scales to 10,000 Cybercabs, and separately suggests a run to $500–550 followed by a possible 30% pullback (to ~$350) if a broader market crash occurs during a bullish phase.

## Market & Competition
1. **Sentiment shift across AI/mega-cap stocks:** The host says he sees a sentiment shift in Micron, Nvidia, Meta and, to a lesser extent, Tesla. He has made Micron his second-largest holding (via in-the-money short puts) and Nvidia his third-largest, with Tesla still dominant in the portfolio; he holds smaller positions in Robinhood, MicroStrategy, and a tracker-share stake in SpaceX (plus short SpaceX calls).
2. **Hyperscaler capex framing:** Citing a Goldman Sachs chart, he argues rising hyperscaler capex (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) doesn't threaten profitability because spending is depreciated over roughly five to six years, and profits generated from that compute exceed the amortized cost — pushing back on bearish "cash flow" narratives (naming Michael Burry as an example of a view he considers wrong).
3. **AI competitive landscape — Grok 4.5, Meta, OpenAI:** Grok 4.5 launched and reportedly performed very strongly on "cursor bench," which the host attributes to the Cursor team's product/harness expertise combined with SpaceX's compute buildout. He also highlighted Meta's new "Muse" frontier model as a surprising leap (crediting Alexander Wang, who joined Meta after Meta paid roughly $15 billion related to Scale AI), and noted OpenAI's new GPT-5.6 model family release, calling it collectively "a very big deal" week for frontier AI.
4. **China/Europe catching up in space and AI:** The host argues the U.S. is not "decades" ahead of China or Europe in rockets or AI — citing Ariane's ~$5,000/kg launch cost vs. SpaceX's ~$35,000/kg (with Starship potentially reaching $1,000–500/kg by around 2027) — and highlights Germany's roughly $30 billion satellite/launch budget and a Munich-based new launch company as evidence Europe is more capable than commonly assumed.
5. **No AI bubble popping from Chinese competition:** Responding to a viewer citing a UK YouTuber's bubble-pop thesis, the host argues cheaper Chinese open-source models (e.g., DeepSeek) won't pop the Nvidia/Micron trade because compute demand is effectively infinite; cheaper intelligence just enables more usage, not less spend.
6. **SpaceX outlook — bearish near-term, bullish medium-term:** He expects SpaceX to be under selling pressure until Q4 due to stock lockup expirations (he's watching for ~60% of shares to become unlocked around November), offset by potential new compute-business announcements (Colossus 2) that could add meaningful profit; he expects a further slowdown from "terrestrial buildout" hitting permitting/policy walls in the back half of 2027, ahead of a longer-term "Space AI" opportunity he pegs around 2028.
