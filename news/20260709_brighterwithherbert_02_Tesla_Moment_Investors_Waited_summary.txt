This panel discussion (Invest Answers' James with Jeff, Cern Basher, and Alexandra Merz) covers the growing convergence of Tesla and SpaceX, fresh signals on Robotaxi and Optimus scaling, modeling of a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger and its share-price implications, and key-man risk around Elon Musk.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla-SpaceX stock convergence:** Panelists note Tesla and SpaceX shares have begun trading in tight lock-step (near-identical daily percentage moves), which Alexandra Merz called premature and potentially unfavorable for Tesla shareholders since it removes upside optionality if SpaceX's price is depressed ahead of a hypothetical merger announcement.
2. **China's humanoid robot scale-up:** A Rand Group report cited forecasts China's humanoid robot production breaching 100,000 units this year, with government mandates pushing robots into factories; Elon Musk and Peter Diamandis both flagged this competitive threat, though Cern Basher argued raw unit counts (e.g., ~$20,000 Unitree bots) don't reflect real capability, and that Tesla's automation advantage and Elon's execution speed remain differentiators.
3. **Tesla stock reaction to delivery numbers:** Panelists expressed puzzlement that Tesla's stock fell (Merz cited roughly an 8% drop) following strong delivery numbers, attributing it partly to negative mainstream media coverage rather than fundamentals.

## Robotaxi
1. **Robotaxi deployment map expanding:** A report from The Information mapped roughly 31 locations across the U.S. (driverless deployment in Texas and Florida, safety-driver testing in Northern California, additional pending markets), which panelists called encouraging though slower than hoped; Alexandra Merz noted uncertainty on whether Las Vegas's reported large vehicle permit request has been approved.
2. **New Austin warehouse signals infrastructure buildout:** A roughly 680,000 sq ft speculative industrial warehouse near Giga Texas (reported by a industry watcher) was speculated to support servicing/repair of the growing robotaxi fleet.
3. **Vehicle staging seen as imminent-launch signal:** Jeff argued that Tesla deploying hundreds of Cybercabs to multiple cities (rather than keeping repair-prone early units near the factory) suggests confidence that a broader unsupervised launch is imminent, alongside hiring across roughly 23 states.
4. **Cybercab TAM framing:** James characterized Cybercab as a $10 trillion total addressable market opportunity, versus $25 trillion for Optimus, combining for $35 trillion over the next five years.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus procurement targets reported:** Citing analyst Ming, the panel relayed that Tesla has reportedly set procurement targets of roughly 1,000 Optimus units/week by September, scaling to about 2,500 units/week by December (implying a ~130,000 unit annual run rate); Jeff cautioned this is a supply-chain signal (with component lead times of 8–30 weeks depending on part type) rather than confirmation of an exact production start, but said combined with automation-readiness signals from a recent interview with Lars Moravy, it suggests some level of Optimus production is imminent this summer.
2. **New Optimus factory construction pace:** Jeff estimated the exterior structure of Tesla's large multi-story Optimus factory could be completed within six to nine months, though interior tooling/production line setup will take longer; the facility is expected to use modular sub-assembly lines feeding a final assembly line.
3. **Optimus vs. Robotaxi revenue timing:** All three panelists agreed Robotaxi will generate meaningful revenue before Optimus; Cern Basher expects the two to ramp within roughly a year of each other longer-term, while Jeff expects Optimus's ramp to be slower due to unresolved questions about component durability and mechanical complexity, though early external Optimus deployments could command high margins.

## Financials
1. **Tesla trades at elevated forward P/E:** Cern Basher noted Tesla's high P/E (cited as 172) partly reflects retail investors pricing in future growth, while much of Wall Street remains focused on near-term financials and largely ignores Tesla until a robotaxi/Optimus revenue ramp materializes, drawing comparisons to Nvidia's and Micron's earlier re-ratings.
2. **Tesla now spans ~20 business lines:** James stated he now tracks roughly 20 distinct Tesla business lines (only two vehicle categories — cars/Cybercab and Semi — with the rest spanning five energy segments, four services/ecosystem lines, four manufacturing/Cortech lines, and three AI/autonomy lines), reinforcing a "no longer just a car company" framing.
3. **Merger valuation modeling:** James's simple model put an implied Tesla merger price at $555 (post a $13 daily gain) under a 50/50 ownership assumption at then-current prices; Alexandra Merz's more detailed model incorporates Tesla's roughly 3.76 billion outstanding shares (including ~400M+ restricted shares tied to Elon's 2025 compensation package that could be forfeited or partially vested depending on merger timing) and estimated Elon's post-merger voting power could reach as high as 69% under certain price scenarios; she believes Tesla shareholders would end up with at least 50% of a combined entity.
4. **SpaceX analyst price targets and lock-step implications:** Panelists cited a recent flurry of sell-side SpaceX price targets averaging $246 (with a high of $800) from major banks; if lock-step trading persists and SpaceX hits that average target within 12 months, the implied Tesla price would be $854, according to James's model.
5. **Options/LEAPS unaffected by merger:** James clarified that existing Tesla LEAPS contracts would simply be adjusted (new strike/contract codes) rather than voided in a merger, citing precedent from past acquisitions (e.g., VMware/Broadcom).

## Market & Competition (continued)
1. **Key-man risk assessment:** All three panelists downplayed long-term key-man risk for Elon Musk, comparing it to Apple's transition after Steve Jobs' death, citing deep management benches at both Tesla and SpaceX and Elon's habit of directly engaging with individual engineers; short-term market impact from any incident was acknowledged as likely severe, however.
2. **Merz's governance concern on SpaceX share structure:** Alexandra Merz raised a specific concern that SpaceX's bylaws (unlike Tesla's) reportedly allow Elon Musk's high-vote Class B shares to pass to his family trust with 10x voting rights intact upon incapacitation or death, contrasting this with Rivian founder RJ Scaringe's shares converting to lower-vote Class A shares in a divorce; she suggested Tesla's board should scrutinize this in any merger negotiation.
