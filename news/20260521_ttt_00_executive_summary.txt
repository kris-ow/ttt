## Autonomous Driving

- **Lithuania approves FSD Supervised; Tesla ends one-time FSD purchases in Europe.** Lithuania became the second EU country to approve FSD Supervised on May 20, activating the feature there 39 days after the Netherlands. Simultaneously, Tesla terminated one-time FSD purchases across Europe effective May 21, shifting to subscription-only monetization. Netherlands FSD usage has already reached 9.32 million miles across 39 days (~239,000 miles/day).

- **Greece expects June approval; Ireland actively in process.** Greece's Secretary General of Transport stated Greece will accept the Netherlands' type approval outright, without waiting for an EU-wide vote, citing North American safety data showing "a very significant reduction in accidents." Ireland's approval process is actively underway. Per brighterwithherbert/Brian White, Tesla is proactively offering test vehicles and data to individual EU member states to accelerate national approvals.

- **China FSD approval targeted for Q3 2026.** An urgent wave of FSD-related data labeler and supervisor job listings in China signals an accelerating local program. Tesla's China data center has been operational for roughly two to three months. Hosts assess broader China FSD approval is more likely Q3 2026 than sooner.

- **FSD 14.3.3 described as a meaningful step; software leads robotaxi builds.** Per the Bhakdi/Cern Basher discussion, version 14.3.3 is a notable improvement over 14.3.2. Cern notes the software running on robotaxi vehicles is presumed to be one development build ahead of consumer FSD. Elon Musk is cited as having said safety for robotaxi operation is solved; remaining work is "convenience" — pickup/drop-off polish, parking lot behavior, passenger comfort edge cases. Cern believes sufficient convenience quality will be reached within one to two months without requiring a version 15 release.

- **Actually Smart Summon max speed raised to 8 mph in FSD 14.3.** Tesla AI confirmed the ~33% speed increase. Per The Tesla Space, this makes the feature more practically usable in real parking lot conditions, which remain among the most complex low-speed autonomous environments.

## Robotaxi

- **Cybercab in Dallas for the first time; Houston unit carries a license plate.** A Cybercab was observed entering a service center in Dallas — the first public sighting in that city. Separately, a production Cybercab at the Houston robotaxi hub was photographed with a license plate. Per Brian White (brighterwithherbert), vehicles are not plated unless deployment is imminent.

- **"Switch flip" thesis: Cern Basher targets 10,000+ robotaxis by year-end 2026.** Cern argues Tesla is accumulating simultaneous readiness across software, production, and regulatory positioning for a large-scale deployment event rather than a smooth linear ramp. He projects Cybercab output could reach thousands per month within three months, reasons Tesla would not sustain a production ramp while parking units indefinitely, and expects a sudden jump from hundreds to tens of thousands. Jo Bhakdi sees Q3 as the more likely inflection point for reaching ~1,000 unsupervised units; Cern assigns higher probability to Q4 for that milestone, but both converge on 10,000+ in service by year-end.

- **Steering wheel removal identified as the clearest near-term deployment signal.** Cern Basher: once Tesla stops manufacturing Cybercabs with steering wheels, driverless commercial deployment is near-certain and imminent.

- **Robotaxi hub permitted in Las Vegas: ~35,000 sq ft, 17 DC chargers, 200+ parking spots.** Per Brian White, a similar facility exists in Dallas. Host estimate: a 200-spot hub can support roughly 1,000 active vehicles in service. The hosts raise the open question of whether dedicated hub infrastructure must be operational in each city before Tesla is comfortable scaling there.

- **Cities targeted for pre-year-end 2026 rollout: Miami, Tampa, Orlando, Las Vegas, Phoenix.** Per Bhakdi/Cern, initial deployments in new cities may begin with only five to six vehicles, potentially still supervised.

- **Institutional investor threshold: 1,000 unsupervised robotaxis.** Jo Bhakdi frames triple-digit unsupervised counts as beginning to generate stock reactions, with 1,000+ making it "irrational for large capital to remain underweight Tesla." Cern raises the possibility of a 10–20% single-day stock surge driven by institutional positioning ahead of a public deployment announcement.

## Humanoid Bots

- **Terafab origin tied directly to Optimus chip supply failure.** Per the Cern Basher/Phil Beisel discussion, Elon approached both Samsung (Taylor, Texas fab) and TSMC about chip supply for Optimus and found Samsung's facility could satisfy only roughly half a year of the first three years of Optimus production plans. The inability to secure sufficient external supply is identified as the key catalyst for Elon deciding to build Terafab internally.

- **SpaceX S-1 confirms Terafab is "very early stages" with no binding commitments.** The S-1 filing describes only "a general framework for future development," with all specific projects, timelines, milestones, and capex subject to separate future negotiations. The filing explicitly states "neither Tesla nor Intel are obligated to remain a part of the project." The same language applies to Macrohard, the joint agentic AI platform. Electrek's Lambert frames the announcements as narrative-building ahead of the IPO.

## Energy

- **Tesla solar gigafactory sited in Bastrop County, Texas; $250M capex, 100 GW annual target.** The facility is co-located with Tesla's Megapack gigafactory already under construction and will integrate the full stack: ingot growth, wafer slicing, cell production, and panel assembly. For context, First Solar projects 17.7 GW by 2027; total US solar installations in all of 2023 were ~32 GW. Brian White notes $250M is highly capital-efficient at this scale, with Tesla's captive demand (Superchargers, Megapack, AI data centers, residential) providing a structural customer base and domestic manufacturing eliminating tariff and shipping costs.

- **Tesla lithium refinery in Corpus Christi discharging ~231,000 gallons/day of black wastewater.** An investigation concluded in February found no heavy metals and no permit violations; an independent test found only trace hexavalent chromium (just above detection limits) and arsenic below drinking water standards. Tesla is in full compliance with current permits, though the permit did not require monitoring for lithium. Long-term fix under discussion is a filtration plant; the drainage district is investigating further but has not filed suit.

## Electric Vehicles

- **California trucking firms have filed subsidy applications for 1,200+ Tesla Semi units** — more than all other electric truck models combined since the incentive program began in 2019. At $290,000 per long-range unit, that represents ~$350M in potential revenue. California's prior incentive structure ($120,000–$150,000 per vehicle) could bring the landed cost to ~$140,000 — at or below a comparable diesel semi's upfront price.

- **Tesla Semi achieves 95% uptime vs. diesel's 82–87%.** The New York Times published a positive early review. Brian White projects a 50,000-unit/year run rate by 2028 (not 2027), contingent on 4680 cell production scaling to ~30–40 GWh annually.

- **Tesla Semi ground-up engineering confirmed by program lead Dan Priestley.** Per The Tesla Space, Tesla discarded all diesel-era design conventions. Roughly half of highway energy goes to aerodynamics; this insight drove every design decision, yielding a unified battery/frame/drivetrain platform. Long-range version carries 822 kWh usable; standard-range 548 kWh. EPTO system delivers up to 25 kW continuously, eliminating separate diesel refrigeration units.
