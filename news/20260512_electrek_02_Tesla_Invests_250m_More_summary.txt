Tesla announced a ~$250 million additional investment in battery cell production at its Grünheide (Giga Berlin) factory, more than doubling the planned capacity target from 8 GWh to 18 GWh annually and adding over 1,500 battery-related jobs.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Giga Berlin recovery underway:** The factory was reportedly running at just 40% capacity earlier this year following a 28% collapse in European sales in 2025 (German registrations down 48%) and the shedding of ~1,700 jobs. Rising demand for the refreshed Model Y has reversed the trajectory — in April Tesla announced plans to hire 1,000 new workers, boost production by 20%, and convert ~500 temporary workers to permanent contracts.

## Energy
1. **Battery capacity expansion:** Tesla raised its Grünheide cell production target from 8 GWh (announced December 2025) to 18 GWh. Total cumulative investment in the site's cell production unit now approaches €1 billion (~$1.2 billion). At 18 GWh, the facility would produce enough cells for an estimated 250,000–350,000 vehicles per year, depending on pack size. Cell production is expected to start in the first half of 2027.

2. **Vertical integration goal:** Tesla's stated ambition is to produce everything from battery cells to finished EVs at a single European location by 2027 — a scale of vertical integration it claims no other European automaker has achieved. Tesla has already begun recruiting for the 1,500+ battery production roles.

## Market & Competition
1. **Union vote controversy:** Two months before this announcement, CEO Elon Musk sent workers a video warning that expansion would not proceed if IG Metall gained a works council majority. IG Metall's vote share fell from 39.4% (2024) to 31.1% in the March 2026 election; Tesla's management-aligned list, Giga United, won 24 of 37 seats. IG Metall has filed a legal challenge alleging unlawful interference. The investment announcement followed immediately after the union's defeat.

## Editorial Stance
Author Fred Lambert notes healthy skepticism is warranted given Tesla's repeated history of announcing and re-announcing Grünheide battery plans since 2021 without delivery, while acknowledging the 18 GWh target, €1 billion cumulative investment, and active recruiting make this more concrete than past promises. He characterizes the union-investment sequence as a "troubling precedent for labor relations in European manufacturing," regardless of one's view of the underlying tactic.
