This episode of The Tesla Space covers three major topics: the growing speculation around a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger driven by shared AI and robotics ambitions, Tesla's new lens-cleaning patent that resembles a mechanical eye (with implications for both Cybercab and Optimus), and the latest Optimus manufacturing developments at Giga Texas. The video's core thesis is that Tesla and SpaceX are not merely partners but are converging into a single strategic enterprise centered on artificial intelligence, robotics, and ultimately Mars colonization. A brief segment also discusses whether Tesla might one day build a Model 3 Plaid.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla-SpaceX Merger Speculation:** According to people familiar with the conversations, Elon Musk has seriously discussed a merger between Tesla and SpaceX with his most trusted colleagues, and the idea is reportedly discussed openly inside Tesla. The video argues the merger is less a surprise than it might seem, given how intertwined the two companies have already become over several years.

2. **Deep Financial Entanglement Between Tesla and SpaceX:** The SpaceX prospectus, filed ahead of what the video describes as potentially the largest stock market debut in history, revealed that SpaceX purchased roughly $697 million worth of Tesla Megapack battery systems in 2024 and 2025 to power AI data centers connected to XAI's Colossus facilities in Memphis, as well as $131 million in Cybertruck purchases during the same period. Tesla also invested $2 billion into XAI earlier in 2026, shortly after which XAI merged with SpaceX. In 2024, Nvidia redirected a $500 million GPU order from Tesla to XAI at Elon Musk's personal request. Suppliers have reportedly begun treating Tesla and SpaceX as a single customer.

3. **Terafab — A Joint Semiconductor Venture:** In March 2026, Elon Musk announced the Terafab, a joint semiconductor fabrication project with an initial facility being built in Texas near the Austin Gigafactory. One side of the project serves Tesla's needs — including FSD, Optimus, and future vehicle programs — while the other is aimed at SpaceX's long-term vision for computing infrastructure in space. The video frames Terafab as something neither company could have justified building independently.

4. **Shared AI Engineering Challenges as the Underlying Driver:** The video argues that Tesla and SpaceX face surprisingly similar engineering constraints — running powerful AI systems with tight limits on power consumption, cooling, reliability, and cost — Tesla inside a moving vehicle and SpaceX in orbit. This convergence of technical problems makes the same expertise in chips, power systems, and AI valuable to both organizations, providing a structural logic for deeper integration beyond financial ties.

## Humanoid Bots

1. **Optimus as a "Von Neumann Probe":** Elon Musk reportedly described Optimus as a Von Neumann probe — a concept from physicist John Von Neumann referring to a self-replicating machine that can build copies of itself from raw materials, enabling exponential growth without additional human labor. The video quotes Musk on X as saying "Optimus will be the first Von Neumann machine capable of building civilization by itself on any viable planet," positioning the robot as central to SpaceX's Mars settlement ambitions.

2. **Dedicated Optimus Factory Construction Begins at Giga Texas:** Construction officially began this week on Tesla's dedicated Optimus manufacturing facility at Giga Texas, with drone footage captured by longtime factory observer Joe Tegtmeyer showing the first steel structure now standing at the site. When completed, the building is expected to stretch nearly the full length of the main Giga Texas factory, making it one of the largest manufacturing facilities Tesla has ever built. Tesla's stated target is production of approximately 10 million Optimus units per year at the facility, equating to roughly 27,000 units per day.

3. **Mechanical Eye Patent — Implications for Optimus:** Tesla was granted a patent for a lens cleaning system that, while never using the words "eye," "blink," or "tear," features a dark circular lens resembling a pupil, a housing shaped like an eye socket, a cleaning nozzle positioned where a tear duct would be, and two curved wiper blades resembling eyelids. The system continuously monitors image quality and, upon detecting degradation, automatically sprays cleaning fluid and sweeps debris with a wiper plate — completing the full cycle in under one second. Since Tesla has confirmed that Optimus shares camera hardware with its vehicles, the video argues that robots operating in real-world environments will eventually require the same self-cleaning capability.

## Autonomous Driving / Robotaxi

1. **Lens Cleaning Patent Critical for Unsupervised Robotaxi Operation:** The video emphasizes that the lens cleaning system is especially important for robotaxi deployment because, unlike a human driver, a robotaxi cannot pull over to wipe a dirty lens. Tesla has already been spotted testing camera cleaning systems on robotaxi vehicles and Cybercab prototypes, suggesting the technology is closer to production than a newly granted patent might indicate.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **Model 3 Plaid — A Dream Deferred:** Tesla VP of vehicle engineering Lars Morvi, appearing on the Ride the Lightning podcast, said when asked about a potential tri-motor Plaid version of the Model 3: "I think about it all the time. I think about carbon sleeves, all the permanent magnets. I definitely think about it." Morvi described it as a "work-forward situation," citing packaging constraints in the smaller Model 3 chassis as the primary challenge. The video notes that Tesla recently retired the Model S and Model X, effectively retiring the Plaid badge with them, and that the next-generation Roadster will not be accessible to most buyers, leaving a performance gap. The current Model 3 Performance hits 0–60 mph in approximately 2.9 seconds; a Plaid version could push that significantly further.
