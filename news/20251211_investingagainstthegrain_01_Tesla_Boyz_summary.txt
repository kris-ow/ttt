This panel discussion (hosted with Nick Gibbs of Investing Against the Grain) centers on Elon Musk's recent claims that unsupervised FSD is "solved" and that driverless Austin robotaxis are roughly three weeks away, alongside his statement that Waymo will not be seen as a real competitor in hindsight. The hosts debate what "solved" means, scrutinize the Optimus teleoperation controversy from a Miami event, and discuss regulatory and competitive dynamics between Tesla and Waymo.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Musk's "solved" and timeline claims:** Musk stated Tesla robotaxis will operate in Austin with no one in the car, including no one in the passenger seat, in about three weeks, calling unsupervised FSD "pretty much a solved problem" now in validation. He said a larger ("order of magnitude greater") model will likely deploy in January or February, followed by reasoning and reinforcement learning improvements; he projected the AI4-based system could become several times better than human drivers (potentially up to 10x), with AI5 (design nearing completion) roughly 40x better than AI4 by some metrics, though he estimated realistically "10 or 11 times." He also said serious scale would likely require Tesla to build a large AI chip fab (referred to as Terafab) producing a few hundred gigawatts-equivalent of AI chips per year.
2. **Panel debates the meaning of "solved":** One host argues "solved" should be judged purely on safety versus human driving, not ride comfort or minor navigation quirks (e.g., wrong-lane attempts), citing his own roughly seven months of FSD driving across multiple states/cities without a "critical disengagement." Another host pushes back, noting he personally experienced FSD attempting to turn the wrong way on a one-way street the prior day, which he considers a critical disengagement, though he stresses it doesn't undermine an overall "solved" characterization for city-approved deployment. The group agrees "solved" should map to statistically safer-than-human performance, not zero errors.
3. **Safety data comparison caveat:** One host flags that comparing Tesla's and Waymo's safety statistics is not apples-to-apples: Waymo's data is only valid within its narrow, controlled geofenced areas (and it pulls cars in adverse weather), while Tesla's cited safety statistics reflect driving across the entire continental United States — a much broader and less controlled operating domain.
4. **European/international FSD demos:** Hosts note thousands of FSD rides in recent European and Australian demonstrations — citing over 700 rides during a three-day demo in Australia — with no reported safety-critical disengagements mentioned in viewer/commenter feedback, which the hosts view as an encouraging signal of shifting public sentiment. FSD is now approved in over a dozen countries per one host's count, including South Korea, Australia, and reportedly gaining traction in China.
5. **AI executive order/federal framework discussion:** The hosts discuss a reported Trump executive order establishing a unified national AI rule, described by a White House AI advisor as covering large language models specifically rather than autonomous vehicle regulation (which falls under the Department of Transportation). They argue the "spirit" of a single federal framework — avoiding a patchwork of state/city rules — was originally inspired by autonomy policy discussions (attributed to Transportation Secretary Duffy), and that competitiveness versus China is cited as a key rationale; the hosts view this as a positive signal that federal frameworks for both autonomy and robotics may be forthcoming, noting government focus is reportedly shifting to robotics policy next year.

## Robotaxi
1. **Waymo vs. Tesla scale comparison:** A Waymo employee (Jeff Dean) claimed Waymo has 96 million "rider-only" autonomous miles and is the most advanced large-scale embodied AI deployment today, prompting hosts to argue that once Tesla scales unsupervised operations, it could match or surpass Waymo's cumulative mileage figures within a short period (a host speculated "in a week"), given Tesla's larger fleet base and ability to cut prices; this is the host's own projection, not a confirmed outcome.
2. **Customer-owned cars joining the network:** Ashok Elluswamy reportedly stated Tesla is working on a certification process (e.g., checking tire tread, safety) that would allow customer-owned cars — including Hardware 3 vehicles — to join the robotaxi network, and indicated Hardware 3 cars are expected to be usable both for FSD and as part of the robotaxi fleet, since Tesla no longer manufactures Hardware 3.
3. **Robotaxi app rollout to 11 countries:** Tesla released its robotaxi app in 11 countries; hosts speculate (without confirmation) this could reflect testing of app-store/regulatory infrastructure, aspirational market planning, or another strategic reason, but explicitly say they don't know the actual rationale.
4. **Android app absence:** Commenters questioned why no Android robotaxi app exists; hosts push back on the idea that this is because Google (a Waymo investor) restricts data sharing, noting Google owns only part of Waymo, and argue Tesla simply hasn't prioritized an Android build yet due to a current shortage of vehicle supply relative to rider demand, not a technical barrier.
5. **Waymo's expansion pace acknowledged:** Hosts credit Waymo for expanding faster than a year prior — moving city to city, entering airports, and operating on highways — while arguing that Tesla could theoretically produce far more robotaxi-capable vehicles (on the Model Y platform) far faster than Waymo could scale its purpose-built fleet, since Tesla could reportedly convert existing production without added shifts.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus teleoperation moment at Miami event:** Footage from a Tesla event in Miami showed an Optimus unit handing out water bottles; when an operator's shift ended, the robot's arms went limp after the person removed their VR headset without first putting Optimus into standby mode — exposing it was being teleoperated in that moment. Hosts note Tesla had separately shown other water-bottle-handling footage described as "100% automated," and argue the Miami clip doesn't retroactively prove all Optimus demos are fake; one host adds the Miami units were reportedly version 2.0 bots, with 2.5 and a version 3 (described as a likely release candidate) still to come. Hosts disagreed on whether a separate popcorn-handling demo (at a different venue) was teleoperated or autonomous, with no resolution reached.
2. **Optimus training infrastructure:** Hosts reference an "Optimus University" reportedly being built in Cortex 2 in Austin, intended to train the robot on a wide range of tasks.

## Market & Competition
1. **Waymo usage vs. Uber:** Hosts cite an Uber statement that in cities where Waymo operates, Waymo ride usage is higher than Uber's and riders are paying more, which the hosts interpret as evidence that consumer comfort with self-driving rides is stronger than skeptics assumed — while also noting there have been isolated incidents of people vandalizing or protesting against robotaxis in some cities.
