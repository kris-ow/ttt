This is a live panel covering Tesla's Q3 earnings call, with pre-show discussion dominated by SpaceX–Tesla merger speculation (following SpaceX's earnings call the day before), followed by live reaction to Tesla's financial deck and executive Q&A on Cybercab, FSD, Optimus, Terafab, and energy. The panel's core thesis: Tesla's underlying fundamentals (robotaxi scaling, FSD attach rate, cash flow) were strong, but Tesla's communication/presentation on the call was viewed as muddled compared to SpaceX's polished call the prior day, and the merger timeline is now the dominant investment question for both stocks.

## Financials
1. **Free cash flow beat expectations:** Tesla posted free cash flow of $1.5 billion in Q3, versus an expected negative figure the panel described as around negative $4.5 billion — described by panelists as an enormous beat.
2. **Automotive margin and cash position:** Automotive gross margin (excluding credits) rose sequentially from 16.3% to 18.1%, with the panel citing a non-GAAP figure near 19.6% at another point; cash and investments ended the quarter above $45 billion, with Tesla securing a new credit facility to borrow up to $30 billion to support capex growth, which it does not plan to draw on this calendar year.
3. **Capex guidance revised down:** Tesla revised full-year capex guidance down to approximately $20 billion, citing efficient capital deployment; R&D spend rose about 69% year-over-year to nearly $3 billion, driven by AI/chip development and new-product ramp costs (Optimus, Megapack 3, Semi).
4. **Net income items:** Net income included a $142 million mark-to-market loss on Tesla's SpaceX equity holding, offset by an approximately $290 million Bitcoin mark-to-market gain.
5. **Backlog and inventory:** Tesla reported its biggest vehicle backlog in over four years and inventory levels the panel noted at around 11 days, which the CFO attributed to deliveries being constrained primarily by production, not demand.

## Robotaxi
1. **Cybercab deployment and scale:** Tesla said cumulative unsupervised robotaxi miles reached roughly 3 million by the time of the call (versus near 2 million at quarter-end per the deck), with the Austin Cybercab fleet described by Elon Musk as having grown roughly 10x since its launch about a month prior. Tesla said next month it will launch in Dallas, with other cities to follow using the same software stack.
2. **Unit economics and cost target:** CFO Vaibhav Taneja said Tesla has not yet reached its target cost of 30 cents per robotaxi mile but sees "a path" to get there as utilization and fleet operations scale; Cybercab production capacity was listed in the deck at greater than 125,000 units/year, though executives said actual production is being run at the rate the robotaxi service needs rather than at full capacity.
3. **Customer ownership timeline:** Lars Moravy said Tesla is targeting delivering Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, with the vehicle framed primarily as a "transport as a service" product rather than a personally-owned car.
4. **Unsupervised FSD in customer cars:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla expects to enable unsupervised operation for the first customer-owned Model Y vehicles (as part of the robotaxi fleet) around Q2 of next year.
5. **Operating hours and safety-driven expansion:** Robotaxi service hours have expanded from a 6am–9pm window toward 24/7 operation by year-end, gated by safety validation rather than by a single blocking issue; executives said six cities are already covered and expansion to more is planned.
6. **Panel critique of disclosure:** Analysts on the panel argued Tesla under-communicated genuinely bullish robotaxi/Cybercab news (e.g., the Dallas launch timing, the planned 2026 customer Cybercab sales) relative to its substance, burying what they called the biggest headline of the call in a brief answer.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **FSD attach rate jumped to 75%:** FSD attach rate on new deliveries rose to over 75% (up from roughly 55% previously per the deck), with CFO Taneja noting this is roughly 20% higher than Q2 and that over 1.7 million paid FSD customers now exist globally, more than half of which are now subscription-based for the first time.
2. **New European FSD approvals:** Tesla announced four additional FSD regulatory approvals in Croatia, Slovenia, Czech Republic, and Slovakia; Elon Musk said he expects FSD approval across the rest of Europe potentially within a few months, saying he'd be surprised if it extends beyond Q1.
3. **New mapping/navigation architecture:** Ashok Elluswamy said Tesla is building an entirely new AI-based mapping and navigation system (not reliant on third-party maps), to be integrated with voice control and personalized/fleet-learned routing, with initial elements appearing as part of the upcoming V15 release.
4. **FSD economics:** An analyst estimated FSD subscription revenue is now approaching roughly $1 billion in annual recurring revenue; Taneja confirmed the order of magnitude was correct and said Tesla will share more FSD-specific metrics as the business scales.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus production begins in Fremont:** Tesla confirmed Optimus production has started on a high-volume line in Fremont, producing what it called the first production-quality bots; Musk said getting to an annualized rate of 20,000 units/week will likely take at least a year, given what he described as thousands of interdependent manufacturing requirements.
2. **Timeline to external delivery:** Musk said he expects the electromechanical design, AI, and reliability/duration issues to be resolved around the end of next year, which he identified as the point Tesla would begin delivering Optimus units to companies outside Tesla, with initial use cases likely industrial/dangerous repetitive tasks.
3. **AI5 chip integration:** Tesla plans to integrate its AI5 chip into Optimus around April of next year, which Musk described as an order-of-magnitude increase in compute versus AI4 and a major capability unlock.
4. **Hand design remains the hardest problem:** Engineers said the hand is the most challenging hardware area, more complex electromechanically than the locomotion actuators, due to the need for durability combined with delicate manipulation and sensor integrity.
5. **New Optimus demo video planned:** Musk said Tesla may release a new Optimus demonstration video in about a month showing non-pre-programmed (i.e., genuinely autonomous) task performance, distinct from what he characterized as scripted demos from other humanoid robot makers.
6. **Austin Optimus factory:** Tesla has also started constructing a second Optimus factory in Austin, in addition to Fremont, to expand production capacity.

## Energy
1. **Energy margins improved sharply:** Energy gross margin improved from 20.4% to 33.1% sequentially, which Taneja attributed primarily to tariff-related fund recognition and warranty true-ups from vendor cost alignment.
2. **Megapack demand and sellout:** Tesla said it is almost sold out of Megapack capacity for 2027 and is ramping production to meet demand; Q3 energy storage deployments were 13.7 GWh, described as the second-best quarter on record.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y sales records:** Vaibhav Taneja said preliminary data indicates the Model Y was the top-selling BEV in Europe and the top-selling passenger car (of any kind) in South Korea in Q3, with new records also set in Australia, Japan, and Taiwan.
2. **Manufacturing targets:** Tesla reiterated a goal of reaching 5 million vehicles per year by approximately 2030, citing ongoing factory capacity investments including a new Semi factory in Reno and permit applications to expand the Berlin factory.

## SpaceX — Corporate & Valuation / Financials
1. **Merger process status:** Elon Musk said the prior day that each company has formed special committees of independent directors to evaluate a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger, that discussions are ongoing without an agreement yet, and that the Tesla earnings call would not address merger questions at all — a stance panelists characterized as inconsistent given SpaceX had addressed it a day earlier.
2. **Panel's merger timeline and pricing debate:** Panelists debated exchange ratios and premiums extensively (ranging from roughly 3-to-1 to 4.2-to-1 SpaceX shares per Tesla share, with premium estimates of 15–50% above various reference prices), largely unconfirmed speculation rather than company guidance; one panelist's own theory tied a potential announcement/closing timeline to Musk's major tax obligation in January 2028 and a possible SpaceX S&P 500 inclusion window.
3. **Shareholder vote mechanics:** A panelist (board/governance analyst) detailed that Tesla has approximately 3.95 billion shares outstanding, of which Musk controls about 1.1 billion (with roughly 400 million of those mirror-voted due to unmet compensation milestones), and noted historical non-voting/broker non-vote rates of roughly 15% and 10-11% respectively, creating a structural hurdle to reaching the 50%-plus threshold needed for merger approval.
4. **Terafab investment scale:** Musk said Terafab capital investment will be in the "tens of billions" through early 2028 and could exceed $100 billion long-term, with the split of costs between SpaceX and Tesla still to be determined; a first test chip is targeted around May or June next year, with volume production of a useful chip targeted around Q3 2028.
