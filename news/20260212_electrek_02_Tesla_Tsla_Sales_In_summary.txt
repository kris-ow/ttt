Tesla's domestic retail sales in China fell 45% year-over-year in January to 18,485 units, the lowest monthly figure since November 2022, per China Passenger Car Association (CPCA) data. The article argues this is masked by a healthier-looking wholesale figure that is driven almost entirely by exports.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Domestic retail collapse vs. wholesale growth:** January domestic retail sales were 18,485 units, down 45% YoY and down 80% from December's record 93,843 units. Wholesale shipments from Giga Shanghai totaled 69,129 units, up 9.3% YoY, but 50,644 of those (73% of output) were exports — the second-highest export month on record behind October 2022's 54,504 units, with exports up 71% YoY. The article notes export share of Shanghai production has risen from about 44% in January 2024 and 47% in January 2025 to 73% in January 2026, framing the wholesale "growth" headline as misleading since it reflects exports rather than Chinese demand.
2. **Model-level competition from Xiaomi:** The Tesla Model Y fell to 20th place among all passenger vehicles sold in China in January, per retail sales data cited in the article. Xiaomi's YU7 took the top spot with 37,869 units — more than double Tesla's entire domestic retail volume that month. Xiaomi's SU7 delivered over 22,000 units versus roughly 8,000 Model 3 units for Tesla; the article notes Xiaomi's SU7 outsold the Model 3 in China for the first time over a full year of production in 2025. Wholesale (including exports) showed Model Y at 38,916 units (+21% YoY) and Model 3 at 30,213 units (-2.5% YoY), which the article says don't reflect actual domestic demand.
3. **Contributing factors cited:** The article attributes part of January's weakness to a pull-forward effect from December 2025 (Tesla's best-ever retail month in China at 93,843 units) ahead of the reinstatement of a 5% NEV purchase tax from January 1, 2026; the expiration of trade-in subsidies in most cities in mid-November 2025; and a broader NEV market downturn — CPCA estimated total China NEV retail sales fell 20% YoY to 596,000 units in January, with BYD's NEV sales down 30% YoY and 50% month-over-month to 210,051 units (still far above Tesla's total).

## Market & Competition
1. **Multi-year downtrend:** The article states 2025 was the first year Tesla saw a YoY decline in domestic China retail sales, with full-year retail sales down 4.78% to 625,698 units and wholesale deliveries down 7.08% to 851,732 units; Tesla's share of China's NEV market fell from 10% in 2024 to 8% in 2025. Tesla has responded with 0% financing, a 7-year 0.5% interest financing program, and RMB 8,000 insurance subsidies on the Model 3, which the article says have slowed but not reversed the decline.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues the wholesale "growth" framing is a misleading spin since it stems entirely from exports, not Chinese consumer demand, and calls Tesla's financing/insurance incentives "the playbook of a brand losing market share, not pushing innovation." He expects a domestic rebound in February/March as Shanghai redirects production to local deliveries, but says the year-over-year trajectory shows no bottom in sight absent a genuinely new product.
