Electrek reports that on Tesla's Q4 2025 earnings call, Elon Musk admitted no Optimus robots are currently doing "useful work" in Tesla's factories, contradicting a string of prior claims by Musk and Tesla over the past two years.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Musk's admission on the Q4 2025 call:** Asked about Optimus, Musk said the program is "still very much at the early stages," "still in the R&D phase," that Optimus has done "some basic tasks" in the factory but is "not in usage in our factories in a material way," framing current deployment as the robot learning rather than performing real work. He added Tesla wouldn't expect "significant Optimus production volume" until probably the end of 2026. He did not directly answer a question on how many Optimus robots Tesla currently has.

2. **History of prior claims:** The article traces a timeline of escalating claims: Tesla's official account said in June 2024 that two Optimus bots were "performing tasks in the factory autonomously"; at the June 2024 shareholder meeting Musk projected a thousand to a couple thousand robots working in Tesla factories by 2025; on the Q4 2024 earnings call (January 2025) Musk said Tesla's internal plan called for roughly 10,000 Optimus robots to be built in 2025 and that he was confident several thousand would be "doing useful things" by year-end. The article notes it previously reported Tesla never came close to producing thousands of Optimus units in 2025, with no evidence of even hundreds built, and cites unspecified supply-chain reports describing the program as being in "shambles," including the departure of the program's head and production delays.

3. **Gen 3 Optimus plans:** Tesla's shareholder update said it plans to unveil the Gen 3 Optimus in Q1 2026, featuring major upgrades over version 2.5 including a new hand design, and described as Tesla's first design intended for mass production. Tesla said it is preparing production lines with start of production planned before the end of 2026 and an eventual planned capacity of 1 million robots per year.

## Editorial Stance
Fred Lambert says he is personally bullish on humanoid robots generally and sees Tesla as having compute/AI development advantages, but argues Tesla is repeating its self-driving pattern of building hardware (citing roughly $20 billion in capex) before the software is ready, and says he doesn't trust Musk's credibility on this effort given the gap between past claims and the current admission of zero useful deployment. A reader comment included in the article pushes back on Lambert's claim of Tesla having a compute/AI advantage, saying no source was given and that longtime robotics competitors may have better capability.
