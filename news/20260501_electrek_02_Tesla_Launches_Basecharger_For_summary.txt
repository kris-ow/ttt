Tesla has launched a "Semi Charging for Business" program enabling fleet operators to purchase and install two distinct Semi charging products: the existing Megacharger and a new lower-power Basecharger. The program includes transparent pricing via an online configurator and a per-kWh fee structure for revenue-generating sites.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Two-product Semi charging lineup:** Tesla is offering the Megacharger (up to 1.2 MW, adds ~60% range in 30 minutes) for en-route fast charging and the new Basecharger (125 kW, adds ~60% range in four hours) for depot and overnight use. Both use the open MCS 3.2 standard and support ISO 15118-2 and OCPI protocols — not locked to Tesla's ecosystem. Payment terminals are optional on both units.

## Energy
2. **Megacharger pricing and specs:** The minimum purchase is two Megacharger posts, starting at $188,000 excluding taxes and installation. The cabinet delivers 1,200 kW shared across two posts, weighs 1,100 kg, and operates at greater than 96% efficiency with cable runs up to 100 meters. The Megacharger is already being deployed, with 66 planned locations and the first public station open in Ontario, California.

3. **Basecharger pricing and availability:** The Basecharger starts at $40,000 — a compact 100 kg unit (340 × 1200 × 2000 mm) with a 6-meter cable, suited for depot layouts. Deliveries are estimated to begin in early 2027. For revenue-generating sites, Tesla charges $0.08/kWh all-inclusive — lower than the $0.10/kWh fee on the Supercharger for Business program.

## Market & Competition
4. **Competitive landscape and Tesla's positioning:** Kempower has deployed MCS-capable infrastructure in California (up to 1.2 MW at EV Realty's hub in San Bernardino); ABB and ChargePoint have announced MCS support but have not deployed at scale in the US. Tesla's vertical integration — building trucks, chargers, and operating a network — is its stated differentiator. MCS 3.2 compliance means Daimler, Volvo, and Scania MCS-compatible trucks could theoretically use Tesla's hardware.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek views the Basecharger as the more strategically significant product, arguing depot charging has been the bigger bottleneck for fleet electrification. The author notes the $0.08/kWh network fee is higher than Tesla's original 2017 claim of $0.07/kWh total electricity cost for Semi customers, which Electrek had previously flagged as unrealistic.
