This video features a roundtable discussion among hosts on the Cern Basher channel analyzing what FSD version 14.3 means for Tesla's robotaxi rollout plans. The core thesis is that 14.3 — delayed roughly three months from its originally expected January release — represents a 10X parameter increase and may be the critical unlock needed to accelerate unsupervised robotaxi deployments, expand to new cities, and begin ramping Cybercab production. The panel argues that the slope of the ramp matters far more than the starting point, and draws parallels and contrasts with Waymo's own early deployment history.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD 14.3 delayed but now imminent:** FSD version 14.3 was originally expected to release in January but was delayed approximately three months. As of the time of recording (late April 2026), the hosts indicate it is very close to wide release — possibly by end of the week. The delay is attributed to the complexity of training a neural network at this scale, which the hosts describe as an inherent challenge of AI development that resists precise timelines. Elon Musk himself described 14.3 as potentially "sentient," which the hosts flag as notable but leave for viewers to evaluate once the version is publicly available.

2. **10X parameter increase as the key milestone:** The hosts emphasize that 14.3 represents a 10X increase in parameters relative to the prior version, characterizing it as a "magnitude jump" and potentially the final major piece of the puzzle for enabling robust unsupervised operation. They suggest this is the primary reason Tesla held back from scaling unsupervised vehicles from roughly 8 to larger numbers — waiting for this version to be ready before aggressively expanding the fleet.

3. **Supervised-first city launch strategy confirmed:** Elon Musk stated as recently as January 22nd that every new city launch would begin with a supervised person in the vehicle for approximately three months to identify and address unusual intersections and edge cases. The hosts acknowledge this as prudent but speculate that the supervised period will shorten over time as Tesla accumulates transferable knowledge across cities — for example, training in Austin may reduce the effort needed to launch Dallas or Houston given similar road layouts across Texas.

4. **FSD data advantage in identifying problem areas:** The hosts note that Tesla's massive FSD fleet data should already identify where the challenging intersections and edge cases are in new cities before boots are even on the ground. This means Tesla can pre-focus training on known problem spots rather than discovering them from scratch during supervised city launches.

## Robotaxi

1. **Unsupervised scaling expected to accelerate sharply post-14.3:** The hosts express that the current ~8 unsupervised vehicles in Austin is not indicative of Tesla's intent — rather, Tesla was deliberately waiting for 14.3 before scaling up. Post-release, they expect a rapid ramp from 8 to 30, 60, and then 200+ unsupervised vehicles. The key framing is that the starting number is irrelevant; what matters is the slope of the ramp.

2. **Multiple cities preparing to launch robotaxi service:** The hosts reference at least four cities where Tesla is visibly preparing to launch the robotaxi service, with five cities mentioned as targets Tesla said it would announce by June 2026. These launches are expected to begin supervised, with hired operators in the vehicles. The hosts also note that the same hired operators can later transition to fleet maintenance roles — cleaning cars, charging at night, working across roughly 15 vehicles rather than being tied to one — making the supervised phase more economically scalable.

3. **Bay Area unsupervised presence noted:** The hosts mention that Tesla has approximately 450 vehicles operating in the Bay Area, though the context implies these are supervised or mixed-use rather than all fully unsupervised robotaxi deployments. This is cited as part of Tesla's early presence-building phase.

4. **Cybercab production expected to begin this quarter:** One host states that Cybercab production is expected to start this quarter (Q2 2026), and that Cybercab deployment and the unsupervised vehicle ramp are expected to come together during this period. The hosts argue that Cybercabs driving themselves with no human visible will be a powerful marketing moment that captures broad public attention.

5. **Waymo historical comparison:** The hosts draw a detailed comparison to Waymo's trajectory. Waymo began supervised rides in Phoenix in December 2018 with a supervisor actually behind the wheel, transitioned to pulling supervisors out in October 2020 (nearly two years later), and approximately nine months into unsupervised operation had around 500–600 robotaxis across two cities. The hosts note Tesla has only been running its service for approximately nine months at the time of the video. The key differentiator the hosts anticipate is that Tesla's ramp slope will be dramatically steeper than Waymo's — at some point in 2026, Tesla is projected to produce as many robotaxis in a single month as Waymo's entire fleet, and within six months of that point, as many in a single week.

## Market & Competition

1. **Federal regulatory framework seen as critical need:** The hosts express strong interest in federal executive action to establish consistent autonomous vehicle standards across states, arguing that a patchwork of state-level rules creates friction and legal exposure. They specifically call for federal protections making it a serious offense to vandalize or harm autonomous vehicles, referencing incidents involving Waymo vehicles. They frame AV adoption as a national competitiveness issue, arguing that lower transportation costs would reduce CPI and free up consumer spending.
