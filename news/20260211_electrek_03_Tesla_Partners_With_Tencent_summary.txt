Tencent Cloud announced a partnership with Tesla to integrate WeChat-linked features into over 1 million Model 3 and Model Y vehicles built at Giga Shanghai, via an over-the-air update, marking a shift toward deeper local software integration in China.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **WeChat integration features:** The update brings two features — "WeChat Connectivity," which lets drivers tap a location in a WeChat chat to send it directly to the car's navigation without manual re-entry, and "Destination Services," which uses Tencent AI to suggest nearby restaurants, parking, or charging based on the destination, served through WeChat Mini Programmes with WeChat Pay support on the dashboard. Tencent Cloud said it is exploring further features, including voice-to-text routing from WeChat conversations.
2. **Tesla's broader China software stack:** Tesla has built a network of Chinese tech partnerships distinct from its Western approach: Baidu for HD lane-level navigation, NetEase and QQ Music for entertainment, and ByteDance/DeepSeek for its "Hey Tesla" voice assistant (necessary since Grok cannot be used in China due to regulations). The article notes Tesla integrated DeepSeek and Doubao AI into China-market cars last year.
3. **Tesla is late to this integration:** Tencent's Intelligent Automobile Cloud already serves nearly 40 Chinese automotive brands (including BMW), and its WeChat-for-vehicles service, launched in 2019, was already available across 15 automakers (including Li Auto, Audi, BMW) by 2022 according to CnEVPost — meaning Tesla is catching up to an industry baseline, per the article.
4. **Tencent's prior Tesla stake:** Tencent took a 5% stake in Tesla in 2017, reportedly worth over $2.2 billion at the time.
5. **Tesla AI training launch:** The article notes Tesla launched AI training capability in China "just last week," though its FSD approval ambitions remain stalled after China reportedly pushed back on Elon Musk's claim that approval was imminent.

## Market & Competition
1. **Tesla's China sales decline:** Tesla confirmed its first year of domestic sales decline in 2025, with Giga Shanghai wholesale deliveries falling 7% year-over-year — an acceleration from a 3.2% decline between 2023 and 2024.
2. **Rival momentum:** BYD surpassed Tesla in all-electric sales for 2025 and secured the global BEV crown, and is equipping its entire lineup—down to the roughly $10,000 Seagull—with advanced driver-assistance systems. Xpeng is rolling out autonomous driving software described as approaching Level 4. Xiaomi posted 175% sales growth in 2025.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert calls the WeChat partnership the right move but "years overdue," arguing WeChat functions as the operating system of daily life in China and that Tesla's prior lack of seamless WeChat integration was embarrassing for a company that prides itself on software; he questions whether the move will help reverse Tesla's declining China demand amid intense competition.
