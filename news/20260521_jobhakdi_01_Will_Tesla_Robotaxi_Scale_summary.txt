Jo Bhakdi hosts Cern Basher for a deep-dive discussion on the pace and timing of Tesla's robotaxi scaling, centered on the question of whether the unsupervised Cybercab fleet will reach 10,000 vehicles by end of 2026. The core thesis is that Cybercab production volume is the most reliable leading indicator of imminent deployment, and that Tesla is likely engineering a "switch flip" moment — moving from a few hundred validation vehicles to thousands of deployed robotaxis nearly overnight — rather than a smooth, linear ramp. Both guests converge on a year-end 2026 target of 10,000+ robotaxis, with Q3 2026 seen as a potential inflection point.

## Robotaxi

1. **"Switch Flip" vs. Linear Ramp Theory:** Cern Basher argues that what many observers interpret as repeated robotaxi delays is actually Tesla deliberately accumulating readiness — in software, production, and regulatory positioning — for a large-scale simultaneous deployment. Rather than a month-by-month stair-step increase, Cern expects a sudden jump from a few hundred vehicles to potentially tens of thousands. He points to Cybercabs being tested in states without favorable robotaxi legislation (including Alaska and rural areas) as evidence Tesla is preparing for a broad national deployment, not just incremental city-by-city rollout.

2. **Cybercab Production as the Primary Leading Indicator:** Both hosts agree that Cybercab manufacturing volume is the most trackable and reliable signal of deployment timing. Cern estimates that hundreds of Cybercabs have already been manufactured within the first month of production, and projects output could reach thousands per month within the next three months. He reasons that Tesla would not ramp production to 1,000+ units per month and then park them indefinitely — the capital cost of doing so would be prohibitive and contrary to Tesla's operational discipline. Therefore, a sustained production ramp with no corresponding deployment would itself be a warning sign.

3. **Steering Wheel Removal as a Deployment Signal:** Cern identifies the transition away from steering-wheel-equipped validation Cybercabs to production units without steering wheels as a critical near-term signal. Validation vehicles (described as having messy, truck-style steering wheels) are currently being used for testing across the country. Once Tesla stops manufacturing Cybercabs with steering wheels entirely, Cern views that as a near-certain indicator that commercial driverless deployment is imminent.

4. **Current Unsupervised Fleet and City Expansion:** The discussion references the existing small unsupervised robotaxi fleet operating in Austin, Dallas, and Houston (with Austin as the primary market), noting the count is currently around 39 unsupervised vehicles per RobotaxiTracker.com. Cern expects additional cities — including Miami, Tampa, Orlando, Las Vegas, and Phoenix — to see some form of robotaxi rollout before year-end 2026, though initial deployments in new cities may start with only five or six vehicles, potentially still supervised.

5. **Timeline Predictions — Q3 vs. Q4 for Scale:** Jo Bhakdi expresses conviction that Q3 2026 (July–September) is a more likely inflection point than Q4 for reaching a meaningful unsupervised fleet, while Cern assigns higher probability to Q4 for hitting ~1,000 unsupervised vehicles, citing unknown FSD issue resolution timelines. However, both agree that if 1,000 unsupervised units are reached in Q3, then by Q4 Tesla could already have close to 10,000 Cybercabs manufactured and ready. Cern's stated prediction is 10,000+ robotaxis in service by end of 2026, a figure Jo and a third commentator referenced as "Herbert" also share.

6. **From 1,000 to 10,000 — Speed of the Next Phase:** Cern argues that once 1,000 unsupervised robotaxis are operational, the jump to 10,000 could happen very rapidly — primarily constrained by Cybercab production rates rather than software or regulatory factors. He anticipates the fleet will shift toward predominantly Cybercabs over time, with Model Y representation in the fleet declining, assuming the Cybercab passes ongoing validation without major hardware issues.

7. **Infrastructure as a Potential Gating Factor:** The hosts raise the question of whether city-specific infrastructure — such as the car wash facility being built in Las Vegas for vehicle cleaning — must be operational before Tesla is comfortable scaling in a given market. Cern notes this is an open question: if Tesla requires dedicated cleaning and servicing infrastructure before each city launch, that introduces an additional milestone that could pace geographic expansion.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Software Progress and the Convenience Gap:** Cern references Elon Musk's comments from a recent earnings call stating that safety for robotaxi operation has been solved and the remaining work is about "convenience" — refining behaviors like pickup and drop-off, handling parking lot slow-speed scenarios, and managing passenger comfort edge cases (e.g., finding covered parking in rain). A newer FSD version, described as version 14.3.3, is noted as a meaningful improvement over the prior 14.3.2 release. Cern believes the software will reach sufficient convenience quality within one to two months, without needing to wait for a version 15 release.

2. **Robotaxi Software Leads Consumer FSD:** Cern notes that the software running on robotaxi vehicles is presumed to be one development build ahead of what consumer FSD owners receive. He points to firsthand experience with consumer FSD improvements as evidence that progress is real and accelerating, arguing it would be a different situation entirely if FSD had not improved materially over the past year.

## Market & Competition

1. **Tesla's Valuation Depends Heavily on Robotaxi and Optimus:** Jo Bhakdi frames the current Tesla stock price (referenced as being around $410–$490) as unsustainable without robotaxi and Optimus delivering on their thesis. He argues that stripping out those two catalysts leaves Tesla's valuation difficult to justify, making the timing of robotaxi deployment critically important not just for bulls but for anyone managing short-to-medium-term capital risk.

2. **Institutional Investor Behavior and Stock Reaction Thresholds:** Both hosts discuss how institutional investors cannot afford to hold Tesla through multi-month delays while other assets (e.g., Nvidia) outperform. Cern and Jo theorize that the unsupervised robotaxi count reaching triple digits (100+) will start generating stock reactions to fleet size milestones, with four-digit counts (1,000+) making it irrational for large capital to remain underweight Tesla. Cern floats the possibility that the stock could surge 10–20% in a single day before the public sees evidence on the roads, driven by informed institutional positioning ahead of a deployment announcement.

3. **Macro Risk Acknowledged:** Jo raises geopolitical and macro risks — specifically referencing potential oil price volatility tied to Iran — as factors that could pressure Tesla's stock in the near term regardless of robotaxi progress, creating buying opportunities for investors who track Cybercab production closely enough to have conviction during drawdowns.
