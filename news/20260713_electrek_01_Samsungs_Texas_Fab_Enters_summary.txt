Samsung has reportedly completed tape-out of Tesla's AI5 self-driving chip and is preparing to produce it on its newest 2-nanometer process at its Taylor, Texas fab, according to a since-deleted LinkedIn post by Samsung Foundry engineer James Kim, a detail the article says was unexpected since 2nm was previously assumed to be reserved for the next-generation AI6 chip.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **AI5 tape-out confirmed at Samsung:** James Kim, a principal engineer at Samsung Foundry, wrote that the Tesla-Samsung AI5 chip has reached tape-out and is scheduled for manufacture at the Taylor fab on Samsung's latest 2nm process, soon to be integrated into Tesla's newest products; he deleted the post after Korean media picked it up. The article explains tape-out locks the chip design before it proceeds through prevalidation, photomask production, and wafer fabrication, then customer qualification before mass production.

2. **Dual-foundry design confirmed earlier by Musk:** The article notes it reported in April that Musk said Tesla had taped out AI5 to both Samsung and TSMC, with each foundry producing slightly different versions because they translate designs to physical form differently. Musk has also shown a Samsung-made AI5 prototype marked "KR 2613," indicating Korean production in the 13th week of 2026, evidence Samsung's Korean lines were producing engineering samples before Taylor took over.

3. **2nm node surprise and yield implications:** The article states the prior industry and its own assumption was that Samsung's 2nm line was tied to AI6, with AI5 expected on more mature nodes at TSMC; Kim's post indicates AI5 itself is going on 2nm at Taylor. The article says this supports market assumptions that Samsung's 2nm yield has crossed roughly 60%, a threshold viewed as the point where the process becomes viable for a high-volume customer like Tesla — the article frames this as an inference, not a confirmed figure. It also references earlier reporting that AI6 had slipped about six months due to Samsung 2nm yield issues, pushing AI6 mass production to Q4 2027 at the earliest.

4. **Timeline and hardware transition milestones:** Samsung held an equipment installation ceremony at Taylor in April and has said it expects volume production of Tesla's AI chips there in the second half of 2027. The article cites Musk as previously saying Tesla needs several hundred thousand completed AI5 boards line-side before switching vehicle production over, with that volume not expected until mid-2027; it also notes Cybercab is currently launching on older AI4 hardware and that an AI4.5 stopgap computer was added to new Model Ys because AI5 kept slipping.

## Market & Competition
1. **Samsung's stakes in Taylor fab:** The article frames Tesla's AI5 as a needed proof point for Samsung, which has trailed TSMC on advanced-node yield and made a multibillion-dollar bet on the Taylor fab while searching for anchor customers. It cites recent reports suggesting Anthropic could manufacture its own AI chips through Samsung Foundry, tying improving yields to Samsung's ability to attract more customers of that caliber; Samsung declined to comment on customer-related matters.

## Editorial Stance
The author (Fred Lambert) argues that while improving Samsung 2nm yield is good news for a real second source to TSMC, tape-out and early production remain milestones rather than a finish line for Tesla, noting AI5 is arriving nearly two years after Musk first said it would be in vehicles and drawing a skeptical parallel to past unmet claims about HW3 and AI4 achieving unsupervised autonomy at scale.
