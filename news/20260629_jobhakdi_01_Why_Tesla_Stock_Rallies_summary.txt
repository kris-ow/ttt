Jo Bhakdi hosts a live session reacting to Tesla's roughly 8–9% single-day stock rally on June 29, 2026, which he attributes primarily to Morgan Stanley raising its Q2 2026 delivery estimate rather than to Cybercab news. He argues the rally simply returns the stock to where it was the previous Monday after what he describes as an unjustified drop, and uses the session to walk through Cybercab scaling signals, his options positioning, and his continued conviction that Tesla is approaching a major re-pricing event tied to robotaxi deployment.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Morgan Stanley delivery upgrade as the primary rally driver:** Jo Bhakdi is confident the day's outsized move was driven by Morgan Stanley raising its Q2 2026 Tesla delivery forecast from approximately 373,000 vehicles to approximately 413,000 vehicles, citing stronger-than-expected sales trends in Europe and China. He notes Europe was up 100% year-over-year and China was also tracking positively. He stresses that Morgan Stanley's distribution reach — covering a large share of institutional fund clients — translates a research note directly into buy orders from investors who may not follow Tesla granularly.

2. **Deliveries now priced in:** Bhakdi cautions viewers not to expect the delivery beat to continue driving the stock after the numbers are officially released, warning of a potential "sell the news" dynamic. He believes the Morgan Stanley upgrade has already front-run the actual delivery print.

## Robotaxi
1. **Texas first-responder interaction plans filed and integrated:** Bhakdi reports that Texas has now officially integrated connected autonomous vehicle first-responder interaction plans into the Texas Department of Public Safety page, mirroring similar documentation already filed for Arizona. He describes these regulatory filings as a necessary precondition for Cybercab commercial launch and notes the effort required to produce and integrate them is a meaningful scaling signal, even if it does not guarantee an imminent launch.

2. **Lars Moravy teases a July 8 announcement tied to Giga Texas scaling:** Bhakdi plays a clip of Lars Moravy stating that approximately one week from the recording — identified by Bhakdi as Tuesday, July 7, 2026 (after the July 4th weekend) — there will be "cool news" about things happening on the Giga Texas campus related to the scaling effort, though Moravy declines to elaborate. Bhakdi acknowledges he does not know what the announcement is and says it could be something relatively modest such as a factory expansion update rather than a Cybercab commercial launch.

3. **Cybercab inventory building in Dallas and Houston:** Bhakdi cites Sawyer Merritt reporting over 70 Cybercabs now visible in a parking lot in Dallas, and notes similar accumulation has been observed in Houston. He also shows footage from Joe Tegtmeyer of Cybercabs rolling off the Giga Texas production line daily. Bhakdi interprets the growing inventory stockpiles as evidence that a deployment event is being prepared, arguing the accumulation is not sustainable indefinitely without an operational purpose.

4. **Cybercab commercial launch timeline — high conviction on July, missed May and June:** Bhakdi acknowledges he predicted a Cybercab launch in May and then in June, both of which did not materialize. He explicitly states he missed his June target (which included a $490 price target). He argues, however, that each missed month raises the probability of the next month, and he now has his highest conviction yet that something significant happens in July. He does not give a specific date. He believes today's rally had nothing to do with Cybercab and that Cybercab news therefore remains unpriced, giving further upside potential.

5. **Re-pricing thresholds and price targets:** Bhakdi outlines his view that any significant Cybercab deployment above roughly 20 units operating commercially could push Tesla to all-time highs. He believes a fleet in the range of a few thousand — with a clear trajectory toward tens of thousands — would trigger a major re-pricing toward $700 and potentially overshoot to $1,000 or beyond. He speculates that 10,000 Cybercabs could be reached within three months but acknowledges it might not occur until December. For a longer horizon, he estimates Tesla could reach $1,800–$2,000 by end of 2027.

## Market & Competition
1. **NASDAQ recovery and Iran de-escalation as macro tailwinds:** Bhakdi notes the NASDAQ was up approximately 2–2.8% on the day, which he attributes to continued de-escalation of the Iran conflict. He frames Tesla's ~8–9% move as roughly double the NASDAQ's beta-adjusted expected return, with the excess driven by the Morgan Stanley delivery upgrade.

2. **Short-term consolidation risk flagged:** Despite his bullishness, Bhakdi switches from long calls (which he says returned roughly 100% on August-expiration $380 strikes bought at approximately $25 during the prior week's dip) back to short puts at strikes of $400, $405, $407.50, $410, and $412. He argues consolidation or a modest pullback is possible in the near term as the delivery news gets priced in and the NASDAQ potentially digests recent gains.

3. **California AB 1777 remote-operator requirement — not a major concern:** In response to a viewer question, Bhakdi dismisses California's AB 1777 legislation requiring remote operators for autonomous vehicles as a minor operational issue. He argues a single remote operator could be on call for a very large fleet and would simply be contacted if a robotaxi is pulled over by police, which he does not view as a meaningful business obstacle.
