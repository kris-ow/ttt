This Munro Live interview features Jason Hill of Munro speaking with Ian Coke, Vice President of Technical Strategy and Customer Relations in North America for Pirelli, about the engineering complexity and innovation behind modern tire technology. The conversation covers how mega-trends in the automotive industry — including EV adoption, vehicle weight increases, torque demands, noise reduction, and sustainability — are driving continuous innovation in tire design, materials science, and manufacturing. The core thesis is that tires are far more technologically sophisticated than most consumers realize, and that Pirelli's position at the premium, high-value end of the market is driven by its engineering capabilities, motorsport participation, and growing use of AI and simulation.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **EV-Specific Tire Challenges — Weight, Torque, and Rolling Resistance:** Ian Coke explains that EVs and hybrids present multiple simultaneous demands on tires: significantly greater vehicle weight (requiring higher load ratings, including "extra load" and "high load" ratings), dramatically higher torque requiring strong grip, the need for ultra-low rolling resistance to maximize battery range, and increased noise sensitivity due to the absence of a combustion engine. Coke notes these demands are often in direct conflict — a stiffer, stronger construction needed to support weight works against ride comfort, while maximizing grip can compromise rolling resistance.

2. **Early EV Tire Wear Concerns:** Coke notes that tire wear was not a high priority for early EV OEMs, which led to real-world wear rates that surprised consumers. He contrasts expected EV tire life — some early tires wearing out at 15,000–20,000 miles — against typical expectations of 40,000–50,000 miles for conventional tires. He states this has since changed, and wear is now a more deliberate design consideration, addressed through "block stability" in tread design and improved compounding that balances mileage with wet braking and rolling resistance.

3. **Noise Cancellation Technology for EVs:** With combustion engine noise eliminated, road and tire noise became newly prominent in EVs. Coke explains Pirelli had already been developing noise cancellation systems before the EV era (originally prompted by increasingly quiet luxury vehicles like Mercedes, BMW 7 Series, and Maybach), making the technology effectively plug-and-play for EV applications. The system works by breaking up the acoustic drum effect inside the tire.

## Market & Competition
1. **Pirelli's Premium Market Positioning — High-Value Technology Focus:** Coke describes Pirelli as a technology-driven premium brand, with approximately 95–96% of its production in what the company calls "high-value" tires — defined as 18-inch and above — encompassing ultra-high performance, noise cancellation systems, ultra-low rolling resistance, and mobility solutions. He contrasts this with mass-market competitors who produce hundreds of millions of tires, while Pirelli produces approximately 70 million tires annually but focuses on highly complex, small lot sizes.

2. **OEM Homologation — Bespoke Tires Per Vehicle Model:** A key differentiator Coke emphasizes is that Pirelli develops tires specifically homologated for individual vehicle models. Even if two different vehicles use the same tire size, their specifications will typically differ because the tire is tuned for that specific vehicle's lateral force, longitudinal traction, footprint, and performance targets. He uses BMW's "star mark" sidewall symbol as a consumer-visible example of this practice.

3. **Motorsport as a Technology Engine:** Pirelli has been a Formula 1 supplier for over 15 years, also participates in the World Rally Series, and produces motorcycle tires with plans to take over GP motorcycle tire supply the following year (implying 2027 per the publish date of 2026-06-04). Coke is explicit that the primary value of motorsport participation is not marketing but the engineering capability it forces — specifically the ability to design, simulate, test, and iterate rapidly under extreme conditions. He cites a dedicated tire engineer per F1 team, real-time telemetry monitoring, and the need to model dimensional rule changes before manufacturing 30,000–35,000 tires per season as examples of the pace and rigor involved.

4. **AI and Virtual Simulation — From Compound Design to Track Performance:** Pirelli invested in a dedicated AI and digital center in Bari, southern Italy, approximately five years ago (implying around 2021), staffed by approximately 50 engineers focused on AI and simulation. This center enables fully virtual tire development: compounds are designed using AI based on vehicle and tire attributes, a complete dynamic virtual tire is created, and then tested under a virtual vehicle model in various conditions (loads, pressures, temperatures). Coke estimates this process brings development approximately 90% of the way to the final physical tire before any physical prototype is built. He acknowledges that modeling snow and mud remains difficult, while other surfaces simulate more accurately.

5. **Connected and Sensorized Tires — The Next Frontier:** Coke identifies tire connectivity and sensorization as what he views as the most exciting near-future development. Beyond basic TPMS, embedded sensors can proactively track temperature and pressure in real time, measure the actual contact patch, detect aquaplaning, and feed data to vehicle systems to improve ABS reaction time and alter suspension dynamically. Pirelli has signed a cooperation agreement with Bosch (which Coke says supplies approximately 70–75% of onboard systems for major vehicle manufacturers) to enable tire sensor data to integrate with vehicle operating systems. Coke also envisions V2V (vehicle-to-vehicle) communication via 5G enabling a sensorized tire's real-time road condition data (e.g., a water patch) to be broadcast to surrounding vehicles. He notes that real-time data upload frequency remains an unresolved challenge.

6. **Sustainability Materials Innovation:** Coke states Pirelli was the first tire manufacturer — and specifically first in the US market — to use FSC (Forest Stewardship Council) certified natural rubber. Pirelli now produces an ultra-high-performance product with more than 75% biodegradable and recyclable materials, incorporating recovered carbon black from tire pyrolysis, recovered metal, and lignin as a sidewall filler reinforcement. Coke frames this as technologically and resource-driven rather than purely marketing-driven, noting that no consumer in their market has ever paid a premium specifically for sustainability.

7. **3D Printing-Inspired Manufacturing and Adaptive Tread Design:** Pirelli uses a strip-injection construction method analogous to 3D printing, building tires up in precise rubber strips to place material exactly where needed — addressing the challenge that rubber does not always conform accurately when extruded conventionally. This enables rapid design changes and small production lot sizes suited to Pirelli's bespoke, low-volume approach. On the tread design side, Coke describes "3D siping" used in winter and all-season tires, where the tread is engineered to increase the contact patch surface area as it wears, maintaining or even improving grip as the tire ages — counterintuitive to most consumers who assume worn tires always grip less.
