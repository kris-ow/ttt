This episode of a SpaceX/Tesla-focused podcast breaks down SpaceX's first quarterly earnings call as a public company, covering segment financials, Starship's flight 13 progress, the Starmine/Starlink compute buildout, and AI revenue disclosures, before pivoting to an extended thesis on Optimus as a future "companion device" whose intelligence lives in SpaceX's Starmine compute network delivered via Starlink — arguing Tesla and SpaceX are converging into effectively one ecosystem.

## Financials
1. **Segment breakdown from Q2 earnings:** Space division revenue was just under $1 billion with an operating loss of about half a billion; connectivity (Starlink) revenue was about $4.2–4.3 billion with $1.66 billion operating income; AI revenue was about $2.5 billion with a $1.2 billion operating loss (adjusted EBITDA positive ~$1.15 billion). Total company revenue was $7.8 billion for the quarter, with roughly $3.5 billion adjusted EBITDA and total capex of $18.3 billion, of which $15.8 billion was AI-side capex.
2. **CFO frames AI capex as near-COGS:** SpaceX's CFO said "not all capex is the same," describing AI compute capex as achieving payback in under a year — "almost bucket-able as COGS" — versus traditional decades-long infrastructure capex like launch sites and factories. The hosts argue the market hasn't yet recognized this distinction and conflates SpaceX's AI capex with the broader hyperscaler capex worries.
3. **$100B ARR trajectory and revenue mix disclosed:** SpaceX said it's on a trajectory (including Cursor) to reach $100 billion annualized recurring revenue by the end of this year, which management said is "not a question mark" — already booked. Disclosed contributors: Anthropic at 15% (~$1.25B/month), Alphabet at ~11% (~$1B/month), and Reflection AI at 2% (~$150M/month); the remaining 72% (~$6B/month) is unannounced.
4. **Trillion-dollar revenue timeline pulled forward:** Elon Musk said SpaceX's internal projection for reaching $1 trillion in revenue moved up from 2031 (pre-IPO projection) to 2030, with a "nonzero chance" of 2029. The host calculated the implied compound annual growth rates from $18.7 billion in 2025 revenue: ~94%/year to hit the target by 2031, ~122%/year by 2030, or ~170%/year by 2029 — growth rates he called "absolutely staggering" and unmatched by any other company he's aware of, while noting this remains a company projection.

## SpaceX — Starship
1. **Catch attempt possibly as soon as next flight:** Musk indicated on the call that SpaceX could attempt to catch the ship (second stage) as soon as the next Starship flight, sooner than many expected, with plans to catch both stages of the Starlink V3-carrying Starship this year. The hosts noted flight 13 showed the heat-shield tiles held up well with only limited damage despite a harder ascent profile, and that recovery footage showed a high degree of automated control over the ship's descent.
2. **Mass-to-orbit growth ambitions:** Musk said SpaceX will deliver 80–90% of total Earth mass to orbit this year via Falcon (about 2,500 tons), with long-term ambitions to scale from 2,500 tons/year to potentially 1–10 million tons/year — which he called "a ridiculously profound difference."
3. **Flight 13 aftermath:** The booster was lost, but the rest of the mission was deemed a success; the ship reportedly splashed down intact and is being towed, likely back toward port for tile inspection, with hosts speculating (unconfirmed) it may eventually be transported by heavy-lift cargo ship.

## SpaceX — AI & Compute
1. **Compute buildout targets:** SpaceX said it expects to end this year with over 2 gigawatts of compute, with cumulative compute potentially reaching close to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027 (or mid-2028 per one host reference) — described as purely terrestrial, separate from the orbital Starmine concept. Musk separately referenced longer-term terrestrial power targets of 15–20 gigawatts.
2. **Known compute assets vs. total target:** Existing named compute assets include Colossus 1 (340 MW), Colossus 2 (~1 GW), and two ~350 MW "Macrohard"-related builds, leaving roughly 8 GW of the ~10 GW target still unaccounted for and unbuilt, which the hosts called "staggering."
3. **Shared hardware stack with Nvidia:** Management indicated the compute architecture being developed for terrestrial AI data centers is designed to double as the deployable unit for the Starmine orbital compute platform, differing mainly in cooling/heat-radiation method. Hosts framed this as a symbiotic Nvidia relationship today, with a future transition to SpaceX/Tesla-designed chips (referred to as a "D3" chip, produced via Terafab).
4. **Memory as the binding constraint:** Musk said the limiting factor for AI growth is currently memory, with output increasing ~20%/year while demand is growing an estimated 200%+/year, implying rising rather than falling compute/memory prices.

## SpaceX — Starlink
1. **Starlink to eventually carry a majority of world internet traffic:** Musk said it's plausible that within under 10 years, Starlink will deliver a majority of the world's internet traffic in countries where it's permitted to operate, aided by major bandwidth gains in newer Starlink V3 satellites (hosts estimate a large but unconfirmed multiple over V2 in downlink bandwidth) tested via around 20 V3 satellites deployed (suborbitally) on the recent Starship flight.
2. **Femtocell/direct-to-cell strategy discussed by Gwynne Shotwell and Musk:** Shotwell described a plan to deploy small "femtocell" Starlink-connected stations on rooftops to provide mobile connectivity without large upfront lowband spectrum capex; Musk elaborated that this could offer better bandwidth than existing cellular providers. One host proposed (his own speculative idea, not confirmed by SpaceX) that Cybercabs and Tesla vehicles — already reported to include Starlink terminals — could serve as mobile femtocell platforms, creating dense urban coverage given their concentration where people are.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Optimus framed as the "next device," with its mind residing in Starmine:** A host laid out an extended thesis (his own framework, not a company statement) that Optimus — rather than wearables or phones — is the next major consumer computing platform, arguing its "mind" will live persistently in SpaceX's Starmine compute network and be delivered via Starlink, with the physical robot and phone as thin edge/companion devices with round-trip latency estimated around 15 milliseconds.
2. **Economic case for in-home Optimus:** The argument is that Optimus's value at home can be measured against existing spend on yard work, cleaning, elder care, nannies, and takeout/restaurant spending — with the suggestion that in-home cooking via Optimus could substitute for restaurant visits entirely.
3. **Shared/leased Optimus model discussed:** Hosts discussed the idea that Optimus need not be individually owned — a single physical unit could be shared across a neighborhood or leased by the hour (e.g., delivered by Cybercab for a few hours), with the "mind"/context following the user rather than the physical body.
4. **Brand and design risk flagged for Tesla:** One host argued Tesla faces a real branding challenge in making Optimus feel approachable rather than "sinister" or "utopian," contrasting Apple's strength in mainstream consumer branding, and noting Tesla's naming conventions (Cybercab, Cybertruck) skew less approachable than "Optimus" itself, which he felt carries positive connotations.
