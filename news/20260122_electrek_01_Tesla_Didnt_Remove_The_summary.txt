Electrek disputes Elon Musk's claim that Tesla "removed the safety monitor" from Robotaxi vehicles in Austin, arguing video evidence shows the monitors were simply relocated to a trailing chase car rather than eliminated.

## Robotaxi
1. **Musk's announcement and market reaction:** Musk posted on X that Tesla "just started Tesla Robotaxi drives in Austin with no safety monitor in the car," and Tesla's stock jumped over 4% on the news, with most of the gain coming after Musk's post; financial press ran celebratory headlines about the milestone.
2. **Video evidence of trailing cars:** Video posted by Joe Tegtmeyer on X reportedly shows two "unsupervised" Robotaxis in Austin each closely followed by a black Tesla vehicle; the article states these trailing cars "undoubtedly" contain safety monitors ready to intervene, meaning the monitor was moved from inside the Robotaxi to a chase vehicle rather than removed.
3. **Davos "solved autonomy" claim:** The move coincides with Musk claiming on stage in Davos that Tesla has again "solved autonomy" — a claim the article notes Musk has made every year for the past six years.
4. **Top comment on supervision math:** A highlighted reader comment (citizenjs, 62 likes) raises a concern that unless two people are in the trailing car, one supervisor may be monitoring two cars simultaneously, questioning whether that is adequate if both vehicles' FSD software makes the same wrong decision at the same time in similar traffic conditions.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Comparison to Waymo:** The article contrasts Tesla's approach with Waymo, stating Waymo vehicles operate without chase cars across multiple cities and have accumulated over 100 million fully driverless miles, though it notes Waymo still uses some remote teleoperation when vehicles get into trouble.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Musk's statement is "technically true but misleading," contending that a chase-car safety net defeats the purpose of claimed autonomy, is not scalable or cost-effective, and reflects Tesla's awareness that the system isn't ready for genuinely unsupervised operation. The author frames this and past stunts (e.g., a one-time "driverless delivery" never repeated) as marketing moves designed to sustain the narrative that full autonomy is imminent, which the author says is central to Tesla's stock valuation thesis.
