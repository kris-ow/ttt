## Executive Summary
Today's digest is Tesla-heavy, driven by hands-on impressions of the new Model Y L (interior, wheels, FSD build) and a notable in-person comment from Lars Moravy denying any vehicle cap on Cybercab/Tesla robotaxi deployment. Also notable: Tesla hit 10 million vehicles produced, federal regulators signaled a lighter-touch AV regulatory push (with a competing signal from Amazon's Zoox winning steering-wheel-free approval), and SpaceX confirmed its first-ever public-company earnings call next week, run on a custom Grok-built Q&A platform rather than SAY.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **Lars Moravy: No Vehicle Cap on Cybercab/Robotaxi:** In an in-person conversation, Sawyer Merritt says Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy told him directly that Tesla and the Cybercab are "NOT limited by any type of vehicle cap." This is a notable on-record denial of a scaling constraint some investors have worried about for robotaxi fleet expansion, though no further detail (regulatory, manufacturing, or otherwise) was given on what "cap" was being referenced.

2. **Model Y L Hands-On Impressions:** Multiple posts detail firsthand impressions of the new Model Y L trim: significantly improved rear visibility versus the standard Model Y, new 20" "Uberhelix" wheels described as the poster's favorite non-Performance wheel design, a redesigned climate control UI with a row of icons letting front passengers quickly jump between rows, and seat interior/build details shown via photos/video. The test unit was also running an unreleased FSD build, version **14.3.7**, with an accompanying release note shared (specific contents not detailed in the post text).

3. **10 Millionth Vehicle Produced; Giga Nevada 10-Year Anniversary:** Tesla announced it has manufactured its 10 millionth vehicle — a Diamond Black Model Y built at Fremont this week — a major cumulative production milestone. Separately, Tesla marked the 10-year anniversary of the Giga Nevada factory opening.

4. **Tesla Diner Traction:** The Tesla Diner reportedly averages 1,600 charging sessions per day; Merritt visited on a "random Wednesday evening" and found all 80 charging stalls occupied, pushing back on narratives that the location is underperforming ("not dead as some claim").

5. **Arizona Solar/Battery PPA for Superchargers:** Tesla signed a long-term power purchase agreement to buy electricity from a large solar-plus-battery project in Arizona to supply its Supercharger network with additional renewable energy — specifically ~458 MW, or about 90% of the facility's peak solar generation capacity.

6. **AV Regulatory Environment — Mixed Signals:** The Trump Administration, via Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, directed NHTSA to pursue several actions to "cut red tape and accelerate American automated vehicle innovation," including work toward a first-ever AV performance standard (full list of directives cut off in the post). Separately — and somewhat in tension with a "Tesla-favorable" reading — Amazon's Zoox won NHTSA approval for limited commercial deployment of steering-wheel-free robotaxis, described as a first for the industry, with a temporary exemption allowing commercial deployment of up to 2,500 vehicles annually. This signals real competitive movement in the driverless robotaxi space alongside the deregulatory tailwind.

7. **SpaceX's First Public-Company Earnings Call:** SpaceX is now accepting investor questions ahead of its first earnings call as a public company, scheduled for the following week. Notably, the company is bypassing third-party platforms like SAY and instead using its own custom platform built with Grok to collect and manage questions.
