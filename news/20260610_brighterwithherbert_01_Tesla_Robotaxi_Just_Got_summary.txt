This video from the "brighterwithherbert" channel covers several developments relevant to Tesla's robotaxi and autonomous driving thesis, including FSD regulatory approvals in Europe, Texas DMV registrations of Tesla robotaxi vehicles, Cybercab energy efficiency advantages over Waymo, and Waymo's acquisition of Apple's Arizona proving ground. Host Herbert is joined by guest Jo Bhakdi, an economist, investor, and tech entrepreneur, to discuss the pace of robotaxi rollout and what catalysts could drive Tesla's stock repricing.

## Autonomous Driving

1. **FSD Supervised Approved in Denmark:** Tesla announced that FSD supervised was officially approved in Denmark, making it the fourth European country to approve FSD. Belgium has publicly indicated it is coming soon and had expected to be among the first approvals. Jo Bhakdi views this as a predictable domino effect among smaller, more agile European nations, citing Denmark, the Netherlands, and Estonia as the "smartest" early movers. He notes that larger countries like the UK, France, and Germany are expected to lag. An EU-wide vote covering approximately 28 member countries could occur by end of summer 2026, though Jo Bhakdi expressed skepticism about the EU as a body, noting individual countries can proceed independently regardless of an EU-level decision.

2. **Dutch Safety Data Validates FSD Performance:** Tesla Europe published safety data from the Netherlands — the first country to approve FSD supervised — covering the last two months of operation. FSD supervised was shown to be over three times safer than manual driving on Dutch roads, with 3.5 times fewer collisions. Additional metrics included 14.9 times fewer automatic emergency braking events, 8.8 times less harsh acceleration, 7.3 times less harsh braking, and 8 times fewer hard swerves. Jo Bhakdi noted that European roads tend to be narrower and slower than U.S. highways, but still considered this strong validation of the thesis.

## Robotaxi

1. **Texas DMV Registrations Reach 59 Vehicles:** As of the time of recording, 59 Tesla robotaxi vehicles were officially registered with the Texas DMV at the level 4 autonomy classification, with 8 vehicles added in a single day. Herbert credited a community tracker named Spencer for monitoring this data, which is publicly accessible on the DMV's website.

2. **Arizona Expansion Broader Than Expected:** A Tesla robotaxi first responder interaction plan appendix, dated April 2026, lists the planned operating areas for SAE Level 4 autonomy in Arizona. The document includes nine cities, significantly more than the two (Phoenix and Tempe) that most observers had anticipated. Herbert described this as evidence that the Arizona expansion will be considerably larger than the public had assumed.

3. **Cybercab Fleet Spotted in Houston and Seattle:** Drone footage captured by SE Robinson over a Houston fleet lot showed a mix of Model Y robotaxi vehicles and Cybercabs. Herbert also noted that friends in Washington State (Seattle and Bellevue areas) have been spotting Cybercabs throughout the region, consistent with Tesla's previously announced hiring activity in Bellevue. Both hosts interpreted this as ongoing FSD validation testing across multiple states.

4. **Rollout Timing Theory — Minimum Fleet Density Required:** Jo Bhakdi argued that Tesla cannot commercially launch robotaxi service with only a handful of cars per city, as insufficient fleet density leads to long wait times, dissatisfied customers, and losses. He suggested a minimum of 300–500 Cybercabs per city is needed before a credible commercial service can be offered. This supports a thesis that Tesla may need to do a large simultaneous launch — what Cathie Wood described as "all at once" — rather than a gradual drip. Herbert noted that Austin users are currently experiencing 20-minute wait times and frequent car pullbacks, reinforcing the density problem.

5. **Cybercab Manufacturing as the Key Forcing Function:** Jo Bhakdi stated that Cybercab manufacturing scale is the most reliable indicator of when robotaxi rollout will occur, more so than hiring signals or regulatory approvals. His reasoning: Tesla cannot allow thousands of Cybercabs to sit idle without deploying them commercially, so manufacturing ramp creates inherent pressure to launch. He noted Cybercabs spotted on a Dallas parking lot with wheel caps installed — a detail he read as a signal of imminent real-world operations rather than pure testing, since wheel caps would be unnecessary for testing purposes alone.

6. **Stock Repricing Thesis by Fleet Milestone:** Jo Bhakdi laid out a staged stock repricing framework: launching ~24 Cybercabs could push the stock up 10–15% toward $490; reaching ~100 vehicles is the next meaningful catalyst; scaling to 1,000–2,000 is where he expects actual market repricing of Tesla's valuation. He does not believe waiting until Tesla surpasses Waymo's current ~3,700 vehicles is necessary for repricing — he argued that once Tesla's fleet trajectory is clearly exponential (e.g., doubling month over month), analysts and investors will project forward and reprice well before parity is reached.

7. **SpaceX IPO and Merger Speculation:** Herbert raised the theory that Tesla's robotaxi rollout may be timed around the SpaceX IPO (described as occurring "this Friday" relative to the video's publication date of 2026-06-10) and a potential SpaceX-Tesla acquisition or merger. Jo Bhakdi pushed back, stating it would be "highly illegal" to time a product rollout around a merger or IPO, constituting stock manipulation, and that Elon Musk would not take that risk. He acknowledged that Tesla may have exercised caution to avoid a high-profile Cybercab accident before the SpaceX IPO, but maintained that the primary driver of timing is FSD readiness and a minimum testing window of approximately three months from first deployment.

## Market & Competition

1. **Cybercab vs. Waymo Energy Efficiency:** The Waymo I-Pace has a confirmed battery efficiency of 562 watt-hours per mile, equivalent to approximately 60 miles per gallon. The Cybercab operates at 165 watt-hours per mile, equivalent to approximately 204 miles per gallon — making the Cybercab 3.4 times more energy-efficient. Herbert argued this means Tesla would need roughly one-third as many Cybercabs as Waymo vehicles to deliver equivalent service coverage, though Jo Bhakdi noted that Tesla's manufacturing capacity makes fleet count less of a constraint than readiness.

2. **Waymo Acquires Apple's Arizona Proving Ground for $220 Million:** Waymo purchased Apple's former 5,500-acre self-driving car proving ground in Arizona for $220 million. The facility includes a 115-acre city course, a 35-acre vehicle dynamics area, a four-mile oval track, and a freeway course for AV testing. Apple had reportedly spent approximately $10 billion over a decade on its self-driving car program before canceling it. Jo Bhakdi interpreted Waymo's acquisition as evidence that Waymo may not yet have fully functional unsupervised autonomy — arguing that a company with 3,700 real-world vehicles and genuine full self-driving capability would not need a private proving ground at this scale. His theory is that Waymo may be transitioning from a LiDAR-based system to a vision-only neural network, and needs the proving ground to train and test that new stack without the need for human oversight that would be required on public roads.

3. **Waymo Skepticism — Remote Monitoring Hypothesis:** Jo Bhakdi reiterated his longstanding theory that Waymo's current commercial operations may be supported by extensive remote human monitoring, which he argued could be executed by a relatively small number of remote operators given the current fleet size and controlled operating conditions. He contrasted this with Tesla's FSD, which operates in millions of privately owned vehicles across diverse, uncontrolled real-world conditions — making remote monitoring structurally impossible and therefore serving as proof that Tesla's autonomy is more genuine. He also cited Waymo's reported loss of approximately $100 per ride as consistent with a heavily labor-subsidized operation.
