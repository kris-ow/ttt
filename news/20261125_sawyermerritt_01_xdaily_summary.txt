## Executive Summary
FSD V15 is reaching more of the fleet, including Model Ys without a front bumper camera. Demand signals for Tesla hardware look healthy: the Cybertruck led U.S. used-truck sales in October, and WattEV took its first Tesla Semis. On the SpaceX side, SpaceX gave up $738 million in federal broadband subsidies in favor of free Starlink hardware for eligible households, while Lucid, Nuro and Uber began fully driverless testing in the Bay Area.

## Key Posts / Themes

1. **FSD V15 rollout widens:** Sawyer reports that FSD V15 is now starting to roll out to Model Ys that have no front bumper camera. The rollout therefore extends to vehicles without the newer camera hardware, not just the latest-spec cars. A separate clip showed V15 driving around an object in the road, with Tesla's Reality View visualization displaying the obstacle. Both posts drew solid engagement (~3.3K–3.4K likes each).

2. **Cybertruck tops the used-truck market:** The Cybertruck was the fastest-selling used truck in the U.S. in October 2026, and the sixth fastest-selling used vehicle overall. It spent an average of just 39 days on the market. In the truck ranking it led the Ford Maverick (hybrid), Rivian R1T and Ford F-150 Lightning. Fast resale turnover points to steady secondary-market demand for the Cybertruck despite its limited remaining new-vehicle lineup.

3. **Starlink forgoes $738M in federal broadband subsidies:** SpaceX agreed to give up $738 million in U.S. federal broadband subsidies. Instead it will provide free Starlink hardware to eligible American households. Per the NTIA, SpaceX will still serve the covered broadband serviceable locations (BSLs) so that no consumer at those locations is left behind. This was the day's most-engaged post (~6.3K likes, 736 reposts). It frames Starlink as able to meet rural-coverage obligations without subsidy dollars, though SpaceX forgoes that revenue in exchange for absorbing hardware costs.

4. **Robotaxi product refinement:** Tesla's Robotaxi service now lets riders extend their pickup wait time to up to 15 minutes. It is a small usability improvement aimed at the ride-hailing experience.

5. **Tesla Semi commercial deliveries:** WattEV has taken delivery of its first Tesla Semis at the Port of Oakland, part of its planned 300+ truck fleet. This adds to the list of commercial fleet operators bringing the Semi into service.

6. **Competitive robotaxi pressure in the Bay Area:** Lucid, Nuro and Uber announced the start of fully driverless robotaxi testing on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area, with no safety driver. Driverless employee rides will begin soon, and a public launch is expected in Q1 2027. California is a market where Tesla has yet to secure robotaxi approval, so this is another well-funded entrant moving toward driverless service there.

## Signal Strength: **Mildly Bullish** — FSD V15 is broadening to more hardware and Cybertruck/Semi demand signals are positive, partly offset by a new driverless competitor advancing in California, where Tesla has not yet launched robotaxi.
