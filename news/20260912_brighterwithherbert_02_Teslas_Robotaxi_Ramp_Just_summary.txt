This roundup from brighterwithherbert covers a week of major Tesla news: the Cybercab reaching 1 million unsupervised robotaxi miles, new rider-reported Cybercab pricing undercutting Model Y and Uber, a NHTSA audit query into Cybercab's federal safety certification, FSD's expansion to a sixth EU country ahead of an October 6 continent-wide vote, mixed China/Berlin vehicle sales data, and unconfirmed supply-chain reports of a large Optimus parts order. The host's overall framing is bullish on the confirmed Cybercab data points (miles, registrations, specs, pricing) while flagging the audit, EU vote, and Optimus figures as unresolved items with dates attached.

## Robotaxi
1. **1 million unsupervised miles milestone:** At the September 3rd Cybercab launch event in Austin, Ashok Elluswamy announced Tesla robotaxi had reached 1 million unsupervised miles. Tesla had disclosed 380,000 unsupervised miles on the Q2 earnings call (July 22), implying roughly 620,000 miles added in about six weeks; the host's contributor calculated this as roughly a 17%-per-week compounded growth rate (versus Tesla's own stated "more than 10% a week" on the Q2 call), explicitly noting this 17% figure is not a Tesla number but derived math. Unsupervised operation is currently active in six cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa) with roughly 200 vehicles running unsupervised; the Bay Area still uses safety monitors. For comparison, Waymo announced 200 million autonomous miles the week before.
2. **Cybercab pricing undercuts Model Y and Uber in rider reports:** Starting September 4th, the Austin robotaxi app began showing Cybercab as a separate, cheaper option next to Model Y for identical routes — rider screenshots showed one trip at $12 (Model Y) vs. $7.77 (Cybercab), and another at ~$17 (Model Y) vs. under $10 (Cybercab) vs. $21 (Uber), roughly 35–40% under Model Y and about half of Uber. Tesla has not published an official per-mile rate, and by the following Monday high demand pushed some Cybercab prices above Model Y with over an hour wait — a supply issue, not evidence of a fixed discount.
3. **Cybercab efficiency specs and cost-per-mile claims:** A spec sheet shared by Sawyer Merritt lists a 47.66 kWh pack, ~3,113 lb weight, and about 6.1 miles per kWh (Tesla's own impact-report figure, not an EPA rating) — versus roughly 4 mi/kWh for Model Y. Elon Musk called Cybercab "optimized for lowest possible cost per mile, fully loaded" in a reply. ARK Invest's newsletter projected roughly 25 cents per mile at scale, versus roughly 70 cents/mile to own a car and about $2/mile for ride-hail — a longstanding ARK projection, not Tesla guidance.
4. **Fleet-partner recruitment form launched:** Just before the launch event, Tesla posted a "help us build our robotaxi network" form covering fleet vehicle purchasing, mobility hubs/infrastructure, and event collaboration; it explicitly states submissions are "a request to be considered," not a purchase order. Eric Early (Tesla's Cybercab engineering lead) said in a since-deleted reply that submissions were in the "tens of thousands"; a circulating figure of 73,000 could not be sourced by the host. Elon Musk described the eventual model as Airbnb-like, letting owners add/remove cars from the fleet, and said he is "highly confident" owner revenue will exceed monthly payments.
5. **Texas registrations and production ramp:** Texas DMV's public automated-vehicle registration list jumped from 314 to 420 vehicles the day before the launch event, reaching 432 by the following Monday (per Jeff Lutz's count); Cybercab-specific registrations rose from 1 to 45 in late August and reached 49 registrations by Tuesday, against roughly 380 registered Model Ys. Drone footage from Joe Tegtmeyer reportedly showed many more Cybercabs at Giga Texas, some equipped for the first time with Starlink hardware in the hatch (not yet active for riders). Elon Musk said Cybercab production is redesigned to run "over five times faster" than conventional auto manufacturing — implying roughly 7-second cycle times versus Model Y's ~34 seconds — but the host notes the previously cited "under 10 seconds" figure is a design target from a prior shareholder meeting, not a confirmed current production rate.

## Autonomous Driving
1. **NHTSA audit query on Cybercab certification:** On launch day, NHTSA opened an audit query titled "Tesla Cybercab FMVSS certification" covering an estimated population of 10,000 vehicles, examining the process and data Tesla used to self-certify the steering-wheel/pedal-free Cybercab as compliant with federal motor vehicle safety standards. This is a review of Tesla's certification process, not a defect investigation or recall; Tesla self-certified (an uncapped route) rather than seeking NHTSA's exemption route (capped at 2,500 vehicles, the path Zoox used). TSLA stock dropped about 6% the following Friday on this news.
2. **FSD expands to a sixth EU country:** Tesla announced FSD Supervised approval in Slovenia (confirmed by Slovenia's traffic safety agency), the first new EU country approval in about three months, following the Netherlands (April), then Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium (May–June). All six recognize the original Dutch approval rather than independent testing; together they represent under 10% of the EU population. A October 6 vote by the EU's technical committee on motor vehicles will determine whether the Dutch approval extends to all 27 countries, requiring 15 countries covering 65% of the population — Tesla says France, Sweden, and Germany have raised specific concerns (a speed-offset feature, speed concerns, ongoing technical review respectively).
3. **New FSD build adds active collision/distraction intervention:** FSD version 14.3.9 began rolling out, adding functionality where FSD Supervised can activate on the driver's behalf if an imminent collision is detected and automatic emergency braking alone would be insufficient, or if the driver appears heavily distracted or has accidentally disengaged FSD; active collision avoidance is highway-only under 85 mph, and wheel-grab intervention works between 22–85 mph, requiring Hardware 4 and an active FSD subscription. The rollout was briefly pulled from the pending queue Tuesday before resuming at small scale.

## Electric Vehicles
1. **Model Y remains global sales leader; Berlin capacity constrained:** Model Y sales were up 19.6% year-to-date (January–July) per Focus2Move, roughly 200,000 units ahead of the RAV4 and the only vehicle in the global top four growing this year. Giga Berlin is running under 6,500 vehicles/week against a new ~7,500/week target for mid-October, prompting three mandatory extra shifts (Sept 18, 19, 25); output is up about 20% since June.
2. **China sales mixed; new discounts announced:** August China retail sales were 50,000 vehicles, down 12.4% year-over-year but up 84% from July; Shanghai exports rose almost 39%, and total wholesale was up about 3.6% year-to-date, with Shanghai now exporting more than it sells domestically (not true a year ago). Tesla ranked fifth in China in August behind BYD, Geely, Leapmotor, and Changan. Tesla China responded Monday with roughly $1,500–$5,000 discounts on in-inventory Model Y and Model 3 units through September 30, stacking with existing paint credits and 5-year 0% financing.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Unconfirmed report of large Optimus parts order:** Chinese outlet GMN News reported, citing supply chain sources, that Tesla placed component orders in early September for about 5,000 Optimus units — described as the program's first production order in the thousands after pilot runs of a few hundred. Supplier guidance reportedly calls for 1,000 units/week in September ramping to 2,500/week by year-end (the 2,500/week figure was previously reported in July, not new). The host stresses this is a supply-chain rumor Tesla has not commented on or confirmed, and that Tesla has given no 2026 production number nor confirmed Fremont has started Optimus production; Elon Musk said in July that Optimus production would be "extremely slow at first as everything is new."
2. **Hand engineering hiring push:** Tesla has open Palo Alto listings for a hand design engineer and a program manager focused on a "mass manufacturable hand," reflecting hands as the current bottleneck for Optimus; these listings were posted by the Optimus account back in June.
3. **Elon Musk's G20 remarks on AI/robot economic impact:** On September 1st, Musk told the G20 innovation meeting that digital AI alone could lift the global economy 20–30% (roughly $20–30 trillion/year), and separately projected "well over a billion humanoid robots in 10 years," each about five times as productive as a person. He followed up September 3rd with a post stating AI and robots will "more than double the global economy in less than 10 years."
