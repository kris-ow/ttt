Tesla stock fell more than 12% (over $140 billion in market value) after Q2 2026 earnings missed on profit and free cash flow, with the selloff worsening after Elon Musk repeated familiar robotaxi and Optimus claims on the earnings call.

## Financials
1. **Revenue and deliveries hit records, but profit collapsed:** Tesla posted record revenue of $28.24 billion (up 26% YoY) and record deliveries of 480,126 vehicles. Operating income fell 57% to $398 million, operating margin dropped to 1.4% from 4.1% a year earlier, and adjusted EPS was $0.33, below the roughly $0.53 Wall Street expected.
2. **Free cash flow turns negative:** Free cash flow was negative $1.09 billion as capital spending jumped 142% to $5.79 billion. Regulatory credit revenue fell 67% to $146 million. The article notes record deliveries were aided by incentives and cheap financing, which pressured automotive margins.
3. **Energy margins soften despite deployment growth:** Energy storage deployments grew 41% to 13.5 GWh, but margins in the energy business declined.

## Humanoid Bots
1. **Musk repeats Optimus positioning:** Musk said "No humanoid robots today can do generalized tasks without programming, but Optimus will be the first to do it." The article argues this is the exact breakthrough every humanoid robotics company is pursuing, and nothing in the quarter demonstrates Tesla is ahead; mass production remains undated.

## Robotaxi / Autonomous Driving
1. **Unsupervised mileage claims disputed on scale:** Tesla cited more than 380,000 unsupervised miles across six cities with "zero notable incidents" (a Tesla-defined term). The article contrasts this with Waymo, which it says logs comparable autonomous mileage in about a day versus roughly six months for Tesla.
2. **Fleet growth has stalled:** Per Tesla's own chart cited in the article, paid Robotaxi miles were flat quarter-over-quarter at roughly 900,000, and the active unsupervised fleet has shrunk to around 21 cars.
3. **New service areas launched, but fleet size unchanged:** Days before earnings, Tesla activated Robotaxi service in Orlando and Tampa. The article argues this expands the map without adding vehicles, so it doesn't reflect real scaling.
4. **Musk cites safety as limiting factor:** Musk again pointed to the "march of nines" — the long tail of edge cases — as the constraint on further deployment, which the article says he has cited as a moving goalpost before.

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues investors are losing trust in Musk's promises after repeated missed self-driving deadlines, calling the stock drop the moment his "word isn't worth what they've been paying for it." He contends FSD v14 is an impressive but supervised Level 2 system, not the unsupervised robotaxi future Tesla has sold, and criticizes Tesla for hitching its valuation to AI/robotics narratives that are now faltering — even as rising oil prices (crude up ~5%, above $90/barrel) would normally favor EV demand and Tesla's core business.
