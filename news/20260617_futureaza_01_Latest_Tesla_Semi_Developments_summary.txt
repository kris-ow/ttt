This episode of FutureAzA, hosted by Brian with guest Mark from The Tesla Life, focuses primarily on new drone and ground footage of the Tesla Semi Giga factory (referred to as "Giga Eye West"), captured by field reporter Zangler. The hosts analyze visual evidence of production progress at the Tesla Semi manufacturing facility, discuss a range of viewer comments on topics ranging from solar charging to FSD legislation, and close with a "Wrong Answers Only" comedy segment.

## Electric Vehicles

1. **New Tesla Semi footage from Zangler:** Fresh aerial and ground-level footage from the Tesla Semi production facility shows a variety of activity. The parking lot previously occupied by large equipment staging has been cleared and now serves as standard employee parking, suggesting a construction/installation phase has concluded. Employee vehicles are a mix of cars, trucks, and motorcycles — indicating a blend of contractors and regular employees — with a growing number of Teslas visible.

2. **Six new-design Tesla Semis spotted in a corral:** Six Tesla Semis were observed grouped together in a cordoned-off area near what is believed to be the end of the assembly line exit door. Two were identified as long-cab variants and four as short-cab variants. The new design is distinguished from the older "Atlas" prototype generation ("Arcus") by a headlight bar that runs all the way across the front. The hosts note that the shorter cab likely corresponds to the standard-range variant, which uses two large battery modules versus three in the long-range version.

3. **Speculation on a short-range "port version":** Brian speculates — based on no inside information, by his own admission — that Tesla could potentially produce a single-battery-module variant with roughly 100 miles of range, optimized for port operations where short-range is sufficient. He suggests this could also help margins and relieve battery supply constraints if demand outpaces battery production.

4. **Gen 1 Tesla Semi still in active use on-site:** An older-generation Tesla Semi (identifiable by the cab window design extending further back) was observed actively moving pallets and performing logistics work on the factory grounds. The hosts note this is a practical use of early prototype builds.

5. **Large custom pallets observed — likely for battery packs:** Oversized metal pallet-like racks with forklift cutouts were spotted at the facility. Brian theorizes these are purpose-built carriers for the Semi's heavy battery packs, given their size (roughly twice that of a standard pallet) and heavy-duty tubular/box steel construction.

6. **Mystery black dots on cab rooftops:** New black dots were observed on the rooflines of all the new-design cabs in the footage. The hosts are uncertain of their purpose — ruling out rear-view cameras as impractical from that position — and invite viewer speculation in the "Wrong Answers Only" format.

7. **ABF Freight adds two Tesla Semis:** A news item dated June 15, 2026 reports that ABF added two Tesla Semis to its fleet. The image accompanying the story appears to show older pilot-model Semis rather than the current production design.

8. **Costco operating older-design Tesla Semis:** The hosts confirm Costco has Tesla Semis but note these are the older design, not the current production variant.

9. **Creature comforts and future iterations:** In response to European feedback that the Tesla Semi lacks creature comforts, Mark notes Tesla iterates its vehicles quickly and issues will be addressed. Brian adds that sleeper cab variants, when introduced, will offer more amenity options; the current version is a day cab.

10. **Solar charging on vehicles — math breakdown:** Brian works through the physics of rooftop solar for EVs. At 100% panel efficiency on a 2 square meter roof, a car could capture 8–10 kWh per day — enough for roughly 24–30 miles. At realistic efficiency (approximately half), this drops to about 12 miles per day, under optimal stationary conditions (no shading, full sun, optimal tilt). Commenter Henry elaborates that automotive-grade solar integration requires curved panels, shatter-safe glass, DCDC converters (30–50V to 1000V), adds cost of $3,000+, and represents only one-third the area of a typical roof-mounted system — making rooftop home solar a far better investment. Brian and Mark conclude the technology is not yet viable for meaningful daily driving range contribution.

11. **Aptera solar EV — disappointment with production delays:** The hosts discuss Aptera, whose three-wheel, two-seat, aerodynamically optimized design is the closest real-world attempt at a solar-sufficient EV. Brian expresses disappointment that Aptera has not yet reached customer deliveries. He notes that when Aptera was pitching at ~$35,000 it was a compelling alternative to $60,000 EVs, but now that EVs can be had for under $35,000, Aptera's value proposition is harder to justify — especially with production timelines still unclear.

12. **FSD in Canada — supervised only:** Mark confirms FSD is in use across Canada but remains supervised. No legislative approvals for unsupervised FSD operation in Canadian provinces have been announced.

13. **Chinese EV tariff legislation and Tesla:** A viewer raises whether a hypothetical U.S. law blocking China-built EVs would apply to Teslas built in Giga Shanghai, given they run U.S. software rather than Chinese software. The hosts suggest Tesla may have an exemption path on that basis, though they acknowledge other Chinese automakers could simply adopt U.S. software to circumvent such a rule.

## Market & Competition

1. **Rivian commercial vans beyond Amazon:** In response to a viewer question, the hosts confirm Rivian is selling commercial vans to customers beyond Amazon, citing HelloFresh/Factor-branded vans and noting that "Also" vans recently seen at an event are a wholly owned Rivian subsidiary.

2. **Horizon Motors positioning against Tesla Semi:** The hosts reference Horizon Motors as a competitor apparently marketing itself to the small segment of buyers who dislike Tesla, rather than competing head-on. Brian interprets this as an implicit concession that they cannot match Tesla's product.
