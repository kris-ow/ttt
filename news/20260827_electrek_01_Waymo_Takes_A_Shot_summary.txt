Waymo published a list of "10 AI lessons" from over 200 million fully autonomous miles that, without naming Tesla, appear to critique Tesla's self-driving approach — arguing that upgrading a driver-assist system into full autonomy is a "false summit," that camera-only sensing can't deliver safe full autonomy, and that end-to-end neural networks are an untrustworthy "black box."

## Autonomous Driving
1. **Waymo's "false summit" argument:** Waymo's head of AI foundations, Srikanth Thirumalai, wrote that "simply improving a driver-assist system (L2) for full autonomy is a false summit," and that true L4 maturity requires a purpose-built system validated on closed courses and "hardened by the uncompromising experience of driving without a human in the car" — a framing the article says is aimed at Tesla's strategy of training FSD on data from customer cars whose drivers remain legally responsible.
2. **Sensor and architecture critiques:** Waymo's list states cameras alone cannot deliver safe, full-scale autonomy, citing its own use of cameras, lidar, and radar for redundancy — contrasted with Tesla's camera-only "Tesla Vision" stack. It also warns against pure end-to-end neural networks that map camera input directly to steering commands, stating "you can't build trust with a black box," which the article ties to Tesla's end-to-end approach used in FSD v14 and v15.
3. **Tesla's unsupervised mileage:** The article states Tesla confirmed last month that its Robotaxi program has driven only 380,000 unsupervised miles in a year, which the article says Waymo covers "in a day."

## Robotaxi
1. **Waymo's scale vs. Tesla's:** Waymo is described as serving more than 500,000 paid, fully driverless rides per week across US markets, targeting one million weekly rides by year-end. Tesla's Robotaxi service, launched in Austin with human safety monitors in the car, is described as small, with the article noting that "by some counts" its fleet has shrunk rather than grown. Musk's repeated promises of widespread "unsupervised" FSD by year-end are noted as a target he has "moved repeatedly."

## Editorial Stance
Electrek's Fred Lambert argues Waymo's uncredited jabs map directly onto Tesla's specific design choices (camera-only sensing, end-to-end black-box AI, incremental L2-to-L4 upgrading) and that Musk has repeatedly declared "false summit" victories on autonomy before. The author states Waymo is ahead and scaling faster, calling its sensor-fusion approach "much more robust" than Tesla's vision-only system, while acknowledging he doesn't know if Waymo's thesis is ultimately correct and that no clear evidence yet supports Tesla fans' expectation of a generalization breakthrough that overtakes Waymo.
